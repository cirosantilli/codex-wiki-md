# Dual space

↑ **Parent:** [Linear algebra](linear-algebra-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_space)

The [dual space](dual-space-split.md) of a [vector space](vector-space-split.md) $V$ over a [field](field.md) $F$ is the [vector space](vector-space-split.md) of all [linear functionals](linear-functional.md) $\varphi:V\to F$. Think of a [vector](vector.md) as an input and a [linear functional](linear-functional.md) as a scalar-valued measurement of that input. For $V=\mathbb R^2$, the rules $(x,y)\mapsto x$, $(x,y)\mapsto y$, and $(x,y)\mapsto2x-y$ are different elements of the [dual space](dual-space-split.md). None needs a notion of [length](length.md) or [angle](angle.md).

Its operations combine measurements pointwise:

$$
(\varphi+\psi)(v)=\varphi(v)+\psi(v),\qquad
(a\varphi)(v)=a\varphi(v).
$$

The [dual basis](dual-basis.md) explains the finite-dimensional structure: specifying a [linear functional](linear-functional.md)'s values on a [basis](basis.md) determines its value on every [vector](vector.md). Thus an $n$-dimensional [vector space](vector-space-split.md) has an $n$-dimensional [dual space](dual-space-split.md). The spaces have the same [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md), but their elements play different roles: an input and a rule that measures an input. [Axler, Linear Algebra Done Right, fourth edition, §3F, definitions 3.109–3.112](linear-algebra-done-right-by-sheldon-axler-4th-edition.md).

This separation is useful when changing coordinates. The [dual pairing](dual-pairing.md) $\varphi(v)$ must stay the same while the component lists for $v$ and $\varphi$ change in opposite ways. [Covector transformation under a change of basis](covector-transformation-under-a-change-of-basis.md) makes the compensation explicit. An [inner product](inner-product-split.md) on a finite-dimensional real [vector space](vector-space-split.md) gives one way to represent each [linear functional](linear-functional.md) by a [vector](vector.md), but that representation uses the chosen [inner product](inner-product-split.md); the [dual space](dual-space-split.md) itself does not require one.

Here [dual space](dual-space-split.md) means the [algebraic dual](dual-space-split.md): all [linear functionals](linear-functional.md). For a [topological vector space](topological-vector-space-split.md), the [continuous dual space](continuous-dual-space-split.md) keeps only the [continuous linear functionals](continuous-linear-functional.md). In infinite [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md), this can be a proper [vector subspace](vector-subspace.md); [discontinuous functional detected by vanishing sequence tails](discontinuous-functional-detected-by-vanishing-sequence-tails.md) gives an example. The distinction matters when a scalar-valued measurement must respond continuously to small input changes.
