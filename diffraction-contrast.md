# Diffraction contrast

↑ **Parent:** [Transmission electron microscopy contrast](transmission-electron-microscopy-contrast.md)

[Diffraction contrast](diffraction-contrast.md) is image-intensity variation caused by spatially varying [electron diffraction](electron-diffraction.md) conditions. JEOL documents a [silicon](silicon.md) semiconductor-interconnect specimen at $200\,\mathrm{kV}$: its differently oriented [crystals](crystal.md) are imaged in [bright-field transmission electron microscopy](bright-field-transmission-electron-microscopy.md) and same-area [dark-field transmission electron microscopy](dark-field-transmission-electron-microscopy.md), alongside an [electron diffraction](electron-diffraction.md) pattern. Selecting the marked diffracted beam makes the corresponding [crystal](crystal.md) bright after it was dark. The preparation route, thickness and exposure are not supplied. The changed [objective aperture](objective-aperture.md) selection provides a comparison within one specimen; the readout supports orientation-dependent contrast rather than a map of surface height or [chemical composition](chemical-composition.md).[Definition and silicon example, panels a–c: 200 kV, same-area comparison and selected diffraction spot](diffraction-contrast-by-jeol.md)

This comparison is useful precisely because the material has not been replaced between the two images. It does not prove that thickness and [chemical composition](chemical-composition.md) never influence [transmission electron microscopy contrast](transmission-electron-microscopy-contrast.md). Instead, it supplies a concrete counterexample to assigning either quantity uniquely from brightness. A proposed extension is to repeat the first aperture setting after the second: recovery of the original contrast tests whether specimen motion or irradiation changed the comparison.
