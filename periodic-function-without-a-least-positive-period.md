# Periodic function without a least positive period

↑ **Parent:** [Fundamental period of a function](fundamental-period-of-a-function.md)

Define the [indicator function](indicator-function.md) of the [rational numbers](rational-number.md) on the [real line](real-line.md) by $f(x)=1$ for rational $x$ and $f(x)=0$ otherwise. Every rational shift preserves rationality and irrationality, so every nonzero rational number is a [period of a function](period-of-a-function.md). Conversely, an irrational shift sends $x=0$ from value one to value zero, so it is not a period. The [period group of a function](period-group-of-a-function.md) is exactly $\mathbb Q$, and the positive periods $1/n$ approach zero without a smallest one. The [function](function-split.md) is [nonconstant](nonconstant-function.md) and discontinuous everywhere; this is why [continuity](continuous-function.md) matters in [continuous periodic functions have a least positive period](continuous-periodic-functions-have-a-least-positive-period.md).
