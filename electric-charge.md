# Electric charge

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_charge)

[Electric charge](electric-charge.md) is an additive, signed property of matter that determines its coupling to the [electromagnetic field](electromagnetic-field.md). Write a particle's [electric charge](electric-charge.md) as $q$, or a collection's total [electric charge](electric-charge.md) as $Q$; the [SI](international-system-of-units.md) unit is the [coulomb](coulomb.md), $\mathrm C$. A stationary test particle in an [electric field](electric-field.md) experiences [electric force](electric-force.md) $\mathbf F=q\mathbf E$, while motion adds the magnetic part of the [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md). [Electric charge](electric-charge.md) both produces an [electric field](electric-field.md) and determines the response to one. For two stationary point particles in [vacuum](vacuum.md), [Coulomb's law](coulomb-s-law.md) makes like signs repel and opposite signs attract. The choice of which sign to call positive is a convention, used consistently: [protons](proton.md) carry $+e$ and [Electrons](electron.md) carry $-e$, where $e$ is the [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md). [Feynman, Leighton and Sands, II.1: electrical forces, fields and the Lorentz force](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_01.html).

Additivity means adding signed [electric charges](electric-charge.md), rather than their magnitudes:

$$
Q=\sum_i q_i,\qquad Q=e(N_p-N_e)
$$

for ordinary matter containing $N_p$ [protons](proton.md), $N_e$ [Electrons](electron.md), and electrically neutral [neutrons](neutron.md). A toy collection with seven [protons](proton.md) and ten [Electrons](electron.md) has $Q=-3e$, however much neutral material accompanies it. [Charge neutrality](charge-neutrality.md) means $Q=0$; it does not mean an absence of charged constituents. Nor does it guarantee an absence of [electric force](electric-force.md): the spatial arrangement of positive and negative [electric charges](electric-charge.md) matters, as illustrated by an [electric dipole](electric-dipole.md). This makes a tiny imbalance in an otherwise nearly neutral object physically important. [Feynman, Leighton and Sands, II.1.1: neutrality, imbalance and residual electrical forces](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_01.html#Ch1-S1).

[Charge conservation](charge-conservation.md) states that a collection closed to transport of [electric charge](electric-charge.md) keeps its total signed $Q$. If $n$ [Electrons](electron.md) move from object A to object B without other exchange, then $\Delta Q_A=+ne$ and $\Delta Q_B=-ne$; the sum is unchanged. The amount of positive [electric charge](electric-charge.md) alone need not be conserved: creation of an [Electron](electron.md) and a [Positron](positron.md) adds opposite [electric charges](electric-charge.md) with zero sum. These bookkeeping rules let us distinguish separation or transport of [electric charge](electric-charge.md) from creation of net [electric charge](electric-charge.md). [Feynman, Leighton and Sands, I.4.4: conservation of net charge](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_04.html#Ch4-S4).

A simple motivating experiment uses two charge-producing paddles, a conducting pail on an insulating support, a grounded outer shield and a high-input-impedance [electrometer](electrometer.md). Zero the pail, then isolate it. Rub the initially neutral paddles together, keeping their charged surfaces away from other objects. Lower each deeply into the pail without touching: the [electrometer](electrometer.md) gives opposite signs. Inserting both together tests cancellation. For a separate contact test, insert one charged paddle without touching and withdraw it: the reading returns to zero. Repeat after zeroing, this time touching the inner wall before withdrawal: a residual reading shows transfer of [electric charge](electric-charge.md). Zero the pail and reinsert the paddle to test how much [electric charge](electric-charge.md) remains on it. PASCO describes these procedures in [Basic Electrostatics System manual, demonstrations 1A and 1B, printed pp. 13–16](https://cdn.pasco.com/product_document/Basic-Electrostatics-Sys-Manual-ES-9080B1.pdf#page=13).

![](electric-charge-transfer-schematic.png)

**[Figure 16](#image-electric-charge-redistribution-versus-transfer-in-a-conducting-pail). Electric charge redistribution versus transfer in a conducting pail**. [Source](electric-charge-transfer-schematic.py). Original qualitative schematic for a positively charged inserted object. Without contact, [electrostatic induction](electrostatic-induction.md) separates negative inner-surface and positive outer-surface [electric charges](electric-charge.md), while the pail's total remains zero. Contact can leave net positive [electric charge](electric-charge.md) after withdrawal. Symbols are not particle counts; no measured readings are plotted.

The crucial control is withdrawal without contact: a response during insertion can come from [electrostatic induction](electrostatic-induction.md), which redistributes the pail's existing [electric charge](electric-charge.md). In the fixed-geometry approximation, the [electrometer](electrometer.md) measures [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) proportional to enclosed [electric charge](electric-charge.md), with conversion set by effective [capacitance](capacitance.md); a voltage reading is not already a measurement in [coulombs](coulomb.md). Opposite readings and their cancellation test additivity and [charge conservation](charge-conservation.md) within resolution. Leakage or accidental contact spoils that interpretation, and contact need not transfer all the paddle's [electric charge](electric-charge.md). This macroscopic experiment cannot resolve [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md) steps: that requires the much smaller particles of the [oil drop experiment](oil-drop-experiment.md).
