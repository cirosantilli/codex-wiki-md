# Seven-triangle Dirichlet transplantation

↑ **Parent:** [Transplantation theorem](transplantation-theorem.md)

Take seven copies of a scalene [triangle](triangle.md), with sides labelled $a,b,c$ opposite its three vertices, and reflect adjacent copies across their glued side. For the first [planar domain](planar-domain.md), the glued pairs are $a:(2,3),(6,7)$, $b:(4,6),(5,7)$, $c:(1,5),(3,7)$; for the second they are $a:(1,3),(5,7)$, $b:(2,6),(3,7)$, $c:(4,5),(6,7)$. Both coloured trees have central tile $7$. For triangles sufficiently close to equilateral the interiors do not overlap. If its three angles are distinct and between $\pi/4$ and $\pi/2$, the only reentrant vertices are the original central vertices, with angles four times the corresponding triangle angles. They force any proposed [isometry](isometry.md) to preserve the labelled central triangle, which is incompatible with the different attachments on its arms.

For the spectral proof index the nonzero vectors and covectors of $\mathbb F_2^3$ by $1,\ldots,7$, using binary coordinates. The [Fano plane](fano-plane.md) incidence matrix is $B_{y,x}=\mathbf1_{y\cdot x=0}$. It satisfies $B^TB=2I+J$, so is invertible, and intertwines the point and line permutations of each of the three elementary transvections. Put $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,1,-1,-1,1)$. The signed reflection matrices for the [Dirichlet boundary condition](dirichlet-boundary-condition.md) are $-DP_sD$ and $-DQ_sD$, hence $C=DBD$ intertwines them. Its polar orthogonal factor gives a [unitary equivalence](unitary-equivalence.md) of the [Dirichlet Laplacians](dirichlet-laplacian.md), preserving both the $L^2$ [inner product](inner-product.md) and the Dirichlet energy of the piecewise functions. This produces a three-parameter family of noncongruent isospectral [planar domains](planar-domain.md).
