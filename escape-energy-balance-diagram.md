# Escape-energy balance diagram

↑ **Parent:** [Escape velocity](escape-velocity.md)

Use the synthetic [standard gravitational parameter](standard-gravitational-parameter.md) $\mu=8\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$ and launch distance $r_0=2\,\mathrm m$. Then [escape speed](escape-velocity.md) is $2\sqrt2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Three outward radial launches give exact comparisons: $v_0=2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ has [specific orbital energy](specific-orbital-energy.md) $-2\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and stops outward at $r=4\,\mathrm m$; $v_0=2\sqrt2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ has zero [specific orbital energy](specific-orbital-energy.md); $v_0=2\sqrt3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ has [specific orbital energy](specific-orbital-energy.md) $+2\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and asymptotic [speed](speed.md) $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The second case has [speed](speed.md) $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $r=4\,\mathrm m$. Doubling $r_0$ divides [escape speed](escape-velocity.md) by $\sqrt2$, whereas doubling $M$ at fixed $r_0$ multiplies it by $\sqrt2$. Changing the [test particle](test-particle.md)'s [mass](mass.md) changes $E$ but cancels from the [escape speed](escape-velocity.md).

Computed curves use $x=r/r_0$ and $\mathcal E=\varepsilon/(\mu/r_0)$. The left panel compares $U/m=-\mu/r$ with constant [specific orbital energies](specific-orbital-energy.md) $\mathcal E=-1/2,0,+1/2$. The right panel shows [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) per unit [mass](mass.md), divided by $\mu/r_0$, equal to $\mathcal E+1/x$. Only the physically allowed outward branch is drawn for negative [energy](energy-split.md), ending at $x=2$. These are synthetic predictions, not measurements.
