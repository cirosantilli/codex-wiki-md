# Primordial helium response to a varying gravitational constant

↑ **Parent:** [Helium sensitivity to the expansion rate](helium-sensitivity-to-the-expansion-rate.md)

If the [Friedmann equation](friedmann-equations.md) remains approximately $H^2=8\pi G\rho/3$, [radiation domination](radiation-domination.md) and fixed weak microphysics give $H\propto\sqrt G\,T^2$ and a neutron-proton conversion rate proportional to $T^5$. [Cosmological weak freeze-out](cosmological-weak-freeze-out.md) then occurs at $T_f\propto G^{1/6}$. Increasing $G$ during this era raises the [neutron-to-proton ratio](neutron-to-proton-ratio.md) at freeze-out and shortens the time for [free-neutron decay after freeze-out](free-neutron-decay-after-freeze-out.md). Both increase the [primordial helium mass fraction](primordial-helium-mass-fraction.md) when [neutron-limited helium synthesis](neutron-limited-helium-synthesis.md) remains efficient. Arbitrary variation of $G$ over time requires its history and the full gravitational field equations; this scaling is a controlled background approximation, rather than a complete model of gravity with arbitrary time dependence.
