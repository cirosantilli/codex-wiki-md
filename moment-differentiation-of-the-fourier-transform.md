# Moment differentiation of the Fourier transform

↑ **Parent:** [Fourier transform](fourier-transform.md)

If $f\in L^1(\mathbb R)$ and $x^kf\in L^1(\mathbb R)$ for $0\le k\le r$, then its [Fourier transform](fourier-transform.md) is $r$ times [continuously differentiable](continuously-differentiable-function.md) and the displayed formula holds. The [dominated convergence theorem](dominated-convergence-theorem.md) permits each [derivative](derivative.md) under the [integral](integral.md). Thus $r+1$ [vanishing moments](vanishing-moment.md) give a zero [Taylor polynomial](taylor-polynomial.md) through degree $r$ at the origin. Unweighted $L^1$ membership only guarantees a [continuous function](continuous-function.md) as the [Fourier transform](fourier-transform.md).
