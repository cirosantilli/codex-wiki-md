# Divergence of the conformal Brownian clock for an entire function

↑ **Parent:** [Conformal Brownian clock](conformal-brownian-clock.md)

For a nonconstant [entire function](entire-function.md), its derivative is nonzero somewhere and therefore bounded away from zero on a small disc. [Recurrence of planar Brownian motion](recurrence-of-planar-brownian-motion.md) and the [Strong Markov property](strong-markov-property.md) imply infinite total occupation time in that disc: repeat entrances into a smaller concentric disc after exits from the larger one. On each entrance there is a uniformly positive chance of remaining inside the larger disc for a fixed positive duration. Iterating the conditional failure bound gives infinitely many such sojourns almost surely.

The displayed integral is bounded below by a positive constant times this occupation time, so it diverges. The same conclusion follows by comparing with the [divergence of a positive planar Brownian occupation integral](divergence-of-a-positive-planar-brownian-occupation-integral.md). At every finite time the clock is finite, because the Brownian path has compact range and $f'$ is continuous. Thus the [conformal Brownian clock](conformal-brownian-clock.md) covers every nonnegative image time; [conformal invariance of planar Brownian motion](conformal-invariance-of-planar-brownian-motion.md) then yields a Brownian image with no missing finite or infinite lifetime.
