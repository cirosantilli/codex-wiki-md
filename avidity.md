# Avidity

↑ **Parent:** [Antibody](antibody.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidity)

[Avidity](avidity.md) is the combined strength of multivalent attachment. An intact [IgG](immunoglobulin-g.md) can make two contacts when accessible [epitopes](epitope.md) have suitable spacing. [Chapter 24, B Cells and Antibodies, A Typical Antibody Has Two Identical Antigen-Binding Sites; The Strength of an Antibody-Antigen Interaction Depends on Both the Number and the Affinity of the Antigen-Binding Sites: avidity](molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others.md) If one contact breaks while the other remains, the detached arm stays nearby and can bind again before the whole [antibody](antibody.md) escapes. That geometric advantage does not require changing either [paratope](paratope.md)'s intrinsic [antibody affinity](antibody-affinity.md). Two arms binding two separate freely moving [antigens](antigen.md) do not automatically provide the same tethering advantage. [Epitope](epitope.md) spacing, accessibility and surface density matter; [avidity](avidity.md) is not obtained by adding two [dissociation constants](dissociation-constant.md).
