# Ribosome

↑ **Parent:** [Biological cell](biological-cell.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome)

A [ribosome](ribosome.md) is the molecular machinery for [biological translation](translation-biology.md): it reads [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) and joins [amino acids](amino-acid.md) into a [polypeptide](polypeptide.md) that can become a [protein](protein.md). Both [prokaryotes](prokaryote.md) and [eukaryotes](eukaryote.md) have [ribosomes](ribosome.md). A [ribosome](ribosome.md) consists of [ribosomal RNA](ribosomal-rna.md) and [proteins](protein.md) organized into small and large subunits, with no enclosing membrane. [Ribosomal RNA](ribosomal-rna.md) contributes to both structure and the [active site](active-site.md) that forms [peptide bonds](peptide-bond.md); a [ribosome](ribosome.md) is therefore not merely a collection of [proteins](protein.md). See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 4.3: ribosome composition](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells) and [Nissen et al., The structural basis of ribosome activity in peptide bond synthesis, Science 289, 920–930 (2000)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10937990/).

Original schematic of a nuclear [protein](protein.md)-coding [gene](gene.md) in a nucleated [eukaryotic](eukaryote.md) [biological cell](biological-cell.md). The upper route tracks [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) to [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) by [transcription](transcription-biology.md), [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) export, then [biological translation](translation-biology.md). The lower example reads three [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) [codons](codon.md) from $5'$ to $3'$ and shows a growing [polypeptide](polypeptide.md) segment from its [N-terminus](n-terminus.md) toward its [C-terminus](c-terminus.md). Shapes and distances are illustrative; processing, flanking signals, additional machinery and termination are omitted.

For a computed decoding example, choose the [reading frame](reading-frame.md) starting at the first displayed [nucleotide](nucleotide.md) of $5'\text{-}\mathrm{AUG\ GCU\ GAA}\text{-}3'$. Under the standard [genetic code](genetic-code.md), AUG specifies [methionine](methionine.md) (Met), GCU specifies [alanine](alanine.md) (Ala), and GAA specifies a [glutamic acid residue](glutamic-acid-residue.md) (Glu) in the growing [polypeptide](polypeptide.md). Nine [nucleotides](nucleotide.md) divide into three [codons](codon.md), giving the ordered three-[amino acid residue](amino-acid-residue.md) segment Met–Ala–Glu. The [ribosome](ribosome.md) reads the [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) $5'\to3'$, and the chain grows from its [N-terminus](n-terminus.md) toward its [C-terminus](c-terminus.md). If synthesis begins at this AUG in an appropriate [eukaryotic](eukaryote.md) initiation context, these are the first three [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md) before any subsequent processing. See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 15.1: standard codon assignments](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/15-1-the-genetic-code).

For this three-[amino acid residue](amino-acid-residue.md) segment, joining the three delivered [amino acids](amino-acid.md) requires two [peptide bonds](peptide-bond.md), one between Met and Ala and another between Ala and Glu. There is no [stop codon](stop-codon.md) in the displayed [nucleotide sequence](nucleic-acid-sequence.md), so this is a growing segment rather than a complete released [protein](protein.md). An isolated nine-[nucleotide](nucleotide.md) [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) is not claimed to supply all signals needed for initiation. This small calculation separates three jobs: the [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) specifies order, [transfer RNA](transfer-rna.md) delivers the [amino acids](amino-acid.md), and the [ribosome](ribosome.md) catalyzes their joining. Each new [amino acid residue](amino-acid-residue.md) is added at the [C-terminus](c-terminus.md). See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 15.5: initiation and chain elongation](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/15-5-ribosomes-and-protein-synthesis).

The beauty of a [ribosome](ribosome.md) is its reusable chemistry: the same machinery can assemble many different [proteins](protein.md) by following different [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) templates. The [genetic code](genetic-code.md) connects an alphabet of [nucleotides](nucleotide.md) to an alphabet of [amino acids](amino-acid.md), while [transfer RNA](transfer-rna.md) links recognition of the message to delivery of building material. Changing the message can therefore change the product without building a different [ribosome](ribosome.md) for every [protein](protein.md).

In 1955 George E. Palade described small particles in the [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md), both free and associated with membranes, in [A small particulate component of the cytoplasm, Journal of Biophysical and Biochemical Cytology 1, 59–68](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2223592/). The paper documents the structural observations, rather than the full modern decoding mechanism. Complementary biochemical work by Hoagland, Stephenson, Scott, Hecht and Zamecnik described a soluble [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) intermediate in [protein](protein.md) synthesis in 1958, helping establish the role now assigned to [transfer RNA](transfer-rna.md); see [A soluble ribonucleic acid intermediate in protein synthesis, Journal of Biological Chemistry 231, 241–257](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13538965/). Together, structural observations and biochemical experiments connected visible cellular machinery with its working parts.
