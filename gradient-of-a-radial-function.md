# Gradient of a radial function

↑ **Parent:** [Gradient](gradient.md)

For $r=\sqrt{x_1^2+\cdots+x_d^2}>0$, the [multivariable chain rule](multivariable-chain-rule.md) gives

$$
\nabla g(r)=g'(r)\frac{(x_1,\ldots,x_d)}r.
$$

Indeed, $\partial r/\partial x_j=x_j/r$. Thus the [gradient](gradient.md) points radially outward when $g'(r)>0$, inward when it is negative, and vanishes when it is zero.

At the origin the displayed quotient does not apply. If $g$ has a finite right [derivative](derivative.md) at zero, the radial function is differentiable there exactly when $g'(0^+)=0$, in which case its [gradient](gradient.md) is zero. Necessity follows by comparing opposite directional difference quotients: their linear parts cannot both equal a common nonzero radial slope. Sufficiency follows from $g(r)-g(0)=o(r)$, the definition of differentiability with zero linear part.
