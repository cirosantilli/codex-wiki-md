# Prime support of an integer

↑ **Parent:** [Radical of an integer](radical-of-an-integer.md)

The [prime support of an integer](prime-support-of-an-integer.md) $n>0$ is the [finite set](finite-set.md) of its distinct [prime factors](prime-factor.md). For example, $P(72)=P(36)=\{2,3\}$, $P(30)=\{2,3,5\}$ and $P(1)$ is the [empty set](empty-set.md). Its [cardinality](cardinality.md) is the [prime omega function](prime-omega-function.md) $\omega(n)$, and the [product](product-of-numbers.md) of its elements is the [radical of an integer](radical-of-an-integer.md) $\operatorname{rad}(n)$. These are two different ways to summarize the same [finite set](finite-set.md): a count and a [product](product-of-numbers.md).
