# Electrical reference potential

↑ **Parent:** [Electric potential](electric-potential.md)

An [electrical reference potential](electrical-reference-potential.md) fixes the arbitrary additive constant of an [electric potential](electric-potential.md). Assigning zero to ground makes other potentials differences from that baseline; it does not assert that the [electric field](electric-field-split.md) vanishes there. If a source calculation gives $\Phi(a)=89.9\,\mathrm V$ and $\Phi(b)=179.8\,\mathrm V$ relative to infinity, choosing $a$ as the zero instead gives $\Phi'(a)=0$ and $\Phi'(b)=89.9\,\mathrm V$, with the same [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md). Grounding an [electrical conductor](electrical-conductor.md) additionally connects it to a reservoir that can exchange [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) to maintain the prescribed [electric potential](electric-potential.md); that physical boundary condition differs from merely relabeling an unchanged solution.[§7.3: potential references, including ground in Figure 7.18](university-physics-volume-2-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others/7-3-calculations-of-electric-potential.md); [Feynman lectures II.6, §6–8: a grounded conducting plane](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_06.html#Ch6-S8).
