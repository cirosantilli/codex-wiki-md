# Relative risk from group compositions

↑ **Parent:** [Risk ratio](risk-ratio.md)

Let $G$ be the [event](event.md) of membership in the first of two groups and $A$ the [event](event.md) of selection. Write $p=P(G)$, $a=P(G\mid A)$ and $q=P(A)>0$, with $0<p,a<1$. [Bayes' theorem](bayes-theorem.md) gives the [conditional probabilities](conditional-probability.md) $r_1=P(A\mid G)=aq/p$ and $r_0=P(A\mid G^c)=(1-a)q/(1-p)$. Their [risk ratio](risk-ratio.md) is the displayed expression: the unknown overall selection [probability](probability.md) cancels. Equivalently, it is the ratio of the group-membership [odds](odds.md) after selection to those before selection.

When both selection [probabilities](probability.md) lie strictly between zero and one, the selection [odds ratio](odds-ratio.md) is $[r_1/(1-r_1)]/[r_0/(1-r_0)]$ and generally depends on $q$. Thus compositions alone do not identify that [odds ratio](odds-ratio.md) or provide sample sizes for a [standard error](standard-error.md). When $a<p$, the first group's observed selection rate is lower; this comparison alone does not establish a [causal effect](causal-effect.md) of group membership.
