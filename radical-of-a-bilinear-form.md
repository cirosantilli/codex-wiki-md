# Radical of a bilinear form

↑ **Parent:** [Degenerate bilinear form](degenerate-bilinear-form.md)

For a [symmetric bilinear form](symmetric-bilinear-form.md) or an [alternating bilinear form](alternating-bilinear-form.md), the radical is the [linear subspace](vector-subspace.md)

$$
\operatorname{rad}B=\{v\in V:B(v,w)=0\text{ for every }w\in V\}.
$$

These [vectors](vector.md) are invisible to all pairings, and the [bilinear form](bilinear-form.md) is [nondegenerate](nondegenerate-bilinear-form.md) exactly when this [linear subspace](vector-subspace.md) is $\{0\}$. In [coordinates](coordinate-linear-algebra.md), $\operatorname{rad}B=\ker A$ for both kinds of [bilinear form](bilinear-form.md), since $A^T=A$ or $A^T=-A$. Thus $\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ has [radical of a bilinear form](radical-of-a-bilinear-form.md) $\operatorname{span}\{(0,1)\}$.

Passing to the [quotient vector space](quotient-vector-space.md) $V/\operatorname{rad}B$ removes precisely the invisible directions. The rule $\overline B([v],[w])=B(v,w)$ is well-defined because adding a [vector](vector.md) in the [radical of a bilinear form](radical-of-a-bilinear-form.md) to either input changes no pairing, and the resulting [bilinear form](bilinear-form.md) is [nondegenerate](nondegenerate-bilinear-form.md). For $B((x,y),(s,t))=xs$, this [quotient vector space](quotient-vector-space.md) retains just the $x$ direction and its form is ordinary multiplication.[Elman, Karpenko and Merkurjev, The Algebraic and Geometric Theory of Quadratic Forms, §1: radical and nondegenerate quotient](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~rse/oldbook/Kniga.pdf)

For a general [bilinear form](bilinear-form.md), distinguish the [left radical of a bilinear form](left-radical-of-a-bilinear-form.md) from the [right radical of a bilinear form](right-radical-of-a-bilinear-form.md); they can be different [linear subspaces](vector-subspace.md) even though their [dimensions](dimension-vector-space.md) agree in finite dimensions.
