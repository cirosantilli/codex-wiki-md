# Dominated differentiation under the integral sign

↑ **Parent:** [Differentiation under the integral sign](differentiation-under-the-integral-sign.md)

Let $t_0$ be interior to a parameter interval. Suppose $h(t,\cdot)$ is a [measurable function](measurable-function.md) for each $t$, $h(t_0,\cdot)$ is a [Lebesgue integrable function](lebesgue-integrable-function.md), and $h(\cdot,x)$ is differentiable near $t_0$ for $x$ [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md). If one integrable nonnegative function $D$ satisfies $|\partial_t h(t,x)|\leq D(x)$ throughout that neighbourhood off one fixed null set, then $F(t)=\int_Xh(t,x)\,d\mu(x)$ is defined there and satisfies the displayed derivative formula at $t_0$.

The [mean value theorem](mean-value-theorem.md) gives $|h(t,x)-h(t_0,x)|\leq|t-t_0|D(x)$, proving local integrability. It also bounds the difference quotient by $D$. That quotient converges [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md) to $\partial_t h(t_0,x)$, so the [dominated convergence theorem](dominated-convergence-theorem.md) permits passing the limit through the [Lebesgue integral](lebesgue-integral.md).
