# Identity (mathematics)

↑ **Parent:** [Equation](equation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_(mathematics))

A [mathematical identity](identity-mathematics.md) is an [equation](equation.md) that holds for every assignment in a specified [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md). For example, the [distributive property](distributive-property.md) gives $(x+1)^2=x^2+2x+1$ for every [real number](real-number.md) $x$. An identity can replace one [mathematical expression](expression-mathematics.md) by another without imposing a new restriction on that domain.

The domain remains essential. The equality $(x^2-1)/(x-1)=x+1$ holds for every real $x\ne1$, but its left side is undefined at $x=1$. Cancelling a factor does not retrospectively define the original [mathematical expression](expression-mathematics.md) at the excluded input. An identity is different from an [identity element](identity-element.md) of an [algebraic operation](algebraic-operation-split.md) or an [identity function](identity-function.md), which are separate concepts despite sharing a name.
