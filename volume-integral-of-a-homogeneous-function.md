# Volume integral of a homogeneous function

↑ **Parent:** [Euler theorem for homogeneous functions](euler-theorem-for-homogeneous-functions.md)

For a sufficiently regular [homogeneous function](homogeneous-function.md) $f$ of degree $n$ on a three-dimensional region, [Euler theorem for homogeneous functions](euler-theorem-for-homogeneous-functions.md) gives $x\cdot\nabla f=nf$. Hence $\nabla\cdot(fx)=(n+3)f$, and the [divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md) gives the displayed reduction when $n\ne-3$. At degree $-3$ only the zero-flux identity follows, so division by $n+3$ is unavailable. Singular functions additionally require an excision or limiting argument; the surface contribution at a removed singularity must not be silently dropped. On the sloping side of a cone with its apex at zero, $x$ is tangent to the side and $x\cdot n=0$, so only the cap contributes to this formula.
