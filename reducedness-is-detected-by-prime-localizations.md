# Reducedness is detected by prime localizations

↑ **Parent:** [Reduced ring](reduced-ring.md)

If a nonzero [nilpotent element](nilpotent.md) $a$ existed in $A$, its proper [annihilator](annihilator-ring-theory.md) would lie in a [maximal ideal](maximal-ideal.md) $\mathfrak m$. No element outside $\mathfrak m$ annihilates $a$, so $a/1$ is nonzero in the [localization at a prime ideal](localization-at-a-prime-ideal.md) $A_{\mathfrak m}$. Conversely, if $(a/s)^n=0$ in a localization, some $u$ in the [multiplicative subset](multiplicatively-closed-set.md) satisfies $ua^n=0$. Then $(ua)^n=0$, so reducedness of $A$ gives $ua=0$ and hence $a/s=0$.
