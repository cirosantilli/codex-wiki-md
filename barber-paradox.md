# Barber paradox

↑ **Parent:** [Russell's paradox](russell-s-paradox.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barber_paradox)

For a [binary relation](binary-relation.md) $S$, no object $b$ can satisfy $S(b,p)\leftrightarrow\neg S(p,p)$ for every $p$. Putting $p=b$ would give $s\leftrightarrow\neg s$ for $s=S(b,b)$. The forward direction derives $\neg s$ by a [negation introduction](negation-introduction-rule.md) subproof assuming $s$; the reverse direction then gives $s$, a [contradiction](contradiction.md). Thus this argument even avoids needing the [law of excluded middle](law-of-excluded-middle.md). The familiar interpretation reads $S(b,p)$ as “$b$ shaves $p$”; the logical obstruction is self-application rather than a physical peculiarity of shaving.
