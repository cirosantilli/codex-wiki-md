# Fractional moment criterion for convergence in probability

↑ **Parent:** [Convergence in probability](convergence-in-probability.md)

For $\alpha>0$, [Markov's inequality](markov-s-inequality.md) gives the displayed bound, so $\mathbb E|Y_n|^\alpha\to0$ implies [convergence in probability](convergence-in-probability.md) to zero even when the first [absolute moment](absolute-moment.md) does not tend to zero. For products $Y_n=\prod_{j=1}^nU_j$ of [independent and identically distributed](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) factors, it suffices that $\mathbb E|U_1|^\alpha<1$. With the [uniform distribution](continuous-uniform-distribution.md) on $(0,b)$, the [product of independent uniform random variables](product-of-independent-uniform-random-variables.md) formula makes this $\alpha\log b<\log(1+\alpha)$. Since $\log(1+\alpha)/\alpha\to1$ as $\alpha\downarrow0$, such an $\alpha$ exists whenever $b<e$.
