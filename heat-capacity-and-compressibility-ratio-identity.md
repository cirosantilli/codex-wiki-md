# Heat-capacity and compressibility ratio identity

↑ **Parent:** [Compressibility](compressibility.md)

In a smooth fixed-composition thermodynamic region, write $a=(\partial S/\partial T)_V$, $b=(\partial S/\partial V)_T$, $c=(\partial p/\partial T)_V$, and $d=(\partial p/\partial V)_T$, with $a,d\ne0$. The [chain rule](chain-rule.md) gives $(\partial S/\partial T)_p=a-bc/d$ and $(\partial p/\partial V)_S=d-bc/a$. Multiplying these expressions by $d$ and $a$, respectively, gives the same [Jacobian determinant](jacobian-determinant.md) $ad-bc$.

Using [heat capacity at constant pressure](heat-capacity-at-constant-pressure.md) $C_p=T(\partial S/\partial T)_p$, [heat capacity at constant volume](heat-capacity-at-constant-volume.md) $C_V=Ta$, [isothermal compressibility](isothermal-compressibility.md) $\kappa_T=-1/(Vd)$ and [isentropic compressibility](isentropic-compressibility.md) $\kappa_S=-1/[V(\partial p/\partial V)_S]$ proves the displayed ratio. The derivation does not require division by thermal expansion, so it still applies when that derivative vanishes. For a stable single phase, the [Maxwell relation](maxwell-relations.md) $b=c$ also explains why $C_p\geq C_V$ and $\kappa_T\geq\kappa_S$.
