# Orthogonal projection of a circle between planes

↑ **Parent:** [Orthogonal projection matrix](orthogonal-projection-matrix.md)

Under [orthogonal projection](orthogonal-projection.md) from one plane onto another, a [circle](circle.md) of [radius](radius.md) $R$ becomes an [ellipse](ellipse.md) with semiaxes $R$ and $R|\cos\alpha|$, where $\alpha$ is the [angle](angle.md) between the planes' unit [normal vectors](normal-vector.md). To prove this, choose one in-plane [unit vector](unit-vector.md) parallel to the planes' intersection and the other perpendicular to it. The first vector is unchanged by the projection; the second has projected length $|\cos\alpha|$ and remains perpendicular to the first. Parametrizing the circle in that [orthonormal basis](orthonormal-basis.md) therefore gives projected coordinates $(R\cos t,R\cos\alpha\sin t)$. Parallel planes have a circular image, and perpendicular planes give the degenerate case of a line segment. An arbitrary center simply projects to the center of the image.
