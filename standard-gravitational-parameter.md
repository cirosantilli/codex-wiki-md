# Standard gravitational parameter

↑ **Parent:** [Newtonian gravitational field](newtonian-gravitational-field.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gravitational_parameter)

The product $\mu=GM$ of the [gravitational constant](gravitational-constant.md) and a central [mass](mass.md) sets the strength of the exterior [Newtonian gravitational field](newtonian-gravitational-field.md). Its dimensions are length cubed divided by time squared. In the exact relative [two-body problem](two-body-problem.md), the parameter is $G(M_1+M_2)$; using $GM$ for one source assumes the [test particle](test-particle.md) approximation.

The combination is useful because orbital and falling-body observations constrain $\mu$ directly. A gravitational [circular orbit](circular-orbit.md) of radius $a$ has [oscillation period](period-of-an-oscillation.md) $T=2\pi\sqrt{a^3/\mu}$, so measuring $a$ and $T$ gives $\mu=4\pi^2a^3/T^2$. That measurement alone does not separately determine $G$ and $M$. Similarly, [radial free fall from rest](radial-free-fall-from-rest.md) has a natural time scale $\sqrt{r_0^3/\mu}$.

Other interactions require a different model. Inverse-square [radiation pressure](radiation-pressure.md) can reduce the effective attraction for a dust [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md); calling that effective coefficient a gravitational parameter must not hide the added [force](force-split.md).
