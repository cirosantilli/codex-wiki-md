<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Again put $A=\lambda I-T$. Surjectivity gives, by [image-kernel orthogonality for an adjoint](../../../../../image-kernel-orthogonality-for-an-adjoint.md),

$$
\ker A^*=(\operatorname{Im}A)^\perp=X^\perp=\{0\}.
$$

If $\lambda$ is real, $A=A^*$, so $\ker A=\{0\}$. If $\lambda$ is nonreal and $Ax=0$, then $Tx=\lambda x$; reality of $\langle Tx,x\rangle=\lambda\|x\|^2$ again implies $x=0$. Thus $A$ is injective as well as surjective, without needing to assume separately that $\lambda$ is not an [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md).

Since $A$ is a [bounded linear operator](../../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md) on the [Banach space](../../../../../banach-space-split.md) $X$, the [bounded inverse theorem](../../../../../bounded-inverse-theorem.md) makes $A^{-1}$ bounded. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\lambda\in\rho(T),\qquad(\lambda I-T)^{-1}\in\mathcal B(X).}
$$

This is exactly membership in the [resolvent set of an operator](../../../../../resolvent-set-of-an-operator.md).
