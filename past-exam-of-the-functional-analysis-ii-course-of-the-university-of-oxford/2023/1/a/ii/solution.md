<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Define the [continuous linear functionals](../../../../../continuous-linear-functional.md) $f_n(z)=\langle x_n,z\rangle$. For each fixed $z\in X$, [weak convergence](../../../../../weak-convergence.md) makes $f_n(z)$ a convergent, hence bounded, scalar sequence. The [Uniform boundedness principle](../../../../../uniform-boundedness-principle.md) states that a pointwise bounded family of [bounded linear operators](../../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md) from a [Banach space](../../../../../banach-space-split.md) to a [normed space](../../../../../normed-vector-space-split.md) has uniformly bounded [operator norms](../../../../../operator-norm.md). Since a [Hilbert space](../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md) is a [Banach space](../../../../../banach-space-split.md), it applies to $(f_n)$.

The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) gives $\|f_n\|\leq\|x_n\|$, and evaluation at $z=x_n/\|x_n\|$ gives equality when $x_n\ne0$; equality also holds at zero. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\sup_n\|x_n\|=\sup_n\|f_n\|<\infty.}
$$
