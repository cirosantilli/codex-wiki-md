The [open mapping theorem](../../../../../open-mapping-theorem-functional-analysis.md) states that a surjective [bounded linear operator](../../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md) $T:X\to Y$ between [Banach spaces](../../../../../banach-space-split.md) is an [open map](../../../../../open-map.md): $T(U)$ is an [open set](../../../../../open-set.md) in $Y$ whenever $U$ is an [open set](../../../../../open-set.md) in $X$. Equivalently, there is $\delta>0$ such that

$$
\boxed{B_Y(0,\delta)\subseteq T(B_X(0,1)),}
$$

where both displayed [balls](../../../../../ball-mathematics.md) are open. In particular, a bijective [bounded linear operator](../../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md) between [Banach spaces](../../../../../banach-space-split.md) has a bounded inverse, the [bounded inverse theorem](../../../../../bounded-inverse-theorem.md).
