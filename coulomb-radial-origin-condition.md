# Coulomb radial origin condition

↑ **Parent:** [Radial Schrodinger equation for the hydrogen atom](radial-schrodinger-equation-for-the-hydrogen-atom.md)

For the usual three-dimensional [Coulomb potential](coulomb-potential-energy.md) [Hamiltonian operator](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md), a separated [radial wavefunction](radial-wavefunction.md) $R(r)$ uses the reduced function $u=rR$, whose radial [norm](norm.md) has measure $dr$. The regular physical [operator domain](operator-domain.md) imposes $u(0)=0$. Near the origin the leading radial equation gives the [indicial equation](indicial-equation.md) $c(c-1)-\ell(\ell+1)=0$ for $u\sim r^c$, with roots $c=\ell+1$ and $c=-\ell$.

The regular root gives $R\sim r^\ell$. The other root gives $R\sim r^{-\ell-1}$, and its radial [probability](probability.md) integral $\int_0^\varepsilon r^2|R|^2dr\sim\int_0^\varepsilon r^{-2\ell}dr$ diverges for $\ell\geq1$. At $\ell=0$, $R\sim1/r$ is locally [square-integrable](square-integrable-function.md) in three dimensions, but violates $u(0)=0$ and has divergent [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) integral $\int_0^\varepsilon r^2|R'|^2dr\sim\int_0^\varepsilon r^{-2}dr$. Thus local [wavefunction normalization](wavefunction-normalization.md) alone is insufficient to select the physical branch in this exceptional case; the standard Coulomb energy domain supplies the missing condition.
