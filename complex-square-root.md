# Complex square root

↑ **Parent:** [Root of a complex number](root-of-a-complex-number.md)

For a nonzero [complex number](complex-number.md) $z=re^{i\theta}$ with $r>0$, its two square roots are $\pm\sqrt r\,e^{i\theta/2}$. They are distinct and differ by a sign. At $z=0$ the only square root is zero. A principal value convention chooses $\theta=\operatorname{Arg}z\in(-\pi,\pi]$ and the plus sign; continuity across the negative real axis then fails. A [holomorphic square root](holomorphic-square-root.md) is a different, domain-dependent notion: it requires a holomorphic function whose square is $z$ on the specified domain. It cannot exist on every punctured neighborhood surrounding zero.
