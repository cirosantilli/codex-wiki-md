# Regression toward the mean

↑ **Parent:** [Probability and statistics](probability-and-statistics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regression_toward_the_mean)

[Regression toward the mean](regression-toward-the-mean.md) is a tendency for measurements selected because they are extreme to be less extreme on repetition. A large first reading can combine a persistent signal with unusually positive [measurement error](measurement-error.md); a fresh error need not repeat that contribution. This can change the selected group's [mean](expected-value-split.md) even when the underlying signal stays fixed and no [intervention](treatment.md) occurs.

The claim concerns a [conditional expectation](conditional-expectation.md), not a rule that every individual moves toward the population [mean](expected-value-split.md). Some second readings are more extreme. Nor does an observed change establish a [causal effect](causal-effect.md): selection alone can produce it. The following original [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md) model makes both distinctions quantitative.
