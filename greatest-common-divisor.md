# Greatest common divisor

↑ **Parent:** [Divisibility](divisibility.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_common_divisor)

For [integers](integer.md) $a,b$ not both zero, their greatest common divisor is the largest positive [integer](integer.md) dividing both. It is unchanged by replacing either input by its negative. The convention $\gcd(0,0)=0$ is useful, but this exceptional value is not a largest positive common [divisor](divisor.md). For positive inputs, the [Euclidean algorithm](euclidean-algorithm.md) computes the answer, and [Bézout's identity](bezout-s-identity.md) expresses it as an integer [linear combination](linear-combination.md) of the inputs.

For $12=2^2\cdot3$ and $18=2\cdot3^2$, a common [divisor](divisor.md) can contain at most one copy of the [prime number](prime-number.md) two and one of the [prime number](prime-number.md) three. Taking the smaller [prime valuation](prime-valuation.md) at each [prime number](prime-number.md) therefore gives

$$
\gcd(12,18)=2^{\min(2,1)}3^{\min(1,2)}=2\cdot3=6.
$$

The check $6=18-12$ is also a [Bézout's identity](bezout-s-identity.md): every common [divisor](divisor.md) divides this difference, and six itself divides both inputs. More generally, every common [divisor](divisor.md) divides the [greatest common divisor](greatest-common-divisor.md), a stronger statement than merely being no larger than it. The companion [least common multiple](least-common-multiple.md) takes the larger [prime valuations](prime-valuation.md), as the diagram there illustrates. For an application, divide the [numerator](numerator.md) and [denominator](denominator.md) of the [fraction](fraction.md) $12/18$ by six to obtain $2/3$; these remaining [integers](integer.md) are [coprime](coprime-integers.md).
