# Local finiteness does not imply finite intensity

↑ **Parent:** [Intensity measure of a point process](intensity-measure-of-a-point-process.md)

Here is an explicit example even within [simple point processes](simple-point-process.md). Let $K$ be a positive integer with [probability mass function](probability-mass-function.md) $\mathbb P(K=k)=1/[k(k+1)]$. The telescoping series sums to one, $K$ is finite [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md), but $\mathbb E[K]=\sum_{k\ge1}1/(k+1)=\infty$. Conditional on $K$, sample $K$ [independent](independent-random-variables.md) points with the [uniform distribution](continuous-uniform-distribution.md) on $(0,1)$. The resulting [point process](point-process.md) has finitely many distinct points [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md), while $\mu((0,1))=\infty$. Thus finite counts in each realization and finite mean counts are separate requirements; local finiteness of the [intensity measure](intensity-measure-of-a-point-process.md) is an integrability assumption.
