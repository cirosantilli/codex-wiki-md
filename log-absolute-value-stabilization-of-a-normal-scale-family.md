# Log absolute value stabilization of a normal scale family

↑ **Parent:** [Variance-stabilizing transformation](variance-stabilizing-transformation.md)

For $Y\sim N(\mu,k\mu^2)$ with $\mu\ne0$ and fixed $k>0$, represent $Y=\mu(1+\sqrt{k}Z)$ with $Z$ having the [standard normal distribution](standard-normal-distribution.md). Taking the [natural logarithm](natural-logarithm.md) of the absolute value gives the displayed identity. Its random second term has a common distribution, independent of $\mu$, so this [variance-stabilizing transformation](variance-stabilizing-transformation.md) gives an exactly constant [variance](variance-split.md). That variance is finite: the normal density is bounded near zero and $\int_0^1|\log t|^2dt<\infty$, while normal tails control logarithmic growth at infinity. The transformation is defined almost surely, loses the sign of the mean, and does not produce an exact [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md). A zero mean makes the original observation identically zero and is outside its domain.
