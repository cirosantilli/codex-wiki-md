# Inner product determined by a basis

↑ **Parent:** [Hermitian inner product](hermitian-inner-product.md)

Each [basis](basis.md) $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of a finite-dimensional complex [vector space](vector-space-split.md) determines a unique [complex inner product](hermitian-inner-product.md) making that [basis](basis.md) [orthonormal](orthonormal-set.md). In coordinates it is $\langle\sum_i u_ie_i,\sum_i v_ie_i\rangle_e=\sum_i u_i\overline{v_i}$, with the first argument linear. The formula is [sesquilinear](sesquilinear-form.md), has [conjugate symmetry](conjugate-symmetry.md), and satisfies $\langle v,v\rangle_e=\sum_i|v_i|^2$, which is positive unless $v=0$. Expanding any [inner product](inner-product-split.md) in the given [basis](basis.md) proves uniqueness. Its codomain is the [complex numbers](complex-number.md); only its diagonal values are necessarily real. The resulting identification with the [dual space](dual-space-split.md) is conjugate-linear, not complex-linear.
