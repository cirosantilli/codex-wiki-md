# Microquasars in Our Own Galaxy by National Radio Astronomy Observatory

↑ **Parent:** [Physics bibliography](physics-bibliography-split.md)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Work by National Radio Astronomy Observatory](work-by-national-radio-astronomy-observatory.md)

[https://www.nrao.edu/pr/2000/vla20/background/superlum/](https://www.nrao.edu/pr/2000/vla20/background/superlum/)

The observatory describes a time sequence of radio images of Galactic microquasar ejecta and explains the apparent faster-than-light separation through [apparent superluminal motion](apparent-superluminal-motion.md) of subluminal approaching material. This is an observational example of [special relativity](special-relativity-split.md) applied to emission times and light-travel delays.
