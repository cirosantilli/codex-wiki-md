<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Write $r_s=2GM/c^2$, $f=1-r_s/r$ and $r_*=r+r_s\ln|r/r_s-1|$, so $dr_*/dr=f^{-1}$. The [ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates](../../../../../ingoing-eddington-finkelstein-coordinates.md) use $v=t+r_*/c$ to label ingoing radial light fronts. Substituting $dt=dv-dr/(cf)$ into the [Schwarzschild metric](../../../../../schwarzschild-spacetime-split.md) gives

$$
\boxed{ds^2=fc^2dv^2-2c\,dv\,dr-r^2d\Omega^2}.
$$

The radial block has [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) $-c^2$ even at $r=r_s$; its coefficients have no horizon divergence. The apparent singularity of Schwarzschild time at the future horizon is therefore a coordinate failure. The physical [curvature singularity](../../../../../curvature-singularity.md) at $r=0$ remains.

The [radial null trajectories in ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates](../../../../../radial-null-trajectories-in-ingoing-eddington-finkelstein-coordinates.md) have

$$
\boxed{v=\text{constant}\quad\text{(ingoing)},\qquad
\frac{dr}{dv}=\frac c2\left(1-\frac{r_s}r\right)\quad\text{(outgoing)}}.
$$

The ingoing family crosses the future horizon smoothly. The outgoing family has zero outward radial coordinate speed at the horizon and decreasing radius inside it; both future null directions then lead toward smaller radius. These advanced coordinates cover the exterior and future black-hole horizon, rather than simultaneously regularizing the past white-hole horizon. Their null time coordinate is not a clock reading of a stationary observer.
