<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Consider an ideal spherical collapse with a [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md) exterior of constant [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $M$. Once [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) support is insufficient, a surface can continue inward and cross the [Schwarzschild event horizon](../../../../../schwarzschild-event-horizon.md) $r_s=2GM/c^2$ in finite comoving [proper time](../../../../../proper-time.md). An [event horizon](../../../../../event-horizon.md) is a causal boundary: [light](../../../../../light.md) emitted from inside cannot reach distant observers. It is not a material shell or, in the classical Schwarzschild solution, a local [curvature singularity](../../../../../curvature-singularity.md) at $r=r_s$.

The [distant view of gravitational collapse](../../../../../distant-view-of-gravitational-collapse.md) differs from the surface clock's history. Exterior [Schwarzschild time](../../../../../schwarzschild-time.md) has $dt/d\tau\propto(1-r_s/r)^{-1}$ for regular infall and diverges as the surface approaches the horizon. Outgoing radial [light](../../../../../light.md) also takes an increasingly long coordinate time to escape: $dt/dr=[c(1-r_s/r)]^{-1}$ has a logarithmically divergent integral at the horizon. Signals received far away are progressively delayed and redshifted; both gravity and the inward emitter's [Doppler shift](../../../../../doppler-effect.md) contribute. [photons](../../../../../photon.md) carry less received [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and arrive at a lower rate, while the available escape directions also narrow.

Thus **a distant observer does not receive a [light](../../../../../light.md) signal announcing the crossing; the image fades and reddens rather than remaining a permanently bright frozen surface**. A finite-band detector with finite sensitivity loses it in finite observation time. The divergence of one coordinate chart does not prevent the material crossing in finite [proper time](../../../../../proper-time.md); horizon-regular coordinates describe that continuation. The pressureless [Oppenheimer-Snyder model](../../../../../oppenheimer-snyder-model.md) makes this distinction explicit, without representing all the [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md), rotation and radiation of a real stellar collapse.[abstract and pp. 455–459, finite comoving collapse time and redshifted distant description](../../../../../on-continued-gravitational-contraction-by-j-robert-oppenheimer-and-hartland-snyder.md)
