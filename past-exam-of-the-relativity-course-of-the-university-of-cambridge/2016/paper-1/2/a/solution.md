<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [weak equivalence principle](../../../../../weak-equivalence-principle.md) states that freely falling, sufficiently small test bodies have the same motion in a given gravitational field irrespective of composition or [mass](../../../../../mass.md), neglecting their own gravity and nongravitational [forces](../../../../../force-split.md). In the Newtonian limit, $m_i\mathbf a=m_g\mathbf g$ makes this universality equivalent to a universal gravitational-to-inertial [mass](../../../../../mass.md) ratio, conventionally set to one.

The [Einstein equivalence principle](../../../../../einstein-equivalence-principle.md) adds that local nongravitational experiments in a freely falling, nonrotating laboratory have the laws of [special relativity](../../../../../special-relativity-split.md), independent of the laboratory's position and [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md). At an event one can choose a [local inertial frame](../../../../../local-inertial-frame.md) with $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\partial_\alpha g_{\mu\nu}=0$. A freely falling observer feels no supporting [force](../../../../../force-split.md), while a supported observer can model a locally uniform gravitational field by an accelerated laboratory. This explains why freely moving particles follow [timelike geodesics](../../../../../timelike-geodesic.md) and [light](../../../../../light.md) follows [null geodesics](../../../../../null-geodesic.md) when other interactions are negligible.

The locality qualification is essential. The [Riemann curvature tensor](../../../../../riemann-curvature-tensor-split.md) generally remains nonzero; separated freely falling bodies have tidal relative acceleration, measured by [geodesic deviation](../../../../../geodesic-deviation.md). A finite-size experiment can therefore distinguish a curved gravitational field from a merely accelerated coordinate description of flat [spacetime](../../../../../spacetime.md). Equivalence alone also does not select the complete [Einstein field equations](../../../../../einstein-field-equations-split.md).

A useful weak-field consequence is the upward [photon](../../../../../photon.md) shift $\Delta\nu/\nu\simeq-gH/c^2$ over laboratory height $H$. In an upward-accelerating laboratory the receiver gains recession [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) during the [light](../../../../../light.md) flight, giving the same leading redshift. The [Pound-Rebka experiment](../../../../../pound-rebka-experiment.md) tested this with resonant absorption of 14.4-keV [gamma rays](../../../../../gamma-ray.md) over about 22.5 m. A controlled source [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) calibrated the Doppler sensitivity; interchanging source and absorber reversed the gravitational contribution while retaining an intrinsic source–absorber offset. Monitoring their [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) difference was essential because lattice-motion shifts could mimic the small signal.[pp. 338–340, baseline, velocity calibration, reversed source/absorber positions and temperature corrections](../../../../../apparent-weight-of-photons-by-robert-vivian-pound-and-glen-rebka.md) This verifies a gravitational clock-shift prediction; by itself it is not a unique test selecting [general relativity](../../../../../general-relativity-split.md) over every alternative metric theory.
