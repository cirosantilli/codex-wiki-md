<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The three classic tests probe orbital motion, the propagation of [light](../../../../light.md), and clock rates in a [gravitational field](../../../../gravitational-field.md).

**[Schwarzschild perihelion precession](../../../../schwarzschild-perihelion-precession.md).** For an orbit with [semi-major axis](../../../../semi-major-axis.md) $a$ and [orbital eccentricity](../../../../orbital-eccentricity.md) $e$, the leading advance per revolution is

$$
\boxed{\Delta\varpi=\frac{6\pi GM}{a(1-e^2)c^2}.}
$$

The relevant observation for [Mercury](../../../../mercury-planet.md) is the residual advance after the much larger ordinary perturbations have been included, approximately $43$ arcseconds per century.[Physics through the 1990s: Gravitation, cosmology and cosmic-ray physics, 1986: discussion of the anomalous perihelion advance](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19870009701/downloads/19870009701.pdf) An orbital fit must account for the other planets and reference-frame effects before interpreting that residual as a relativistic contribution.

**[Schwarzschild light deflection](../../../../schwarzschild-light-deflection.md).** A ray with large [impact parameter](../../../../impact-parameter.md) $b$ passing a nearly spherical mass is deflected through

$$
\boxed{\alpha\simeq\frac{4GM}{bc^2}.}
$$

At the [Sun](../../../../sun.md)'s limb this is about $1.75$ arcseconds. The [Eddington experiment](../../../../eddington-experiment.md) used the 1919 [solar eclipse](../../../../solar-eclipse.md) to photograph background [stars](../../../../star.md) near the obscured [Sun](../../../../sun.md), then compared their positions with reference photographs. The measured effect is an apparent outward displacement of the background [stars](../../../../star.md), not a direct photograph of a curved light path. Telescope focus, plate scale and atmospheric effects must be controlled when extracting such small shifts.[1920 paper, observational comparison and deflection analysis](../../../../a-determination-of-the-deflection-of-light-by-the-sun-s-gravitational-field-by-frank-watson-dyson-and-others.md)

**[Gravitational redshift](../../../../gravitational-redshift.md).** For a photon emitted lower in a weak static field and received a height $H$ above it,

$$
\boxed{\frac{\nu_{\rm rec}-\nu_{\rm em}}{\nu_{\rm em}}\simeq-\frac{gH}{c^2}.}
$$

The [Pound-Rebka experiment](../../../../pound-rebka-experiment.md) used the narrow recoil-free nuclear resonance supplied by the [Mössbauer effect](../../../../mossbauer-effect-split.md). A controlled source motion supplies a small [Doppler shift](../../../../doppler-effect.md) that compensates the gravitational shift, allowing a change in resonant absorption to reveal it across a laboratory tower.[1960 experiment: gamma-ray source and resonant absorber at different heights](../../../../apparent-weight-of-photons-by-robert-vivian-pound-and-glen-rebka.md) The leading weak-field redshift follows from the [equivalence principle](../../../../equivalence-principle.md) and therefore does not, by itself, distinguish every competing metric theory of gravity.
