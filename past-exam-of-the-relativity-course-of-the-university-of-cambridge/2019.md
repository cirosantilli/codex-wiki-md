## Section A

↑ **Parent:** [2019](2019.md)

<h3 id="section-a/1">1</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section A](#section-a)

<h4 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-a/1)

The conserved initial [four-momentum](../special-relativity.md#four-momentum) of a particle with positive [rest mass](../special-relativity.md#invariant-mass) satisfies $P_\mu P^\mu=m^2c^2>0$. A single [photon](../quantum-mechanics.md#photon) instead has null [four-momentum](../special-relativity.md#four-momentum), $K_\mu K^\mu=0$. [Four-momentum conservation](../special-relativity.md#four-momentum-conservation) for an isolated one-body decay would require $P^\mu=K^\mu$, contradicting these [Lorentz invariants](../special-relativity.md#lorentz-invariance). Thus **decay into a single photon is impossible in every inertial frame**.

Equivalently, the incoming [energy](../energy.md) and [momentum](../momentum.md) obey $E^2-c^2|\mathbf p|^2=m^2c^4$, whereas a single outgoing [photon](../quantum-mechanics.md#photon) obeys $E=c|\mathbf p|$. Changing the laboratory [speed](../classical-mechanics.md#speed) cannot change this mismatch of the invariant [mass shell](../special-relativity.md#mass-shell).

<h3 id="section-a/2">2</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section A](#section-a)

<h4 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-a/2)

For motion along one spatial axis, introduce the [rapidity](../special-relativity.md#rapidity) $\eta$ so that the [four-velocity](../special-relativity.md#four-velocity) is $u^\mu=c(\cosh\eta,\sinh\eta,0,0)$. Its [four-acceleration](../special-relativity.md#four-acceleration) is

$$
a^\mu=c\frac{d\eta}{d\tau}(\sinh\eta,\cosh\eta,0,0),\qquad a_\mu a^\mu=-c^2\left(\frac{d\eta}{d\tau}\right)^2.
$$

A constant signed [proper acceleration](../special-relativity.md#proper-acceleration) $g$ therefore gives $d\eta/d\tau=g/c$, hence $\eta=\eta_0+g\tau/c$. Differentiating the [four-velocity](../special-relativity.md#four-velocity) twice yields

$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2u^\mu}{d\tau^2}=\frac{g^2}{c^2}u^\mu.}
$$

The zero transverse components also satisfy this equation. The constant acceleration measured on board is the [proper acceleration](../special-relativity.md#proper-acceleration), rather than a constant coordinate acceleration in the laboratory.

<h3 id="section-a/3">3</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section A](#section-a)

<h4 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#section-a/3)

Use the [geodesic Lagrangian](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-lagrangian) $L=\tfrac12(y^2\dot x^2+x^2\dot y^2)$, where dots denote an [affine parameter](../riemannian-geometry.md#affine-parameter) derivative. The [Euler-Lagrange equations](../analysis.md#euler-lagrange-equation) give

$$
\boxed{y^2\ddot x+2y\dot y\dot x-x\dot y^2=0,\qquad
x^2\ddot y+2x\dot x\dot y-y\dot x^2=0.}
$$

These are the [geodesic equations](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-equation) wherever $xy\ne0$, so the [Riemannian metric](../riemannian-metric.md) is nondegenerate. Substitution of $y=mx$, $\dot y=m\dot x$ and $\ddot y=m\ddot x$ makes both equations proportional to

$$
x\ddot x+\dot x^2=\frac{d}{d\lambda}(x\dot x)=0.
$$

Thus $\boxed{x^2=A\lambda+B,\quad y=mx}$ is a family of affinely parametrized [geodesics](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic), on intervals staying within one quadrant. In particular, $m\ne0$ and $x\ne0$ keep the metric nonsingular; the coordinate axes themselves are not regular points of this metric. The coordinate $x$ need not be linear in the [affine parameter](../riemannian-geometry.md#affine-parameter), despite the straight coordinate-space shape of these curves.

## Section B

↑ **Parent:** [2019](2019.md)

<h3 id="section-b/b4">B4</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section B](#section-b)

<h4 id="section-b/b4/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B4](#section-b/b4)

Use signature $(+---)$ and local inertial coordinates $x^0=ct$. Write $u^\mu=\gamma(c,\mathbf v)$ and $w=\rho c^2+p$, where $\rho$ is rest [mass density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density). The [perfect fluid in general relativity](../stress-energy-tensor.md#perfect-fluid-in-general-relativity) has components

$$
T^{00}=w\gamma^2-p,\qquad T^{0i}=T^{i0}=\frac{w\gamma^2v_i}{c},\qquad
T^{ij}=\frac{w\gamma^2v_iv_j}{c^2}+p\delta_{ij}.
$$

Here $T^{00}$ is [energy density](../statistical-physics.md#energy-density), $cT^{i0}$ is [energy flux](../mathematical-physics.md#energy-flux), $T^{0i}/c$ is [momentum density](../stress-energy-tensor.md#momentum-density), and $T^{ij}$ is the flux of the $j$th [momentum](../momentum.md) component through a surface normal to $i$. In the local rest frame these reduce to [energy density](../statistical-physics.md#energy-density) $\rho c^2$, zero energy and momentum flow, and isotropic [pressure](../pressure.md) $p$. The equation $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$ expresses local [stress-energy conservation](../stress-energy-tensor.md#stress-energy-conservation). Its temporal and spatial components give energy and momentum balance; in flat coordinates, for example, $\partial_tT^{00}+\partial_i(cT^{i0})=0$.

For [relativistic dust](../stress-energy-tensor.md#relativistic-dust), $T^{\mu\nu}=\rho u^\mu u^\nu$. Contracting its divergence with $u_\nu$, and using [metric compatibility](../vector-bundle.md#metric-compatibility) together with $u_\nu u^\nu=c^2$, gives

$$
0=u_\nu\nabla_\mu(\rho u^\mu u^\nu)
=c^2\nabla_\mu(\rho u^\mu)+\frac\rho2u^\mu\nabla_\mu(u_\nu u^\nu)
=c^2\nabla_\mu(\rho u^\mu).
$$

Consequently $\nabla_\mu(\rho u^\mu)=0$. Substituting this continuity equation back into the divergence leaves $\rho u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu=0$. Wherever matter is present,

$$
\boxed{u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu=0,}
$$

so the individual particles follow [geodesics](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic).

The electromagnetic [four-force](../special-relativity.md#four-force) law $f^\mu=qF^{\mu\nu}u_\nu$ must preserve the particle's [rest mass](../special-relativity.md#invariant-mass). Thus $u_\mu f^\mu=0$, and requiring this for arbitrary timelike [four-velocities](../special-relativity.md#four-velocity) makes the [electromagnetic field tensor](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#electromagnetic-field-tensor) antisymmetric. Its six independent components comprise the [electric field](../electric-field.md) and [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md). With the stated signature and $x^0=ct$, one consistent convention is

$$
F^{i0}=E_i/c,\qquad F^{0i}=-E_i/c,\qquad F^{ij}=-\epsilon_{ijk}B_k.
$$

It gives the spatial force $d\mathbf p/dt=q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)$ and energy transfer $dE/dt=q\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v$. Under a [Lorentz transformation](../special-relativity.md#lorentz-transformation), $F^{\mu\nu}$ transforms as a rank-two [contravariant tensor](../multilinear-algebra.md#contravariant-tensor); its electric and magnetic components consequently mix. A four-potential supplies $F_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_\mu A_\nu-\nabla_\nu A_\mu$, unchanged by $A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu+\nabla_\mu\chi$. The [Maxwell equations](../maxwell-equations.md) are

$$
\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0j^\nu,\qquad
\nabla_\alpha F_{\beta\gamma}+\nabla_\beta F_{\gamma\alpha}+\nabla_\gamma F_{\alpha\beta}=0.
$$

These combine the source equations and the homogeneous equations into covariant tensor statements; $j^\mu$ is the [four-current](../electric-charge.md#four-current).

For [charged relativistic dust](../stress-energy-tensor.md#charged-relativistic-dust), the matter tensor exchanges [four-momentum](../special-relativity.md#four-momentum) with the electromagnetic field: $\nabla_\mu T_{\rm m}^{\mu\nu}=F^{\nu\alpha}j_\alpha$, while their total [stress-energy tensor](../stress-energy-tensor.md) is conserved. Identical particles of [rest mass](../special-relativity.md#invariant-mass) $m$ and [electric charge](../electric-charge.md) $q$ have $j^\alpha=(q/m)\rho u^\alpha$. Contraction with $u_\nu$ again gives mass continuity because $u_\nu F^{\nu\alpha}u_\alpha=0$. Removing that term gives

$$
\boxed{u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu=\frac qmF^{\nu\alpha}u_\alpha.}
$$

Using zero divergence for the matter tensor alone in this last step would omit the electromagnetic [Lorentz force](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lorentz-force).

<h3 id="section-b/b5">B5</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section B](#section-b)

<h4 id="section-b/b5/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B5](#section-b/b5)

Write $A(r)=1-2\mu/r$, with $\mu=GM/c^2$. On the equatorial plane, the [geodesic Lagrangian](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-lagrangian) is

$$
L=\frac12\left(c^2A\dot t^2-A^{-1}\dot r^2-r^2\dot\phi^2\right).
$$

The coordinates $t$ and $\phi$ are cyclic, giving $\boxed{A\dot t=k,\quad r^2\dot\phi=h}$. The conserved physical [energy](../energy.md) per unit [rest mass](../special-relativity.md#invariant-mass) is $c^2k$, while $h$ is the signed [angular momentum](../momentum.md#angular-momentum) per unit [rest mass](../special-relativity.md#invariant-mass). The radial [Euler-Lagrange equation](../analysis.md#euler-lagrange-equation), with $A'=2\mu/r^2$, is

$$
\boxed{A^{-1}\ddot r+\frac{\mu c^2}{r^2}\dot t^2
-\frac{\mu}{r^2A^2}\dot r^2-r\dot\phi^2=0.}
$$

These are the requested [geodesic equations](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-equation). The equatorial condition is consistent with the polar equation by the spherical symmetry of the [Schwarzschild metric](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md).

The normalization of the [four-velocity](../special-relativity.md#four-velocity) is $c^2=c^2A\dot t^2-A^{-1}\dot r^2-r^2\dot\phi^2$. Insert the two conserved quantities and multiply by $A$ to obtain

$$
\boxed{\dot r^2+\frac{h^2}{r^2}\left(1-\frac{2\mu}r\right)-\frac{2\mu c^2}r
=c^2(k^2-1).}
$$

This is a one-dimensional radial energy equation. Half of its left side is radial kinetic energy per unit mass plus an [effective potential](../mathematical-physics.md#effective-potential): the Newtonian centrifugal and gravitational terms acquire the extra attractive contribution $-\mu h^2/r^3$.

A [timelike circular Schwarzschild geodesic](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#timelike-circular-schwarzschild-geodesic) requires $\dot r=\ddot r=0$. Equivalently, set the derivative of $V(r)=h^2/r^2-2\mu h^2/r^3-2\mu c^2/r$ to zero. At $r=R$ this gives $h^2(R-3\mu)=\mu c^2R^2$, and substitution in the radial energy equation yields

$$
\boxed{h^2=\frac{\mu c^2R^2}{R-3\mu},\qquad
k^2=\frac{(R-2\mu)^2}{R(R-3\mu)}.}
$$

Such timelike circular orbits require $R>3\mu$. Their coordinate [angular velocity](../rigid-body-dynamics.md#angular-velocity) satisfies $\Omega^2=\mu c^2/R^3$, and their proper clock rate is $d\tau_s/dt=A/k=\sqrt{1-3\mu/R_s}$.

For the static North-Pole clock, $d\tau_E/dt=\sqrt{1-2\mu/R_E}$. Consecutive overhead passages occur one coordinate orbital period apart, and the repeated Earth-satellite geometry gives equal signal travel delays. The [comparison of circular-orbit and static Schwarzschild clocks](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#comparison-of-circular-orbit-and-static-schwarzschild-clocks) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{\tau_s}{\tau_E}=\sqrt{\frac{1-3\mu/R_s}{1-2\mu/R_E}}.}
$$

This includes both gravitational and orbital-motion effects; a separate Newtonian time-dilation correction must not be added again.

For an estimate, [Kepler's third law](../mathematical-physics.md#kepler-s-third-law) gives $R_s=42100\,\mathrm{km}\,(3/24)^{2/3}=10525\,\mathrm{km}$. The same approximate geostationary radius and period give $\mu=4\pi^2R_{\rm geo}^3/(c^2T_{\rm geo}^2)\simeq4.4\,\mathrm{mm}$. Expanding the ratio to first order,

$$
\frac{\tau_s}{\tau_E}\simeq1+\frac\mu{R_E}-\frac{3\mu}{2R_s}
\simeq\boxed{1+6.4\times10^{-11}}.
$$

The satellite clock consequently gains about $0.7\,\mu\mathrm s$ over a three-hour orbit in this approximation. The surface observer is taken to be static, and the spacetime model is the spherical [Schwarzschild metric](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md) specified in the problem.

<h3 id="section-b/b6">B6</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section B](#section-b)

<h4 id="section-b/b6/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#section-b/b6)

A [fundamental observer](../cosmology.md#comoving-observer) follows the mean cosmic matter flow and is at rest with respect to the homogeneous cosmological frame. [Cosmological isotropy](../cosmology.md#cosmological-isotropy) permits no preferred spatial acceleration, rotation vector or anisotropic expansion for this flow: the congruence is geodesic, irrotational and shear-free, with isotropic expansion. Its orthogonal homogeneous spatial slices define simultaneity. Choosing fixed spatial labels along these worldlines and their common [proper time](../special-relativity.md#proper-time) as $t$ gives [synchronous coordinates](../general-relativity.md#synchronous-coordinates), $g_{tt}=c^2$ and $g_{ti}=0$.

In the [Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric](../cosmology.md#friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric), $t$ is this cosmic [proper time](../special-relativity.md#proper-time) and $\chi$ is radial [comoving coordinate](../cosmology.md#comoving-coordinate). On a constant-$t$ radial slice, the [proper distance in cosmology](../cosmology.md#proper-distance-in-cosmology) from $0$ to a fixed $\chi_0$ is $D(t)=a(t)\chi_0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\dot D=H(t)D,\qquad H(t)=\frac{\dot a(t)}{a(t)}.}
$$

This is the [Hubble parameter](../cosmology.md#hubble-parameter). The metric describes expansion when $\dot a>0$; its form alone also permits static or contracting histories, so expansion additionally requires that sign of the [scale factor](../cosmology.md#scale-factor-cosmology) derivative.

For a radial [photon](../quantum-mechanics.md#photon), the metric components relevant to its motion are $g_{tt}=c^2$ and $g_{\chi\chi}=-a^2(t)$, both independent of $\chi$. The angular momentum components vanish. The covariant-momentum form of the [geodesic equation](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-equation) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{dp_\chi}{d\lambda}=\frac12\partial_\chi g_{\nu\rho}p^\nu p^\rho=0.}
$$

Only this radial contraction is needed: angular metric coefficients can depend on $\chi$, but multiply zero angular components. The conserved quantity is the lower-index $p_1=p_\chi$, rather than the contravariant component $p^1$.

For outward travel put $P=-p_\chi>0$. The null condition $c^2(p^t)^2-a^2(p^\chi)^2=0$ gives

$$
p^\chi=\frac P{a^2},\qquad p^t=\frac P{ac},\qquad\boxed{p_0=p_t=c^2p^t=\frac{cP}{a(t)}.}
$$

Here the time coordinate is $t$, rather than $ct$. A [comoving observer](../cosmology.md#comoving-observer) has $u^t=1$ and measures [photon energy](../quantum-mechanics.md#photon-energy) $E=p_\mu u^\mu=p_t$. Thus [energy](../energy.md) and [frequency](../wave.md#frequency) decrease as $a^{-1}$, giving the [cosmological redshift](../cosmology.md#cosmological-redshift)

$$
\boxed{1+z=\frac{\nu_E}{\nu_R}=\frac{a(t_R)}{a(t_E)}.}
$$

The reciprocal relation for [wavelengths](../wave-equation.md#wavelength) follows from their common local propagation speed $c$.

Finally center the spatial coordinates on the receiving [comoving observer](../cosmology.md#comoving-observer). At the source, the transverse spatial line element is $dl=a(t_E)S_K(\chi)\,d\theta$. For a short transverse rod and a small observed [angle](../geometry-and-topology.md#angle) $\Delta\theta$,

$$
l=a(t_E)S_K(\chi)\Delta\theta
=\boxed{\frac{a(t_R)S_K(\chi)}{1+z}\Delta\theta.}
$$

Thus the [angular diameter distance](../cosmology.md#angular-diameter-distance) is $d_A=a(t_E)S_K(\chi)$. Writing the reception epoch as $t_0=t_R$ gives the displayed formula in the question. The length is the proper transverse length at emission; $t_0$ cannot be replaced by an unrelated epoch while retaining that expression.
