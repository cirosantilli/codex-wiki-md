<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

On a coordinate patch with $f\ne0$, $g_{xx}=1$, $g_{yy}=f^2$ and the only nonzero [metric tensor](../../../../../metric-tensor.md) [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) is $\partial_xg_{yy}=2ff'$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\Gamma^x{}_{yy}=-ff',\qquad
\Gamma^y{}_{xy}=\Gamma^y{}_{yx}=\frac{f'}f}.
$$

All remaining [Christoffel symbols](../../../../../christoffel-symbol.md) vanish. In the supplied [Riemann tensor](../../../../../riemann-curvature-tensor-split.md) convention,

$$
R^x{}_{yxy}=\partial_x(-ff')-\Gamma^y{}_{yx}\Gamma^x{}_{yy}
=-(f'^2+ff'')+f'^2,
\qquad
\boxed{R^x{}_{yxy}=-ff''}.
$$

For example, $f=x$ is flat away from the polar-coordinate origin, while $f=\sin x$ gives positive [Gaussian curvature](../../../../../gaussian-curvature.md) $-f''/f=1$.
