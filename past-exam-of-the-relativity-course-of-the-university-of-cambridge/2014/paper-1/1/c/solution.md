<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use the largest Newtonian tidal-tensor [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md), the radial [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) gradient $2GM/r^3$. Imposing the supplied gradient threshold gives

$$
\boxed{r_{\min}\simeq\left(\frac{2GM}{100\ \mathrm{s^{-2}}}\right)^{1/3}
=1.59\times10^6\ \mathrm m}.
$$

This is **about $1590\ \mathrm{km}$ from the centre**, or $1580\ \mathrm{km}$ above the stated surface. The tangential [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) have half the radial magnitude. Since $GM/(r_{\min}c^2)\simeq1.4\times10^{-3}$, the Newtonian tidal estimate is adequate; this calculation uses the given threshold without inferring any additional material-response model.
