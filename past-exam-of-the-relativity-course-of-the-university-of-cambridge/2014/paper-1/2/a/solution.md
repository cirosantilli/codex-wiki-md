<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[inertial mass](../../../../../inertial-mass.md) sets the response to [force](../../../../../force-split.md), $\mathbf F=m_i\mathbf a$. Passive [gravitational mass](../../../../../gravitational-mass.md) sets the response to a given [gravitational potential](../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md), $\mathbf F_g=-m_g\nabla\Phi$; active [gravitational mass](../../../../../gravitational-mass.md) describes a body's gravitational sourcing. Universality of the passive-to-inertial mass ratio makes freely falling trajectories independent of composition. [torsion balance](../../../../../torsion-balance.md) comparisons and differential free-fall experiments test this [weak equivalence principle](../../../../../weak-equivalence-principle.md) by searching for composition-dependent acceleration, with environmental [forces](../../../../../force-split.md) and instrumental asymmetries controlled.

The [Einstein equivalence principle](../../../../../einstein-equivalence-principle.md) further says that local nongravitational experiments in a freely falling laboratory obey [special relativity](../../../../../special-relativity-split.md), independent of the laboratory's location and [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md). A local inertial coordinate system can remove [Christoffel symbols](../../../../../christoffel-symbol.md) at one event, but cannot remove a nonzero [Riemann tensor](../../../../../riemann-curvature-tensor-split.md) throughout a finite region. Relative acceleration of separated freely falling particles reveals tidal [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md). Thus a uniformly accelerating laboratory in flat [spacetime](../../../../../spacetime.md) can mimic a local gravitational field, while extended tidal measurements distinguish genuine curvature. Equality of the measured mass responses does not make every gravitational field globally equivalent to acceleration.
