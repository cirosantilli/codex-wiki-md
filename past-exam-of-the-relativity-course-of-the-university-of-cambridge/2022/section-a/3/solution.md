<h1 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

For a grazing [null geodesic](../../../../null-geodesic.md), take the [impact parameter](../../../../impact-parameter.md) as $b\simeq R=6.0\times10^6\,\mathrm m$. The leading [Schwarzschild light deflection](../../../../schwarzschild-light-deflection.md) is

$$
\delta=\frac{4GM}{bc^2}.
$$

With the [gravitational constant](../../../../gravitational-constant.md) $G\simeq6.6743\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ and [speed of light](../../../../speed-of-light.md) $c\simeq2.998\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$,

$$
\boxed{\delta\simeq9.9\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{rad}\simeq0.0567^\circ\simeq204\text{ arcsec}\simeq3.4\text{ arcmin}.}
$$

Here $GM/(Rc^2)\simeq2.5\times10^{-4}\ll1$, so the weak-field approximation is appropriate. A ray passing at a larger [impact parameter](../../../../impact-parameter.md) has a correspondingly smaller deflection.
