<h1 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Use [angular size under a collinear relativistic boost](../../../../angular-size-under-a-collinear-relativistic-boost.md). For a small distant source, the two [angular diameters](../../../../angular-diameter.md) have ratio $e^{|\eta|}$, where $\eta$ is the relative [rapidity](../../../../rapidity.md). Thus $e^{|\eta|}=2$, and the relative [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) satisfies

$$
\boxed{\frac{|v|}{c}=\tanh(\log2)=\frac{2^2-1}{2^2+1}=\frac35.}
$$

The speed is therefore $0.6c$. The observers are momentarily coincident, so this is [relativistic aberration](../../../../relativistic-aberration.md), rather than a difference in their distance from the planet. The observer moving faster towards the planet measures the smaller angular diameter under the source-direction aberration convention.
