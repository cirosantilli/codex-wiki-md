<h1 id="section-b/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write $f(r)=1-2\mu/r$. In the equatorial plane, the [geodesic Lagrangian](../../../../../geodesic-lagrangian.md) is $\mathcal L=\frac12(fc^2\dot t^2-f^{-1}\dot r^2-r^2\dot\phi^2)$. The [cyclic coordinates](../../../../../cyclic-coordinate.md) $t$ and $\phi$ give the conserved quantities

$$
\boxed{f\dot t=k,\qquad r^2\dot\phi=h.}
$$

The squared tangent norm is constant along an affinely parametrized [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md), since metric compatibility gives $d[g(\dot X,\dot X)]/d\tau=2g(D\dot X/d\tau,\dot X)=0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{fc^2\dot t^2-f^{-1}\dot r^2-r^2\dot\phi^2=E.}
$$

For a massive particle parametrized by [proper time](../../../../../proper-time.md), $E=c^2$; a [photon](../../../../../photon.md) has a null tangent, so $E=0$. This $E$ denotes the tangent norm, not the photon's physical energy. A future-directed exterior photon has $k>0$, proportional to its positive [Killing energy](../../../../../killing-energy.md). Its affine parameter can be rescaled, so there is no mass-shell normalization fixing $k$ to a particular positive value.
