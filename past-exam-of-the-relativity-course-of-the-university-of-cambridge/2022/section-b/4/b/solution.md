<h1 id="section-b/4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For radial motion, use the [geodesic Lagrangian](../../../../../geodesic-lagrangian.md)

$$
\mathcal L=\frac12(c^2\dot t^2-a(t)^2\dot\chi^2).
$$

The [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) for $t$ gives $c^2\ddot t+a(da/dt)\dot\chi^2=0$. Since $\chi$ is a [cyclic coordinate](../../../../../cyclic-coordinate.md), its conjugate momentum is constant. Hence

$$
\boxed{\frac d{d\tau}(c^2\dot t)+a\frac{da}{dt}\dot\chi^2=0,\qquad a^2\dot\chi=C.}
$$

For a massive particle, take $\tau$ as [proper time](../../../../../proper-time.md); the [four-velocity](../../../../../four-velocity.md) normalization is $c^2\dot t^2-a^2\dot\chi^2=c^2$. For a [photon](../../../../../photon.md), use an [affine parameter](../../../../../affine-parameter.md) rather than proper time, and the corresponding tangent is a [null vector](../../../../../null-vector.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\dot t^2=1+\frac{a^2\dot\chi^2}{c^2}\quad\text{(massive)},\qquad
\dot t^2=\frac{a^2\dot\chi^2}{c^2}\quad\text{(massless)}.}
$$

The normalization of the null affine parameter remains arbitrary.
