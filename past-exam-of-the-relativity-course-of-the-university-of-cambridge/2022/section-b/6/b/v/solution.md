<h1 id="section-b/6/b/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Use the specified hyperbolic trajectory. The emitted right-moving [photon](../../../../../../photon.md) follows $x=L+t-t_0$, so its retarded [null coordinate](../../../../../../null-coordinate.md) is $p=t_0-L$. Interception at astronaut [proper time](../../../../../../proper-time.md) $\tau$ requires

$$
t_0-L=-\frac1a e^{-a\tau}.
$$

The astronaut has velocity $v=\tanh(a\tau)$ relative to the star and $\gamma=\cosh(a\tau)$. The collinear [relativistic Doppler effect](../../../../../../relativistic-doppler-effect.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\nu_{\rm obs}=\nu_0\gamma(1-v)=\nu_0e^{-a\tau}=a\nu_0(L-t_0).}
$$

This is the [Doppler shift for a uniformly accelerated receiver](../../../../../../doppler-shift-for-a-uniformly-accelerated-receiver.md). A finite reception time exists precisely when $t_0<L$, so **the limiting emission time is $L$**, approached from below. At $t_0=L$ the photon lies on the [Rindler horizon](../../../../../../rindler-horizon.md) and never reaches the astronaut. If reception is restricted to $\tau\ge0$, the full emission range is $L-1/a\le t_0<L$; for emission after $t=0$, it is $0\le t_0<L$.
