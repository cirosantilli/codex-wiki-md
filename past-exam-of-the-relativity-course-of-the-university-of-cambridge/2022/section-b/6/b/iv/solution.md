<h1 id="section-b/6/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For the stated outward-accelerating hyperbola, take positive signed [proper acceleration](../../../../../../proper-acceleration.md) and an initially stationary spaceship. Then $\eta=a\tau$, so $\dot p=e^{-a\tau}$ and $\dot q=e^{a\tau}$. The initial values are $p(0)=-1/a$, $q(0)=1/a$. [Integration](../../../../../../integral-split.md) gives

$$
\boxed{p(\tau)=-\frac1a e^{-a\tau},\qquad q(\tau)=\frac1a e^{a\tau}.}
$$

Taking their half-sum and half-difference yields the [hyperbolic motion](../../../../../../hyperbolic-motion.md)

$$
\boxed{t(\tau)=\frac1a\sinh(a\tau),\qquad x(\tau)=\frac1a\cosh(a\tau).}
$$

The printed position data alone do not specify the initial velocity. If the initial [rapidity](../../../../../../rapidity.md) is an arbitrary $\eta_0$, the equally valid positive-acceleration solution is

$$
t(\tau)=\frac{\sinh(\eta_0+a\tau)-\sinh\eta_0}{a},\qquad
x(\tau)=\frac1a+\frac{\cosh(\eta_0+a\tau)-\cosh\eta_0}{a}.
$$

Thus the requested special expression implicitly selects $\eta_0=0$.
