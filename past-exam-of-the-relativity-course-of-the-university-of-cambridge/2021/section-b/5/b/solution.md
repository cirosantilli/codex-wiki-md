<h1 id="section-b/5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $\Psi=k_\alpha x^\alpha$. For a nonzero symmetric amplitude, substituting the [plane wave](../../../../../plane-wave.md) into the wave equation gives $-(k_\sigma k^\sigma)\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\cos\Psi=0$. The gauge condition gives $-k_\sigma\epsilon^{\nu\sigma}\sin\Psi=0$. Thus a propagating mode satisfies

$$
\boxed{k_\sigma k^\sigma=0,\qquad
k_\sigma\epsilon^{\nu\sigma}=0,\qquad
\epsilon^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{\nu\mu}.}
$$

The [wavevector](../../../../../wavevector.md) is null, so the [gravitational wave](../../../../../gravitational-wave-split.md) propagates at the vacuum [speed of light](../../../../../speed-of-light.md). The amplitude is transverse as a four-dimensional tensor in [Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity](../../../../../lorenz-gauge-in-linearized-gravity.md). This condition alone does not set all time components or the [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) to zero: [residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity](../../../../../residual-gauge-symmetry-of-linearized-gravity.md) remains. Of the ten symmetric components, four Lorenz constraints and four independent residual coordinate choices leave two physical [gravitational wave polarizations](../../../../../gravitational-wave-polarization.md).
