<h1 id="section-b/5/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

An independent physical solution is the [cross polarization](../../../../../cross-polarization.md)

$$
\boxed{\epsilon_\times^{\mu\nu}
=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\0&0&h_\times&0\\0&h_\times&0&0\\0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

It is symmetric, traceless and transverse to $k^\mu=(\omega,0,0,\omega)$. Unlike the pure coordinate mode, it has nonzero [linearized Riemann curvature operator](../../../../../linearized-riemann-curvature-operator.md): for example, in the convention $R_{0i0j}^{(1)}=-\partial_t^2h_{ij}/2$,

$$
R_{0102}^{(1)}=\frac12\omega^2h_\times\cos[\omega(t-z)].
$$

Changing the curvature sign convention reverses this component but cannot make it vanish. [Gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor](../../../../../gauge-invariance-of-the-linearized-riemann-tensor.md) therefore proves that this nonzero mode cannot be removed by a [coordinate transformation](../../../../../smooth-transition-map.md).

The principal stretch directions are rotated by $45$ degrees relative to the [plus polarization](../../../../../plus-polarization.md). In the same proper-length convention as part (c), its first-order angular response is $\delta\ell/\Delta=-\tfrac12h_\times\sin(2\theta)\cos[\omega(t-z_0)]$. The two independent transverse patterns are the two propagating gravitational degrees of freedom.
