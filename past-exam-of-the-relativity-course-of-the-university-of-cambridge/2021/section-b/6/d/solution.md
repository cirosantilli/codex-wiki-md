<h1 id="section-b/6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Differentiating the supplied coordinates gives

$$
dX=\cosh(\alpha t)dx+\alpha x\sinh(\alpha t)dt,\qquad
c\,dT=\sinh(\alpha t)dx+\alpha x\cosh(\alpha t)dt.
$$

Subtract the squares. The cross terms cancel and $\cosh^2-\sinh^2=1$, yielding

$$
\boxed{ds^2=c^2dT^2-dX^2.}
$$

The spacetime is flat [Minkowski spacetime](../../../../../minkowski-spacetime.md) in [Rindler coordinates](../../../../../rindler-coordinates.md). $x>0$ covers $X>|cT|$, the right [Rindler wedge](../../../../../rindler-wedge.md), while $x<0$ covers the left wedge $X<-|cT|$; neither is the whole spacetime.

The null combinations are

$$
X+cT=x e^{\alpha t},\qquad X-cT=x e^{-\alpha t}.
$$

For $x=C e^{-\alpha t}$ the first is constant; for $x=C e^{\alpha t}$ the second is constant. Thus part (a)'s exponentials are simply the inertial light rays $X\pm cT=\text{constant}$. Their coordinate-time asymptotes reflect the chart's [Rindler horizon](../../../../../rindler-horizon.md), not a failure of light to move at $c$ in an inertial frame.
