<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the [rest frame](../../../../../rest-frame.md), [conservation of energy](../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) would require $E_\gamma=mc^2$, whereas [conservation of momentum](../../../../../momentum-conservation.md) would require the [photon](../../../../../photon.md) [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) to vanish. These contradict $|\mathbf p_\gamma|=E_\gamma/c$. More generally, [four-momentum](../../../../../four-momentum.md) conservation would equate the initial massive [four-momentum](../../../../../four-momentum.md) to a null [four-momentum](../../../../../four-momentum.md), although their [Minkowski norms](../../../../../minkowski-norm.md) differ:

$$
P_\mu P^\mu=m^2c^2>0,\qquad p_{\gamma\mu}p_\gamma^\mu=0.
$$

**An isolated massive particle cannot decay into one [photon](../../../../../photon.md) in any [inertial frame](../../../../../inertial-frame.md).** Directly, a laboratory [speed](../../../../../speed.md) $u<c$ gives $E=\gamma mc^2$ and $|\mathbf p|=\gamma mu$; matching a [photon](../../../../../photon.md) would require $E=c|\mathbf p|$, hence $u=c$. Recoil against another object could change the kinematics, but would introduce an additional participant.
