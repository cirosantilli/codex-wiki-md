<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write the [four-velocity](../../../../../four-velocity.md) $U^\mu=\gamma(c,\mathbf v)$ and [four-acceleration](../../../../../four-acceleration.md) $A^\mu=dU^\mu/d\tau$. Since $U_\mu A^\mu=0$, spatial [orthogonality](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) $\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf A=0$ implies $U^0A^0=0$. Thus $A^0=c\,d\gamma/d\tau=0$ and **the [speed](../../../../../speed.md) is constant**. If “[acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md)” is read as the laboratory three-acceleration instead, $\gamma\mathbf v\cdot d\mathbf v/dt=0$ immediately gives the same conclusion.

For [four-acceleration in uniform circular motion](../../../../../four-acceleration-in-uniform-circular-motion.md), the [speed](../../../../../speed.md) is $r|\omega|<c$, so $\gamma=(1-r^2\omega^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ is constant. Differentiating the [four-velocity](../../../../../four-velocity.md) using $d/d\tau=\gamma\,d/dt$ gives

$$
\boxed{A^\mu=\left(0,-\gamma^2r\omega^2\cos\omega t,
-\gamma^2r\omega^2\sin\omega t,0\right).}
$$

The [proper acceleration](../../../../../proper-acceleration.md) is $\sqrt{-A_\mu A^\mu}=\gamma^2r\omega^2$. The vanishing time component reflects constant [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md); the spatial components describe the changing [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) direction.
