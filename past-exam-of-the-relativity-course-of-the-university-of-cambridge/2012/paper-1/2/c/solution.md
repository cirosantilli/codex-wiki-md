<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

With Cartesian [Minkowski spacetime](../../../../../minkowski-spacetime.md) coordinates $x^\mu=(ct,x,y,z)$, introduce the [four-current](../../../../../four-current.md) $J^\mu=(c\rho,\mathbf J)$ and [electromagnetic four-potential](../../../../../electromagnetic-four-potential.md) $\mathcal A^\mu=(\phi/c,\mathbf A)$. The antisymmetric [electromagnetic field tensor](../../../../../electromagnetic-field-tensor.md) $F^{\mu\nu}=\partial^\mu\mathcal A^\nu-\partial^\nu\mathcal A^\mu$ has components $F^{0i}=-E_i/c$ and $F^{ij}=-\epsilon^{ijk}B_k$. [Maxwell equations](../../../../../maxwell-equations-split.md) become

$$
\boxed{\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0J^\nu,\qquad
\partial_\mu\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=0,}
\qquad
\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma},
\quad \epsilon^{0123}=+1.
$$

The time and space components reproduce [Gauss's law](../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) and the [Ampère-Maxwell equation](../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md); the dual equation gives absence of [magnetic monopoles](../../../../../magnetic-monopole.md) and [Faraday's law of induction](../../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md). Antisymmetry gives [charge conservation](../../../../../charge-conservation.md) $\partial_\nu J^\nu=0$. The [relativistic Lorentz force](../../../../../relativistic-lorentz-force.md) is $dp^\mu/d\tau=qF^{\mu\nu}u_\nu$. Every displayed equation transforms tensorially under a [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md); the [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) and [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) are different components of the same object.

Two independent algebraic [electromagnetic field invariants](../../../../../electromagnetic-field-invariants.md) are

$$
\boxed{F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=2(B^2-E^2/c^2),\qquad
F_{\mu\nu}\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=-4\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B/c.}
$$

The second is a [pseudoscalar](../../../../../pseudoscalar.md): it is invariant under proper [Lorentz transformations](../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) but changes sign under [spatial reflection](../../../../../spatial-reflection.md). For perpendicular fields, the sign of the first distinguishes a frame with purely [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) from one with purely [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md). Both invariants vanish for a [plane electromagnetic wave](../../../../../plane-electromagnetic-wave.md) with $\mathbf E\perp\mathbf B$ and $E=cB$, although the field itself is nonzero.
