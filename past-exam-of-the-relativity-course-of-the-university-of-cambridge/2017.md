<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Use the [Christoffel symbol](../riemannian-geometry.md#christoffel-symbol) formula with its first index raised:

$$
\Gamma^i{}_{jk}=\frac12g^{i\ell}(\partial_jg_{\ell k}+\partial_kg_{\ell j}-\partial_\ell g_{jk}).
$$

The diagonal [metric tensor](../general-relativity.md#metric-tensor) has $g_{rr}=e^{2\chi}$ and $g_{\theta\theta}=r^2e^{2\chi}$, with inverse components $g^{rr}=e^{-2\chi}$ and $g^{\theta\theta}=r^{-2}e^{-2\chi}$. Since $\partial_r\chi=\chi'$ and all angular derivatives vanish,

$$
\boxed{\Gamma^r{}_{rr}=\chi',\qquad
\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}=-r-r^2\chi',\qquad
\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r}=\Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta}=\frac1r+\chi'.}
$$

For example, $\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}=-\tfrac12e^{-2\chi}\partial_r(r^2e^{2\chi})$. The other components vanish. The formula supplied in the paper gives the first-kind coefficients $\Gamma_{ijk}$; multiplication by the inverse [metric tensor](../general-relativity.md#metric-tensor) is needed for the requested second-kind coefficients. These [polar coordinates](../multivariable-calculus.md#polar-coordinates) are understood away from $r=0$.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

For a [scalar field](../classical-field-theory.md#scalar-field), $\nabla_\mu\phi=\partial_\mu\phi$. Apply the [product rule](../calculus.md#product-rule) to the [stress-energy tensor](../stress-energy-tensor.md), using [metric compatibility](../vector-bundle.md#metric-compatibility) to pass the [covariant derivative](../general-relativity.md#covariant-derivative) through $g^{\mu\nu}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}
={}&(\Box\phi)\nabla^\nu\phi
+\nabla^\mu\phi\,\nabla_\mu\nabla^\nu\phi\\
&-\nabla_\sigma\phi\,\nabla^\nu\nabla^\sigma\phi
+m^2\phi\nabla^\nu\phi.
\end{aligned}
$$

The middle terms cancel because the [Levi-Civita connection](../general-relativity.md#levi-civita-connection) gives a [symmetric Hessian of a scalar field](../riemann-curvature-tensor.md#symmetric-hessian-of-a-scalar-field). Thus, with the sign conventions of this question, the [scalar stress-energy divergence identity](../classical-field-theory.md#scalar-stress-energy-divergence-identity) becomes

$$
\boxed{\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=(\Box\phi+m^2\phi)\nabla^\nu\phi=0.}
$$

The last equality is the [Klein-Gordon equation](../wave-equation.md#klein-gordon-equation). This [stress-energy conservation](../stress-energy-tensor.md#stress-energy-conservation) follows from the field equation and [metric compatibility](../vector-bundle.md#metric-compatibility); it does not require imposing the [Einstein field equations](../einstein-field-equations.md).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Take [metric signature](../topological-manifold.md#metric-signature) $(+,-,-,-)$ and the supplied covariant [electromagnetic field tensor](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#electromagnetic-field-tensor), so $F_{0i}=E_i$ and $F_{ij}=-\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$. Raising both indices reverses the time-space components but leaves the spatial components unchanged. Its complete contraction is therefore

$$
F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=2(B^2-E^2).
$$

Define the [dual electromagnetic field tensor](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#dual-electromagnetic-field-tensor) by $\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}$, with $\varepsilon^{0123}=+1$. Direct contraction gives

$$
\boxed{E^2-B^2=-\frac12F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu},\qquad
\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B=-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}.}
$$

These [electromagnetic field invariants](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#electromagnetic-field-invariants) are complete tensor contractions. The second is a [pseudoscalar](../quantum-mechanics.md#pseudoscalar): it is invariant under proper [Lorentz transformations](../special-relativity.md#lorentz-transformation) and changes sign under spatial inversion.

For $T^{0i}$ the metric trace term vanishes. Since $F^0{}_j=E_j$,

$$
F^0{}_{\rho}F^{i\rho}=\sum_jE_j(-\epsilon_{ijk}B_k)=-(\mathbf E\times\mathbf B)_i.
$$

Consequently the [Poynting vector](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-vector) is exactly the time-space row in the stated $c=1$ units:

$$
\boxed{(T^{01}_{\rm em},T^{02}_{\rm em},T^{03}_{\rm em})
=\frac1{\mu_0}\mathbf E\times\mathbf B.}
$$

↑ **Parent:** [2017](2017.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

The three classic tests probe orbital motion, the propagation of [light](../optics.md#light), and clock rates in a [gravitational field](../physics.md#gravitational-field).

**[Schwarzschild perihelion precession](../general-relativity.md#schwarzschild-perihelion-precession).** For an orbit with [semi-major axis](../celestial-mechanics.md#semi-major-axis) $a$ and [orbital eccentricity](../celestial-mechanics.md#orbital-eccentricity) $e$, the leading advance per revolution is

$$
\boxed{\Delta\varpi=\frac{6\pi GM}{a(1-e^2)c^2}.}
$$

The relevant observation for [Mercury](../planetary-science.md#mercury-planet) is the residual advance after the much larger ordinary perturbations have been included, approximately $43$ arcseconds per century.[Physics through the 1990s: Gravitation, cosmology and cosmic-ray physics, 1986: discussion of the anomalous perihelion advance](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19870009701/downloads/19870009701.pdf) An orbital fit must account for the other planets and reference-frame effects before interpreting that residual as a relativistic contribution.

**[Schwarzschild light deflection](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#schwarzschild-light-deflection).** A ray with large [impact parameter](../classical-mechanics.md#impact-parameter) $b$ passing a nearly spherical mass is deflected through

$$
\boxed{\alpha\simeq\frac{4GM}{bc^2}.}
$$

At the [Sun](../stellar-astrophysics.md#sun)'s limb this is about $1.75$ arcseconds. The [Eddington experiment](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#eddington-experiment) used the 1919 [solar eclipse](../astronomy.md#solar-eclipse) to photograph background [stars](../stellar-astrophysics.md#star) near the obscured [Sun](../stellar-astrophysics.md#sun), then compared their positions with reference photographs. The measured effect is an apparent outward displacement of the background [stars](../stellar-astrophysics.md#star), not a direct photograph of a curved light path. Telescope focus, plate scale and atmospheric effects must be controlled when extracting such small shifts.[1920 paper, observational comparison and deflection analysis](../physics-bibliography.md#a-determination-of-the-deflection-of-light-by-the-sun-s-gravitational-field-by-frank-watson-dyson-and-others)

**[Gravitational redshift](../general-relativity.md#gravitational-redshift).** For a photon emitted lower in a weak static field and received a height $H$ above it,

$$
\boxed{\frac{\nu_{\rm rec}-\nu_{\rm em}}{\nu_{\rm em}}\simeq-\frac{gH}{c^2}.}
$$

The [Pound-Rebka experiment](../general-relativity.md#pound-rebka-experiment) used the narrow recoil-free nuclear resonance supplied by the [Mössbauer effect](../mossbauer-effect.md). A controlled source motion supplies a small [Doppler shift](../wave.md#doppler-effect) that compensates the gravitational shift, allowing a change in resonant absorption to reveal it across a laboratory tower.[1960 experiment: gamma-ray source and resonant absorber at different heights](../physics-bibliography.md#apparent-weight-of-photons-by-robert-vivian-pound-and-glen-rebka) The leading weak-field redshift follows from the [equivalence principle](../general-relativity.md#equivalence-principle) and therefore does not, by itself, distinguish every competing metric theory of gravity.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Nearby freely falling particles can accelerate relative to one another even though each follows a [geodesic](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic). A local [inertial frame](../mathematical-physics.md#inertial-frame) removes the common gravitational acceleration at one event; it cannot remove the variation across an extended laboratory. That variation is a [tidal force](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#tidal-force), measured covariantly by [geodesic deviation](../riemann-curvature-tensor.md#geodesic-deviation).

Let $u^\mu$ be the reference [four-velocity](../special-relativity.md#four-velocity) and $\xi^\mu$ an infinitesimal separation to a neighboring [geodesic](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic), with matching [affine parameters](../riemannian-geometry.md#affine-parameter). With the convention $[\nabla_\beta,\nabla_\gamma]V^\mu=R^\mu{}_{\nu\beta\gamma}V^\nu$,

$$
\boxed{\frac{D^2\xi^\mu}{D\tau^2}=R^\mu{}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}u^\alpha u^\beta\xi^\gamma.}
$$

This relates measured relative [acceleration](../classical-mechanics.md#acceleration) to the [Riemann curvature tensor](../riemann-curvature-tensor.md). Reversing the curvature-tensor convention reverses the displayed curvature sign, without changing the physical relative motion.

In a small freely falling frame outside a spherical mass, the weak-field [tidal tensor](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#tidal-tensor) has radial and transverse components proportional to $2,-1,-1$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\ddot\xi_r=\frac{2GM}{r^3}\xi_r,\qquad
\ddot\xi_{\perp}=-\frac{GM}{r^3}\xi_{\perp}.}
$$

An initially spherical cloud stretches radially and compresses in both transverse directions. Two separated test masses, with their changing separation measured by light, form the basic idea of a gravity gradiometer; the common falling motion drops out. A uniform apparent acceleration in an accelerated laboratory can occur even in flat [Minkowski spacetime](../special-relativity.md#minkowski-spacetime), so it is the separation-dependent tidal response that diagnoses curvature.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Write $r_s=2GM/c^2$ and $f=1-r_s/r$. The [Schwarzschild tortoise coordinate](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#schwarzschild-tortoise-coordinate) satisfies $dr_*/dr=1/f$ and can be chosen as $r_*=r+r_s\ln|r/r_s-1|$. Define the advanced time $v=t+r_*/c$. Substituting $dt=dv-dr/(cf)$ into the [Schwarzschild metric](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md) gives the [ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#ingoing-eddington-finkelstein-coordinates) form

$$
\boxed{ds^2=f c^2dv^2-2c\,dv\,dr-r^2d\Omega^2.}
$$

The coefficients are regular at $r=r_s$ and the radial-block determinant is $-c^2$, so the exterior Schwarzschild-time divergence is a coordinate singularity at the future [event horizon](../black-hole.md#event-horizon). The physical curvature singularity at $r=0$ remains.

The two families of [radial null trajectories in ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#radial-null-trajectories-in-ingoing-eddington-finkelstein-coordinates) obey

$$
\boxed{v=\mathrm{constant}\quad\text{(ingoing)},\qquad
\frac{dr}{dv}=\frac c2\left(1-\frac{r_s}{r}\right)\quad\text{(outgoing)}.}
$$

Outside the [event horizon](../black-hole.md#event-horizon), the outgoing family increases $r$; at the horizon it generates the horizon itself; inside, even the outgoing future-directed family decreases $r$. An infaller crosses the future horizon at finite $v$ and finite [proper time](../special-relativity.md#proper-time). The chart makes this causal geometry visible without mistaking Schwarzschild coordinate time for the infaller's clock.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2017](2017.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

Let $x^\mu(\tau)$ denote the spacetime coordinates, with dots meaning differentiation with respect to an [affine parameter](../riemannian-geometry.md#affine-parameter); for a massive particle take [proper time](../special-relativity.md#proper-time) $\tau$. The [metric tensor](../general-relativity.md#metric-tensor) determines the quadratic [geodesic Lagrangian](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-lagrangian) $L=g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu$. The [Euler-Lagrange equations](../analysis.md#euler-lagrange-equation) are

$$
\frac d{d\tau}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot x^\mu}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial x^\mu}=0.
$$

Symmetry of the [metric tensor](../general-relativity.md#metric-tensor) gives $\partial L/\partial\dot x^\mu=2g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\nu$. Hence the covariant [four-momentum](../special-relativity.md#four-momentum) is $p_\mu=mg_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\nu=(m/2)\partial L/\partial\dot x^\mu$. If $x^\mu$ is a [cyclic coordinate](../lagrangian-mechanics.md#cyclic-coordinate), its [Euler-Lagrange equation](../analysis.md#euler-lagrange-equation) immediately gives $\dot p_\mu=0$.

For the rotating metric, $t,\phi,z$ are all cyclic. Thus

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
p_t&=m[(c^2-\omega^2r^2)\dot t-\omega r^2\dot\phi],\\
p_\phi&=-mr^2(\dot\phi+\omega\dot t),\qquad p_z=-m\dot z.
\end{aligned}}
$$

Combining the first two equations cancels their $r$ dependence:

$$
\boxed{\dot t=\frac{p_t-\omega p_\phi}{mc^2}=\mathrm{constant}.}
$$

The radial [Euler-Lagrange equation](../analysis.md#euler-lagrange-equation) is $-2\ddot r=-2r(\dot\phi+\omega\dot t)^2$, so

$$
\boxed{\ddot r=r(\dot\phi+\omega\dot t)^2=\frac{p_\phi^2}{m^2r^3}.}
$$

This is the [geodesic equation in a uniformly rotating Minkowski chart](../mathematical-physics.md#geodesic-equation-in-a-uniformly-rotating-minkowski-chart). With inertial angle $\Phi=\phi+\omega t$, $-p_\phi$ is the inertial axial [angular momentum](../momentum.md#angular-momentum). The outward radial-coordinate acceleration expresses the familiar centrifugal term associated with angular motion: the underlying free trajectory is a straight line in [Minkowski spacetime](../special-relativity.md#minkowski-spacetime). It does not imply an external radial force. Holding a particle at rest in the [rotating reference frame](../mathematical-physics.md#rotating-reference-frame) instead requires a real inward force.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Let the [inertial frame](../mathematical-physics.md#inertial-frame) $S'$ move at speed $v$ in the positive $x$ direction relative to $S$, with parallel axes and coincident origins at zero time. The inverse [Lorentz transformation](../special-relativity.md#lorentz-transformation) gives

$$
dt=\gamma_v(dt'+v\,dx'/c^2),\quad dx=\gamma_v(dx'+v\,dt'),\quad dy=dy',\quad dz=dz'.
$$

Here $\gamma_v=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ and $\mathbf u'=d\mathbf x'/dt'$ is the particle's [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) in $S'$. Dividing spatial differentials by $dt$ gives the [relativistic velocity addition](../special-relativity.md#velocity-addition-formula) law

$$
\boxed{u_x=\frac{u_x'+v}{1+vu_x'/c^2},\qquad
u_y=\frac{u_y'}{\gamma_v(1+vu_x'/c^2)},\qquad
u_z=\frac{u_z'}{\gamma_v(1+vu_x'/c^2)}.}
$$

For the collision, set $c=1$ during the algebra. Let the incident projectile have [energy](../energy.md) $E>m_1$ and [momentum](../momentum.md) $p=\sqrt{E^2-m_1^2}$. The target is initially at rest. The squared [invariant mass](../special-relativity.md#invariant-mass), boost speed and [Lorentz factor](../special-relativity.md#lorentz-factor) of the [center-of-momentum frame](../special-relativity.md#center-of-momentum-frame) are

$$
s=m_1^2+m_2^2+2m_2E,\qquad V=\frac p{E+m_2},\qquad\Gamma=\frac{E+m_2}{\sqrt s}.
$$

In that frame the projectile has $p_*=m_2p/\sqrt s$ and $E_*=(m_1^2+m_2E)/\sqrt s$. An [elastic collision](../classical-mechanics.md#elastic-collision) changes the direction of its [momentum](../momentum.md), through an angle $\varphi$, while preserving these magnitudes. Transforming the outgoing [four-momentum](../special-relativity.md#four-momentum) back to the laboratory gives

$$
\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\varphi}{\Gamma(\cos\varphi+B)},\qquad
B=\frac{VE_*}{p_*}=\frac{m_1^2+m_2E}{m_2(E+m_2)}>1.
$$

The maximum occurs at $\cos\varphi=-1/B$. Since

$$
\Gamma^2(B^2-1)=\frac{m_1^2-m_2^2}{m_2^2},
$$

the [maximum laboratory deflection in relativistic elastic scattering](../classical-mechanics.md#maximum-laboratory-deflection-in-relativistic-elastic-scattering) is

$$
\boxed{\tan\theta_{\max}=\frac{m_2}{\sqrt{m_1^2-m_2^2}},\qquad
\sin\theta\leq\sin\theta_{\max}=\frac{m_2}{m_1}.}
$$

The printed strict inequality needs correction: equality is kinematically attainable. For example, in $c=1$ units take $m_1=2$, $m_2=1$, and incident $E=3$. A projectile scattered through $30^\circ$ with final [energy](../energy.md) $16/7$ has [momentum](../momentum.md) $(3\sqrt5/7,\sqrt{15}/7)$. The target then has [energy](../energy.md) $12/7$ and [momentum](../momentum.md) $(4\sqrt5/7,-\sqrt{15}/7)$; both outgoing particles satisfy their [mass shells](../special-relativity.md#mass-shell) and conserve total [four-momentum](../special-relativity.md#four-momentum). This gives $\sin\theta=1/2=m_2/m_1$ exactly. The inequality is strict only away from the maximizing direction.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [2017](2017.md)

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**Curvature contractions.** Use the index contraction stipulated in the question. In three dimensions,

$$
R_{jk}=g^{il}K(g_{ik}g_{jl}-g_{il}g_{jk})=K(g_{jk}-3g_{jk})=-2Kg_{jk}.
$$

The same contraction in four dimensions gives

$$
R_{\beta\gamma}=-\frac\Lambda3(g_{\beta\gamma}-4g_{\beta\gamma})=\Lambda g_{\beta\gamma}.
$$

Then the [Ricci scalar](../general-relativity.md#ricci-scalar) is $R=4\Lambda$ and the [Einstein tensor](../riemann-curvature-tensor.md#einstein-tensor) is $G_{\mu\nu}=-\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$. Thus $R_{\mu\nu}=\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ is precisely the vacuum [Einstein field equations](../einstein-field-equations.md) $G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=0$.

**The static solution.** For $A=1+\lambda^2r^2$, $A'=2\lambda^2r$ and $A''=2\lambda^2$. The given [Ricci tensor](../riemann-curvature-tensor.md#ricci-tensor) components become

$$
R_{00}=-3\lambda^2A,\quad R_{11}=\frac{3\lambda^2}{A},\quad
R_{22}=3\lambda^2r^2,\quad R_{33}=3\lambda^2r^2\sin^2\theta.
$$

Each equals $-3\lambda^2$ times the corresponding [metric tensor](../general-relativity.md#metric-tensor) component. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\Lambda=-3\lambda^2.}
$$

This is [Anti-de Sitter spacetime](../general-relativity.md#anti-de-sitter-spacetime) in [static coordinates on anti-de Sitter spacetime](../general-relativity.md#static-coordinates-on-anti-de-sitter-spacetime), with curvature radius $1/\lambda$. The overall [metric signature](../topological-manifold.md#metric-signature) is opposite to the convention used in some descriptions of those coordinates; the geodesic constants below use the question's $(+,-,-,-)$ convention.

**Conserved quantities and radial motion.** In the equatorial plane, the [geodesic Lagrangian](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-lagrangian) is $L=A\dot t^2-\dot r^2/A-r^2\dot\phi^2$. Its [cyclic coordinates](../lagrangian-mechanics.md#cyclic-coordinate) give

$$
\boxed{k=A\dot t,\qquad h=r^2\dot\phi.}
$$

For rest [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) $m$, $mk=p_t$ is the conserved Killing [energy](../energy.md) and $mh=-p_\phi$ is the axial [angular momentum](../momentum.md#angular-momentum). Timelike normalization $L=1$ gives

$$
\boxed{\dot r^2=k^2-A\left(1+\frac{h^2}{r^2}\right)
=k^2-1-\lambda^2h^2-\lambda^2r^2-\frac{h^2}{r^2}.}
$$

For purely radial motion $h=0$, differentiating away from a turning point gives $\ddot r=-\lambda^2r$, and continuity extends that equation through the turning point. An outward launch from the origin at $\tau=0$ has

$$
\boxed{r(\tau)=\frac{\sqrt{k^2-1}}\lambda\sin(\lambda\tau),\qquad
a=\frac{\sqrt{k^2-1}}\lambda,\quad b=\lambda.}
$$

This is the [radial timelike geodesic in anti-de Sitter spacetime](../general-relativity.md#radial-timelike-geodesic-in-anti-de-sitter-spacetime). Its first return to the origin is

$$
\boxed{\tau_{\rm return}=\frac\pi\lambda,}
$$

independent of the outward launch speed for every nontrivial launch $k>1$. The coordinate $r$ is nonnegative, so this sine expression describes the excursion up to that return; subsequent passage through the origin requires changing the radial direction or using a signed diameter coordinate. The speed changes the amplitude, not the harmonic frequency.

**Circular periods.** The effective radial potential is $V_{\rm eff}=A(1+h^2/r^2)$. For a circle at $r_0$, $V_{\rm eff}'=0$ gives $h^2=\lambda^2r_0^4$, and normalization gives $k=A(r_0)$. Thus $\dot t=1$ and $\dot\phi=\pm\lambda$. A [stable circular timelike geodesic in anti-de Sitter spacetime](../general-relativity.md#stable-circular-timelike-geodesic-in-anti-de-sitter-spacetime) therefore has

$$
\boxed{\Delta t=\Delta\tau=\frac{2\pi}\lambda,}
$$

independently of radius. This is the same isochronous behavior as the central harmonic radial equation. A static clock placed at the orbit's radius instead measures $\sqrt{1+\lambda^2r_0^2}\,\Delta t$, which does depend on radius; it is a different clock from the orbiting particle's [proper time](../special-relativity.md#proper-time).
