<h1 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

For motion along one spatial axis, introduce the [rapidity](../../../../rapidity.md) $\eta$ so that the [four-velocity](../../../../four-velocity.md) is $u^\mu=c(\cosh\eta,\sinh\eta,0,0)$. Its [four-acceleration](../../../../four-acceleration.md) is

$$
a^\mu=c\frac{d\eta}{d\tau}(\sinh\eta,\cosh\eta,0,0),\qquad a_\mu a^\mu=-c^2\left(\frac{d\eta}{d\tau}\right)^2.
$$

A constant signed [proper acceleration](../../../../proper-acceleration.md) $g$ therefore gives $d\eta/d\tau=g/c$, hence $\eta=\eta_0+g\tau/c$. Differentiating the [four-velocity](../../../../four-velocity.md) twice yields

$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2u^\mu}{d\tau^2}=\frac{g^2}{c^2}u^\mu.}
$$

The zero transverse components also satisfy this equation. The constant acceleration measured on board is the [proper acceleration](../../../../proper-acceleration.md), rather than a constant coordinate acceleration in the laboratory.
