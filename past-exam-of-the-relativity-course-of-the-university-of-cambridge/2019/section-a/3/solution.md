<h1 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Use the [geodesic Lagrangian](../../../../geodesic-lagrangian.md) $L=\tfrac12(y^2\dot x^2+x^2\dot y^2)$, where dots denote an [affine parameter](../../../../affine-parameter.md) derivative. The [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) give

$$
\boxed{y^2\ddot x+2y\dot y\dot x-x\dot y^2=0,\qquad
x^2\ddot y+2x\dot x\dot y-y\dot x^2=0.}
$$

These are the [geodesic equations](../../../../geodesic-equation.md) wherever $xy\ne0$, so the [Riemannian metric](../../../../riemannian-metric-split.md) is nondegenerate. Substitution of $y=mx$, $\dot y=m\dot x$ and $\ddot y=m\ddot x$ makes both equations proportional to

$$
x\ddot x+\dot x^2=\frac{d}{d\lambda}(x\dot x)=0.
$$

Thus $\boxed{x^2=A\lambda+B,\quad y=mx}$ is a family of affinely parametrized [geodesics](../../../../geodesic.md), on intervals staying within one quadrant. In particular, $m\ne0$ and $x\ne0$ keep the metric nonsingular; the coordinate axes themselves are not regular points of this metric. The coordinate $x$ need not be linear in the [affine parameter](../../../../affine-parameter.md), despite the straight coordinate-space shape of these curves.
