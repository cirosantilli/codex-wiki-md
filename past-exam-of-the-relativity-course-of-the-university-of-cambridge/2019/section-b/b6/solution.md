<h1 id="section-b/b6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](../b6.md)

A [fundamental observer](../../../../comoving-observer.md) follows the mean cosmic matter flow and is at rest with respect to the homogeneous cosmological frame. [Cosmological isotropy](../../../../cosmological-isotropy.md) permits no preferred spatial acceleration, rotation vector or anisotropic expansion for this flow: the congruence is geodesic, irrotational and shear-free, with isotropic expansion. Its orthogonal homogeneous spatial slices define simultaneity. Choosing fixed spatial labels along these worldlines and their common [proper time](../../../../proper-time.md) as $t$ gives [synchronous coordinates](../../../../synchronous-coordinates.md), $g_{tt}=c^2$ and $g_{ti}=0$.

In the [Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric](../../../../friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md), $t$ is this cosmic [proper time](../../../../proper-time.md) and $\chi$ is radial [comoving coordinate](../../../../comoving-coordinate.md). On a constant-$t$ radial slice, the [proper distance in cosmology](../../../../proper-distance-in-cosmology.md) from $0$ to a fixed $\chi_0$ is $D(t)=a(t)\chi_0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\dot D=H(t)D,\qquad H(t)=\frac{\dot a(t)}{a(t)}.}
$$

This is the [Hubble parameter](../../../../hubble-parameter.md). The metric describes expansion when $\dot a>0$; its form alone also permits static or contracting histories, so expansion additionally requires that sign of the [scale factor](../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md) derivative.

For a radial [photon](../../../../photon.md), the metric components relevant to its motion are $g_{tt}=c^2$ and $g_{\chi\chi}=-a^2(t)$, both independent of $\chi$. The angular momentum components vanish. The covariant-momentum form of the [geodesic equation](../../../../geodesic-equation.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{dp_\chi}{d\lambda}=\frac12\partial_\chi g_{\nu\rho}p^\nu p^\rho=0.}
$$

Only this radial contraction is needed: angular metric coefficients can depend on $\chi$, but multiply zero angular components. The conserved quantity is the lower-index $p_1=p_\chi$, rather than the contravariant component $p^1$.

For outward travel put $P=-p_\chi>0$. The null condition $c^2(p^t)^2-a^2(p^\chi)^2=0$ gives

$$
p^\chi=\frac P{a^2},\qquad p^t=\frac P{ac},\qquad\boxed{p_0=p_t=c^2p^t=\frac{cP}{a(t)}.}
$$

Here the time coordinate is $t$, rather than $ct$. A [comoving observer](../../../../comoving-observer.md) has $u^t=1$ and measures [photon energy](../../../../photon-energy.md) $E=p_\mu u^\mu=p_t$. Thus [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) and [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) decrease as $a^{-1}$, giving the [cosmological redshift](../../../../cosmological-redshift.md)

$$
\boxed{1+z=\frac{\nu_E}{\nu_R}=\frac{a(t_R)}{a(t_E)}.}
$$

The reciprocal relation for [wavelengths](../../../../wavelength.md) follows from their common local propagation speed $c$.

Finally center the spatial coordinates on the receiving [comoving observer](../../../../comoving-observer.md). At the source, the transverse spatial line element is $dl=a(t_E)S_K(\chi)\,d\theta$. For a short transverse rod and a small observed [angle](../../../../angle.md) $\Delta\theta$,

$$
l=a(t_E)S_K(\chi)\Delta\theta
=\boxed{\frac{a(t_R)S_K(\chi)}{1+z}\Delta\theta.}
$$

Thus the [angular diameter distance](../../../../angular-diameter-distance.md) is $d_A=a(t_E)S_K(\chi)$. Writing the reception epoch as $t_0=t_R$ gives the displayed formula in the question. The length is the proper transverse length at emission; $t_0$ cannot be replaced by an unrelated epoch while retaining that expression.
