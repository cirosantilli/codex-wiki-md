## Section A

↑ **Parent:** [2022](2022.md)

<h3 id="section-a/1">1</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section A](#section-a)

<h4 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-a/1)

Use [angular size under a collinear relativistic boost](../mathematical-physics.md#angular-size-under-a-collinear-relativistic-boost). For a small distant source, the two [angular diameters](../geometry-and-topology.md#angular-diameter) have ratio $e^{|\eta|}$, where $\eta$ is the relative [rapidity](../special-relativity.md#rapidity). Thus $e^{|\eta|}=2$, and the relative [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) satisfies

$$
\boxed{\frac{|v|}{c}=\tanh(\log2)=\frac{2^2-1}{2^2+1}=\frac35.}
$$

The speed is therefore $0.6c$. The observers are momentarily coincident, so this is [relativistic aberration](../mathematical-physics.md#relativistic-aberration), rather than a difference in their distance from the planet. The observer moving faster towards the planet measures the smaller angular diameter under the source-direction aberration convention.

<h3 id="section-a/2">2</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section A](#section-a)

<h4 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-a/2)

The [metric tensor](../general-relativity.md#metric-tensor) and its inverse are diagonal: $g_{xx}=1$, $g_{yy}=f^2$, $g^{xx}=1$, $g^{yy}=f^{-2}$ on a patch where $f\ne0$. Only $\partial_xg_{yy}=2ff'$ is nonzero. The [Christoffel symbol](../riemannian-geometry.md#christoffel-symbol) formula therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\Gamma^x_{yy}=-ff',\qquad\Gamma^y_{xy}=\Gamma^y_{yx}=\frac{f'}f,}
$$

with all other components zero. Using the stated [Riemann curvature tensor](../riemann-curvature-tensor.md) convention,

$$
R^x{}_{yxy}=\partial_x\Gamma^x_{yy}-\Gamma^y_{yx}\Gamma^x_{yy}
=-(f'^2+ff'')+f'^2,
$$

so $\boxed{R^x{}_{yxy}=-ff''}$. This is the [curvature of a two-dimensional warped metric](../second-fundamental-form.md#curvature-of-a-two-dimensional-warped-metric); its [Gaussian curvature](../second-fundamental-form.md#gaussian-curvature) is $-f''/f$.

<h3 id="section-a/3">3</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section A](#section-a)

<h4 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#section-a/3)

For a grazing [null geodesic](../special-relativity.md#null-geodesic), take the [impact parameter](../classical-mechanics.md#impact-parameter) as $b\simeq R=6.0\times10^6\,\mathrm m$. The leading [Schwarzschild light deflection](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#schwarzschild-light-deflection) is

$$
\delta=\frac{4GM}{bc^2}.
$$

With the [gravitational constant](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#gravitational-constant) $G\simeq6.6743\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ and [speed of light](../special-relativity.md#speed-of-light) $c\simeq2.998\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$,

$$
\boxed{\delta\simeq9.9\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{rad}\simeq0.0567^\circ\simeq204\text{ arcsec}\simeq3.4\text{ arcmin}.}
$$

Here $GM/(Rc^2)\simeq2.5\times10^{-4}\ll1$, so the weak-field approximation is appropriate. A ray passing at a larger [impact parameter](../classical-mechanics.md#impact-parameter) has a correspondingly smaller deflection.

## Section B

↑ **Parent:** [2022](2022.md)

<h3 id="section-b/4">4</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section B](#section-b)

<h4 id="section-b/4/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#section-b/4)

<h5 id="section-b/4/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#section-b/4/a)

A [fundamental observer](../cosmology.md#comoving-observer) follows the coarse-grained cosmological matter, with fixed [comoving coordinates](../cosmology.md#comoving-coordinate). The [cosmological principle](../cosmology.md#cosmological-principle) asserts [spatial homogeneity](../cosmology.md#spatial-homogeneity) and [cosmological isotropy](../cosmology.md#cosmological-isotropy) on large scales for this family of observers. A moving observer generally sees a preferred direction, for example a Doppler dipole in the [cosmic microwave background](../cosmic-microwave-background.md), even when the underlying cosmology is isotropic.

In an [FLRW metric](../cosmology.md#friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric), these worldlines are orthogonal to homogeneous spatial slices. Synchronize their clocks to define [cosmic time](../cosmology.md#cosmic-time) $t$, which is their [proper time](../special-relativity.md#proper-time). Along a fixed spatial label, $ds^2=c^2dt^2$, giving $g_{tt}=c^2$. Orthogonality of the spatial coordinate directions to the observers' [four-velocity](../special-relativity.md#four-velocity) gives $g_{ti}=0$. Thus, in the $+---$ [metric signature](../topological-manifold.md#metric-signature),

$$
\boxed{ds^2=c^2dt^2+g_{ij}(t,\mathbf x)\,dx^idx^j,}
$$

where the spatial matrix $g_{ij}$ is negative definite. [Spatial homogeneity](../cosmology.md#spatial-homogeneity) and [cosmological isotropy](../cosmology.md#cosmological-isotropy) further restrict it to $-a(t)^2\gamma_{ij}(\mathbf x)$, with $\gamma$ a constant-curvature spatial [metric](../topological-analysis.md#metric).

<h4 id="section-b/4/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#section-b/4)

<h5 id="section-b/4/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-b/4/b)

For radial motion, use the [geodesic Lagrangian](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-lagrangian)

$$
\mathcal L=\frac12(c^2\dot t^2-a(t)^2\dot\chi^2).
$$

The [Euler-Lagrange equation](../analysis.md#euler-lagrange-equation) for $t$ gives $c^2\ddot t+a(da/dt)\dot\chi^2=0$. Since $\chi$ is a [cyclic coordinate](../lagrangian-mechanics.md#cyclic-coordinate), its conjugate momentum is constant. Hence

$$
\boxed{\frac d{d\tau}(c^2\dot t)+a\frac{da}{dt}\dot\chi^2=0,\qquad a^2\dot\chi=C.}
$$

For a massive particle, take $\tau$ as [proper time](../special-relativity.md#proper-time); the [four-velocity](../special-relativity.md#four-velocity) normalization is $c^2\dot t^2-a^2\dot\chi^2=c^2$. For a [photon](../quantum-mechanics.md#photon), use an [affine parameter](../riemannian-geometry.md#affine-parameter) rather than proper time, and the corresponding tangent is a [null vector](../special-relativity.md#null-vector). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\dot t^2=1+\frac{a^2\dot\chi^2}{c^2}\quad\text{(massive)},\qquad
\dot t^2=\frac{a^2\dot\chi^2}{c^2}\quad\text{(massless)}.}
$$

The normalization of the null affine parameter remains arbitrary.

<h4 id="section-b/4/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#section-b/4)

<h5 id="section-b/4/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#section-b/4/c)

Choose the null [affine parameter](../riemannian-geometry.md#affine-parameter) so that the [photon](../quantum-mechanics.md#photon) tangent is its [four-momentum](../special-relativity.md#four-momentum) $P^\mu$. A [fundamental observer](../cosmology.md#comoving-observer) has $U^\mu=(1,0,0,0)$ in cosmic-time coordinates, so the measured energy is $E=P_\mu U^\mu=c^2P^t$. The null normalization and conserved radial quantity in part (b) give

$$
P^t=\frac{|C|}{ac},\qquad E=\frac{c|C|}{a}.
$$

Thus [redshifting of peculiar momentum](../cosmology.md#redshifting-of-peculiar-momentum) gives $E\propto a^{-1}$. Since $E=h\nu$, with $h$ the [Planck constant](../quantum-mechanics.md#planck-constant), the received-to-emitted [frequency](../wave.md#frequency) ratio is $\nu_R/\nu_E=a(t_E)/a(t_R)$. By the definition of [cosmological redshift](../cosmology.md#cosmological-redshift),

$$
\boxed{1+z=\frac{\nu_E}{\nu_R}=\frac{a(t_R)}{a(t_E)}.}
$$

<h4 id="section-b/4/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#section-b/4)

<h5 id="section-b/4/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#section-b/4/d)

A nearby [fundamental observer](../cosmology.md#comoving-observer) measures radial proper distance $d\ell=a\,d\chi$ and proper time $dt$. Thus the signed radial [peculiar velocity](../cosmology.md#peculiar-velocity) is

$$
\boxed{v=a\frac{d\chi}{dt}=\frac{a\dot\chi}{\dot t}.}
$$

Substitute $a^2\dot\chi=C$ and the massive normalization from part (b):

$$
\boxed{v(a)=\frac{C/a}{\sqrt{1+C^2/(a^2c^2)}},\qquad a\gamma v=C.}
$$

Equivalently, for velocity $v_0$ at scale factor $a_0$,

$$
v(a)=\frac{v_0a_0}{\sqrt{a^2(1-v_0^2/c^2)+a_0^2v_0^2/c^2}}.
$$

This is the exact law of [redshifting of peculiar momentum](../cosmology.md#redshifting-of-peculiar-momentum). In the nonrelativistic limit, $v\propto a^{-1}$; an initially relativistic speed stays below $c$ and decreases as the universe expands.

<h3 id="section-b/5">5</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section B](#section-b)

<h4 id="section-b/5/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#section-b/5)

<h5 id="section-b/5/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#section-b/5/a)

Write $f(r)=1-2\mu/r$. In the equatorial plane, the [geodesic Lagrangian](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-lagrangian) is $\mathcal L=\frac12(fc^2\dot t^2-f^{-1}\dot r^2-r^2\dot\phi^2)$. The [cyclic coordinates](../lagrangian-mechanics.md#cyclic-coordinate) $t$ and $\phi$ give the conserved quantities

$$
\boxed{f\dot t=k,\qquad r^2\dot\phi=h.}
$$

The squared tangent norm is constant along an affinely parametrized [geodesic](../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic), since metric compatibility gives $d[g(\dot X,\dot X)]/d\tau=2g(D\dot X/d\tau,\dot X)=0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{fc^2\dot t^2-f^{-1}\dot r^2-r^2\dot\phi^2=E.}
$$

For a massive particle parametrized by [proper time](../special-relativity.md#proper-time), $E=c^2$; a [photon](../quantum-mechanics.md#photon) has a null tangent, so $E=0$. This $E$ denotes the tangent norm, not the photon's physical energy. A future-directed exterior photon has $k>0$, proportional to its positive [Killing energy](../killing-vector-field.md#killing-energy). Its affine parameter can be rescaled, so there is no mass-shell normalization fixing $k$ to a particular positive value.

<h4 id="section-b/5/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#section-b/5)

<h5 id="section-b/5/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-b/5/b)

Set $E=0$ in part (a), substitute the two conserved quantities, and multiply the normalization by $f$:

$$
c^2k^2-\dot r^2-f\frac{h^2}{r^2}=0.
$$

Consequently the [null effective potential in Schwarzschild spacetime](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#null-effective-potential-in-schwarzschild-spacetime) is

$$
\boxed{\frac12\dot r^2+V_{\rm eff}(r)=\frac12c^2k^2,\qquad
V_{\rm eff}(r)=\frac{h^2}{2r^2}\left(1-\frac{2\mu}{r}\right).}
$$

Only radii where the total radial energy is at least this [effective potential](../mathematical-physics.md#effective-potential) are accessible.

<h4 id="section-b/5/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#section-b/5)

<h5 id="section-b/5/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#section-b/5/c)

For $h\ne0$, the [effective potential](../mathematical-physics.md#effective-potential) vanishes at $r=2\mu$, rises to a maximum, and then falls to zero as $r\to\infty$. Its [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) is $V'_{\rm eff}=h^2(3\mu-r)/r^4$, so the only circular [null geodesic](../special-relativity.md#null-geodesic) has

$$
\boxed{r=3\mu,\qquad V_{\rm eff}=\frac{h^2}{54\mu^2}.}
$$

The second derivative there is $V''_{\rm eff}=-h^2/(81\mu^4)<0$, proving instability by the [effective potential stability criterion](../mathematical-physics.md#effective-potential-stability-criterion). These circular orbits make up the [photon sphere](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#photon-sphere). If the formula is continued inside the horizon, the potential is negative and diverges to $-\infty$ as $r\downarrow0$. For purely radial light, $h=0$, the potential is identically zero and there is no circular orbit.

<h4 id="section-b/5/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#section-b/5)

<h5 id="section-b/5/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#section-b/5/d)

A photon arriving from infinity crosses the barrier precisely when $c^2k^2/2$ exceeds the maximum in part (c):

$$
\boxed{k^2>\frac{h^2}{27\mu^2c^2}.}
$$

At infinity its spatial tangent has speed in affine-parameter units $ck$, while $h$ is its angular momentum per unit affine normalization. A straight asymptotic ray with [impact parameter](../classical-mechanics.md#impact-parameter) $b$ therefore has $|h|=bck$. Thus

$$
\boxed{b=\frac{|h|}{ck},\qquad b<3\sqrt3\mu.}
$$

At equality, the incoming ray approaches the unstable [photon sphere](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#photon-sphere) asymptotically and is not captured. A larger impact parameter gives an outer turning point and scattering back to infinity.

<h4 id="section-b/5/e">e</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#section-b/5)

<h5 id="section-b/5/e/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#section-b/5/e)

Measure $\alpha$ in the [orthonormal tetrad](../general-relativity.md#orthonormal-frame-in-spacetime) of a static observer at $r>2\mu$. This specifies the local frame needed to define an angle. If $E_\infty$ is the conserved photon energy, [gravitational redshift](../general-relativity.md#gravitational-redshift) gives $E_{\rm loc}=E_\infty/\sqrt{1-2\mu/r}$. The tangential local [momentum](../momentum.md) is $E_{\rm loc}\sin\alpha/c$, so the [impact parameter](../classical-mechanics.md#impact-parameter) is

$$
b=\frac{r\sin\alpha}{\sqrt{1-2\mu/r}}.
$$

Inside the [photon sphere](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#photon-sphere), an outward ray must have $b<3\sqrt3\mu$ to cross the potential barrier. The [Schwarzschild photon escape cone](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#schwarzschild-photon-escape-cone) therefore has critical half-angle

$$
\boxed{\alpha_{\rm crit}(r)=\arcsin\left[\frac{3\sqrt3\mu}{r}\sqrt{1-\frac{2\mu}{r}}\right],\qquad 0\le\alpha<\alpha_{\rm crit}.}
$$

The upper bound is strict: a critical ray only approaches the circular orbit. At $r=3\mu$, the critical half-angle is $\pi/2$, and exactly tangential light is circular. As $r\downarrow2\mu$, the cone shrinks to zero opening. No static observer exists exactly on the [Schwarzschild event horizon](../schwarzschild-spacetime.md#schwarzschild-event-horizon), and no future-directed photon emitted there escapes to exterior infinity; the zero-angle result is an exterior limit.

<h3 id="section-b/6">6</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section B](#section-b)

<h4 id="section-b/6/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#section-b/6)

<h5 id="section-b/6/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#section-b/6/a)

At any event on a massive observer's [worldline](../special-relativity.md#world-line), choose an [orthonormal tetrad](../general-relativity.md#orthonormal-frame-in-spacetime) with timelike basis vector $e_{(0)}=U/c$. Its components obey $g(e_{(a)},e_{(b)})=\eta_{ab}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, so locally $ds^2=\eta_{ab}\,dX^{(a)}dX^{(b)}$: the [Minkowski metric](../special-relativity.md#minkowski-metric). A [local inertial frame](../mathematical-physics.md#local-inertial-frame) can also remove the [Christoffel symbols](../riemannian-geometry.md#christoffel-symbol) at that event. Curvature cannot be removed on a finite region, and a frame following an accelerated observer need not remain inertial in its neighbourhood.

In general coordinates, $X^\mu(\tau)$ labels the trajectory, while the [four-velocity](../special-relativity.md#four-velocity) and [four-acceleration](../special-relativity.md#four-acceleration) are

$$
U^\mu=\frac{dX^\mu}{d\tau},\qquad
A^\mu=\frac{DU^\mu}{d\tau}=\frac{dU^\mu}{d\tau}+\Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}U^\nu U^\rho.
$$

Coordinate values $X^\mu$ are not themselves a vector under arbitrary nonlinear coordinate changes; their tangent $U$ is. For fixed rest mass $m$, the [four-momentum](../special-relativity.md#four-momentum) is $P^\mu=mU^\mu$ and the [four-force](../special-relativity.md#four-force) is $f^\mu=DP^\mu/d\tau=mA^\mu$. With the $+---$ [metric signature](../topological-manifold.md#metric-signature),

$$
\boxed{U^\mu U_\mu=c^2,\qquad U^\mu A_\mu=0,\qquad A^\mu A_\mu=-a^2\le0,}
$$

where $a$ is the [proper acceleration](../special-relativity.md#proper-acceleration). The middle identity follows by differentiating the constant velocity norm; in the instantaneous [rest frame](../mathematical-physics.md#rest-frame), $U=(c,\mathbf0)$ and $A=(0,\mathbf a)$, proving the last identity.

<h4 id="section-b/6/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#section-b/6)

<h5 id="section-b/6/b/i">i</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-b/6/b)

<h6 id="section-b/6/b/i/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#section-b/6/b/i)

The inverse transformation is $t=(p+q)/2$, $x=(q-p)/2$. Thus

$$
ds^2=\frac14\big[(dp+dq)^2-(dq-dp)^2\big]=dp\,dq.
$$

The [metric tensor](../general-relativity.md#metric-tensor) in these [light-cone coordinates](../special-relativity.md#light-cone-coordinates) is therefore

$$
\boxed{(g_{ab})_{(p,q)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/2\\1/2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
(g^{ab})=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\2&0\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Both off-diagonal entries are needed, since the quadratic line element sums over both index orders.

<h5 id="section-b/6/b/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-b/6/b)

<h6 id="section-b/6/b/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#section-b/6/b/ii)

The [Lorentz transformation](../special-relativity.md#lorentz-transformation) is $t'=\gamma(t-vx)$, $x'=\gamma(x-vt)$ in units $c=1$. Adding and subtracting gives

$$
p'=\gamma(1+v)(t-x),\qquad q'=\gamma(1-v)(t+x).
$$

Since $\gamma(1+v)=\sqrt{(1+v)/(1-v)}$ and the other factor is its reciprocal,

$$
\boxed{p'=\alpha p,\qquad q'=q/\alpha,\qquad\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{1+v}{1-v}}=e^\eta.}
$$

Here $\eta$ is the boost [rapidity](../special-relativity.md#rapidity). Their product is unchanged, as required by invariance of the [Minkowski interval](../special-relativity.md#minkowski-interval).

<h5 id="section-b/6/b/iii">iii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-b/6/b)

<h6 id="section-b/6/b/iii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#section-b/6/b/iii)

The [proper time](../special-relativity.md#proper-time) normalization of the astronaut's [four-velocity](../special-relativity.md#four-velocity) gives $\dot t^2-\dot x^2=1$. In the [light-cone coordinates](../special-relativity.md#light-cone-coordinates) this is

$$
\boxed{\dot p\dot q=1.}
$$

The [four-acceleration](../special-relativity.md#four-acceleration) has squared norm $\ddot t^2-\ddot x^2=-a^2$, because $a$ is the constant [proper acceleration](../special-relativity.md#proper-acceleration). The same coordinate change therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\ddot p\ddot q=-a^2.}
$$

Equivalently write $\dot q=e^\eta$, $\dot p=e^{-\eta}$. Their second derivatives give $\ddot p\ddot q=-\dot\eta^2$, so the [rapidity form of one-dimensional proper acceleration](../special-relativity.md#rapidity-form-of-one-dimensional-proper-acceleration) makes $\dot\eta=\pm a$, with sign fixing the acceleration direction.

<h5 id="section-b/6/b/iv">iv</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-b/6/b)

<h6 id="section-b/6/b/iv/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#section-b/6/b/iv)

For the stated outward-accelerating hyperbola, take positive signed [proper acceleration](../special-relativity.md#proper-acceleration) and an initially stationary spaceship. Then $\eta=a\tau$, so $\dot p=e^{-a\tau}$ and $\dot q=e^{a\tau}$. The initial values are $p(0)=-1/a$, $q(0)=1/a$. [Integration](../integral.md) gives

$$
\boxed{p(\tau)=-\frac1a e^{-a\tau},\qquad q(\tau)=\frac1a e^{a\tau}.}
$$

Taking their half-sum and half-difference yields the [hyperbolic motion](../special-relativity.md#hyperbolic-motion)

$$
\boxed{t(\tau)=\frac1a\sinh(a\tau),\qquad x(\tau)=\frac1a\cosh(a\tau).}
$$

The printed position data alone do not specify the initial velocity. If the initial [rapidity](../special-relativity.md#rapidity) is an arbitrary $\eta_0$, the equally valid positive-acceleration solution is

$$
t(\tau)=\frac{\sinh(\eta_0+a\tau)-\sinh\eta_0}{a},\qquad
x(\tau)=\frac1a+\frac{\cosh(\eta_0+a\tau)-\cosh\eta_0}{a}.
$$

Thus the requested special expression implicitly selects $\eta_0=0$.

<h5 id="section-b/6/b/v">v</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-b/6/b)

<h6 id="section-b/6/b/v/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#section-b/6/b/v)

Use the specified hyperbolic trajectory. The emitted right-moving [photon](../quantum-mechanics.md#photon) follows $x=L+t-t_0$, so its retarded [null coordinate](../general-relativity.md#null-coordinate) is $p=t_0-L$. Interception at astronaut [proper time](../special-relativity.md#proper-time) $\tau$ requires

$$
t_0-L=-\frac1a e^{-a\tau}.
$$

The astronaut has velocity $v=\tanh(a\tau)$ relative to the star and $\gamma=\cosh(a\tau)$. The collinear [relativistic Doppler effect](../wave.md#relativistic-doppler-effect) gives

$$
\boxed{\nu_{\rm obs}=\nu_0\gamma(1-v)=\nu_0e^{-a\tau}=a\nu_0(L-t_0).}
$$

This is the [Doppler shift for a uniformly accelerated receiver](../special-relativity.md#doppler-shift-for-a-uniformly-accelerated-receiver). A finite reception time exists precisely when $t_0<L$, so **the limiting emission time is $L$**, approached from below. At $t_0=L$ the photon lies on the [Rindler horizon](../special-relativity.md#rindler-horizon) and never reaches the astronaut. If reception is restricted to $\tau\ge0$, the full emission range is $L-1/a\le t_0<L$; for emission after $t=0$, it is $0\le t_0<L$.
