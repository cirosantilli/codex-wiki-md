<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Assume an emitter at rest on the stellar surface and a static observer at infinity. Write $f(r)=1-2GM/(rc^2)$. The time-translation [Killing energy](../../../../../killing-energy.md) of a [photon](../../../../../photon.md) is conserved, while the locally measured [photon](../../../../../photon.md) [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is proportional to $1/\sqrt{f(r)}$. Consequently

$$
\frac{\nu_\infty}{\nu_R}=\sqrt{f(R)},\qquad
\boxed{1+z=\frac1{\sqrt{1-2GM/(Rc^2)}}.}
$$

Using $G=6.67430\times10^{-11}\ {\rm m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$, $c=299792458\ {\rm m\,s^{-1}}$, $M=4\times10^{30}\ {\rm kg}$ and $R=1.1\times10^4\ {\rm m}$,

$$
\frac{2GM}{Rc^2}=0.54008438,\qquad
\boxed{z=0.4745548\simeq0.475.}
$$

The [Schwarzschild radius](../../../../../schwarzschild-radius.md) is approximately $5.94\ {\rm km}$, below the given surface radius. The positive shift is therefore a [gravitational redshift](../../../../../gravitational-redshift.md) of an escaping [photon](../../../../../photon.md), with received wavelength about $1.475$ times the emitted wavelength. A moving emitter would introduce an additional [Doppler factor](../../../../../doppler-factor.md); that is outside this static-surface model. The weak-field estimate $z\simeq GM/(Rc^2)=0.270$ is insufficient at this compactness. These stated constants are numerical conventions, not a claim about an unavailable historical handbook's last digits.
