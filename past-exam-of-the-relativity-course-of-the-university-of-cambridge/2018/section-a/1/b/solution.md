<h1 id="section-a/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The required invariant is obtained by [tensor contraction](../../../../../tensor-contraction.md), rather than by an ordinary sum of squares of the entries of the [electromagnetic field tensor](../../../../../electromagnetic-field-tensor.md). With $\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, raising both indices gives

$$
F^{0i}=-F_{0i},\qquad F^{ij}=F_{ij}\quad(i,j=1,2,3).
$$

The [electromagnetic field tensor](../../../../../electromagnetic-field-tensor.md) is an [antisymmetric second-rank tensor](../../../../../antisymmetric-second-rank-tensor.md), so every independent off-diagonal entry occurs twice in its contraction. The three electric contributions each acquire a minus sign, whereas the magnetic contributions retain their sign. Consequently

$$
F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
=2\left[(B^1)^2+(B^2)^2+(B^3)^2
-\frac{(E^1)^2+(E^2)^2+(E^3)^2}{c^2}\right].
$$

All indices are contracted, so this quantity is a [Lorentz scalar](../../../../../lorentz-scalar.md). Since the [speed of light](../../../../../speed-of-light.md) is invariant,

$$
\boxed{c^2|\mathbf B|^2-|\mathbf E|^2=\frac{c^2}{2}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}}
$$

is [Lorentz invariant](../../../../../lorentz-invariance.md). Both terms have the units of squared [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md), $\mathrm{V^2\,m^{-2}}$ in SI units.
