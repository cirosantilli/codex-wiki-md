# Rayleigh scattering wavelength dependence

↑ **Parent:** [Rayleigh scattering](rayleigh-scattering.md)

For a fixed [sphere](sphere.md) and fixed [refractive index](refractive-index.md), both the [Rayleigh scattering cross-section](rayleigh-scattering-by-a-small-sphere.md) and [scattering efficiency](scattering-efficiency.md) are proportional to $\lambda^{-4}$. Halving [wavelength](wavelength.md) multiplies either by $16$, as long as the shorter [wavelength](wavelength.md) still satisfies $ka\ll1$. If [optical dispersion](dispersion-optics.md) changes $m$, the material factor changes too; the pure fourth-power comparison then needs correction.

For equal incident [light intensities](light-intensity.md) at blue [wavelength](wavelength.md) $450\,\mathrm{nm}$ and red [wavelength](wavelength.md) $650\,\mathrm{nm}$, the same toy [sphere](sphere.md) gives

$$
\frac{\sigma(450\,\mathrm{nm})}{\sigma(650\,\mathrm{nm})}=\left(\frac{650}{450}\right)^4=\frac{28561}{6561}\simeq4.35315.
$$

Blue [light](light.md) therefore produces about $4.35$ times the scattered [power](power.md). This compares the particle's response at two [wavelengths](wavelength.md); it does not assert that an actual sky has this ratio of observed blue and red [light intensities](light-intensity.md).
