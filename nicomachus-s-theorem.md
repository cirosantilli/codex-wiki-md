<h1 id="nicomachus-s-theorem">Nicomachus's theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Squared triangular number](squared-triangular-number.md)

For every [nonnegative integer](natural-number.md) $n$,

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^3=\left(\frac{n(n+1)}2\right)^2=T_n^2.
$$

For example, $1^3+2^3+3^3+4^3=1+8+27+64=100=T_4^2$. A short proof uses the [triangular number recurrence](triangular-number-recurrence.md) and the [triangular number square-complement identity](triangular-number-square-complement-identity.md):

$$
T_k^2-T_{k-1}^2=(T_k-T_{k-1})(T_k+T_{k-1})=k\cdot k^2=k^3.
$$

Summing this [difference of squares](difference-of-two-squares.md) identity gives a [telescoping sum](telescoping-sum.md); all intermediate [squared triangular numbers](squared-triangular-number.md) cancel. [Nicomachus](nicomachus.md) describes a related grouping of the [odd integers](odd-integer.md): take one, then the next two, then the next three, producing $1,3+5,7+9+11,\ldots$, which are $1^3,2^3,3^3,\ldots$. This historical grouping is recorded in [Introduction to Arithmetic by Nicomachus (Hahn teacher edition)](introduction-to-arithmetic-by-nicomachus-hahn-teacher-edition.md) (Book II, Chapter 20, §5, printed p. 75).
