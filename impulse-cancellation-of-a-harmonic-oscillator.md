# Impulse cancellation of a harmonic oscillator

↑ **Parent:** [Jump condition for an impulse](jump-condition-for-an-impulse.md)

For the [harmonic oscillator equation](simple-harmonic-motion.md) $y''+\omega^2y=J\delta(t-t_*)$, with $\omega>0$, the displacement is continuous and the velocity increases by $J$ at the impulse. If the incoming displacement is zero and $J=-y'(t_*-)$, the outgoing displacement and velocity both vanish. The subsequent [homogeneous solution](homogeneous-solution.md) is identically zero. Equivalently, the causal forced response $(J/\omega)H(t-t_*)\sin(\omega(t-t_*))$ cancels the pre-existing oscillation, where $H$ is the [Heaviside step function](heaviside-step-function.md).
