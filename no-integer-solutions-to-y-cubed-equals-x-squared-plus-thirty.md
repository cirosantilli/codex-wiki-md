# No integer solutions to y cubed equals x squared plus thirty

↑ **Parent:** [Diophantine equation](diophantine-equation.md)

The equation has no [integer](integer-split.md) solutions. Work in the [ring of integers](ring-of-integers.md) $\mathbb Z[\theta]$ for $\theta=\sqrt{-30}$. If a rational prime divides both $y$ and thirty, it divides $x$, and the valuation of $x^2+30$ is one because thirty is squarefree, contradicting the valuation of a cube. Thus $\gcd(y,30)=1$. The two [principal ideals](principal-ideal.md) $(x+\theta),(x-\theta)$ are coprime: any common [prime ideal](prime-ideal.md) lies above a prime dividing both $N(2\theta)=120$ and $y$. Their product is $(y)^3$, so unique [prime ideal](prime-ideal.md) factorization gives $(x+\theta)=A^3$. The [Ideal class group of Q of square root minus thirty](ideal-class-group-of-q-of-square-root-minus-thirty.md) has exponent two, forcing $A$ to be principal. The only units are $\pm1$ and both are cubes, so $x+\theta=(a+b\theta)^3$ for [integers](integer-split.md) $a,b$. Comparison of the coefficient of $\theta$ gives $1=3b(a^2-10b^2)$, impossible modulo three.
