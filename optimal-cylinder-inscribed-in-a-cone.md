# Optimal cylinder inscribed in a cone

↑ **Parent:** [Cone (geometry)](cone-geometry.md)

For a coaxial cylinder standing on the base of a right circular [cone](cone-geometry.md) of height $h>0$ and semi-vertical [angle](angle.md) $0<\alpha<\pi/2$, similar triangles give radius $r=(h-x)\tan\alpha$ at cylinder height $x$. Its [volume](volume.md) is $V=\pi x(h-x)^2\tan^2\alpha$. Differentiation gives $V'=\pi\tan^2\alpha(h-x)(h-3x)$, so the unique positive-volume [maximum](maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) has $x=h/3$ and occupies $4/9$ of the cone's [volume](volume.md). Its curved [surface area](surface-area.md) is instead $A=2\pi x(h-x)\tan\alpha$, maximized at $x=h/2$. Changing the optimized quantity changes the optimal shape, even though the geometric constraint is identical.
