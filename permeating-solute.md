# Permeating solute

↑ **Parent:** [Tonicity](tonicity.md)

A [permeating solute](permeating-solute.md) can cross the specified membrane appreciably during the observation interval. This is a property of a [solute](solute.md)-membrane-time comparison, not an immutable label attached to a [molecule](molecule-split.md). Passage can reduce a [concentration](concentration.md) difference through [passive transport](passive-transport.md) while [water](water.md) is also moving; it does not require [water](water.md) to wait.

For an original dilute ideal example, stipulate a flexible model [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md) with $20\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$ retained particles and an external reservoir with $10\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$ retained particles plus $10\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$ freely permeating neutral [solute](solute.md). Initially the total [osmolarities](osmotic-concentration.md) are equal. Suppose the permeating [solute](solute.md) eventually has equal internal and external [concentrations](concentration.md), there is negligible [pressure](pressure-split.md) difference, no [active transport](active-transport.md), and the internal retained particle amount is conserved. The permeating contributions cancel from the final osmotic balance; the internal retained [concentration](concentration.md) must fall from $20$ to $10\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$. The required [volume](volume.md) is twice its initial value. This illustrates an initially equal-[osmolarity](osmotic-concentration.md) but [hypotonic solution](hypotonic-solution.md); it assumes the boundary can survive that expansion and predicts no universal time course.
