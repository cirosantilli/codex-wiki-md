# Immunoglobulin G

↑ **Parent:** [Antibody](antibody.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunoglobulin_G)

[Immunoglobulin G](immunoglobulin-g.md) is an [antibody](antibody.md) class whose conventional four-chain architecture supplies the example above.

An ordinary [IgG](immunoglobulin-g.md) has two matching [immunoglobulin heavy chains](immunoglobulin-heavy-chain.md) and two matching [immunoglobulin light chains](immunoglobulin-light-chain.md), abbreviated $H_2L_2$. Its gamma-type [immunoglobulin heavy chains](immunoglobulin-heavy-chain.md) contribute both to recognition and to interactions with immune partners.[Gerald M. Edelman, Antibody Structure and Molecular Immunology, printed pp. 35–37, Table 1 and Figure 3: gamma chains, four-chain architecture and separate functional regions](antibody-structure-and-molecular-immunology-by-gerald-m-edelman.md)

**[Figure 3](#image-igg-chain-continuity-and-protein-disulfide-bond-connectivity). IgG chain continuity and protein disulfide bond connectivity**. Original chain-connectivity schematic. Gold crossbars denote interchain [protein disulfide bonds](protein-disulfide-bond.md); intrachain crosslinks are omitted. The drawn crossbar count and geometry illustrate connectivity, rather than atomic coordinates or a universal [IgG](immunoglobulin-g.md) subclass inventory.

A useful way to read this picture is to follow a single [polypeptide chain](polypeptide.md) continuously from its [N-terminus](n-terminus.md) to its [C-terminus](c-terminus.md), then examine the crosslinks separately. The same [immunoglobulin heavy chain](immunoglobulin-heavy-chain.md) contributes to a [Fab fragment](fragment-antigen-binding.md) and to the [Fc fragment](fragment-crystallizable-region.md); these named fragments are not pre-existing separate chains. Conversely, one [Fab fragment](fragment-antigen-binding.md) contains material from two different chains. The [protein primary structure](protein-primary-structure.md) and the fragment inventory therefore answer different questions about the same [protein](protein-split.md).
