# Irreducible elements of a principal ideal domain are prime

↑ **Parent:** [Principal ideal domain](principal-ideal-domain.md)

Let $\pi$ be an [irreducible element](irreducible-element.md) of a [principal ideal domain](principal-ideal-domain.md) $R$, and suppose $\pi\nmid a$. The [ideal](ideal.md) $(\pi,a)=(d)$ has generator $d$ dividing $\pi$. Irreducibility makes $d$ a [unit](unit-in-a-ring.md) or an associate of $\pi$; the latter would imply $\pi\mid a$. Hence $(\pi,a)=R$, giving $u\pi+va=1$ for some $u,v\in R$. If $\pi\mid ab$, multiply this identity by $b$: both $u\pi b$ and $vab$ are divisible by $\pi$, so $\pi\mid b$. This is precisely the [prime element](prime-element.md) property.
