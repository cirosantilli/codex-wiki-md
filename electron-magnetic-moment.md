# Electron magnetic moment

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_magnetic_moment)

The [electron magnetic moment](electron-magnetic-moment.md) couples an [electron](electron.md)'s [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) to a [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md). Its intrinsic [spin](spin.md) contribution is

$$
\boldsymbol\mu_s=-g_s\mu_B\frac{\mathbf S}{\hbar},\qquad
\mu_B=\frac{e\hbar}{2m_e},\qquad e>0.
$$

Here $\mu_B$ is the [Bohr magneton](bohr-magneton.md), and $g_s$ is the positive [electron spin g-factor](electron-spin-g-factor.md) under this sign convention. The minus sign makes [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) antiparallel to [spin angular momentum](spin.md). An [electron](electron.md) in a [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) can also contribute an orbital [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md); the measured response of a whole atomic level combines these contributions.

Measuring a tiny departure of $g_s$ from $2$ tests how well a theory predicts an [electron](electron.md)'s interaction with its surroundings. The [Dirac equation](dirac-equation.md) gives $g_s=2$ before [radiative corrections](radiative-correction.md); the [anomalous magnetic moment](anomalous-magnetic-moment.md) makes a small correction experimentally accessible.[pp. 299–300: spin moment and Dirac prediction](the-magnetic-moment-of-the-electron-by-polykarp-kusch.md) A useful strategy is to compare two atomic [energy levels](energy-level.md) whose orbital and [spin](spin.md) contributions have different weights, rather than trying to calibrate an absolute [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) more accurately.
