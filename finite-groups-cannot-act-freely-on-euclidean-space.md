# Finite groups cannot act freely on Euclidean space

↑ **Parent:** [Free group action](free-group-action.md)

If a nontrivial finite group acted freely on $\mathbb R^n$, a prime-order cyclic subgroup $C_p$ would too. The quotient is an $n$-manifold with contractible universal cover. The singular version of [cellular free resolution from a contractible universal cover](cellular-free-resolution-from-a-contractible-universal-cover.md) identifies its cohomology with [group cohomology](group-cohomology.md) of $C_p$. The [periodic resolution of a finite cyclic group](periodic-resolution-of-a-finite-cyclic-group.md) gives $H^{2j}(C_p;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/p$ for every $j\geq1$. But [Poincaré duality for noncompact manifolds](poincare-duality-for-noncompact-manifolds.md) makes the quotient cohomology zero above dimension $n$. This contradiction excludes the action, for arbitrary homeomorphisms, not only Euclidean isometries.
