# Point transitivity

↑ **Parent:** [Topological dynamics](topological-dynamics.md)

A [continuous map](continuous-map.md) on a nonempty [topological space](topological-space.md) is point-transitive if it has a [dense](dense-set.md) forward [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md). This is one convention for [topological transitivity](topological-transitivity.md), but on spaces with isolated points it must be distinguished from the open-set intersection definition. On a nonempty [compact metric space](compact-metric-space.md), the positive-time open-set intersection property implies point transitivity: for each member $U_j$ of a countable [basis of a topology](basis-of-a-topology.md), $\bigcup_{n\geq1}f^{-n}(U_j)$ is open and dense, and the [Baire category theorem](baire-category-theorem.md) supplies a point in their intersection. Conversely, a [surjective](surjective-function.md) point-transitive [continuous map](continuous-map.md) has every tail of its dense orbit dense, since $f^k(X)=X$ and continuity sends its orbit closure into the closure of that tail. The map $f(1/j)=1/(j+1)$, $f(0)=0$ on $\{0\}\cup\{1/j:j\geq1\}$ is point-transitive but fails the open-set intersection property.
