# Constructible circle

↑ **Parent:** [Constructible point](constructible-point.md)

A [constructible circle](constructible-circle.md) has a [constructible point](constructible-point.md) as its [centre](center-geometry.md) and [radius](radius.md) equal to the [distance](distance.md) between two [constructible points](constructible-point.md). When those coordinates lie in a real [field](field.md) $F$, its equation $(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2$ has [coefficients](coefficient.md) in $F$ because the squared [radius](radius.md) is a sum of squared coordinate differences. Intersecting it with a [constructible line](constructible-line.md) reduces to a [quadratic equation](quadratic-equation.md). Intersecting two distinct [circles](circle.md) reduces to the same situation by subtracting their normalized equations to obtain the radical axis. Thus each isolated real [intersection](set-intersection.md) requires at most a real quadratic [field extension](field-extension.md).
