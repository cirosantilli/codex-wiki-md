# Energy fluxes of a strong adiabatic shock

↑ **Parent:** [Strong-shock Rankine-Hugoniot conditions](strong-shock-rankine-hugoniot-conditions.md)

In the rest frame of a strong planar [shock wave](shock-wave.md), a perfect gas has $\rho_2/\rho_1=(\gamma+1)/(\gamma-1)$, $u_2/u_1=(\gamma-1)/(\gamma+1)$ and $p_2=2\rho_1u_1^2/(\gamma+1)$. Its downstream [specific internal energy](specific-internal-energy.md) is $\epsilon_2=p_2/[(\gamma-1)\rho_2]=2u_1^2/(\gamma+1)^2$. Multiplying by mass flux $j=\rho_1u_1$ gives the internal-energy flux in the title. The lost bulk [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) flux instead equals $j(u_1^2-u_2^2)/2=\gamma F_{\rm int}$: this includes downstream pressure work and is the increase of [specific enthalpy](specific-enthalpy.md) flux in the cold-upstream limit. Neither quantity is a temperature change per unit time of a fixed target mass without further information about that target.
