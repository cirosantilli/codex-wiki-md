# Node (physics)

↑ **Parent:** [Normal mode](normal-mode.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Node_(physics))

A node of an ideal [standing wave](standing-wave.md) is a position whose oscillation [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) is zero: its [displacement](displacement.md) stays zero for all time. For a nontrivial [normal mode](normal-mode.md) $u(x,t)=\phi(x)q(t)$, this means $\phi(x)=0$. A momentary zero caused by $q(t)=0$ does not make every position a node. In more dimensions the zeros of a [mode shape](mode-shape.md) can form curves or surfaces.

For the $n$th [normal mode](normal-mode.md) of a uniform fixed-end [wave equation on a string](wave-equation-on-a-string.md), the [nodes of a standing wave](node-physics.md) are

$$
x_j=\frac{jL}{n},\qquad j=0,1,\ldots,n.
$$

There are $n+1$ [nodes of a standing wave](node-physics.md) including the two endpoints, and $n-1$ interior ones. For $n=3$ and $L=1\,\mathrm m$, they lie at $0$, $1/3$, $2/3$, and $1\,\mathrm m$. Each remains stationary through every snapshot in the [normal mode](normal-mode.md) diagram; an [antinode](antinode.md) instead marks a maximum oscillation [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md). [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, Section 16.6](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/16-6-standing-waves-and-resonance)
