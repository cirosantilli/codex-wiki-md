# Vector

↑ **Parent:** [Vector space](vector-space-split.md)

A [vector](vector.md) is an element of a [vector space](vector-space-split.md). Its defining operations are [vector addition](vector-addition.md) and [scalar multiplication](scalar-multiplication.md). An arrow in a [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md) is one example; a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) or a [function](function-split.md) can also be a [vector](vector.md) when the chosen [vector space](vector-space-split.md) uses the corresponding addition and [scalar multiplication](scalar-multiplication.md).

For instance, the [polynomials](polynomial-split.md) $p(x)=1+x$ and $q(x)=x-x^2$ belong to the real [vector space](vector-space-split.md) of [polynomials](polynomial-split.md) of degree at most two. Their [vector addition](vector-addition.md) gives $(p+q)(x)=1+2x-x^2$, and [scalar multiplication](scalar-multiplication.md) gives $(2p)(x)=2+2x$. In the [monomial basis](monomial-basis.md) $(1,x,x^2)$ these are the [coordinate vectors](coordinate-vector.md) $(1,1,0)$, $(0,1,-1)$, $(1,2,-1)$ and $(2,2,0)$ respectively. The [vectors](vector.md) are the [polynomials](polynomial-split.md); these tuples are their representations in a chosen [basis](basis.md).

A general [vector space](vector-space-split.md) does not itself prescribe lengths or [angles](angle.md). A [norm](norm.md) supplies a size, while an [inner product](inner-product-split.md) also supplies angle information and [orthogonality](orthogonal-vectors.md). The familiar arrow geometry below belongs to [Euclidean vectors](euclidean-vector.md), where this extra structure is available.
