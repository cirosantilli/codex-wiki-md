The [central dogma of molecular biology](../../../../central-dogma-of-molecular-biology.md) concerns sequence information: [DNA](../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) can be copied by [DNA replication](../../../../dna-replication.md); [transcription](../../../../transcription-biology.md) makes [RNA](../../../../ribonucleic-acid.md) from [DNA](../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md); and [translation](../../../../translation-geometry.md) makes a [protein](../../../../protein-split.md) from messenger [RNA](../../../../ribonucleic-acid.md). The familiar $\mathrm{DNA}\to\mathrm{RNA}\to\mathrm{protein}$ diagram is not an assertion that all information transfer is one-way: [reverse transcription](../../../../reverse-transcription.md) and [RNA replication](../../../../rna-replication.md) are compatible with the dogma. Its key exclusion is transfer of a [protein](../../../../protein-split.md)'s amino-acid sequence back into nucleic-acid sequence by a general templating mechanism.

[DNA](../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) is a polymer of deoxyribonucleotides with a sugar–phosphate backbone joined by [phosphodiester bonds](../../../../phosphodiester-bond.md). Its usual [DNA double helix](../../../../dna-double-helix.md) contains antiparallel strands with complementary bases, adenine paired with thymine and guanine with cytosine. The base sequence stores inherited information; complementary pairing supports accurate copying and repair. A strand has distinct $5'$ and $3'$ ends, and ordinary chain synthesis extends the $3'$ end. Packaging into chromosomes compacts and regulates access to the genetic material.

[RNA](../../../../ribonucleic-acid.md) uses ribose, with a $2'$ hydroxyl group, and uracil in place of thymine. It is usually single-stranded but forms extensive paired secondary and tertiary structures. Messenger [RNA](../../../../ribonucleic-acid.md) conveys coding information, transfer [RNA](../../../../ribonucleic-acid.md) couples codons to amino acids, and ribosomal [RNA](../../../../ribonucleic-acid.md) provides both structural organization and catalysis within the [ribosome](../../../../ribosome.md). Other [RNA](../../../../ribonucleic-acid.md) molecules regulate expression or catalyse reactions. Thus [RNA](../../../../ribonucleic-acid.md) is not merely a passive intermediate.

[Proteins](../../../../protein-split.md) are polypeptides of amino acids linked by [peptide bonds](../../../../peptide-bond.md), read from an amino terminus to a carboxyl terminus. Their primary [sequence](../../../../sequence.md) guides [protein folding](../../../../protein-folding.md); backbone [hydrogen bonds](../../../../hydrogen-bond.md) organize helices and sheets, while hydrophobic, electrostatic and other interactions produce tertiary and sometimes quaternary [protein structure](../../../../protein-structure.md). Their functions include [catalysis](../../../../catalysis.md), transport, signalling, mechanical support and molecular movement. Sequence alone does not guarantee function: folding, modifications, partners and cellular conditions matter. During [translation](../../../../translation-geometry.md), a [ribosome](../../../../ribosome.md) reads successive triplet [codons](../../../../codon.md) in messenger [RNA](../../../../ribonucleic-acid.md), with charged transfer [RNA](../../../../ribonucleic-acid.md) molecules supplying the corresponding amino acids; initiation and termination define the reading frame.
