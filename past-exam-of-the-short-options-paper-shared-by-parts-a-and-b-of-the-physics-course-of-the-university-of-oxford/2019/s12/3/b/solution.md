<h1 id="s12/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For an ideal circular pupil, the [point spread function](../../../../../point-spread-function-split.md) along a central line is

$$
\boxed{I(x)=I(0)\left[\frac{2J_1(q)}q\right]^2,\qquad q=\frac{2\pi\mathrm{NA}}{\lambda}\frac{x}{M},\quad M=100,\ \mathrm{NA}=1.3.}
$$

Here $x$ is image-plane position and the central ratio is interpreted by its limit at $q=0$. The [Airy disk](../../../../../airy-disk.md) has a bright central maximum, a first zero at object-space radius $0.61\lambda/\mathrm{NA}$ and weaker side lobes. The [Rayleigh criterion](../../../../../rayleigh-criterion.md) gives two-source [optical resolution](../../../../../optical-resolution.md) $d\simeq0.61\lambda/\mathrm{NA}$. At an illustrative $\lambda=550\,\mathrm{nm}$ this is $258\,\mathrm{nm}$ in object space or $25.8\,\mu\mathrm m$ in the image. No unique dimensional width follows without a wavelength. The right panel of the figure in [solution](../../2/c/solution.md) uses wavelength-normalized coordinates.

For one isolated emitter of known [point spread function](../../../../../point-spread-function-split.md), fitting its centre to many detected [photons](../../../../../photon.md) estimates a position far more precisely than the width. For approximately Gaussian width $s$ and $N$ independent signal [photons](../../../../../photon.md), the ideal localization [standard deviation](../../../../../standard-deviation.md) scales as $s/\sqrt N$. Finite pixels, background and read noise, aberrations, uncertain [point spread function](../../../../../point-spread-function-split.md), drift, bleaching and overlapping emitters worsen this precision. Single-source localization precision is a different task from resolving two unknown nearby sources.
