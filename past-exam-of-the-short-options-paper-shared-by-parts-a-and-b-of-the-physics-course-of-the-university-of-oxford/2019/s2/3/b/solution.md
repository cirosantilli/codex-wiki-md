<h1 id="s2/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Treat the [gravitational field](../../../../../gravitational-field.md) as approximately spherical. The [circular orbit](../../../../../circular-orbit.md) condition gives

$$
\boxed{M(<8\,\mathrm{kpc})=\frac{v_c^2r}{G}=9.0\times10^{10}M_\odot,\qquad \Upsilon=\frac{M/M_\odot}{L/L_\odot}=4.50.}
$$

If every star had a representative mass $m$ and obeyed $L/L_\odot=(m/M_\odot)^3$, its [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) would be $(m/M_\odot)^{-2}$, giving $\boxed{m\simeq0.47M_\odot}$. This is a one-mass estimate, not an actual average inferred uniquely from the data: a [galactic stellar mass function](../../../../../galaxy-stellar-mass-function.md), stellar remnants, dust and nonstellar mass alter the relation. The flattening of the [galactic disk](../../../../../galactic-disk.md) also changes the spherical conversion by an order-one factor.
