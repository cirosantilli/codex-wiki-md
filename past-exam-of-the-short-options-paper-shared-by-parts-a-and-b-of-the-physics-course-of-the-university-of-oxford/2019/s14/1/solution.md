The [Scientific Revolution](../../../../scientific-revolution.md) is a useful name for linked transformations in European [natural philosophy](../../../../natural-philosophy.md) during roughly the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, rather than a single event with an agreed beginning or end. Its strongest scientific content is a change in how motion, the heavens and natural phenomena were represented and investigated: increasingly mathematical accounts, new instruments, controlled experiments and public argument altered what could count as a persuasive explanation. The conventional span from [Nicolaus Copernicus](../../../../nicolaus-copernicus.md) in 1543 to [Isaac Newton](../../../../isaac-newton.md) in 1687 captures important developments, but excludes relevant earlier work and later consolidation.

In [astronomy](../../../../astronomy-split.md), the [heliocentric model](../../../../heliocentric-model.md) changed the arrangement of the planets without initially discarding all older mathematical devices. [Johannes Kepler](../../../../johannes-kepler.md)'s [elliptic orbits](../../../../elliptic-orbit.md) and empirical [Kepler's laws of planetary motion](../../../../kepler-s-laws-of-planetary-motion.md) then displaced uniform circular motion as a universal premise. Telescopic observations associated with [Galileo Galilei](../../../../galileo-galilei.md), including the phases of Venus and satellites of Jupiter, challenged specific inherited cosmological claims. Their significance depended on arguments that the [telescope](../../../../telescope.md) reliably revealed celestial phenomena, not simply on looking through an uncontroversial window.

In [mechanics](../../../../mechanics.md), idealized mathematical treatments of falling bodies and projectiles helped establish quantitative relations between motion and causes. [Newton's laws of motion](../../../../newton-s-laws-of-motion.md) and [Newton's law of universal gravitation](../../../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation-split.md) linked terrestrial falling bodies with celestial [orbits](../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) in a single framework. The importance of this synthesis lay in detailed quantitative problems, such as the relation between inverse-square [force](../../../../force-split.md) and planetary [motion](../../../../motion.md), not merely in announcing that mathematics applied to nature.[Books I and III, mathematical motion and the system of the world](../../../../philosophiae-naturalis-principia-mathematica-by-isaac-newton.md)

Experimental practices changed alongside theory. Air-pump work associated with [Robert Boyle](../../../../robert-boyle.md) made carefully described apparatus, witnessed trials and replication central to disputes about air and the [vacuum](../../../../vacuum.md). [Francis Bacon](../../../../francis-bacon.md) promoted organized inquiry, while [René Descartes](../../../../rene-descartes.md) pursued a mathematical and mechanical account of nature; their differences caution against inventing a single timeless “scientific method”. Scientific societies, correspondence, printed diagrams and journals helped stabilize results and reputations beyond an individual's laboratory.

The term remains selective. Mathematics, craft knowledge, instruments and observations had long histories, including substantial contributions beyond Europe. Older and newer explanatory styles coexisted; natural philosophers did not suddenly become present-day professional scientists. Religious, courtly and commercial settings often supported the same work later narrated as a victory over tradition. The [Scientific Revolution](../../../../scientific-revolution.md) therefore describes a consequential reorganization of scientific questions, practices and authority, while requiring care about geographical scope, continuity and the uneven pace of change.
