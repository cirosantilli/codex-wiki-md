<h1 id="s14/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

[Sadi Carnot](../../../../nicolas-leonard-sadi-carnot.md)'s ideal engine was deeply indebted to practical [heat engines](../../../../heat-engine.md) as a source of questions and physical constraints, while its decisive result depended on deliberate idealization. In 1824 steam power was an established technology, and variations in engine economy made the conversion of heat into useful [work](../../../../work.md) a problem with immediate industrial meaning. Comparing different working substances and operating temperatures required a theory more general than the description of one machine.

Real engines supplied the essential architecture: a working fluid, heating from a hot source, expansion producing [work](../../../../work.md), cooling or condensation, and return to an initial condition. Their losses also suggested what an ideal comparison had to remove. Friction, leakage, finite temperature differences during heat exchange and incomplete expansion waste useful performance. The idea of obtaining more work by using expansion rather than simply discarding pressure connected the argument directly to engineering experience. [Sadi Carnot](../../../../nicolas-leonard-sadi-carnot.md)'s opening discussion explicitly places the inquiry in the context of the steam engine's practical power.[Opening discussion of steam power and the idealized sequence of heating, expansion and cooling](../../../../reflexions-sur-la-puissance-motrice-du-feu-et-sur-les-machines-propres-a-developper-cette-puissance-by-sadi-carnot.md)

The theoretical advance was to replace those irreversible operations by a closed [thermodynamic cycle](../../../../thermodynamic-cycle.md). In its modern description the [Carnot cycle](../../../../carnot-cycle.md) contains an [isothermal expansion](../../../../isothermal-expansion.md) at $T_h$, an [adiabatic expansion](../../../../adiabatic-expansion.md), an [isothermal compression](../../../../isothermal-compression.md) at $T_c$, and an [adiabatic compression](../../../../adiabatic-compression.md). The fluid returns to its initial state, so the result can be compared repeatedly rather than consuming a hidden initial store. Reversibility permits an engine to be run backwards and paired with another engine, providing a powerful limiting argument independent of detailed construction.

It is essential not to attribute the whole modern interpretation to 1824. [Sadi Carnot](../../../../nicolas-leonard-sadi-carnot.md) reasoned within a [caloric theory](../../../../caloric-theory.md) in which heat was conserved and motive power was associated with its passage from hot to cold, rather like a fall between levels. The modern [first law of thermodynamics](../../../../first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) instead gives $W=Q_h-Q_c$, and the reversible [entropy](../../../../entropy-split.md) balance gives $Q_h/T_h=Q_c/T_c$. Thus the modern result

$$
\eta_{\rm rev}=1-\frac{T_c}{T_h}
$$

expresses a lasting structure of his argument in a later thermodynamic language; it should not be presented as his original derivation with contemporary concepts.

Actual engines therefore did more than decorate an abstract proof: they provided the phenomenon to explain, the operating ingredients and the direction in which improvements were sought. Yet no real engine executes a perfectly reversible [Carnot cycle](../../../../carnot-cycle.md); finite power requires finite rates, and practical machines face material and transfer constraints. The engine is most valuable as a comparative upper limit and an argument for universality. Its historical interest lies in extracting such a general limit from engineering knowledge using an initially mistaken theory of heat, not in either pure deduction without machinery or mere generalization of workshop measurements.
