<h1 id="s25/3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [transient climate response to cumulative carbon emissions](../../../../../transient-climate-response-to-cumulative-carbon-emissions.md) is $\boxed{\kappa\simeq\Delta T/\mathcal E}$, where $\mathcal E$ is cumulative emitted carbon dioxide in a stated [mass](../../../../../mass.md) unit. Here the unit is tonnes of $\mathrm{CO_2}$, rather than tonnes of carbon. Increasing concentration weakens the incremental [radiative forcing](../../../../../radiative-forcing.md) per added atmospheric tonne because forcing is approximately logarithmic. Over a substantial range this is partly offset by a weakening of carbon sinks and by the changing ratio between transient warming and forcing as ocean heat uptake evolves. These approximate compensations make warming nearly proportional to cumulative emissions; no exact cancellation follows from the logarithmic forcing law alone. The approximation is range- and pathway-dependent, and is not a theorem valid for arbitrary emissions histories.
