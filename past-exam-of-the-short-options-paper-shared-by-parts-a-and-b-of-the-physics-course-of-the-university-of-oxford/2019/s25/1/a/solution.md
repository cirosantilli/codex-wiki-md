<h1 id="s25/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a unit [mass](../../../../../mass.md) of [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md), $h=u+P/\rho=u+R_aT$. Since $u$ depends only on [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), $c_p=dh/dT=c_v+R_a$, giving $\boxed{R_a=c_p-c_v}$. For a dry parcel in adiabatic, quasistatic ascent, the [first law of thermodynamics](../../../../../first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) is $c_p\,dT=\rho^{-1}dP$. Environmental [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) gives $dP/dz=-\rho g$, so a convectively well-mixed layer has the [dry adiabatic lapse rate](../../../../../dry-adiabatic-lapse-rate.md)

$$
\boxed{\Gamma_d=-\frac{dT}{dz}=\frac{g}{c_p}.}
$$

This assumes negligible condensation, approximately constant $g$, well-mixed composition and efficient convection; a radiative or stably stratified layer need not follow it. For mole fractions $x_N,x_C$ and molar masses $M_N,M_C$, $R_a=R/(x_NM_N+x_CM_C)$ and $c_p=[x_NC_{p,N}+x_CC_{p,C}]/(x_NM_N+x_CM_C)$. Molecular vibrational excitation can make $c_p$ depend on [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md); then $\Gamma_d=g/c_p(T)$. Nitrogen dominance near terrestrial temperatures gives the familiar order $10\,\mathrm{K\,km^{-1}}$.
