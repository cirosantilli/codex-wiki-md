<h1 id="s7/1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The orbiting planet produces a potential $V(r,\vartheta,\varphi-\Omega t)$. It has explicit time dependence and exerts an azimuthal [torque](../../../../../torque.md), so generally $\dot E=V_t$ and $\dot L_z=-V_\varphi$ are nonzero. Nevertheless a simultaneous [time-translation symmetry](../../../../../time-translation-symmetry.md) $\delta t=\lambda$ and [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) $\delta\varphi=\Omega\lambda$ leaves the [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md) and $dt$ unchanged. [Noether's theorem](../../../../../noether-conserved-quantity-for-a-mechanical-point-symmetry.md) gives $Q=\Omega p_\varphi-E$ and hence

$$
\boxed{E_J=E-\Omega L_z=\text{constant}.}
$$

Equivalently $V_t=-\Omega V_\varphi$ implies $d(E-\Omega L_z)/dt=0$. This [Jacobi integral](../../../../../jacobi-integral.md) assumes a rigidly rotating potential with constant $\Omega$ and negligible back-reaction from the [test particle](../../../../../test-particle.md).
