<h1 id="s7/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Define the [canonical momentum](../../../../../canonical-momentum.md) $p_i=\partial L/\partial\dot q_i$ and the [energy function](../../../../../energy-function.md) $E=\sum_i p_i\dot q_i-L$. Along an [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) solution,

$$
\frac{dE}{dt}=\sum_i\left(\dot p_i-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}\right)\dot q_i-\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}=-\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}.
$$

Thus an autonomous [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md) has conserved $E$. For a [simple harmonic oscillator](../../../../../simple-harmonic-motion.md), $L=m\dot x^2/2-kx^2/2$ and $\boxed{E=m\dot x^2/2+kx^2/2}$ is constant. For ordinary time-independent mechanical coordinates this is the total [mechanical energy](../../../../../mechanical-energy.md).
