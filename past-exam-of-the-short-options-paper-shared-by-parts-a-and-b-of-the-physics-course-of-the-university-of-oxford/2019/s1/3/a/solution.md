[Cauchy's integral theorem](../../../../../cauchy-s-integral-theorem.md) states that a [holomorphic function](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on a simply connected open region has zero [contour integral](../../../../../contour-integral.md) around every closed piecewise smooth [contour](../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) in that region. More generally the same holds for a [contour](../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) homologous to zero in its domain of [holomorphy](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md). Having no singularity on the curve alone is insufficient: $1/z$ has a nonzero [contour integral](../../../../../contour-integral.md) around the unit circle.
