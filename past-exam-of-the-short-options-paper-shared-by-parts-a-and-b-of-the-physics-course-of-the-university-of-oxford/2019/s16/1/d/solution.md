<h1 id="s16/1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Put $x=E_I/(k_BT)$. With $\zeta=1/2$, the [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md) formula becomes

$$
50=\frac94+\frac16\left(\frac32+x\right)^2,
\quad x=\sqrt{286.5}-\frac32=15.4263.
$$

Taking $E_I=13.6\,\mathrm{eV}$ gives $\boxed{T=1.02\times10^4\,\mathrm K}$. At half [ionization](../../../../../ionization.md), $n_en_p/n_H=n_e$, so the [Saha ionization equation](../../../../../saha-ionization-equation.md) directly gives

$$
\boxed{n_e=\left(\frac{2\pi m_ek_BT}{h^2}\right)^{3/2}e^{-x}\simeq5.0\times10^{20}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}.}
$$

This uses $2g_p/g_H=1$ as specified in part (a); a different internal-state convention must be carried consistently into the numerical [electron density](../../../../../electron-density.md). The original nuclei [density](../../../../../density.md) is $n_0=2n_e\simeq10^{21}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$.
