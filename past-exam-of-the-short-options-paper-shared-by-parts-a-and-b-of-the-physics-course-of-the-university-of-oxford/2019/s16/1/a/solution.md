<h1 id="s16/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume a dilute, nondegenerate, nonrelativistic [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) in [local thermodynamic equilibrium](../../../../../local-thermodynamic-equilibrium.md), with ground-state atomic [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md), [protons](../../../../../proton.md) and [electrons](../../../../../electron.md), and neglect excited atomic levels and interactions. Let the conserved number of hydrogen nuclei per volume be $n_0$, so $n_e=n_p=\zeta n_0$ and $n_H=(1-\zeta)n_0$. Equality of [chemical potentials](../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) for $H\rightleftharpoons p+e$ gives the [Saha ionization equation](../../../../../saha-ionization-equation.md)

$$
\frac{n_en_p}{n_H}=\frac{2g_p}{g_H}\left(\frac{2\pi m_ek_BT}{h^2}\right)^{3/2}e^{-E_I/(k_BT)}.
$$

The factor two is electron spin; $g_p$ and $g_H$ must be defined consistently with nuclear spin. The small difference between the atomic and proton translational [partition functions](../../../../../canonical-partition-function.md) has been neglected. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{\zeta^2}{1-\zeta}=AT^{3/2}e^{-E_I/(k_BT)},\quad A=\frac{2g_p}{g_Hn_0}\left(\frac{2\pi m_ek_B}{h^2}\right)^{3/2}.}
$$

For the ground-state convention $g_p=1$, $g_H=2$, the statistical prefactor $2g_p/g_H$ is one. At fixed [volume](../../../../../volume.md) $n_0$ is constant, whereas an isobaric heating process would not have a constant $A$.
