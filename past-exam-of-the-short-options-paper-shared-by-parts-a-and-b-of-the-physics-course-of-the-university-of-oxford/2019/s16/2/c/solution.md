<h1 id="s16/2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Assume a cold, unmagnetized, weakly collisional [plasma](../../../../../plasma-physics.md), a small fractional bandwidth and $\omega\gg\omega_p$. The [dispersion relation](../../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) is $\omega^2=c^2k^2+\omega_p^2$, with $\omega_p^2=n_ee^2/(\epsilon_0m_e)$. The [group velocity](../../../../../group-velocity.md) and propagation time are

$$
v_g=\frac{d\omega}{dk}=c\sqrt{1-\frac{\omega_p^2}{\omega^2}},\qquad
\tau(\omega)=\frac Lc\left(1-\frac{\omega_p^2}{\omega^2}\right)^{-1/2}
\simeq\frac Lc\left(1+\frac{\omega_p^2}{2\omega^2}\right).
$$

The lower frequencies arrive later. Taking the magnitude of the frequency derivative over $\Delta\omega$ gives

$$
\boxed{\Delta\tau\simeq\frac{L\Delta\omega}{c\omega^3}\frac{n_ee^2}{\epsilon_0m_e}.}
$$

For a nonuniform line of sight, replace $n_eL$ by the [dispersion measure](../../../../../dispersion-measure.md) $\int n_e\,dl$. The light travels at the [group velocity](../../../../../group-velocity.md), not at the [phase velocity](../../../../../phase-velocity.md).
