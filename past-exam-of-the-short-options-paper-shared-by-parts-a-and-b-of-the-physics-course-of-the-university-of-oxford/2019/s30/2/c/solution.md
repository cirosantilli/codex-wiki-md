<h1 id="s30/2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

At $T=100\,\mathrm K$ with $\mu=2.3$, $c_s=\sqrt{k_BT/(\mu m_p)}\simeq599\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. A solar-mass [Keplerian disk](../../../../../keplerian-disk.md) has $v_K\simeq29.8\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ at $1\,\mathrm{AU}$, so $\boxed{h/r\simeq0.020}$: the thin-disk approximation is consistent.

For fixed central [mass](../../../../../mass.md), $h/r\propto T^{1/2}r^{1/2}$. If $T\propto r^{-q}$, then

$$
\boxed{h/r\propto r^{(1-q)/2},\qquad\text{flaring requires }q<1.}
$$

A flared outer disk intercepts more stellar radiation than a flat or self-shadowed disk, affecting its [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), chemistry, vertical structure and observed [spectral energy distribution](../../../../../spectral-energy-distribution.md). Heating changes the [sound speed](../../../../../speed-of-sound.md) and therefore transport and dispersal; it can also enhance irradiation-driven mass loss. The actual coupled evolution depends on optical properties and transport, so flaring alone does not fix a unique lifetime. “Mid-planet” in the source is read as mid-plane.
