<h1 id="s30/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A cold, self-gravitating region in a [molecular cloud](../../../../../molecular-cloud.md) can collapse when [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) overcomes thermal, turbulent and magnetic support. Increasing central [density](../../../../../density.md) produces a [protostar](../../../../../protostar.md), while radiation removes part of the released [gravitational energy](../../../../../gravitational-energy.md). The cloud initially carries [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md); material that cannot fall radially to the centre settles into a rotating [protoplanetary disk](../../../../../protoplanetary-disk.md). Collisions and cooling reduce random vertical motion, while angular-momentum transport allows some material to accrete and some to move outwards. Bipolar outflows and magnetic stresses can remove additional angular [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md).

The schematic sequence is $\text{rotating cloud}\to\text{protostar plus disk}\to\text{accreting solids and gas}\to\text{planetary system plus cleared disk}$. In the cool disk, grains condense and collide; settling and concentration can produce [planetesimals](../../../../../planetesimal.md), whose gravitational growth leads to planetary embryos. Massive cores may capture gas before the disk disperses; sufficiently massive unstable disks can also form bound condensations. Migration and interactions modify the final [orbits](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md). Radiation, accretion and outflows eventually disperse the remaining gas. Thus planet formation uses the same rotating reservoir produced by ordinary [star formation](../../../../../star-formation.md), although the efficiency and final architecture are not universal and individual growth stages remain active research topics.
