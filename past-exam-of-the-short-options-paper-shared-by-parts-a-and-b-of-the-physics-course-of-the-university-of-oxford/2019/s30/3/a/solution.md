<h1 id="s30/3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [planetary equilibrium temperature](../../../../../planetary-equilibrium-temperature.md) is the effective planetary [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) at which absorbed starlight balances emitted thermal radiation, neglecting internal power. With uniform reradiation over the full surface,

$$
(1-\alpha)\pi R_p^2\sigma T_{\rm eff}^4\frac{R_\star^2}{d^2}=4\pi R_p^2\epsilon\sigma T_{\rm eq}^4.
$$

Cancelling areas gives

$$
\boxed{T_{\rm eq}=T_{\rm eff}\left(\frac{R_\star}{2d}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/4}.}
$$

Using the supplied [Bond albedos](../../../../../bond-albedo.md) and taking the thermal [emissivity](../../../../../emissivity.md) $\epsilon=1$ yields $\boxed{T_{\rm eq,\oplus}\simeq255\,\mathrm K,\quad T_{\rm eq,J}\simeq103\,\mathrm K}$. “Perfect black bodies” must here mean perfect infrared emitters, since a perfect absorber at all wavelengths would instead have $\alpha=0$, inconsistent with retaining the supplied reflected fractions. If both albedos were set to zero, the corresponding values would be about $279$ and $122\,\mathrm K$.

Earth's mean surface [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) of about $288\,\mathrm K$ exceeds its emission [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) mainly because of the [greenhouse effect](../../../../../greenhouse-effect.md). Jupiter also radiates substantial internal [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) from cooling and contraction, including interior differentiation processes, so absorbed sunlight alone underpredicts its emission. Jupiter has no solid surface; the quoted $150\,\mathrm K$ is a representative atmospheric level and is not identical to an effective radiating [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md).
