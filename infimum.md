# Infimum

↑ **Parent:** [Infimum and supremum](infimum-and-supremum.md)

For a nonempty [set](set-split.md) $A\subseteq\mathbb R$, a real number $\ell$ is its [infimum](infimum.md) precisely when it is a [lower bound](lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) and every other [lower bound](lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) is at most $\ell$. It is the [greatest lower bound](greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md), not necessarily the [minimum](minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md).

For $C=\{1/n:n\in\mathbb N_{>0}\}$, zero is a [lower bound](lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). Every positive candidate $\varepsilon$ fails: the [Archimedean property](archimedean-property.md) gives an [integer](integer.md) $n>1/\varepsilon$, hence $1/n<\varepsilon$. Thus $\inf C=0$, although zero is never a member of $C$. This is the lower-edge counterpart of approximating an unattained [supremum](supremum.md).
