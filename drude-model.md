# Drude model

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md)

The [Drude model](drude-model.md) explains finite [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) by balancing [charge carrier](charge-carrier.md) [acceleration](acceleration.md) in an [electric field](electric-field-split.md) against loss of directed [momentum](momentum-split.md) through [collisions](collision.md). Its useful surprise is that continuous microscopic motion can produce a constant macroscopic [current density](current-density.md): the applied [electric field](electric-field-split.md) continually restores the directed [momentum](momentum-split.md) lost to the material. The simplest version has one mobile species, constant [number density](number-density.md) $n$, [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $q$, [mass](mass.md) $m>0$, and [momentum relaxation time](momentum-relaxation-time.md) $\tau>0$, in a stationary, homogeneous, [isotropic](isotropy.md) material. At zero [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md), its mean [drift velocity](drift-velocity.md) and [current density](current-density.md) obey

$$
m\frac{d\mathbf v_d}{dt}=q\mathbf E(t)-\frac{m\mathbf v_d}{\tau},\qquad
\mathbf J=nq\mathbf v_d,\qquad
\tau\frac{d\mathbf J}{dt}+\mathbf J=\sigma_0\mathbf E(t),\qquad
\sigma_0=\frac{nq^2\tau}{m}.
$$

For [electrons](electron.md), $q=-e$ with positive [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md) $e$, so $\sigma_0=ne^2\tau/m$. The [electron](electron.md) [drift velocity](drift-velocity.md) points against a constant [electric field](electric-field-split.md), whereas [conventional current](conventional-current.md) points along it. Random microscopic [velocities](velocity.md) can have zero mean without being zero individually; [drift velocity](drift-velocity.md) is this population mean, rather than each particle's speed.[pp. 2–3, Eqs. (1.2)–(1.11): collision averaging, electron force, drift and conductivity](lecture-notes-in-solid-state-3-classical-free-electron-model-for-metals-the-drude-model-by-eytan-grosfeld.md)

The same [first-order linear differential equation](first-order-linear-differential-equation.md) predicts both [Drude current relaxation](drude-current-relaxation.md) after switching an [electric field](electric-field-split.md) and the [Drude frequency response](drude-frequency-response.md) to a sinusoidal [electric field](electric-field-split.md). Together they let a measured [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) be interpreted in terms of a density, a [mass](mass.md), and a time scale; a single constant-field measurement determines only their combination $nq^2\tau/m$.

The original 1900 paper [Zur Elektronentheorie der Metalle by Paul Drude](zur-elektronentheorie-der-metalle-by-paul-drude.md) sought a common account of electrical transport and [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) using mobile charged particles and ideas from the [kinetic theory](kinetic-theory-split.md) of gases. Its introduction, especially printed pp. 568–569, explains that comparing different transport phenomena could constrain the populations of free and bound charged particles. The modern [momentum relaxation time](momentum-relaxation-time.md) formulation above is a useful presentation of the model, not a verbatim translation of the original notation or its historical assumptions about [mass](mass.md).[pp. 568–569: motivation for combining transport phenomena; OCR has spelling and formula errors](zur-elektronentheorie-der-metalle-by-paul-drude.md)
