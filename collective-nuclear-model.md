# Collective nuclear model

↑ **Parent:** [Atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md)

A description of an [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md) in terms of coordinated motion of many [nucleons](nucleon.md), especially changes in shape and orientation. The [nuclear shell model](nuclear-shell-model.md) organizes individual [nucleon](nucleon.md) states in a [nuclear mean field](nuclear-mean-field.md); the [collective nuclear model](collective-nuclear-model.md) organizes low-lying [energy levels](energy-level.md) by [nuclear deformation](nuclear-deformation.md), [nuclear rotational bands](nuclear-rotational-band.md) and [nuclear quadrupole vibrations](nuclear-quadrupole-vibration.md). These are complementary organizations of the same nuclear [many-body problem](many-body-problem.md). A deformed [nuclear mean field](nuclear-mean-field.md) can support individual-particle states while its orientation supplies a slow collective coordinate.

In his 1975 lecture, [Ben Roy Mottelson](ben-roy-mottelson.md) described coupling individual-particle motion to nuclear shape coordinates, and distinguished observed rotational spectra from vibrations about spherical equilibrium. This connects the two descriptions without treating the collective coordinates as extra constituent particles.[pp. 236–237, coupled Hamiltonian and interpretation of low-energy excitation spectra](elementary-modes-of-excitation-in-the-nucleus-by-ben-roy-mottelson.md)

[James Rainwater](james-rainwater.md)'s 1975 account identifies unexpectedly large [electric quadrupole moments](electric-quadrupole-moment.md) as a motivation for a deformable [nuclear mean field](nuclear-mean-field.md). The challenge concerned their magnitude, even where spherical-shell trends had the correct sign.[p. 264, Fig. 3 and discussion of the 1949 quadrupole compilation](background-for-the-spheroidal-nuclear-model-proposal-by-james-rainwater.md)

A simple original [potential energy](potential-energy.md) example makes the coupling tangible. Let $q$ be a dimensionless signed shape coordinate for a chosen intrinsic configuration, $C>0$ its restoring stiffness, and $f$ a configuration-dependent coupling, both with units of [energy](energy-split.md). Suppose

$$
V(q)=\frac12Cq^2-fq
=\frac C2\left(q-\frac fC\right)^2-\frac{f^2}{2C}.
$$

The minimum is $q_*=f/C$, with [energy](energy-split.md) gain $f^2/(2C)$. For chosen values $C=20u$ and $f=4u$, where $u$ is an arbitrary positive [energy](energy-split.md) unit, $q_*=0.20$ and the gain is $0.40u$. This illustrates how a configuration can favor deformation when the core can respond. It is not a fitted nuclear surface or a claim that every configuration chooses the same shape; competing configurations and [nuclear pairing](nuclear-pairing.md) can favor a spherical minimum.

The predictive attraction is economy: one collective scale relates several [energy levels](energy-level.md), while [electric quadrupole transitions](electric-quadrupole-transition.md) independently test their common structure. Agreement in both is much more restrictive than assigning a separate free parameter to every level. [Nuclear magnetic moments](nuclear-magnetic-moment.md) test magnetic structure and [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) assignments; they are distinct observables from the [electric quadrupole moments](electric-quadrupole-moment.md) used to infer charge deformation.
