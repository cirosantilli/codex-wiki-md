# Conormal derivative

↑ **Parent:** [Normal derivative](normal-derivative.md)

For a divergence-form [differential operator](differential-operator.md) $-\nabla\cdot K\nabla$, its conormal derivative is the flux factor appearing in [integration by parts](integration-by-parts.md). It generally differs from the ordinary [normal derivative](normal-derivative.md) $\partial_nu=\mathbf n\cdot\nabla u$ when $K$ is anisotropic. Decomposing the [gradient](gradient.md) along a smooth boundary into its normal and tangential components gives $\partial_{n,K}u=(\mathbf n\cdot K\mathbf n)\partial_nu+(\mathbf n\cdot K\mathbf t)\partial_{\mathbf t}u$ in two dimensions. Thus zero normal derivative does not in general remove the boundary term in the usual symmetric [weak formulation](weak-formulation.md). A zero conormal derivative does remove it and produces the natural homogeneous [Neumann boundary condition](neumann-boundary-condition.md) for that energy.
