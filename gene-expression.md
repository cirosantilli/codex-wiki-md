# Gene expression

↑ **Parent:** [Molecular genetics](molecular-genetics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_expression)

[Gene expression](gene-expression.md) uses a [gene](gene.md)'s information to produce functional [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) or a [protein](protein.md). [Transcription](transcription-biology.md) makes [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) from a [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) template; for a [protein](protein.md)-coding [gene](gene.md), [biological translation](translation-biology.md) then uses [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) to specify an [amino acid](amino-acid.md) chain. Many [genes](gene.md) instead produce functional [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md), including [transfer RNA](transfer-rna.md) and [ribosomal RNA](ribosomal-rna.md), without a translated [protein](protein.md) product. See [Alberts et al., Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition (2002): from DNA to RNA](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26887/).

Follow the chosen [coding strand](coding-strand.md) $5'\text{-}\mathrm{ATGGCTTAA}\text{-}3'$: [transcription](transcription-biology.md) of its complementary template gives $5'\text{-}\mathrm{AUGGCUUAA}\text{-}3'$ in [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md). In the stated [reading frame](reading-frame.md), its three [codons](codon.md) are AUG, GCU and UAA: [methionine](methionine.md), [alanine](alanine.md) and a [stop codon](stop-codon.md). Thus nine [nucleotides](nucleotide.md) specify two [amino acid](amino-acid.md) residues followed by termination, not three residues. This is an illustrative coding segment embedded in suitable expression and initiation context, not a claim that an isolated nine-[nucleotide](nucleotide.md) [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) is sufficient to initiate [biological translation](translation-biology.md).

Gene expression: oriented DNA template, messenger RNA codons and the resulting amino acid order**. The [coding strand](coding-strand.md) matches the displayed [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) after T is replaced by U; [RNA polymerase](rna-polymerase.md) uses the opposite, complementary [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) strand. The [ribosome](ribosome.md) reads AUG, GCU and UAA from $5'$ to $3'$. AUG and GCU specify [methionine](methionine.md) and [alanine](alanine.md); UAA signals termination without supplying an [amino acid](amino-acid.md). The chain is shown from its [N-terminus](n-terminus.md) to its [C-terminus](c-terminus.md). The labels summarize successive operations, not simultaneous binding of all depicted components; flanking signals and machinery are omitted.

The distinction between copying and use explains how [biological cells](biological-cell.md) can preserve the same [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) yet produce different products. [DNA replication](dna-replication.md) duplicates the record; [gene expression](gene-expression.md) selects and uses parts of it. [Transcription](transcription-biology.md) does not use all [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) at once, and [gene expression](gene-expression.md) can be controlled at several steps, including [transcription](transcription-biology.md), [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) processing and [biological translation](translation-biology.md). See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e: regulation of gene expression](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/16-1-regulation-of-gene-expression). The arrows describe template-directed synthesis: the [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) does not physically turn into a [protein](protein.md), and the [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) does not supply the [amino acids](amino-acid.md) from which that [protein](protein.md) is assembled.
