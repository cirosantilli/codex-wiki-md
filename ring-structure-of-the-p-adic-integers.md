# Ring structure of the p-adic integers

↑ **Parent:** [p-adic integer](p-adic-integer.md)

The [p-adic integers](p-adic-integer.md) form a [complete metric space](complete-metric-space.md) and a [discrete valuation ring](discrete-valuation-ring.md). Every nonzero $x$ is uniquely $p^k u$ with $k=v_p(x)\geq0$ and $u$ a [p-adic unit](p-adic-unit.md). The prime $p$ is a [uniformizer](uniformizer.md), and reduction modulo $p$ gives the [residue field](residue-field.md) $\mathbb F_p$. The element $p$ has no inverse inside $\mathbb Z_p$, so the ring is not a [field](field.md).

For a short ideal-classification outline, take a nonzero [ideal](ideal.md) and choose an element of smallest [P-adic valuation](p-adic-valuation.md) $k$, which exists by the [well-ordering principle](well-ordering-principle-for-the-natural-numbers.md). Its unit factor is invertible, so the ideal contains $p^k$. Every other element is divisible by $p^k$, proving that the ideal is $p^k\mathbb Z_p$. Completeness follows because $\mathbb Z_p$ is a [closed subset](closed-set.md) of the [complete metric space](complete-metric-space.md) $\mathbb Q_p$. The zero ideal is separate from this nonzero-ideal classification.
