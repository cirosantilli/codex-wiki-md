# Strong induction and accumulated hypotheses

↑ **Parent:** [Strong induction](strong-induction.md)

[Strong induction](strong-induction.md) does not prove more than ordinary [mathematical induction](mathematical-induction.md). Put $Q(n)=\bigwedge_{k=n_0}^{n}P(k)$. Ordinary [mathematical induction](mathematical-induction.md) on $Q$ retains the earlier cases and obtains the next one, establishing the same conclusion. Conversely, an ordinary [inductive step](inductive-step.md) can ignore the extra cases allowed in a [strong induction](strong-induction.md) hypothesis.[§5.3, printed pp. 129–130: equivalence of induction formats and well ordering](mathematics-for-computer-science-by-eric-lehman-and-others.md)

The [well-ordering principle](well-ordering-principle-for-the-natural-numbers.md) gives another view. A least [counterexample](counterexample.md) would have all smaller cases true, so the [strong induction](strong-induction.md) step would establish that allegedly false case. The starting case excludes the beginning.
