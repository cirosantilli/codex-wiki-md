# Angular circulation obstruction to a cylindrical potential

↑ **Parent:** [Conservative vector field](conservative-vector-field.md)

Let $g$ be a continuous $2\pi$-[periodic function](periodic-function.md) and $\mathbf F=g(\theta)\mathbf e_\theta/r$ on $r>0$. On a [circle](circle.md) about the axis, $d\mathbf r=r\mathbf e_\theta\,d\theta$, giving the displayed [line integral](line-integral.md). A single-valued [potential of a conservative vector field](potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md) on the full punctured space exists if and only if this [integral](integral-split.md) is zero. Necessity is the [fundamental theorem for line integrals](fundamental-theorem-for-line-integrals.md). For sufficiency, the primitive $P(\theta)=\int_0^\theta g(s)\,ds$ is $2\pi$-periodic when the mean is zero, and the [gradient in cylindrical coordinates](gradient-in-cylindrical-coordinates.md) gives $\nabla P=\mathbf F$.

On a domain with one angular cut removed, the same primitive gives a [potential of a conservative vector field](potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md) even when the mean is nonzero, because the angle has a single-valued continuous branch there. For example $g(\theta)=\sqrt{1+\cos\theta}$ has [integral](integral-split.md) $4\sqrt2$ around the full [circle](circle.md) but primitive $2\sqrt2\sin(\theta/2)$ on $-\pi<\theta<\pi$.
