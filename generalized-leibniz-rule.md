# Generalized Leibniz rule

↑ **Parent:** [Leibniz rule](leibniz-rule.md)

For $m$ functions with sufficiently many [derivatives](derivative.md), the $n$th [derivative](derivative.md) of their product distributes the $n$ differentiations among all factors. If factor $j$ is differentiated $k_j$ times, its term has the [multinomial coefficient](multinomial-coefficient.md) $n!/(k_1!\cdots k_m!)$, where the nonnegative integers $k_j$ sum to $n$. Induction on the number of factors proves this from the two-factor [Leibniz rule](leibniz-rule.md): splitting off the final factor gives a [binomial coefficient](binomial-coefficient-split.md), which multiplies the previous multinomial coefficient to give the one displayed above. Equivalently, count the ways to choose which of the $n$ successive applications of the [product rule](product-rule.md) differentiate each factor.
