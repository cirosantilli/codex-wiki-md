# Square-root reduction of a quadratic eikonal equation

↑ **Parent:** [Eikonal equation](eikonal-equation.md)

On an open set where $u>0$, put $v=\sqrt u$. The [chain rule](chain-rule.md) gives $\nabla u=2v\nabla v$, so the quadratic [eikonal equation](eikonal-equation.md) is equivalent to $|\nabla v|=1$. For constant positive data $v=v_0$ on a smooth initial curve, the tangential derivative vanishes and the two allowed normal derivatives are $\partial_nv=\pm1$. Locally the two solutions are $v=v_0\pm d$, where $d$ is oriented [distance](distance.md) along the normal rays; hence $u=(v_0\pm d)^2$ wherever the chosen $v$ stays positive and the normal-coordinate map remains invertible.

There are two separate obstructions to using this initial-data chart. A [characteristic caustic](characteristic-caustic.md) occurs when normal rays focus and the projection [Jacobian determinant](jacobian-determinant.md) vanishes. Alternatively $v$ can reach zero while the geometric rays remain separated: the square-root transformation then ceases to apply and the original [Charpit's equations](charpit-s-equations.md) have a degenerate zero-gradient state. A continuation of $u$ beyond either obstruction needs separate justification; squaring an algebraic expression alone does not preserve a prescribed positive square-root branch.
