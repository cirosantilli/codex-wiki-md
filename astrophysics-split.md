# Astrophysics

↑ **Parent:** [Branches of physics](branches-of-physics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics)

Astrophysics uses [physics](physics-split.md) to explain [stars](star.md), [planets](planet.md), [galaxies](galaxy-split.md), and their [electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation-split.md); [cosmology](cosmology-split.md) extends the questions to the universe as a whole. Much of its evidence arrives as [light](light.md). The challenge is to infer the source's physical properties from what reaches an observer: a bright [star](star.md) might be nearby, unusually luminous, or both. [Stellar parallax](stellar-parallax.md) and [observed radiative flux](observed-radiative-flux.md) make this distinction quantitative.

This is an [inverse problem](inverse-problem-split.md). A [forward problem](forward-problem.md) predicts observations from a source model and the intervening [radiative transfer](radiative-transfer.md); the [inverse problem](inverse-problem-split.md) works backwards from observations to possible models. A [spectral line](spectral-line.md) can constrain [chemical elements](chemical-element.md), [temperature](temperature-split.md), and motion through the [Doppler effect](doppler-effect.md), but its strength also depends on the [stellar atmosphere](stellar-atmosphere.md). Combining different measurements is therefore more informative than interpreting brightness alone. The attraction is that a tiny change in the direction of [light](light.md) can supply a distance, turning received [radiative flux](radiative-flux.md) into intrinsic [luminosity](luminosity.md). [ESA: Parallax, spectra, and physical properties](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Gaia/How_does_Gaia_study_the_Milky_Way).
