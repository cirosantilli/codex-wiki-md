# Viscous force

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous stress tensor](viscous-stress-tensor.md)

The viscous force on a fluid region is the resultant of the [traction](traction.md) supplied by the [viscous stress tensor](viscous-stress-tensor.md) $\boldsymbol\tau$. With $\mathbf n$ the outward [unit normal](unit-normal.md) of the region, its surface contribution is the displayed [surface integral](surface-integral.md). For an incompressible [Newtonian fluid](newtonian-fluid.md), $\boldsymbol\tau=\eta[\nabla\mathbf u+(\nabla\mathbf u)^T]$, where $\eta$ is [dynamic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity-split.md) and $\mathbf u$ is [velocity](velocity.md). A force scale is $\eta UL$ for a body of size $L$ moving at speed $U$, while the inertial force scale is $\rho U^2L^2$; their ratio is the [Reynolds number](reynolds-number.md). Total hydrodynamic [drag](drag-physics.md) on a body includes both viscous traction and the pressure traction induced by the flow. For example, the full [Stokes drag law](stokes-s-law.md) gives $6\pi\eta RU$ for a no-slip sphere, rather than just the viscous-traction part alone.
