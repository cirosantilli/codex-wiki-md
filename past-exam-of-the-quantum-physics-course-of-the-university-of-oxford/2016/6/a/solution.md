<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For two [spin one-half](../../../../spin-one-half.md) particles, $s_1^2=s_2^2=3\hbar^2/4$. Squaring $\mathbf S=\mathbf s_1+\mathbf s_2$ gives

$$
\boxed{f=a+\frac b2\left(S^2-\frac32\hbar^2\right).}
$$

Take $a,b$ real so that $f$ is [Hermitian](../../../../hermitian-operator.md). Since $[S^2,S_z]=0$, the displayed [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) implies $[f,S^2]=[f,S_z]=0$. There is a common [orthonormal](../../../../orthonormal-set.md) [eigenbasis](../../../../eigenbasis.md), so all three observables can be simultaneously certain **in a common [eigenstate](../../../../eigenstate.md)**. Commutation does not mean that every [quantum superposition](../../../../quantum-superposition.md) has definite values of all three.
