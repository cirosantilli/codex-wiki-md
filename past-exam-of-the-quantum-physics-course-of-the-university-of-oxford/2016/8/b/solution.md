<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [spherical harmonics](../../../../spherical-harmonic.md) satisfy

$$
L^2Y_l^m=\hbar^2l(l+1)Y_l^m,\qquad L_zY_l^m=\hbar mY_l^m,
\quad l=0,1,\ldots,\quad -l\le m\le l.
$$

Commuting $L^2$ or $L_z$ past $L_\pm$ therefore gives

$$
\boxed{L^2(L_\pm Y_l^m)=\hbar^2l(l+1)(L_\pm Y_l^m),\qquad L_z(L_\pm Y_l^m)=\hbar(m\pm1)(L_\pm Y_l^m).}
$$

For a nonzero result these are the [eigenvalue equations](../../../../eigenvalue-equation.md) with unchanged $l$ and shifted [magnetic quantum number](../../../../magnetic-quantum-number.md). Taking the squared [norm](../../../../norm.md), using $L_\mp L_\pm=L^2-L_z^2\mp\hbar L_z$, fixes the conventional coefficient:

$$
L_\pm Y_l^m=\hbar\sqrt{l(l+1)-m(m\pm1)}\,Y_l^{m\pm1}.
$$

At the appropriate endpoint the coefficient is zero. The zero [vector](../../../../vector.md) is not itself an [eigenstate](../../../../eigenstate.md).
