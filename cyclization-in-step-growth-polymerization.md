# Cyclization in step-growth polymerization

↑ **Parent:** [Step-growth polymerization](step-growth-polymerization.md)

In [cyclization in step-growth polymerization](cyclization-in-step-growth-polymerization.md), compatible [functional groups](functional-group.md) on the same chain react and close it into a ring. They consume groups without merging two [molecules](molecule.md). If $R$ ring-closing events and $J$ intermolecular joins occur in an otherwise balanced bifunctional feed, the group conversion is $p=(J+R)/N_0$, while $N=N_0-J=N_0(1-p)+R$. Thus

$$
X_n=\frac{N_0}{N_0(1-p)+R}.
$$

For example, an $AB$ dimer can close its remaining $A$ and $B$ ends: two group pairings have occurred among two starting [monomer molecules](monomer-molecule.md), giving $p=1$, but the resulting single cyclic [molecule](molecule.md) has only two [monomeric units](monomeric-unit.md). This counterexample shows exactly where the linear [Carothers equation](carothers-equation.md) fails. Rings have no chain ends, so counting every chain-bearing [molecule](molecule.md) as two ends also fails.
