<h3 id="a/1">1</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/1/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

Use [International System of Units](../physics.md#international-system-of-units), with $c^{-2}=\mu_0\varepsilon_0$. The [Maxwell equations](../maxwell-equations.md) with [charge density](../electric-charge.md#charge-density) $\rho$ and [current density](../electric-charge.md#current-density) $\mathbf J$ are

$$
\boxed{\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\rho/\varepsilon_0,\qquad\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,\qquad\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\partial_t\mathbf B,\qquad\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf J+c^{-2}\partial_t\mathbf E.}
$$

Locally, or in a suitable contractible region, the zero [divergence](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence) of $\mathbf B$ allows a [magnetic vector potential](../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-vector-potential) $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$. [Faraday's law](../maxwell-equations.md#faraday-s-law-of-induction) then makes $\mathbf E+\partial_t\mathbf A$ [curl](../multivariable-calculus.md#curl)-free, so a [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) can be chosen with

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E=-\nabla\Phi-\partial_t\mathbf A,\qquad\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A.}
$$

These formulas automatically satisfy the two homogeneous [Maxwell equations](../maxwell-equations.md). The local topology assumption rules out obstructions to expressing a closed field as a [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) or [curl](../multivariable-calculus.md#curl).

For any sufficiently smooth scalar $\chi$, the [gauge transformation](../electromagnetism.md#gauge-transformation)

$$
\boxed{\mathbf A\mapsto\mathbf A+\nabla\chi,\qquad\Phi\mapsto\Phi-\partial_t\chi}
$$

leaves both physical fields unchanged, because [curl](../multivariable-calculus.md#curl) of a [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) is zero and spatial and time [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) commute. In such a region this accounts for the freedom between potentials for the same fields.

The [Lorenz gauge](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lorenz-gauge-condition) is $\nabla\cdot\mathbf A+c^{-2}\partial_t\Phi=0$. Substitution into the sourced [Maxwell equations](../maxwell-equations.md), using $\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf A)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)-\nabla^2\mathbf A$, gives the decoupled [wave equations](../wave-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{(\nabla^2-c^{-2}\partial_t^2)\Phi=-\rho/\varepsilon_0,\qquad(\nabla^2-c^{-2}\partial_t^2)\mathbf A=-\mu_0\mathbf J.}
$$

For example, Gauss's law gives $-\nabla^2\Phi-\partial_t\nabla\cdot\mathbf A=\rho/\varepsilon_0$; the gauge condition turns this into the scalar equation. The remaining gauge freedom preserving the condition has $(\nabla^2-c^{-2}\partial_t^2)\chi=0$.

Put $\theta=\omega t-Kz$ for the given wave. Direct differentiation gives $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=0$ and $\nabla\times\mathbf E=K\mathbf E$. Integrating [Faraday's law](../maxwell-equations.md#faraday-s-law-of-induction) in time yields the wave's [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md)

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B=\frac K\omega\,\widehat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf E=\frac{KE}{\omega}(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta,0).}
$$

One could superpose a time-independent [divergence](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence)-free and [curl](../multivariable-calculus.md#curl)-free [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md); the expression displayed chooses no such extra background. It has zero [divergence](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence) and $\partial_t\mathbf B=-K\mathbf E$, so the magnetic Gauss and Faraday equations hold. Moreover

$$
\nabla\times\mathbf B=\frac{K^2}{\omega^2}\partial_t\mathbf E.
$$

Since the time [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) is nonzero, the vacuum [Ampere-Maxwell law](../maxwell-equations.md#ampere-s-circuital-law) holds precisely when

$$
\boxed{K^2=\omega^2/c^2.}
$$

This verifies all four source-free equations, including both [divergence](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence) constraints. The sign of $\omega/K$ selects the direction of propagation.

A wave has [circular polarization](../electromagnetic-wave.md#circular-polarization) when, at a fixed spatial point, its transverse electric [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) has constant nonzero magnitude and rotates uniformly through a circle. Here $E_x^2+E_y^2=E^2$ and the angle changes at rate $\omega$, so **the wave is circularly polarized**. Its magnetic [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) is perpendicular to the electric [vector](../vector-space.md#vector), has magnitude $|E|/c$ under the [dispersion relation](../dispersion-relation.md), and rotates with it. A handedness label additionally requires a convention for the observer's viewing direction.

The [Lorenz-gauge potentials for a circularly polarized plane wave](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lorenz-gauge-potentials-for-a-circularly-polarized-plane-wave) give a convenient choice. Take $\Phi=0$ and $\mathbf A=\omega^{-2}\partial_t\mathbf E$. Because $\partial_t^2\mathbf E=-\omega^2\mathbf E$,

$$
-\partial_t\mathbf A=\mathbf E,\qquad
\nabla\times\mathbf A=\omega^{-2}\partial_t(K\mathbf E)=\frac K\omega\widehat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf E=\mathbf B.
$$

Also $\nabla\cdot\mathbf A=\omega^{-2}\partial_t\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=0$. Together with $\partial_t\Phi=0$, this proves **these potentials satisfy the [Lorenz gauge](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lorenz-gauge-condition)**. They satisfy the source-free potential [wave equations](../wave-equation.md) because every component has the same [dispersion relation](../dispersion-relation.md) just obtained.

<h3 id="a/2">2</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/2/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

Take $m,\omega>0$ and use the usual common invariant domain for the [position operator](../quantum-mechanics.md#position-operator), [momentum operator](../quantum-mechanics.md#momentum-operator) and their products. Since $X$ and $P$ are [self-adjoint](../linear-operator-theory.md#self-adjoint-operator),

$$
\boxed{a_+=\frac{P+im\omega X}{\sqrt{2m\omega\hbar}}.}
$$

Expanding the [commutator](../lie-algebra.md#commutator) and using $[P,X]=-i\hbar I$ gives

$$
[a_-,a_+]=\frac{im\omega[P,X]-im\omega[X,P]}{2m\omega\hbar}=I.
$$

The two products are

$$
a_+a_-=\frac{P^2+m^2\omega^2X^2-m\omega\hbar}{2m\omega\hbar},\qquad
a_-a_+=\frac{P^2+m^2\omega^2X^2+m\omega\hbar}{2m\omega\hbar}.
$$

Consequently the [quantum harmonic oscillator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md) [Hamiltonian operator](../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics) is

$$
\boxed{H=\hbar\omega(a_+a_-+\tfrac12 I)=\hbar\omega(a_-a_+-\tfrac12 I).}
$$

The identity $[AB,C]=A[B,C]+[A,C]B$ now gives

$$
\boxed{[H,a_-]=-\hbar\omega a_-,\qquad[H,a_+]=\hbar\omega a_+.}
$$

Thus $H(a_\pm\psi)=(E\pm\hbar\omega)a_\pm\psi$. **Each ladder image is an [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) if it is nonzero**; a zero [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) is not an [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate). The distinction is needed for lowering the [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state). Their squared [norms](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) are

$$
\boxed{\|a_-\psi\|^2=\left(\frac E{\hbar\omega}-\frac12\right)\|\psi\|^2,\qquad
\|a_+\psi\|^2=\left(\frac E{\hbar\omega}+\frac12\right)\|\psi\|^2.}
$$

These follow by moving the operator to the other side of the [inner product](../inner-product.md) with its [adjoint operator](../hilbert-space.md#adjoint-operator). The first inequality implies $E\geq\hbar\omega/2$ for every [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate).

For a lowest-energy [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state), $a_-\psi_0$ must vanish, since otherwise it would be an [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) with lower energy. The [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) formula then forces $E_0=\hbar\omega/2$. Raising never vanishes and produces

$$
\boxed{E_n=\hbar\omega(n+\tfrac12),\qquad\psi_n=(a_+)^n\psi_0,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.}
$$

To see that there are no other [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue), repeatedly lower any [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate). If $E/(\hbar\omega)-1/2$ were not a nonnegative integer, there would be a final nonzero lowered [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) with energy strictly between $\hbar\omega/2$ and $3\hbar\omega/2$. Its lower image would have positive [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) but energy below the lower bound, a contradiction. Thus every [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) reaches the ground eigenspace after an integer number of lowerings. On a positive level, lowering is injective by its strictly positive [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) factor. The assumed one-dimensional ground eigenspace therefore makes every eigenspace one-dimensional, so the displayed raised [quantum states](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) give all [eigenstates](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) up to nonzero multiples. If $\psi_0$ is normalized, their [norms](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) are $\|\psi_n\|^2=n!$.

For $k\geq1$, $(a_+)^k\psi$ has [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $E+k\hbar\omega$, distinct from $E$. [eigenstates](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) of a [self-adjoint operator](../linear-operator-theory.md#self-adjoint-operator) with distinct [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) are [orthogonal](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors): applying self-adjointness to their [inner product](../inner-product.md) gives $(E'-E)\langle\psi,\phi\rangle=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\langle\psi,(a_+)^k\psi\rangle=0.}
$$

The [expectation](../expected-value.md) of $(a_-)^k$ also vanishes, either by lowering and [orthogonality](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) or by conjugating the adjoint relation.

Solving the ladder definitions for $X$ and $P$ gives

$$
T=\frac{\hbar\omega}{4}(a_-+a_+)^2,\qquad V=-\frac{\hbar\omega}{4}(a_+-a_-)^2,
$$

so $T-V=\frac12\hbar\omega((a_+)^2+(a_-)^2)$. Both terms have zero [expectation](../expected-value.md) by the preceding [orthogonality](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors). Since $T+V=H$, the oscillator form of the [quantum virial theorem](../quantum-mechanics.md#quantum-virial-theorem) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E_\psi(T)=\mathbb E_\psi(V)=E/2.}
$$

Expectations here are divided by $\|\psi\|^2$ if the [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) is not normalized; no [normalization](../mathematics.md#normalization) assumption was used in the ladder argument.

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/3/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

For a nonzero [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) $\psi$ and a [self-adjoint operator](../linear-operator-theory.md#self-adjoint-operator) $A$, the [expectation value](../quantum-system.md#expectation-value) and [standard deviation](../variance.md#standard-deviation) of its measurement are

$$
\boxed{a=\mathbb E_\psi(A)=\frac{\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi,\psi\rangle},\qquad
\Delta_\psi(A)=\frac{\|(A-aI)\psi\|}{\|\psi\|}=\sqrt{\mathbb E_\psi(A^2)-a^2}.}
$$

The squared deviation is also called the [variance](../variance.md) of the [observable](../quantum-system.md#observable). All displayed products are understood on [quantum states](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) for which the relevant moments exist. Self-adjointness gives $\overline{\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle}=\langle A\psi,\psi\rangle=\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle$, so $a$ is real. Expanding the squared [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) gives the [variance](../variance.md) expression. A [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) is nonnegative and is zero exactly for the zero [vector](../vector-space.md#vector), hence $\Delta_\psi(A)=0$ precisely when $A\psi=a\psi$. Thus **zero dispersion is equivalent to being an [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate)**.

For bounded $H$, the [operator exponential](../linear-operator-theory.md#operator-exponential) is the [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm)-convergent power series

$$
U_t=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-itH/\hbar)^n}{n!}.
$$

For a general unbounded [self-adjoint](../linear-operator-theory.md#self-adjoint-operator) [Hamiltonian operator](../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics), the [spectral theorem](../hilbert-space.md#spectral-theorem) defines the same notation by

$$
U_t=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-itE/\hbar}\,dP_H(E).
$$

This definition does not require the power series to converge on every [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state). [complex conjugation](../complex-analysis.md#complex-conjugation) of the spectral multiplier gives $U_t^*=U_{-t}$, and multiplication of the phases gives $U_{-t}U_t=I$. Thus **$U_t$ is [unitary](../vector-bundle.md#unitary-connection)**, preserves the [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm), and has $U_0=I$.

For initial data in the domain of $H$, differentiating the spectral expression yields $\partial_tU_t\psi_0=-(i/\hbar)HU_t\psi_0$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\psi_t=U_t\psi_0}
$$

solves the [Schrödinger equation](../mathematical-physics.md#schrodinger-equation) with the prescribed initial [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state). Uniqueness follows by differentiating $U_{-t}\psi_t$: the two terms cancel, leaving the constant $\psi_0$.

In the two-dimensional energy [basis](../vector-space.md#basis), expand the initial [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) as $\psi_0=c_1\Psi_1+c_2\Psi_2$. Each energy [eigenvector](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector) receives its own phase under the [operator exponential](../linear-operator-theory.md#operator-exponential), giving

$$
\boxed{\psi_t=c_1e^{-itE_1/\hbar}\Psi_1+c_2e^{-itE_2/\hbar}\Psi_2.}
$$

This is the general solution because every initial [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) has a unique [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) expansion.

In that [orthonormal basis](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-basis) the other [observable](../quantum-system.md#observable) has the real symmetric [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix)

$$
[A]=\begin{pmatrix}\cos2\alpha&\sin2\alpha\\\sin2\alpha&-\cos2\alpha\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its conjugate transpose equals itself, so it is [self-adjoint](../linear-operator-theory.md#self-adjoint-operator). Also $A^2=I$ and $\operatorname{tr}A=0$, giving [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $+1$ and $-1$. Direct substitution gives the normalized [orthogonal](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) [eigenvectors](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector)

$$
\boxed{v_+=\cos\alpha\,\Psi_1+\sin\alpha\,\Psi_2,\qquad
v_-=-\sin\alpha\,\Psi_1+\cos\alpha\,\Psi_2.}
$$

An irrelevant global phase may multiply either [vector](../vector-space.md#vector). With the chosen lower [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) initially,

$$
\boxed{\psi_t=-\sin\alpha\,e^{-itE_1/\hbar}\Psi_1+\cos\alpha\,e^{-itE_2/\hbar}\Psi_2.}
$$

The [Born rule](../wave-function.md#born-rule) says that the [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) for the higher [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) is $|\langle v_+,\psi_t\rangle|^2$. Its amplitude is $\sin\alpha\cos\alpha(e^{-itE_2/\hbar}-e^{-itE_1/\hbar})$. Using $|e^{-iu}-e^{-iv}|^2=4\sin^2((u-v)/2)$ gives

$$
\boxed{P_+(t)=\sin^2(2\alpha)\sin^2\left(\frac{(E_2-E_1)t}{2\hbar}\right).}
$$

This is the [transition probability for a rotated two-level observable](../quantum-system.md#transition-probability-for-a-rotated-two-level-observable). Its time dependence comes from the relative phase between two energy [eigenstates](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate). If their energies coincide, or if the measured [observable](../quantum-system.md#observable) has the energy [eigenvectors](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector) as its [eigenvectors](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector), this transition [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) is zero at all times.

<h3 id="b/4">4</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/4/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#b/4)

In the scalar two-particle model, the [fermionic exchange symmetry](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#fermi-dirac-statistics) and [bosonic exchange symmetry](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#bosonic-exchange-symmetry) conditions are

$$
\boxed{\Psi(x_2,x_1)=-\Psi(x_1,x_2)\text{ for fermions},\qquad
\Psi(x_2,x_1)=\Psi(x_1,x_2)\text{ for bosons}.}
$$

For particles with spin, exchange acts on all their labels; the stated coordinate-only model fixes the spatial symmetry sector. Define $S\Psi(x_1,x_2)=\Psi(x_2,x_1)$. Equal masses make the kinetic operator invariant under exchange, and the assumed potential is invariant too, so $HS=SH$. Therefore $H\Psi=E\Psi$ implies $H(S\Psi)=E(S\Psi)$. The components

$$
\Psi_{\mathrm B}=\tfrac12(\Psi+S\Psi),\qquad
\Psi_{\mathrm F}=\tfrac12(\Psi-S\Psi)
$$

are respectively symmetric and antisymmetric eigenfunctions whenever nonzero. Their sum is the original nonzero [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state), so at least one is nonzero. An eigenspace may contain both sectors if the energy is degenerate.

The [normal modes of two harmonically coupled identical particles](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#normal-modes-of-two-harmonically-coupled-identical-particles) provide the separation. The [coordinate transformation](../differential-geometry.md#smooth-transition-map) to $y_+,y_-$ is [orthogonal](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors), with absolute Jacobian one. Exchanging the particles leaves $y_+$ fixed and changes $y_-$ to $-y_-$. Hence **bosonic [quantum states](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) are even and fermionic [quantum states](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) odd in the relative coordinate**. The [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) gives

$$
\partial_{x_1}=\frac{\partial_{y_+}+\partial_{y_-}}{\sqrt2},\qquad
\partial_{x_2}=\frac{\partial_{y_+}-\partial_{y_-}}{\sqrt2},\qquad
\partial_{x_1}^2+\partial_{x_2}^2=\partial_{y_+}^2+\partial_{y_-}^2.
$$

Also $x_1^2+x_2^2=y_+^2+y_-^2$ and $(x_1-x_2)^2=2y_-^2$. Combining the two trapping potentials with the interaction therefore gives the separated [Hamiltonian operator](../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics)

$$
H=\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\partial_{y_+}^2+\frac12m\omega^2y_+^2\right]
+\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\partial_{y_-}^2+\frac12m\omega^2\cos^2\alpha\,y_-^2\right].
$$

These are independent oscillators of frequencies $\omega$ and $\omega\cos\alpha$. Both frequencies are positive in the stated range. Their product [eigenstates](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) have energies

$$
\boxed{E_{n_+,n_-}=\hbar\omega\left[n_++\tfrac12+(n_-+\tfrac12)\cos\alpha\right],\qquad n_+,n_-=0,1,2,\ldots.}
$$

The [parity](../quantum-mechanics.md#parity) of an oscillator level is $(-1)^n$. Consequently [bosons](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#boson) allow even $n_-$ and [fermions](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#fermion) odd $n_-$, while $n_+$ is unrestricted.

The bosonic [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) uses $n_+=n_-=0$, and the fermionic [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) uses $n_+=0,n_-=1$. The oscillator length in the relative mode is

$$
\boxed{\tau^2=\frac{\hbar}{m\omega\cos\alpha}=\frac{\hbar\sec\alpha}{m\omega}.}
$$

Using the normalized [Gaussian](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution) for each mode gives

$$
\boxed{\Phi_{\mathrm B}(y_+,y_-)=\psi_\sigma(y_+)\psi_\tau(y_-),\qquad
\Phi_{\mathrm F}(y_+,y_-)=\psi_\sigma(y_+)\phi_\tau(y_-).}
$$

Indeed $\phi_\tau(y)=\sqrt2\,y\psi_\tau(y)/\tau$ by differentiating the [Gaussian](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution), so it is the normalized odd first [excited state](../energy-eigenvalue.md#excited-state). The ground energies are $E_{\mathrm B}=\hbar\omega(1+\cos\alpha)/2$ and $E_{\mathrm F}=\hbar\omega(1+3\cos\alpha)/2$.

Choose normalized factors for a normalized product [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state); this can always be done by reciprocal rescaling of its factors. Since the coordinate Jacobian has absolute value one,

$$
\mathbb E[f(y_-)]=\iint f(y_-)\,|\psi(y_+)|^2|\phi(y_-)|^2\,dy_+dy_-
=\boxed{\int f(y)|\phi(y)|^2\,dy.}
$$

If the supplied factors are not individually normalized, the general expression has an additional factor $\|\psi\|^2$; choosing normalized factors is the convention in the displayed formula.

For the [ground states](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state), put $\rho(y)=|\psi_\tau(y)|^2=e^{-y^2/\tau^2}/(\sqrt\pi\tau)$. Then $|\phi_\tau(y)|^2=2y^2\rho(y)/\tau^2$ and $\rho'=-2y\rho/\tau^2$. Thus, for differentiable $f$ with suitable [Gaussian](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution) integrability and vanishing boundary terms,

$$
\mathbb E_{\mathrm F}[f]=\frac2{\tau^2}\int y^2f(y)\rho(y)\,dy
=-\int yf(y)\rho'(y)\,dy
=\boxed{\int\bigl(f(y)+yf'(y)\bigr)\rho(y)\,dy.}
$$

This is [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts), and every polynomial satisfies the required endpoint conditions. The [relative-coordinate moments in even and odd oscillator ground states](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#relative-coordinate-moments-in-even-and-odd-oscillator-ground-states) follow by substituting $f(y)=y^n$:

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E_{\mathrm F}[y_-^n]=(n+1)\mathbb E_{\mathrm B}[y_-^n].}
$$

For odd $n$, both expectations vanish by [parity](../quantum-mechanics.md#parity), so this is an equality of zeros rather than an increase in signed mean separation. The even moments show the physical difference. In particular $\mathbb E_{\mathrm B}[y_-^2]=\tau^2/2$ and $\mathbb E_{\mathrm F}[y_-^2]=3\tau^2/2$, so the fermionic root-mean-square particle separation is $\sqrt3$ times the bosonic value. The fermionic [probability density](../wave-function.md#probability-density) vanishes at coincidence $y_-=0$, expressing [Pauli exclusion principle](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#pauli-exclusion-principle) in this spatial symmetry sector. This is an exchange constraint on the [wave function](../wave-function.md), not an additional classical force. As $\alpha$ approaches $\pi/2$, the relative oscillator softens and its width diverges; the excluded endpoint would no longer have this normalizable oscillator [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state).

<h4 id="b/5/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

Use an expansion for the [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) continuing the nondegenerate level, with intermediate [normalization](../mathematics.md#normalization) $\langle\psi_0,\psi(\epsilon)\rangle=1$:

$$
E(\epsilon)=E_0+\epsilon E^{(1)}+\epsilon^2E^{(2)}+O(\epsilon^3),\qquad
\psi(\epsilon)=\psi_0+\epsilon\chi+\epsilon^2\eta+\cdots,\qquad\langle\psi_0,\chi\rangle=0.
$$

Insert these expressions into the [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) equation. At first order,

$$
(H_0-E_0)\chi=-(H'-E^{(1)})\psi_0.
$$

Taking the [inner product](../inner-product.md) with $\psi_0$ eliminates the left side by self-adjointness. Thus

$$
\boxed{E^{(1)}=\langle\psi_0,H'\psi_0\rangle,\qquad
E(\epsilon)=\langle\psi_0,H\psi_0\rangle+O(\epsilon^2).}
$$

For $j\ne0$, projection onto $\psi_j$ instead gives

$$
\langle\psi_j,\chi\rangle=\frac{\langle\psi_j,H'\psi_0\rangle}{E_0-E_j}.
$$

At second order, projecting the [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) equation onto $\psi_0$ gives $E^{(2)}=\langle\psi_0,H'\chi\rangle$. Expand $\chi$ in the orthonormal energy [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) and use self-adjointness of $H'$:

$$
\boxed{\epsilon^2E^{(2)}=\epsilon^2\sum_{j\ne0}\frac{|\langle\psi_j,H'\psi_0\rangle|^2}{E_0-E_j}.}
$$

Nondegeneracy ensures the denominators for this level do not vanish; convergence and validity of the expansion are among the assumed perturbation-theory hypotheses.

For the [cosine perturbation of the ground level in a one-dimensional box](../quantum-mechanics.md#cosine-perturbation-of-the-ground-level-in-a-one-dimensional-box), write $u=\pi x/a$. The product-to-sum identity gives

$$
\cos(2u)\sin u=\tfrac12\bigl(\sin(3u)-\sin u\bigr),
$$

so, with the given common [normalization](../mathematics.md#normalization) of the sine eigenfunctions,

$$
\boxed{V\phi_1=\frac\epsilon2(\phi_3-\phi_1).}
$$

The diagonal [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) element is $-\epsilon/2$, and the only nonzero off-diagonal [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) element out of the [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) is $\langle\phi_3,V\phi_1\rangle=\epsilon/2$. Let $E_{\mathrm{box}}=\pi^2\hbar^2/(2ma^2)$. Since $\mathcal E_3=9E_{\mathrm{box}}$, the ground energy is

$$
E_{\mathrm{ground}}=E_{\mathrm{box}}-\frac\epsilon2+\frac{\epsilon^2/4}{E_{\mathrm{box}}-9E_{\mathrm{box}}}+O(\epsilon^3)
=\boxed{\frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2}-\frac\epsilon2-\frac{ma^2\epsilon^2}{16\pi^2\hbar^2}+O(\epsilon^3).}
$$

The second-order term is negative, as expected for a nondegenerate ground level: every excited-[quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) denominator is negative. The first-order shift is also negative for positive $\epsilon$, because the ground-[quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) density is largest near the center, where this perturbing potential is negative.

<h3 id="b/6">6</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/6/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

Use the [Dirac notation](../quantum-system.md#bra-ket-notation) convention that the [inner product](../inner-product.md) is linear in the ket. Orthonormality of the computational [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) gives

$$
\langle k|f\rangle=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z_N}f(\ell)\langle k|\ell\rangle=f(k).
$$

The finite geometric sum satisfies $\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\lambda^{rk}=N$ if $r=0$ in $\mathbb Z_N$, and zero otherwise: in the second case it equals $(1-\lambda^{rN})/(1-\lambda^r)=0$. Therefore

$$
\langle\widehat m|\widehat n\rangle=\frac1N\sum_k\lambda^{(n-m)k}=\delta_{mn}.
$$

There are $N$ such orthonormal [vectors](../vector-space.md#vector) in an $N$-dimensional space, so they form a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis). The inverse expansion is verified by the same sum:

$$
\boxed{\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_j\lambda^{-jk}|\widehat j\rangle
=\frac1N\sum_{j,\ell}\lambda^{j(\ell-k)}|\ell\rangle=|k\rangle.}
$$

This is the [unitary](../vector-bundle.md#unitary-connection) [discrete Fourier transform](../discrete-fourier-transform.md) and its inverse.

For the [basis-copying isometry](../quantum-theory.md#basis-copying-isometry), the images $|k\rangle|k\rangle$ are orthonormal, so $C$ is a linear [isometry](../riemannian-geometry.md#isometry), with $C^*C=I$. Its [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) elements give

$$
\langle Ck|\,|f\rangle|g\rangle
=\langle k|f\rangle\langle k|g\rangle=f(k)g(k)=\langle k|f.g\rangle.
$$

Applying the definition of the [adjoint operator](../hilbert-space.md#adjoint-operator), the coefficient of $|k\rangle$ in $C^*|f\rangle|g\rangle$ is exactly this expression. Thus

$$
\boxed{C^*|f\rangle|g\rangle=|f.g\rangle.}
$$

Equivalently $C^*|k\rangle|\ell\rangle=\delta_{k\ell}|k\rangle$. The adjoint of [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) copying acts as pointwise multiplication of coefficient [functions](../function.md).

For the [Fourier transform of a basis-copying isometry](../quantum-theory.md#fourier-transform-of-a-basis-copying-isometry), the [normalization](../mathematics.md#normalization) matters:

$$
f_m(k)f_n(k)=\frac{\lambda^{(m+n)k}}N=\frac1{\sqrt N}f_{m+n}(k).
$$

Hence $C^*|\widehat m\rangle|\widehat n\rangle=N^{-1/2}|\widehat{m+n}\rangle$. Taking adjoint [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) elements, or expanding both output factors in the inverse Fourier [basis](../vector-space.md#basis), yields

$$
\boxed{C|\widehat j\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{m+n=j}|\widehat m\rangle|\widehat n\rangle.}
$$

**The printed formula is missing the factor $1/\sqrt N$.** There are $N$ mutually [orthogonal](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) terms in its sum, so without that factor its right side has [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) $\sqrt N$, whereas its left side has [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) one because $C$ is an [isometry](../riemannian-geometry.md#isometry). The formula above is the normalized identity consistent with all the preceding definitions. For $N=1$ the distinction disappears, but the no-cloning assertion is substantive for $N\geq2$.

The normalized output is not $|\widehat j\rangle|\widehat j\rangle$. Its coefficient [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) pairs every Fourier label $m$ with $j-m$; tracing out either factor gives $I/N$. Thus it is a [maximally entangled state](../quantum-theory.md#maximally-entangled-state), while two clones of a pure Fourier [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) would have pure reduced [quantum states](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state). A copier for the computational [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) therefore does not copy the Fourier [basis](../vector-space.md#basis).

More generally, suppose a linear [isometry](../riemannian-geometry.md#isometry) with a fixed blank input could clone every normalized [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state). Its restriction to $|\psi\rangle|\mathrm{blank}\rangle$ would define a linear map $D$ with $D|k\rangle=|k\rangle|k\rangle$ on every computational [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state). Linearity forces $D=C$, which has just been shown to fail on Fourier [quantum states](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state). This proves the [no-cloning theorem](../quantum-theory.md#no-cloning-theorem) in the pure-[quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) isometric setting.

The inner-product proof makes the obstruction independent of the chosen [basis](../vector-space.md#basis). If an [isometry](../riemannian-geometry.md#isometry) cloned two [quantum states](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) with overlap $s=\langle\psi|\phi\rangle$, preservation of inner products would give $s=s^2$, or, allowing irrelevant output phases, $|s|=|s|^2$. Distinct nonorthogonal rays have $0<|s|<1$, which is impossible. Even allowing an output environment does not help: [unitary](../vector-bundle.md#unitary-connection) evolution would give $|s|=|s|^2|\langle e_\psi|e_\phi\rangle|\leq|s|^2$, the same contradiction. **Arbitrary unknown [quantum states](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) cannot be cloned**, although any fixed [orthonormal basis](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-basis) can be copied and a one-dimensional [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) space is trivial.
