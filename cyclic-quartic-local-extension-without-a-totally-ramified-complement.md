# Cyclic quartic local extension without a totally ramified complement

↑ **Parent:** [Unramified base change preserves total ramification](unramified-base-change-preserves-total-ramification.md)

Let $K=\mathbb Q_3$, $L=K(i)$ with $i^2=-1$, and $\alpha^2=3(1+i)$. The extension $L/K$ is [unramified](unramified-extension.md) quadratic, whereas $N=L(\alpha)$ is [totally ramified](totally-ramified-extension.md) quadratic over $L$, by the [Eisenstein polynomial](eisenstein-polynomial.md) $T^2-3(1+i)$. Also $i=\alpha^2/3-1$, so $N=K(\alpha)$. The [Hensel lemma](hensel-s-lemma.md) gives $r\in K$ with $r^2=-2$. Put $\beta=(1+i)/r$; then $\beta^2=-i$ and $\beta\overline\beta=-1$. Consequently $i\mapsto-i$, $\alpha\mapsto\beta\alpha$ defines an automorphism whose square fixes $i$ and negates $\alpha$. It generates the cyclic [Galois group](galois-group.md) of order four. Its unique quadratic subfield is $L$, which is unramified. A decomposition as an unramified field and a totally ramified field would require a totally ramified quadratic subfield, and therefore cannot exist.
