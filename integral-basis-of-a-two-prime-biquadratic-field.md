# Integral basis of a two-prime biquadratic field

↑ **Parent:** [Integral basis](integral-basis.md)

For an odd prime $p\equiv3\pmod4$, traces to the three quadratic subfields show that an integer has coefficients $(a+b\sqrt p+c\sqrt2+d\sqrt{2p})/2$ with rational integers $a,b,c,d$. Its norm to $\mathbb Q(\sqrt p)$ forces $a,b$ even and $c\equiv d\pmod2$. Hence an [integral basis](integral-basis.md) is $1,\sqrt2,\sqrt p,(1+\sqrt p)\sqrt2/2$. The last generator satisfies the monic [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) $X^4-(p+1)X^2+(p-1)^2/4$, proving integrality as well as spanning. The subfield used for the norm matters: the norm to $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ has different coefficients.
