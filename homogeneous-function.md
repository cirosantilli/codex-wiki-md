# Homogeneous function

↑ **Parent:** [Real analysis](real-analysis-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_function)

A [function](function-split.md) is homogeneous of degree $k$ when rescaling its arguments by a common [scalar](scalar.md) $\lambda$ rescales its value by $\lambda^k$, whenever the arguments and that power are defined. For integer $k$ over a [vector space](vector-space-split.md), one can require every nonzero scalar; a [homogeneous polynomial](homogeneous-polynomial.md) gives an example. On a domain stable under positive real rescaling, the positive-scaling version allows real $k$. Degree-one [positive homogeneity](positively-homogeneous-function-degree-one.md) is the convention used for [norms](norm.md) and many [convex functions](convex-function.md). The [Euler theorem for homogeneous functions](euler-theorem-for-homogeneous-functions.md) turns this scaling law into a differential identity for a [differentiable function](differentiable-function.md).
