<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [cosmological constant](../../../../../cosmological-constant.md) has fixed density and [equation of state](../../../../../equation-of-state.md) $w=p/(\rho c^2)=-1$. A separately conserved dynamical component obeys

$$
\rho_{\rm de}(z)=\rho_{{\rm de},0}\exp\!\left[3\int_0^z\frac{1+w(z')}{1+z'}\,dz'\right].
$$

If $w$ departs from $-1$, its history changes the [Hubble parameter](../../../../../hubble-parameter.md), [luminosity distances](../../../../../luminosity-distance.md) and angular-diameter distances. Type Ia supernovae probe a relative distance-redshift relation, [baryon acoustic oscillations](../../../../../baryon-acoustic-oscillation.md) provide standard-ruler distances and expansion information, and the [cosmic microwave background](../../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-split.md) fixes early-time scales and geometric combinations. Combining these breaks some degeneracies with [matter density](../../../../../matter-density.md), [curvature](../../../../../curvature.md) and [calibration](../../../../../calibration-differential-geometry.md). Distance observables integrate the expansion history, so they smooth rapid variations in $w$ and cannot determine an arbitrary function exactly.

Structure-growth measurements add a dynamical test. Galaxy clustering, [redshift-space distortions](../../../../../redshift-space-distortions.md) and [weak gravitational lensing](../../../../../weak-gravitational-lensing.md) constrain the growth of matter perturbations and metric potentials. The late [Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect](../../../../../integrated-sachs-wolfe-effect.md) probes potential evolution through CMB-matter correlations. A scalar-field dark-energy model can have perturbations and an effective [sound speed](../../../../../speed-of-sound.md) different from other models with a similar background expansion. Comparisons of geometry and growth can therefore distinguish some models and test gravity assumptions.

**A statistically significant, robust departure from $w=-1$ or from constant dark-energy density would disfavor a pure [cosmological constant](../../../../../cosmological-constant.md) within the stated framework.** Consistency with $w=-1$ over a finite redshift range does not prove exact constancy: a slowly rolling field can mimic it. Supernova [calibration](../../../../../calibration-differential-geometry.md)/evolution, [galaxy bias](../../../../../galaxy-bias.md), [photometric redshift](../../../../../photometric-redshift.md) errors, [intrinsic galaxy alignments](../../../../../intrinsic-galaxy-alignment.md) and foregrounds must be controlled; modified gravity can also change growth. Parameterizations such as $w(a)=w_0+w_a(1-a)$ are useful finite-dimensional tests rather than a complete classification of all dark-energy histories.
