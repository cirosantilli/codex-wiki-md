<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The baryon excess requires a mechanism that produces different net [baryon numbers](../../../../../baryon-number.md) from an initially symmetric ensemble. In the usual local, CPT-invariant framework, the [Sakharov conditions](../../../../../sakharov-conditions.md) are baryon-number violation, C and [CP violation](../../../../../cp-violation.md), and departure from [thermal equilibrium](../../../../../thermal-equilibrium-split.md). Baryon conservation would preserve zero net [baryon number](../../../../../baryon-number.md); exact conjugation symmetries equate the conjugate production processes; equilibrium inverse reactions and [detailed balance](../../../../../detailed-balance.md) erase a would-be asymmetry. These necessary conditions do not determine its magnitude.[1967 original statement of the conditions](../../../../../violation-of-cp-invariance-c-asymmetry-and-baryon-asymmetry-of-the-universe-by-andrei-sakharov.md)

For example, let equal numbers of a heavy particle $X$ and its [antiparticle](../../../../../antiparticle.md) decay into channels $i$ with [baryon numbers](../../../../../baryon-number.md) $B_i$. Their mean net yield per pair is

$$
\epsilon_{\rm pair}=\sum_i B_i\left[\operatorname{Br}(X\to i)-\operatorname{Br}(\bar X\to\bar i)\right].
$$

It can be nonzero even though CPT requires equal total widths: the [branching fractions](../../../../../branching-fraction.md) differ among channels carrying different [baryon numbers](../../../../../baryon-number.md). A nonzero decay-rate asymmetry requires interference with both a CP-odd [weak phase](../../../../../weak-phase-cp-violation.md) and an [absorptive phase](../../../../../absorptive-phase-of-a-decay-amplitude.md). If the expansion rate overtakes the inverse-reaction rate, decays can occur out of equilibrium; later baryon-violating washout must then be weak enough for the asymmetry to survive.

In electroweak [baryogenesis](../../../../../baryogenesis.md), CP-asymmetric transport near a first-order phase-transition boundary can bias baryon-violating [sphaleron](../../../../../sphaleron.md) transitions. [CP violation](../../../../../cp-violation.md) alone is insufficient: the transition and washout dynamics also matter. In [leptogenesis](../../../../../leptogenesis.md), out-of-equilibrium decays of heavy [Majorana neutrinos](../../../../../majorana-neutrino.md) can generate [lepton](../../../../../lepton.md) asymmetry, part of which [sphalerons](../../../../../sphaleron.md) convert into baryon asymmetry while conserving $B-L$. The CKM phase demonstrates [CP violation](../../../../../cp-violation.md) in the [Standard Model](../../../../../standard-model-split.md) but does not by itself supply a successful cosmological mechanism. **The key quantity is a surviving net charge yield, not merely the existence of a CP-violating reaction.**
