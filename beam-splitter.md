# Beam splitter

↑ **Parent:** [Optical instrument](optical-instrument-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beam_splitter)

A [beam splitter](beam-splitter.md) is an [optical instrument](optical-instrument-split.md) that routes an incident beam of [light](light.md) into transmitted and reflected beams. It also combines incident [complex amplitudes](complex-amplitude.md): two inputs can contribute to the same output. A common geometry has four [optical ports](optical-port.md), with two chosen as inputs and two as outputs. A [beam splitter](beam-splitter.md) changes the field; a [photodetector](photodetector.md) supplies a measurement.[Photon quantum mechanics and beam splitters, §IV, printed p. 262, figure 4: two input and two output ports](photon-quantum-mechanics-and-beam-splitters-by-charles-h-holbrow-and-others.md)

For one illuminated input of a passive [beam splitter](beam-splitter.md), at specified [wavelength](wavelength.md), [angle of incidence](angle-of-incidence.md) and [electromagnetic polarization](polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave.md), define power [transmittance](transmittance.md) $T=P_{\rm transmitted}/P_{\rm in}$, [reflectance](reflectance.md) $R=P_{\rm reflected}/P_{\rm in}$ and [radiometric absorptance](radiometric-absorptance.md) $A=P_{\rm absorbed}/P_{\rm in}$. In steady operation, with no gain or other source, no net stored [energy](energy-split.md) change, and all escaping [light](light.md) included in the transmitted or reflected collection,

$$
T+R+A=1.
$$

This is [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) divided by incident [power](power.md). If $T$ and $R$ count only the intended output beams, include a separate scattered or otherwise uncollected fraction $L$: then $T+R+A+L=1$. Missing output [power](power.md) need not be [wave absorption](wave-absorption.md). For an original specified example, $P_{\rm in}=10\,\mathrm{mW}$, $T=0.45$, $R=0.50$ and $A=0.05$ give $4.5\,\mathrm{mW}$ transmitted, $5.0\,\mathrm{mW}$ reflected and $0.5\,\mathrm{mW}$ absorbed; these are assumed values, not measurements.

A 50:50 [beam splitter](beam-splitter.md) specifies equal output [powers](power.md) for one input, not equal [complex amplitudes](complex-amplitude.md) without a relative [wave phase](phase-waves.md). Recombination needs the [beam splitter scattering matrix](beam-splitter-scattering-matrix.md), including its [wave phases](phase-waves.md). A [nonpolarizing beam splitter](nonpolarizing-beam-splitter.md) approximately divides both orthogonal [linear polarizations](linear-polarization.md) in the same power ratio over its specified operating range; a [polarizing beam splitter](polarizing-beam-splitter.md) deliberately routes the two differently.

![](beam-splitter-four-port-interferometer-geometry.png)

**[Figure 4](#image-four-optical-ports-of-a-beam-splitter-and-a-michelson-interferometer). Four optical ports of a beam splitter and a Michelson interferometer**. Original schematic. Left: the transmitted paths are $a_1\to b_1$ and $a_2\to b_2$; the reflected paths exchange direction. This ordering defines the [beam splitter scattering matrix](beam-splitter-scattering-matrix.md) below. Right: one source is split into two mirror arms and the returning fields meet the same [beam splitter](beam-splitter.md) again; the [photodetector](photodetector.md) supplies the readout. Separate arrows show outward and returning paths, not separate physical beams. The schematic omits alignment steering mirrors and is not to scale.

The useful surprise is that the same component divides [power](power.md) with one input and steers [power](power.md) by [wave interference](interference-wave-propagation.md) with two inputs. In a [Michelson interferometer](michelson-interferometer.md) or [Mach-Zehnder interferometer](mach-zehnder-interferometer.md), mirror motion changes relative [wave phase](phase-waves.md), turning a small change in [optical path length](optical-path-length.md) into a measurable change in output [power](power.md).
