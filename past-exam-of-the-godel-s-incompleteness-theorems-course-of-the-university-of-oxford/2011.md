## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Write $BA=P(\ulcorner A\urcorner)$, with the corner notation understood as the numeral for the [Gödel number](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-number). An [arithmetical provability predicate](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-provability-predicate) numerates the theorems of $S$ in the standard natural numbers and satisfies the [Hilbert-Bernays-Löb derivability conditions](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions):

$$
\begin{aligned}
& S\vdash A\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\vdash BA,\qquad\text{an external rule;}\\
& S\vdash B(A\to C)\to(BA\to BC);\\
& S\vdash BA\to B(BA).
\end{aligned}
$$

The last two conditions are internal theorems, not rules authorizing reflection from $BA$ to $A$.

Here $P(x)=\exists y\,\operatorname{Prov}(x,y)$ numerates the theorem set by the given assumption. If $S\vdash A$, its proof supplies a genuine natural-number witness, making $BA$ a true [Sigma-1 formula](../computability-theory.md#sigma-1-formula). [Sigma-1 completeness](../computability-theory.md#sigma-1-completeness) gives $S\vdash BA$, proving the first condition. The second condition is supplied explicitly. Since $BA$ is itself a [Sigma-1 formula](../computability-theory.md#sigma-1-formula), the given formalized [Sigma-1 completeness](../computability-theory.md#sigma-1-completeness) yields $S\vdash BA\to B(BA)$, the third condition. Thus **the specified existential proof predicate is an [arithmetical provability predicate](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-provability-predicate)**.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Write $BA=\exists y\,\operatorname{Prov}(\ulcorner A\urcorner,y)$ and fix a refutable [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) $\bot$, so $S\vdash\neg\bot$ and $\operatorname{Con}_S=\neg B\bot$. First prove the internal equivalence

$$
\boxed{S\vdash G\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Con}_S.}
$$

Because $\bot\to G$ is provable, the [derivability conditions](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions) give $B\bot\to BG$. The fixed-point equivalence $G\leftrightarrow\neg BG$ therefore yields $G\to\neg B\bot$.

For the reverse implication, necessitating $G\to\neg BG$ and applying distribution gives $BG\to B(\neg BG)$. [positive introspection](../mathematical-logic.md#positive-introspection) gives $BG\to B(BG)$. Finally the provable implication $BG\to(\neg BG\to\bot)$, necessitated and distributed, gives

$$
B(BG)\to\bigl(B(\neg BG)\to B\bot\bigr).
$$

Consequently $S\vdash BG\to B\bot$, so $\neg B\bot\to\neg BG\to G$. The same argument works with any chosen refutable [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) in place of $\bot$; their formal consistency statements are provably equivalent.

By the classical [deduction theorem for propositional logic](../mathematical-logic.md#deduction-theorem-for-propositional-logic) applied to adjoining a [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence), $S+\{G\}$ is consistent exactly when $S\nvdash\neg G$. The displayed equivalence makes $S+\{G\}$ and $S+\{\operatorname{Con}_S\}$ deductively equivalent. Therefore

$$
\boxed{S\nvdash\neg G\iff S+\{\operatorname{Con}_S\}\text{ is consistent}.}
$$

No soundness assumption on that extension is being substituted for consistency.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

If $S$ is inconsistent, it proves $\neg R$ by [explosion](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-falsity-elimination-rule). Conversely, suppose $S$ is consistent and, towards a contradiction, has a proof of $\neg R$ with standard code $n$. Consistency excludes any proof of $R$. In particular, for each $k\leq n$, the closed bounded statement $\neg\operatorname{Prov}(\ulcorner R\urcorner,\overline k)$ is true and is provable in $S$ by [Sigma-1 completeness](../computability-theory.md#sigma-1-completeness). The true bounded statement $\operatorname{Prov}(\ulcorner\neg R\urcorner,\overline n)$ is also provable.

Using the given finite-numeral exhaustion below $\overline n$, these finitely many nonproof facts give

$$
S\vdash\forall y\leq\overline n\,\neg\operatorname{Prov}(\ulcorner R\urcorner,y).
$$

For arbitrary $y$, the given comparison splits into $y\leq\overline n$ or $\overline n\leq y$. In the first case the antecedent $\operatorname{Prov}(\ulcorner R\urcorner,y)$ is false. In the second case the known negative proof at code $\overline n$ supplies the required witness $z\leq y$. Hence $S$ proves

$$
\forall y\left(\operatorname{Prov}(\ulcorner R\urcorner,y)\to\exists z\leq y\,\operatorname{Prov}(\ulcorner\neg R\urcorner,z)\right).
$$

The [Rosser sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#rosser-sentence) equivalence now gives $S\vdash R$, contradicting consistency. Thus

$$
\boxed{S\nvdash\neg R\iff S\text{ is consistent}.}
$$

Only a fixed finite list of closed bounded facts was used; no assumption that the [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) is omega-consistent is needed.

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

The preceding [Gödel sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-sentence) argument gives $S\vdash G\to\operatorname{Con}_S$. The permitted arithmetized positive half of the [Rosser sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#rosser-sentence) argument is

$$
S\vdash\operatorname{Con}_S\to\neg BR.
$$

Pure first-order logic gives $\neg BR\to R$: if there is no proof of $R$, each antecedent in its defining universal implication is false. Therefore

$$
\boxed{S\vdash G\to\operatorname{Con}_S\to\neg BR\to R,\qquad S\vdash G\to R.}
$$

This uses the supplied internal Rosser implication, rather than replacing an external nonprovability fact by an unproved internal [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence).

<h3 id="1/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#1/e)

Let $T=S+\{\operatorname{Con}_S\}$ and assume $T$ is consistent. The allowed arithmetizations of the previous equivalences give

$$
S\vdash\neg B(\neg R)\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Con}_S,\qquad S\vdash\neg B(\neg G)\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Con}(T).
$$

Suppose $S\vdash R\to G$. Its contrapositive is $\neg G\to\neg R$, and the [Hilbert-Bernays-Löb derivability conditions](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions) then give

$$
S\vdash B(\neg G)\to B(\neg R),\qquad S\vdash\neg B(\neg R)\to\neg B(\neg G).
$$

Combining with the two internal equivalences yields $S\vdash\operatorname{Con}_S\to\operatorname{Con}(T)$. Since $T$ contains $\operatorname{Con}_S$, it would prove $\operatorname{Con}(T)$, contrary to the given [Gödel second incompleteness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-second-incompleteness-theorem) for this consistent extension. Hence

$$
\boxed{S\nvdash R\to G\quad\text{if }S+\{\operatorname{Con}_S\}\text{ is consistent}.}
$$

Together with the preceding implication, this is the [Strict Gödel-to-Rosser implication](../mathematical-logic.md#strict-godel-to-rosser-implication) under the stated consistency hypothesis.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

[Löb's theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#lob-s-theorem) states that, for a [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) with the stated provability and diagonalization properties,

$$
\boxed{S\vdash(BA\to A)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\vdash A,}
$$

where $BA=P_S(\ulcorner A\urcorner)$. It does not require an extra consistency assumption.

By the [diagonal lemma](../mathematical-logic.md#diagonal-lemma), choose $D$ with $S\vdash D\leftrightarrow(BD\to A)$. [necessitation rule](../mathematical-logic.md#necessitation-rule) and distribution applied to $D\to(BD\to A)$ give

$$
S\vdash BD\to B(BD\to A),\qquad S\vdash BD\to(B(BD)\to BA).
$$

Using [positive introspection](../mathematical-logic.md#positive-introspection) $BD\to B(BD)$ gives the unconditional theorem $S\vdash BD\to BA$. If $S\vdash BA\to A$, it follows that $S\vdash BD\to A$, and the reverse fixed-point implication gives $S\vdash D$. Necessitating gives $S\vdash BD$, hence $S\vdash BA$ and finally $S\vdash A$.

The same derivation also gives the [Internal form of Löb's theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#internal-form-of-lob-s-theorem), needed for modal soundness. Put $H=(BA\to A)$. From the unconditional $BD\to BA$, propositional reasoning and the fixed point give $S\vdash H\to D$. [necessitation rule](../mathematical-logic.md#necessitation-rule) and distribution imply $S\vdash BH\to BD$, and hence

$$
\boxed{S\vdash B(BA\to A)\to BA.}
$$

This internal statement was derived without necessitating a temporary assumption.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

The [Gödel second incompleteness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-second-incompleteness-theorem) says that a consistent, effectively axiomatized [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) with enough arithmetic and its standard derivability and diagonalization properties cannot prove its [formal consistency statement](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-consistency-statement) $\operatorname{Con}_S=\neg B\bot$.

If a consistent $S$ proved $\operatorname{Con}_S$, it would prove $B\bot\to\bot$ by [classical propositional logic](../mathematical-logic.md#classical-propositional-logic). Apply [Löb's theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#lob-s-theorem) to $A=\bot$. This would give $S\vdash\bot$, contradicting consistency. Therefore

$$
\boxed{S\text{ consistent}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\nvdash\operatorname{Con}_S.}
$$

For an inconsistent [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) the conclusion need not hold, since it proves every [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence).

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Assume $S\vdash\neg X$ and $S\vdash BX\to X$. Combining these theorems gives $S\vdash\neg BX$. Because $X$ is refutable, $\neg BX$ is a legitimate [formal consistency statement](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-consistency-statement) for $S$: the standard contradiction [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) and $X$ are provably interderivable, and the [derivability conditions](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions) transfer those implications to their provability statements.

Thus if $S$ were consistent, the [Gödel second incompleteness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-second-incompleteness-theorem) would contradict $S\vdash\neg BX$. Consequently the assumed reflection theorem forces $S$ to be inconsistent; [explosion](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-falsity-elimination-rule) then gives $S\vdash X$. This establishes the instance of [Löb's theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#lob-s-theorem)

$$
\boxed{S\vdash BX\to X\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\vdash X}
$$

for every $X$ refutable in $S$, using only the second incompleteness theorem.

<h4 id="2/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/b/iii)

Assume $S\vdash B_SX\to X$ and put $T=S+\{\neg X\}$. Then $T\vdash\neg B_SX$. The given extension-provability implication, with $Y=\bot$, gives

$$
S\vdash B_T\bot\to B_S(\neg X\to\bot).
$$

The provable classical implication $(\neg X\to\bot)\to X$, together with distribution, gives $S\vdash B_S(\neg X\to\bot)\to B_SX$. Hence

$$
T\vdash B_T\bot\to B_SX,\qquad T\vdash\neg B_T\bot=\operatorname{Con}(T).
$$

If $T$ were consistent, this would violate its [Gödel second incompleteness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-second-incompleteness-theorem). Thus $T$ is inconsistent. The [deduction theorem for propositional logic](../mathematical-logic.md#deduction-theorem-for-propositional-logic) and double-negation elimination give $S\vdash\neg\neg X$, hence $S\vdash X$. Therefore **the second incompleteness theorem for $S+\{\neg X\}$ yields [Löb's theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#lob-s-theorem) for arbitrary $X$**.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Use just the assumed local reflection instance with $Y=\neg X$:

$$
S+\{X\}\vdash B_S(\neg X)\to\neg X.
$$

By the [deduction theorem for propositional logic](../mathematical-logic.md#deduction-theorem-for-propositional-logic), $S\vdash X\to(B_S(\neg X)\to\neg X)$. [Classical propositional logic](../mathematical-logic.md#classical-propositional-logic) makes this imply the unconditional reflection statement $S\vdash B_S(\neg X)\to\neg X$: if $X$ is false the consequent is already true, and if $X$ is true the displayed implication supplies it. [Löb's theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#lob-s-theorem) applied to $\neg X$ now gives

$$
\boxed{S\vdash\neg X.}
$$

Thus [full local reflection in a one-sentence extension is inconsistent](../mathematical-logic.md#full-local-reflection-in-a-one-sentence-extension-is-inconsistent); in this argument even the single instance concerning $\neg X$ suffices.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

Put $T=S+\{X\}$ and choose its [Gödel sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-sentence) $Z$, so $T\vdash Z\leftrightarrow\neg B_TZ$. The assumed reflection scheme gives the particular theorem

$$
T\vdash B_S(X\to Z)\to(X\to Z).
$$

The given extension-provability implication is $S\vdash B_TZ\to B_S(X\to Z)$. The fixed-point equivalence gives $T\vdash\neg Z\to B_TZ$, so these implications compose to

$$
T\vdash\neg Z\to(X\to Z).
$$

The supplied propositional [tautology](../mathematical-logic.md#tautology) $((\neg Z\to(X\to Z))\to(X\to Z))$ gives $T\vdash X\to Z$, and $T\vdash X$ then yields $T\vdash Z$. The first of the [derivability conditions](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions) for $T$ gives $T\vdash B_TZ$, while its Gödel equivalence gives $T\vdash\neg B_TZ$. Hence $T$ is inconsistent. By the [deduction theorem for propositional logic](../mathematical-logic.md#deduction-theorem-for-propositional-logic),

$$
\boxed{S\vdash\neg X.}
$$

This proof uses diagonalization and proof transfer for the extension, and invokes neither [Löb's theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#lob-s-theorem) nor the second incompleteness theorem.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

The [Gödel-Löb logic](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-lob-logic) $\mathrm{GL}$ uses propositional variables, $\bot$, implication, and a unary [modal operator](../mathematical-logic.md#modal-operator) $\Box$. Other Boolean connectives are abbreviations; for example $\neg A$ means $A\to\bot$ and $\top$ means $\bot\to\bot$.

Its axioms are all classical propositional tautologies and the two schemes

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathrm K:&\quad\Box(A\to B)\to(\Box A\to\Box B),\\
\mathrm L:&\quad\Box(\Box A\to A)\to\Box A.
\end{aligned}}
$$

Its rules are the [modus ponens rule](../mathematical-logic.md#implication-elimination-rule) and the [necessitation rule](../mathematical-logic.md#necessitation-rule), $\mathrm{GL}\vdash A\Rightarrow\mathrm{GL}\vdash\Box A$. Uniform [propositional substitution](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-substitution) is allowed, or equivalently built into the axiom schemes. [necessitation rule](../mathematical-logic.md#necessitation-rule) applies to theorems; a temporary assumption cannot simply be boxed. In [provability logic](../mathematical-logic.md#provability-logic), $\Box A$ is read as the formal assertion that $A$ is provable.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Induct on a derivation in [Gödel-Löb logic](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-lob-logic). Propositional axiom instances translate to theorems of [Peano arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#peano-arithmetic). The image of $\bot$ is a refutable sentence, so it behaves as falsity in those translated propositional arguments.

The translation of axiom $\mathrm K$ is the distribution clause of the [Hilbert-Bernays-Löb derivability conditions](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions). The translation of axiom $\mathrm L$ is

$$
\operatorname{PA}\vdash B(BA\to A)\to BA,
$$

the [Internal form of Löb's theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#internal-form-of-lob-s-theorem), whose derivation was given above and applies to the stipulated [arithmetical provability predicate](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-provability-predicate) for [Peano arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#peano-arithmetic). Thus every axiom has a provable translation.

The [modus ponens rule](../mathematical-logic.md#implication-elimination-rule) is preserved because the interpretation commutes with implication. The [necessitation rule](../mathematical-logic.md#necessitation-rule) is preserved by the first derivability condition: if $\operatorname{PA}\vdash\mathcal I_i(A)$, then

$$
\operatorname{PA}\vdash Pr(\ulcorner\mathcal I_i(A)\urcorner)=\mathcal I_i(\Box A).
$$

Here the equality denotes the definition of the translated formula, not an arithmetic equality of truth values. A propositional substitution merely changes the arithmetic sentences assigned to the variables. The induction proves the [Arithmetical soundness of GL](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-soundness-of-gl):

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{GL}\vdash A\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{PA}\vdash\mathcal I_i(A).}
$$

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Take $Y(p)=p\to q$ and put $\top=(\bot\to\bot)$, $H=Y(\top)$ and $F_0=Y(\Box H)=\Box H\to q$. The first supplied substitution identity reads

$$
\mathrm{GL}\vdash\Box H\leftrightarrow\Box Y(\Box H)=\Box F_0.
$$

Use the supplied replacement of equivalents in the context $D(p)=p\to q$. It gives

$$
\mathrm{GL}\vdash F_0\leftrightarrow(\Box F_0\to q).
$$

But $H=(\top\to q)$ is equivalent to $q$. Replacement under a box gives $\Box H\leftrightarrow\Box q$, so $F_0$ is equivalent to

$$
\boxed{F=(\Box q\to q).}
$$

Replacing equivalents once more transfers the fixed-point equation to $F$, proving $\mathrm{GL}\vdash F\leftrightarrow(\Box F\to q)$.

A direct check of this [GL fixed point for a provability implication](../mathematical-logic.md#gl-fixed-point-for-a-provability-implication) is useful: the Löb axiom gives $\Box F\to\Box q$, and necessitating the [tautology](../mathematical-logic.md#tautology) $q\to F$ gives $\Box q\to\Box F$ by $\mathrm K$. Thus $\Box F\leftrightarrow\Box q$, and replacing the antecedent in $\Box F\to q$ recovers $F$.

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

Use the preceding fixed point with $q=\bot$. If the chosen arithmetic interpretation sends $\bot$ to a PA-refutable sentence $Z$, it gives the arithmetic sentence

$$
C=(BZ\to Z),\qquad\operatorname{PA}\vdash C\leftrightarrow(BC\to Z).
$$

This follows from [Arithmetical soundness of GL](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-soundness-of-gl). Since [Peano arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#peano-arithmetic) proves $\neg Z$, the two implications are equivalent to negated provability:

$$
\operatorname{PA}\vdash C\leftrightarrow\neg BZ,\qquad\operatorname{PA}\vdash C\leftrightarrow\neg BC.
$$

Thus $C$ expresses the [formal consistency statement](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-consistency-statement) for [Peano arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#peano-arithmetic) and is also a negative provability fixed point, like its [Gödel sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-sentence) $G$.

Apply the supplied modal uniqueness formula to the context with a [modalized occurrence](../mathematical-logic.md#modalized-occurrence) $X(p)=(\Box p\to\bot)$, with two fresh variables interpreted as $G$ and $C$. A [modalized occurrence](../mathematical-logic.md#modalized-occurrence) means that each indicated variable occurrence lies inside a box. [Peano arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#peano-arithmetic) proves both arithmetic fixed-point equations. The first derivability condition boxes those equations, so the translated uniqueness formula gives

$$
\operatorname{PA}\vdash B(G\leftrightarrow C).
$$

One must not infer $G\leftrightarrow C$ by reflection. Instead, distribution and the provability of the two propositional projections give $\operatorname{PA}\vdash BG\leftrightarrow BC$. Negating and using the two fixed-point equations now yields

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{PA}\vdash G\leftrightarrow C\leftrightarrow\neg BZ=\operatorname{Con}(\operatorname{PA}).}
$$

This proves the [Equivalence of a Gödel sentence and a consistency statement](../mathematical-logic.md#equivalence-of-a-godel-sentence-and-a-consistency-statement) using the required modal soundness, fixed point and uniqueness results.
