The [Gödel-Löb logic](../../../../godel-lob-logic.md) $\mathrm{GL}$ uses propositional variables, $\bot$, implication, and a unary [modal operator](../../../../modal-operator.md) $\Box$. Other Boolean connectives are abbreviations; for example $\neg A$ means $A\to\bot$ and $\top$ means $\bot\to\bot$.

Its axioms are all classical propositional tautologies and the two schemes

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathrm K:&\quad\Box(A\to B)\to(\Box A\to\Box B),\\
\mathrm L:&\quad\Box(\Box A\to A)\to\Box A.
\end{aligned}}
$$

Its rules are the [modus ponens rule](../../../../implication-elimination-rule.md) and the [necessitation rule](../../../../necessitation-rule.md), $\mathrm{GL}\vdash A\Rightarrow\mathrm{GL}\vdash\Box A$. Uniform [propositional substitution](../../../../propositional-substitution.md) is allowed, or equivalently built into the axiom schemes. [necessitation rule](../../../../necessitation-rule.md) applies to theorems; a temporary assumption cannot simply be boxed. In [provability logic](../../../../provability-logic.md), $\Box A$ is read as the formal assertion that $A$ is provable.
