<h1 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Assume $S\vdash B_SX\to X$ and put $T=S+\{\neg X\}$. Then $T\vdash\neg B_SX$. The given extension-provability implication, with $Y=\bot$, gives

$$
S\vdash B_T\bot\to B_S(\neg X\to\bot).
$$

The provable classical implication $(\neg X\to\bot)\to X$, together with distribution, gives $S\vdash B_S(\neg X\to\bot)\to B_SX$. Hence

$$
T\vdash B_T\bot\to B_SX,\qquad T\vdash\neg B_T\bot=\operatorname{Con}(T).
$$

If $T$ were consistent, this would violate its [Gödel second incompleteness theorem](../../../../../godel-second-incompleteness-theorem.md). Thus $T$ is inconsistent. The [deduction theorem for propositional logic](../../../../../deduction-theorem-for-propositional-logic.md) and double-negation elimination give $S\vdash\neg\neg X$, hence $S\vdash X$. Therefore **the second incompleteness theorem for $S+\{\neg X\}$ yields [Löb's theorem](../../../../../lob-s-theorem.md) for arbitrary $X$**.
