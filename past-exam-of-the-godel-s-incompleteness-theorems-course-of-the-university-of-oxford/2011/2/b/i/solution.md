↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [Gödel second incompleteness theorem](../../../../../godel-second-incompleteness-theorem.md) says that a consistent, effectively axiomatized [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) with enough arithmetic and its standard derivability and diagonalization properties cannot prove its [formal consistency statement](../../../../../formal-consistency-statement.md) $\operatorname{Con}_S=\neg B\bot$.

If a consistent $S$ proved $\operatorname{Con}_S$, it would prove $B\bot\to\bot$ by [classical propositional logic](../../../../../classical-propositional-logic.md). Apply [Löb's theorem](../../../../../lob-s-theorem.md) to $A=\bot$. This would give $S\vdash\bot$, contradicting consistency. Therefore

$$
\boxed{S\text{ consistent}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\nvdash\operatorname{Con}_S.}
$$

For an inconsistent [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) the conclusion need not hold, since it proves every [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md).
