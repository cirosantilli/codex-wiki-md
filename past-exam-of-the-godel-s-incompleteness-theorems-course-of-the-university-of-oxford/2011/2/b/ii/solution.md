<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Assume $S\vdash\neg X$ and $S\vdash BX\to X$. Combining these theorems gives $S\vdash\neg BX$. Because $X$ is refutable, $\neg BX$ is a legitimate [formal consistency statement](../../../../../formal-consistency-statement.md) for $S$: the standard contradiction [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) and $X$ are provably interderivable, and the [derivability conditions](../../../../../hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions.md) transfer those implications to their provability statements.

Thus if $S$ were consistent, the [Gödel second incompleteness theorem](../../../../../godel-second-incompleteness-theorem.md) would contradict $S\vdash\neg BX$. Consequently the assumed reflection theorem forces $S$ to be inconsistent; [explosion](../../../../../logical-falsity-elimination-rule.md) then gives $S\vdash X$. This establishes the instance of [Löb's theorem](../../../../../lob-s-theorem.md)

$$
\boxed{S\vdash BX\to X\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\vdash X}
$$

for every $X$ refutable in $S$, using only the second incompleteness theorem.
