# Ideal multisite mineral activity

↑ **Parent:** [Solid solution](solid-solution.md)

For ideal independent mixing on crystallographic sublattices, an endmember’s [thermodynamic activity](thermodynamic-activity.md) is the [product](product-of-numbers.md) of its required site fractions, with multiplicities. If one component occupies $q$ equivalent mixing positions per formula unit and has site fraction $X$, then $a=X^q$, not generally $X$.

For a binary example, the mixing [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md) per mole of formula units is $qRT[X\ln X+(1-X)\ln(1-X)]$. Differentiating the total free energy with respect to the amount of the all-component endmember gives a mixing chemical-potential term $qRT\ln X$. Comparison with $RT\ln a$ proves $a=X^q$. This derivation assumes ideal mixing and equal site fractions; nonideal interactions and preferential site occupancy require a different model. Mg endmembers of [biotite](biotite.md) and [garnet](garnet.md) commonly use $X_{\mathrm{Mg}}^3$ in this simplified three-site approximation.
