# Quantum-dot versus antidot resonance topology

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum Hall antidot](quantum-hall-antidot.md)

A [quantum dot](quantum-dot.md) is an electron-confining potential well, whereas an [antidot](quantum-hall-antidot.md) is a depleted potential hill. At the same [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md), their closed guiding-centre drift has opposite circulation. A series dot needs transmission through two barriers; a side-coupled [antidot](quantum-hall-antidot.md) can connect already propagating [quantum Hall edge states](quantum-hall-edge-state.md) and induce backscattering. Both can have discrete energies and charging effects, but a [electrical conductance](electrical-conductance.md) peak or dip depends on lead connectivity rather than solely on the existence of a closed orbit.
