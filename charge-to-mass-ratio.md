# Charge-to-mass ratio

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md)

The [charge-to-mass ratio](charge-to-mass-ratio.md) is a body's [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $q$ divided by its [mass](mass.md) $m$. Its [SI](international-system-of-units.md) unit is the [coulomb](coulomb.md) per kilogram, $\mathrm{C\,kg^{-1}}$. In a prescribed [electric field](electric-field-split.md) and [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md), nonrelativistic [acceleration](acceleration.md) depends on $q/m$ through the [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md). Two bodies with the same initial [position](position.md), [velocity](velocity.md), and [charge-to-mass ratio](charge-to-mass-ratio.md) therefore have the same trajectory in this ideal model. For an [electron](electron.md), write $q=-e$ with $e>0$ the [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md): $q/m=-e/m$, whereas the positive number $e/m$ is the magnitude of the [charge-to-mass ratio](charge-to-mass-ratio.md).
