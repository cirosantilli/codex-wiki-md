# Renormalization group

↑ **Parent:** [Critical phenomenon](critical-phenomenon-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renormalization_group)

A [renormalization group](renormalization-group.md) compares models at different observation lengths. A [Wilsonian coarse-grained statistical Hamiltonian](wilsonian-coarse-grained-statistical-hamiltonian.md) sums over short-distance fluctuations; a [renormalization-group transformation](renormalization-group-transformation.md) rescales coordinates and fields for comparison. [Kenneth Geddes Wilson](kenneth-geddes-wilson.md)'s 1982 lecture treats fluctuations across successive length scales to explain [critical phenomena](critical-phenomenon-split.md).[Section II, pp. 103–104: successive scale integration; inspected in an AGH-hosted reproduction](the-renormalization-group-and-critical-phenomena-by-kenneth-geddes-wilson.md)

The experimental motivation is [Andrews's carbon dioxide experiment](andrews-s-carbon-dioxide-experiment.md). Dried [carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md) occupied a [capillary tube](capillary-tube.md) above [mercury](mercury-element.md); a screw compressed the transmitting [water](water.md). A second air-filled tube gauged [pressure](pressure-split.md). Flowing [water](water.md) controlled [temperature](temperature-split.md), and a [cathetometer](cathetometer.md) read column positions for [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md). The [liquid meniscus](meniscus-liquid-surface.md) vanished near the [liquid-gas critical point](liquid-gas-critical-point.md). Residual air and imperfect air-gauge corrections limited accuracy.[pp. 576–578: preparation, apparatus, calibration and pressure caveats; pp. 583–584: critical-temperature observations](the-bakerian-lecture-on-the-continuity-of-the-gaseous-and-liquid-states-of-matter-by-thomas-andrews.md) These observations motivate critical-point theory without determining a [correlation-length critical exponent](correlation-length-critical-exponent.md). The [renormalization group](renormalization-group.md) explains how critical measurements can become insensitive to many molecular details.

In 1972, [Kenneth Geddes Wilson](kenneth-geddes-wilson.md) and [Michael Ellis Fisher](michael-ellis-fisher.md) used an [epsilon expansion](epsilon-expansion.md) about $d=4$ to compute nonclassical [critical exponents](critical-exponent.md). Their paper distinguishes a [Gaussian fixed point](gaussian-fixed-point.md) from an interacting [renormalization-group fixed point](renormalization-group-fixed-point.md), tunes the thermal parameter, and extracts powers from a linearized recursion. Its Ising-like result is $\gamma=1+\epsilon/6+O(\epsilon^2)$ for $d=4-\epsilon$.[Critical Exponents in 3.99 Dimensions, Physical Review Letters 28 (1972), pp. 240–242, abstract and equations (7), (10)–(14); p. 243 discusses the more general interaction and accuracy of the approximation](critical-exponents-in-3-99-dimensions-by-kenneth-geddes-wilson-and-michael-ellis-fisher.md) The worked model below isolates this mechanism with chosen coefficients; it is not a precision calculation for the three-dimensional [Ising model](ising-model.md).
