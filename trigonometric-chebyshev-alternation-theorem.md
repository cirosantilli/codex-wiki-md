# Trigonometric Chebyshev alternation theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Equioscillation theorem](equioscillation-theorem.md)

For real [continuous](continuous-function.md) $2\pi$-periodic $f$, a degree-at-most-$n$ real [trigonometric polynomial](trigonometric-polynomial.md) $p$ is its unique [best uniform approximation](best-uniform-approximation.md) exactly when $f-p$ has $2n+2$ alternating extrema of magnitude $\|f-p\|_\infty$ at distinct ordered points in one period. The closing interval around the circle also counts. A strictly improving approximant would force its difference from $p$ to have at least $2n+2$ zeros, whereas a nonzero degree-$n$ [trigonometric polynomial](trigonometric-polynomial.md) has at most $2n$.
