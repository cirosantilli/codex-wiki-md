# Linear space (geometry)

↑ **Parent:** [Incidence geometry](incidence-geometry.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_space_(geometry))

A linear space is a structure in [incidence geometry](incidence-geometry.md) with a [set](set-split.md) of points and a collection of [subsets](subset.md) called lines: any two distinct points lie on exactly one line, and every line contains at least two points. The lines here are incidence-theoretic objects, without a required coordinate realization. A [finite linear space](finite-linear-space.md) imposes finiteness; its nontrivial form excludes a line containing all points. This is distinct from a [vector space](vector-space-split.md), whose structure involves addition and scalar multiplication.
