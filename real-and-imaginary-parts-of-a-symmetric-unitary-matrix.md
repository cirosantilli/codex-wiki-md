# Real and imaginary parts of a symmetric unitary matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Unitary matrix](unitary-matrix.md)

Suppose a [unitary matrix](unitary-matrix.md) $U$ is symmetric under ordinary [transpose](transpose.md), and write its entrywise real and imaginary parts as $A$ and $B$. They are real [symmetric matrices](symmetric-matrix.md). Expanding $U^\dagger U=I$ gives $A^2+B^2+i(AB-BA)=I$, proving the displayed identities. Commuting real [symmetric matrices](symmetric-matrix.md) admit [simultaneous diagonalization](simultaneous-diagonalization.md): diagonalize $A$, then diagonalize the restriction of $B$ to each invariant [eigenspace](eigenspace.md) of $A$. A common real orthonormal [eigenbasis](eigenbasis.md) gives

$$
U=Q\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta_j})Q^T,\qquad A=Q\operatorname{diag}(\cos\theta_j)Q^T,\qquad B=Q\operatorname{diag}(\sin\theta_j)Q^T.
$$

If $A$ has [simple eigenvalues](simple-eigenvalue.md), every [eigenvector](eigenvector.md) of $A$ is already an [eigenvector](eigenvector.md) of $B$, because its one-dimensional [eigenspace](eigenspace.md) is invariant. The [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) of $A$ and $B$ are paired as the real and imaginary parts of the same [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) of $U$.
