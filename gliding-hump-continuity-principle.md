# Gliding-hump continuity principle

↑ **Parent:** [Separating space of a linear map](separating-space-of-a-linear-map.md)

Let $S:E\to Y$ be linear between [Banach spaces](banach-space-split.md), let $T_n:E\to E$ be bounded, and let $U_n:Y\to Z_n$ be bounded. If $U_nSP_m$ is [continuous](continuous-function.md) whenever $m>n$, then $U_nSP_n$ is [continuous](continuous-function.md) for all sufficiently large $n$. After rescaling the [bounded operators](continuous-linear-operator.md), assume their [norms](norm.md) are at most one. If the conclusion failed, discontinuity would allow successively tiny [vectors](vector.md) $x_j$ whose diagonal images dominate all previous contributions. Choose their [norms](norm.md) also to control the [continuous](continuous-function.md) maps $U_{n_i}SP_{n_i+1}$ on subsequent tails. The convergent sum $x=\sum_jP_{n_j}x_j$ would then satisfy $\|U_{n_j}Sx\|>j$ despite $\|U_{n_j}Sx\|\le\|Sx\|$, a contradiction. The tail is factored through $P_{n_i+1}$ before applying $S$, so no [continuity](continuous-function.md) of $S$ is assumed.
