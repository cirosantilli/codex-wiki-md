# Zero-pole sum of an elliptic function

↑ **Parent:** [Elliptic function](elliptic-function.md)

For a nonzero [elliptic function](elliptic-function.md) with [period lattice](period-lattice.md) $\Lambda$, its [zeros of a function](zero-of-a-function.md) $a_j$ and [poles](pole.md) $b_j$, repeated with their [multiplicities](multiplicity-mathematics.md), have equal total number and satisfy $\sum_j a_j-\sum_j b_j\in\Lambda$. Choose a fundamental parallelogram avoiding all zeros and poles on its boundary. The [argument principle](argument-principle.md) applied to $f'/f$ gives equality of the numbers. Applying the [residue theorem](residue-theorem.md) to $zf'/f$ gives their difference of sums. Pairing opposite edges, the extra factors are the two lattice generators multiplied by integrals of $f'/f$ along an edge. These integrals are integer multiples of $2\pi i$, because the endpoints have the same nonzero function value. Thus the difference of sums is a lattice element. This links zeros of a pulled-back line to the [chord-and-tangent group law](chord-and-tangent-group-law.md).
