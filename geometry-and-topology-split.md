# Geometry and topology

↑ **Parent:** [Area of mathematics](area-of-mathematics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_and_topology)

How much information about a shape do we need to keep? [Euclidean geometry](euclidean-geometry-split.md) retains [lengths](length.md) and [angles](angle.md), allowing questions about the diagonal of a [rectangle](rectangle.md) or the shortest route between two points. [Topology](topology-split.md) retains the structure preserved by a [homeomorphism](homeomorphism.md), allowing questions about [connectedness](connected-space.md) and whether a loop can shrink to a point, as recorded by the [fundamental group](fundamental-group.md). These are different levels of description of the same object.

For example, stretching a [circle](circle.md) horizontally produces an [ellipse](ellipse.md). The stretching map and its inverse are [continuous maps](continuous-map.md), so it gives a [homeomorphism](homeomorphism.md): the two curves have the same [topology](topology-split.md). It changes some [Euclidean distances](euclidean-distance.md), however, so it is not an [isometry](isometry.md). This distinction helps separate essential structure from a particular drawing, while explaining why measuring a shape requires more information than recognizing its [topology](topology-split.md).

The choice of geometry matters too. A [Euclidean geometry](euclidean-geometry-split.md) [triangle](triangle.md) has interior [angles](angle.md) summing to $\pi$, but a [spherical triangle](spherical-triangle.md) can have three [right angles](right-angle.md). [Spherical geometry](spherical-geometry.md) turns this apparent surprise into a measurement: the excess of the [angle](angle.md) sum over $\pi$ determines the [area](surface-area.md). Begin with the [Pythagorean theorem](pythagorean-theorem.md) for flat measurements, then compare the [spherical triangle](spherical-triangle.md) below to see [curvature](curvature.md) change the rules.
