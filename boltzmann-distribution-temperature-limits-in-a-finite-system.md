# Boltzmann distribution temperature limits in a finite system

↑ **Parent:** [Boltzmann distribution](boltzmann-distribution.md)

Keep the gap $\Delta$ fixed and vary positive [temperature](temperature-split.md). With $x=k_BT/\Delta$, the [Boltzmann factor](boltzmann-factor.md) is $q=e^{-1/x}$. In the four-[microstate](microstate.md) example, as $T\to0^+$, $q\to0$: the unique [ground state](ground-state.md) has [probability](probability.md) tending to one, and the other [microstates](microstate.md) have [probabilities](probability.md) tending to zero. If several [microstates](microstate.md) instead shared the lowest [energy](energy-split.md), their [state degeneracy](state-degeneracy.md) would split that limiting population equally.

As $T\to\infty$ in this fixed finite model, all four individual [Boltzmann factors](boltzmann-factor.md) tend to one, so all four individual [microstates](microstate.md) have [probability](probability.md) tending to $1/4$. The three aggregate [probabilities](probability.md) instead approach

$$
\bigl(P(0),P(\Delta),P(2\Delta)\bigr)\longrightarrow
\left(\frac14,\frac12,\frac14\right).
$$

The equal-population limit applies to this finite set of [microstates](microstate.md). An infinite collection of [microstates](microstate.md) cannot all have the same positive normalized [probability](probability.md), so the statement cannot be extended to an unbounded [energy](energy-split.md) spectrum. Real systems may also acquire additional accessible [microstates](microstate.md) as [temperature](temperature-split.md) rises.

**[Figure 20](#image-aggregate-boltzmann-distribution-populations-versus-positive-temperature-for-state-degeneracies-1-2-1). Aggregate Boltzmann distribution populations versus positive temperature for state degeneracies 1, 2, 1**.

The plotted crossing occurs at $x=1/\ln2$, where $P(0)=P(\Delta)=4/9$. For larger positive [temperatures](temperature-split.md), the middle [macrostate](macrostate.md) is more probable than the lowest-[energy](energy-split.md) [macrostate](macrostate.md), although the lowest-[energy](energy-split.md) individual [microstate](microstate.md) remains more probable than either individual middle [microstate](microstate.md). The [expected value](expected-value-split.md) of [energy](energy-split.md) rises from zero toward $\Delta$ in this finite model.
