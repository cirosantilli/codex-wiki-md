# Piston-cylinder pressure apparatus

↑ **Parent:** [High-pressure experimental metrology](high-pressure-experimental-metrology.md)

In a functional model, a driven [piston](piston.md) reduces the chamber's [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md). A surrounding cylinder carries the wall [stress](stress.md); a deformable packing or gasket closes a potential leak path around a moving or removable closure. A sample and a separate [pressure sensor](pressure-sensor.md) occupy the chamber. A transmitting [liquid](liquid.md) can load the sample on all sides; the sensor's signal passes to an external readout. This is a division of experimental functions, not a scale reconstruction of one historical machine.

For a slowly moving frictionless drive [piston](piston.md) of effective [surface area](surface-area.md) $A$, external [pressure](pressure-split.md) $p_{\rm ext}$ and inward applied [force](force-split.md) $F$, [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) at mechanical balance gives

$$
F+p_{\rm ext}A-pA=0,\qquad p-p_{\rm ext}=F/A.
$$

Thus $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ on a stipulated $20\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ effective [surface area](surface-area.md) corresponds ideally to $1\,\mathrm{GPa}$ of differential [pressure](pressure-split.md). These are illustrative numbers, not apparatus specifications. The effective [surface area](surface-area.md) is the one associated with the [pressure](pressure-split.md) difference, not necessarily an external ram's visible cross section. With resisting [friction](friction.md) $F_f$ during inward motion, the balance becomes $p-p_{\rm ext}=(F-F_f)/A$. During outward motion the sign of the [friction](friction.md) contribution reverses. An applied-load reading can therefore show [hysteresis](hysteresis.md) even when the chamber's actual [pressure](pressure-split.md) is well defined.

A [resistance pressure gauge](resistance-pressure-gauge.md) instead supplies an internal signal. In a generic electrical readout, measure an [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $V_e$ with a known [electric current](electric-current.md) $I$ and use [Ohm's law](ohm-s-law.md) to infer [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) $R=V_e/I$. This describes a possible readout principle; the lecture passage cited below identifies the sensor material and calibration landmarks without specifying this wiring.
