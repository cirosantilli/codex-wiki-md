# Superconductivity

↑ **Parent:** [Statistical physics](statistical-physics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity)

[Superconductivity](superconductivity.md) is a phase of matter that supports steady [electric current](electric-current.md) with zero bulk [electrical resistivity](electrical-resistivity.md) and exhibits the [Meissner effect](meissner-effect.md): equilibrium screening of a sufficiently weak, static [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) from the interior. It occurs below a material-dependent [superconducting transition temperature](superconducting-transition-temperature.md), provided the [electric current](electric-current.md) and applied [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) are also sufficiently small. [Milind N. Kunchur (2004), limits on dissipation-free superconducting current](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0409402) Bulk expulsion means that a sample much thicker than its [London penetration depth](london-penetration-depth.md) has an almost field-free interior; the [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) need not vanish at its surface. Conventional [BCS theory](bcs-theory.md) explains [superconductivity](superconductivity.md) through correlated [Electrons](electron.md) forming [Cooper pairs](cooper-pair.md). [Hoffman laboratory, introduction to superconductivity](https://hoffman.physics.harvard.edu/materials/SCintro.php)

The two observations answer different questions. A [resistance-versus-temperature experiment for superconductivity](resistance-versus-temperature-experiment-for-superconductivity.md) asks whether a steady [electric current](electric-current.md) requires a measurable [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md). A [Field-cooled Meissner experiment](field-cooled-meissner-experiment.md) asks whether a [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) already present in the material is expelled when it enters [superconductivity](superconductivity.md). An ideal [perfect conductor](perfect-conductor.md) with zero [electrical resistivity](electrical-resistivity.md) can preserve that pre-existing [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md); zero [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) alone therefore does not establish the [Meissner effect](meissner-effect.md).

The usefulness follows directly from [Joule heating](joule-heating.md), $P=I^2R$: maintaining a steady [electric current](electric-current.md) through a segment with zero [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) produces no resistive heating in that segment. This makes persistent [electric currents](electric-current.md) attractive for producing steady [magnetic fields](magnetic-field.md). The beauty is that one phase has both a transport property and an equilibrium magnetic response, and the second cannot be inferred just by making an ordinary [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) very small. The [London equations](london-equations.md) encode this extra magnetic response. [F. and H. London (1935), The electromagnetic equations of the supraconductor](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1935.0048)
