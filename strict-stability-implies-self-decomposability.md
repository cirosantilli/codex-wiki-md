# Strict stability implies self-decomposability

↑ **Parent:** [Strictly stable distribution](strictly-stable-distribution.md)

Let $X$ have a [strictly stable distribution](strictly-stable-distribution.md) of index $\alpha$, and let $X'$ be an independent copy. For integers $1\leq k<n$, split a sum of $n$ independent copies into blocks of sizes $k$ and $n-k$. Strict stability of each block and of the full sum gives

$$
(k/n)^{1/\alpha}X+(1-k/n)^{1/\alpha}X'\overset d=X.
$$

For any $0<c<1$, choose rationals $k_m/n_m\to c^\alpha$. On the fixed [probability space](probability-space-split.md) carrying $X,X'$, the left side converges [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md) to $cX+(1-c^\alpha)^{1/\alpha}X'$. Thus it converges in [distribution](distribution-mathematical-analysis.md); since its [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) is constant, the limit also has the law of $X$. This proves a [self-decomposable distribution](self-decomposable-distribution.md) with the displayed remainder. The proof does not require any finite [moments](moment.md).
