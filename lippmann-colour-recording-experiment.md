# Lippmann colour-recording experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Lippmann plate](lippmann-plate.md)

In his 14 December 1908 Nobel lecture, [Gabriel Lippmann](gabriel-lippmann.md) described a [photographic plate](photographic-plate.md) bearing a transparent, even, extremely fine sensitive layer. A holder put [Mercury](mercury-planet.md) against that layer as a mirror during exposure. Incident and reflected [light](light.md) formed a [standing wave](standing-wave.md) within the [photographic emulsion](photographic-emulsion.md); after [photographic processing](photographic-processing.md) and drying, white [light](light.md) revealed colour by selective [reflection](reflection-mathematics.md). The reflector served during recording; the developed structure supplied the later colour selection.[Colour photography, PDF p. 1, paragraphs beginning The method is very simple and This result is due: apparatus, exposure, development and reflected readout](colour-photography-by-gabriel-lippmann.md)

The lecture's control was to project a spectrum photograph, wet it, and project it again. Its colours disappeared as the gelatin swelled, then returned during drying, red before the shorter [wavelengths](wavelength.md). This tested a spacing-dependent explanation. Lippmann also reported roughly one-minute sunlight exposures and identified portrait exposure time as a limitation.[PDF p. 1, wetting demonstration; p. 2, final paragraphs on exposure time](colour-photography-by-gabriel-lippmann.md)

A reversible colour change supports a structural explanation, but does not independently measure every fringe spacing or the [refractive index](refractive-index.md). For a conceptual comparison, record a narrow band and compare reflected [optical spectra](optical-spectrum.md) at fixed illumination and viewing [angles](angle.md); an unexposed processed plate tests background. A second comparison changes only viewing geometry. A third compares the dry state before and after a reversible spacing change. These are proposed discriminating controls, not additional experiments attributed to Lippmann. Background surface [reflection](reflection-mathematics.md) must be separated from the selective signal. A photograph's appearance also depends on the illuminating [optical spectrum](optical-spectrum.md); viewing under an arbitrary lamp is not a calibrated spectral measurement.
