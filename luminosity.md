# Luminosity

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysics](astrophysics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminosity)

Luminosity is emitted radiative [power](power.md): the [energy](energy-split.md) carried away by [electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation-split.md) per unit time, measured in watts. [Bolometric luminosity](luminosity.md) includes all [wavelengths](wavelength.md); a measurement through one [photometric passband](photometric-passband.md) captures only part of it. [Spectral luminosity](spectral-luminosity.md) records how that [power](power.md) is distributed over [frequency](frequency.md). Received [observed radiative flux](observed-radiative-flux.md) has different units, watts per square metre, because it measures what crosses an observer's collecting area. [IAU 2015 Resolution B2: Radiative luminosity and irradiance](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.06262).

For example, an ideal source with [luminosity](luminosity.md) $10^{26}\,\mathrm W$ emits $10^{26}\,\mathrm J$ each second regardless of whether it is viewed from $20$ or $40$ [parsecs](parsec.md). Its [observed radiative flux](observed-radiative-flux.md) changes with distance, while its [luminosity](luminosity.md) remains fixed. Combining [stellar parallax](stellar-parallax.md) with [observed radiative flux](observed-radiative-flux.md) therefore lets us compare the actual outputs of [stars](star.md) rather than their apparent brightnesses.
