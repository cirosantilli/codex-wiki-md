# Arrival-time compression for a moving source

↑ **Parent:** [Retarded time](retarded-time.md)

For a stationary distant detector, the arrival time of a light signal emitted at $(t,\mathbf x(t))$ is $t_a=t+|\mathbf x_D-\mathbf x(t)|/c$. Its [derivative](derivative.md) is $dt_a/dt=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u/c$, where $\mathbf n$ points from the source to the detector. This exact relation follows by differentiating the distance; a constant viewing direction is a far-field approximation. For a source approaching near the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md), a long emission interval can correspond to a short arrival interval. Combining it with the $1/\gamma$ cone of [relativistic beaming](relativistic-beaming.md) yields [synchrotron pulse compression](synchrotron-pulse-compression.md) of order $r/(c\gamma^3)$.
