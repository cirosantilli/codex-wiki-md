# White dwarf

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](stellar-evolution.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_dwarf)

A white dwarf is a compact [stellar remnant](stellar-remnant.md) supported mainly by [electron degeneracy pressure](electron-degeneracy-pressure.md). Its [ions](ion.md) carry most of the [mass](mass.md), while its [electrons](electron.md) provide most of the supporting [pressure](pressure-split.md). The [Pauli exclusion principle](pauli-exclusion-principle.md) makes compression fill [quantum states](quantum-state.md) with larger [momenta](momentum-split.md); support therefore persists even when thermal motion becomes small. Cooling and [hydrostatic equilibrium](hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) are separate questions: a [white dwarf](white-dwarf.md) can radiate stored [energy](energy-split.md) while retaining degeneracy support.

The surprising trend is that a more massive cold [white dwarf](white-dwarf.md) is smaller. Its higher [mass density](density.md) raises the [Fermi momentum](fermi-momentum.md), eventually requiring [special relativity](special-relativity-split.md). The [degenerate-electron pressure crossover](degenerate-electron-pressure-crossover.md) explains why this response approaches a limiting stiffness and why the [Chandrasekhar limit](chandrasekhar-limit.md) exists. [Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar](subrahmanyan-chandrasekhar.md)'s 1983 lecture develops this connection between [quantum statistics](quantum-ideal-gas-statistics-split.md), [stellar structure](stellar-structure-split.md) and [stellar evolution](stellar-evolution.md).[Sections 4–5, printed pp. 146–151, equations 22–46 and Figures 2–3](on-stars-their-evolution-and-their-stability-by-subrahmanyan-chandrasekhar.md)
