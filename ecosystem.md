# Ecosystem

↑ **Parent:** [Ecology](ecology.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem)

An [ecosystem](ecosystem.md) is a system of interacting [organisms](organism.md) and their nonliving surroundings. A pond study might include [algae](algae.md), [fish](fish.md), [bacteria](bacteria.md), [water](water.md), dissolved [chemical compounds](chemical-compound.md) and incoming [light](light.md). The useful step is to state the boundary and time interval: the pond surface, bottom and shoreline might enclose the study region, but [water](water.md), [organisms](organism.md) and [energy](energy.md) can cross them. The chosen boundary specifies what counts as internal storage, input or output; it does not make the [ecosystem](ecosystem.md) sealed. See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 46.1: ecosystems and their components](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/46-1-ecology-of-ecosystems).

For a toy pond [water](water.md) budget over one day, begin with $1000\,\mathrm{m^3}$, add $120\,\mathrm{m^3}$ crossing inward, and remove $90\,\mathrm{m^3}$ crossing outward. With all other transfers omitted by assumption, the final amount is $1000+120-90=1030\,\mathrm{m^3}$. The same bookkeeping asks whether a change inside an [ecosystem](ecosystem.md) came from internal processes or exchanges with its surroundings. Changing the boundary to include an upstream reservoir changes which transfers are internal, while preserving the accounting.
