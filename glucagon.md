# Glucagon

↑ **Parent:** [Hormone](hormone.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucagon)

[Glucagon](glucagon.md) is a [peptide hormone](peptide-hormone.md) made by [alpha cells](alpha-cell.md) in the [pancreatic islets](pancreatic-islets.md). Its mature [polypeptide](polypeptide.md) has 29 [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md). [Alpha cells](alpha-cell.md) store [glucagon](glucagon.md) in [secretory granules](secretory-granule.md) and release it by [exocytosis](exocytosis.md). [Glucagon](glucagon.md) is a message about fuel availability; [glucose](glucose.md) is the [sugar](sugar.md) whose supply it helps regulate. A falling [blood glucose](blood-sugar.md) [concentration](concentration.md) favors [glucagon](glucagon.md) release, whereas a rise usually suppresses it. [Amino acids](amino-acid.md) can also stimulate release, so a meal containing [protein](protein.md) need not produce the same [hormone](hormone.md) pattern as a meal dominated by [carbohydrates](carbohydrate.md). [Glucagon Physiology](endotext/glucagon-physiology.md)

The principal [blood glucose](blood-sugar.md)-raising target is the [liver](liver.md). In a [hepatocyte](hepatocyte.md), the [glucagon receptor](glucagon-receptor.md) is a [G protein-coupled receptor](g-protein-coupled-receptor.md): activation of a stimulatory [G protein](g-protein.md) drives [adenylyl cyclase](adenylyl-cyclase.md), increasing the [second messenger](second-messenger.md) [cAMP](cyclic-adenosine-monophosphate.md) and activating [protein kinase A](protein-kinase-a.md). Downstream [phosphorylation](phosphorylation.md), including the [phosphorylase kinase](phosphorylase-kinase.md) relay to [glycogen phosphorylase](glycogen-phosphorylase.md), favors [glycogenolysis](glycogenolysis.md). [Glucagon](glucagon.md) also supports [gluconeogenesis](gluconeogenesis.md). These are different supplies: [glycogenolysis](glycogenolysis.md) draws on a finite [glycogen](glycogen.md) reserve, whereas [gluconeogenesis](gluconeogenesis.md) makes [glucose](glucose.md) from other [substrates](substrate-biochemistry.md) and depends on their availability. Longer fasting depletes the [glycogen](glycogen.md) reserve; a stronger [hormone](hormone.md) signal cannot release material that is no longer there. [Glucagon Physiology](endotext/glucagon-physiology.md) [17.9 The Endocrine Pancreas](anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/17-9-the-endocrine-pancreas.md)

Do not copy the [liver](liver.md) pathway into every target of [insulin](insulin.md). [Skeletal muscle](skeletal-muscle.md) uses its own [glycogen](glycogen.md) reserve locally; its [glucose](glucose.md) uptake and the [liver](liver.md)'s [glucose](glucose.md) output are different flows. Nor is direct [glucagon](glucagon.md)-stimulated [lipolysis](lipolysis.md) in human [adipose tissue](adipose-tissue.md) as firmly established as its action in the [liver](liver.md). [Glucagon](glucagon.md) action depends on the tissue, other [hormones](hormone.md), and available [substrates](substrate-biochemistry.md). [Glucagon Physiology](endotext/glucagon-physiology.md)

There is a useful surprise inside the [pancreatic islets](pancreatic-islets.md): local [glucagon](glucagon.md) can support [insulin](insulin.md) release from neighboring [beta cells](beta-cell.md). The [islet perifusion experiment](islet-perifusion-experiment.md) distinguishes that interaction from [glucagon](glucagon.md)'s action on the [liver](liver.md). Thus the two [hormones](hormone.md) can oppose each other's effects on [blood glucose](blood-sugar.md) while cooperating locally. [Delta cells](delta-cell.md) supply another local brake through [somatostatin](somatostatin.md).
