# Finite covariance range weak law

↑ **Parent:** [Weak law of large numbers](weak-law-of-large-numbers.md)

Suppose the [variances](variance-split.md) of a sequence of [random variables](random-variable-split.md) are bounded by $C$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)=0$ whenever $|i-j|>r$, for a fixed nonnegative integer $r$. The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) bounds each remaining covariance in absolute value by $C$. In the [variance of a sum](variance-of-a-sum.md), there are at most $rn$ off-diagonal pairs, proving the displayed bound. The [Chebyshev inequality](chebyshev-s-inequality.md) makes the average minus its mean converge to zero in probability. If all means equal $\mu$, the average therefore converges in probability to $\mu$. Independence of every summand is unnecessary.
