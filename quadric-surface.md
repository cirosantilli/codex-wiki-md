# Quadric surface

↑ **Parent:** [Euclidean geometry](euclidean-geometry-split.md)

A quadric surface in three-dimensional [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md) is the zero set of a real [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) of degree two. Its quadratic part can be written with a [symmetric matrix](symmetric-matrix.md) $A$, since the [antisymmetric matrix](skew-symmetric-matrix.md) part contributes zero to $x^TAx$. An [orthogonal diagonalization](orthogonal-diagonalization-of-a-real-symmetric-matrix.md) and a [translation](translation-geometry.md) expose its geometry: examples include an [ellipsoid](ellipsoid.md), an [elliptic cone](elliptic-cone.md), and a [hyperboloid](hyperboloid.md). This affine geometric description allows singular surfaces such as a cone's vertex.
