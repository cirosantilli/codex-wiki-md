<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Orient the flow so $q>0$, and let subscripts 1 and 2 denote upstream and downstream. The mechanical [energy flux](../../../../../energy-flux.md) is $\rho q B$, where $B=u^2/2+gh$. Inserting the relation for $q^2$ in [i](../i.md) gives the [hydraulic jump head loss](../../../../../hydraulic-jump-head-loss.md),

$$
B_1-B_2=\frac{q^2}{2}\left(\frac1{h_1^2}-\frac1{h_2^2}\right)+g(h_1-h_2)=\frac{g(h_2-h_1)^3}{4h_1h_2}.
$$

An admissible jump dissipates mechanical [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), so $\rho q(B_1-B_2)>0$. The denominator is positive, hence

$$
\boxed{h_2>h_1}.
$$

The zero depth-change case is continuous flow. A depth-decreasing discontinuity would require an energy input at the jump; it is not the dissipative [hydraulic jump](../../../../../hydraulic-jump.md) selected by this model.
