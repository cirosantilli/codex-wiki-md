<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $H$ be the depth behind the bore. The wall's [kinematic boundary condition](../../../../../kinematic-boundary-condition.md) makes the [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) there $U$; ahead of the bore the depth and [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) are $h_0$ and zero. Across a rightward [hydraulic bore](../../../../../hydraulic-bore.md) moving at $V$, the [Rankine-Hugoniot conditions](../../../../../rankine-hugoniot-conditions.md) are

$$
V(H-h_0)=HU,\qquad VHU=HU^2+\frac g2(H^2-h_0^2).
$$

The first gives $R=H/h_0=V/(V-U)=v/(v-1)>1$. Substituting into the second gives $F^2v=(R^2-1)/2$, where $v=V/U$ and $F=U/\sqrt{gh_0}$. Hence the [piston-driven shallow-water bore](../../../../../piston-driven-shallow-water-bore.md) obeys

$$
\boxed{F^2v^2=\frac{v(2v-1)}{2(v-1)^2}=1+\frac{3}{2(v-1)}+\frac1{2(v-1)^2},\qquad v>1}.
$$

The positive depth increase is consistent with the energy-loss condition in [ii](../../a/ii.md), now viewed in the bore's own [shock frame](../../../../../shock-frame.md).
