# Apsidal speed sum for an attractive inverse-square orbit

↑ **Parent:** [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md)

For unit mass with [inverse-square potential](inverse-square-potential.md) $-Q/r$, $Q>0$, and positive conserved [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) $h$, a bounded orbit has $-Q^2/(2h^2)\le E<0$. Its [periapsis distance](pericentre-distance.md) and [apoapsis distance](apocentre-distance.md) are roots of $|E|r^2-Qr+h^2/2=0$, so their sum is $Q/|E|$ and product $h^2/(2|E|)$. Speed is largest at the [periapsis](periapsis.md) and smallest at the [apoapsis](apoapsis.md), because $v^2=2(E+Q/r)$ decreases with $r$. Radial velocity is zero there, so the two speeds are $h/r_{\min}$ and $h/r_{\max}$. Their sum is $h(r_{\min}+r_{\max})/(r_{\min}r_{\max})=2Q/h$, independent of $E$, including the circular limit.
