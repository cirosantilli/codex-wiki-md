<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

An [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md) is a nonrotating frame in which all force-free test bodies follow straight trajectories with constant [velocity](../../../velocity.md). The qualification “all” matters: a specially chosen coordinate system can make one accelerated trajectory look straight without making the frame inertial. In practice, approximately isolated test bodies and [gyroscopes](../../../gyroscope.md) help identify [inertial motion](../../../inertial-motion.md); identifying the relevant forces and calibrating instruments are part of testing the theory. The definition does not select a privileged state of uniform rest.

In its usual coordinate formulation, [Newtonian mechanics](../../../newtonian-mechanics-split.md) states $m\ddot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf F$ in an [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md). A [Galilean transformation](../../../galilean-transformation.md), $t'=t+b$, $\mathbf x'=R\mathbf x+\mathbf Vt+\mathbf a$ with constant orthogonal $R$ and constant $\mathbf V$, maps the class of [inertial frames](../../../inertial-frame.md) to itself. It preserves zero [acceleration](../../../acceleration.md); a time-dependent translation generally does not. In the translating frame $\mathbf x'=\mathbf x-\mathbf a(t)$, the same physical law reads $m\ddot{\mathbf x}'=\mathbf F-m\ddot{\mathbf a}$. The extra [fictitious force](../../../fictitious-force.md) records the frame's [acceleration](../../../acceleration.md), rather than a new interaction.

It is possible to state [Newtonian mechanics](../../../newtonian-mechanics-split.md) without mentioning an [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md) explicitly. On [Galilean spacetime](../../../galilean-spacetime.md), let $u$ be a trajectory tangent normalized by the absolute time covector, $t_a u^a=1$. Then

$$
m\nabla_u u=F
$$

uses an [affine connection](../../../affine-connection.md) to define [acceleration](../../../acceleration.md). The free-particle equation is $\nabla_u u=0$. This formulation removes a chosen coordinate frame but retains the geometric distinction between [inertial motion](../../../inertial-motion.md) and accelerated motion. Omitting both the [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md) language and all equivalent inertial structure would leave the law unable to specify which trajectories count as unforced.

The same distinction applies to [special relativity](../../../special-relativity-split.md). Einstein's original presentation uses a class of [inertial frames](../../../inertial-frame.md) and calibrated rods, clocks and [light](../../../light.md) signals.[sections 1–3: synchronization, postulates and coordinate transformation](../../../on-the-electrodynamics-of-moving-bodies-by-albert-einstein.md) A geometric presentation instead uses [Minkowski spacetime](../../../minkowski-spacetime.md), its [Lorentzian metric](../../../lorentzian-metric.md) and its [Levi-Civita connection](../../../levi-civita-connection.md). A free massive particle obeys $\nabla_u u=0$, now with [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) parametrization and $g(u,u)=-c^2$. There is no preferred inertial rest frame, but there is a distinguished family of inertial trajectories and a physical [Minkowski interval](../../../minkowski-interval.md).

Accelerating coordinates are allowed in [special relativity](../../../special-relativity-split.md). They produce nonzero connection coefficients even though the [Riemann curvature tensor](../../../riemann-curvature-tensor-split.md) remains zero. [General relativity](../../../general-relativity-split.md) is needed when spacetime curvature and dynamical gravitation matter, not merely because the chosen observer accelerates. Thus neither theory requires a particular [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md) in its coordinate-free statement; each still needs the structure that determines [inertial motion](../../../inertial-motion.md).
