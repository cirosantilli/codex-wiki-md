<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

There are two distinct explanatory questions. One asks why a proposed system of equations has a particular [dynamical symmetry](../../../dynamical-symmetry.md). The other asks why the common spacetime geometry is the right geometry for the behavior of physical rods, clocks and fields. A geometric account and a dynamical account give different directions of explanation, and their merits cannot be decided by a change of notation alone.

For the geometric account, [Minkowski spacetime](../../../minkowski-spacetime.md) has a fixed [Lorentzian metric](../../../lorentzian-metric.md); its [spacetime isometries](../../../spacetime-isometry.md) form the [Poincaré group](../../../poincare-group.md). If the matter equations use that [metric tensor](../../../metric-tensor.md) and no additional symmetry-breaking [background structure](../../../background-structure.md), with couplings and boundary assumptions appropriate to the transformation, these [spacetime isometries](../../../spacetime-isometry.md) take solutions into solutions. For example, the [Klein-Gordon equation](../../../klein-gordon-equation.md)

$$
\left(\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu-\frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\right)\phi=0
$$

in [metric signature](../../../metric-signature.md) $(-,+,+,+)$ is invariant under the [Poincaré group](../../../poincare-group.md). This provides one common reason for the same transformation rules appearing in many laws. An individual solution, such as a particular [plane wave](../../../plane-wave.md), need not possess every symmetry of the equation.

Geometry alone is insufficient unless the assumed coupling to geometry is included. In the [covariant equation with a preferred timelike vector](../../../covariant-equation-with-a-preferred-timelike-vector.md),

$$
(\eta^{\mu\nu}-\alpha n^\mu n^\nu)\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi=0,
$$

the fixed timelike $n$ selects a preferred rest frame. All the quantities can be transformed consistently under a [coordinate transformation](../../../smooth-transition-map.md), but boosts that change $n$ are not symmetries with the physical background fixed. Thus [general covariance](../../../general-covariance.md), tensor notation and the mere presence of the [Minkowski metric](../../../minkowski-metric.md) do not by themselves establish [Einstein's principle of relativity](../../../einstein-s-principle-of-relativity.md).

A dynamical account instead starts with the common invariance of the actual interactions and asks why their composite rods and clocks measure a common interval. If their equations and suitable equilibrium solutions transform consistently under [Lorentz transformations](../../../lorentz-transformation.md), their moving configurations can exhibit [length contraction](../../../length-contraction.md) and their periodic processes [time dilation](../../../time-dilation.md). This is the explanatory strategy defended by [Harvey R. Brown](../../../harvey-r-brown.md) and [Oliver Pooley](../../../oliver-pooley.md), who deny that merely redescribing these effects geometrically supplies their microscopic explanation.[sections 3–5: constructive explanation, rods and clocks, and the direction of explanation](../../../minkowski-space-time-a-glorious-non-entity-by-harvey-r-brown-and-oliver-pooley.md) Such an account must still explain why independent interactions have the same relevant symmetry and why a particular clock is a good clock; it cannot simply infer those facts from the word “dynamics”.

The positions therefore need not disagree about experimental predictions. The geometric presentation makes a shared constraint explicit and permits economical deductions. The constructive presentation investigates why material systems realize that constraint. Defining geometry from universal dynamical behavior is a substantive proposal; deriving behavior from geometry also requires substantive matter-coupling assumptions. Neither direction is made compulsory by the formal equivalence of the descriptions.
