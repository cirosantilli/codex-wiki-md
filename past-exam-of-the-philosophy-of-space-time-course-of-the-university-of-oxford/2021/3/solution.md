<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The age comparison is a local comparison of [clocks](../../../clock.md) at two shared [spacetime events](../../../event-relativity.md): departure and reunion. No convention about the simultaneity of distant events is needed to read two adjacent [clocks](../../../clock.md). The result is determined by the [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) along each complete [world line](../../../world-line.md). For an ideal [clock](../../../clock.md) following a [timelike curve](../../../timelike-curve.md) in the home observer's [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md),

$$
\Delta\tau=\int_0^T\sqrt{1-\frac{\dot x(t)^2}{c^2}}\,dt.
$$

The home observer stays at $x=0$, whereas the traveler goes outward at [velocity](../../../velocity.md) $v$ and returns at [velocity](../../../velocity.md) $-v$. Ignoring the duration of a short turnaround gives home age $T$ and traveler age $T/\gamma$. The [twin paradox with fixed reunion events](../../../twin-paradox-with-fixed-reunion-events.md) derives this directly. There is no contradiction because the traveler does not remain in one [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md) throughout the trip.

[Relativity of simultaneity](../../../relativity-of-simultaneity.md) becomes essential to a different question: how can the traveler consistently describe the home [clock](../../../clock.md) as running slowly during both straight legs? The statement about a distant [clock](../../../clock.md)'s rate refers to a sequence of remote events selected as simultaneous by a specified [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md). Changing that frame changes the selected sequence. Adding the two slow-rate comparisons while silently preserving one distant-time assignment is the error.

Let turnaround be $P=(t,x)=(T/2,vT/2)$ in the home frame. Constant outward-frame time means $t-vx/c^2$ is constant. Thus the home event simultaneous with $P$ according to the outward frame occurs at

$$
t_H^-=\frac T2\left(1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}\right).
$$

For the return frame, constant time means $t+vx/c^2$ is constant, so its simultaneous home event occurs at

$$
t_H^+=\frac T2\left(1+\frac{v^2}{c^2}\right).
$$

These are two different remote [spacetime events](../../../event-relativity.md), even though both assignments pass through the same turnaround event. The [twin paradox simultaneity change](../../../twin-paradox-simultaneity-change.md) is

$$
t_H^+-t_H^-=\frac{v^2T}{c^2}.
$$

Each leg contributes $T/(2\gamma^2)$ to the home [clock](../../../clock.md) advance assigned within that leg's frame. Including the reassignment gives $T/\gamma^2+v^2T/c^2=T$, exactly the home reading at reunion. For the illustrative values $v=0.6c$ and $T=10$ years, the traveler's elapsed [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) is eight years; the two leg assignments give $3.2+3.2$ years of home advance, and the frame change supplies the missing $3.6$ years. The distant home [clock](../../../clock.md) does not physically jump.

Nor should simultaneity assignments be confused with received [light](../../../light.md) signals. An observer sees emissions after a propagation delay and measures a [Doppler shift](../../../doppler-effect.md). The received history remains consistent through a finite turnaround, but it is not the same sequence of events as a constant-coordinate-time slice. The [Lorentz transformation](../../../lorentz-transformation.md) relates the latter; signal travel times must be included separately when discussing what is seen.

An instantaneous turnaround is only an ideal limit. In the [twin paradox with finite acceleration](../../../twin-paradox-with-finite-acceleration.md), the [world line](../../../world-line.md) and the momentarily comoving frame vary continuously. The distant home-time assignment is $t_H(t)=t-v(t)x(t)/c^2$, so

$$
\frac{dt_H}{dt}=1-\frac{v(t)^2}{c^2}-\frac{x(t)}{c^2}\frac{dv}{dt}.
$$

The reversal at positive $x$ makes the last contribution positive. Its integrated effect replaces the ideal reassignment. By the [clock hypothesis](../../../clock-hypothesis.md), the traveler's own elapsed [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) has no extra term depending directly on [acceleration](../../../acceleration.md); physical disturbances of a particular [clock](../../../clock.md) are a separate experimental issue.

Thus [relativity of simultaneity](../../../relativity-of-simultaneity.md) explains the consistency of the traveler's successive inertial descriptions. It is not a convention-dependent cause of the final age difference. The [invariance of reunion ages under clock resynchronization](../../../invariance-of-reunion-ages-under-clock-resynchronization.md) makes that distinction explicit: remote time labels can change while the two reunited [clock](../../../clock.md) readings remain unchanged.
