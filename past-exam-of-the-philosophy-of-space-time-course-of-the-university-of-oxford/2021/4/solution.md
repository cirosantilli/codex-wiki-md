<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

The numerical coordinates assigned to an event can be changed without changing the event. It does not follow that every relation between coordinate systems is devoid of physical content. A standard [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md) includes a family of resting measuring standards and synchronized [clocks](../../../clock.md). Its coordinates are normalized so that the [Minkowski metric](../../../minkowski-metric.md) has the same standard form. The relation between two such descriptions therefore records properties of the standards and of [spacetime](../../../spacetime.md), not just an arbitrary choice of names.

Write $x^\mu=(ct,x,y,z)$ and use [metric signature](../../../metric-signature.md) $(-,+,+,+)$. A [Lorentz transformation](../../../lorentz-transformation.md) satisfies

$$
x'^\mu=\Lambda^\mu{}_{\nu}x^\nu,\qquad
\Lambda^T\eta\Lambda=\eta,\qquad
\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1).
$$

An origin translation can also be included without changing the [Minkowski interval](../../../minkowski-interval.md). The displayed condition preserves the interval between events, including the [null cone](../../../light-cone.md) and [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) along a [timelike curve](../../../timelike-curve.md). Its physical significance lies in the claim that comparisons made with actual idealized rods and [clocks](../../../clock.md) obey this same metric structure.

A more general passive relabeling is allowed, but then the metric components must change with the labels. For example, let $t'=2t$ and leave $x,y,z$ fixed. In the new coordinates,

$$
ds^2=-\frac{c^2}{4}\,dt'^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2.
$$

Nothing physical has changed, but $t'$ no longer has the original clock-normalized meaning of $t$. Retaining the old metric components as well as the new labels would change the mathematical model. This distinction is part of the [physical content of a passive Lorentz transformation](../../../physical-content-of-a-passive-lorentz-transformation.md): the transformation between standard inertial coordinate systems preserves a specified metric form, whereas arbitrary relabelings need not preserve that form.

For an operational example, a [clock](../../../clock.md) moving at [velocity](../../../velocity.md) $v$ between two events on its [world line](../../../world-line.md) accumulates

$$
\Delta\tau=\Delta t\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}
$$

in the first frame. In its own rest frame the same result is simply the interval between its local readings. The equality is a physical agreement about one [clock](../../../clock.md), derived using the preserved [Minkowski interval](../../../minkowski-interval.md). It is not a claim that the two coordinate-time differences are identical. Similarly, an observer measures a [light](../../../light.md) angular [frequency](../../../frequency.md) through $\omega=-k_\mu u^\mu$, where $k^\mu$ is the [wave four-vector](../../../four-wavevector.md) and $u^\mu$ the observer's [four-velocity](../../../four-velocity.md). Transforming both sets of components leaves this scalar unchanged. Comparing two different observers gives the physical [relativistic Doppler effect](../../../relativistic-doppler-effect.md) rather than a disagreement over one observer's measurement.

There is a further distinction between passive transformations of the same situation and active changes to a situation. Re-expressing a particular experiment in different coordinates is always possible if all geometric fields are transformed consistently. Preparing a second, moving copy of the apparatus is a different physical arrangement. Its relation to the first is governed by a [dynamical symmetry](../../../dynamical-symmetry.md) only if the relevant laws and background structure have the appropriate invariance. An [active Lorentz transformation of a vector field](../../../active-lorentz-transformation-of-a-vector-field.md) makes the changed field configuration explicit.

This is why mere [Lorentz covariance](../../../lorentz-covariance.md) of notation must not be confused with the [Lorentz invariance](../../../lorentz-invariance.md) of the dynamics. A physically preferred [timelike vector field](../../../timelike-vector-field.md) can be transformed as a vector under passive relabeling while distinguishing particular states of motion. Holding that preferred field fixed can destroy the active boost symmetry of the matter dynamics. The [covariant equation with a preferred timelike vector](../../../covariant-equation-with-a-preferred-timelike-vector.md) is a concrete example.

Coordinates are therefore labels, but the [Lorentz transformation](../../../lorentz-transformation.md) relates labels tied to shared physical standards and a definite metric structure. Its measurable consequences follow from that structure and the dynamical assumptions, not from the bare act of changing labels.[sections 1–4: operational definitions of time and length, and the transformation of moving standards](../../../on-the-electrodynamics-of-moving-bodies-by-albert-einstein.md)
