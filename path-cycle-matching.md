# Path-cycle matching

↑ **Parent:** [Graph theory](graph-theory-split.md)

A [path-cycle matching](path-cycle-matching.md) in a finite [simple graph](simple-graph.md) is a collection of mutually [vertex-disjoint paths](vertex-disjoint-paths.md) and [cycles in a graph](cycle-in-a-graph.md). Its used [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) form a [subgraph](subgraph.md) with every [degree of a vertex](degree-graph-theory.md) at most two. Conversely, every finite [connected component of a graph](component-graph-theory.md) with maximum [degree](degree-of-a-polynomial.md) at most two is a [graph path](path-in-a-graph.md), a [cycle in a graph](cycle-in-a-graph.md), or an [isolated vertex](isolated-vertex.md); following successive [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) proves this classification. Discarding unused [isolated vertices](isolated-vertex.md) therefore converts any such [subgraph](subgraph.md) into a [path-cycle matching](path-cycle-matching.md) without changing its [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) count. On a [bipartite graph](bipartite-graph-split.md), maximizing the used [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) is exactly [bipartite b-matching](bipartite-b-matching.md) with every $b_v=2$.
