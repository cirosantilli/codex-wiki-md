# Kinetic energy

↑ **Parent:** [Energy](energy-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy)

[Kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) is the [energy](energy-split.md) associated with motion. For a nonrelativistic [point mass](point-mass.md) of [mass](mass.md) $m>0$, measured in a specified [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md),

$$
K=\frac12m\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf v=\frac12mv^2,
\qquad v=|\mathbf v|.
$$

Here $\mathbf v$ is [velocity](velocity.md) and $v$ is [speed](speed.md); $T$ is another common symbol for [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md). The [dot product](dot-product.md) makes $K$ nonnegative regardless of the direction of [velocity](velocity.md). At fixed [mass](mass.md), doubling [speed](speed.md) quadruples [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md): a $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ [point mass](point-mass.md) at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ has $4\,\mathrm J$, whereas at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ it has $16\,\mathrm J$. The [SI](international-system-of-units.md) units are $\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s^2}=\mathrm J$. Different [reference frames](reference-frame.md) can assign different [velocities](velocity.md) and therefore different [kinetic energies](kinetic-energy.md). [OpenStax University Physics 1, §7.2: Kinetic energy](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/7-2-kinetic-energy)

A short outline explains why [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) has this formula. In a nonrelativistic [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md), constant [mass](mass.md) and [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) give, using the [chain rule](chain-rule.md),

$$
\frac{dK}{dt}=m\mathbf v\cdot\frac{d\mathbf v}{dt}
=\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}\cdot\mathbf v.
$$

Integrating over [time](time-in-physics.md) proves the [work-energy theorem](work-energy-theorem.md), $W_{\mathrm{net}}=K_f-K_i$. The net [force](force-split.md) is the sum of all [forces](force-split.md), and $W_{\mathrm{net}}$ is their total [work](work.md), not merely the [work](work.md) of a chosen driving [force](force-split.md). This route to final [speed](speed.md) is useful when the [forces](force-split.md) as [functions](function-split.md) of [position](position.md) are known but the full motion as a function of [time](time-in-physics.md) would be harder to find. [OpenStax University Physics 1, §7.3: Work–energy theorem](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/7-3-work-energy-theorem)

In the running example, a $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ [point mass](point-mass.md) starts at rest and receives a constant net $3\,\mathrm N$ [force](force-split.md) through $4\,\mathrm m$. Its net [work](work.md) is $12\,\mathrm J$, so

$$
K_f=12\,\mathrm J,
\qquad v_f=\sqrt{\frac{2K_f}{m}}
=\sqrt{12}\,\mathrm{m/s}\approx3.4641\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

At any intermediate distance $x\ge0$, $K(x)=(3\,\mathrm N)x$ and $v(x)=\sqrt{2Fx/m}$: [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) is a [linear function](linear-function.md) of distance, while [speed](speed.md) is proportional to the [square root](square-root.md) of distance. If a net opposing $3\,\mathrm N$ braking [force](force-split.md) subsequently acts for $4\,\mathrm m$, it does $-12\,\mathrm J$ of [work](work.md) and brings this [point mass](point-mass.md) to rest. Negative net [work](work.md) decreases [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md); it does not make [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) negative. Beyond the stopping point the motion must be reconsidered, since the [point mass](point-mass.md) cannot keep moving forward with a negative $K$.

[Kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) also makes the exchange with [potential energy](potential-energy.md) intuitive: a falling body's loss of [gravitational potential energy](gravitational-energy.md) can appear as increased [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md). The formula above applies when [speed](speed.md) is small compared with the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md); at higher [speeds](speed.md), [special relativity](special-relativity-split.md) changes the expression.
