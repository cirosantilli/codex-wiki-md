# Random variable

↑ **Parent:** [Probability theory](probability-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variable)

A real-valued random variable is a [measurable function](measurable-function.md) $X$ from a [probability space](probability-space.md) $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$ to the [real numbers](real-number.md) with their [Borel sigma-algebra](borel-sigma-algebra.md). More general targets give [random elements](random-element.md). For every [Borel set](borel-set.md) $A$, measurability ensures that $\{\omega:X(\omega)\in A\}$ is an [event](event.md), so the question "does the observed value lie in $A$?" has a defined [probability](probability.md).

The [function](function-split.md) $X$ is fixed; its input outcome is uncertain. This separates what we measure from how the uncertainty is modeled. A [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md) is the resulting [pushforward measure](pushforward-measure.md), $\mathbb P_X(A)=\mathbb P(X\in A)$. A [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md), [probability mass function](probability-mass-function.md), or [probability density function](probability-density-function.md) describes that [measure](measure.md) in an appropriate setting. The random variable and its [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md) are different objects: the former also retains its relationship to other measurements on the same [sample space](sample-space.md).

For two independent uniform dice, let $U,V$ record their results and define $S=U+V$ and $M=\max(U,V)$. The 36 ordered pairs are equally likely, but the resulting [probability mass functions](probability-mass-function.md) differ:

$$
\mathbb P(S=s)=\frac{6-|s-7|}{36}\quad(2\leq s\leq12),\qquad
\mathbb P(M=m)=\frac{2m-1}{36}\quad(1\leq m\leq6).
$$

For the sum, count the diagonal of the outcome grid having total $s$. For the maximum, subtract the $(m-1)^2$ pairs with both results below $m$ from the $m^2$ pairs with both results at most $m$. These examples show how a deterministic [function](function-split.md) transforms a simple input [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md) into a nonuniform output one.

**[Figure 1](#image-different-random-variables-on-the-same-equally-likely-dice-outcome-space). Different random variables on the same equally likely dice outcome space**.

The corresponding [expected values](expected-value.md) are $\mathbb E S=7$ and $\mathbb E M=161/36$. The first follows immediately from [linearity of expectation](linearity-of-expectation.md); the second follows from $\sum_{m=1}^6m(2m-1)/36$. Neither number must be an attainable result. An [expected value](expected-value.md) describes an average under the [probability measure](probability-measure.md), not a prediction that the next observation will equal it.

The distinction between a random variable and its [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md) matters for dependence. $U$ and $V$ have the same [discrete uniform distribution](discrete-uniform-distribution.md) and are [independent random variables](independent-random-variables.md). $U$ and a second copy of $U$ also have that same marginal [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md), but they always agree. Their joint [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md) is concentrated on the diagonal; the marginals alone cannot say which relationship holds. This is why a model for several measurements must specify their joint law or an appropriate [independence](independent-random-variables.md) assumption.

[Indicator random variables](indicator-random-variable.md) turn [events](event.md) into numbers: $\mathbf1_A$ equals one on $A$ and zero elsewhere, and $\mathbb E\mathbf1_A=\mathbb P(A)$. This connects event counting with [expected value](expected-value.md) and [variance](variance-split.md), and lets tools for [random variables](random-variable-split.md) answer questions about [probabilities](probability.md). More complicated [functions](function-split.md) of observations can describe totals, waiting times, or estimation errors using the same framework.
