# Aperture

↑ **Parent:** [Optics](optics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aperture)

An [aperture](aperture.md) is an opening that admits [light](light.md) into or through an [optical instrument](optical-instrument-split.md). Its shape and size select the transmitted [light rays](light-ray.md); an [aperture](aperture.md) alone does not bend them as an [optical lens](lens-optics.md) does. The [aperture stop](aperture-stop.md) is the opening that most restricts the bundle from an on-axis object point. The [entrance pupil](entrance-pupil.md) is the image of that [aperture stop](aperture-stop.md) formed by the preceding [optical lens](lens-optics.md) elements, viewed from the object side. Consequently the effective collecting [diameter](diameter.md) $D$ is the [entrance pupil](entrance-pupil.md) [diameter](diameter.md), which can differ from the physical hole's [diameter](diameter.md). For example, preceding [optical lens](lens-optics.md) elements that enlarge a $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ [aperture stop](aperture-stop.md) by a factor of two give $D=20\,\mathrm{mm}$; measuring the physical hole alone would misidentify the [focal ratio](focal-ratio.md). [Rüdiger Paschotta, RP Photonics Encyclopedia, Entrance and Exit Pupil](https://www.rp-photonics.com/entrance_and_exit_pupil.html).

For an unobscured circular [entrance pupil](entrance-pupil.md) under a uniform beam normal to its plane, the collecting [area](surface-area.md) and admitted [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) are

$$
A=\frac{\pi D^2}{4},\qquad \Phi_{\rm admitted}=E_{\rm pupil}A,
$$

where $E_{\rm pupil}$ is incident [irradiance](irradiance.md). Doubling $D$ quadruples admitted [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md). Obstructions, nonuniform illumination and losses after the [entrance pupil](entrance-pupil.md) require the actual illuminated [area](surface-area.md) and transmission to be included. The same $D$ also sets an angular [diffraction-limited resolution](diffraction-limited-resolution.md) scale $\lambda/D$ at [wavelength](wavelength.md) $\lambda$. Collection and [diffraction](diffraction.md) therefore answer different questions from [field of view](field-of-view.md): an [aperture](aperture.md) selects the bundle from each object point, while the receiving plane's extent helps select which object directions appear in the image.

NASA/JPL's published pinhole demonstration uses two white cards, foil taped over a window in one card, and a small hole pierced in the foil. Sunlight passes through the hole onto the second card; the projected image is viewed on that card. The activity describes a larger image when the card separation increases, improved visibility when the receiving card is shaded, and a separate image from each of several holes. These are qualitative observations of [image formation](image-formation.md), rather than calibrated measurements of [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) or [diffraction](diffraction.md). [NASA/JPL Education, How to Make a Pinhole Camera, apparatus and projection observations](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/edu/resources/project/how-to-make-a-pinhole-camera/).

A proposed quantitative extension replaces sunlight by a steadily illuminated asymmetric target and puts the foil and a translucent receiving screen in a dark box. Record target distance $u$, hole-to-screen distance $b$, hole [diameter](diameter.md) $d$, image height, orientation and edge blur. Measure screen brightness with a fixed [photodetector](photodetector.md), or record the screen with an [optical camera](optical-camera.md) whose integration time and gain stay fixed. Exchange centred foils of measured $d$ at fixed $u,b$; then vary $b$ with the same foil. [Geometrical optics](geometrical-optics-split.md) predicts an inverted image with [transverse magnification](transverse-magnification.md) $m=-b/u$ and a point's geometric blur [diameter](diameter.md) $d(1+b/u)$. Thus a larger hole admits more [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) without changing the central image scale, whereas increasing $b$ enlarges the image. These are predictions to test, not reported data. Keep target illumination and detector position fixed, subtract a hole-covered dark reading, repeat the original foil to check drift, and avoid clipping the detector response. Foil thickness and ragged edges can block oblique [light rays](light-ray.md); at sufficiently small $d$, [diffraction](diffraction.md) invalidates the claim that ever smaller holes always sharpen the image. A lensless [optical camera](optical-camera.md) has no [optical lens](lens-optics.md) [focal length](focal-length.md): $b$ is a propagation distance, not that [focal length](focal-length.md).
