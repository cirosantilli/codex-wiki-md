# Mean curvature of a sphere and cylinder

↑ **Parent:** [Mean curvature](mean-curvature.md)

For a [sphere](sphere.md) of [radius](radius.md) $R>0$, its outward [unit normal](unit-normal.md) is $N(p)=p/R$. Applying the [shape operator](shape-operator.md) gives $S(v)=-v/R$, so the two [principal curvatures](principal-curvature.md) coincide:

$$
k_1=k_2=-\frac1R,\qquad H=-\frac1R,\qquad K=\frac1{R^2}.
$$

For the lateral surface of a [circular cylinder](circular-cylinder.md) with outward [unit normal](unit-normal.md), moving around a circle turns $N$ at rate $1/R$, while moving along the axis leaves $N$ constant. Thus the circumferential and axial [principal curvatures](principal-curvature.md) give

$$
k_{\mathrm{circ}}=-\frac1R,\qquad k_{\mathrm{axial}}=0,\qquad H=-\frac1{2R},\qquad K=0.
$$

Here $K$ is [Gaussian curvature](gaussian-curvature.md). The [circular cylinder](circular-cylinder.md) bends in only one [principal direction](principal-direction.md), so its [mean curvature](mean-curvature.md) is half that of the equally sized [sphere](sphere.md). These formulas concern the smooth lateral surface, excluding any edges where end caps meet it.
