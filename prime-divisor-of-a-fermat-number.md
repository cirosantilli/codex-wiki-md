# Prime divisor of a Fermat number

↑ **Parent:** [Fermat number](fermat-number.md)

If a [prime number](prime-number.md) $p$ divides the [Fermat number](fermat-number.md) $F_n$, it is odd and $2^{2^n}\equiv-1\pmod p$. The [multiplicative order](multiplicative-order.md) of $2$ divides $2^{n+1}$ but not $2^n$, so it is exactly $2^{n+1}$. [Fermat's little theorem](fermat-little-theorem.md) then gives $2^{n+1}\mid p-1$. For $n\geq1$, every prime divisor is $1$ modulo $4$. A prime need not divide any Fermat number merely because it is $1$ modulo $4$: order $12$ excludes the prime $13$ from the whole family.
