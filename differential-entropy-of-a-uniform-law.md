# Differential entropy of a uniform law

↑ **Parent:** [Differential entropy](differential-entropy.md)

For a [continuous uniform distribution](continuous-uniform-distribution.md) on an interval of length $L>0$, the [probability density](probability-density.md) is $1/L$. Direct integration gives

$$
h_e(X)=-\int_a^{a+L}\frac1L\ln\frac1L\,dx=\ln L.
$$

Thus **a uniform law on an interval of length $1/2$ has differential entropy $-\ln2$**, while length two gives $\ln2$. Both laws have total [probability](probability.md) one. The shorter interval has larger [probability density](probability-density.md), making $-\ln f(X)$ negative; this is neither a negative [probability](probability.md) nor a negative discrete [information entropy](information-entropy.md).
