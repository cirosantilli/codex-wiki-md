# Fixed point theorem for summably Lipschitz iterates

↑ **Parent:** [Iterated contraction](iterated-contraction.md)

Let $T$ be a self-map of a nonempty closed subset of a [Banach space](banach-space-split.md), with the displayed bounds. Then $T$ has a unique [fixed point](fixed-point.md) $p$ and every orbit $x_n=T^nx_0$ converges to it. Indeed, $\|x_{n+1}-x_n\|\leq\alpha_n\|x_1-x_0\|$ makes the orbit Cauchy by summing successive increments. The first bound makes $T$ continuous, so the limit is fixed. Since $\alpha_n\to0$, the bounds also imply uniqueness. The tail sum gives $\|p-x_n\|\leq\sum_{k\geq n}\alpha_k\|x_1-x_0\|$ for $n\geq1$. A [Volterra integral equation](volterra-integral-equation.md) supplies factorially decreasing bounds even when the first iterate is not a [contraction mapping](contraction-mapping.md).
