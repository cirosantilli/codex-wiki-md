# Orthogonality of trigonometric functions

↑ **Parent:** [Fourier series](fourier-series-split.md)

For positive integers $m,n$, the [sine](sine.md) functions form an [orthogonal](orthogonal-vectors.md) family on $(0,L)$:

$$
\int_0^L\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\sin\left(\frac{m\pi x}{L}\right)\,dx=\frac L2\delta_{mn}.
$$

The same formula holds for the corresponding cosine functions when both indices are positive. Product-to-sum identities reduce the distinct-index products to cosines with nonzero integer frequencies, each with zero integral; for matching indices, the squared sine or cosine has mean $1/2$. This proves the formula and explains why separate [normal modes](normal-mode.md) of a uniform fixed-end string make additive contributions to its [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and [potential energy](potential-energy.md). The constant cosine has squared norm $L$ rather than $L/2$.
