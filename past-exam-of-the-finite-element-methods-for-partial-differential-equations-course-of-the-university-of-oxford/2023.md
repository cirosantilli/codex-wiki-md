<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

For a sufficiently regular solution, put $w=-\Delta u$, with $\Delta$ the [Laplacian](../multivariable-calculus.md#laplacian). Then $-\Delta u-w=0$ and $u-\Delta w=u+\Delta^2u=f$. The zero [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) on $u$ and $\Delta u$ become zero [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) on $u$ and $w$. Conversely, eliminating $w$ from these two equations recovers the fourth-order equation with the [biharmonic operator](../multivariable-calculus.md#biharmonic-operator). For the [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) we will construct, these interior identities hold as [distributional identities](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-identity); zero [Sobolev traces](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator) are imposed on $u$ and $w$, without assuming classical fourth [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) of $u$.

Let $V=H_0^1(\Omega)\times H_0^1(\Omega)$, the [Cartesian product](../set-theory.md#cartesian-product) of two [zero-boundary Sobolev spaces](../sobolev-space.md#zero-boundary-sobolev-space), with [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm)

$$
\|(p,q)\|_V^2=\|p\|_1^2+\|q\|_1^2.
$$

This is a [Hilbert space](../hilbert-space.md). Applying [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) separately to the two equations gives the [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation)

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
(\nabla u,\nabla v)_0-(w,v)_0&=0&&\text{for every }v\in H_0^1(\Omega),\\
(u,z)_0+(\nabla w,\nabla z)_0&=(f,z)_0&&\text{for every }z\in H_0^1(\Omega).
\end{aligned}}
$$

Equivalently, for $U=(u,w)$ and $T=(v,z)$, define the [bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md) and [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional)

$$
\begin{aligned}
b((p,q),(v,z))&=(\nabla p,\nabla v)_0+(\nabla q,\nabla z)_0-(q,v)_0+(p,z)_0,\\
F(v,z)&=(f,z)_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

We seek $U\in V$ with $b(U,T)=F(T)$ for every $T\in V$.

The [skew-coupled elliptic system](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#skew-coupled-elliptic-system) has a useful [bounded bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#bounded-bilinear-form) estimate with constant one. Indeed, write the [integrand](../integral.md#integrand) as the [dot product](../inner-product.md#dot-product) of $(\nabla p,\nabla q,-q,p)$ and $(\nabla v,\nabla z,v,z)$. The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) gives

$$
|b((p,q),(v,z))|\leq\|(p,q)\|_V\|(v,z)\|_V,
\qquad |F(v,z)|\leq\|f\|_0\|(v,z)\|_V.
$$

For equal arguments, the two zeroth-order coupling terms cancel. If $K$ is the constant in the supplied [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality), then

$$
b((p,q),(p,q))=\|\nabla p\|_0^2+\|\nabla q\|_0^2
\geq\frac1{1+K}\|(p,q)\|_V^2.
$$

Thus $b$ is a [coercive bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#coercive-bilinear-form), although it is not a [symmetric bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#symmetric-bilinear-form). The [Lax-Milgram theorem](../functional-analysis.md#lax-milgram-theorem) applies and yields

$$
\boxed{\text{There is exactly one }(u,w)\in H_0^1(\Omega)\times H_0^1(\Omega)\text{ solving the weak system.}}
$$

In particular, $\|(u,w)\|_V\leq(1+K)\|f\|_0$. Testing with compactly supported smooth [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) identifies $-\Delta u=w$ and $-\Delta w=f-u$ as [distributional identities](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-identity). This gives the asserted relation to the original [boundary value problem](../boundary-value-problem.md) at the available regularity.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Take $V_h=\mathcal U_h\times\mathcal W_h\subset V$. The [Galerkin method](../partial-differential-equation.md#galerkin-method) asks for $U_h=(u_h,w_h)\in V_h$ such that

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
(\nabla u_h,\nabla v_h)_0-(w_h,v_h)_0&=0&& (v_h\in\mathcal U_h),\\
(u_h,z_h)_0+(\nabla w_h,\nabla z_h)_0&=(f,z_h)_0&& (z_h\in\mathcal W_h).
\end{aligned}}
$$

Since a [finite-dimensional subspace is closed](../normed-vector-space.md#finite-dimensional-subspace-is-closed), $V_h$ is itself a [Hilbert space](../hilbert-space.md). The [bounded bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#bounded-bilinear-form) and [coercive bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#coercive-bilinear-form) estimates in part (a) restrict to $V_h$ with the same constants. The [Lax-Milgram theorem](../functional-analysis.md#lax-milgram-theorem) therefore gives exactly one $U_h$ for every $h$.

Put $E=U-U_h=(e_u,e_w)$. Subtracting the continuous and discrete [weak formulations](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) gives [Galerkin orthogonality](../finite-element-method.md#galerkin-orthogonality):

$$
b(E,T_h)=0\qquad(T_h\in V_h).
$$

For any $Y_h=(v_h,z_h)\in V_h$, the difference $Y_h-U_h$ also belongs to $V_h$, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|\nabla e_u\|_0^2+\|\nabla e_w\|_0^2
&=b(E,E)\\
&=b(E,U-Y_h)+b(E,Y_h-U_h)\\
&=b(E,U-Y_h)\\
&\leq\|E\|_V\|U-Y_h\|_V.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last step uses the constant-one [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) estimate from part (a). Bounding the four terms separately by a sum would obscure the precise constant requested here.

The two [minimization problems](../mathematical-optimization.md#minimization-problem) over $\mathcal U_h$ and $\mathcal W_h$ are independent. Each has a [best approximation](../normed-vector-space.md#best-approximation-in-a-normed-space) because its [vector subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) is finite-dimensional. Consequently, if

$$
D_h^2=\min_{v_h\in\mathcal U_h}\|u-v_h\|_1^2+\min_{z_h\in\mathcal W_h}\|w-z_h\|_1^2,
$$

then the preceding inequality gives exactly

$$
\boxed{\|\nabla(u-u_h)\|_0^2+\|\nabla(w-w_h)\|_0^2\leq\|U-U_h\|_V D_h.}
$$

Both errors belong to the [zero-boundary Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md#zero-boundary-sobolev-space). Applying the [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) componentwise now yields

$$
\frac1{1+K}\|U-U_h\|_V^2\leq\|U-U_h\|_V D_h.
$$

When $U=U_h$ the desired estimate is automatic; otherwise divide by the nonzero [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm). Thus

$$
\boxed{\left(\|u-u_h\|_1^2+\|w-w_h\|_1^2\right)^{1/2}\leq(1+K)\left(\min_{v_h\in\mathcal U_h}\|u-v_h\|_1^2+\min_{z_h\in\mathcal W_h}\|w-z_h\|_1^2\right)^{1/2}.}
$$

The constant $C=1+K$ is independent of $h$. This is the [Céa lemma](../finite-element-method.md#cea-s-lemma) estimate for this particular [skew-coupled elliptic system](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#skew-coupled-elliptic-system).

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Use the continuous [piecewise-linear](../function.md#piecewise-linear-function) nodal [interpolants](../interpolation.md#interpolant) $I_hu$ and $I_hw$ on the [shape-regular mesh](../finite-element-method.md#shape-regular-mesh). Since $u,w\in H^2(\Omega)$ in two dimensions, the [Sobolev embedding theorem](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-embedding-theorem) supplies continuous representatives, so the nodal values are well-defined. Their zero [Sobolev traces](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator) imply zero boundary nodal values. Hence $I_hu\in\mathcal U_h$ and $I_hw\in\mathcal W_h$ for the usual full [conforming finite element spaces](../finite-element-method.md#conforming-finite-element-space).

The permitted [finite element interpolation estimates](../finite-element-method.md#finite-element-interpolation-estimate) give constants $C_u,C_w$ determined by the [shape regularity](../finite-element-method.md#shape-regular-mesh) and $\Omega$, independent of $h$, such that

$$
\|u-I_hu\|_1\leq C_u h\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)},\qquad
\|w-I_hw\|_1\leq C_w h\|w\|_{H^2(\Omega)}.
$$

Substitute these choices in the [best approximation](../normed-vector-space.md#best-approximation-in-a-normed-space) bound from part (b):

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\left(\|u-u_h\|_1^2+\|w-w_h\|_1^2\right)^{1/2}&\leq C_*h,\\
C_*&=(1+K)\left(C_u^2\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}^2+C_w^2\|w\|_{H^2(\Omega)}^2\right)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

For fixed $u,w$, this constant is independent of $h$. A [shape-regular mesh](../finite-element-method.md#shape-regular-mesh) suffices; no uniformity of the individual element sizes is needed, since $h$ is their maximum diameter.

The inclusion of these [interpolants](../interpolation.md#interpolant) is an essential assumption. If the wording “consist of” were read as permitting arbitrary smaller [vector subspaces](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace), taking $\mathcal U_h=\mathcal W_h=\{0\}$ for every $h$ would prevent the claimed convergence for a nonzero solution. The full [conforming finite element space](../finite-element-method.md#conforming-finite-element-space) interpretation is also needed for the dimension assertion in part (d).

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

Let $x_1,\ldots,x_N$ be the interior nodes, and let $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_N$ be the nodal [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) of the common [conforming finite element space](../finite-element-method.md#conforming-finite-element-space), with $\phi_j(x_i)=\delta_{ij}$ and zero boundary values. These [piecewise-linear hat functions](../finite-element-method.md#piecewise-linear-hat-function) give the expansions

$$
u_h=\sum_{j=1}^N U_j\phi_j,\qquad w_h=\sum_{j=1}^N W_j\phi_j.
$$

Testing the first equation with $\phi_i$ and then the second equation with $\phi_i$ gives the [linear system](../linear-algebra.md#system-of-linear-equations)

$$
\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}S&-M\\M&S\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}U\\W\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\F\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S_{ij}=\int_\Omega\nabla\phi_j\cdot\nabla\phi_i,\quad
M_{ij}=\int_\Omega\phi_j\phi_i,\quad
F_i=\int_\Omega f\phi_i.}
$$

Here $S$ is the diffusion [stiffness matrix](../finite-element-method.md#stiffness-matrix) and $M$ the [mass matrix](../finite-element-method.md#mass-matrix). Their entries are symmetric in $i,j$, so both are [symmetric matrices](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-matrix).

For $c\in\mathbb R^N$, put $v_c=\sum_jc_j\phi_j$. The two [quadratic forms](../quadratic-form.md) are

$$
c^{\mathsf T}Mc=\|v_c\|_0^2,\qquad c^{\mathsf T}Sc=\|\nabla v_c\|_0^2.
$$

If $c\ne0$, the [linear independence](../vector-space.md#linear-independence) of the [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) ensures $v_c\ne0$, so $c^{\mathsf T}Mc>0$. If $c^{\mathsf T}Sc=0$, the [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) and zero [Dirichlet boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) force $v_c=0$, contradicting $c\ne0$. Thus both $S$ and $M$ are [positive-definite matrices](../quadratic-form.md#positive-definite-matrix).

For $X=(p,q)\in\mathbb R^N\times\mathbb R^N$, symmetry of $M$ cancels the cross terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
X^{\mathsf T}AX
&=p^{\mathsf T}Sp-p^{\mathsf T}Mq+q^{\mathsf T}Mp+q^{\mathsf T}Sq\\
&=p^{\mathsf T}Sp+q^{\mathsf T}Sq.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{X^{\mathsf T}AX>0\quad(X\ne0),\qquad \frac{A+A^{\mathsf T}}2=\begin{pmatrix}S&0\\0&S\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

This is the positivity of a [quadratic form](../quadratic-form.md) intended by the last sentence of the question. For $N>0$, $A$ is a [nonsymmetric matrix](../vector-space.md#nonsymmetric-matrix), since $M\ne0$. Under the convention that “[positive-definite matrix](../quadratic-form.md#positive-definite-matrix)” includes symmetry, that last sentence needs this qualification: the [symmetric part of a matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-part-of-a-matrix) is positive definite. The [quadratic lower bound for a nonsymmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#quadratic-lower-bound-for-a-nonsymmetric-matrix) still implies that $A$ is an [invertible matrix](../linear-algebra.md#invertible-matrix): $AX=0$ would give $X^{\mathsf T}AX=0$, hence $X=0$.

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Label the [polygon vertices](../euclidean-geometry.md#vertex-of-a-polygon) of the nondegenerate [triangle](../euclidean-geometry.md#triangle) $K$ as $a_1,a_2,a_3$, and put $m_{ij}=(a_i+a_j)/2$ for its three edge [midpoints](../convex-set.md#midpoint). The six [monomials](../polynomial.md#monomial) $1,x_1,x_2,x_1^2,x_1x_2,x_2^2$ form a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) of $\mathcal P_2(K)$, so its [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) is six. Nondegeneracy matters: $K$ has nonempty interior, and a [polynomial](../polynomial.md) vanishing on that interior is the zero [polynomial](../polynomial.md).

Let $\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3$ be the [affine functions](../vector-space.md#affine-function) of the [barycentric coordinate system](../algebraic-topology.md#barycentric-coordinate-system), characterized by $\lambda_i(a_j)=\delta_{ij}$ and $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$. Define six [quadratic polynomials](../polynomial.md#quadratic-polynomial)

$$
N_i=\lambda_i(2\lambda_i-1)\quad(i=1,2,3),\qquad
N_{ij}=4\lambda_i\lambda_j\quad(1\leq i<j\leq3).
$$

At $a_k$, $N_i(a_k)=\delta_{ik}$ and all $N_{ij}(a_k)$ vanish. At $m_{kl}$, exactly $\lambda_k$ and $\lambda_l$ equal $1/2$, while the third is zero. Thus every $N_i$ vanishes at all three edge [midpoints](../convex-set.md#midpoint), and $N_{ij}(m_{kl})$ is one for the matching unordered pair and zero otherwise.

These six [polynomials](../polynomial.md) have the cardinal property for the six [point evaluation functionals](../topological-vector-space.md#point-evaluation-functional). Evaluating any vanishing [linear combination](../vector-space.md#linear-combination) at the nodes forces each of its [coefficients](../vector-space.md#coefficient) to vanish. They are therefore six [linearly independent](../vector-space.md#linear-independence) members of a six-dimensional [vector space](../vector-space.md), hence a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis).

In particular, every $p\in\mathcal P_2(K)$ has the unique expansion

$$
\boxed{p=\sum_{i=1}^3p(a_i)\lambda_i(2\lambda_i-1)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq3}p(m_{ij})\,4\lambda_i\lambda_j.}
$$

This proves both existence and uniqueness for arbitrary six prescribed nodal values, which is precisely an [unisolvent finite element](../finite-element-method.md#unisolvent-finite-element). It also identifies the [finite element degrees of freedom](../finite-element-method.md#finite-element-degree-of-freedom) and shape functions of the [quadratic Lagrange triangle](../finite-element-method.md#quadratic-lagrange-triangle) explicitly.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Use the [Sobolev space with a partial Dirichlet condition](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-space-with-a-partial-dirichlet-condition)

$$
\boxed{V=\{v\in H^1(\Omega):Tv=0\text{ almost everywhere on }\Gamma_D\},}
$$

where $T$ is the [Sobolev trace operator](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator). The restricted [Sobolev trace operator](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator) is continuous into $L^2(\Gamma_D)$; its [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is consequently a [closed vector subspace](../vector-space.md#closed-vector-subspace) of $H^1(\Omega)$. Thus $V$, with the first-order [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm), is a [Hilbert space](../hilbert-space.md). The endpoint convention for $\Gamma_D$ and $\Gamma_N$ does not affect this definition: finitely many boundary points have zero boundary length. It would be inappropriate to impose pointwise boundary values on arbitrary $H^1$ functions in two dimensions.

For a smooth solution, [Green's first identity](../partial-differential-equation.md#green-s-first-identity) gives

$$
\int_\Omega(-\Delta u+u)v
=\int_\Omega(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+uv)-\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_nu\,Tv.
$$

The boundary term vanishes because $Tv=0$ on $\Gamma_D$ and the [normal derivative](../differential-geometry.md#normal-derivative) is zero on $\Gamma_N$. This suggests the [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation)

$$
\boxed{u\in V,\qquad a(u,v)=\ell(v)\quad(v\in V),\qquad
a(p,v)=(\nabla p,\nabla v)_0+(p,v)_0,\quad\ell(v)=(f,v)_0.}
$$

Here $a$ is exactly the first-order [Sobolev inner product](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-inner-product). In particular the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) gives

$$
|a(p,v)|\leq\|p\|_1\|v\|_1,\qquad a(v,v)=\|v\|_1^2,\qquad
|\ell(v)|\leq\|f\|_0\|v\|_1.
$$

Thus $a$ is a [bounded bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#bounded-bilinear-form) and a [coercive bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#coercive-bilinear-form), both with constant one. The [Lax-Milgram theorem](../functional-analysis.md#lax-milgram-theorem) proves existence and uniqueness, together with

$$
\boxed{\|u\|_1\leq\|f\|_0.}
$$

The positive reaction term already controls the entire [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm), so no [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) is needed here. The zero [Neumann boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition) is the natural part of the [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation); it is not an additional restriction on the test space. For this [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution), $-\Delta u+u=f$ holds as a [distributional identity](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-identity), and the zero [normal derivative](../differential-geometry.md#normal-derivative) on $\Gamma_N$ is understood through the corresponding [Green's first identity](../partial-differential-equation.md#green-s-first-identity).

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

For a conforming triangular [finite element mesh](../finite-element-method.md#finite-element-mesh), take

$$
V_h=\{v_h\in C^0(\overline\Omega):v_h|_K\in\mathcal P_2(K)\text{ for every }K\in\mathcal M_h,\quad v_h|_{\Gamma_D}=0\}.
$$

This is the quadratic [conforming finite element space](../finite-element-method.md#conforming-finite-element-space). Across a common [edge of a polygon](../euclidean-geometry.md#side-of-a-polygon), the restrictions from neighboring [triangles](../euclidean-geometry.md#triangle) are univariate [quadratic polynomials](../polynomial.md#quadratic-polynomial). Their agreement at the two endpoints and the [midpoint](../convex-set.md#midpoint) makes their difference a degree-at-most-two [polynomial](../polynomial.md) with three distinct [roots of a polynomial](../polynomial.md#root-of-a-polynomial), so it vanishes on the entire common [edge of a polygon](../euclidean-geometry.md#side-of-a-polygon). Thus matching the local [finite element degrees of freedom](../finite-element-method.md#finite-element-degree-of-freedom) produces the required continuity. Similarly, setting the three nodal values on every boundary edge carrying the [Dirichlet boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) to zero makes its entire restriction vanish.

Continuity across element interfaces means the piecewise [gradients](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) give the global [weak gradient](../distribution-theory.md#weak-gradient), with no jump contribution, so $V_h\subset V$. There are finitely many local [finite element degrees of freedom](../finite-element-method.md#finite-element-degree-of-freedom), hence $V_h$ is finite-dimensional. The [Galerkin method](../partial-differential-equation.md#galerkin-method) seeks

$$
\boxed{u_h\in V_h,\qquad (\nabla u_h,\nabla v_h)_0+(u_h,v_h)_0=(f,v_h)_0\quad(v_h\in V_h).}
$$

The [bounded bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#bounded-bilinear-form) and [coercive bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#coercive-bilinear-form) estimates from part (b) remain valid on $V_h$. Since a [finite-dimensional subspace is closed](../normed-vector-space.md#finite-dimensional-subspace-is-closed), the [Lax-Milgram theorem](../functional-analysis.md#lax-milgram-theorem) gives exactly one $u_h$. Alternatively, the [stiffness matrix](../finite-element-method.md#stiffness-matrix) plus the [mass matrix](../finite-element-method.md#mass-matrix) is a [positive-definite matrix](../quadratic-form.md#positive-definite-matrix) in any [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) of $V_h$. The same existence and uniqueness conclusion holds for any finite-dimensional [vector subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) of this full space.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

Let $e=u-u_h$. Subtracting the two [weak formulations](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) gives [Galerkin orthogonality](../finite-element-method.md#galerkin-orthogonality), $a(e,v_h)=0$ for every $v_h\in V_h$. Since $a$ is the first-order [Sobolev inner product](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-inner-product), for every $v_h\in V_h$ we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|e\|_1^2
&=a(e,u-v_h)+a(e,v_h-u_h)\\
&=a(e,u-v_h)\leq\|e\|_1\|u-v_h\|_1.
\end{aligned}
$$

If $e\ne0$, division proves $\|e\|_1\leq\|u-v_h\|_1$; if $e=0$, the same conclusion is immediate. Thus the [Céa lemma](../finite-element-method.md#cea-s-lemma) has constant one in this case.

Use the quadratic nodal [interpolant](../interpolation.md#interpolant) $I_hu$. The assumed $H^3(\Omega)$ regularity gives continuous nodal values by the [Sobolev embedding theorem](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-embedding-theorem), and its zero [Sobolev trace](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator) makes the nodal values on $\Gamma_D$ zero. The [quadratic Lagrange triangle](../finite-element-method.md#quadratic-lagrange-triangle) construction then gives $I_hu\in V_h$. The allowed quadratic [finite element interpolation estimate](../finite-element-method.md#finite-element-interpolation-estimate) on a [shape-regular mesh](../finite-element-method.md#shape-regular-mesh) is

$$
\|u-I_hu\|_1\leq C_Ih^2\|u\|_{H^3(\Omega)},
$$

where $C_I$ depends on the fixed [triangle](../euclidean-geometry.md#triangle) and [shape regularity](../finite-element-method.md#shape-regular-mesh), but not on $h$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\|u-u_h\|_{H^1(\Omega)}\leq\|u-I_hu\|_{H^1(\Omega)}\leq C_1h^2\|u\|_{H^3(\Omega)},\qquad C_1=C_I.}
$$

This step uses the inclusion $I_hu\in V_h$ explicitly permitted by the question. Arbitrary smaller [vector subspaces](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) without the same approximation property would not give this rate.

<h3 id="2/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#2/e)

Put $e=u-u_h\in V$. The [Aubin–Nitsche duality argument](../finite-element-method.md#aubin-nitsche-duality-argument) uses the dual [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation)

$$
z\in V,\qquad a(v,z)=(e,v)_0\quad(v\in V).
$$

By symmetry of $a$ and the [Lax-Milgram theorem](../functional-analysis.md#lax-milgram-theorem), this has a unique solution. Taking $v=z$ and using the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) shows

$$
\|z\|_1^2=(e,z)_0\leq\|e\|_0\|z\|_0\leq\|e\|_0\|z\|_1,
\qquad \|z\|_1\leq\|e\|_0.
$$

We must justify $H^2$ [elliptic regularity](../distribution-theory.md#elliptic-regularity) for this dual problem from the result actually supplied. That result concerns $\Delta$, whereas the dual equation is $-\Delta z+z=e$. Put $g=z-e\in L^2(\Omega)$. The given result produces $w\in H^2(\Omega)\cap V$ with $\Delta w=g$, zero [normal derivative](../differential-geometry.md#normal-derivative) on $\Gamma_N$, and

$$
\|w\|_{H^2(\Omega)}\leq C_3\|z-e\|_0\leq2C_3\|e\|_0.
$$

By [Green's first identity](../partial-differential-equation.md#green-s-first-identity), for every $v\in V$,

$$
(\nabla w,\nabla v)_0=-(g,v)_0=(e-z,v)_0=(\nabla z,\nabla v)_0.
$$

Set $v=w-z$. Then $\nabla(w-z)=0$. On the connected [triangle](../euclidean-geometry.md#triangle), a [Sobolev function with zero weak gradient](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-function-with-zero-weak-gradient) is constant, and its zero [Sobolev trace](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator) on $\Gamma_D$ forces that constant to be zero. Hence $z=w$, proving

$$
\boxed{z\in H^2(\Omega)\cap V,\qquad\|z\|_{H^2(\Omega)}\leq2C_3\|e\|_0.}
$$

This proves the needed [reaction-diffusion regularity from Laplacian regularity](../distribution-theory.md#reaction-diffusion-regularity-from-laplacian-regularity) without assuming the dual solution already has second [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) in the [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space).

Only $H^2$ regularity is available for $z$. Use its continuous [piecewise-linear](../function.md#piecewise-linear-function) [interpolant](../interpolation.md#interpolant) $L_hz$, which belongs to the quadratic [conforming finite element space](../finite-element-method.md#conforming-finite-element-space) $V_h$ because $\mathcal P_1(K)\subset\mathcal P_2(K)$. It has the same zero [Dirichlet boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) and satisfies the [finite element interpolation estimate](../finite-element-method.md#finite-element-interpolation-estimate)

$$
\|z-L_hz\|_1\leq C_Lh\|z\|_{H^2(\Omega)}.
$$

Thus no $H^3$ assumption on the dual solution is necessary. Taking $v=e$ in the dual [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) and using [Galerkin orthogonality](../finite-element-method.md#galerkin-orthogonality) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|e\|_0^2
&=a(e,z)=a(e,z-L_hz)\\
&\leq\|e\|_1\|z-L_hz\|_1\\
&\leq2C_3C_Lh\|e\|_1\|e\|_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

If $\|e\|_0=0$, the desired bound holds. Otherwise divide by $\|e\|_0$ and use part (d):

$$
\boxed{\|u-u_h\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\leq2C_3C_Lh\|u-u_h\|_{H^1(\Omega)}\leq C_2h^3\|u\|_{H^3(\Omega)},\qquad C_2=2C_3C_LC_1.}
$$

All constants depend only on the fixed [triangle](../euclidean-geometry.md#triangle) and [shape regularity](../finite-element-method.md#shape-regular-mesh). The acute-angle assumption enters through the supplied mixed-boundary [elliptic regularity](../distribution-theory.md#elliptic-regularity) result.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

The [infinitesimal strain tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#infinitesimal-strain-tensor) $\varepsilon(v)=\tfrac12(\nabla v+(\nabla v)^{\mathsf T})$ and the [divergence](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence) are linear in $v$. Consequently the given $a$ is a [symmetric bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#symmetric-bilinear-form), and

$$
a(v,v)=2\mu\|\varepsilon(v)\|_0^2+\lambda\|\operatorname{div}v\|_0^2\geq0.
$$

For a matrix-valued [function](../function.md), $\|\cdot\|_0$ here uses the [Frobenius inner product](../inner-product.md#frobenius-inner-product) pointwise and then the [L2 norm](../real-analysis.md#l2-norm). All expressions are finite for $v\in H^1(\Omega;\mathbb R^3)$.

Expand the quadratic term at $\theta v+(1-\theta)w$. The [linearity](../vector-space.md#linearity) of $\ell$ cancels its contribution to the difference, giving

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta J(v)+(1-\theta)J(w)-J(\theta v+(1-\theta)w)
&=\frac{\theta(1-\theta)}2\bigl(a(v,v)-2a(v,w)+a(w,w)\bigr)\\
&=\frac{\theta(1-\theta)}2a(v-w,v-w)\\
&=\theta(1-\theta)\left(\mu\|\varepsilon(v-w)\|_0^2+\frac\lambda2\|\operatorname{div}(v-w)\|_0^2\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

For $0\leq\theta\leq1$, this is nonnegative. Therefore

$$
\boxed{J(\theta v+(1-\theta)w)\leq\theta J(v)+(1-\theta)J(w),}
$$

so $J$ is a [convex functional](../real-analysis.md#convex-functional) on the whole [Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md). No boundary condition is needed for this conclusion. The argument uses nonnegativity of the quadratic term; uniqueness on the [zero-boundary Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md#zero-boundary-sobolev-space) will instead use the [Korn inequality](../sobolev-space.md#korn-inequality) in part (b).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Write $V=H_0^1(\Omega;\mathbb R^3)$. For any $v\in V$ and any real $t$, $u+tv$ is admissible. Expanding the functional gives

$$
J(u+tv)-J(u)=t\bigl(a(u,v)-\ell(v)\bigr)+\frac{t^2}2a(v,v).
$$

Since the left side is nonnegative for positive and negative $t$, its [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) at zero vanishes. Thus the [first variation](../calculus-of-variations.md#first-variation) gives the [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation)

$$
\boxed{u\in V,\qquad a(u,v)=\ell(v)\quad(v\in V).}
$$

To identify its differential equation, define the [stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor)

$$
\sigma(u)=2\mu\varepsilon(u)+\lambda(\operatorname{div}u)I.
$$

This is a [symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-matrix) field. The [Frobenius inner product](../inner-product.md#frobenius-inner-product) of a [symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-matrix) with a [skew-symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#skew-symmetric-matrix) is zero, so $\sigma(u):\nabla v=\sigma(u):\varepsilon(v)$. Since $\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon(v)=\operatorname{div}v$, we obtain

$$
a(u,v)=\int_\Omega\sigma(u):\nabla v.
$$

For smooth compactly supported [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function), [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) in each row gives

$$
\int_\Omega\sigma(u):\nabla v=-\langle\operatorname{div}\sigma(u),v\rangle.
$$

Hence $-\operatorname{div}\sigma(u)=f$ as a [distributional identity](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-identity), that is,

$$
-2\mu\operatorname{div}\varepsilon(u)-\lambda\nabla(\operatorname{div}u)=f.
$$

Membership in $V$ supplies the zero [Sobolev trace](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator). This identifies precisely the required [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) of the [linear elasticity](../elasticity-physics.md#linear-elasticity) problem, with row-wise [divergence](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence).

For uniqueness, use the exact form of the [Korn inequality](../sobolev-space.md#korn-inequality) supplied in the paper. Put $E=\varepsilon(v)$ and $d=\operatorname{tr}E=\operatorname{div}v$. The [deviatoric strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#deviatoric-strain) is $D=E-\tfrac13dI$. Because $\operatorname{tr}D=0$ and $I:I=3$, [orthogonality](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) for the [Frobenius inner product](../inner-product.md#frobenius-inner-product) gives

$$
|E|_F^2=|D|_F^2+\frac13d^2,\qquad |D|_F^2\leq|E|_F^2.
$$

If $C_K$ is the supplied [Korn inequality](../sobolev-space.md#korn-inequality) constant and $K$ the permitted [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) constant, then

$$
\|v\|_1^2\leq(1+K)\|\nabla v\|_0^2
\leq(1+K)C_K\|D\|_0^2
\leq(1+K)C_K\|\varepsilon(v)\|_0^2.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{a(v,v)\geq\alpha\|v\|_1^2,\qquad\alpha=\frac{2\mu}{(1+K)C_K}>0.}
$$

If $u_1,u_2$ are two [weak solutions](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution), their difference $d_u\in V$ satisfies $a(d_u,v)=0$ for every $v\in V$. Choosing $v=d_u$ and using this [coercive bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#coercive-bilinear-form) estimate forces $d_u=0$. The minimizer therefore gives the unique [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution).

For later use, $|\varepsilon(v)|_F\leq|\nabla v|_F$ and $|\operatorname{div}v|\leq\sqrt3|\nabla v|_F$ also give the [bounded bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#bounded-bilinear-form) and [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) estimates

$$
|a(p,v)|\leq(2\mu+3\lambda)\|p\|_1\|v\|_1,\qquad
|\ell(v)|\leq\|f\|_0\|v\|_1.
$$

Together with the [coercive bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#coercive-bilinear-form) estimate and the [Lax-Milgram theorem](../functional-analysis.md#lax-milgram-theorem), these would establish existence independently of the assumed minimizer.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

The finite-dimensional [vector subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) $V_h\subset V$ is closed, so it is a [Hilbert space](../hilbert-space.md) with the inherited [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm). The [bounded bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#bounded-bilinear-form), [coercive bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#coercive-bilinear-form), and bounded [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) estimates from part (b) restrict to $V_h$. Applying the [Lax-Milgram theorem](../functional-analysis.md#lax-milgram-theorem) proves

$$
\boxed{\text{There is exactly one }u_h\in V_h\text{ with }a(u_h,v_h)=\ell(v_h)\quad(v_h\in V_h).}
$$

For any $v_h\in V_h$, put $d_h=v_h-u_h\in V_h$. Expansion using symmetry of the [bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
J(v_h)-J(u_h)
&=a(u_h,d_h)-\ell(d_h)+\frac12a(d_h,d_h)\\
&=\frac12a(d_h,d_h)\geq0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $u_h$ minimizes $J$ on $V_h$, uniquely by the [coercive bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#coercive-bilinear-form) estimate. Similarly, the continuous [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) gives the exact identity

$$
J(u_h)-J(u)=\frac12a(u_h-u,u_h-u)\geq0.
$$

Combining the two identities proves

$$
\boxed{J(v_h)\geq J(u_h)\geq J(u)\qquad(v_h\in V_h).}
$$

More precisely, $J(v_h)-J(u_h)=\tfrac12\|v_h-u_h\|_a^2$ and $J(u_h)-J(u)=\tfrac12\|u_h-u\|_a^2$, with the [energy norm](../finite-element-method.md#energy-norm) justified next. These identities connect the energy excess directly to the squared error.

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

On $V$, the [bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md) $a$ is symmetric, and part (b) proves $a(v,v)\geq\alpha\|v\|_1^2$. Thus $a$ is an [inner product](../inner-product.md): $a(v,v)\geq0$, and equality forces $v=0$. Its induced [energy norm](../finite-element-method.md#energy-norm) is $\|v\|_a=\sqrt{a(v,v)}$. For any real scalar $t$,

$$
\|tv\|_a=|t|\|v\|_a.
$$

The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) for this [inner product](../inner-product.md) follows directly by expanding $a(p-tq,p-tq)\geq0$ and minimizing over $t$ when $q\ne0$. Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|p+q\|_a^2
&=\|p\|_a^2+2a(p,q)+\|q\|_a^2\\
&\leq\bigl(\|p\|_a+\|q\|_a\bigr)^2,
\end{aligned}
$$

which proves the [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality). This verifies every [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) axiom. In fact, the estimates from part (b) give the [equivalent norms](../normed-vector-space.md#equivalent-norms)

$$
\boxed{\sqrt\alpha\|v\|_1\leq\|v\|_a\leq\sqrt{2\mu+3\lambda}\|v\|_1.}
$$

Subtract the discrete [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) from the continuous one to get [Galerkin orthogonality](../finite-element-method.md#galerkin-orthogonality),

$$
a(u-u_h,v_h)=0\qquad(v_h\in V_h).
$$

For any $v_h\in V_h$, write $u-v_h=(u-u_h)+(u_h-v_h)$. The second summand is in $V_h$, so expansion and [Galerkin orthogonality](../finite-element-method.md#galerkin-orthogonality) give the [Pythagorean theorem](../euclidean-geometry.md#pythagorean-theorem) in the [energy norm](../finite-element-method.md#energy-norm):

$$
\boxed{\|u-v_h\|_a^2=\|u-u_h\|_a^2+\|u_h-v_h\|_a^2.}
$$

The last term is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when $v_h=u_h$. Choosing $v_h=u_h$ attains the lower bound, proving

$$
\boxed{\|u-u_h\|_a=\min_{v_h\in V_h}\|u-v_h\|_a.}
$$

Thus $u_h$ is the unique [best approximation](../normed-vector-space.md#best-approximation-in-a-normed-space); the map $u\mapsto u_h$ is the [orthogonal projector](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection) onto $V_h$ for the [linear elasticity](../elasticity-physics.md#linear-elasticity) [energy norm](../finite-element-method.md#energy-norm).
