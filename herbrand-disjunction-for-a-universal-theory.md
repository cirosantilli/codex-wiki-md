# Herbrand disjunction for a universal theory

↑ **Parent:** [Universal first-order theory](universal-first-order-theory.md)

If a universal [first-order theory](first-order-theory.md) in a [first-order language](first-order-language.md) with a constant entails an [existential sentence](existential-sentence.md) with quantifier-free matrix, it entails a finite disjunction of closed-term instances of that matrix. Otherwise a [first-order model](model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of the [first-order theory](first-order-theory.md) and all negated ground instances would have a [ground-term substructure](ground-term-substructure.md) satisfying the [first-order theory](first-order-theory.md). An [existential witness](existential-witness.md) there is a [tuple](finite-tuple.md) of closed-term values, contradicting its negated ground instance. [Compactness theorem](compactness-theorem.md) extracts finitely many instances.
