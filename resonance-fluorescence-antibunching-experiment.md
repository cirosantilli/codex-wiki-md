# Resonance fluorescence antibunching experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum optics](quantum-optics-split.md)

In 1977, [H. Jeff Kimble](harry-jeffrey-kimble.md), [Mario Dagenais](mario-dagenais.md), and [Leonard Mandel](leonard-mandel.md) excited a dilute [sodium](sodium.md) atomic beam with a resonant [dye laser](dye-laser.md). Apertures restricted the observed atoms; a [microscope objective](microscope-objective.md) collected [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) perpendicular to both beams. A [beam splitter](beam-splitter.md) sent the collected [light](light.md) to two [photomultipliers](photomultiplier.md). Shaped pulses drove the start and stop inputs of a time-to-digital converter; its delay histogram rose away from zero delay, indicating [photon antibunching](photon-antibunching.md). Turning the atomic beam off measured scattered-laser backgrounds. More than one contributing atom and scattered [light](light.md) limited the comparison with an ideal isolated [two-level system](two-level-system.md); the paper explicitly treated corrections.[1977, p. 693, figure 2, equation (6), apparatus and atomic-beam-off/on control; MIT mirror](photon-antibunching-in-resonance-fluorescence-by-harry-jeffrey-kimble-and-others.md)

A simple reason for suppressed immediate re-emission is visible in an ideal [two-level system](two-level-system.md). After [spontaneous emission](spontaneous-emission.md), the atom is in $|g\rangle$. Its emission operator $\sigma_-=|g\rangle\langle e|$ obeys $\sigma_-^2=0$, so two instantaneous emissions are impossible. Excitation must rebuild the excited-state population before another emission. This argument explains the zero-delay idealization; the actual [photon counting](photon-counting.md) record additionally depends on collection, background, timing, and the number of contributing atoms.
