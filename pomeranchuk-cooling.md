# Pomeranchuk cooling

↑ **Parent:** [Helium-3 superfluidity](helium-3-superfluidity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranchuk_cooling)

Low-[temperature](temperature-split.md) [solid](solid.md) [helium-3](helium-3.md) can have greater [entropy](entropy-split.md) than the [liquid](liquid.md) through disordered nuclear [spins](spin.md), enabling compression cooling. [Richardson, 1996 lecture, p.91, introduction and Fig.1](the-pomeranchuk-effect-by-robert-coleman-richardson.md)

The sign follows from the [Clapeyron equation](clausius-clapeyron-relation.md). Let $S_l,S_s$ be molar [entropies](entropy-split.md) and $V_l,V_s$ [molar volumes](molar-volume.md) of [liquid](liquid.md) and [solid](solid.md), respectively, on their [phase coexistence curve](phase-coexistence-curve.md). Equality of their [chemical potentials](chemical-potential-split.md), with $d\mu=-S\,dT+V\,dP$, gives

$$
\frac{dP}{dT}=\frac{S_l-S_s}{V_l-V_s}.
$$

With $S_s>S_l$ and $V_l>V_s$, the slope is negative. Reversibly [freezing](freezing.md) one mole at fixed [temperature](temperature-split.md) requires [heat](heat-split.md) into the sample,

$$
Q_{l\to s}=T(S_s-S_l)>0,
\qquad L_{s\to l}=T(S_l-S_s)<0.
$$

Here $L_{s\to l}$ uses the melting convention for [latent heat of fusion](latent-heat-of-fusion.md). This is the opposite sign from ordinary [ice](ice.md) melting. The inequalities specify the regime; [freezing](freezing.md) does not universally cool a substance. In an approximately isolated sample, the growing higher-[entropy](entropy-split.md) phase draws on the sample's thermal [energy](energy-split.md) instead of an external heater. Irreversible losses and incoming [heat](heat-split.md) oppose the cooling.
