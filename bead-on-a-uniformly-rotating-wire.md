# Bead on a uniformly rotating wire

↑ **Parent:** [Classical mechanics](classical-mechanics-split.md)

A smooth wire has instantaneous shape $z=f(r)$ in [cylindrical coordinates](cylindrical-coordinate-system.md) and rotates at constant [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) $\dot\theta=\omega$. The [tangent vector](tangent-vector-split.md) at fixed time is $\mathbf e_r+f'(r)\mathbf e_z$. Its [dot product](dot-product.md) with the [constraint force](constraint-force.md) vanishes. Projecting [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) along this [tangent vector](tangent-vector-split.md) gives the equation in the title.

Multiplying that equation by $\dot r$ proves conservation of

$$
\mathcal H=\frac12(1+f'(r)^2)\dot r^2+U(r),\qquad
U(r)=gf(r)-\frac12\omega^2r^2.
$$

This is a [conserved quantity](conserved-quantity.md) in the rotating description; the motor can exchange ordinary [mechanical energy](mechanical-energy.md) with the bead. A relative [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) at $a>0$ satisfies $U'(a)=gf'(a)-\omega^2a=0$. Its [linearized equation](linearized-equation.md) is $(1+f'(a)^2)\ddot\epsilon=-U''(a)\epsilon$. A strict local minimum with $U''(a)>0$ gives [Lyapunov stability](lyapunov-stability.md), while $U''(a)<0$ gives an [unstable equilibrium](unstable-equilibrium.md). If $U''(a)=0$, higher orders are needed.
