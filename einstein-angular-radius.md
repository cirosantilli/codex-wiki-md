# Einstein angular radius

↑ **Parent:** [Einstein radius](einstein-radius.md)

The [Einstein angular radius](einstein-angular-radius.md) is the angular form of the [Einstein radius](einstein-radius.md): $R_E=D_l\theta_E$ in the lens plane. Its displayed value applies to a [point-mass gravitational lens](point-mass-gravitational-lens.md) with the [angular diameter distances](angular-diameter-distance.md) defined above. It depends on source distance as well as lens [mass](mass.md) and position. At fixed distances, multiplying $M$ by four doubles $\theta_E$.

At exact alignment, the vector [thin gravitational lens equation](thin-gravitational-lens-equation.md) is $\boldsymbol0=(1-\theta_E^2/r^2)\boldsymbol\theta$. For a nonzero image direction it requires $r=\theta_E$ and leaves the direction unrestricted: all azimuths form an [Einstein ring](einstein-ring.md). The signed equation's roots $\theta=\pm\theta_E$ are only a diameter cut through that circle. A source of finite angular size makes an annulus or arcs with finite received light; the infinite point-source [lensing magnification](lensing-magnification.md) at alignment is an idealization. An [Einstein ring](einstein-ring.md) expresses rotational symmetry and source alignment, not a circular arrangement of emitting stars.
