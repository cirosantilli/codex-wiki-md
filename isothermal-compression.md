# Isothermal compression

↑ **Parent:** [Isothermal process](isothermal-process.md)

An [isothermal compression](isothermal-compression.md) reduces the [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) of a [thermodynamic system](thermodynamic-system.md) at fixed [temperature](temperature.md). For a fixed-composition [ideal gas](ideal-gas.md) compressed reversibly from $V_i$ to $V_f<V_i$, [isothermal ideal-gas work](isothermal-ideal-gas-work.md) gives

$$
W_{\rm by,rev}=nRT\ln(V_f/V_i)<0,\qquad
Q=W_{\rm by,rev}<0,\qquad \Delta U=0.
$$

The surroundings supply positive [work](work.md) $W_{\rm on}$, and the [gas](gas.md) rejects the same amount of [heat](heat.md) to the [heat bath](heat-bath.md). Compressing $1\,\mathrm{mol}$ at $300\,\mathrm K$ from $20$ to $10\,\mathrm L$ requires approximately $1.729\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of [work](work.md) input and rejects $1.729\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of [heat](heat.md). If that [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) cannot keep up, the [temperature](temperature.md) rises: the process departs from [isothermal compression](isothermal-compression.md) toward [adiabatic compression](adiabatic-compression.md). The [finite-rate isothermal piston model](finite-rate-isothermal-piston-model.md) explains the required small [temperature](temperature.md) excess.
