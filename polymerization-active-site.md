# Polymerization-active site

↑ **Parent:** [Chain-growth polymerization](chain-growth-polymerization.md)

A [polymerization-active site](polymerization-active-site.md) is the reactive location that enables the next [chain-growth propagation](chain-growth-propagation.md) step. In the schematic

$$
P_j^*+M\longrightarrow P_{j+1}^*,
$$

$M$ is one free [monomer molecule](monomer-molecule.md), $j$ is the chain's [degree of polymerization](degree-of-polymerization.md) in incorporated [monomeric units](monomeric-unit.md), and the star marks the [polymerization-active site](polymerization-active-site.md). The site's reactive character survives the growth step, although the [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) carrying it can change. The star is not an extra unit, a literal particle, or necessarily a positive or negative [electric charge](electric-charge.md). Actual [reaction mechanisms](reaction-mechanism.md) can involve an unpaired [Electron](electron.md), a [cation](cation.md), an [anion](anion.md), or a metal-associated site; the abstract notation does not identify which. [John McMurry, Organic Chemistry, §31.1: successive reactive intermediates and radical, cationic and anionic initiation](organic-chemistry-by-john-mcmurry/31-1-chain-growth-polymers.md)

For bookkeeping below, each linear active chain has exactly one [polymerization-active site](polymerization-active-site.md). This is a stipulated model, not a property of every [chain polymerization](chain-growth-polymerization.md): a chain can have several reactive sites, and a reversibly inactive chain can remain capable of future growth. Count currently active sites separately from all chains capable of later reactivation.
