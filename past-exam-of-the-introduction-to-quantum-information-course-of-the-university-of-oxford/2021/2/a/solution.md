<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Each local [reduced density operator](../../../../reduced-density-matrix.md) of the [Bell state](../../../../bell-state-split.md) is $I/2$. Alice's unrecorded local measurement leaves Bob's reduced state unchanged: he still sees a fair random bit. Only after learning her outcome through a classical channel can he identify the corresponding conditional state. Alice cannot choose the random outcome to encode a bit, so the correlations obey the [no-signalling theorem](../../../../quantum-no-signalling.md).

If the shared state is the trusted pure Bell state, measuring both sides in the $Z$ basis gives identical, uniformly random raw bits. A pure joint state of Alice and Bob has no correlations with an external system, so these bits can form a shared secret. In an actual [quantum key distribution](../../../../quantum-key-distribution.md) protocol, the parties must verify the necessary state/device assumptions and authenticate their classical communication; correlation alone, without a security test, does not certify secrecy against an untrusted source.
