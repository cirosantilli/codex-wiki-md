# Polynomial remainder theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Remainder of polynomial division](remainder-of-polynomial-division.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_remainder_theorem)

Dividing a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) $f$ by $X-a$ leaves the constant [polynomial remainder](remainder-of-polynomial-division.md) $f(a)$. [Polynomial division](polynomial-division.md) makes the [polynomial remainder](remainder-of-polynomial-division.md) constant because its [polynomial degree](degree-of-a-polynomial.md) must be below one; [polynomial evaluation](polynomial-evaluation.md) at $a$ identifies that constant. In particular, it is zero exactly when $a$ is a [root of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) of $f$, which is the [factor theorem](factor-theorem.md).[Fields and Galois Theory, version 5.10, Chapter 1, p. 10, §1.7: division by X−a and evaluation of the constant remainder](fields-and-galois-theory-by-james-s-milne.md)

In the worked example, division by $X-1$ leaves $6$ and division by $X+2$ leaves $12$. Division by their product leaves $-2X+8$, a [polynomial remainder](remainder-of-polynomial-division.md) that stores both values at once. The argument also holds over a [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md) for $a$ in that [ring](ring.md), because $X-a$ is a [monic polynomial](monic-polynomial.md).
