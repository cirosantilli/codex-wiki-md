# Fourier orthogonality

↑ **Parent:** [Fourier series](fourier-series-split.md)

The complex [exponential functions](exponential-function.md) $e^{int}$, for integers $n$, are orthogonal under the normalized integral [inner product](inner-product.md) on a full period. If $n=m$ the integrand is one; otherwise integrating the exponential gives zero because its endpoint values coincide. Sine and cosine orthogonality follow by taking real and imaginary parts.
