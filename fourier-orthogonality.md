# Fourier orthogonality

↑ **Parent:** [Fourier series](fourier-series-split.md)

The [complex exponential functions](complex-exponential-function.md) $e^{int}$, for [integers](integer-split.md) $n$, are [orthogonal](orthogonal-vectors.md) under the normalized [integral](integral-split.md) [inner product](inner-product-split.md) over a complete [period](period-of-a-function.md). If $n=m$, the integrand $e^{i(n-m)t}$ is one; otherwise its [antiderivative](antiderivative.md) has equal endpoint values, giving zero. For the real modes, $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos^2(nt)\,dt=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin^2(nt)\,dt=\pi$ for $n\geq1$, while every mixed sine-cosine integral and every unequal-frequency pairing vanishes.

This makes the coefficient formulas useful: multiply a [trigonometric polynomial](trigonometric-polynomial.md) by one mode and integrate, and all other modes cancel. For $f(x)=1/2+\cos x+\tfrac12\sin(2x)$, integration against $\cos x$ gives $\pi$, and against $\sin(2x)$ gives $\pi/2$, recovering $a_1=1$ and $b_2=1/2$. Division by the squared [norm](norm.md) of the mode supplies the normalization. A full [period](period-of-a-function.md) matters; truncating the integration interval generally destroys these cancellations.
