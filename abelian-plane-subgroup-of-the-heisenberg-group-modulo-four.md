# Abelian plane subgroup of the Heisenberg group modulo four

↑ **Parent:** [Heisenberg group over a commutative ring](heisenberg-group-over-a-commutative-ring.md)

In the [Heisenberg group over a commutative ring](heisenberg-group-over-a-commutative-ring.md) with $R=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$, the subset $H=\{A(t,y,t):t,y\in R\}$ is an [Abelian subgroup](abelian-subgroup.md) of [group order](order-of-a-finite-group.md) 16. Its product has central coordinate $y+y'+tt'$, symmetric in the two factors. Its [inverse element](inverse-element.md) is $A(-t,t^2-y,-t)$. The map $A(x,y,z)\mapsto x-z$ is a surjective [group homomorphism](group-homomorphism.md) to the additive group of $R$, with [kernel of a group homomorphism](kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) $H$. Thus $H$ is a [normal subgroup](normal-subgroup.md) and has four [cosets](coset.md) in the 64-element [group](group-split.md).

Unlike the odd-prime case, division by two is unavailable. In fact take $u=A(1,0,1)$ and $v=A(2,0,2)$. Then $u^n=A(n,\binom n2,n)$, where the [binomial coefficient](binomial-coefficient-split.md) is computed as an [integer](integer-split.md) before reduction modulo four. Hence $u^4=A(0,2,0)$ and $u$ has [element order](order-of-a-group-element.md) eight. The element $v$ has [element order](order-of-a-group-element.md) two and is not a power of $u$: the powers with outer coordinates two have central coordinates one or three. Their generated [subgroups](subgroup.md) intersect trivially, so the 16-element [Abelian group](abelian-group.md) is the [direct product of groups](direct-product-of-groups.md) $C_8\times C_2$.
