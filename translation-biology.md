# Translation (biology)

↑ **Parent:** [Gene expression](gene-expression.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_(biology))

[Biological translation](translation-biology.md) uses a [ribosome](ribosome.md) to read [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) [codons](codon.md) $5'\to3'$ and assemble the corresponding [amino acid](amino-acid.md) chain. [Transfer RNA](transfer-rna.md) adaptors match [codons](codon.md) through [base pairing](base-pairing.md) and carry the [amino acids](amino-acid.md); the [genetic code](genetic-code.md) supplies the correspondence. The chain grows from the [N-terminus](n-terminus.md) toward the [C-terminus](c-terminus.md), by adding each new residue at the [C-terminus](c-terminus.md). See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e: ribosomes, transfer RNA and protein synthesis](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/15-5-ribosomes-and-protein-synthesis).

In the [gene expression](gene-expression.md) diagram, read $5'\text{-}\mathrm{AUG\ GCU\ UAA}\text{-}3'$ in the chosen [reading frame](reading-frame.md). Under the standard [genetic code](genetic-code.md), AUG specifies [methionine](methionine.md) and GCU specifies [alanine](alanine.md); UAA is a [stop codon](stop-codon.md). The result before any subsequent processing is the two-residue chain N–[methionine](methionine.md)–[alanine](alanine.md)–C, followed by termination. The [stop codon](stop-codon.md) contributes zero [amino acids](amino-acid.md). See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e: the standard genetic code](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/15-1-the-genetic-code). This example assumes appropriate initiation at the displayed AUG in a suitable context in a [eukaryote](eukaryote.md); the diagram displays the coding segment, not a complete independently translatable [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md).

Initiation establishes the starting position and [reading frame](reading-frame.md), using a [ribosome](ribosome.md), an initiating [transfer RNA](transfer-rna.md) and additional factors. At a standard [stop codon](stop-codon.md), a release factor promotes release of the chain rather than delivery of another [amino acid](amino-acid.md) by [transfer RNA](transfer-rna.md). See [Clark, Douglas and Choi: initiation and termination of translation](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/15-5-ribosomes-and-protein-synthesis). A short two-residue example illustrates decoding, not the size or folding of a typical functional [protein](protein-split.md).

The useful connection is between two different alphabets: [nucleotides](nucleotide.md) specify the order of [amino acids](amino-acid.md) through the [genetic code](genetic-code.md). [Base pairing](base-pairing.md) helps select [transfer RNA](transfer-rna.md), while the [ribosome](ribosome.md) catalyzes joining the delivered [amino acids](amino-acid.md). The [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) remains a template rather than being consumed as the chain's building material. Separating template, adaptor and product makes clear why [transcription](transcription-biology.md) and [biological translation](translation-biology.md) are distinct mechanisms.
