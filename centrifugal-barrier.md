# Centrifugal barrier

↑ **Parent:** [Effective potential](effective-potential.md)

For a central [gravitational potential](newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md) and nonzero [specific angular momentum](specific-angular-momentum.md) $L$, the positive $L^2/(2r^2)$ contribution to the radial [effective potential](effective-potential.md) can prevent the orbit reaching the centre at finite energy. It dominates the attractive $-\mu/r$ term at small $r$ in a [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md). A more singular attractive term may overcome it: for $\Phi=-\mu/r-\mu a/r^2$, the net inverse-square coefficient is $(L^2-2\mu a)/2$, so a central barrier exists only when that coefficient is positive.
