# Hypersurface orthogonality

↑ **Parent:** [Frobenius theorem](frobenius-theorem.md)

A nowhere-zero [differential one-form](one-form.md) $n$ is hypersurface orthogonal if locally $n=F\,dt$ with $F\ne0$. Its kernel consists of tangent vectors to the level hypersurfaces of $t$. [Frobenius theorem](frobenius-theorem.md) makes this equivalent to $n\wedge dn=0$, where $d$ is the [exterior derivative](exterior-derivative.md). With a torsion-free [Levi-Civita connection](levi-civita-connection.md), this is $n_{[\alpha}\nabla_\beta n_{\gamma]}=0$. For a timelike [unit normal](unit-normal.md), projecting the derivative on both indices gives zero antisymmetric part, which explains why [hypersurface orthogonality implies symmetric extrinsic curvature](hypersurface-orthogonality-implies-symmetric-extrinsic-curvature.md).
