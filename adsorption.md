# Adsorption

↑ **Parent:** [Physical chemistry](physical-chemistry-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adsorption)

[Adsorption](adsorption.md) is accumulation of a substance at an interface. Binding a [solute](solute.md) to a packing surface can delay its passage through a [chromatography](chromatography-split.md) column. Ideal [size-exclusion chromatography](size-exclusion-chromatography.md) separates through [SEC pore access](sec-pore-access.md) without such binding; extra retention from [adsorption](adsorption.md) requires a different interpretation.

For a [gas](gas.md) contacting a [solid](solid.md), [adsorption](adsorption.md) concentrates [molecules](molecule-split.md) at the boundary; [absorption](absorption-chemistry.md) takes material into the bulk. A porous [solid](solid.md) can have a large internal [surface area](surface-area.md): binding on pore walls is still [adsorption](adsorption.md), even though those walls lie inside the particle. Both processes can occur together. [Desorption](desorption.md) returns adsorbed material to the surrounding phase. The useful question is therefore not just how much material disappeared from the [gas](gas.md), but where it went and whether it returns when the [pressure](pressure-split.md) falls.

An [adsorption site](adsorption-site.md) model turns this spatial distinction into counting. With twelve identical [adsorption sites](adsorption-site.md) and three bound [molecules](molecule-split.md), the [surface coverage](surface-coverage.md) is $\theta=3/12=1/4$. Filling six gives $\theta=1/2$; filling twelve gives a complete [monolayer](monolayer.md). These are configurations illustrating the count, rather than measured arrangements or claims that all real surfaces have twelve sites.
