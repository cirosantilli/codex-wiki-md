# Random-walk maximum limit from Donsker invariance

↑ **Parent:** [Donsker's theorem](donsker-s-theorem.md)

For a [random walk](random-walk.md) with centered [independent](independent-random-variables.md) identically distributed increments of [variance](variance-split.md) one, the maximum of its polygonal interpolation is the maximum at its grid vertices. The maximum functional is Lipschitz for the [supremum norm](supremum-norm.md), so the [continuous mapping theorem](continuous-mapping-theorem.md) applied to the [Donsker invariance principle](donsker-s-theorem.md) proves the displayed [convergence in distribution](convergence-in-distribution.md). The [Brownian reflection principle](reflection-principle-wiener-process.md) then gives upper-tail limits $2(1-\Phi(a))$ at all $a>0$, since the limiting maximum has no atom there.
