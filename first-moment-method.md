# First moment method

↑ **Parent:** [Probability inequality](probability-inequality-split.md)

The [first moment method](first-moment-method.md) uses the [expected value](expected-value.md) of a count to control whether any counted occurrence exists. For a [random variable](random-variable-split.md) $N$ taking nonnegative [integer](integer.md) values, [Markov's inequality](markov-s-inequality.md) gives

$$
\mathbb P(N\geq1)\leq\mathbb EN.
$$

Consequently, $\mathbb EN<1$ implies $\mathbb P(N=0)\geq1-\mathbb EN>0$, proving that an outcome with no counted occurrence exists. For a sequence of such counts, $\mathbb EN\to0$ shows that $N=0$ with [probability](probability.md) tending to one. The [first moment method](first-moment-method.md) uses information about the average count, while the complementary [second moment method](second-moment-method.md) also uses its [variance](variance-split.md).
