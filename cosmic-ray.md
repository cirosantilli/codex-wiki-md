# Cosmic ray

↑ **Parent:** [Interstellar medium](interstellar-medium.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray)

A cosmic ray is a high-energy charged particle propagating through space. Galactic cosmic-ray electrons emit synchrotron and inverse-Compton radiation, while cosmic-ray nuclei produce gamma rays through collisions with gas.

In modern terminology, a [primary cosmic ray](primary-cosmic-ray.md) arriving at Earth's atmosphere is usually a [proton](proton.md) or another [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md), with an [electron](electron.md) component as well. Atmospheric collisions generate [secondary cosmic rays](secondary-cosmic-ray.md) and [air showers](air-shower.md), including [muons](muon.md), [electrons](electron.md) and [photons](photon.md). A [gamma ray](gamma-ray.md) can also initiate an [air shower](air-shower.md), although it is electrically neutral. Consequently, detecting atmospheric [ionization](ionization.md) does not establish that an incoming [primary cosmic ray](primary-cosmic-ray.md) passed through the detector. [Magnetic fields](magnetic-field-split.md) bend [charged particles](charged-particle.md), while [gamma rays](gamma-ray.md) provide a more direct directional connection to their sources.[Opening composition and atmospheric-interaction paragraphs; Cosmic accelerators, photon showers and magnetic deflection](cosmic-rays-particles-from-outer-space-by-cern.md)

The [Hess cosmic-ray balloon experiment](hess-cosmic-ray-balloon-experiment.md) asks how atmospheric shielding and separation from the ground affect [ionization](ionization.md). Its power comes from changing the environment around an enclosed detector instead of identifying each incoming particle.
