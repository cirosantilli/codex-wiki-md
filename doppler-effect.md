# Doppler effect

↑ **Parent:** [Wave](wave-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doppler_effect)

The [Doppler effect](doppler-effect.md) is a change between emitted and received [frequency](frequency.md) caused by motion of the source or receiver. An approaching siren can send more [wave crests](wave-crest.md) per second to a [microphone](microphone.md) than the source emits per second; a receding siren sends fewer. The source need not change its own [oscillation](oscillation.md). The [Doppler shift](doppler-effect.md) is $\Delta f=f_o-f_s$, where $f_s>0$ is emitted [frequency](frequency.md) and $f_o>0$ is received [frequency](frequency.md), both measured in [hertz](hertz.md).

For [acoustic waves](acoustic-wave.md), motion must be specified relative to the material carrying the [wave](wave-split.md). The [speed of sound](speed-of-sound.md) is measured in that material's rest [reference frame](reference-frame.md). Moving the source changes the spacing of successive [wavefronts](wavefront.md); moving the receiver changes how quickly it crosses those [wavefronts](wavefront.md). These two mechanisms give different exact factors in the [classical Doppler effect for sound](classical-doppler-effect-for-sound.md), even for the same closing [relative velocity](relative-velocity.md).

This makes a [microphone](microphone.md) a motion probe: a stable tone supplies a repeating clock, and changes in arrival spacing reveal the source's motion toward or away from the receiver. An [acoustic Doppler measurement](acoustic-doppler-measurement.md) can test the prediction directly. A passing source also illustrates why geometry matters: its speed can stay constant while the received [frequency](frequency.md) changes continuously, as in [Doppler frequency along a trajectory](doppler-frequency-along-a-trajectory.md).

The following sound model assumes [linear acoustics](linear-acoustics-split.md) in uniform stationary material with [isotropy](isotropy.md), constant [speed of sound](speed-of-sound.md) and negligible [wave dispersion](wave-dispersion.md). Source and receiver speeds are below the [speed of sound](speed-of-sound.md), and there is a single direct propagation path. Air motion requires transforming source and receiver [velocities](velocity.md) into the air's rest [reference frame](reference-frame.md); a nonuniform flow requires a more general propagation model. At the sonic threshold the simple periodic-arrival picture fails; [moving-point acoustic wavefronts](moving-point-acoustic-wavefronts.md) and the [Mach cone](mach-cone.md) describe the relevant geometry, while finite [shock waves](shock-wave.md) require further physics. [Light](light.md) in [vacuum](vacuum.md) instead requires the [relativistic Doppler effect](relativistic-doppler-effect.md): the acoustic formula cannot be converted into its exact optical counterpart just by replacing the [speed of sound](speed-of-sound.md) with the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md).
