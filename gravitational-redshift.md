# Gravitational redshift

↑ **Parent:** [General relativity](general-relativity-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_redshift)

In a static spacetime with $g_{tt}=-N^2$, a photon has conserved [Killing energy](killing-energy.md) $E=-p_t$. A static observer with four-velocity $N^{-1}\partial_t$ measures frequency proportional to $E/N$. Hence light emitted at one lapse and received at another obeys the displayed ratio. For $N=1+\Phi/c^2+O(\Phi^2/c^4)$ it gives the weak-field shift $(\nu_{\mathrm r}-\nu_{\mathrm e})/\nu_{\mathrm e}=(\Phi_{\mathrm e}-\Phi_{\mathrm r})/c^2$. This local clock effect does not require light-ray bending.

An upward [photon](photon.md) has lower received frequency when compared with the receiver's local clock: $\Phi_{\mathrm r}>\Phi_{\mathrm e}$ makes the shift negative. The corresponding upper clock runs faster in a comparison using the same static time coordinate. These are reciprocal descriptions of [gravitational redshift](gravitational-redshift.md) and [gravitational time dilation](gravitational-time-dilation.md), not opposite predictions. A locally measured [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) remains $c$.
