# Surface tension

↑ **Parent:** [Fluid mechanics](fluid-mechanics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_tension)

[Surface tension](surface-tension-split.md) $\gamma$ is the tensile [force](force-split.md) per unit length across a line drawn in a [liquid](liquid.md) interface. For a clean interface with [isotropy](isotropy.md), it has the same value in every tangential direction: each side pulls the other along the surface, perpendicular to the line. A straight segment of length $\ell$ therefore transmits a [force](force-split.md) of magnitude $\gamma\ell$ from one interface. Here we assume constant $\gamma>0$, with fixed [temperature](temperature-split.md) and composition. The same coefficient measures the reversible [work](work.md) required to create [surface area](surface-area.md):

$$
F_{\text{one interface}}=\gamma\ell,\qquad dE_{\mathrm s}=\gamma\,dA,\qquad
[\gamma]=\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}=\mathrm{J\,m^{-2}}.
$$

The equality of units follows from $1\,\mathrm J=1\,\mathrm{N\,m}$; the equality of the mechanical coefficients follows from the moving-rim argument below. [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §11.8: surface tension and the sliding wire](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/11-8-cohesion-and-adhesion-in-liquids-surface-tension-and-capillary-action) [Tomas Bohr and Bernhard Scheichl, Surface Tension and Energy Conservation in a Moving Fluid, equations (5) and (13)](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.11403v2)

For a concrete example, take an ideal flat soap film spanning a rectangular wire frame with a straight sliding rim of length $\ell$. Pull the rim outwards in the plane of the film, perpendicular to the rim. Each of the film's two [liquid](liquid.md)–[gas](gas.md) interfaces pulls the rim back towards the film with [force](force-split.md) $\gamma\ell$, so the outward holding [force](force-split.md) is $F=2\gamma\ell$. The two faces are two interfaces, each with its own [surface tension](surface-tension-split.md). With the assumed toy value $\gamma=0.072\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$ and $\ell=20\,\mathrm{mm}=0.020\,\mathrm m$, the required [force](force-split.md) is $2.88\,\mathrm{mN}$; one interface would contribute $1.44\,\mathrm{mN}$. This value of $\gamma$ is a stipulated model parameter, rather than a measured value for a particular soap solution.

**[Figure 1](#image-two-film-faces-double-the-force-on-a-sliding-rim). Two film faces double the force on a sliding rim**. The plan view shows the outward pull and the inward [surface tension](surface-tension-split.md) [force](force-split.md) on a sliding rim; the two interfaces overlap in this view. The graph compares one and two interfaces at the same assumed constant $\gamma=0.072\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$, with rim length in millimetres and [force](force-split.md) in millinewtons.

Why does an interface resist being enlarged? Its [molecules](molecule-split.md) have a different environment from those in the bulk [liquid](liquid.md); changing that environment has an [energy](energy-split.md) cost. In the constant-[surface tension](surface-tension-split.md) model, reducing [surface area](surface-area.md) lowers [surface energy](surface-energy.md). This connects an easily measured rim [force](force-split.md) to the shape of a droplet, the [Young–Laplace equation](young-laplace-equation.md) for its [pressure](pressure-split.md), and the restoring mechanism of [capillary waves](capillary-wave.md). It is useful because one coefficient links [forces](force-split.md), [energy](energy-split.md) and geometry. [David Tong, Fluid Mechanics, §4.1.3, pp. 121–123](https://davidtong.org/pdfs/teaching/fluid-mechanics/fluids3.pdf)

The moving-rim argument also explains the factor of two without memorizing a special formula. An outward displacement $dx$ creates [surface area](surface-area.md) $\ell\,dx$ on each face, hence $dA_{\mathrm{total}}=2\ell\,dx$. Slow pulling without [viscous dissipation](viscous-dissipation.md) requires [work](work.md) $F\,dx=\gamma\,dA_{\mathrm{total}}$, giving $F=2\gamma\ell$. For one interface, the same argument gives $F=\gamma\ell$. A [surface tension](surface-tension-split.md) gradient instead produces [Marangoni stress](marangoni-effect.md); the constant-$\gamma$ assumption excludes that additional effect.
