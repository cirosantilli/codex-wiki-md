# Countable product compactness implies countable choice

↑ **Parent:** [Axiom of countable choice](axiom-of-countable-choice.md)

This concerns arbitrary [compact spaces](compact-space.md), with no [Hausdorff](hausdorff-space.md) restriction. For nonempty sets $A_j$, adjoin a distinguished point $\infty_j$ and use the four-open-set [topology](topology-split.md) $\{\varnothing,A_j,\{\infty_j\},A_j\cup\{\infty_j\}\}$. Each resulting space is compact without choice. The product has the canonical all-infinity point. The closed coordinate conditions $x_j\in A_j$ have the [finite intersection property](finite-intersection-property.md), since finite choice is provable without the [axiom of choice](axiom-of-choice.md). If the countable product is compact, their intersection is nonempty, and any point in it supplies a [choice function](choice-function.md).
