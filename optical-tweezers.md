# Optical tweezers

↑ **Parent:** [Optics](optics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_tweezers)

[Optical tweezers](optical-tweezers.md) hold and move a small particle using the [force](force-split.md) exerted by a tightly focused beam of [light](light.md), usually from a [laser](laser-split.md). They can position transparent beads in a liquid and manipulate suitable [biological cells](biological-cell-split.md). Attaching a [molecule](molecule-split.md) to a trapped bead turns the bead into a handle: a measured bead [displacement](displacement.md), together with a calibrated [spring constant](spring-constant.md), reveals the [force](force-split.md) exerted on the attachment. This makes microscopic manipulation and measurement possible without a mechanical tool touching the bead. Living [biological cells](biological-cell-split.md) were manipulated with [infrared](infrared.md) beams by Ashkin, Dziedzic and Yamane in 1987; Svoboda and Block used trapped beads to measure [forces](force-split.md) from individual [molecules](molecule-split.md) in 1994. [Ashkin, Dziedzic and Yamane, Optical trapping and manipulation of single cells using infrared laser beams, 1987](https://doi.org/10.1038/330769a0) [Svoboda and Block, Force and Velocity Measured for Single Kinesin Molecules, 1994](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/42-620/handouts/svoboda_cell_94.pdf).

Why can [light](light.md) pull a bead towards a focus? [Light](light.md) carries [momentum](momentum-split.md). A transparent bead with a higher [refractive index](refractive-index.md) than the surrounding liquid bends transmitted [light rays](light-ray.md) by [refraction](refraction.md), changing their [momentum](momentum-split.md); [conservation of momentum](momentum-conservation.md) requires an opposite transfer to the bead. In the [geometrical optics](geometrical-optics-split.md) picture, unequal illumination of a displaced bead produces a net [restoring force](restoring-force.md) towards the brighter region. For a particle much smaller than the [wavelength](wavelength.md), a [physical optics](physical-optics-split.md) description replaces that ray picture. The detailed [force](force-split.md) depends on particle size, [refractive index](refractive-index.md), beam shape and [wavelength](wavelength.md); the qualitative attraction does not supply a universal formula for every particle.

The [optical gradient force](optical-gradient-force.md) draws a suitable transparent bead towards high illumination, while the [optical scattering force](optical-scattering-force.md) generally pushes along beam propagation. A stable three-dimensional [optical trap](optical-tweezers.md) needs a balance with a [restoring force](restoring-force.md) in each direction: transverse confinement alone does not ensure axial confinement. Strong focusing can make the backward axial gradient contribution large enough to balance the forward scattering contribution, with the [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) displaced from the focus. Higher [refractive index](refractive-index.md) alone is insufficient; absorbing particles, different index contrasts and different beam geometries can behave differently. [Ashkin, Dziedzic, Bjorkholm and Chu, Observation of a single-beam gradient force optical trap for dielectric particles, 1986, pp. 288–289](observation-of-a-single-beam-gradient-force-optical-trap-for-dielectric-particles-by-arthur-ashkin-and-others.md).

Historically, Ashkin's 1970 experiments accelerated and trapped particles using [radiation pressure](radiation-pressure.md). The 1986 paper by Ashkin, Dziedzic, Bjorkholm and Chu demonstrated the single-beam gradient [optical trap](optical-tweezers.md) for transparent particles. These are distinct steps: the earlier work established optical manipulation, and the later work supplied the tightly focused single-beam arrangement. [Ashkin, Acceleration and Trapping of Particles by Radiation Pressure, 1970](acceleration-and-trapping-of-particles-by-radiation-pressure-by-arthur-ashkin.md) [Ashkin, Dziedzic, Bjorkholm and Chu, 1986](observation-of-a-single-beam-gradient-force-optical-trap-for-dielectric-particles-by-arthur-ashkin-and-others.md).

Near a stable [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md), a [harmonic optical trap](harmonic-optical-trap.md) behaves like a [spring](spring.md); [thermal calibration of an optical trap](thermal-calibration-of-an-optical-trap.md) uses its [Brownian motion](brownian-motion-split.md) to determine the effective [spring constant](spring-constant.md). The later [passage through a translating optical trap](passage-through-a-translating-optical-trap.md) sections instead consider a moving localized [potential energy](potential-energy.md), which need not remain harmonic throughout a passage.
