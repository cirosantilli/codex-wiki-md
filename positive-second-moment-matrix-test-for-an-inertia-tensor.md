# Positive second-moment matrix test for an inertia tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Inertia tensor](inertia-tensor.md)

For a nonnegative [mass distribution](mass-distribution.md) relative to an origin, the second-moment matrix is $\mathsf S=\int\mathbf r\mathbf r^{\mathsf T}\,dm$ and the [inertia tensor](inertia-tensor.md) is $\mathsf I=\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf S)\mathsf 1-\mathsf S$. Taking the trace gives the displayed inverse formula, which must be [positive semidefinite](positive-semidefinite-matrix.md). Conversely, any such $\mathsf S$ can be realized by pairs of point masses along its orthogonal principal directions. Thus this is also a realizability test when total mass and dimensions are unrestricted. In principal coordinates it is equivalent to the [moment-of-inertia triangle inequalities](moment-of-inertia-triangle-inequalities.md): $I_2+I_3-I_1=2S_{11}\ge0$, with cyclic counterparts. A symmetric positive definite matrix alone need not be a physical [inertia tensor](inertia-tensor.md).
