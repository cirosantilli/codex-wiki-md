# Compensated semiconductor

↑ **Parent:** [Semiconductor doping](semiconductor-doping.md)

A compensated [semiconductor](semiconductor-split.md) contains both [donor impurities](donor-impurity.md) and [acceptor impurities](acceptor-impurity.md). In a nondegenerate equilibrium model with both species fully ionized, [charge neutrality](charge-neutrality.md) gives $n-p=\Delta=N_D-N_A$, while the semiconductor [semiconductor mass-action law](semiconductor-mass-action-law.md) gives $np=n_i^2$. Solving yields

$$
n=\frac{\Delta+\sqrt{\Delta^2+4n_i^2}}2,\qquad p=\frac{-\Delta+\sqrt{\Delta^2+4n_i^2}}2.
$$

For $\Delta>0$ and $n_i\ll\Delta$, $n\simeq\Delta$ and $p\simeq n_i^2/\Delta$. Numerically the minority concentration is better obtained as $n_i^2/n$ than by subtracting nearly equal numbers. If dopants are only partly ionized, replace $N_D,N_A$ in charge neutrality by their ionized concentrations and determine the [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) self-consistently.
