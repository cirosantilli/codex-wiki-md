# Clique number

↑ **Parent:** [Clique (graph theory)](clique-graph-theory.md)

The [clique number](clique-number.md) $\omega(G)$ is the maximum number of [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md) in a [clique](clique-graph-theory.md) of a finite [graph](graph-split.md):

$$
\omega(G)=\max\{|C|:C\text{ is a clique in }G\}.
$$

We use $\omega(G)=0$ for the [graph](graph-split.md) with no [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md). For a nonempty [complete graph](complete-graph.md), $\omega(K_n)=n$; for the five-[vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) example, $\omega(G)=3$.

If the [graph](graph-split.md) contains $K_k$ and no $K_{k+1}$ as [subgraphs](subgraph.md), its [clique number](clique-number.md) is exactly $k$, because every larger [clique](clique-graph-theory.md) contains a [clique](clique-graph-theory.md) with $k+1$ [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md). Also $\chi(G)\geq\omega(G)$: a [proper vertex coloring](proper-vertex-coloring.md) must assign different colors to all members of any [clique](clique-graph-theory.md). This lower bound need not be an equality; the [cycle graph](cycle-graph.md) $C_5$ has [clique number](clique-number.md) $2$ and [chromatic number](chromatic-number.md) $3$.
