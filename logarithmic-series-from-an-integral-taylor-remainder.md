# Logarithmic series from an integral Taylor remainder

↑ **Parent:** [Integral remainder in Taylor theorem](integral-remainder-in-taylor-theorem.md)

The [integral remainder in Taylor theorem](integral-remainder-in-taylor-theorem.md) for $\log(1-t)$ gives the exact finite identity

$$
-\log(1-t)=\sum_{k=1}^N\frac{t^k}{k}+\int_0^t\frac{(t-x)^N}{(1-x)^{N+1}}\,dx.
$$

For $0\leq x\leq t<1$, $(t-x)/(1-x)\leq t$, so the nonnegative remainder is at most $t^{N+1}/(1-t)$ and tends to zero. At $t=1/2$ it is at most $2^{-N}$, proving both convergence to $\log2$ and an explicit error bound without assuming the logarithmic [power series](power-series.md) beforehand.
