# Continuous-offer bargaining acceptance lemma

↑ **Parent:** [Finite-horizon alternating-offer bargaining](finite-horizon-alternating-offer-bargaining.md)

Let a proposer divide a pie of size $M>0$ by continuously offering its responder $q\in[0,M]$. Rejection gives fixed continuation [payoffs](payoff.md) $b$ to the proposer and $c$ to the responder, with $0\leq c<M$ and $b<M-c$. Then a [subgame perfect equilibrium](subgame-perfect-equilibrium.md) must have the responder accept $q=c$ with [probability](probability.md) one. The proposer offers exactly $c$, retaining $M-c$.

Sequential rationality forces rejection for $q<c$ and acceptance for $q>c$. If acceptance at $c$ had probability $a<1$, that offer would give the proposer $a(M-c)+(1-a)b<M-c$. Every larger offer gives less than $M-c$ and can be improved by a smaller offer still above $c$; every smaller offer gives $b<M-c$. The [supremum](supremum.md) $M-c$ would therefore be unattained. Randomizing offers cannot attain it either: the nonnegative gap from $M-c$ would be strictly positive almost surely and hence have positive [expectation](expected-value-split.md). No [best response](best-response.md) would exist, contradicting equilibrium. Acceptance at $c$ removes this gap and makes $q=c$ the unique optimal proposal. The assumptions $c<M$ and $b<M-c$ exclude degenerate indifference of the proposer.
