# Waveplate

↑ **Parent:** [Birefringence](birefringence.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waveplate)

A [waveplate](waveplate.md) uses [birefringence](birefringence.md) to change [polarization of an electromagnetic wave](polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave.md) through [phase retardation](phase-retardation.md). Its fast axis has the smaller [refractive index](refractive-index.md), hence the larger [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md); its slow axis has the larger index. Consider normal incidence with the [optic axis](optic-axis.md) in the plate face, so both components follow the same straight path. A [linear polarization](linear-polarization.md) at $45^\circ$ to these axes excites equal [wave amplitudes](wave-amplitude.md).

A complementary experiment uses two sheet [polarizers](polarizer.md) with a cellophane sheet between them. First cross the [polarizers](polarizer.md) and remove the sheet: the field is dark. Insert and rotate the sheet about the beam: light returns at intermediate orientations, but extinction recurs at two perpendicular orientations. The sheet has changed the [polarization of an electromagnetic wave](polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave.md), without requiring a resolved double image. With white [light](light.md), different [wavelengths](wavelength.md) can produce different transmitted colors.[I §33–3, Fig. 33–3 and the paragraphs describing removal, rotation and white-light colors of cellophane](the-feynman-lectures-on-physics-volume-i-by-richard-feynman-and-others.md). Brightness alone does not establish [circular polarization](circular-polarization.md): an ideal rotating linear [polarizer](polarizer.md) also gives orientation-independent [light intensity](light-intensity.md) for [unpolarized light](unpolarized-light.md).
