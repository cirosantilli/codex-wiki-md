# Chemical bond

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_bond)

A [chemical bond](chemical-bond.md) associates [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) or groups of [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) into a sufficiently stable structure to treat it as a distinct entity. The concept covers connections within [molecules](molecule.md) and in extended solids; see the [IUPAC Gold Book, chemical bond](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/CT07009). It is useful because composition alone does not specify how the [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) are connected, and those connections help determine a [chemical substance](chemical-substance.md)'s properties and possible [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction.md).

In a [covalent bond](covalent-bond.md), [Electrons](electron.md) are shared between [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). Two [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) can form a [molecular hydrogen](molecular-hydrogen.md) [molecule](molecule.md), with its [Electrons](electron.md) interacting with both [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus.md). In the usual description of this diatomic [molecule](molecule.md), the [potential energy](potential-energy.md) as a function of nuclear separation has a minimum: moving the nuclei too close or too far raises the [energy](energy.md). See [7.2 Covalent Bonding](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/7-2-covalent-bonding.md).

Separating bonded [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) requires [energy](energy.md); forming the corresponding [chemical bonds](chemical-bond.md) releases [energy](energy.md). Whether an overall [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md) releases [energy](energy.md) depends on both the [chemical bonds](chemical-bond.md) broken and those formed. The lines in the [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md) diagram indicate connections schematically, rather than literal rods or a sequence of microscopic events.
