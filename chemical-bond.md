# Chemical bond

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_bond)

A [chemical bond](chemical-bond.md) associates [chemical atoms](physics.md#atom-physics) or groups of [chemical atoms](physics.md#atom-physics) into a sufficiently stable structure to treat it as a distinct entity. The concept covers connections within [molecules](molecule.md) and in extended solids; see the [IUPAC Gold Book, chemical bond](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/CT07009). It is useful because composition alone does not specify how the [chemical atoms](physics.md#atom-physics) are connected, and those connections help determine a [chemical substance](chemical-substance.md)'s properties and possible [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction.md).

In a [covalent bond](covalent-bond.md), [electrons](physics.md#electron) are shared between [chemical atoms](physics.md#atom-physics). Two [hydrogen](chemical-substance.md#hydrogen) [chemical atoms](physics.md#atom-physics) can form a [molecular hydrogen](chemical-substance.md#molecular-hydrogen) [molecule](molecule.md), with its [electrons](physics.md#electron) interacting with both [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus.md). In the usual description of this diatomic [molecule](molecule.md), the [potential energy](energy.md#potential-energy) as a function of nuclear separation has a minimum: moving the nuclei too close or too far raises the [energy](energy.md). See [7.2 Covalent Bonding](chemistry-bibliography.md#chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/7-2-covalent-bonding).

Separating bonded [chemical atoms](physics.md#atom-physics) requires [energy](energy.md); forming the corresponding [chemical bonds](chemical-bond.md) releases [energy](energy.md). Whether an overall [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md) releases [energy](energy.md) depends on both the [chemical bonds](chemical-bond.md) broken and those formed. The lines in the [stoichiometry](chemical-reaction.md#stoichiometry) diagram indicate connections schematically, rather than literal rods or a sequence of microscopic events.

## Siloxane bond

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

An Si–O–Si linkage. [hydrolysis](chemical-reaction.md#hydrolysis) produces [silanol](chemical-substance.md#silanol) groups; their condensation can regenerate an Si–O–Si linkage.

## Delta bonding

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

Bonding with two nodal planes containing the internuclear or coordination axis. [metal](chemical-substance.md#metal) $d_{xy}$ and $d_{x^2-y^2}$ orbitals can interact with ring combinations of matching delta symmetry; symmetry alone does not imply a strong bond.

## Bonding trends in second-period single bonds

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

Compact valence orbitals favor strong carbon-carbon bonding, but neighboring lone-pair repulsion can weaken nitrogen-nitrogen bonds. Bond strengths depend on [orbital overlap](chemistry.md#orbital-overlap), polarity and electronic occupancy together; atom size alone is insufficient.

## Bond stretching force constant

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

In a local harmonic approximation, the vibrational wavenumber obeys $\widetilde\nu=(2\pi c)^{-1}\sqrt{k/\mu}$. Comparisons at fixed [reduced mass](classical-mechanics.md#reduced-mass) therefore use squared frequency ratios. A local stretching force constant is not identical to a [bond-dissociation energy](#bond-dissociation-energy).

## Resonance in chemistry

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

Representation of an electronic structure using multiple Lewis contributors with the same nuclear connectivity. The contributors are not separate species interconverting through ordinary [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction.md).

## Multicentre bonding

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

### Bridging hydrogen

↑ **Parent:** [Multicentre bonding](#multicentre-bonding)

A [hydrogen atom](mathematical-physics.md#hydrogen-atom) associated with more than one skeletal centre. Removal as a [proton](physics.md#proton) must be distinguished from [hydride](chemical-substance.md#hydride) transfer; the cluster charge and [electron](physics.md#electron) count change differently in the two operations.

### Polyhedral skeletal electron pair theory

↑ **Parent:** [Multicentre bonding](#multicentre-bonding)

An electron-counting approach relating [skeletal electron pairs](#skeletal-electron-pair) to a cluster vertex count $n$. A [closo](molecule.md#closo-cluster) deltahedron has $n+1$ pairs; [nido](molecule.md#nido-cluster) and [arachno](molecule.md#arachno-cluster) frameworks have $n+2$ and $n+3$. The count excludes electrons in externally directed bonds or [lone pairs](covalent-bond.md#lone-pair). It is a structural guide with a defined domain, not a rule that every molecule with the same total valence count has the same shape.

#### Skeletal electron count

↑ **Parent:** [Polyhedral skeletal electron pair theory](#polyhedral-skeletal-electron-pair-theory)

The number of electrons allocated to the connected cluster framework after separating external bonds and appropriate [lone pairs](covalent-bond.md#lone-pair). For $\mathrm{B_nH_m^{q-}}$ with one terminal B–H bond per vertex it is $n+m+q$.

##### Skeletal electron pair

↑ **Parent:** [Skeletal electron count](#skeletal-electron-count)

A pair of electrons allocated to cluster bonding. [closo](molecule.md#closo-cluster), [nido](molecule.md#nido-cluster) and [arachno](molecule.md#arachno-cluster) counts distinguish the number of missing vertices relative to a closed parent polyhedron.

## Metal-metal bond

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

A [metal-metal bond](#metal-metal-bond) joins two [metal](chemical-substance.md#metal) centers directly through overlap of their [atomic orbitals](chemistry.md#atomic-orbital). A single bond contributes one [electron](physics.md#electron) to the local count at each [metal](chemical-substance.md#metal) in conventional [electron counting](chemistry.md#electron-counting). [Bridging ligands](chemistry.md#bridging-ligand) can accompany direct bonding, but a short bridged separation alone does not establish its [bond order](chemistry.md#bond-order).

## Inductive effect

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

## Negative hyperconjugation

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

[Negative hyperconjugation](#negative-hyperconjugation) is donation from an occupied lone-pair or related orbital into an antibonding sigma orbital. Nitrogen-to-P–X sigma-star donation contributes to [phosphazene](chemical-substance.md#phosphazene) bonding. Short P–N bonds therefore do not require a model dominated by phosphorus $3d$ pi orbitals.

## Conjugated system

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

Adjacent compatible p [atomic orbitals](chemistry.md#atomic-orbital) allow [electron density](chemistry.md#electron-density) to delocalize across more than two atoms. [Resonance contributors](covalent-bond.md#resonance-structure) describe aspects of this shared electronic structure.

### Pi conjugation

↑ **Parent:** [Conjugated system](#conjugated-system)

Overlap between adjacent p-type [atomic orbitals](chemistry.md#atomic-orbital) permits delocalized electronic states across more than one [chemical bond](chemical-bond.md). A cyclic path with suitable occupancy can support [aromaticity](molecule.md#aromaticity); an external [lone pair](covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) can act as a donor without contributing an extra independent pair to every ring count.

#### Pi electron

↑ **Parent:** [Pi conjugation](#pi-conjugation)

An [electron](physics.md#electron) occupying a pi-type [molecular orbital](chemistry.md#molecular-orbital). In a conjugated planar [molecule](molecule.md), the pi subspace is commonly built from [p orbitals](chemistry.md#p-orbital) perpendicular to the molecular plane; its electron population excludes the sigma framework.

### Bond-length alternation

↑ **Parent:** [Conjugated system](#conjugated-system)

Alternation between shorter and longer [chemical bonds](chemical-bond.md) along a [conjugated system](#conjugated-system). In the [Hückel method](chemistry.md#huckel-method), unequal nearest-neighbour couplings split the frontier [molecular orbitals](chemistry.md#molecular-orbital). An electronic [energy](energy.md) gain must be compared at a stated constraint on the mean coupling and against the geometric distortion cost.

## Metallic bonding

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallic_bonding)

Cohesion in a [metal](chemical-substance.md#metal) involving delocalized valence [electrons](physics.md#electron) and positive ion cores. A dispersion-only sum between neutral molecular units omits this collective electronic contribution.

## Bond-dissociation energy

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond-dissociation_energy)

A [bond-dissociation energy](#bond-dissociation-energy) specifies the [energy](energy.md) needed for a stated dissociation into separated fragments. An [energy](energy.md) from the lowest vibrational state, an [energy](energy.md) from the bottom of a [potential energy](energy.md#potential-energy) well, and a finite-temperature molar dissociation [enthalpy](enthalpy.md) require different conventions; do not silently mix them in an [electronegativity](electronegativity.md) formula.

Which fragments, in which [quantum states](quantum-system.md#quantum-state), and at which [temperature](temperature.md)? For a neutral gaseous [molecule](molecule.md) with a specified two-electron [covalent bond](covalent-bond.md), the usual thermochemical convention is [homolysis](#homolysis),

$$
\mathrm{A{-}B}(g)\longrightarrow\mathrm A^{\bullet}(g)+\mathrm B^{\bullet}(g).
$$

Each fragment receives one of the two bonding [electrons](physics.md#electron); the dots denote unpaired [electrons](physics.md#electron). The final fragments are isolated from each other, with their own relaxed [molecular geometry](molecule.md#molecular-geometry) and specified electronic [quantum states](quantum-system.md#quantum-state). The [energy](energy.md) cost includes this relaxation, rather than merely stretching the initial [chemical bond](chemical-bond.md) while freezing everything else. For a neutral parent, [heterolysis](#heterolysis) instead produces $\mathrm A^+(g)+\mathrm B^-(g)$, or the opposite assignment of [electric charges](electric-charge.md); those are different final states and different [energies](energy.md). A value for gaseous neutral fragments cannot be transferred to [ions](chemistry.md#ion), a [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry), or a [solid](thermodynamics.md#solid) without accounting for those changed conditions.[definition of bond-dissociation energy: molar enthalpy for homolysis of a specified molecular entity](chemistry-bibliography.md#iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/bond-dissociation-energy)

The distinction is useful beyond terminology. [Pauling electronegativity](electronegativity.md#pauling-electronegativity) compares consistently defined [bond-dissociation energies](#bond-dissociation-energy); [photodissociation](#photodissociation) can reveal a specified ground-state threshold; a thermal [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md) balance requires compatible [enthalpies](enthalpy.md). A threshold is also different from an [activation energy](chemical-kinetics.md#activation-energy): it compares initial and final [quantum states](quantum-system.md#quantum-state), whereas a barrier can obstruct the path between them.

### Bond potential-well depth

↑ **Parent:** [Bond-dissociation energy](#bond-dissociation-energy)

For a [diatomic molecule](molecule.md#diatomic-molecule), let $V(r)$ be the electronic [potential energy](energy.md#potential-energy) at [bond length](#bond-length) $r$, with the [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus.md) treated as fixed while finding the electronic [energy](energy.md). Choose zero at infinitely separated [chemical atoms](physics.md#atom-physics) in the specified electronic [quantum states](quantum-system.md#quantum-state). If the minimum occurs at $r_e$, the [bond potential-well depth](#bond-potential-well-depth) is

$$
D_e=V(\infty)-V(r_e)=-V(r_e).
$$

The bottom of this curve describes an equilibrium [bond length](#bond-length), not an attainable motionless vibrational [quantum state](quantum-system.md#quantum-state). Changing the zero of [potential energy](energy.md#potential-energy) shifts both endpoints equally and leaves $D_e$ unchanged. A [quantum harmonic oscillator](quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md) approximates the curve near its minimum but rises without limit, so that local approximation alone cannot supply a dissociation asymptote. A [Morse potential](quantum-system.md#morse-potential) supplies both a minimum and a finite separated-fragment limit.[definition: subscripts e and 0 distinguish the potential-energy minimum and ground state](chemistry-bibliography.md#iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/dissociation-energy)

### Ground-state dissociation energy

↑ **Parent:** [Bond-dissociation energy](#bond-dissociation-energy)

The [ground-state dissociation energy](#ground-state-dissociation-energy) starts at the lowest vibrational [quantum state](quantum-system.md#quantum-state), with the lowest allowed rotational [quantum state](quantum-system.md#quantum-state) and the fragment states explicitly specified. For a [diatomic molecule](molecule.md#diatomic-molecule) whose fragments are ground-state [chemical atoms](physics.md#atom-physics), omitting any additional rotational correction,

$$
D_0=D_e-E_{\rm ZP},
$$

where $E_{\rm ZP}$ is the vibrational [zero-point energy](quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#zero-point-energy) measured upward from the well minimum. The initial [molecule](molecule.md) already has this [energy](energy.md), so less additional [energy](energy.md) is needed to reach the same final limit.[definition of bond-dissociation energy in theoretical chemistry: D0 = De minus zero-point vibrational energy](chemistry-bibliography.md#iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/bond-dissociation-energy-in-theoretical-chemistry)

For fragments that are themselves [molecules](molecule.md), include their [zero-point energies](quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#zero-point-energy) too. Define $D_e$ as the electronic [energy](energy.md) difference between separately relaxed products and reactant; then, with consistent rotational conventions,

$$
D_0=D_e+\sum_{\rm fragments}E_{\rm ZP}-E_{\rm ZP,parent}.
$$

Thus “subtract the parent's [zero-point energy](quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#zero-point-energy)” is sufficient for atomic products but generally incomplete for molecular products. An excited final [quantum state](quantum-system.md#quantum-state) raises its channel threshold by its excitation [energy](energy.md); that threshold does not become the ground-product $D_0$ just because a detector selects it.

### Bond-dissociation enthalpy

↑ **Parent:** [Bond-dissociation energy](#bond-dissociation-energy)

A [bond-dissociation enthalpy](#bond-dissociation-enthalpy) is the molar [enthalpy](enthalpy.md) change for the specified [homolysis](#homolysis) at [temperature](temperature.md) $T$, with reactant and products in specified [standard states](chemical-potential.md#standard-state). Its usual units are $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. The superscript $\circ$ specifies [standard states](chemical-potential.md#standard-state); it does not fix $T$ to room [temperature](temperature.md).[definition and symbols: standard molar enthalpy for homolysis, Δdiss H°](chemistry-bibliography.md#iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/bond-dissociation-enthalpy)

Write $\delta h_i(T)=H_{m,i}^{\circ}(T)-H_{m,i}^{\circ}(0)$ for each species' molar thermal [enthalpy](enthalpy.md) increment. With $D_0$ an [energy](energy.md) per parent [molecule](molecule.md),

$$
\Delta_{\rm diss}H^{\circ}(T)=N_{\rm A}D_0+\delta h_A(T)+\delta h_B(T)-\delta h_{AB}(T).
$$

Here $N_{\rm A}$ is the [Avogadro constant](physics.md#avogadro-constant). The thermal increments include translational [kinetic energy](energy.md#kinetic-energy), [molecular rotation](molecule.md#molecular-rotation), thermally excited [molecular vibration](molecule.md#molecular-vibration) and electronic states when populated, and the $pV$ contribution to [enthalpy](enthalpy.md). They are additional to the [zero-point energies](quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#zero-point-energy) already included in $D_0$. If values of [standard enthalpy of formation](chemistry.md#standard-enthalpy-of-formation) at the same $T$ are available, [Hess's law](chemistry.md#hess-s-law) gives the same quantity as $\Delta_fH^{\circ}(A)+\Delta_fH^{\circ}(B)-\Delta_fH^{\circ}(AB)$.

Units alone do not identify the convention. Multiplying either $D_e$ or $D_0$ by the [Avogadro constant](physics.md#avogadro-constant) gives molar [energy](energy.md), but does not add thermal [enthalpy](enthalpy.md). One [electronvolt](electronegativity.md#electronvolt) per dissociation corresponds to $96.485332\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. A [spectroscopic wavenumber](wave-equation.md#spectroscopic-wavenumber) $\widetilde\nu$ represents [energy](energy.md) $hc\widetilde\nu$, using the [Planck constant](quantum-mechanics.md#planck-constant) $h$ and [speed of light](special-relativity.md#speed-of-light) $c$; $1\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ corresponds to $0.011962657\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.[SI defining constants h, c, e and Avogadro constant; conversions calculated from them](chemistry-bibliography.md#fundamental-physical-constants-complete-listing-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology-and-others)

### Stipulated bond dissociation model

↑ **Parent:** [Bond-dissociation energy](#bond-dissociation-energy)

Take an invented [diatomic molecule](molecule.md#diatomic-molecule) with a [Morse potential](quantum-system.md#morse-potential), setting the separated-atom [energy](energy.md) to zero:

$$
V(r)=D_e\left[\left(1-e^{-a(r-r_e)}\right)^2-1\right],\qquad
D_e=2.0000\,\mathrm{eV},\qquad A=\hbar\omega_e=0.2000\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

Here $a$ is an inverse length, $r_e$ the equilibrium [bond length](#bond-length), and $\omega_e=a\sqrt{2D_e/\mu}$, with [reduced mass](classical-mechanics.md#reduced-mass) $\mu$. These stipulated parameters describe a simple model, not a measured [chemical substance](chemical-substance.md). For the one-dimensional [Morse oscillator](quantum-system.md#morse-oscillator), [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue.md) above the minimum are

$$
\epsilon_v=A\left(v+\tfrac12\right)-\frac{A^2}{4D_e}\left(v+\tfrac12\right)^2,
\qquad v=0,1,\ldots,\quad v+\tfrac12<\frac{2D_e}{A}.
$$

Consequently,

$$
E_{\rm ZP}=\frac A2-\frac{A^2}{16D_e}=0.09875\,\mathrm{eV},\qquad
D_0=2.0000-0.09875=1.90125\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

The harmonic estimate $A/2=0.1000\,\mathrm{eV}$ is slightly larger than this model's exact [zero-point energy](quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#zero-point-energy). The physical bound-level restriction matters: the quadratic expression is not an infinite ladder of vibrational [quantum states](quantum-system.md#quantum-state).

<a id="image-bond-dissociation-well-and-zero-point"></a>
![](bond-dissociation-well-and-zero-point.png)

**[Figure 1](#image-bond-dissociation-well-and-zero-point). Bond potential-well depth and ground-state dissociation energy reach the same separated-atom limit from different starting energies**. Original stipulated [Morse potential](quantum-system.md#morse-potential) with $D_e=2\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $A=0.2\,\mathrm{eV}$. The horizontal coordinate is $a(r-r_e)$; the horizontal $v=0$ line is an [energy eigenvalue](energy-eigenvalue.md), not a classical trajectory or a measured spectrum.

For a separate thermal illustration using this same stipulated $D_0$, assume an [ideal gas](ideal-gas.md), fully active [molecular rotation](molecule.md#molecular-rotation) of the parent, negligible thermal [molecular vibration](molecule.md#molecular-vibration), and negligible electronic excitation for all species. Each monatomic product contributes $\delta h=5RT/2$; the [diatomic molecule](molecule.md#diatomic-molecule) contributes $7RT/2$. Thus the net increment is $3RT/2$, with [molar gas constant](ideal-gas.md#molar-gas-constant) $R$. At $T=300\,\mathrm K$,

$$
N_{\rm A}D_e\approx192.971\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}},\qquad
N_{\rm A}D_0\approx183.443\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}},\qquad
\Delta_{\rm diss}H^{\circ}\approx187.184\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

This last result follows from the stated thermal model, not a universal correction for every [chemical bond](chemical-bond.md). Frozen rotations, accessible excited states, different fragment composition, or a condensed [phase](wave.md#phase-waves) change the thermal increments.

### Sequential bond-dissociation energies

↑ **Parent:** [Bond-dissociation energy](#bond-dissociation-energy)

Equivalent [chemical bonds](chemical-bond.md) in an intact [molecule](molecule.md) need not have equal successive [bond-dissociation enthalpies](#bond-dissociation-enthalpy). Once one [chemical bond](chemical-bond.md) is removed, the next reactant has a different electronic [quantum state](quantum-system.md#quantum-state) and may relax to a different [molecular geometry](molecule.md#molecular-geometry). The successive steps compare different species. This distinction also explains why a tabulated average [chemical bond](chemical-bond.md) strength is not necessarily the cost of the first cleavage.[“Bond Strength: Covalent Bonds”, methane paragraph: equivalent initial bonds and unequal sequential breaking costs](chemistry-bibliography.md#chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/7-5-strengths-of-ionic-and-covalent-bonds)

For an invented species $AB_2$, stipulate the following gas-phase steps, with all [enthalpies](enthalpy.md) at the same [temperature](temperature.md) and the same [standard states](chemical-potential.md#standard-state):

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB_2(g)&\longrightarrow AB(g)+B(g),&D_1&=300\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}},\\
AB(g)&\longrightarrow A(g)+B(g),&D_2&=400\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The symbols stand for hypothetical species with consistently chosen electronic states. They are not values for [water](chemical-substance.md#water) or another measured [molecule](molecule.md). By [Hess's law](chemistry.md#hess-s-law), the total [enthalpy of atomization](chemistry.md#enthalpy-of-atomization) for $AB_2(g)\to A(g)+2B(g)$ is $700\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$; the [arithmetic mean](arithmetic.md#arithmetic-mean) per broken [chemical bond](chemical-bond.md) is $350\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Neither individual step equals that mean. Nor must the second step be cheaper: here it is deliberately more expensive. An exact sequence sums exactly; substituting averages taken from other [molecules](molecule.md) generally gives only an estimate.

**[Figure 2](#image-bond-energy-conventions-and-sequential-cleavage). Two stipulated examples: changing the energy convention for one dissociation, and changing the reactant during sequential cleavage**. ## Homolysis

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

[Homolysis](#homolysis) splits a [covalent bond](covalent-bond.md) with one bonding [electron](physics.md#electron) assigned to each fragment. A neutral closed-shell [molecule](molecule.md) thereby produces fragments with unpaired [electrons](physics.md#electron), each a [radical](chemistry.md#radical-chemistry). The usual [bond-dissociation enthalpy](#bond-dissociation-enthalpy) uses this product convention.

## Heterolysis

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

[Heterolysis](#heterolysis) assigns both bonding [electrons](physics.md#electron) to one fragment. For an initially neutral [molecule](molecule.md), the resulting [cation](chemistry.md#cation) and [anion](chemistry.md#anion) define a different dissociation channel from [homolysis](#homolysis); their isolated gas-phase [energy](energy.md) differs from that of solvated [ions](chemistry.md#ion).

## Photodissociation

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photodissociation)

[Photodissociation](#photodissociation) separates a [molecule](molecule.md) into fragments after [photon absorption](quantum-mechanics.md#photon-absorption). For an isolated parent with a single absorbed [photon](quantum-mechanics.md#photon), [conservation of energy](mathematical-physics.md#conservation-of-energy) gives

$$
h\nu+E_{\rm initial,int}=D_0+E_{\rm products,int}+E_{\rm recoil}.
$$

Internal excitation [energies](energy.md) here are relative to the parent and product ground states used to define $D_0$; $E_{\rm recoil}$ is the total product translational [kinetic energy](energy.md#kinetic-energy) in the parent's [centre of mass](classical-mechanics.md#center-of-mass) frame. Measuring one product's [kinetic energy](energy.md#kinetic-energy) is insufficient by itself. For two fragments of [masses](classical-mechanics.md#mass) $m_A,m_B$, [conservation of momentum](momentum.md#momentum-conservation) gives $E_{\rm recoil}=E_A(m_A+m_B)/m_B$ when $A$ is detected. Unknown product excitation can conceal part of the available [energy](energy.md).

### Quantum yield

↑ **Parent:** [Photodissociation](#photodissociation)

The number of specified photochemical events per absorbed photon. A wavelength-dependent channel yield must be included when converting absorption into a chemical rate.

### Photolysis rate coefficient

↑ **Parent:** [Photodissociation](#photodissociation)

The first-order rate coefficient for a specified [photodissociation](#photodissociation) channel. It integrates spectral [actinic flux](radiometry.md#actinic-flux), absorption [cross section](physics.md#cross-section-physics) and channel [quantum yield](#quantum-yield). Absorption without dissociation does not contribute to that channel's rate. Its units are inverse time.

#### Ozone-column photolysis shielding

↑ **Parent:** [Photolysis rate coefficient](#photolysis-rate-coefficient)

In a direct-beam model with an effective [ozone](chemical-substance.md#ozone) cross section $\sigma$, removal of an overlying column $\Delta N$ multiplies the transmitted [photodissociation](#photodissociation) flux by $e^{\sigma\Delta N}$. A real [photodissociation](#photodissociation) coefficient integrates wavelength-dependent cross section, [quantum yield](#quantum-yield) and [actinic flux](radiometry.md#actinic-flux), including scattering and the local radiation field.

### Photofragment-ion imaging

↑ **Parent:** [Photodissociation](#photodissociation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photofragment-ion_imaging)

[Photofragment-ion imaging](#photofragment-ion-imaging) detects fragment [velocities](classical-mechanics.md#velocity) by ionizing neutral products and mapping their motion onto a position-sensitive detector using an [electric field](electric-field.md). In a velocity-mapping implementation, the image radius measures transverse speed under fixed lens settings. Selecting an arrival-time slice helps extract the central plane of the three-dimensional distribution. Species identification and electronic [quantum state](quantum-system.md#quantum-state) selection are essential for turning a radius into a particular dissociation threshold.

#### Nitrogen dissociation threshold from fragment images

↑ **Parent:** [Photofragment-ion imaging](#photofragment-ion-imaging)

A [molecular nitrogen](chemical-substance.md#molecular-nitrogen) experiment used a $0.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ pulsed nozzle and two skimmers. A $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ tube raised the rotational [temperature](temperature.md). Opposed vacuum-ultraviolet [laser](laser.md) pulses excited selected molecular levels and, about $10\,\mathrm{ns}$ later, selectively ionized excited [nitrogen](chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) fragments. A [microchannel plate detector](tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#microchannel-plate-detector), phosphor screen and camera recorded radii.[Lu, Jiang and Gao, §2, printed manuscript pp. 2–3: nozzle, tube, pump/probe and imaging readout](chemistry-bibliography.md#accurate-measurements-of-the-bond-dissociation-energies-of-14n2-14n15n-and-15n2-by-liya-lu-and-others)

The measured squared radii were fitted against molecular excitation [energies](energy.md), referenced to the parent [ground state](quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state):

$$
E_j=D_{0,\rm channel}+\kappa R_j^2.
$$

The intercept at zero radius gave the excited-product threshold; the fitted slope absorbed the detector scale. A wavemeter monitored the excitation source; scanning the probe covered the recoil [Doppler shift](wave.md#doppler-effect). Subtracting the atomic excitation [energies](energy.md) converted the intercept to ground-product $D_0$.[§1: image-radius equation; §2: wavemeter and probe scan; §3 and Figure 4: fits; §4: atomic excitation correction](chemistry-bibliography.md#accurate-measurements-of-the-bond-dissociation-energies-of-14n2-14n15n-and-15n2-by-liya-lu-and-others)

An apertured grounded plate and detector timing rejected faster competing channels. Selecting fragment electronic states distinguished nearby thresholds. Agreement of $D_e$ across [isotopes](chemical-substance.md#isotope) after [zero-point energy](quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#zero-point-energy) correction tested consistency. The method still requires correctly assigned levels, adequate near-threshold signal and stable imaging; a signal drop alone can reflect another channel or a barrier.[§2: shielding plate and timing; §3: state-selected channels; §4 and Table 2: isotope consistency and limitations](chemistry-bibliography.md#accurate-measurements-of-the-bond-dissociation-energies-of-14n2-14n15n-and-15n2-by-liya-lu-and-others)

This measurement determines a state-resolved [ground-state dissociation energy](#ground-state-dissociation-energy). Obtaining a finite-[temperature](temperature.md) [bond-dissociation enthalpy](#bond-dissociation-enthalpy) requires the thermal increments above; obtaining a [bond potential-well depth](#bond-potential-well-depth) requires the [zero-point energy](quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#zero-point-energy) correction. The three quantities describe different starting conditions for the same specified separation.

## Bond length

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_length)

A [bond length](#bond-length) describes the separation of the [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus.md) of two bonded [chemical atoms](physics.md#atom-physics). Distinguish an equilibrium geometry from an instantaneous or experimentally averaged separation. Methods of determining [molecular geometry](molecule.md#molecular-geometry) can use different averaging conventions, so reported [bond lengths](#bond-length) need their measurement context. See the [IUPAC Gold Book: bond length](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/BT07003). ### Equilibrium bond length

↑ **Parent:** [Bond length](#bond-length)

The nuclear separation at the minimum of an electronic potential-energy curve. It differs from effective separations inferred from vibrationally averaged [rotational constants](molecule.md#rotational-constant).

## Partial atomic charge

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)

A partial atomic charge assigns an [electric charge](electric-charge.md) to a [chemical atom](physics.md#atom-physics) by partitioning the actual [electron density](chemistry.md#electron-density). The result depends on the partitioning convention and need not be an integer. It differs from [formal charge](covalent-bond.md#formal-charge), which divides bonding [electrons](physics.md#electron) equally in a specified [Lewis structure](covalent-bond.md#lewis-structure).

## Electron pair

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_pair)

An [electron pair](#electron-pair) consists of two [electrons](physics.md#electron) with opposite [spins](angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) sharing the same spatial part of their [quantum state](quantum-system.md#quantum-state). The [Lewis structure](covalent-bond.md#lewis-structure) distinction between a [bond pair](covalent-bond.md#bond-pair) and a [lone pair](covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) records whether that pair is assigned to a connection between [chemical atoms](physics.md#atom-physics) or to one [chemical atom](physics.md#atom-physics).

## Hydrogen bond

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_bond)

A [hydrogen bond](#hydrogen-bond) is an attractive association involving a [hydrogen](chemical-substance.md#hydrogen) attached to another [chemical atom](physics.md#atom-physics), and an accepting atom or group. In the IUPAC 2011 definition, the atom $X$ in $X-\mathrm H$ is more [electronegative](electronegativity.md) than [hydrogen](chemical-substance.md#hydrogen), and evidence of bond formation is required. The familiar strong examples use O–H or N–H groups, but restricting the concept to only nitrogen, oxygen and fluorine misses weaker cases. A short distance alone is not the entire definition.[Arunan et al., Definition of the hydrogen bond, IUPAC Recommendations 2011](https://publications.iupac.org/pac/pdf/2011/pdf/8308x1637.pdf)

<a id="image-two-water-molecules-play-different-hydrogen-bond-roles-while-retaining-their-own-covalent-o-h-bonds"></a>
![](water-hydrogen-bond-roles.png)

**[Figure 3](#image-two-water-molecules-play-different-hydrogen-bond-roles-while-retaining-their-own-covalent-o-h-bonds). Two water molecules play different hydrogen-bond roles while retaining their own covalent O–H bonds**.

In the depicted [water](chemical-substance.md#water) pair, one O–H group is the [hydrogen bond donor](#hydrogen-bond-donor); the other [oxygen](chemical-substance.md#oxygen) supplies the [hydrogen bond acceptor](#hydrogen-bond-acceptor). The solid O–H line denotes a [covalent bond](covalent-bond.md) within a [water](chemical-substance.md#water) [molecule](molecule.md); the dotted H···O line denotes the [hydrogen bond](#hydrogen-bond) between them. One [water](chemical-substance.md#water) [molecule](molecule.md) can play both roles in a larger arrangement because it has O–H groups and [lone pairs](covalent-bond.md#lone-pair).

This makes [hydrogen bonds](#hydrogen-bond) useful for understanding organization beyond a single [molecular formula](chemical-substance.md#molecular-formula). They connect [water](chemical-substance.md#water) [molecules](molecule.md) and contribute to [protein](protein.md) folding and recognition between [molecules](molecule.md). The same types of [chemical atoms](physics.md#atom-physics) can support different associations depending on which donor and acceptor sites are exposed and how they are oriented. Alberts and colleagues discuss these noncovalent interactions in [Molecular Biology of the Cell, the chemical components of a cell](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26883/).

[Hydrogen bonds](#hydrogen-bond) are often directional, with an approximately straight donor–H–acceptor arrangement favourable in familiar cases; strength and preferred geometry depend on the system. The IUPAC definition uses converging experimental or theoretical evidence rather than one universal distance threshold. The author-hosted [2009 task-group draft, §§3.1–3.3](https://ipc.iisc.ac.in/~ea/iupac/iupac/IUPAC_finalreport_Nov_2009TG.pdf) explains the definition and typical criteria leading to the recommendation.

### Aminopyridine hydrogen-bonded dimer

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen bond](#hydrogen-bond)

A self-complementary pair linked by amino-NH donation to pyridine-N acceptors.

### Carboxylic-acid hydrogen-bonded dimer

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen bond](#hydrogen-bond)

A cyclic pair of [carboxylic acids](chemical-substance.md#carboxylic-acid) linked by two reciprocal O–H···O=C [hydrogen bonds](#hydrogen-bond).

### Water dimer

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen bond](#hydrogen-bond)

Two [water](chemical-substance.md#water) [molecules](molecule.md) associated by a donor O–H···O [hydrogen bond](#hydrogen-bond). Gas-phase pair energy does not include the complete [solvent](mixture.md#solvent) and [entropy](entropy.md) changes governing association in solution.

### Hydrogen-bond complementarity

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen bond](#hydrogen-bond)

[Hydrogen-bond complementarity](#hydrogen-bond-complementarity) matches donor and acceptor positions in a binding interface. Two strong sites need not cooperate if their spacing or direction is incompatible; preorganization can favor a multiply [hydrogen](chemical-substance.md#hydrogen)-bonded complex.

Comparison of competing donor–acceptor pairings using their interaction strengths.

### Hydrogen bonding

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen bond](#hydrogen-bond)

Formation of [hydrogen bonds](#hydrogen-bond), either within a molecule or between interacting molecules.

### Functional group interaction profile

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen bond](#hydrogen-bond)

A [functional group interaction profile](#functional-group-interaction-profile) maps the free-energy contribution of a solute donor–acceptor contact after accounting for competing solvent contacts. Solvent polarity and preferential solvation determine favourable and unfavourable regions.

### Hydrogen bond donor

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen bond](#hydrogen-bond)

A [hydrogen bond donor](#hydrogen-bond-donor) is the $X-\mathrm H$ group supplying the attached [hydrogen](chemical-substance.md#hydrogen) in a [hydrogen bond](#hydrogen-bond) $X-\mathrm H\cdots Y$. The donor atom $X$, or the whole [molecule](molecule.md) containing it, may also be called the donor. The terminology does not imply transfer of a free [proton](physics.md#proton): in the ordinary [water](chemical-substance.md#water) pair, the O–H [covalent bond](covalent-bond.md) remains. Familiar donors include O–H groups in [water](chemical-substance.md#water) and N–H groups in [ammonia](chemical-substance.md#ammonia). See [Andrew Glasfeld, Intermolecular Attraction, pp. 12–13: donor and acceptor roles](https://people.reed.edu/~glasfeld/Chem391/notes/ChIMF_2018.pdf).

<a id="image-hydrogen-bond-donor-and-hydrogen-bond-acceptor-roles-depend-on-attached-hydrogen-protonation-and-chemical-resonance"></a>
![](hydrogen-bond-donor-acceptor-site-map.png)

**[Figure 4](#image-hydrogen-bond-donor-and-hydrogen-bond-acceptor-roles-depend-on-attached-hydrogen-protonation-and-chemical-resonance). Hydrogen bond donor and hydrogen bond acceptor roles depend on attached hydrogen, protonation, and chemical resonance**. Toy site map for ordinary O–H and N–H associations, not measured geometry or an exhaustive list of [hydrogen bonds](#hydrogen-bond). $R$ and $R'$ denote groups attached through [carbon](chemical-substance.md#carbon). Coloured labels identify possible sites, rather than count the number of simultaneous contacts.

The attached [hydrogen](chemical-substance.md#hydrogen) is essential to this role. At the [nitrogen](chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) site, an unprotonated [amine](chemical-substance.md#amine) $R_3\mathrm N$ can be a [hydrogen bond acceptor](#hydrogen-bond-acceptor), but has no N–H [hydrogen bond donor](#hydrogen-bond-donor). [Protonation](chemical-reaction.md#protonation) gives $R_3\mathrm{NH}^+$: the new N–H group can donate, while that [nitrogen](chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) no longer has the accepting [lone pair](covalent-bond.md#lone-pair). This is a change of chemical form, not a permanent label attached to a [chemical element](chemical-substance.md#chemical-element). Here $R$ denotes a group attached through [carbon](chemical-substance.md#carbon); the statement concerns the [nitrogen](chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) site and does not rule out weaker C–H associations elsewhere. See [McMurry, Organic Chemistry, 24.3: amine protonation](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/24-3-basicity-of-amines) and [Glasfeld: protonated amines as donors](https://people.reed.edu/~glasfeld/Chem391/notes/ChIMF_2018.pdf).

An [amide](chemical-substance.md#amide) with an N–H [covalent bond](covalent-bond.md) can donate even though its [nitrogen](chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) is ordinarily not an accepting site. For example, in $R-\mathrm C(=\mathrm O)-\mathrm{NH}-R'$, N–H is a [hydrogen bond donor](#hydrogen-bond-donor) and the [carbonyl group](#carbonyl-group)'s [oxygen](chemical-substance.md#oxygen) is a [hydrogen bond acceptor](#hydrogen-bond-acceptor). Replacing the remaining N–H by another group attached through [carbon](chemical-substance.md#carbon) removes this N–H donor without removing the accepting [oxygen](chemical-substance.md#oxygen). The analogous division of roles in a [peptide bond](protein.md#peptide-bond) helps explain [protein](protein.md) structure. See [Glasfeld: amide roles and biological interactions](https://people.reed.edu/~glasfeld/Chem391/notes/ChIMF_2018.pdf). The useful question is therefore which site supplies the [hydrogen](chemical-substance.md#hydrogen), rather than whether the whole substance is simply “hydrogen bonding”.

#### Hydrogen-bond donor parameter

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen bond donor](#hydrogen-bond-donor)

An empirical measure of donor strength on a specified scale, including weak interactions when the scale defines them. A small nonzero value does not require a conventional O–H or N–H group.

### Hydrogen bond acceptor

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen bond](#hydrogen-bond)

A [hydrogen bond acceptor](#hydrogen-bond-acceptor) supplies the electron-rich atom or group $Y$ interacting with the attached [hydrogen](chemical-substance.md#hydrogen) of a [hydrogen bond donor](#hydrogen-bond-donor) in $X-\mathrm H\cdots Y$. An [oxygen](chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [lone pair](covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) in [water](chemical-substance.md#water) is the familiar example. Acceptance describes the association, not necessarily acquisition of a [proton](physics.md#proton). A single [molecule](molecule.md), such as [water](chemical-substance.md#water) or [ammonia](chemical-substance.md#ammonia), can offer both [hydrogen bond donor](#hydrogen-bond-donor) and [hydrogen bond acceptor](#hydrogen-bond-acceptor) sites. See [Glasfeld: water and nitrogen-containing accepting sites](https://people.reed.edu/~glasfeld/Chem391/notes/ChIMF_2018.pdf).

A [lone pair](covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) drawn in a [Lewis structure](covalent-bond.md#lewis-structure) is a useful clue, not a universal test. At an ordinary neutral [amide](chemical-substance.md#amide) [nitrogen](chemical-substance.md#nitrogen), the nominal [lone pair](covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) participates in [chemical resonance](covalent-bond.md#resonance-chemistry) with the [carbonyl group](#carbonyl-group), so this [nitrogen](chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) is ordinarily excluded from lists of [hydrogen bond acceptors](#hydrogen-bond-acceptor); the [carbonyl group](#carbonyl-group)'s [oxygen](chemical-substance.md#oxygen) remains an accepting site. [Protonation](chemical-reaction.md#protonation) changes the test again: $\mathrm{NH}_4^+$ has four N–H [covalent bonds](covalent-bond.md) and no [nitrogen](chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) [lone pair](covalent-bond.md#lone-pair), whereas [ammonia](chemical-substance.md#ammonia) has three and one. These distinctions follow the actual chemical form, and accepting ability also depends on the partner and whether it can approach the site. See [McMurry: amide resonance and protonation](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/24-3-basicity-of-amines) and [Glasfeld: limitations of nitrogen accepting sites](https://people.reed.edu/~glasfeld/Chem391/notes/ChIMF_2018.pdf).

Conversely, an accepting group need not supply an atom-centred [lone pair](covalent-bond.md#lone-pair). The following [phenol](chemical-substance.md#phenol)–[benzene](chemical-substance.md#benzene) experiment probes an O–H donor interacting with the ring's distributed $\pi$ [electron density](chemistry.md#electron-density).

<a id="image-infrared-spectroscopy-distinguishes-free-and-associated-phenol-schematic-experimental-logic-no-plotted-or-simulated-spectra"></a>
![](hydrogen-bond-phenol-infrared-controls.png)

**[Figure 5](#image-infrared-spectroscopy-distinguishes-free-and-associated-phenol-schematic-experimental-logic-no-plotted-or-simulated-spectra). Infrared spectroscopy distinguishes free and associated phenol: schematic experimental logic, no plotted or simulated spectra**. Original toy layout of the comparisons reported by Saggu, Levinson and Boxer (2011). The connector identifies an O–H donor and a ring accepting region; it does not specify a measured [bond length](#bond-length) or the calculated contact geometry.

Saggu, Levinson and Boxer (2011) used [infrared spectroscopy](chemistry.md#infrared-spectroscopy) to compare $200\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$ [phenol](chemical-substance.md#phenol) in different [solvents](mixture.md#solvent). The O–H stretching peak was at $3611\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ in $\mathrm{CCl}_4$ and $3555.9\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ in [benzene](chemical-substance.md#benzene). To distinguish association from a general [solvent](mixture.md#solvent) effect, they diluted the ring-containing [chemical solutions](mixture.md#solution-chemistry) with $\mathrm{CCl}_4$: two peaks appeared, with the free peak nearly fixed while the associated peak depended on the partner. Replacing the O–H [hydrogen](chemical-substance.md#hydrogen) with [deuterium](chemical-substance.md#deuterium) reproduced the ordering of shifts for different partners. Thus the perturbed O–H group identifies the [hydrogen bond donor](#hydrogen-bond-donor); the ring supplies the [hydrogen bond acceptor](#hydrogen-bond-acceptor). A peak shift alone does not specify a unique [molecular geometry](molecule.md#molecular-geometry). [Saggu, Levinson and Boxer, Direct measurements of electric fields in weak OH···π hydrogen bonds, JACS 133 (2011), Figure 1, Table 1 and dilution controls](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3202663/)

The roles make recognition between [molecules](molecule.md) intelligible: exposed [hydrogen bond donors](#hydrogen-bond-donor) need compatible [hydrogen bond acceptors](#hydrogen-bond-acceptor) in reachable positions. A count of sites is a starting description, not a prediction of binding strength. In [water](chemical-substance.md#water), forming a contact between two partners can also replace their existing [hydrogen bonds](#hydrogen-bond) to [water](chemical-substance.md#water); the [Gibbs free energy](thermodynamic-potential.md#gibbs-free-energy) of association concerns the whole change. See [Glasfeld, pp. 15–16: competition with solvent interactions](https://people.reed.edu/~glasfeld/Chem391/notes/ChIMF_2018.pdf).

## Valence electron

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valence_electron)

A [valence electron](#valence-electron) belongs to the electronic arrangement used to describe a [chemical atom](physics.md#atom-physics)'s [chemical bonds](chemical-bond.md) and ordinary [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction.md). For the neutral [hydrogen](chemical-substance.md#hydrogen), [carbon](chemical-substance.md#carbon), [nitrogen](chemical-substance.md#nitrogen), and [oxygen](chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [chemical atoms](physics.md#atom-physics) used here, the [valence electrons](#valence-electron) occupy the outermost occupied [electron shell](chemistry.md#electron-shell). Their respective [electron configurations](chemistry.md#electron-configuration) are $1s^1$, $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^2$, $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^3$, and $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^4$, giving one, four, five, and six [valence electrons](#valence-electron). See [Chemistry 2e, 6.4: electron configurations and valence electrons](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/6-4-electronic-structure-of-atoms-electron-configurations).

[Lewis structures](covalent-bond.md#lewis-structure) show these [valence electrons](#valence-electron) while omitting the inner [electrons](physics.md#electron). Consequently, eight drawn [valence electrons](#valence-electron) in [water](chemical-substance.md#water) do not mean eight total [electrons](physics.md#electron): neutral [water](chemical-substance.md#water) has ten, with two inner [oxygen](chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [electrons](physics.md#electron) omitted. Neutral [ammonia](chemical-substance.md#ammonia) likewise has ten total [electrons](physics.md#electron) but eight [valence electrons](#valence-electron); neutral [carbon dioxide](chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) has twenty-two total [electrons](physics.md#electron) but sixteen [valence electrons](#valence-electron).

This distinction makes the small counts useful: connections and possible [lone pairs](covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) can be organized without representing every [electron](physics.md#electron) or solving the entire many-[electron](physics.md#electron) [wavefunction](wave-function.md). The outermost-[electron shell](chemistry.md#electron-shell) shortcut applies to the examples above, rather than serving as a universal definition for all [chemical elements](chemical-substance.md#chemical-element); the electronic arrangement relevant to [chemical bonds](chemical-bond.md) can also involve other [electron subshells](chemistry.md#electron-subshell).
