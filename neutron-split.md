# Neutron

↑ **Parent:** [Nucleon](nucleon.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron)

The neutron is the electrically neutral spin-one-half baryon found with [protons](proton.md) in most atomic nuclei.

A neutron is a [nucleon](nucleon.md): it contributes to an [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md) without adding [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md). Its [spin one-half](spin-one-half.md) makes it a [fermion](fermion.md), and it belongs to the [baryons](baryon.md). Electrical neutrality does not mean an absence of interactions: a neutron can transfer [momentum](momentum-split.md) and [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) to an [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md), producing a charged [nuclear recoil](nuclear-recoil.md) that a [particle detector](particle-detector.md) can register. This indirect route is central to [neutron detection by proton recoil](neutron-detection-by-proton-recoil.md).

The beautiful feature of the [Chadwick neutron discovery experiment](chadwick-neutron-discovery-experiment.md) is that an unseen projectile acquired a measurable [mass](mass.md) through the motion of things it struck. Penetration alone left competing explanations open; combining [nuclear recoils](nuclear-recoil.md) from different target [masses](mass.md) supplied a much stronger test.
