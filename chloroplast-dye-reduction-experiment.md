# Chloroplast dye-reduction experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Hill reaction](hill-reaction.md)

The Practical Biology protocol uses cold [sucrose](sucrose.md) in [buffer solution](buffer-solution.md), ground green leaves, muslin filtration, [centrifugation](centrifugation.md) and cold pellet resuspension. A $100\,\mathrm{W}$ lamp illuminates extract plus [DCPIP](dichlorophenolindophenol.md) from $12$–$15\,\mathrm{cm}$; a red-filter colorimeter monitors blue-color loss.[Apparatus and Chemicals; Technical note 2; Procedure a–k; Sample results, Using a bench centrifuge: EEL colorimeter with red filter](investigating-the-light-dependent-reaction-in-photosynthesis-at-practical-biology.md)

Compare extract plus dye in darkness, illuminated dye without extract, extract without dye, and [supernatant](supernatant.md) plus dye: controls for [light](light.md), extract dependence, background color and incomplete separation. Green [supernatant](supernatant.md) can remain active; a [centrifugation pellet](centrifugation-pellet.md) establishes neither purity nor intact [chloroplasts](chloroplast.md).[Procedure i–k, five-tube table; Teaching notes; Questions 1–3 and answers: control interpretations and green-supernatant limitation](investigating-the-light-dependent-reaction-in-photosynthesis-at-practical-biology.md)

Here is an original logical check of the controls. Suppose only the illuminated extract-plus-dye condition loses blue color, while the dark mixture and illuminated dye-only mixture retain it. Removing [light](light.md) or removing extract each removes the effect in those comparisons. That supports a joint requirement under the tested conditions. If the dye-only mixture also loses blue color, the proposed attribution to extract has failed its control; a faster change in the extract mixture would require a quantitative comparison before attribution. These are conditional cases, not experimental results.

Even the favorable case leaves three distinct questions. Dye [reduction](reduction-chemistry.md) demonstrates delivery of [electrons](electron.md) to an artificial [electron acceptor](electron-acceptor.md). An independently calibrated [molecular oxygen](molecular-oxygen.md) readout is needed to measure [molecular oxygen](molecular-oxygen.md) evolution. Demonstrating [carbon fixation](carbon-fixation.md) additionally requires evidence that [carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md) becomes incorporated into organic material. None of the latter quantities is supplied by a blue-color endpoint alone. An [absorbance](absorbance.md) change also needs background and scattering controls before it can be treated as dye [concentration](concentration.md); [Beer-Lambert law](beer-lambert-law.md) states the relevant assumptions.

In particular, two hypothetical preparations can give the same dye endpoint while differing in whether their stromal [Calvin cycle](calvin-cycle.md) is functional. Observing only the dye discards that distinction. The counterexample establishes why dye decolorization cannot alone prove complete [photosynthesis](photosynthesis.md), [carbon fixation](carbon-fixation.md), or an intact [chloroplast](chloroplast.md). [Temperature](temperature-split.md), extract loading and illumination must also be matched when comparing rates: a lamp can alter both [light](light.md) exposure and heating.
