# Pseudo-first-order reaction

↑ **Parent:** [Order of reaction](order-of-reaction.md)

In a [second-order reaction](second-order-reaction.md) $\mathrm A+\mathrm B\longrightarrow\mathrm P$ with [rate law](rate-law.md) $-d[\mathrm A]/dt=k_2[\mathrm A][\mathrm B]$, a large excess of B can keep $[\mathrm B]\approx[\mathrm B]_0$ during the observation window. Then

$$
-\frac{d[\mathrm A]}{dt}\approx k_1[\mathrm A],\qquad k_1=k_2[\mathrm B]_0,\qquad
[\mathrm A](t)\approx[\mathrm A]_0e^{-k_1t}.
$$

This is a [pseudo-first-order reaction](pseudo-first-order-reaction.md): the approximate [first-order reaction](first-order-reaction.md) describes that window, while changing the excess B [concentration](concentration.md) changes $k_1$. Its units are $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, distinct from the [second-order reaction](second-order-reaction.md) [rate constant](rate-constant.md) $k_2$. For one-to-one [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md), the maximum fractional depletion of B is $[\mathrm A]_0/[\mathrm B]_0$; an original $100:1$ initial excess bounds its change by $1\%$. A twofold excess permits $50\%$ depletion and does not provide the same control.[Glossary of Terms Used in Physical Organic Chemistry, IUPAC Recommendations 1994, entry “order of reaction”: constant B, observed coefficient and pseudo-first-order coefficient](glossary-of-terms-used-in-physical-organic-chemistry-iupac-recommendations-1994-n-and-o-entries-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry.md)
