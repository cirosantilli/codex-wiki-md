# Bounded rational sequences can converge outside the rationals

↑ **Parent:** [Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem](bolzano-weierstrass-theorem.md)

The [rational numbers](rational-number.md) $q_n=\lfloor10^n\sqrt2\rfloor/10^n$ lie in $[1,2]$ and obey $0\leq\sqrt2-q_n<10^{-n}$. They form a [convergent sequence](convergent-sequence.md) in the [real numbers](real-number.md), with [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) $\sqrt2$. Every [subsequence](subsequence.md) has that same real limit. Because $\sqrt2$ is an [irrational number](irrational-number.md), no [subsequence](subsequence.md) converges to a point in the [rational numbers](rational-number.md) with their usual [metric](metric.md). Boundedness alone therefore does not give the corresponding theorem in every [metric space](metric-space.md); [completeness of the real numbers](completeness-of-the-real-numbers.md) is doing real work here. The [floor function](floor-function.md) simply truncates the decimal expansion in this example.
