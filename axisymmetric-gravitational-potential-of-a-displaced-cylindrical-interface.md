# Axisymmetric gravitational potential of a displaced cylindrical interface

↑ **Parent:** [Newtonian potential of a point mass](newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md)

Consider a uniform-density cylinder with an axisymmetric radial [Fourier mode](fourier-mode.md) $\eta=\epsilon e^{ikz}$. Its displaced boundary gives a first-order [surface mass density](projected-surface-mass-density.md) $\rho\eta$, although the interior density perturbation vanishes. The gravitational [Poisson equation](poisson-s-equation.md) reduces to [Laplace equation](laplace-equation.md) away from the interface. Regularity internally and decay externally give $\psi_{\rm in}=AI_0(kr)e^{ikz}$ and $\psi_{\rm out}=BK_0(kr)e^{ikz}$. Continuity at $R$ and the derivative jump $\psi'_{\rm out}-\psi'_{\rm in}=4\pi G\rho\epsilon e^{ikz}$, together with $I_0(\kappa)K'_0(\kappa)-I'_0(\kappa)K_0(\kappa)=-1/\kappa$, give the title amplitude. The prime differentiates the [modified Bessel function](modified-bessel-function.md) argument. This axial mode is different from an azimuthal mode on a [gravitational potential of a displaced cylindrical interface](gravitational-potential-of-a-displaced-cylindrical-interface.md).
