# Cesaro theorem for convergent sequences

↑ **Parent:** [Cesaro mean](cesaro-mean.md)

If a [sequence](sequence.md) converges, its [Cesaro means](cesaro-mean.md) have the same limit. Given a small tolerance, split the average error into a fixed finite initial segment and a tail whose individual errors are below that tolerance. The first contribution is a fixed constant divided by $n$, while the second is bounded by the tolerance. The converse fails: the alternating [sequence](sequence.md) $(-1)^n$ has means tending to zero while its terms do not converge.
