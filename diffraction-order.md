# Diffraction order

↑ **Parent:** [Diffraction grating](diffraction-grating.md)

A diffraction order is the [integer](integer-split.md) $m$ labeling a principal [constructive interference](constructive-interference.md) direction of a [diffraction grating](diffraction-grating.md): adjacent elements differ in path length by $m$ [wavelengths](wavelength.md). In the normal-incidence transmission geometry described above,

$$
\sin\theta_m=\frac{m\lambda}{d},\qquad \left|\frac{m\lambda}{d}\right|\leq1.
$$

Positive and negative [diffraction orders](diffraction-order.md) lie on opposite sides of the forward normal. The condition expresses the range of the [sine](sine.md) of a real [angle](angle.md), so it also limits the propagating [diffraction orders](diffraction-order.md).

For $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $d=2\,\mathrm{\mu m}$, $\sin\theta_m=0.3m$. Taking the [inverse sine](inverse-sine.md) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
m=0 &: \quad \theta_0=0^\circ,\\
m=\pm1 &: \quad \theta_m=\pm17.458^\circ,\\
m=\pm2 &: \quad \theta_m=\pm36.870^\circ,\\
m=\pm3 &: \quad \theta_m=\pm64.158^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

These are seven allowed propagating [diffraction orders](diffraction-order.md). Order $m=4$ would require $\sin\theta_4=1.2$, and $m=-4$ would require $-1.2$, so neither has a propagating far-field direction. The allowed [diffraction orders](diffraction-order.md) are equally spaced in $\sin\theta$, while their actual [angles](angle.md) become farther apart toward grazing propagation.

Each principal peak has finite width. For the ideal uniform coherent array, the nearest zeros around order $m$ obey

$$
\sin\theta_{m,\pm}=\frac{m\lambda}{d}\pm\frac{\lambda}{Nd}.
$$

Thus the one-sided distance from the order direction to the first zero is $\Delta(\sin\theta)=\lambda/(Nd)$. Near the first [diffraction order](diffraction-order.md) in our example, $N=10$ puts the zeros at $0.27$ and $0.33$, while $N=40$ puts them at $0.2925$ and $0.3075$. The respective one-sided widths are $0.03$ and $0.0075$; the full zero-to-zero widths are $0.06$ and $0.015$. These are calculated coherent peak widths, with the slit envelope omitted, and do not describe a measured instrument response. [Richard Fitzpatrick: Principal maxima and their neighbouring zeros, equations 10.39-10.42](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/315/Waveshtml/node93.html).

Four times as many illuminated slits therefore give a fourfold narrowing in this coordinate without moving the [diffraction order](diffraction-order.md). That sharper directional selection helps distinguish nearby [wavelengths](wavelength.md) in a [spectrograph](spectrograph.md). Actual performance also depends on illumination, [optical coherence](optical-coherence.md), and source width; the separate [slit-limited resolving power of a grating](slit-limited-resolving-power-of-a-grating.md) describes the contribution of the entrance slit.
