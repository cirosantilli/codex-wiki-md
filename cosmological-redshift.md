# Cosmological redshift

↑ **Parent:** [Scale factor (cosmology)](scale-factor-cosmology.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_redshift)

The [cosmological redshift](cosmological-redshift.md) is the stretching of a freely propagating [photon](photon.md)'s [wavelength](wavelength.md) between emission at [cosmic time](cosmic-time.md) $t_e$ and reception at $t_0$ in an [FLRW metric](friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md). For a [comoving observer](comoving-observer.md) at each endpoint,

$$
1+z=\frac{\lambda_0}{\lambda_e}=\frac{a(t_0)}{a(t_e)},\qquad
\frac{\nu_0}{\nu_e}=\frac{1}{1+z}.
$$

Here $\lambda_e,\lambda_0$ are emitted and received [wavelengths](wavelength.md), and $\nu_e,\nu_0$ are the corresponding [frequencies](frequency.md). A positive [cosmological redshift](cosmological-redshift.md) means that the [scale factor](scale-factor-cosmology.md) grew while the [photon](photon.md) travelled. The name describes a shift toward longer [wavelengths](wavelength.md); it applies to light beyond the visible range as well.

For a computed example, let the [scale factor](scale-factor-cosmology.md) double from $a(t_e)=0.5$ to $a(t_0)=1$. An emitted [wavelength](wavelength.md) of $500\,\mathrm{nm}$ becomes $1000\,\mathrm{nm}$, giving $z=1000/500-1=1$. The received [frequency](frequency.md) is half the emitted [frequency](frequency.md). This determines a ratio of spatial rulers, not a recession speed: using $cz$ as an actual [velocity](velocity.md) at $z=1$ is unjustified. The [wavelength](wavelength.md) panel in the illustration under [Hubble's law](hubble-s-law.md) shows this doubling.

A short derivation follows successive [wave](wave.md) crests along a [radial null geodesic in FLRW spacetime](radial-null-geodesic-in-flrw-spacetime.md). Their source and receiver have fixed [comoving coordinates](comoving-coordinate.md), so each crest traverses the same [comoving distance](comoving-radial-distance.md):

$$
\chi=\int_{t_e}^{t_0}\frac{c\,dt}{a(t)}.
$$

Shift both endpoint times to the next crest while keeping $\chi$ fixed. To first order in the very short periods $\delta t_e,\delta t_0$, this gives $\delta t_0/a(t_0)=\delta t_e/a(t_e)$. Locally, [wavelength](wavelength.md) equals the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) times the crest period, yielding the displayed [cosmological redshift](cosmological-redshift.md) relation. These periods are much shorter than the timescale on which the [scale factor](scale-factor-cosmology.md) changes.

The relation isolates the smooth expansion contribution. An observed [redshift](redshift.md) can also contain a [Doppler effect](doppler-effect.md) from source or observer [peculiar velocity](peculiar-velocity.md) and a [gravitational redshift](gravitational-redshift.md). See [combined kinematic and cosmological redshift](combined-kinematic-and-cosmological-redshift.md) for the multiplicative combination of endpoint motion and expansion. Treating every observed [redshift](redshift.md) as pure [cosmological redshift](cosmological-redshift.md) can therefore misassign a nearby source's distance.

The useful insight is that a received [wavelength](wavelength.md) ratio records how much the [scale factor](scale-factor-cosmology.md) changed during the [photon](photon.md)'s journey. Turning that ratio into a distance requires an expansion history, through the [Hubble parameter](hubble-parameter.md), and a specified convention such as [luminosity distance](luminosity-distance.md) or [proper distance in cosmology](proper-distance-in-cosmology.md); the ratio alone supplies neither.
