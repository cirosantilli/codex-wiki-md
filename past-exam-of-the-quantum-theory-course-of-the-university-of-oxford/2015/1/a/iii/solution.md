<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

By [linearity](../../../../../linearity.md) of the [time-independent Schrödinger equation](../../../../../time-independent-schrodinger-equation.md), the even and odd parts

$$
\psi_+(x)=\frac{\psi(x)+\psi(-x)}2,\qquad \psi_-(x)=\frac{\psi(x)-\psi(-x)}2
$$

each satisfy the same [energy eigenvalue](../../../../../energy-eigenvalue-split.md) equation whenever they are nonzero. They obey $R\psi_+=\psi_+$ and $R\psi_-=-\psi_-$, so they have definite [parity](../../../../../parity.md). At least one is nonzero, and $\psi=\psi_++\psi_-$. Each nonzero part can be normalized separately.

Therefore **a basis of stationary solutions can be chosen to have definite [parity](../../../../../parity.md)**, and solving the even and odd sectors loses no [energy eigenvalues](../../../../../energy-eigenvalue-split.md). This does not assert that every possible [quantum superposition](../../../../../quantum-superposition.md) has definite [parity](../../../../../parity.md); a superposition of the two sectors need not. For one-dimensional [bound states](../../../../../bound-state.md) in the usual regular setting, [nondegeneracy of one-dimensional bound states](../../../../../nondegeneracy-of-one-dimensional-bound-states.md) further implies that each individual [energy eigenstate](../../../../../energy-eigenstate.md) has definite [parity](../../../../../parity.md).
