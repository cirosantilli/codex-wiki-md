<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $\lambda=3/4$, the [adjoint operator](../../../../../adjoint-operator.md) relation $J_+^*=J_-$ and the given product identity imply

$$
\|J_+\psi\|^2=\langle\psi,J_-J_+\psi\rangle
=\hbar^2\left(\frac34-\frac14-\frac12\right)=0.
$$

A vector of zero [norm](../../../../../norm.md) is zero, so $J_+\psi=0$. Part (i) gives

$$
\boxed{\|\varphi\|^2=\hbar^2,\qquad \|\varphi\|=\hbar.}
$$

The [commutator](../../../../../commutator.md) $[J^2,J_-]=0$ also gives $J^2\varphi=(3/4)\hbar^2\varphi$, while $J_3\varphi=-(\hbar/2)\varphi$. Applying the other product identity to this unnormalized vector,

$$
\|J_-\varphi\|^2=\langle\varphi,J_+J_-\varphi\rangle
=\hbar^2\left(\frac34-\frac14-\frac12\right)\|\varphi\|^2=0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{J_+\psi=0,\qquad J_-\varphi=0.}
$$

These are the two [angular momentum ladder endpoints](../../../../../angular-momentum-ladder-endpoint.md) of a [spin one-half](../../../../../spin-one-half.md) pair. In particular, $\psi_2=\varphi/\hbar$ has unit [norm](../../../../../norm.md), and $J_+\psi_2=\hbar\psi_1$ because $J_+J_-\psi=\hbar^2\psi$.
