<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Centre the [position operator](../../../../position-operator.md) and [momentum operator](../../../../momentum-operator.md) by their real [expectation values](../../../../expectation-value.md):

$$
A=X-x_0I,\quad B=P-p_0I,\qquad x_0=\mathbb E_\psi(X),\quad p_0=\mathbb E_\psi(P).
$$

Scalar multiples of the [identity operator](../../../../identity-operator.md) commute with every [linear operator](../../../../linear-operator.md), so the [canonical commutation relation](../../../../canonical-commutation-relation.md) gives

$$
[A,B]=[X,P]=i\hbar I,\qquad C=\hbar I,\qquad \mathbb E_\psi(C)=\hbar.
$$

The second moments of the centred [linear operators](../../../../linear-operator.md) are the [variances](../../../../variance-split.md), $\mathbb E_\psi(A^2)=\Delta_\psi(X)^2$ and $\mathbb E_\psi(B^2)=\Delta_\psi(P)^2$. Applying the preceding inequality and taking nonnegative square roots proves the [Heisenberg uncertainty relation](../../../../heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md):

$$
\Delta_\psi(X)^2\Delta_\psi(P)^2\geq\frac{\hbar^2}4,\qquad \boxed{\Delta_\psi(X)\Delta_\psi(P)\geq\frac\hbar2.}
$$

**Subtracting the means is essential**: the uncentred second moments alone do not equal the [variances](../../../../variance-split.md). The [equality case of the Heisenberg uncertainty relation](../../../../equality-case-of-the-heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md) requires the centred vectors to be imaginary multiples, as in the preceding equality conditions.
