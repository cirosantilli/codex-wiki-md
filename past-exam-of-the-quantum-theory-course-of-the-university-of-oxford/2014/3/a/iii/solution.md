<h1 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $a=\mathbb E_\psi(A)$, which is real. The dispersion, or [quantum uncertainty](../../../../../quantum-uncertainty.md), is the nonnegative [standard deviation](../../../../../standard-deviation.md)

$$
\boxed{\Delta_\psi(A)=\sqrt{\mathbb E_\psi(A^2)-\mathbb E_\psi(A)^2}=\|(A-aI)\psi\|.}
$$

Indeed, [self-adjointness](../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md) and normalization give

$$
\|(A-aI)\psi\|^2=\langle\psi,(A-aI)^2\psi\rangle=\mathbb E_\psi(A^2)-2a\mathbb E_\psi(A)+a^2=\mathbb E_\psi(A^2)-a^2.
$$

A squared [norm](../../../../../norm.md) is nonnegative, so the [square root](../../../../../square-root.md) is defined. It vanishes precisely when $(A-aI)\psi=0$, namely when $\psi$ is an [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $A$. Conversely, an [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) with [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$ has $a=\lambda$ and hence zero dispersion. Thus **zero dispersion is equivalent to being an eigenstate**.
