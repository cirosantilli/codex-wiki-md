↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [commutator](../../../../../commutator.md) of two [linear operators](../../../../../linear-operator.md) is $\boxed{[A,B]=AB-BA}$. Write $T^\dagger$ for the [adjoint operator](../../../../../adjoint-operator.md), using the [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) convention linear in its second argument. Since $A^\dagger=A$ and $B^\dagger=B$,

$$
[A,B]^\dagger=B^\dagger A^\dagger-A^\dagger B^\dagger=BA-AB=-[A,B].
$$

Thus the [commutator](../../../../../commutator.md) is anti-self-adjoint. With $C=-i[A,B]$, conjugation of the scalar gives

$$
\boxed{C^\dagger=i[A,B]^\dagger=-i[A,B]=C.}
$$

All products are understood on the stated common [operator domain](../../../../../operator-domain.md); for physical unbounded [linear operators](../../../../../linear-operator.md), the state must lie in the relevant product domains and have the finite moments used below.
