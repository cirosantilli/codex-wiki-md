<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A normalized [quantum state](../../../../../quantum-state.md) satisfies $\langle\psi,\psi\rangle=1$. Its [expectation value](../../../../../expectation-value.md) is $\boxed{\mathbb E_\psi(A)=\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle}$. The conjugate symmetry of the [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) and [self-adjointness](../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md) give

$$
\overline{\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle}=\langle A\psi,\psi\rangle=\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle,
$$

so the [expectation value](../../../../../expectation-value.md) is real. If $A\phi=\lambda\phi$ with $\phi\ne0$, normalize $\phi$ by dividing by its [norm](../../../../../norm.md). Then

$$
\lambda=\langle\phi/\|\phi\|,A\phi/\|\phi\|\rangle\in\mathbb R.
$$

**Every eigenvalue of a self-adjoint operator is real.**
