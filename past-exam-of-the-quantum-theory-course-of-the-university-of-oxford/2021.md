## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2021](2021.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

For a [free particle](../quantum-mechanics.md#free-particle), the [Hamiltonian operator](../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics) is $H=P^2/(2m)$, where $P=-i\hbar\,d/dx$ is the [momentum operator](../quantum-mechanics.md#momentum-operator). The [time-independent Schrödinger equation](../mathematical-physics.md#time-independent-schrodinger-equation) is therefore

$$
\boxed{-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2}=E\psi,\qquad \psi(x+2\pi)=\psi(x).}
$$

The full [stationary state](../quantum-system.md#stationary-state) is $\Psi(x,t)=e^{-iEt/\hbar}\psi(x)$. The [periodic boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#periodic-boundary-conditions) identifies $x$ and $x+2\pi$, giving a [particle on a circle](../quantum-mechanics.md#particle-in-a-ring) with [wavefunction normalization](../wave-function.md#wavefunction-normalization) over one period.

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

For a [momentum eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#momentum-eigenstate) with [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $p$, the equation $-i\hbar\psi'=p\psi$ has solution $\psi(x)=Ce^{ipx/\hbar}$. The [periodic boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#periodic-boundary-conditions) imposes $e^{2\pi ip/\hbar}=1$, so $p/\hbar\in\mathbb Z$. The [wavefunction normalization](../wave-function.md#wavefunction-normalization) gives $1=2\pi|C|^2$. Choosing a convenient [global phase](../quantum-system.md#global-phase) yields

$$
\boxed{\psi_n(x)=\frac{e^{inx}}{\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad p_n=n\hbar,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

These [momentum eigenstates](../energy-eigenvalue.md#momentum-eigenstate) form an [orthonormal basis](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-basis), the [Fourier basis](../fourier-series.md#fourier-basis) for the [particle on a circle](../quantum-mechanics.md#particle-in-a-ring):

$$
\langle\psi_n,\psi_k\rangle=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{i(k-n)x}\,dx=\delta_{nk}.
$$

Here $\delta_{nk}$ is the [Kronecker delta](../multilinear-algebra.md#kronecker-delta).

<h4 id="1/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/a/iii)

Since $P\psi_n=n\hbar\psi_n$, applying the [Hamiltonian operator](../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics) gives

$$
H\psi_n=\frac{P^2}{2m}\psi_n=\frac{n^2\hbar^2}{2m}\psi_n.
$$

Thus the [energy eigenvalues](../energy-eigenvalue.md) and corresponding full [stationary states](../quantum-system.md#stationary-state) are

$$
\boxed{E_n=\frac{n^2\hbar^2}{2m},\qquad \Psi_n(x,t)=e^{-in^2\hbar t/(2m)}\psi_n(x).}
$$

The [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) is $\psi_0$ with [energy](../energy.md) zero and [quantum degeneracy](../quantum-mechanics.md#degenerate-energy-levels) one. For each $n=1,2,\ldots$, the positive [energy level](../quantum-mechanics.md#energy-level) $n^2\hbar^2/(2m)$ has [quantum degeneracy](../quantum-mechanics.md#degenerate-energy-levels) two: its [energy eigenspace](../energy-eigenvalue.md#energy-eigenspace) is $\operatorname{span}\{\psi_n,\psi_{-n}\}$. This is the [free particle spectrum on a circle](../quantum-mechanics.md#free-particle-spectrum-on-a-circle).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

The identity $\cos^2x=\tfrac12+\tfrac14e^{2ix}+\tfrac14e^{-2ix}$ gives the [momentum eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#momentum-eigenstate) expansion

$$
\Psi(x,0)=A\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac12\psi_0+\frac14\psi_2+\frac14\psi_{-2}\right).
$$

Using the [orthonormal basis](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-basis) from part (a), the [wavefunction normalization](../wave-function.md#wavefunction-normalization) is

$$
1=|A|^2(2\pi)\left(\frac14+\frac1{16}+\frac1{16}\right)=|A|^2\frac{3\pi}{4}.
$$

Hence $|A|=2/\sqrt{3\pi}$. The [wavefunction normalization](../wave-function.md#wavefunction-normalization) leaves a [global phase](../quantum-system.md#global-phase) free; the positive real choice used in the requested expansion gives

$$
\boxed{A=\frac2{\sqrt{3\pi}},\qquad \Psi(x,0)=\sqrt{\frac23}\left(\psi_0(x)+\frac12\psi_2(x)+\frac12\psi_{-2}(x)\right).}
$$

The two nonzero [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) of the [momentum operator](../quantum-mechanics.md#momentum-operator) have the same [energy eigenvalue](../energy-eigenvalue.md) $E_\star=2\hbar^2/m$. Under [unitary time evolution](../quantum-system.md#unitary-time-evolution), their coefficients acquire the same [dynamical phase](../quantum-system.md#dynamical-phase). With $\Omega=E_\star/\hbar=2\hbar/m$,

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Psi(x,t)&=\sqrt{\frac23}\left(\psi_0(x)+\frac{e^{-i\Omega t}}2\psi_2(x)+\frac{e^{-i\Omega t}}2\psi_{-2}(x)\right)\\
&=\frac{1+e^{-i\Omega t}\cos(2x)}{\sqrt{3\pi}}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

At $t=0$, $1+\cos(2x)=2\cos^2x$ recovers the initial [wavefunction](../wave-function.md). Its [probability density](../wave-function.md#probability-density), useful below, is

$$
|\Psi(x,t)|^2=\frac{1+\cos^2(2x)+2\cos(2x)\cos(\Omega t)}{3\pi}.
$$

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/c/i)

The [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) is the single [energy eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#energy-eigenstate) $\psi_0$. Its coefficient in the normalized [wavefunction](../wave-function.md) is $\sqrt{2/3}$, so the [Born rule](../wave-function.md#born-rule) gives

$$
\boxed{\Pr(E=0)=\left|\sqrt{\frac23}\right|^2=\frac23.}
$$

This [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) is independent of $t$ because [unitary time evolution](../quantum-system.md#unitary-time-evolution) changes only the [dynamical phases](../quantum-system.md#dynamical-phase) of the [energy eigenstates](../energy-eigenvalue.md#energy-eigenstate).

<h4 id="1/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/c/ii)

The coefficient of the [momentum eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#momentum-eigenstate) $\psi_2$ is $e^{-i\Omega t}/\sqrt6$. The [Born rule](../wave-function.md#born-rule) therefore gives $\Pr(P=2\hbar)=1/6$. Conditional on this outcome, the [projective measurement](../quantum-measurement.md#projective-measurement) prepares the [post-measurement state](../quantum-measurement.md#post-measurement-state) $e^{-i\Omega t}\psi_2$, equivalent to $\psi_2$ up to [global phase](../quantum-system.md#global-phase).

Since $H\psi_2=(4\hbar^2/(2m))\psi_2$, the subsequent [energy](../energy.md) measurement has [conditional probability](../probability-theory.md#conditional-probability) one of giving the specified [energy eigenvalue](../energy-eigenvalue.md). The [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) for the entire sequence is consequently

$$
\boxed{\Pr\left(P=2\hbar\ \text{then}\ E=\frac{4\hbar^2}{2m}\right)=\frac16\times1=\frac16.}
$$

Any intervening [unitary time evolution](../quantum-system.md#unitary-time-evolution) under the same [Hamiltonian operator](../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics) preserves this [energy eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#energy-eigenstate), up to [global phase](../quantum-system.md#global-phase).

<h4 id="1/c/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/c/iii)

For a [position measurement](../quantum-measurement.md#position-measurement), the [Born rule](../wave-function.md#born-rule) integrates the [probability density](../wave-function.md#probability-density) over the chosen interval. The three terms from part (b) have integrals

$$
\int_0^{\pi/4}1\,dx=\frac\pi4,\qquad
\int_0^{\pi/4}\cos^2(2x)\,dx=\left[\frac x2+\frac{\sin4x}{8}\right]_0^{\pi/4}=\frac\pi8,
$$

and

$$
\int_0^{\pi/4}2\cos(2x)\cos(\Omega t)\,dx=\left[\sin(2x)\cos(\Omega t)\right]_0^{\pi/4}=\cos(\Omega t).
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\Pr\left(0\leq x\leq\frac\pi4\right)=\frac18+\frac1{3\pi}\cos\left(\frac{2\hbar t}{m}\right).}
$$

The oscillating term comes from [quantum interference](../quantum-system.md#quantum-interference) between the zero and positive [energy](../energy.md) components. The [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) lies between $1/8-1/(3\pi)$ and $1/8+1/(3\pi)$, both within $[0,1]$; integrating the same [probability density](../wave-function.md#probability-density) over the whole circle gives one.

<h4 id="1/c/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#1/c/iv)

The [parity operator](../quantum-mechanics.md#parity-operator) exchanges $\psi_n$ and $\psi_{-n}$. Their coefficients in the [wavefunction](../wave-function.md) are equal, so

$$
(R\Psi)(x,t)=\Psi(-x,t)=\frac{1+e^{-i\Omega t}\cos(-2x)}{\sqrt{3\pi}}=\Psi(x,t).
$$

Thus the [wavefunction](../wave-function.md) is a [parity operator](../quantum-mechanics.md#parity-operator) [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) with [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $+1$ at every time. The [orthogonal projection](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection) onto the $-1$ [eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) is $(I-R)/2$, which annihilates it. By the [Born rule](../wave-function.md#born-rule),

$$
\boxed{\Pr(R=-1)=0.}
$$

↑ **Parent:** [2021](2021.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

The [position operators](../quantum-mechanics.md#position-operator) and [momentum operators](../quantum-mechanics.md#momentum-operator) are [self-adjoint operators](../linear-operator-theory.md#self-adjoint-operator). Using conjugate linearity of the [adjoint operator](../hilbert-space.md#adjoint-operator), $(cA+dB)^*=\overline cA^*+\overline dB^*$, gives

$$
\boxed{a_j^*=\frac{m\omega X_j-iP_j}{\sqrt{2m\omega\hbar}}.}
$$

Write $D=\sqrt{2m\omega\hbar}$. Bilinearity and antisymmetry of the [commutator](../lie-algebra.md#commutator), together with the [canonical commutation relations](../quantum-mechanics.md#canonical-commutation-relation), give $[P_j,X_k]=-i\hbar\delta_{jk}I$. Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
D^2[a_j,a_k]&=im\omega[X_j,P_k]+im\omega[P_j,X_k]\\
&=-m\omega\hbar\delta_{jk}I+m\omega\hbar\delta_{jk}I=0,\\
D^2[a_j,a_k^*]&=-im\omega[X_j,P_k]+im\omega[P_j,X_k]\\
&=2m\omega\hbar\delta_{jk}I.
\end{aligned}
$$

The terms involving two [position operators](../quantum-mechanics.md#position-operator) or two [momentum operators](../quantum-mechanics.md#momentum-operator) vanish by the [canonical commutation relations](../quantum-mechanics.md#canonical-commutation-relation). Finally, reversing products under the [adjoint operator](../hilbert-space.md#adjoint-operator), $(AB)^*=B^*A^*$, gives $[a_j,a_k]^*=-[a_j^*,a_k^*]$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{[a_j,a_k]=[a_j^*,a_k^*]=0,\qquad [a_j,a_k^*]=\delta_{jk}I.}
$$

Here $I$ is the [identity operator](../vector-space.md#identity-operator). These are the relations for two independent pairs of [creation and annihilation operators](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#creation-and-annihilation-operators). The [operator algebra](../functional-analysis.md#operator-algebra) in this question is understood on a common invariant [operator domain](../vector-space.md#operator-domain), such as finite [linear combinations](../vector-space.md#linear-combination) of the [number states](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#number-state) constructed in part (c).

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

First apply the [commutator product rule](../lie-algebra.md#commutator-product-rule) in the form $[AB,C]=A[B,C]+[A,C]B$:

$$
[N,a_j^*]=\sum_{k=1}^2\left(a_k^*[a_k,a_j^*]+[a_k^*,a_j^*]a_k\right)=a_j^*.
$$

Repeated use of the [commutator product rule](../lie-algebra.md#commutator-product-rule) gives, for $n\geq1$,

$$
[N,(a_j^*)^n]=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}(a_j^*)^r[N,a_j^*](a_j^*)^{n-1-r}
=n(a_j^*)^n.
$$

This is the [commutator with powers of an eigenoperator](../lie-algebra.md#commutator-with-powers-of-an-eigenoperator). For $n=0$, the [commutator](../lie-algebra.md#commutator) with the [identity operator](../vector-space.md#identity-operator) is zero, agreeing with the formula.

Each $a_k^*a_k$ is unchanged by taking the [adjoint operator](../hilbert-space.md#adjoint-operator), so $N^*=N$ in this algebra. Taking the [adjoint of a commutator](../hilbert-space.md#adjoint-of-a-commutator) yields $[N,(a_j^*)^n]^*=[a_j^n,N]=na_j^n$. By antisymmetry,

$$
\boxed{[N,(a_j^*)^n]=n(a_j^*)^n,\qquad [N,a_j^n]=-na_j^n,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.}
$$

In particular, each [creation operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#creation-operator) raises the [number operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#number-operator) [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) by one, and each [annihilation operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#annihilation-operator) lowers it by one.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Inverting the definitions of the [creation and annihilation operators](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#creation-and-annihilation-operators) gives

$$
X_j=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(a_j+a_j^*),\qquad
P_j=-i\sqrt{\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}}(a_j-a_j^*).
$$

Substituting in the [orbital angular momentum](../quantum-mechanics.md#orbital-angular-momentum) $L$ gives

$$
L=-\frac{i\hbar}{2}\left[(a_1+a_1^*)(a_2-a_2^*)-(a_2+a_2^*)(a_1-a_1^*)\right].
$$

Operators belonging to distinct modes commute. Therefore the terms with two [annihilation operators](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#annihilation-operator) and those with two [creation operators](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#creation-operator) cancel, and $a_1^*a_2=a_2a_1^*$, $a_2^*a_1=a_1a_2^*$. The surviving terms give the [orbital angular momentum in oscillator ladder operators](../quantum-mechanics.md#orbital-angular-momentum-in-oscillator-ladder-operators):

$$
\boxed{L=i\hbar(a_1a_2^*-a_2a_1^*).}
$$

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

From part (a), $[N,a_j]=-a_j$ and $[N,a_j^*]=a_j^*$. The [commutator product rule](../lie-algebra.md#commutator-product-rule) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
[N,a_1a_2^*]&=[N,a_1]a_2^*+a_1[N,a_2^*]=-a_1a_2^*+a_1a_2^*=0,\\
[N,a_2a_1^*]&=[N,a_2]a_1^*+a_2[N,a_1^*]=-a_2a_1^*+a_2a_1^*=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{[N,L]=0.}
$$

Each term in $L$ transfers one excitation between the two modes, preserving the total [number operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#number-operator) [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue). This is an instance of the [oscillator bilinear commutator](../quantum-mechanics.md#oscillator-bilinear-commutator); every [number operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#number-operator) [eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) is an [invariant subspace](../representation-theory.md#invariant-subspace) of $L$.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/c/i)

The common [Fock vacuum](../quantum-field-theory.md#fock-vacuum) obeys $N\psi=\sum_j a_j^*(a_j\psi)=0$. Put $B=(a_1^*)^{n_1}(a_2^*)^{n_2}$. By the [commutator product rule](../lie-algebra.md#commutator-product-rule) and part (a),

$$
[N,B]=n_1B+n_2B=(n_1+n_2)B.
$$

Moving $N$ through $B$ therefore gives

$$
NB\psi=BN\psi+[N,B]\psi=(n_1+n_2)B\psi.
$$

Dividing by $\sqrt{n_1!n_2!}$ proves

$$
\boxed{N\psi_{n_1,n_2}=(n_1+n_2)\psi_{n_1,n_2}.}
$$

The constructed [number state](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#number-state) is nonzero: the relation $a_j(a_j^*)^r\psi=r(a_j^*)^{r-1}\psi$ follows from $[a_j,a_j^*]=I$ and $a_j\psi=0$. Commuting different modes and successively moving each [creation operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#creation-operator) across the [inner product](../inner-product.md) yields

$$
\|B\psi\|^2=n_1!n_2!\,\|\psi\|^2=n_1!n_2!.
$$

Thus the given factorial denominator also gives unit [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm), as expected for a [number state](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#number-state).

<h4 id="2/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/c/ii)

Write $u=\psi_{1,0}=a_1^*\psi$ and $v=\psi_{0,1}=a_2^*\psi$. The [canonical commutation relations](../quantum-mechanics.md#canonical-commutation-relation) for the [creation and annihilation operators](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#creation-and-annihilation-operators) give

$$
a_1u=\psi,\qquad a_2u=0,\qquad a_1v=0,\qquad a_2v=\psi.
$$

For example, $a_1a_1^*\psi=(I+a_1^*a_1)\psi=\psi$. The expression for $L$ from part (b), with distinct modes reordered, then yields

$$
Lu=i\hbar v,\qquad Lv=-i\hbar u.
$$

Thus $\operatorname{span}\{u,v\}$ is an [invariant subspace](../representation-theory.md#invariant-subspace), and the [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) of $L$ in the ordered [orthonormal basis](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-basis) $(u,v)$ is

$$
[L]_{(u,v)}=\hbar\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its [characteristic polynomial](../linear-operator-theory.md#characteristic-polynomial) is $\ell^2-\hbar^2$, giving the [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) and normalized [eigenstates](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate)

$$
\boxed{\ell_+=+\hbar,\qquad \chi_+=\frac{\psi_{1,0}+i\psi_{0,1}}{\sqrt2};\qquad
\ell_-=-\hbar,\qquad \chi_-=\frac{\psi_{1,0}-i\psi_{0,1}}{\sqrt2}.}
$$

Indeed, $L(u\pm iv)=\pm\hbar(u\pm iv)$ and $\|u\pm iv\|^2=2$ by the assumed [orthonormality](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set). Both [eigenstates](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) remain in the $N=1$ [eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace).

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

The [angular momentum eigenstates](../angular-momentum-operator.md#angular-momentum-eigenstate) have $J^2$ [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $\hbar^2j(j+1)$, where the [angular momentum quantum number](../quantum-system.md#angular-momentum-quantum-number) is $j=0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,\ldots$. For each $j$, the [magnetic quantum number](../quantum-mechanics.md#magnetic-quantum-number) runs in unit steps from $-j$ to $j$. Here that [magnetic quantum number](../quantum-mechanics.md#magnetic-quantum-number) is $+1/2$, so only half-integer $j$ are compatible:

$$
\boxed{\lambda=j(j+1),\qquad j=\frac12,\frac32,\frac52,\ldots,\qquad
\lambda\in\left\{\frac34,\frac{15}4,\frac{35}4,\ldots\right\}.}
$$

Integer $j$ cannot contain a $J_3$ [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $\hbar/2$. Applying the [angular momentum lowering operator](../angular-momentum-operator.md#angular-momentum-lowering-operator) and using $[J_3,J_-]=-\hbar J_-$ gives

$$
J_3\varphi=J_3J_-\psi=(J_-J_3-\hbar J_-)\psi
=\boxed{-\frac\hbar2\varphi}.
$$

The lowered vector is nonzero, since the [adjoint operator](../hilbert-space.md#adjoint-operator) identity $J_-^*=J_+$ gives

$$
\|\varphi\|^2=\langle\psi,J_+J_-\psi\rangle
=\hbar^2\left(\lambda-\frac14+\frac12\right)
=\hbar^2\left(\lambda+\frac14\right)>0.
$$

Finally, $J_3$ is a [self-adjoint operator](../linear-operator-theory.md#self-adjoint-operator), so its [eigenstates](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) for distinct [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) are [orthogonal](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors). Explicitly,

$$
-\frac\hbar2\langle\varphi\mid\psi\rangle
=\langle J_3\varphi\mid\psi\rangle
=\langle\varphi\mid J_3\psi\rangle
=\frac\hbar2\langle\varphi\mid\psi\rangle,
$$

and hence $\boxed{\langle\varphi\mid\psi\rangle=0}$.

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

For $\lambda=3/4$, the [adjoint operator](../hilbert-space.md#adjoint-operator) relation $J_+^*=J_-$ and the given product identity imply

$$
\|J_+\psi\|^2=\langle\psi,J_-J_+\psi\rangle
=\hbar^2\left(\frac34-\frac14-\frac12\right)=0.
$$

A vector of zero [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) is zero, so $J_+\psi=0$. Part (i) gives

$$
\boxed{\|\varphi\|^2=\hbar^2,\qquad \|\varphi\|=\hbar.}
$$

The [commutator](../lie-algebra.md#commutator) $[J^2,J_-]=0$ also gives $J^2\varphi=(3/4)\hbar^2\varphi$, while $J_3\varphi=-(\hbar/2)\varphi$. Applying the other product identity to this unnormalized vector,

$$
\|J_-\varphi\|^2=\langle\varphi,J_+J_-\varphi\rangle
=\hbar^2\left(\frac34-\frac14-\frac12\right)\|\varphi\|^2=0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{J_+\psi=0,\qquad J_-\varphi=0.}
$$

These are the two [angular momentum ladder endpoints](../quantum-mechanics.md#angular-momentum-ladder-endpoint) of a [spin one-half](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin-one-half) pair. In particular, $\psi_2=\varphi/\hbar$ has unit [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm), and $J_+\psi_2=\hbar\psi_1$ because $J_+J_-\psi=\hbar^2\psi$.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

In the ordered [orthonormal basis](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-basis) $(\psi_1,\psi_2)$, the [Hamiltonian operator](../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics) has [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix)

$$
H=\hbar\omega\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}=\hbar\omega\sigma_1,
$$

where $\sigma_1$ is a [Pauli matrix](../algebra.md#pauli-matrices). Its symmetric and antisymmetric [eigenvectors](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector) give

$$
\boxed{E_+=+\hbar\omega,\qquad \chi_+=\frac{\psi_1+\psi_2}{\sqrt2};\qquad
E_-=-\hbar\omega,\qquad \chi_-=\frac{\psi_1-\psi_2}{\sqrt2}.}
$$

The [energy eigenstates](../energy-eigenvalue.md#energy-eigenstate) $\chi_\pm$ are normalized and [orthogonal](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors); each [energy eigenvalue](../energy-eigenvalue.md) is nondegenerate. Their full [stationary states](../quantum-system.md#stationary-state) are $e^{-i\omega t}\chi_+$ and $e^{i\omega t}\chi_-$. Since they form an [orthonormal basis](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-basis), the general solution to the [Time-dependent Schrödinger equation](../mathematical-physics.md#time-dependent-schrodinger-equation) is

$$
\boxed{\Psi(t)=c_+e^{-i\omega t}\chi_++c_-e^{i\omega t}\chi_-,\qquad c_+,c_-\in\mathbb C.}
$$

For a normalized [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state), $|c_+|^2+|c_-|^2=1$; the constants are determined by its initial [inner products](../inner-product.md) with $\chi_\pm$.

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

The initial [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) is $\psi_1$, up to [global phase](../quantum-system.md#global-phase), and $\psi_1=(\chi_++\chi_-)/\sqrt2$. Applying [unitary time evolution](../quantum-system.md#unitary-time-evolution) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Psi(t)&=\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(e^{-i\omega t}\chi_++e^{i\omega t}\chi_-\right)\\
&=\cos(\omega t)\psi_1-i\sin(\omega t)\psi_2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $J_3\psi_1=(\hbar/2)\psi_1$ and $J_3\psi_2=-(\hbar/2)\psi_2$, the [Born rule](../wave-function.md#born-rule) gives

$$
\boxed{\Pr\left(J_3(t)=+\frac\hbar2\right)=|\langle\psi_1,\Psi(t)\rangle|^2=\cos^2(\omega t).}
$$

The other outcome has [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $\sin^2(\omega t)$, verifying that the two [probabilities](../probability-theory.md#probability) sum to one. This is a [two-level quantum return probability](../wave-function.md#two-level-quantum-return-probability).

<h4 id="3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/b/iii)

Continue with the initial [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) $\psi_1$ from part (ii), and let $\Delta t=T/N$. Conditional on every preceding outcome being $+\hbar/2$, the [post-measurement state](../quantum-measurement.md#post-measurement-state) after each ideal [projective measurement](../quantum-measurement.md#projective-measurement) is again $\psi_1$, up to [global phase](../quantum-system.md#global-phase). Part (ii) therefore gives the same [conditional probability](../probability-theory.md#conditional-probability) $\cos^2(\omega\Delta t)$ for each successive outcome. Multiplying these [conditional probabilities](../probability-theory.md#conditional-probability) yields the [repeated projective-measurement survival probability](../quantum-measurement.md#repeated-projective-measurement-survival-probability)

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P_N(T)=\left[\cos^2\left(\frac{\omega T}{N}\right)\right]^N.}
$$

This multiplication uses the [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state) reset at each successful [projective measurement](../quantum-measurement.md#projective-measurement); the outcomes need not be assumed statistically independent.

For fixed $T$, put $z=\omega T$. The [Taylor expansion](../calculus.md#taylor-expansion) $\cos^2u=1-u^2+O(u^4)$ gives the [quadratic short-time quantum survival probability](../quantum-system.md#quadratic-short-time-quantum-survival-probability). For sufficiently large $N$, taking logarithms gives

$$
\log\mathbb P_N(T)=N\log\left(1-\frac{z^2}{N^2}+O(N^{-4})\right)
=-\frac{z^2}{N}+O(N^{-3})\longrightarrow0.
$$

Here the remainder uses [Big O notation](../real-analysis.md#big-o-notation). Exponentiating proves

$$
\boxed{\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb P_N(T)=1,\qquad
\mathbb P_N(T)=1-\frac{(\omega T)^2}{N}+O(N^{-2}).}
$$

This is the [quantum Zeno effect](../quantum-measurement.md#quantum-zeno-effect): increasingly frequent ideal [projective measurements](../quantum-measurement.md#projective-measurement) inhibit the transition away from the initial [quantum state](../quantum-system.md#quantum-state). The departure [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) in a short interval is quadratic in its duration, so $N$ intervals of duration $T/N$ produce a total leading departure [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) proportional to $T^2/N$. Without intervening measurements, the return [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) at time $T$ would be $\cos^2(\omega T)$. The result concerns obtaining $+\hbar/2$ at every measurement in the ideal limit of instantaneous measurements with [unitary time evolution](../quantum-system.md#unitary-time-evolution) between them.
