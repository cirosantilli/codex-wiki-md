<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $f_l(\theta,\phi)=\sin^l\theta\,e^{-il\phi}$. For the regular, single-valued [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) states, $l$ is a nonnegative integer. Differentiation immediately gives

$$
L_3f_l=-i\hbar\partial_\phi f_l=(-i\hbar)(-il)f_l=\boxed{-l\hbar f_l}.
$$

For $0<\theta<\pi$, the derivatives needed for $L^2$ are

$$
\partial_\theta f_l=l\cot\theta f_l,\qquad \partial_\theta^2f_l=(l^2\cot^2\theta-l\csc^2\theta)f_l,\qquad \partial_\phi^2f_l=-l^2f_l.
$$

Consequently the angular differential expression inside the parentheses of $-L^2/\hbar^2$ equals

$$
\left[(l^2+l)\cot^2\theta-(l+l^2)\csc^2\theta\right]f_l=-l(l+1)f_l,
$$

using $\csc^2\theta-\cot^2\theta=1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{L^2f_l=l(l+1)\hbar^2f_l.}
$$

The expressions extend regularly through the polar coordinate singularities: $f_l$ is the restriction of the polynomial $(x-iy)^l$ to the unit sphere. It is proportional to the lowest-projection [spherical harmonic](../../../../../spherical-harmonic.md) of degree $l$; for $l=0$ it is the constant state.
