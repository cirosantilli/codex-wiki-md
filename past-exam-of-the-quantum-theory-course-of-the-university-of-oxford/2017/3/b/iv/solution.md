<h1 id="3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Use the normalized $L_3$ [eigenstates](../../../../../eigenstate.md) from part (ii). The [quantum state](../../../../../quantum-state.md) found in part (iii) has the decomposition

$$
\psi_x=\sqrt{\frac3{4\pi}}\frac{\sin\theta e^{i\phi}+\sin\theta e^{-i\phi}}2=\frac{\chi_{+1}+\chi_{-1}}{\sqrt2}.
$$

There is no $\chi_0$ component. Equivalently, its [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) with $\chi_0$ contains $\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\phi\,d\phi=0$. The [Born rule](../../../../../born-rule.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\Pr(L_3=0)=|\langle\chi_0,\psi_x\rangle|^2=0.}
$$

For completeness the only outcomes are $L_3=\pm\hbar$, each with [probability](../../../../../probability.md) $1/2$. Thus $\langle L_3\rangle=0$ does not mean that zero is a possible result in this state: its [quantum uncertainty](../../../../../quantum-uncertainty.md) in $L_3$ is $\hbar$, even though $L_1$ is exactly zero.
