**No: these two strict comparisons violate the [independence axiom for lottery preferences](../../../../../independence-axiom-for-lottery-preferences.md).** Let $z$ denote the certain zero-money [lottery](../../../../../lottery.md). [Reduction of compound lotteries](../../../../../reduction-of-compound-lotteries.md) gives

$$
\ell_1=\frac14\ell_3+\frac34z,\qquad
\ell_2=\frac14\ell_4+\frac34z.
$$

For example, the second expression assigns [probability](../../../../../probability.md) $(1/4)(4/5)=1/5$ to the nonzero reward and $4/5$ to zero. The claimed $\ell_3\succ\ell_4$ must therefore imply

$$
\frac14\ell_3+\frac34z\succ\frac14\ell_4+\frac34z,
$$

which is $\ell_1\succ\ell_2$, contradicting the other claimed [strict preference](../../../../../strict-preference.md).

The same obstruction is visible in any proposed [expected utility](../../../../../expected-utility.md) representation. Subtract $u(0)$ from the [utility function](../../../../../utility-function-split.md), and write $a=u(3000)-u(0)$ and $b=u(4000)-u(0)$. The first comparison requires $(1/5)b>(1/4)a$, hence $(4/5)b>a$, whereas the second requires $a>(4/5)b$. No real $a,b$ satisfy both. This argument requires neither [risk neutrality](../../../../../risk-neutrality.md) nor an assumption that the [utility function](../../../../../utility-function-split.md) is increasing in money.
