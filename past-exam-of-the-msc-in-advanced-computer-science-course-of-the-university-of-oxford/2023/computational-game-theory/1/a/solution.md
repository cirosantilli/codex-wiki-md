<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Under the [expected utility hypothesis](../../../../../expected-utility-hypothesis.md), use [positive affine uniqueness of affine preference representations](../../../../../positive-affine-uniqueness-of-affine-preference-representations.md) to normalize the extreme outcomes to $u(\omega_1)=1$ and $u(\omega_4)=0$. The stated [indifference relations](../../../../../indifference-relation.md) then give

$$
u(\omega_2)=\frac35,\qquad u(\omega_3)=\frac15.
$$

The two [lotteries](../../../../../lottery.md) have [expected utilities](../../../../../expected-utility.md)

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(\ell_1)&=\frac14\left(1+\frac35+\frac15+0\right)=\frac9{20}=0.45,\\
U(\ell_2)&=\frac3{20}+\frac12\frac35+\frac3{20}\frac15=\frac{12}{25}=0.48.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence **$\ell_2\succ\ell_1$**, with normalized [expected utility](../../../../../expected-utility.md) difference $3/100$. Equivalently, substitution reduces them to [lotteries](../../../../../lottery.md) with probabilities $0.45$ and $0.48$ of the better extreme outcome. The inference uses the [independence axiom for lottery preferences](../../../../../independence-axiom-for-lottery-preferences.md); the three displayed outcome comparisons alone would not determine preferences over all compound [lotteries](../../../../../lottery.md) without the [expected utility hypothesis](../../../../../expected-utility-hypothesis.md).
