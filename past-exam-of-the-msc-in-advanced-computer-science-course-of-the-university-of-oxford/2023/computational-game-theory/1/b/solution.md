<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**Yes: the stated order has an [expected utility](../../../../../expected-utility.md) representation.** For outcomes whose monetary amounts are $1,2,4$, respectively, choose

$$
u(1)=2,\qquad u(2)=0,\qquad u(4)=3.
$$

Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(\ell_1)&=\tfrac12\,0+\tfrac12\,3=\tfrac32,\\
U(\ell_2)&=\tfrac35\,2+\tfrac25\,3=\tfrac{12}{5},\\
U(\ell_3)&=\tfrac9{10}\,2+\tfrac1{10}\,0=\tfrac95,\\
U(\ell_4)&=\tfrac35\,0+\tfrac25\,2=\tfrac45.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $12/5>9/5>3/2>4/5$, this [utility function](../../../../../utility-function-split.md) gives exactly the required [strict preferences](../../../../../strict-preference.md).

The distinction is that the [expected utility hypothesis](../../../../../expected-utility-hypothesis.md) does not by itself require the [utility function](../../../../../utility-function-split.md) to increase with monetary amount. This [nonmonotone monetary utility](../../../../../nonmonotone-monetary-utility.md) ranks the outcome paying 2 below the outcome paying 1. If increasing monetary [utility](../../../../../utility.md) were imposed as an additional assumption, the stated order would fail: $\ell_3\succ\ell_4$ requires $u(1)>u(2)$ because

$$
U(\ell_3)-U(\ell_4)=\tfrac12\bigl(u(1)-u(2)\bigr).
$$

That additional monotonicity assumption is not printed in this part.
