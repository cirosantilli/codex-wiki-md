<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $A$ and $B$ for the two deterministic outcomes. Under the [expected utility hypothesis](../../../../../expected-utility-hypothesis.md), indifference between them implies $u(A)=u(B)=c$. The [expected utility](../../../../../expected-utility.md) of the fair [lottery](../../../../../lottery.md) is consequently

$$
U\left(\tfrac12 A+\tfrac12 B\right)=\tfrac12u(A)+\tfrac12u(B)=c.
$$

It must therefore be indifferent to each deterministic outcome, contradicting the asserted strict preference. **These [preferences](../../../../../preference-relation.md) do not admit an [expected utility](../../../../../expected-utility.md) representation over the stated outcomes.** Equivalently, [affine utility on probability measures](../../../../../affine-utility-on-probability-measures.md) preserves this indifference under mixing, as required by the [independence axiom for lottery preferences](../../../../../independence-axiom-for-lottery-preferences.md).

A concern for the fairness of the procedure can be perfectly intelligible. It would require enlarging the outcome description to include the procedure; it cannot be represented by an [expected utility](../../../../../expected-utility.md) over only the two final gift recipients while retaining the stated strict preference.
