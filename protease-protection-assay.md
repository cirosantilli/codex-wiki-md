# Protease protection assay

↑ **Parent:** [Microsomal protein import experiment](microsomal-protein-import-experiment.md)

A [protease protection assay](protease-protection-assay.md) asks whether a barrier prevents an added [protease](protease.md) from reaching a [protein](protein-split.md). A membrane-disruption comparison tests whether protection depends on that barrier.

![](protein-targeting-protease-protection-controls.png)

**[Figure 9](#image-protease-protection-assay-accessibility-and-control-logic). Protease protection assay: accessibility and control logic**. Original qualitative decision diagram for a completed soluble cargo. Icons and words describe expected outcomes under stated assumptions; they are not an [autoradiography](autoradiography.md) readout, copied historical lanes, measured survival fractions or simulated experimental intensities. [Detergent](detergent.md) is added after the import reaction. The historical [nascent chain](nascent-chain.md) experiment also retained small [ribosome](ribosome.md)-protected fragments, which this completed-cargo model deliberately distinguishes.

The membrane-disruption test was performed by [David D. Sabatini](david-d-sabatini.md) and [Günter Blobel](gunter-blobel.md) in 1970. Rat-liver [microsomes](microsome.md) purified by [sucrose](sucrose.md) [centrifugation](centrifugation.md) incorporated radioactive [leucine](leucine.md) during [cell-free translation](cell-free-translation.md). Samples received cold [trypsin](trypsin.md) and [chymotrypsin](chymotrypsin.md), with or without membrane-solubilizing [detergents](detergent.md). [Electron microscopy](electron-microscopy.md) checked vesicle preservation; [size-exclusion chromatography](size-exclusion-chromatography.md) separated protected fragment sizes. Large membrane-associated fragments lost protection after disruption, whereas small [ribosome](ribosome.md)-protected fragments persisted. An exposed [ribosome](ribosome.md) preparation remained digestible when mixed with [microsomes](microsome.md), checking that membranes did not simply inhibit the [proteases](protease.md).[Controlled proteolysis of nascent polypeptides in rat liver cell fractions. II, Methods p. 147; activity control p. 148; Figure 5C–D and text p. 152; Discussion pp. 155–156](controlled-proteolysis-of-nascent-polypeptides-in-rat-liver-cell-fractions-ii-by-david-d-sabatini-and-gunter-blobel.md)

To read the qualitative diagram, first compare untreated aliquots: they establish that detectable cargo exists before digestion. Next compare external [protease](protease.md) with intact versus disrupted membranes. Persistence only while the barrier remains intact supports enclosure, provided the exposed-cargo control confirms active [protease](protease.md) in both chemical conditions. The [detergent](detergent.md)-only aliquot checks that disruption itself does not destroy recovery or detection. Adding membranes after synthesis tests a different alternative: capture of a completed product rather than entry during [biological translation](translation-biology.md).

This is an argument from controlled accessibility. Binding predicts membrane association but generally leaves an exposed cargo digestible. Enclosure predicts protection that depends on membrane integrity. However, [protein aggregation](protein-aggregation.md), intrinsic resistance to [proteolysis](proteolysis.md), inactive [protease](protease.md) or undetected small fragments can obscure this distinction. For a [transmembrane protein](transmembrane-protein.md), expect protection of the enclosed portion rather than necessarily the full chain. Combine fragment size, compartment controls and [membrane topology](membrane-topology.md); a protected signal alone does not uniquely specify the whole [protein](protein-split.md)'s location.
