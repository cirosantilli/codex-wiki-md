# Orthohydrogen

↑ **Parent:** [Spin isomers of hydrogen](spin-isomers-of-hydrogen.md)

[Orthohydrogen](orthohydrogen.md) uses the three symmetric [spin-one-half triplet states](spin-one-half-triplet-state.md),

$$
\chi_{11}=|\uparrow\uparrow\rangle,\qquad
\chi_{10}=\frac{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle+|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad
\chi_{1,-1}=|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle.
$$

These three states give a nuclear-spin multiplicity of three, in addition to the rotational multiplicity $2J+1$. Thus the lowest [orthohydrogen](orthohydrogen.md) level, $J=1$, has nine states, whereas the lowest [parahydrogen](parahydrogen.md) level, $J=0$, has one. The familiar high-[temperature](temperature-split.md) $3:1$ ratio is not the ratio $9:1$ of these two lowest levels: at high [temperature](temperature-split.md), many even and odd rotational levels contribute.
