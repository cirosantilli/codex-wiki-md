# Eddington experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Schwarzschild light deflection](schwarzschild-light-deflection.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddington_experiment)

The 1919 [solar eclipse](solar-eclipse.md) expeditions organized by [Frank Watson Dyson](frank-watson-dyson.md) and [Arthur Eddington](arthur-eddington.md) compared background-star positions near the obscured [Sun](sun.md) with reference photographs. The optical train used a slowly rotating flat mirror to send the light into a horizontal photographic telescope. A smaller telescope at Sobral provided a separate set of plates. Focus and plate-scale changes had to be distinguished from the radial pattern predicted by [Schwarzschild light deflection](schwarzschild-light-deflection.md). The experiment therefore tested a spatial pattern and its amplitude, not merely whether a star image had moved.[1920 report of the 1919 observations](a-determination-of-the-deflection-of-light-by-the-sun-s-gravitational-field-by-frank-watson-dyson-and-others.md) [Equipment taken and the Sobral expedition: horizontal telescopes, tracking mirrors and reference plates](https://www.royalobservatorygreenwich.org/articles.php?article=1283)
