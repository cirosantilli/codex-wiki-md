# Ideals of a finite product of rings

↑ **Parent:** [Direct product of rings](direct-product-of-rings.md)

Every two-sided [ideal](ideal.md) $I$ of a finite [direct product of rings](direct-product-of-rings.md) $T=\prod_{j=1}^nR_j$ is $\prod_jI_j$, where $I_j$ is its image under the $j$th coordinate [ring homomorphism](ring-homomorphism.md). These images are [ideals](ideal.md) because the coordinate maps are surjective. To prove the reverse inclusion, lift $a_j\in I_j$ to $b_j\in I$ and multiply by the coordinate [idempotent](idempotent.md) $e_j=(0,\ldots,1,\ldots,0)$. Then $\sum_je_jb_j=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in I$. Finiteness matters: in $\prod_{j\geq0}\mathbb Z$, the finitely supported sequences form an [ideal](ideal.md) whose every coordinate image is $\mathbb Z$, but which is not the whole product.
