# Continuous maps from an infinite cofinite space to a Hausdorff space are constant

↑ **Parent:** [Cofinite topology](cofinite-topology.md)

If $X$ is an [infinite set](infinite-set.md) with its [cofinite topology](cofinite-topology.md) and $Y$ is a [Hausdorff space](hausdorff-space.md), every [continuous map](continuous-map.md) $f:X\to Y$ is a [constant function](constant-function.md). Otherwise two distinct image points would have [disjoint](disjoint-sets.md) [open](open-set.md) [neighbourhoods](neighbourhood-mathematics.md). Their [inverse images](preimage.md) would be nonempty [disjoint](disjoint-sets.md) [open sets](open-set.md) of $X$, contradicting its [hyperconnected space](hyperconnected-space.md) property.

For example, $f(n)=n$ from $\mathbb N_{>0}$ with its [cofinite topology](cofinite-topology.md) into the [real line](real-line.md) with its usual [Euclidean metric](euclidean-metric.md) is not [continuous](continuous-function.md): the [inverse image](preimage.md) of the [open interval](open-interval.md) $(1/2,3/2)$ is $\{1\}$, which is not [open](open-set.md) in the infinite domain. A [continuous](continuous-function.md) real-valued readout on that domain can only report one value. Finite cofinite domains are different: their [discrete topology](discrete-space.md) allows arbitrary [functions](function-split.md) into any [topological space](topological-space.md).
