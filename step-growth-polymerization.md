# Step-growth polymerization

↑ **Parent:** [Polymerization](polymerization.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Step-growth_polymerization)

In [step-growth polymerization](step-growth-polymerization.md), suitable reactive groups on [molecules](molecule.md) of different chain lengths can join. The linear count and its assumptions are given in [polymerization](polymerization.md).

A one-unit [monomer](monomer.md), a two-unit chain, and a twenty-unit chain can all be joining partners if their [functional groups](functional-group.md) are compatible and accessible. Schematically, $P_j+P_k\longrightarrow P_{j+k}$: the subscripts count [monomeric units](monomeric-unit.md), and end-group chemistry is suppressed. In [chain-growth propagation](chain-growth-propagation.md), by contrast, $P_j^*+M\longrightarrow P_{j+1}^*$ requires a free [monomer](monomer.md) and a [polymerization-active site](polymerization-active-site.md). An arbitrary pair of existing chains is not a propagation pair in that mechanism. The practical consequence of [step-growth polymerization](step-growth-polymerization.md) is that most [monomers](monomer.md) can have joined something while the average chain is still short.

Whether a joining step releases a small [molecule](molecule.md) is a separate question. [Polycondensation](polycondensation.md) releases small products during the joining chemistry, as in the [Nylon 6,10](nylon-6-10.md) demonstration in [polymerization](polymerization.md). [Polyaddition](polyaddition.md) joins suitable chains without that elimination: reacting a [diol](diol.md) with a molecule bearing two [isocyanate](isocyanate.md) groups can build a [polyurethane](polyurethane.md). Thus [step-growth polymerization](step-growth-polymerization.md) and [polycondensation](polycondensation.md) are not synonyms.[polyaddition, P04720, definition and note 1; polycondensation, P04722, definition and growth-step equation; source recommendation p. 2307](iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry.md)
