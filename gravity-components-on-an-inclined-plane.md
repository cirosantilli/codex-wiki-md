# Gravity components on an inclined plane

↑ **Parent:** [Inclined plane](inclined-plane.md)

Choose a unit [vector](vector.md) $\mathbf e_d$ pointing downhill and a perpendicular unit [vector](vector.md) $\mathbf e_n$ pointing outward from the [inclined plane](inclined-plane.md). The [gravitational weight](weight-physics.md) points vertically downward. Its [scalar projections](scalar-projection.md) onto these directions give

$$
\mathbf W=mg\sin\theta\,\mathbf e_d-mg\cos\theta\,\mathbf e_n.
$$

The [angle](angle.md) between the downward vertical and the inward normal is $\theta$: the inward component uses [cosine](cosine.md), and the downhill component uses [sine](sine.md). These are two components of one [force](force-split.md). They replace its vertical arrow in a component calculation; adding them to that same arrow would count [gravitational weight](weight-physics.md) twice.

Continued contact with a stationary straight [inclined plane](inclined-plane.md) gives zero perpendicular [acceleration](acceleration.md). The perpendicular component of [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) therefore gives

$$
N-mg\cos\theta=0,\qquad N=mg\cos\theta.
$$

This is why the [normal force](normal-force.md) generally differs from $mg$: it balances the inward component, rather than the whole [gravitational weight](weight-physics.md). An extra lifting [force](force-split.md), a curved surface, or motion of the support would require a new perpendicular balance.[University Physics Volume 1 by William Moebs and others; “Friction and the Inclined Plane”, Example 6.11 strategy and Figure 6.14; perpendicular balance and $N=mg\cos\theta$](university-physics-volume-1-by-william-moebs-and-others/6-2-friction.md)

The same geometry explains the ramp's usefulness. A frictionless block pulled uphill at constant [velocity](velocity.md) needs a parallel [force](force-split.md) $P=mg\sin\theta$. Travelling a distance $L$ raises it through height $h=L\sin\theta$, so the pulling [work](work.md) is $PL=mgh$, its gain in [gravitational potential energy](gravitational-energy.md). The smaller [force](force-split.md) costs a larger [displacement](displacement.md); the [work-energy theorem](work-energy-theorem.md) keeps the accounting consistent.
