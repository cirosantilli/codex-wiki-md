# Thermal conductivity

↑ **Parent:** [Fourier's law](fourier-s-law.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_conductivity)

[Thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md) is the material response coefficient in [Fourier's law](fourier-s-law.md). For an [isotropic](isotropy.md) material it is a [scalar](scalar.md) $k$, with units $\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$; larger positive $k$ means a larger conductive [heat flux](heat-flux-density.md) for the same [temperature gradient](temperature-gradient.md). It can depend on [temperature](temperature.md) and composition. Direction-dependent response in an [anisotropic](anisotropy.md) material is described by a [thermal conductivity tensor](thermal-conductivity-tensor.md). The two worked examples use stipulated positive constants, rather than measured values for named materials.

[Thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md) describes material response, whereas [thermal resistance](thermal-resistance.md) also includes geometry: for the steady bar, $R_{\rm th}=L/(kA)$. Doubling $k$ at fixed [length](length.md), cross-sectional [area](surface-area.md) and end [temperatures](temperature.md) halves [thermal resistance](thermal-resistance.md) and doubles the rate of [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md). Doubling [length](length.md) changes [thermal resistance](thermal-resistance.md) without changing the material's [thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md). This distinction lets a designer improve cooling either by choosing a higher [thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md) or by shortening and widening the path. It also explains why the less conductive layer in [thermal resistance](thermal-resistance.md) takes the greater [temperature](temperature.md) drop.

[Thermal diffusivity](thermal-diffusivity.md) answers a different question: how rapidly a [temperature](temperature.md) disturbance spreads. Its usual model $\kappa=k/(\rho c_p)$ also involves [mass density](density.md) $\rho$ and [specific heat capacity](specific-heat-capacity.md) $c_p$, so equal [thermal conductivities](thermal-conductivity.md) need not imply equal rates of warming.
