# Nonspherical rigid body with isotropic inertia

↑ **Parent:** [Inertia tensor](inertia-tensor.md)

An [inertia tensor](inertia-tensor.md) can be isotropic even when the body is not a sphere. An example is a uniform thin disc of radius $2a$ and mass $M$, with a thin rod of length $\sqrt3a$ and mass $2M$ attached normally at its center. The combined center of mass is one third of the rod length from the disc. Standard disc and rod moments and the [parallel axis theorem](parallel-axis-theorem.md) give all three principal moments equal to $2Ma^2$, so $J=2Ma^2I$. The angular momentum is therefore a fixed scalar times the [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md). In torque-free motion, conservation of the inertial angular-momentum vector makes the angular-velocity vector constant for every initial spin. Rotational symmetry of the shape is not required; equality of its second moments is enough.
