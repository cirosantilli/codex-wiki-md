# Symmetric derivative criterion for monotonicity

↑ **Parent:** [Derivative criterion for monotonicity](derivative-criterion-for-monotonicity.md)

A [continuous function](continuous-function.md) on an open [interval](interval-mathematics.md) with positive [symmetric derivative](symmetric-derivative.md) everywhere is [strictly increasing](strictly-increasing-function.md). One proof takes an [antiderivative](antiderivative.md) $F$ using the [fundamental theorem of calculus](fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md). For small positive $h$, the identity

$$
F(x+h)-2F(x)+F(x-h)=\int_0^h(f(x+t)-f(x-t))\,dt>0
$$

follows from $f^s(x)>0$. If $F$ exceeded a chord, its difference from the chord would attain a positive interior [maximum](maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md), contradicting this symmetric difference [inequality](inequality-mathematics.md). Thus $F$ is a [convex function](convex-function.md), and its [derivative](derivative.md) $f$ is [nondecreasing](nondecreasing-function.md). Equality at two different points would make $f$ constant between them and force an interior [symmetric derivative](symmetric-derivative.md) to vanish, proving strict [monotonicity](monotonic-function.md).
