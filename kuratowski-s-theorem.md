<h1 id="kuratowski-s-theorem">Kuratowski's theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Planar graph](planar-graph-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuratowski's_theorem)

A finite [simple graph](simple-graph.md) is a [planar graph](planar-graph-split.md) if and only if it contains no [graph subdivision](graph-subdivision.md) of the [complete graph](complete-graph.md) $K_5$ or the [complete bipartite graph](complete-bipartite-graph.md) $K_{3,3}$ as a [subgraph](subgraph.md); equivalently, neither is a [topological minor](topological-minor.md). The easy direction follows by tracing each replacement [graph path](path-in-a-graph.md) as one drawn [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md). The converse asserts that every failure of planarity contains one of these two patterns.[§4.4, Theorem 4.4.6(iii), printed p. 111](graph-theory-by-reinhard-diestel/planar-graphs-author-hosted-chapter-preview.md)

[Graph Theory by Reinhard Diestel](graph-theory-by-reinhard-diestel.md) (Planar Graphs preview, §4.4, Theorem 4.4.6, printed p. 111) dates [Kuratowski's theorem](kuratowski-s-theorem.md) to 1930 and the equivalent [graph minor](graph-minor-split.md) characterization to 1937. These dates are that textbook's historical attribution.
