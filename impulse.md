# Impulse

↑ **Parent:** [Collision mechanics](collision-mechanics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impulse)

The [impulse](impulse.md) of a [force](force.md) over a [time](time-in-physics.md) interval is its [vector](vector.md) [integral](integral.md):

$$
\mathbf J=\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\mathbf F(t)\,dt.
$$

Its [International System of Units](international-system-of-units.md) unit is $\mathrm{N\,s}=\mathrm{kg\,m/s}$. The [impulse-momentum theorem](impulse-momentum-theorem.md) equates the net [impulse](impulse.md) to the change in [momentum](momentum.md). For one fixed-[mass](mass.md) [point mass](point-mass.md), integrate its net [force](force.md); for a closed collection obeying [Newton's third law](newton-s-third-law.md), integrate the sum of external [forces](force.md). In one dimension, the signed area under the [force](force.md) versus [time](time-in-physics.md) graph gives the [impulse](impulse.md); negative [force](force.md) contributes negative area. [OpenStax University Physics Volume 1, section 9.2: Impulse and Collisions](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/9-2-impulse-and-collisions)

Three stipulated rightward net [force](force.md) pulses illustrate the same $J=6\,\mathrm{N\,s}$: constant $12\,\mathrm N$ for $0.5\,\mathrm s$, a triangle rising from zero to $24\,\mathrm N$ at $0.25\,\mathrm s$ and falling to zero at $0.5\,\mathrm s$, and constant $3\,\mathrm N$ for $2\,\mathrm s$. Their areas are

$$
(12\,\mathrm N)(0.5\,\mathrm s)
=\frac12(24\,\mathrm N)(0.5\,\mathrm s)
=(3\,\mathrm N)(2\,\mathrm s)
=6\,\mathrm{N\,s}.
$$

Applied separately to the same fixed [mass](mass.md) $m=2\,\mathrm{kg}$, each gives $\Delta v=J/m=3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Their peak [forces](force.md) differ: $12$, $24$, and $3\,\mathrm N$. Thus [impulse](impulse.md) fixes the final [momentum](momentum.md) change, while the [force](force.md) history determines how abruptly it occurs.

<a id="image-three-force-pulses-have-the-same-impulse-but-different-durations-and-peak-forces"></a>
![](impulse-force-pulses.png)

**[Figure 36](#image-three-force-pulses-have-the-same-impulse-but-different-durations-and-peak-forces). Three force pulses have the same impulse but different durations and peak forces**.

The mean net [force](force.md) over the chosen interval is $\overline{\mathbf F}=\mathbf J/(t_f-t_i)$. Extending the interval for a fixed [impulse](impulse.md) reduces this mean [force](force.md). A lower peak [force](force.md) additionally depends on the pulse shape: the two short pulses above have the same mean $12\,\mathrm N$ but different peaks. This explains why controlling the duration and shape of a stopping [impulse](impulse.md) matters in cushioning a [collision](collision.md).
