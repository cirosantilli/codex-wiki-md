# Thermocapillary migration of an insulating bubble

↑ **Parent:** [Marangoni effect](marangoni-effect.md)

For a clean spherical gas bubble with negligible internal [dynamic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) and [thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md), a weak temperature gradient produces the displayed migration [velocity](velocity.md), where $\gamma_T=d\gamma/dT$. The exterior [Papkovich–Neuber representation](papkovich-neuber-representation.md) can use only the scalar potential $\chi=-a^3\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf r/(2r^3)$. Its surface normal velocity equals $\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf n$, and its tangential [traction](traction.md) balances minus the surface [surface tension](surface-tension.md) gradient from the [temperature dipole around an insulating sphere](temperature-dipole-around-an-insulating-sphere.md). For the usual $\gamma_T<0$ the bubble moves toward warmer liquid. Small [Reynolds number](reynolds-number.md), [Péclet number](peclet-number.md), [capillary number](capillary-number.md), and fractional surface-tension variation justify the approximation; [surfactants](surfactant.md) can oppose the temperature-driven [Marangoni stress](marangoni-effect.md).
