<h1 id="artin-wedderburn-theorem">Artin–Wedderburn theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Central simple algebra](central-simple-algebra.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin–Wedderburn_theorem)

Every finite-dimensional semisimple algebra is a finite product of matrix algebras over division algebras. In particular, every [central simple algebra](central-simple-algebra.md) over $k$ is isomorphic to $M_r(D)$ for a finite-dimensional central division algebra $D$ over $k$. The integer $r$ and the isomorphism class of $D$ are unique.

Indeed, decompose the right regular module of a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra $A$ as

$$
A_A\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^sS_i^{n_i},
$$

where the $S_i$ are pairwise nonisomorphic [simple modules](irreducible-module.md). The [Schur lemma](schur-s-lemma.md) makes $D_i=\operatorname{End}_A(S_i)$ a [division ring](division-ring.md) and gives $\operatorname{Hom}_A(S_i,S_j)=0$ for $i\ne j$. Left multiplication identifies

$$
A\cong\operatorname{End}_A(A_A)
\cong\prod_{i=1}^sM_{n_i}(D_i).
$$

Conversely, the column module over $M_n(D)$ is simple and the regular module is a direct sum of $n$ copies of it, so finite products of such matrix rings are semisimple. The simple-module decomposition also proves uniqueness up to permuting the factors.
