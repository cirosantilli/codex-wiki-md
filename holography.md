# Holography

↑ **Parent:** [Physical optics](physical-optics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holography)

[Holography](holography.md) records the [wave interference](interference-wave-propagation.md) between a [holographic object wave](holographic-object-wave.md) and a known [holographic reference wave](holographic-reference-wave.md), then uses that record to reconstruct a [wavefront](wavefront.md). The record is a [hologram](hologram.md). A [photographic plate](photographic-plate.md) or [photodetector](photodetector.md) measures [light intensity](light-intensity.md); the relative [wave phase](phase-waves.md) is encoded in the positions and contrast of [interference fringes](interference-fringe.md), rather than measured directly as an optical oscillation. Reillumination makes the recorded pattern act as a [diffraction grating](diffraction-grating.md) that produces an image-bearing field.

The useful change of viewpoint is to preserve a field that can propagate again, instead of only a focused brightness picture. Recovered [complex amplitude](complex-amplitude.md) permits refocusing with the [Fresnel propagator](fresnel-propagator.md); a spatially extended [hologram](hologram.md) can also supply different viewing directions. The recoverable information is limited by the collected [aperture](aperture.md), recorded [spatial frequencies](spatial-frequency.md), [optical coherence](optical-coherence.md) and separation of unwanted [diffraction orders](diffraction-order.md).
