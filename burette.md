# Burette

↑ **Parent:** [Weak acid dissociation measurement](weak-acid-dissociation-measurement.md)

A [burette](burette.md) delivers an adjustable measured [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) of a [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md); the change in its reading gives the delivered [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) in an [acid-base titration](acid-base-titration.md).

Its downward-increasing scale measures a change in reading, not the initial liquid inventory. For initial reading $R_i$ and final reading $R_f$ without refilling, the nominal delivered [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) is $v=R_f-R_i$. An illustrative $2.35\,\mathrm{mL}$ to $12.35\,\mathrm{mL}$ reading therefore represents $10.00\,\mathrm{mL}$. There is no requirement to start at zero. An air bubble disappearing from the tip uses part of the apparent delivery to fill the tip, and a hanging drop has not yet entered the sample; both break the intended volume account.

**[Figure 5](#titration-apparatus-and-burette-readings). A pipette selects the sample and a burette measures added titrant by two liquid meniscus readings**. Original schematic, not a photograph or experimental readout. The apparatus shows a clamped [burette](burette.md), mixed sample, and optional [glass electrode](glass-electrode.md) beside the stirring bar. Enlarged illustrative scale readings increase downward; the bottom of the curved [liquid meniscus](meniscus-liquid-surface.md) gives $R_i=2.35\,\mathrm{mL}$ and $R_f=12.35\,\mathrm{mL}$. Their difference is the added [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md).

For transparent aqueous samples, read the bottom of the [liquid meniscus](meniscus-liquid-surface.md) at eye level; viewing from above or below shifts its apparent alignment. The mark is a comparison between a curved liquid surface and a scale, not a direct measure of [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md). Eye-level reading and the distinction between contained and delivered [volumes](volume.md) are part of [solution preparation and volumetric calibration](solution-preparation-and-volumetric-calibration.md).[Laboratory Experiment 1. Calibration of Volumetric Glassware, p. 1, eye-level bottom-of-meniscus reading](calibration-of-volumetric-glassware-buffalo-state-laboratory-handout.md)

Both readings contribute [measurement uncertainty](measurement-uncertainty.md). If each has independent [standard deviation](standard-deviation.md) $\sigma_R$, their difference has [standard deviation](standard-deviation.md) $\sqrt{2}\sigma_R$; a common additive reading offset cancels, while a scale-factor error remains. For the inventory $n=c_Bv$, uncertainty in [titrant](titrant.md) [concentration](concentration.md) remains even with perfect readings. This is why repeat delivery, independent volume calibration, and [titrant standardization](titrant-standardization.md) answer different questions.
