# Negative-vacuum Friedmann lifetime bound

↑ **Parent:** [Friedmann effective potential for matter, radiation and vacuum](friedmann-effective-potential-for-matter-radiation-and-vacuum.md)

For a [FRW metric](friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md) with positive [scale factor](scale-factor-cosmology.md), nonnegative dust and radiation, and negative vacuum density, the [Friedmann acceleration equation](friedmann-acceleration-equation.md) gives $a_{tt}\leq-\omega^2a$, with $\omega^2=8\pi G|\rho_\Lambda|/3$. At maximum expansion, $a(0)=a_{\max}$ and $a_t(0)=0$. The forced-oscillator integral formula gives $a(t)\leq a_{\max}\cos(\omega t)$ until the first zero, since its forcing is nonpositive and its sine kernel is nonnegative for $0\leq t\leq\pi/(2\omega)$. Thus each half of the expansion-recollapse history lasts at most $\pi/(2\omega)$. Nonzero dust or radiation makes the endpoint a physical density singularity; the matter-free limit instead bounds the open anti-de Sitter coordinate patch.
