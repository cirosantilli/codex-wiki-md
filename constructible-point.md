# Constructible point

↑ **Parent:** [Constructible number](constructible-number.md)

A point of the [Euclidean plane](euclidean-plane.md) is constructible if it can be obtained from $(0,0)$ and $(1,0)$ by finitely many straightedge-and-compass operations: draw a [line](straight-line.md) through two obtained distinct points, draw a [circle](circle.md) with obtained [centre](center-geometry.md) and [radius](radius.md) equal to an obtained segment length, and retain isolated [intersection](set-intersection.md) points. Its two coordinates are [constructible numbers](constructible-number.md). Conversely, constructible real coordinates can be placed on perpendicular axes and their coordinate [lines](straight-line.md) intersected, giving the point. Coordinates created at each isolated [intersection](set-intersection.md) require only solving [linear](linearity.md) or [quadratic equations](quadratic-equation.md) over the current coordinate [field](field.md); hence they lie in a real [field extension](field-extension.md) obtained by a finite tower of quadratic extensions. Coincident curves supply no isolated new [intersection](set-intersection.md) points.
