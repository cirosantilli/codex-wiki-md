<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For [Karl Popper](../../../karl-popper.md), scientific theories may be provisionally preferred and used because of their performance in tests. They are not thereby established as true, or assigned a high inductive [probability](../../../probability.md) of truth. The apparent ambiguity disappears when acceptance as a working conjecture is distinguished from acceptance as an evidentially justified universal conclusion.

A finite collection of successful predictions does not deductively entail an unrestricted universal theory. [Karl Popper](../../../karl-popper.md) therefore rejects a method which first collects unquestionable observations and then derives a theory from them by [inductive reasoning](../../../inductive-reasoning.md). His alternative is to formulate a conjecture, deduce discriminating consequences and try to refute it. A theory which survives demanding tests receives [scientific corroboration](../../../scientific-corroboration.md), a retrospective appraisal of its test performance. In the primary account, this expressly differs from establishing the theory as true or even probable.[Sections 2–3; the primary selection's part A, II, Deductive Testing of Theories, especially printed pp. 100–101](../../../the-logic-of-scientific-discovery-by-karl-popper.md)

There is consequently a legitimate evidential preference. Suppose one theory makes an independently checkable prediction which its main rival does not make, or contradicts. Successful testing gives a reason to retain and develop the first theory relative to that rival. Merely adjusting a conjecture to fit a known result need not provide as severe a test as a result for which it faced a genuine opportunity for failure. Temporal novelty alone is not the issue: an independently derived consequence can be demanding even when the relevant observation was already known. Repeating easy observations does not automatically provide the same [scientific corroboration](../../../scientific-corroboration.md) as exposing a theory to a new and discriminating risk. Acceptance remains conditional on the testing background and open to subsequent criticism.[Chapter 1: risky predictions, genuine attempts at refutation and conventionalist protection of a theory](../../../science-conjectures-and-refutations-by-karl-popper.md)

Acceptance also occurs at the level of [basic statements](../../../basic-statement.md). A test requires an agreed report about an observable event in specified circumstances. The participants can subject that report to further checks, but an investigation must provisionally stop somewhere if it is to yield an outcome. In sections 29–30, [Karl Popper](../../../karl-popper.md) describes that stopping point as a rule-governed decision or agreement. Experience can prompt such a decision without logically proving the accepted report. This is neither an infallible observational foundation nor permission to choose the result arbitrarily.[Sections 29–30, especially the distinction between a decision to accept a basic statement and justification by experience](../../../the-logic-of-scientific-discovery-by-karl-popper.md)

The [Duhem problem](../../../duhem-problem.md) adds a qualification. If $T\land A\Rightarrow E$, failure of $E$ contradicts the conjunction, not uniquely $T$. The [auxiliary hypotheses](../../../auxiliary-hypothesis.md) $A$ include calibration and initial conditions. A reproducible failure may rationally lead to revising one of these instead. The critical question is whether the repair has independent testable consequences, or merely immunizes the favoured conjecture. Logical [falsifiability](../../../falsifiability.md) alone does not decide that methodological issue.

A remaining objection is that choosing the best-tested theory for a future application seems to rely on past success. [Karl Popper](../../../karl-popper.md) offers a critical preference among available conjectures, not a proof that the next application will succeed. Whether that answers the practical aspect of the [problem of induction](../../../problem-of-induction.md) is disputable. It should not be resolved by silently redefining [scientific corroboration](../../../scientific-corroboration.md) as predictive certainty. **Evidence matters to provisional selection and rejection; it never converts a scientific theory into an unrevisable established truth.**
