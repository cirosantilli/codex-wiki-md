↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Nelson Goodman](../../../../nelson-goodman.md)'s [new riddle of induction](../../../../new-riddle-of-induction.md) asks which descriptions of an observed regularity are fit for projection. [David Hume](../../../../david-hume.md)'s [problem of induction](../../../../problem-of-induction.md) asks how a projection beyond experience can be justified at all. The problems are related, but accepting that experience supports some expectations does not settle which predicates an acceptable inference may project.

Fix a future time $t$ and let $E_t(x)$ mean that $x$ was examined before $t$. Define [grue](../../../../grue.md) by

$$
G_t(x)\iff\bigl(E_t(x)\land\operatorname{green}(x)\bigr)\lor\bigl(\neg E_t(x)\land\operatorname{blue}(x)\bigr).
$$

Suppose every emerald examined so far is green. Each is then also [grue](../../../../grue.md). The same positive instances fit both a green generalization and a [grue](../../../../grue.md) generalization, although the latter assigns blue to an emerald first examined after the cutoff. This does **not** require an already examined green emerald to change colour at $t$: its classification uses whether it was examined before $t$, not whether the present time is before $t$. The problem concerns incompatible projections to unexamined objects.[Chapter III, section 4, especially pp. 72–80 in the fourth edition; original examined-before-cutoff definition](../../../../fact-fiction-and-forecast-by-nelson-goodman.md)

It does not resolve the difficulty to object that [grue](../../../../grue.md) is artificially disjunctive. Introduce the converse predicate, bleen, for examined-before-$t$ blue objects and other green objects. Green can then be defined using [grue](../../../../grue.md), bleen and $E_t$. Syntactic simplicity depends on which predicates are primitive. Nor does calling green a genuine [natural kind](../../../../natural-kind.md) settle matters unless one explains how the relevant natural structure is identified without simply presupposing the preferred projection. Evidence about stable material composition can provide a scientific reason to project a colour response, but that reason goes beyond counting instances.

[David Hume](../../../../david-hume.md)'s argument exposes a different gap. The denial of an unobserved continuation is not a contradiction. An appeal to the past success of extrapolation already extrapolates from past cases, so cannot supply a noncircular demonstration of its general reliability. His discussion of [necessary connection](../../../../necessary-connection.md) similarly distinguishes experienced successions from an independently perceived necessity binding events together. These claims concern the basis of causal and predictive inference, not merely the spelling of a predicate.[Section IV, parts I–II; section VII, part II, on customary inference and necessary connexion: [https://davidhume.org/texts/e/7](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/7)](../../../../an-enquiry-concerning-human-understanding-by-david-hume.md)

[Predicate entrenchment](../../../../predicate-entrenchment.md) is [Nelson Goodman](../../../../nelson-goodman.md)'s proposed way of distinguishing established projective practices. Green has a history of actual use in projections which its newly constructed rival lacks. This gives a practice-based account of [projectibility](../../../../projectibility.md), rather than an unconditional guarantee that familiar classifications are true. Its weakness is that an account must still permit scientifically successful innovations and correction of entrenched mistakes.[Chapter IV, sections 2–4: actual projections, entrenchment and presumptive projectibility](../../../../fact-fiction-and-forecast-by-nelson-goodman.md)

Thus [Nelson Goodman](../../../../nelson-goodman.md) sharpens the problem facing [scientific confirmation](../../../../scientific-confirmation.md): a purely formal positive-instance rule is too permissive. A defensible response combines explicit background assumptions with revisable standards for selecting predicates. That explains why two syntactically parallel generalizations need not receive equal support; it does not provide the noncircular guarantee demanded by the strongest reading of [David Hume](../../../../david-hume.md)'s challenge.
