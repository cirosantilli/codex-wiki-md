<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

A useful response to the [demarcation problem](../../../demarcation-problem.md) should identify practices which make claims answerable to evidence without equating scientific status with either truth or prestige. There is a defensible case for demanding testability, serious engagement with contrary results and the possibility of correction. There is a much weaker case for one simple criterion which assigns every past and present activity unambiguously to [science](../../../science.md) or [pseudoscience](../../../pseudoscience.md).

[Karl Popper](../../../karl-popper.md)'s [falsifiability](../../../falsifiability.md) criterion makes an important distinction. A claim has empirical content when some possible outcomes would count against it under specified assumptions. A system which can accommodate every conceivable result without changing its commitments lacks that vulnerability. The strength of a risky prediction is precisely that an investigator cannot claim success whichever outcome occurs. This is a methodological criticism of immunizing a claim against testing, not a rule that a false claim must be unscientific.[Chapter 1: risky predictions, falsifiability and conventionalist strategies for avoiding refutation](../../../science-conjectures-and-refutations-by-karl-popper.md)

A simple counterexample shows why [falsifiability](../../../falsifiability.md) alone cannot settle the whole question. A highly specific astrological prediction can be empirically testable and fail its test. Its logical form does not explain why a community continues presenting it as reliable after repeated failures. Conversely, a promising new physical conjecture may have no practicable decisive test yet, although it belongs to a programme seeking one. Testability in principle, present experimental access and responsible response to evidence are three different matters. Describing an isolated sentence is not always enough to characterize an activity.

Nor does a failed prediction logically identify its culprit. If a theory $T$ and an [auxiliary hypothesis](../../../auxiliary-hypothesis.md) $A$ imply an outcome $E$, a contrary outcome establishes $\neg(T\land A)$, not uniquely $\neg T$. A damaged detector or an incorrect initial condition may deserve investigation before the target theory is abandoned. The [Duhem problem](../../../duhem-problem.md) therefore rules out the naive demand to discard a theory after every anomalous reading. It does not license endless rescue: independent calibration, further predictions and interventions can distinguish a justified repair from an [ad hoc hypothesis](../../../ad-hoc-hypothesis.md) introduced only to evade a result.[Part II, chapter VI, section 2: experimental testing concerns a group of theoretical assumptions](../../../the-aim-and-structure-of-physical-theory-by-pierre-duhem.md)

[Imre Lakatos](../../../imre-lakatos.md) accordingly evaluates a [scientific research programme](../../../scientific-research-programme.md) through its development. Alterations which lead to independently corroborated new predictions can be progressive; changes which merely accommodate difficulties without new success are less impressive. The relevant contrast concerns a pattern of inquiry over time. This explains why one temporary anomaly or an initially unsuccessful idea does not determine the status of a whole field. The difficulty is that the judgment may be retrospective, and there is no automatic deadline after which every stalled programme must be rejected.[Discussion of research programmes, novel facts and progressive versus degenerating changes](../../../science-and-pseudoscience-by-imre-lakatos.md)

A defensible assessment therefore combines several considerations: specificity of claims, quality and independence of tests, handling of uncertainty, disclosure of methods, willingness to revise, and success in producing further research. None is individually sufficient. [Scientific replication](../../../scientific-replication.md) can be poor, consensus can be mistaken, and a genuine [experiment](../../../experiment.md) can investigate a false proposal. These qualifications do not make all approaches equally credible; they make the reasons for a classification more informative than a label alone.

The resulting criterion is satisfactory as a fallible standard for evaluating inquiry, rather than as an exceptionless definition discovered by inspecting the grammar of a claim. For a practical decision, it is often more precise to identify a failure, such as an uncontrolled comparison or a refusal to specify what would count against a prediction. That criticism can remain valid when the exact boundary of [science](../../../science.md) is disputed. The absence of a universal necessary-and-sufficient formula for [science](../../../science.md) does not erase the difference between a tested account and one protected from every possible correction.
