**Vagueness and theory dependence create a burden of clarification for [constructive empiricism](../../../constructive-empiricism.md), but neither yields an immediate contradiction.** The harder issue is whether observability deserves the special role in belief that the position assigns to it.

[Constructive empiricism](../../../constructive-empiricism.md), associated with [Bas van Fraassen](../../../bas-van-fraassen.md), distinguishes acceptance of a theory from belief in its full truth. Acceptance involves belief in [empirical adequacy](../../../empirical-adequacy.md), together with commitments to use and develop the theory. [Empirical adequacy](../../../empirical-adequacy.md) concerns all observable phenomena, including unobserved and future ones; it is stronger than fitting the observations already collected. The position interprets theoretical claims literally but allows suspension of belief about unobservable entities. It is therefore not the claim that theoretical language is meaningless.[section III, opening discussion under “More than Lip-Service?”: empirical adequacy, acceptance and belief](../../../constructive-empiricism-now-by-bas-van-fraassen.md)

Observability is a capacity notion. An object can be observable although nobody has observed it: a distant large rock might be seen if a person were suitably situated. Conversely, seeing a detector display does not by itself mean seeing the microscopic entity invoked to explain the display. [Van Fraassen](../../../bas-van-fraassen.md) treats instruments as devices producing new observable phenomena, which theories must account for, rather than automatically as transparent windows onto whatever their interpretation postulates.[section II, “Window or Engine?”: instrumental detection and newly produced observable effects](../../../constructive-empiricism-now-by-bas-van-fraassen.md)

Consider first vagueness. Gradually reduce the size or contrast of a visible mark. There need be no uniquely defensible final size at which it ceases to be visible for a human observer. It does not follow that there is no difference between an ordinary tree and an individual [electron](../../../electron.md). Many distinctions have clear central cases and disputed margins. A [constructive empiricist](../../../constructive-empiricism.md) can specify the relevant human community and conditions, allow unsettled borderline cases, and suspend judgement where the classification is unclear. This response preserves the core position, although it prevents the epistemic policy from being an exact mechanical rule for every proposition.

Now distinguish kinds of [theory-ladenness](../../../theory-ladenness.md). Learning to recognize a detector pattern may involve theoretical training. Describing the pattern as evidence of an [electron](../../../electron.md) invokes a further interpretation. Neither point shows that a visible arrangement of marks can be accepted only if the whole [electron](../../../electron.md) theory is true. Competing accounts may agree on the marks while disagreeing about what produced them. Public calibration, comparison between observers and independent experimental procedures can constrain descriptions without requiring an entirely theory-free vocabulary.

A stronger circularity objection concerns using [optics](../../../optics-split.md) and [physiology](../../../physiology-split.md) to determine the limits of observability. If those theories mention unobservable mechanisms, does taking their account of those limits seriously require believing in the mechanisms? One response is to accept the theories' observable consequences about human performance, while remaining agnostic about their full interpretation. Measurements of which marks people discriminate need not establish every microscopic explanation of that performance. The response works best when observable constraints on the capacity claim can be identified independently of the disputed mechanism; simply repeating the theory's own classification would be insufficient.

Nevertheless, the realist retains an important objection. Both ordinary observation and instrument-mediated detection rely on causal processes, calibration and background assumptions. Why should a small change in human perceptual capacity determine the boundary of warranted belief? Independent methods that converge on the same [electron](../../../electron.md) [electric charge](../../../electric-charge-split.md) appear to offer a reason for belief extending beyond unaided perception. The [constructive empiricist](../../../constructive-empiricism.md) can allow such belief to be rational while denying that acceptance of science requires it, but should acknowledge the resulting permissiveness of the position.

Vagueness therefore limits precision rather than destroying the observable/unobservable distinction. [Theory-ladenness](../../../theory-ladenness.md) defeats a naive foundationalism, not every use of observational evidence. The deepest unresolved question is normative: whether withholding belief beyond [empirical adequacy](../../../empirical-adequacy.md) is a compelling policy, or merely one coherent attitude toward the same scientific achievements.
