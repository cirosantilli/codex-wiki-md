**The distinction supplies a coherent strategy for an empiricist realism, but does not by itself justify that realism.** It separates an object's relatively stable physical constitution from its effects on perceivers. The strongest defensible version concerns the explanatory roles of properties, rather than a division between wholly real properties and wholly unreal sensations.

For [John Locke](../../../john-locke.md), [primary qualities](../../../primary-quality.md) include extension, shape, solidity, number and [motion](../../../motion.md); [secondary qualities](../../../secondary-quality.md) are powers of bodies to produce experiences such as colours and tastes through their constitution. The experienced colour need not resemble the physical cause of that experience. Importantly, a power belonging to a body is not thereby imaginary: the [primary and secondary qualities](../../../primary-secondary-quality-distinction.md) distinction need not deny that an unobserved object has dispositions to affect perceivers.[book II, chapter VIII, sections 8–15 and 23–26: qualities as powers, primary qualities, and resemblance](../../../an-essay-concerning-human-understanding-by-john-locke.md)

This helps [empiricism](../../../empiricism.md) accommodate perceptual variation without abandoning an external world. A surface can produce different colour experiences under different illumination while keeping approximately the same [spectral reflectance](../../../spectral-reflectance.md). A warm and a cool hand can give different sensations in the same water; this need not mean that the water has two incompatible [temperatures](../../../temperature-split.md). The observer's initial state belongs in the explanation. Controlled changes in illumination, viewing conditions and bodily state allow repeatable predictions about experience. An empiricist can therefore treat physical objects as explanatory sources of regularities in experience, rather than demand that every appearance be copied into the object.

However, this explanatory success does not establish that ideas of [primary qualities](../../../primary-quality.md) resemble mind-independent properties. We cannot step outside every perceptual and instrumental relation to compare an experience directly with its alleged original. Moreover, the [motion](../../../motion.md) and arrangement of unobserved constituents are themselves theoretical commitments. If [empiricism](../../../empiricism.md) permits only what is immediately given in sensation, the distinction introduces precisely the inference beyond sensation that requires defence. If [empiricism](../../../empiricism.md) permits fallible explanatory inference, [scientific realism](../../../scientific-realism.md) becomes possible, but the justification comes partly from that additional account of inference.

[George Berkeley](../../../george-berkeley.md)'s challenge is that perceived extension and shape vary with viewpoint and perceiver just as colour does, and that extension cannot be experienced wholly apart from sensible qualities.[part I, sections 9–15: primary qualities, perceptual variation and inseparability](../../../a-treatise-concerning-the-principles-of-human-knowledge-by-george-berkeley.md) This undermines the simple test “variable appearance means merely secondary”. It does not prove that stable physical properties are absent. An obliquely viewed circular disc can produce an elliptical image; [geometrical optics](../../../geometrical-optics-split.md) explains the relation between the disc and that image. Variation can be evidence for a systematic relation, rather than evidence against an external cause.

Modern [physics](../../../physics-split.md) also discourages treating Locke's list as an unrevisable inventory. [Special relativity](../../../special-relativity-split.md) makes measured [length](../../../length.md) depend on the relation between an object and an [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md); [motion](../../../motion.md) was relational even in [Principle of Galilean relativity](../../../principle-of-galilean-relativity.md). Neither fact makes the measurements arbitrary. Conversely, [spectral reflectance](../../../spectral-reflectance.md) is an objective, measurable property even though perceived colour depends on illumination and the perceiver. Objectivity, relationality, perceptual variability and explanatory fundamentality are distinct issues.

The plausible distinction is consequently between an experience and the physical properties and relations that explain it, with the explanation open to correction. An empiricist realism can use this distinction to explain why appearances change while a world persists. It cannot obtain a proof of the external world, or a privileged catalogue of intrinsic properties, simply by naming some qualities primary. The gain is an empirically disciplined model of perception; its limit is that the realist interpretation still needs an argument.
