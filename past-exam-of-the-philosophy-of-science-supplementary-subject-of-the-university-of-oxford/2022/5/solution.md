↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

**The [covering-law model](../../../covering-law-model.md) explains an event by showing how it falls under general laws in specified circumstances.** It captures a major form of scientific understanding, but entailment and predictability do not by themselves capture explanatory relevance or direction.

In the [deductive-nomological model](../../../deductive-nomological-model.md), let $L$ be laws, $C$ relevant circumstances, and $E$ the event description. The explanans is $L,C$; the explanandum is $E$. The argument has the form

$$
\boxed{L,C\ \vdash\ E.}
$$

The [deduction](../../../deductive-reasoning.md) must be valid, at least one law must be essential to it, the explanans must have empirical content, and its premises must be true for a sound explanation. Merely writing a valid argument from arbitrary premises is insufficient. [Carl Hempel](../../../carl-gustav-hempel.md) and [Paul Oppenheim](../../../paul-oppenheim.md) also identify a structural affinity between explanation and prediction: supplied beforehand, the same premises could in principle support the prediction.[section 3: deductive schema, adequacy conditions and the explanation/prediction relationship](../../../studies-in-the-logic-of-explanation-by-carl-hempel-and-paul-oppenheim.md)

For an ideal [simple pendulum](../../../simple-pendulum-split.md) at small [amplitude](../../../wave-amplitude.md), [Newtonian mechanics](../../../newtonian-mechanics-split.md) and the specified constraints yield

$$
\ddot\theta+\frac{g}{\ell}\theta=0,\qquad T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{g}}.
$$

Here $ell$ is the pendulum's [length](../../../length.md), $g$ the local [gravitational acceleration](../../../gravitational-acceleration.md), and $T$ its [oscillation period](../../../period-of-an-oscillation.md). The [deduction](../../../deductive-reasoning.md) connects an observed regularity to more general laws and explains why changing [length](../../../length.md) changes the [oscillation period](../../../period-of-an-oscillation.md). The approximation must be stated: a finite-amplitude, dissipative [simple pendulum](../../../simple-pendulum-split.md) does not exactly satisfy these premises. A useful model-based explanation can be approximately correct even when the ideal premises are not literally true.

The model's appeal is that it requires more than an evocative story. It displays assumptions and shows why they constrain the phenomenon. It can also explain laws through wider laws: [geometrical optics](../../../geometrical-optics-split.md) can recover a [refraction](../../../refraction.md) law from suitable descriptions of [wave propagation](../../../wave-propagation.md). A [principle theory](../../../principle-theory.md) can explain by constraints without supplying a detailed [constructive theory](../../../constructive-theory.md) of every constituent process. This is a strength of the account's generality.

The first difficulty is asymmetry. For a vertical pole of vertical [length](../../../length.md) $h$ on level ground and the Sun's [elevation angle](../../../altitude.md) $\alpha$, [geometrical optics](../../../geometrical-optics-split.md) gives the shadow's [length](../../../length.md)

$$
s=h\cot\alpha,\qquad h=s\tan\alpha,\qquad 0<\alpha<\frac\pi2.
$$

With suitable premises, either [deduction](../../../deductive-reasoning.md) is valid. Yet the pole and illumination explain the shadow, whereas measuring the shadow ordinarily allows inference to the pole's vertical [length](../../../length.md) without explaining why the pole has that vertical [length](../../../length.md). The same equation supports both calculations. Its logical form does not encode the relevant direction of dependence. Context can change the question, but the distinction between explanation and evidential reconstruction remains.

The second difficulty is irrelevance. Adding the premise that a laboratory wall is blue to a correct [simple pendulum](../../../simple-pendulum-split.md) derivation preserves validity and the essential use of physical laws. The extra premise contributes nothing to explaining the [oscillation period](../../../period-of-an-oscillation.md). More elaborate examples show the same problem when irrelevant factors are hidden inside statistical regularities. The explanatory account needs a way to identify what matters under relevant changes, not just what accompanies a valid derivation.

The [inductive-statistical model](../../../inductive-statistical-model.md) extends the programme to explanations using statistical laws and high [conditional probability](../../../conditional-probability.md), relative to appropriate available information. This is an extension beyond the deductive focus of the 1948 article. High [probability](../../../probability.md) is nevertheless not necessary for every explanation. [Radioactive decay](../../../radioactive-decay.md) can explain an infrequent detector event through a physical process and its [nuclear decay constant](../../../nuclear-decay-constant.md), although that particular event in a short interval was unlikely. Nor does a high [conditional probability](../../../conditional-probability.md) automatically establish explanatory relevance: a reliable indicator may be an effect of the outcome or share another cause with it.

A better assessment therefore retains law-based derivations as one explanatory resource while adding dependence, relevance and the question being asked. [Mechanistic explanations](../../../mechanistic-explanation.md) identify productive processes; accounts based on interventions ask which changes would alter the outcome. Neither proposal needs to force every mathematical or constraint-based explanation into a causal story. The [covering-law model](../../../covering-law-model.md) illuminates how laws make phenomena intelligible, but it is too broad as a sufficient test and too restrictive as a universal necessary form.
