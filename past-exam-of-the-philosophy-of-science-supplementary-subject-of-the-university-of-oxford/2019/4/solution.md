<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Two problems central to physics concern definite outcomes in [quantum measurement](../../../quantum-measurement-split.md) and the temporal direction of [entropy](../../../entropy-split.md) increase. They expose different gaps between a dynamical description and the phenomena explained; neither can be resolved merely by saying that physics predicts observations successfully.

For the [measurement problem](../../../measurement-problem.md), combine a two-state system with an apparatus initially in a ready state. If an ideal [unitary operator](../../../unitary-operator.md) correlates basis states with distinct pointer states, its [linearity](../../../linearity.md) gives

$$
(\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle)|A_0\rangle
\longmapsto
\alpha|0\rangle|A^{(0)}\rangle+\beta|1\rangle|A^{(1)}\rangle.
$$

The same dynamics that gives reliable correlations therefore gives an entangled final state, rather than a rule choosing one pointer value. The problem is not that the state has failed to predict possible outcomes, but how definite outcomes and their probabilities are related to that state.

[Quantum decoherence](../../../quantum-decoherence.md) explains why interaction with an environment suppresses interference in the apparatus's [reduced density matrix](../../../reduced-density-matrix.md): its off-diagonal term is multiplied by the [inner product](../../../inner-product-split.md) of the corresponding environmental states. Near-orthogonal records make this term very small. Tracing out the environment, however, does not by itself select one term in the global superposition. An [objective-collapse theory](../../../objective-collapse-theory.md) changes the dynamics, while a [many-worlds interpretation](../../../many-worlds-interpretation.md) changes what is required of an outcome. Distinguishable modifications can be tested; interpretations reproducing the same predictions raise additional explanatory and conceptual questions. Decoherence solves a substantial dynamical part of the problem without automatically settling every interpretation.

The second issue is the [thermodynamic arrow of time](../../../thermodynamic-arrow-of-time.md). In a classical model, [time reversal in classical mechanics](../../../time-reversal-in-classical-mechanics.md) can pair an entropy-increasing trajectory with a reversed entropy-decreasing one. [Liouville theorem in Hamiltonian mechanics](../../../liouville-s-theorem-hamiltonian.md) preserves phase-space volume, so reversible Hamiltonian dynamics does not imply a universal monotonic increase of [fine-grained entropy](../../../fine-grained-gibbs-entropy.md). The [Second law of thermodynamics](../../../second-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) therefore needs a statistical and boundary-condition account, not a deduction from reversibility alone.

[Boltzmann entropy](../../../boltzmann-s-entropy-formula.md) assigns larger entropy to a macrostate occupying more compatible phase-space volume. Starting from a suitable low-entropy condition, overwhelmingly more accessible microstates can evolve toward larger macrostates. But an unrestricted typicality argument applied at an arbitrary low-entropy instant generally points toward increasing entropy in both temporal directions. The [past hypothesis](../../../past-hypothesis.md) supplies an asymmetric low-entropy boundary condition in the past; one must still justify the probability measure and the macroscopic description used.

These are genuine partial resolutions rather than interchangeable cures. Decoherence concerns the accessibility of interference and records; a past boundary condition concerns temporal asymmetry in a statistical explanation. A satisfactory account must say which extra assumptions it introduces and which observable consequences follow. Neither problem licenses dismissing successful physics, but both show why a predictive formalism and a complete interpretation of its success are different achievements.
