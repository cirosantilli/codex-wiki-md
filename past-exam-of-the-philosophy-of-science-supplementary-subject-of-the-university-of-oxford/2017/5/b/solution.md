<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $R(x)$ mean that $x$ is a [raven](../../../../raven.md) and $B(x)$ that it is black. The hypothesis

$$
H:\ \forall x\,[R(x)\Rightarrow B(x)]
$$

is [logically equivalent](../../../../logical-equivalence.md) to $\forall x\,[\neg B(x)\Rightarrow\neg R(x)]$. If the observed paper is both nonblack and a nonraven, it is a positive instance of the second formulation. Combining an unrestricted positive-instance rule with preservation of [scientific confirmation](../../../../scientific-confirmation.md) under [logical equivalence](../../../../logical-equivalence.md) produces the [raven paradox](../../../../raven-paradox.md).[opening derivation of the Nicod and equivalence conditions](../../../../how-bayesian-confirmation-theory-handles-the-paradox-of-the-ravens-by-branden-fitelson-and-james-hawthorne.md)

Whether the observation actually supplies evidence depends on what was already known and how the object was selected. In [Bayesian confirmation](../../../../bayesian-confirmation.md), with background $K$, support means $P(H\mid E,K)>P(H\mid K)$. If the observer already knows the selected item is a nonraven and its colour is conditionally independent of $H$, then $P(E\mid H,K)=P(E\mid\neg H,K)$: the [likelihood ratio](../../../../likelihood-ratio.md) is one and there is no confirmation. If all of $E$ was already known, learning it again also gives no update.

Under a different sampling scheme, the same classification can provide slight support. In the [finite-population model of raven confirmation](../../../../finite-population-model-of-raven-confirmation.md), fix $r$ [ravens](../../../../raven.md) and $M$ nonblack objects. Compare no nonblack raven with exactly one. Sampling uniformly among the nonblack objects gives a nonraven with probabilities $1$ and $1-1/M$, hence likelihood ratio $(1-1/M)^{-1}>1$. If $M$ is large this is tiny evidence, not a proof. This example establishes possibility under explicit assumptions, not that every white-paper observation has that evidential role.

The appropriate answer is therefore conditional: mere logical instantiation does not settle the support supplied by observing this paper. State the background and sampling procedure. A familiar paper deliberately selected as a known nonraven normally supplies no new evidence about raven colour; a specified alternative sampling model may yield a small positive update.
