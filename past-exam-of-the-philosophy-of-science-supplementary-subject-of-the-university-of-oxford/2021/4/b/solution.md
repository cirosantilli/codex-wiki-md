<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**[Falsificationism](../../../../falsificationism.md) is more plausible than a requirement of conclusive verification, but a fallible theory of testing should retain positive evidential support as well as criticism.** The comparison changes if [verificationism](../../../../verificationism.md) is weakened to mean empirical confirmability rather than proof.

First separate a methodological thesis from a thesis about meaning. Methodological [verificationism](../../../../verificationism.md) says that scientific theories should be established by verifying observations. A stronger semantic version treats verifiability as a criterion of meaningful factual assertion. These positions are not identical. A theory might be meaningful and empirically significant even though no finite test can establish its unrestricted truth.

The central asymmetry is logical. For a universal claim $\forall x\,F(x)$, observing $F(a_1),\ldots,F(a_n)$ leaves unexamined cases open. A genuine counterinstance $\neg F(b)$ contradicts that universal claim. A finite sequence of favourable tests of an unrestricted [physical law](../../../../physical-law.md) therefore differs from a conclusive [deduction](../../../../deductive-reasoning.md) of it. This gives [falsificationism](../../../../falsificationism.md) a substantial advantage over strong [verificationism](../../../../verificationism.md): it makes scientific risk possible without demanding an unattainable final proof.

[Karl Popper](../../../../karl-popper.md)'s methodological proposal is not to invent theories that are false, but to formulate conjectures with empirical content and subject them to severe tests. Surviving such tests amounts to corroboration, not conclusive verification.[chapter 1, section I, numbered conclusions 1–7 on testability and corroborating evidence](../../../../science-conjectures-and-refutations-by-karl-popper.md) A theory that prohibits nothing cannot gain comparable credit simply by retelling whatever happens.

However, the logical asymmetry does not make scientific decisions automatic. The alleged counterinstance depends on identifying the object, measuring the relevant quantity and applying the theory in an appropriate domain. In $T\land A\Rightarrow E$, failure of $E$ refutes the conjunction, not uniquely $T$. The [Duhem problem](../../../../duhem-problem.md) therefore defeats a naive rule that every discrepant reading immediately falsifies the central theory. Independent tests of the [auxiliary hypotheses](../../../../auxiliary-hypothesis.md) can make rejection well justified, but that is additional evidential work.

[Statistical models](../../../../statistical-model-split.md) create another difficulty. For $n$ mutually independent trials, each governed by a [Bernoulli distribution](../../../../bernoulli-distribution.md) with success [probability](../../../../probability.md) $0<p<1$, a specified sequence with $k$ successes has [probability](../../../../probability.md) $p^k(1-p)^{n-k}>0$. An unusual sequence is therefore not a logical counterexample to this model. An appropriate [statistical hypothesis test](../../../../statistical-hypothesis-test-split.md) instead asks how surprising a specified discrepancy would be under the model, allowing controlled but nonzero error. The decision depends on the test protocol, sample size and alternatives. Recasting such testing as literal contradiction would erase the distinction between impossible and improbable outcomes.

The universal-claim argument also has a converse. An existential claim can sometimes be established by one well-authenticated instance, although no finite failed search proves that there are no instances anywhere. Therefore the logical asymmetry concerns forms of claims; it is not a proof that every scientific statement must have exactly the same testing structure.

A defender of weak [verificationism](../../../../verificationism.md) can accept all this and require only that observations can increase support. [Bayesian confirmation](../../../../bayesian-confirmation.md), for example, compares a theory's [likelihood](../../../../likelihood-function.md) with those of alternatives. That avoids treating favourable evidence as a proof. It does inherit questions about [prior probabilities](../../../../prior-probability.md) and the [problem of induction](../../../../problem-of-induction.md), but criticism does not eliminate those questions either. Scientists still need reasons to use one surviving theory rather than another when planning further work.

Thus [falsificationism](../../../../falsificationism.md) wins against conclusive [verificationism](../../../../verificationism.md) by recognizing the openness of empirical theory and the importance of possible failure. It does not win a monopoly on rational scientific reasoning. A stronger account combines demanding tests, scrutiny of [auxiliary hypotheses](../../../../auxiliary-hypothesis.md), appropriate statistical treatment and defeasible positive support. The main achievement is disciplined error correction, rather than replacing one promise of certainty with another.
