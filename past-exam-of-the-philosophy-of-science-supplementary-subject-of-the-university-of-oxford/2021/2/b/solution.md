**Explicit reference to laws is not necessary for every good [scientific explanation](../../../../scientific-explanation.md). Laws remain indispensable in many explanations, but their occurrence alone is not sufficient either.** The issue turns partly on whether a law means a contingent regularity of nature, any supporting generalization, or even a mathematical necessity.

The [deductive-nomological model](../../../../deductive-nomological-model.md) offers a strong positive case. Laws and circumstances entail the phenomenon, and at least one law is essential to the derivation. For example, [Newton's second law](../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) and an inverse-square [gravitational force](../../../../gravitational-force.md), with appropriate initial conditions, yield predictions for an idealized two-body [orbit](../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md). A universal relation unifies cases that otherwise look like unrelated observations. [Carl Hempel](../../../../carl-gustav-hempel.md) and [Paul Oppenheim](../../../../paul-oppenheim.md) explicitly develop adequacy conditions for such deductive explanations; their 1948 paper should not be read as a finished treatment of every statistical explanation.[section 3, the deductive schema and conditions of adequacy](../../../../studies-in-the-logic-of-explanation-by-carl-hempel-and-paul-oppenheim.md)

However, derivability does not always give the right explanatory direction. In a simplified [geometrical optics](../../../../geometrical-optics-split.md) model, a vertical pole of height $h$ on level ground casts a shadow of length $\ell=h/\tan\theta$ at solar elevation $\theta$. Given $h$ and $\theta$, one derives $\ell$; given $\ell$ and $\theta$, one derives $h$. The same geometrical relations enter both derivations. Yet the shadow does not normally explain why the pole was built to that height. The reverse calculation is a way to measure its height. The difference concerns which features produce or constrain which others, rather than deductive validity alone.

[Mechanistic explanation](../../../../mechanistic-explanation.md) is another challenge to a compulsory law-based format. A clock stopped because debris blocked a particular wheel, thereby preventing the mechanism from advancing. Inspecting the obstruction, removing it and observing restored operation can answer the causal question with considerable precision. The explanation describes organization and a relevant difference-making event without stating a universal law. It is more informative about this failure than the observation that every component obeyed [Newtonian mechanics](../../../../newtonian-mechanics-split.md).

A defender of the [covering-law model](../../../../covering-law-model.md) can reply that the causal account relies implicitly on regularities: debris that offers no resistance would not stop the wheel. This is a fair warning against treating a bare chronology as an explanation. But it does not establish that explanatory understanding requires identifying exceptionless fundamental laws, or that replacing the account with a complete microscopic derivation would improve the answer. Stable, tested relations in the actual operating regime may be sufficient.

An explanation can also turn on a mathematical constraint. Suppose an engineer asks why a connected network with four odd-degree junctions cannot be inspected by a route using every segment exactly once. Represent junctions as [vertices](../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) and segments as [edges](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) of an [undirected graph](../../../../undirected-graph.md). Every intermediate visit pairs an incoming and an outgoing [edge](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md), so only the two endpoints of an open [trail](../../../../trail-in-a-graph.md) can have odd [vertex degree](../../../../degree-graph-theory.md). Four such [vertices](../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) therefore exclude an [Eulerian trail](../../../../eulerian-trail.md). The impossibility persists if segment lengths, construction materials or walking speeds change while the connections remain fixed. This is the obstruction in the [Seven Bridges of Königsberg](../../../../seven-bridges-of-konigsberg.md).[sections 6, 9 and 20: connectivity data, the four-odd-region obstruction and the general rule](../../../../solutio-problematis-ad-geometriam-situs-pertinentis-by-leonhard-euler.md)

The physical application requires that the [graph](../../../../graph-split.md) represent the allowed routes correctly. Once that is granted, the explanatory necessity comes from [parity](../../../../parity.md), rather than a contingent [physical law](../../../../physical-law.md). Calling every mathematical constraint a law would preserve the original claim only by greatly widening its meaning. A critic may restrict “scientific” to causal explanations, but that restriction needs justification when applied mathematics answers a concrete engineering question.

Thus laws are powerful explanatory resources, and good explanations normally support relevant what-if judgements. Those judgements can be grounded in laws, mechanisms or mathematical constraints. Requiring explicit reference to laws imposes an unnecessarily narrow format; requiring some disciplined account of dependence is much more defensible.
