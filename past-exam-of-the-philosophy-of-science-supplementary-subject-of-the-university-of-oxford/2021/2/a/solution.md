<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**Old evidence challenges a simple account of confirmation as actual conditional updating, but does not refute every probabilistic treatment of the [raven paradox](../../../../raven-paradox.md).** The strongest response distinguishes support from newly acquired information and makes the sampling procedure explicit.

Let $H$ say that every [raven](../../../../raven.md) is black. Its contrapositive says that every nonblack thing is a nonraven. Since these statements are [logically equivalent](../../../../logical-equivalence.md), evidence should support them equally. Combining this equivalence requirement with the unrestricted rule that every positive instance confirms a universal generalization appears to make discovering a white shoe support $H$. The tension is between that result and the judgement that such a discovery is irrelevant.[section 5, the paradoxes generated by instance confirmation and equivalence](../../../../studies-in-the-logic-of-confirmation-i-by-carl-hempel.md)

[Bayesian confirmation](../../../../bayesian-confirmation.md) replaces an automatic instance rule with a comparison of [likelihoods](../../../../likelihood-function.md). For background $K$, positive [prior probability](../../../../prior-probability.md) for both $H$ and its alternative, and evidence $E$ of positive [probability](../../../../probability.md),

$$
P(H\mid E,K)>P(H\mid K)
\iff P(E\mid H,K)>P(E\mid\neg H,K).
$$

The equivalence follows directly from [Bayes' theorem](../../../../bayes-theorem.md). A statement's grammatical appearance as a positive instance does not determine either [likelihood](../../../../likelihood-function.md).

Consider an intentionally simple finite model. There are $r$ [ravens](../../../../raven.md) and $M$ nonblack objects, with these counts fixed under both hypotheses. Under $H$ none of the nonblack objects is a [raven](../../../../raven.md); under the sole alternative $H_1$, exactly one is. Selecting uniformly among the nonblack objects and discovering a nonraven has [likelihoods](../../../../likelihood-function.md) $1$ and $1-1/M$. Selecting uniformly among the [ravens](../../../../raven.md) and discovering a black one has [likelihoods](../../../../likelihood-function.md) $1$ and $1-1/r$. With equal [prior probabilities](../../../../prior-probability.md), the respective [posterior probabilities](../../../../posterior-probability.md) of $H$ are

$$
\boxed{\frac{1}{2-1/M}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\frac{1}{2-1/r}.}
$$

If $M\gg r$, the first discovery supplies far less support. For $M=10^6$ and $r=100$, the increases over $1/2$ are approximately $2.5\times10^{-7}$ and $0.00251$. This illustrates a possible resolution of the intuitive disparity, not a theorem that every nonblack nonraven always confirms $H$. More complex alternatives can reverse the comparison, and examining something already known to be a shoe need supply no relevant new information.[Introduction and the analysis conditioning confirmation on background assumptions and sampling information](../../../../how-bayesian-confirmation-theory-handles-the-paradox-of-the-ravens-by-branden-fitelson-and-james-hawthorne.md)

Now suppose $E$ is already in $K$. Then $P(E\mid K)=1$, and ordinary conditioning gives

$$
P(H\mid E,K)=P(H\mid K).
$$

There is no further increase from learning the same fact again. Yet an already established observation can become an important reason to accept a subsequently developed theory. [Clark Glymour](../../../../clark-glymour.md)'s [problem of old evidence](../../../../problem-of-old-evidence.md) exposes this mismatch between actual updating and scientific assessments of evidential support.[chapter 3 of Theory and Evidence, discussion of the old-evidence/new-theory problem](../../../../why-i-am-not-a-bayesian-by-clark-glymour.md)

One response evaluates the observation relative to an earlier or counterfactual background that does not contain it. That can represent how strongly a theory accounts for the observation compared with alternatives. It must specify that background rather than pretend to remove knowledge uniquely from a person's present beliefs. Another response concerns learning that a theory successfully accounts for the observation. That discovery can be new even when the observation is old. Modelling it requires attention to limited reasoning and changing hypotheses; an ideal agent already knowing every logical consequence cannot simply learn an entailment it previously overlooked.

Neither manoeuvre licenses counting the same evidence twice, or ignoring that parameters were selected to fit it. Neither establishes a unique complete theory of confirmation. Nevertheless, the old-evidence objection concerns the interpretation and completeness of a probabilistic framework; it does not invalidate the [likelihood](../../../../likelihood-function.md) calculation for genuinely new sampling information.

The preferable solution combines a background-sensitive account of support with rejection of unrestricted instance confirmation. It preserves [logical equivalence](../../../../logical-equivalence.md) while allowing a white shoe to provide tiny support, no support or contrary support under different justified models. This is more explanatory than assigning confirmation solely from logical form, although assessing old evidence requires additional work beyond routine conditioning.
