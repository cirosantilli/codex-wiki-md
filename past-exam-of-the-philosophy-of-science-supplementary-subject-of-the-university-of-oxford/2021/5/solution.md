<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

**It is implausible to deny every case of [revolutionary theory change](../../../revolutionary-theory-change.md), but reasonable to reject the claim that such changes are instantaneous, complete or wholly disconnected from earlier science.** Much turns on the strength of the proposed notion of revolution.

[Thomas Kuhn](../../../thomas-kuhn.md) challenges a picture of scientific development as the steady addition of true propositions. His contrast between [normal science](../../../normal-science.md) and revolutionary episodes concerns changes in accepted problems, methods and exemplary achievements as well as changes in propositions. A community can revise the framework within which it decides what needs explaining.[Introduction, contrasting cumulative development with changes in the commitments governing scientific practice](../../../the-structure-of-scientific-revolutions-by-thomas-kuhn.md) This is a historical proposal, not the claim that every important discovery is a revolution.

The transition from [Newtonian mechanics](../../../newtonian-mechanics-split.md) to [special relativity](../../../special-relativity-split.md) supplies a clear physical example of a deep conceptual change. Consider two spatially separated events, and let their differences in one [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md) be $\Delta t$ and $\Delta x$. A [Lorentz transformation](../../../lorentz-transformation.md) gives

$$
\Delta t'=\gamma\left(\Delta t-\frac{v\Delta x}{c^2}\right),\qquad
\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}.
$$

For $\Delta t=0$, $\Delta x\ne0$ and $v\ne0$, they are not simultaneous in the second [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md). By contrast, the [Galilean transformation](../../../galilean-transformation.md) takes $t'=t$, so simultaneity is invariant. The change concerns how time coordinates assigned by separated clocks enter physical measurement, not merely one corrected numerical constant. [Albert Einstein](../../../albert-einstein.md) begins by defining simultaneity through a clock procedure and then derives the transformation from his postulates.[sections 1–3, clock synchronization and the transformation equations](../../../on-the-electrodynamics-of-moving-bodies-by-albert-einstein.md)

One reason to deny revolutions is precisely that the new account retains old achievements. For $|v|/c\ll1$, and within regimes where relativistic corrections to the relevant measurements are negligible, [Newtonian mechanics](../../../newtonian-mechanics-split.md) remains useful. Experimental records, instruments, mathematical methods and many successful predictions cross the transition. That continuity is real. It does not show that the physical interpretation of simultaneity remained unchanged. Numerical approximation in a restricted domain is weaker than identity of conceptual commitments.

A second objection is that major changes emerge gradually. Earlier theories and experiments prepare the transition; different researchers adopt it at different times; its consequences take decades to develop. This undermines a picture of one decisive moment transforming all science. It does not undermine a notion of [revolutionary theory change](../../../revolutionary-theory-change.md) as an extended reorganization of a field. Political revolutions are themselves extended events; the metaphor need not require an instantaneous rupture.

A third objection concerns rational comparison. If a revolution changes standards and meanings, perhaps rival theories cannot be compared at all. But this extreme conclusion is unnecessary. Rival accounts can share instrument records, limited predictions and mathematical limiting relations while differing about their interpretation. Explaining why those records agree in one domain and discriminate theories in another is itself a rational comparison. Conceptual change can make translation partial and difficult without making all evidence inaccessible across the divide.

These qualifications also constrain the historical claim. A local change in one field need not create a universal new worldview. Nor should every anomaly be described as a crisis: [normal science](../../../normal-science.md) routinely works on discrepancies without replacing its framework. Whether an episode is revolutionary should depend on evidence of the extent and character of reorganization, not on a dramatic label attached afterwards.

The defensible conclusion is therefore selective. Denying total, unprepared and irrational breaks may be historically plausible. Denying substantial transformations of fundamental concepts and research standards is much less so. The example of simultaneity shows how continuity of successful calculation can coexist with a change deep enough to merit the name revolution.
