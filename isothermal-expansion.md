# Isothermal expansion

↑ **Parent:** [Isothermal process](isothermal-process.md)

An [isothermal expansion](isothermal-expansion.md) increases the [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) of a [thermodynamic system](thermodynamic-system.md) at fixed [temperature](temperature.md). For a reversible [ideal gas](ideal-gas.md) path, the [gas](gas.md) delivers positive [work](work.md) and absorbs positive [heat](heat.md), with $Q=W_{\rm by,rev}=nRT\ln(V_f/V_i)$ and $\Delta U=0$. The [heat bath](heat-bath.md) supplies the expansion [energy](energy.md); without sufficient [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md), doing [work](work.md) instead lowers the [temperature](temperature.md). This transfer is the hot step of a [Carnot cycle](carnot-cycle.md), and it is why an expanding [gas](gas.md) need not cool when its surroundings continuously replenish its [internal energy](internal-energy.md).

An insulated [free expansion](free-expansion.md) into an evacuated compartment is different. In the idealization with no [heat](heat.md) exchange, no external [work](work.md) and no container [energy](energy.md) change, $Q=W_{\rm by}=0$. Since [internal energy of an ideal gas](internal-energy-of-an-ideal-gas.md) depends only on [temperature](temperature.md), its final equilibrium [temperature](temperature.md) equals its initial value. The transient need not have a uniform [temperature](temperature.md) or [pressure](pressure.md), so equal endpoint [temperatures](temperature.md) do not make it a reversible isotherm. Do not apply the logarithmic [isothermal ideal-gas work](isothermal-ideal-gas-work.md) formula to this irreversible process. Its [entropy](entropy.md) still increases by $\Delta S=nR\ln(V_f/V_i)$; [entropy change in free expansion](entropy-change-in-free-expansion.md) evaluates this [state function](state-function.md) difference using a hypothetical reversible path.[Equations 6.144–6.148 and the negligible-container-heat-capacity assumption](thermodynamics-and-statistical-mechanics-by-richard-fitzpatrick/free-expansion-of-gas.md)

For a [real gas](real-gas.md), even equal-[temperature](temperature.md) equilibrium endpoints need not have equal [internal energy](internal-energy.md). An insulated [free expansion](free-expansion.md) can therefore change the [temperature](temperature.md), while a controlled [isothermal expansion](isothermal-expansion.md) must provide the [heat](heat.md) needed for both its [internal energy](internal-energy.md) change and its delivered [work](work.md). The [internal energy at constant temperature](internal-energy-at-constant-temperature.md) article gives the [Van der Waals equation](van-der-waals-equation.md) example.[Equations 6.163–6.166: attractive interactions and free-expansion cooling](thermodynamics-and-statistical-mechanics-by-richard-fitzpatrick/van-der-waals-gas.md)
