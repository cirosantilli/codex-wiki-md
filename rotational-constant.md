# Rotational constant

↑ **Parent:** [Linear rigid rotor](linear-rigid-rotor.md)

In this article the [rotational constant](rotational-constant.md) is a [frequency](frequency.md),

$$
B=\frac{h}{8\pi^2I}.
$$

Its dimensions are $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, since the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md) has dimensions $\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ and a [moment of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md) has dimensions $\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. Some authors instead call $hB=\hbar^2/(2I)$ the energy-valued [rotational constant](rotational-constant.md); this is the convention in the earlier synthetic [molecular beam electric resonance](molecular-beam-electric-resonance.md) diagram. A third convention uses the [spectroscopic wavenumber](spectroscopic-wavenumber.md) $\widetilde B=B/c$, with $c$ the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). Always check units before using $\nu=2B(J+1)$.

If $B$ is known and the two [masses](mass.md) are specified, the rigid two-atom model gives the [bond length](bond-length.md) $r=\sqrt{h/(8\pi^2\mu B)}$. This is why [rotational spectroscopy](rotational-spectroscopy.md) can constrain [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md). A real fitted constant can depend on [molecular vibration](molecular-vibration.md), so this inference alone does not establish an equilibrium [bond length](bond-length.md).
