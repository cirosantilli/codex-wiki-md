# Finite-aperture stabilization of an inverted liquid surface

↑ **Parent:** [Rayleigh-Taylor instability](rayleigh-taylor-instability.md)

For a deep inviscid liquid above a gas of negligible inertia, an inverted [free surface](free-surface.md) has [dispersion relation](dispersion-relation-split.md) $\omega^2=q(\sigma q^2/\rho-g)$, $q=|k|$. The [Rayleigh-Taylor instability](rayleigh-taylor-instability.md) affects $0<q<k_c=\sqrt{\rho g/\sigma}$. A bounded aperture can suppress it if its smallest allowed [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) is at least $k_c$. The [capillary length](capillary-length.md) is $\ell_c=k_c^{-1}$ and the marginal [wavelength](wavelength.md) is $2\pi\ell_c$. A pinned one-dimensional span of width $L$ with a half-sine lowest mode instead has $k_{min}=\pi/L$, giving $L\le\pi\ell_c$. A numerical maximum container size therefore also requires aperture geometry and contact-line [boundary conditions](boundary-condition.md); the marginal wavelength alone is not a universal diameter.
