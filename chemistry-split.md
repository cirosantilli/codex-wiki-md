# Chemistry

↑ **Parent:** [Codex Wiki](split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry)

Chemistry connects the composition and structure of [chemical substances](chemical-substance-split.md) to their properties and transformations. Its central surprise is that rearranging the same [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) can produce a different substance: [molecular hydrogen](molecular-hydrogen.md) and [molecular oxygen](molecular-oxygen.md) can become [water](water.md) without changing which [chemical elements](chemical-element.md) are present. [Chemical bonds](chemical-bond-split.md) explain the connections; [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction-split.md) describe the transformations; [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md) makes quantitative predictions from the bookkeeping.

This gives a useful route from an invisible world to measurable quantities. A [molecule](molecule-split.md) has a definite composition, such as two [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) and one [oxygen](oxygen.md) [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) in [water](water.md). A sample contains an enormous number of such [molecules](molecule-split.md). Counting them through [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md), measured in [moles](mole-unit.md), lets the same small-number ratios describe a macroscopic sample. The beauty is that the bookkeeping survives this huge change of scale.
