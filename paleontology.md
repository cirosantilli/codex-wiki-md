## Ghost lineage

↑ **Parent:** [Paleontology](paleontology.md)

An inferred interval in which a lineage must have existed although it has not yet been sampled in the [fossil](#fossil) record.

## Palaeoproteomics

↑ **Parent:** [Paleontology](paleontology.md)

Study of ancient proteins, including sequence information used to test relationships of extinct organisms.

## Cambrian

↑ **Parent:** [Paleontology](paleontology.md)

A geological period beginning approximately 539 million years ago, with exceptional deposits documenting diverse early animals, including early [vertebrates](organism.md#vertebrate). Increased [fossil](#fossil) visibility and biological diversification must be distinguished.

## Ediacaran

↑ **Parent:** [Paleontology](paleontology.md)

The final geological period preceding the [Cambrian](#cambrian). Its [fossil](#fossil) communities provide evidence of animal complexity before the conspicuous [Cambrian](#cambrian) radiation.

## Fossil record

↑ **Parent:** [Paleontology](paleontology.md)

The preserved and recovered evidence of past life. First observed occurrences supply minimum ages; uneven preservation and sampling prevent interpreting every absence as a biological absence.

## Apidima

↑ **Parent:** [Paleontology](paleontology.md)

A Greek cave locality whose incomplete human crania have generated debated reconstructions, dates and taxonomic assignments. [Nature 571, pp. 500–504; reconstruction and uranium-series dating of Apidima 1 and 2](ecology-and-evolution-bibliography.md#apidima-cave-fossils-provide-earliest-evidence-of-homo-sapiens-in-eurasia-by-katerina-harvati-and-others)

## Qafzeh

## Skhul

## Misliya

↑ **Parent:** [Paleontology](paleontology.md)

An Israeli cave locality with a maxilla attributed to [Homo sapiens](organism.md#homo-sapiens) dated to approximately 177–194 thousand years ago. [Science 359, pp. 456–459; maxillary morphology and 177–194 ka dating interval](ecology-and-evolution-bibliography.md#the-earliest-modern-humans-outside-africa-by-israel-hershkovitz-and-others)

## Dmanisi

↑ **Parent:** [Paleontology](paleontology.md)

A Georgian archaeological and fossil locality with evidence of early [Pleistocene](planetary-science.md#pleistocene) human occupation. The earliest archaeological levels and the hominin-bearing deposits have different stratigraphic positions. [Results, stratigraphy and palaeomagnetic chronology; distinguish earliest occupation from hominin-bearing beds](ecology-and-evolution-bibliography.md#earliest-human-occupations-at-dmanisi-georgian-caucasus-dated-to-1-85-1-78-ma-by-reid-ferring-and-others)

## Taphonomy

↑ **Parent:** [Paleontology](paleontology.md)

Study of processes between an organism's death and observation of its remains, including transport, decay, burial and preservation. These processes bias the fossil and archaeological record.

### Fossilization

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Processes through which biological remains or traces enter the [fossil record](#fossil-record). Complete replacement by new minerals is only one possible pathway.

### Exceptional preservation

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Preservation retaining anatomical information normally removed by decay or burial alteration. It reflects particular preservational pathways, not an unbiased sample of all life.

### Reworking of fossils

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Erosion and redeposition of previously buried remains into a younger deposit, potentially confusing environmental and chronological interpretation.

### Taphonomic transport

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Movement of remains by currents, waves, gravity or organisms. Sorting depends on size, density, shape and articulation rather than solely on taxon abundance.

#### Hydraulic sorting

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomic transport](#taphonomic-transport)

Differential movement or deposition of remains by flowing water, dependent on their shape, density, size, orientation and articulation.

### Disarticulation

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Separation of connected skeletal elements as supporting tissues fail or external forces act. An articulated skeleton need not have been buried instantly.

### Post-mortem decay

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Transformation and loss of biological tissues after death through [autolysis](#autolysis), microbial activity and associated processes. Decay rates differ among anatomical characters.

#### Autolysis

↑ **Parent:** [Post-mortem decay](#post-mortem-decay)

Enzymatic breakdown of cells by their own components after loss of normal regulation.

### Biostratinomy

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Processes affecting remains before final burial, including decay, [disarticulation](#disarticulation), transport and surface modification. These mechanisms can continue or recur during reworking.

### Diagenesis

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Physical and chemical alteration after burial, potentially modifying [fossil](#fossil) tissues and isotopic compositions.

#### Fossil recrystallization

↑ **Parent:** [Diagenesis](#diagenesis)

Change in the crystal organization of [fossil](#fossil) material during alteration, potentially obscuring original histological or chemical information.

#### Diagenetic mineralization

↑ **Parent:** [Diagenesis](#diagenesis)

[precipitation](chemical-reaction.md#precipitation-chemistry) or replacement by minerals during burial alteration. Early mineral formation can retain fine biological detail, while later alteration can destroy or obscure it.

#### Sedimentary compaction

↑ **Parent:** [Diagenesis](#diagenesis)

Reduction of [sediment](planetary-science.md#sediment) volume during burial that can flatten, distort or fracture enclosed remains.

#### Phosphatization

↑ **Parent:** [Diagenesis](#diagenesis)

Preservation or replication by [calcium phosphate](chemical-substance.md#calcium-phosphate) minerals, sometimes retaining soft-tissue detail when [precipitation](chemical-reaction.md#precipitation-chemistry) occurs before its destruction.

### Exceptional fossil preservation

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Preservation retaining anatomical information normally lost, especially soft tissues. Such deposits reveal otherwise hidden diversity but still select particular environments and tissue types.

#### Chengjiang

↑ **Parent:** [Exceptional fossil preservation](#exceptional-fossil-preservation)

An exceptionally preserved early [Cambrian](#cambrian) biota from China, including [Haikouichthys](organism.md#haikouichthys) and [Myllokunmingia](organism.md#myllokunmingia).

#### Burgess Shale

↑ **Parent:** [Exceptional fossil preservation](#exceptional-fossil-preservation)

A [Cambrian](#cambrian) fossil-bearing succession in Canada preserving diverse animal [anatomy](anatomy.md), including [Metaspriggina](organism.md#metaspriggina).

### Carcass decay

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Postmortem loss and alteration of tissues. Replicated observations show that loss can be anatomically selective, so a decayed specimen may look systematically less derived.[Experimental character-loss sequences](ecology-and-evolution-bibliography.md#non-random-decay-of-chordate-characters-causes-bias-in-fossil-interpretation-by-robert-s-sansom-and-others)

### Time averaging

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Mixing remains produced at different times in one sampled assemblage. It can occur locally without long-distance transport, blurring short ecological events.

### Fossil reworking

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Erosion of an older deposit and incorporation of its [fossil](#fossil) material into a younger one. It can create a misleading apparent last occurrence.

### Transport of fossil remains

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Movement before final burial can sort remains by size, shape and [density](fluid-mechanics.md#density). Articulation alone cannot prove local origin, and disarticulation alone cannot prove transport.

### Fossil assemblage

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

A collection of [fossil](#fossil) remains associated in a specified deposit or sample. Its members may have different original habitats or ages; [transport of fossil remains](#transport-of-fossil-remains) and [time averaging](#time-averaging) affect its interpretation.

#### Allochthonous fossil assemblage

↑ **Parent:** [Fossil assemblage](#fossil-assemblage)

An assemblage containing remains transported from their original living sites. Hydraulic sorting can enrich durable parts or mix habitats; transport does not invariably disarticulate a carcass.

#### Autochthonous fossil assemblage

↑ **Parent:** [Fossil assemblage](#fossil-assemblage)

An assemblage whose organisms are preserved where they lived. Local origin does not eliminate [taphonomic bias](#taphonomic-bias): soft tissues can disappear and resistant parts can accumulate through time.

### Taphonomic bias

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomy](#taphonomy)

Systematic distortion of biological information through differential preservation and recovery. [teeth](anatomy.md#tooth) and delicate soft tissues need not have the same probability of entering the [fossil record](#fossil-record); even a locally buried assemblage can therefore give a biased account of its living community.

#### Stemward slippage

↑ **Parent:** [Taphonomic bias](#taphonomic-bias)

Erroneous placement toward the base of a lineage when loss of diagnostic characters through [carcass decay](#carcass-decay) is interpreted as an ancestral biological absence. Coding unobservable characters as unknown and comparing preservation across specimens can reduce the problem.

## Fossil

↑ **Parent:** [Paleontology](paleontology.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil)

### Transitional fossil

↑ **Parent:** [Fossil](#fossil)

A [fossil](#fossil) with a combination of ancestral and derived characters informative about an evolutionary transition. It need not be the direct ancestor of a named living group.

#### Missing link

↑ **Parent:** [Transitional fossil](#transitional-fossil)

A historical metaphor for a predicted but unsampled evolutionary connection. Modern phylogenetic hypotheses concern branching relationships and character changes rather than one obligatory intermediate.

### Trace fossil

↑ **Parent:** [Fossil](#fossil)

Preserved evidence of biological activity, such as a track or burrow. It can reveal movement or feeding even without body remains, but usually does not uniquely identify the producer.

### Rhynie chert

↑ **Parent:** [Fossil](#fossil)

A fossil deposit preserving early land-plant anatomy, including reproductive structures interpreted as [gametophytes](reproduction.md#gametophyte) and [sporophytes](reproduction.md#sporophyte).[Anatomy and proposed generation associations](plant-biology-bibliography.md#the-gametophyte-generation-of-some-early-devonian-land-plants-by-winfried-remy-and-others)
