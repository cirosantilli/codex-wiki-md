# Action summand can fail for an infinite-dimensional simple module

↑ **Parent:** [Action map of a Lie algebra representation](action-map-of-a-lie-algebra-representation.md)

Take the [Verma module](verma-module.md) for the [sl2 Lie algebra](sl2-lie-algebra.md) of highest weight $-2$, with basis $v_m$ and $fv_m=v_{m+1}$, $hv_m=(-2-2m)v_m$, $ev_m=-m(m+1)v_{m-1}$. It is simple. In $\mathfrak{sl}_2\otimes V$, the highest vectors of weight $-2$ are multiples of $w=e\otimes v_1-h\otimes v_0=f(e\otimes v_0)$. Every homomorphism to $V$ kills $e\otimes v_0$, whose weight is zero, and hence kills $w$. Every embedded copy of $V$ would have its highest vector proportional to $w$, so no projection onto such a copy can exist. Thus semisimplicity of the Lie algebra alone does not provide an action summand in infinite dimension.
