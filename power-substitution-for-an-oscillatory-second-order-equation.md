# Power substitution for an oscillatory second-order equation

↑ **Parent:** [Second-order linear differential equation](second-order-linear-differential-equation.md)

For $\gamma\ne0$ and $x>0$, the [change of variables](change-of-variables-formula.md) $u=x^\gamma$ converts

$$
x^{2-2\gamma}y''+(1-\gamma)x^{1-2\gamma}y'+\gamma^2y=0
$$

into $Y_{uu}+Y=0$, where $y(x)=Y(u)$. The [chain rule](chain-rule.md) cancels the first-derivative terms. Thus $\cos(x^\gamma)$ and $\sin(x^\gamma)$ form a pair of [linearly independent](linear-independence.md) solutions, with [Wronskian](wronskian.md) $\gamma x^{\gamma-1}$. This is a variable-speed reparametrization of the [harmonic oscillator equation](simple-harmonic-motion.md).
