# Surface energy

↑ **Parent:** [Surface tension](surface-tension.md)

For a [liquid](liquid.md) interface with constant [surface tension](surface-tension.md) $\gamma$, the mechanical [surface energy](surface-energy.md) is the [energy](energy.md) associated with its [surface area](surface-area.md) $A$:

$$
E_{\mathrm s}=\gamma A+C,\qquad
E_{\mathrm s}(A_2)-E_{\mathrm s}(A_1)=\gamma(A_2-A_1).
$$

The additive constant $C$ does not affect the [work](work.md) needed to change [surface area](surface-area.md). For several interfaces with the same [surface tension](surface-tension.md), $A$ means their total [surface area](surface-area.md); the two faces of a thin soap film must both be counted. [Tomas Bohr and Bernhard Scheichl, Surface Tension and Energy Conservation in a Moving Fluid, equation (13)](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.11403v2)

With assumed $\gamma=0.072\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$, creating $1\,\mathrm{cm^2}=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ of one interface requires $7.2\,\mathrm{\mu J}$ of reversible [work](work.md). In the sliding-rim example illustrated under [surface tension](surface-tension.md), moving a $20\,\mathrm{mm}$ rim outwards by $1\,\mathrm{mm}$ creates $4.0\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ across the two faces and requires $2.88\,\mathrm{\mu J}$. Independently, multiplying its $2.88\,\mathrm{mN}$ holding [force](force.md) by the displacement gives the same [work](work.md).

This makes the preference for a round isolated droplet precise. At fixed [volume](volume.md) $V$, the three-dimensional [isoperimetric inequality](isoperimetric-inequality.md) gives

$$
A^3\geq36\pi V^2,
$$

with equality for a [sphere](sphere.md). Thus a [sphere](sphere.md) minimizes [surface energy](surface-energy.md) among sufficiently regular closed shapes enclosing the same [volume](volume.md), when [surface tension](surface-tension.md) is uniform and other [energy](energy.md) contributions are negligible. This is an application of the [isoperimetric inequality](isoperimetric-inequality.md), rather than a proof of it. Walls, [wetting](wetting.md), and [gravity](gravity.md) can change the shape by adding constraints or other [energy](energy.md) contributions. [Francis E. Su et al., Isoperimetric Inequality, Math Fun Facts](https://math.hmc.edu/funfacts/isoperimetric-inequality/)

The differential rule $dE_{\mathrm s}=\gamma\,dA$ integrates directly to $E_{\mathrm s}=\gamma A+C$ because [surface tension](surface-tension.md) is constant. At fixed [liquid](liquid.md) [volume](volume.md), decreasing [surface area](surface-area.md) can drive [viscous fluid flow](viscous-fluid-flow-split.md); the released [surface energy](surface-energy.md) can supply [viscous dissipation](viscous-dissipation.md). This mechanical bookkeeping is useful for predicting which deformations can release [energy](energy.md). If [surface tension](surface-tension.md) depends on [temperature](temperature.md) or composition, distinguish this mechanical area-[work](work.md) relation from the complete interfacial [thermodynamic free energy](thermodynamic-free-energy.md) and [internal energy](internal-energy.md).
