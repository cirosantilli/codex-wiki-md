# Critical-layer regularity of a neutral Hazel mode

↑ **Parent:** [Neutral mode of the equal-width Hazel model](neutral-mode-of-the-equal-width-hazel-model.md)

For $0<k<1$, a [neutral mode of the equal-width Hazel model](neutral-mode-of-the-equal-width-hazel-model.md) behaves as $w\sim z^{1-k}$ near $z=0$. Its [derivative](derivative.md) diverges as $z^{-k}$, so it is not a classical continuously differentiable mode across the [critical level of an internal gravity wave](critical-level-of-an-internal-gravity-wave.md). The horizontal [velocity](velocity.md) is proportional to $w'/k$; its local [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) is finite only for $k<1/2$, because $\int_0^\epsilon z^{-2k}\,dz$ converges precisely then. One possible neutral-mode convention is the boundary value from $U-c$ with $c_i\downarrow0$, which fixes the phase of the power on the negative-$z$ side. The [Miles–Howard theorem](miles-howard-theorem.md) concerns growing modes with nonreal $c$ and does not rule out such singular neutral limits.
