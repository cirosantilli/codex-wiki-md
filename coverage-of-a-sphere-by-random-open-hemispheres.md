# Coverage of a sphere by random open hemispheres

↑ **Parent:** [Probability theory](probability-theory-split.md)

The probability that $N\geq1$ [independent](independent-random-variables.md) uniformly oriented open [hemispheres](hemisphere.md) cover a [sphere](sphere.md) is the displayed quantity. Coverage fails exactly when their normals lie in a closed hemisphere. With probability one, no three normals are coplanar and no pair is antipodal; a closed hemisphere containing the normals can then be perturbed into an open one. Condition on the unoriented axes of the normals. Their $N$ orientation signs remain independent fair bits, and the [regions of a general-position great-circle arrangement](regions-of-a-general-position-great-circle-arrangement.md) realize exactly $N(N-1)+2$ of the $2^N$ possible sign vectors. Each such vector corresponds to normals that fit into one open hemisphere, proving the complementary probability. The argument counts equiprobable orientation choices, not equal-area regions.
