# Unimodality of binomial coefficients

↑ **Parent:** [Binomial coefficient](binomial-coefficient-split.md)

For fixed nonnegative [integer](integer-split.md) $n$, the row $\binom n0,\ldots,\binom nn$ increases to its middle and then decreases. Indeed, for $0\le k<n$, the [ratio](ratio.md) of consecutive [binomial coefficients](binomial-coefficient-split.md) is $(n-k)/(k+1)$, which is greater than, equal to, or less than $1$ according as $k$ is less than, equal to, or greater than $(n-1)/2$. Thus even $n$ has one [maximum](maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) at $k=n/2$, while odd $n$ has two equal [maxima](maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) at $k=(n-1)/2,(n+1)/2$.
