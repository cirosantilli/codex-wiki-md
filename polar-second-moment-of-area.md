# Polar second moment of area

↑ **Parent:** [Second moment of area](second-moment-of-area.md)

The polar second moment of a planar area about a perpendicular axis is the integral of squared distance from that axis over area. It has units $\mathrm{m^4}$ and equals the sum of second moments about two perpendicular in-plane axes through the same origin. For a disk of radius $a$, $J_p=\int_0^a2\pi r^3\,dr=\pi a^4/2$. It is geometric, not a mass [moment of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md); a uniform cylinder has axial mass moment $\rho LJ_p$.
