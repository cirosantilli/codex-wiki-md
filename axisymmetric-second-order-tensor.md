# Axisymmetric second-order tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Tensor](tensor.md)

A Cartesian [tensor](tensor.md) invariant under all proper [rotations](rotation-mathematics.md) fixing a unit [vector](vector.md) $n$ is axisymmetric. Without assuming a [symmetric tensor](symmetric-tensor.md), its general second-order form is the displayed combination of the transverse identity, axial projector and axial antisymmetric part. For $n=e_3$ this is

$$
T=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\omega&0\\-\omega&\alpha&0\\0&0&\beta\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Indeed, imposing $RTR^T=T$ for a quarter-turn about $e_3$ forces $T_{11}=T_{22}$, $T_{12}=-T_{21}$ and all mixed axial-transverse entries to vanish. The planar block is a linear combination of the identity and the planar rotation generator, so it commutes with every rotation about the axis. Thus quarter-turn invariance already suffices for a second-order tensor. A [symmetric tensor](symmetric-tensor.md) has $\omega=0$, recovering the [axisymmetric symmetric rank-two tensor](axisymmetric-symmetric-rank-two-tensor.md) form. The antisymmetric term is allowed because only proper rotations about the fixed axis are imposed.
