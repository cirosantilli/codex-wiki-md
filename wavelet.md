# Wavelet

↑ **Parent:** [Fourier analysis](fourier-analysis-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelet)

A wavelet is a localized analyzing [function](function-split.md) whose shifted and scaled copies examine a [signal](signal.md) at different positions and resolutions. Its usefulness is to separate **where a change occurs** from **how large a region that change spans**. A brief feature can contribute to a few nearby [wavelet](wavelet.md) coefficients instead of being described entirely through global oscillations.

The [Haar wavelet](haar-wavelet.md) is the simplest example: compare the first half of an [interval](interval-mathematics.md) with the second. A constant value contributes nothing to this contrast, while a difference between the halves survives. Moving the [function](function-split.md) changes which part of the [signal](signal.md) is examined; shrinking it compares smaller neighbouring regions. In an [orthonormal wavelet](orthonormal-wavelet.md) construction, these comparisons fit together without duplicating squared-norm contributions.

A [scaling function](scaling-function.md) retains the coarse information that such contrasts omit. The [discrete wavelet transform](discrete-wavelet-transform.md) computes both coarse values and details, and the [wavelet series](wavelet-series.md) recombines them. This gives [signal processing](signal-processing.md) a representation that can expose local structure and support [image compression](image-compression.md). The transform itself is reversible in the complete orthonormal representation; discarding coefficients introduces approximation error. [Gilbert Strang, Wavelet Transforms versus Fourier Transforms (1993): localized bases and reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9304214)
