# Spacecraft tracking and the limits of the escape model

↑ **Parent:** [Escape velocity](escape-velocity.md)

Operational evidence comes from reconstructing a spacecraft's motion. A ground antenna transmits a coded signal; the spacecraft returns it. Corrected round-trip [time](time-in-physics.md) constrains distance, while the [Doppler effect](doppler-effect.md) of a coherent carrier constrains the line-of-sight [relative velocity](relative-velocity.md). NASA's navigation account describes calibration of onboard turnaround delays before launch, calibration of ground delays before use, and corrections for the [terrestrial atmosphere](terrestrial-atmosphere.md). Measurements over multiple stations and epochs constrain the [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md); range and [Doppler effect](doppler-effect.md) alone do not directly give all transverse motion. Navigators compare a fitted [spacecraft trajectory](spacecraft-trajectory.md) with later tracking and remeasure after propulsion to assess the maneuver. The account identifies [radiation pressure](radiation-pressure.md) and imperfect propulsion as disturbances. This provides practical checks of a dynamical model and its corrections, rather than a laboratory measurement of a universal [escape speed](escape-velocity.md). The simple [inverse-square potential](inverse-square-potential.md) predicts an unpowered exterior arc; accurate navigation includes additional [forces](force-split.md) and measurement delays.[Chapter 13: Navigation](basics-of-space-flight-2017-edition-by-dave-doody/chapter-13-navigation.md)
