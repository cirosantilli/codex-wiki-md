# Real analysis

↑ **Parent:** [Analysis](analysis-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_analysis)

Real analysis studies [limits](limit-of-a-function.md), [continuity](continuous-function.md), [differentiation](differentiation.md), [integration](integral-split.md), and convergence of [sequences](sequence.md) and [functions](function-split.md) taking values in the [real numbers](real-number.md). Its central question is how finite approximations determine an exact answer: what does an arbitrarily small error mean, and which operations respect that error? A [derivative](derivative.md) is a [limit](limit-of-a-function.md) of quotients, while a [Riemann integral](riemann-integral.md) is obtained from increasingly fine [Riemann sums](riemann-sum.md). Both need conditions that make the answer independent of the approximations chosen.

Two organizing ideas are [completeness of the real numbers](completeness-of-the-real-numbers.md), which ensures that suitable approximations have a destination, and [uniform convergence](uniform-convergence.md), which controls approximation error over an entire domain. [Pointwise convergence](pointwise-convergence.md) supplies separate guarantees at separate inputs; [uniform convergence](uniform-convergence.md) supplies one guarantee for all inputs. This distinction explains why replacing an approximating [function](function-split.md) by its [limit](limit-of-a-function.md) can preserve [continuity](continuous-function.md) or [integrals](integral-split.md) under appropriate hypotheses, yet fail without them.

For historical context, Cauchy's 1821 _Cours d'analyse_ explicitly discusses convergence of [series](series-mathematics.md). Its Note VII treats double [series](series-mathematics.md) and compares summing by rows and by columns under stated convergence assumptions: controlling an infinite process was already a concrete problem. This is historical context for questions about [limits](limit-of-a-function.md), rather than an attribution of the modern definitions below. [Cauchy, Cours d'analyse (1821), Note VII, pp. 537–539, original scan](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/8543076.pdf).
