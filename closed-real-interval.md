# Closed real interval

↑ **Parent:** [Interval (mathematics)](interval-mathematics.md)

A bounded [closed interval](closed-real-interval.md) includes both of its endpoints:

$$
[a,b]=\{x\in\mathbb R:a\leq x\leq b\},\qquad a\leq b.
$$

If $a=b$, it consists of one point. Its length is $b-a$. In contrast, the [open interval](open-interval.md) $(a,b)$ excludes both endpoints; half-open [real intervals](interval-mathematics.md) include just one.

Including the endpoints has a useful consequence for [limits](limit-of-a-function.md): a convergent [sequence](sequence.md) whose terms lie in $[a,b]$ has its [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) in $[a,b]$ too. Finite endpoints also make this a [bounded set](bounded-set.md), so it is a [compact set](compact-space.md) by the [Heine-Borel theorem](heine-borel-theorem.md). These two properties support both the [nested interval theorem](nested-interval-theorem.md) and the [extreme value theorem](extreme-value-theorem.md).
