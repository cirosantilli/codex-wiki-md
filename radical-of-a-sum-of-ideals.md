# Radical of a sum of ideals

↑ **Parent:** [Radical of an ideal](radical-of-an-ideal.md)

In a [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md), the [radical of an ideal](radical-of-an-ideal.md) is monotone and idempotent: inclusion $I\subseteq J$ gives $\sqrt I\subseteq\sqrt J$, and $a^n\in\sqrt I$ means $a^{nm}\in I$ for some $m$, giving $\sqrt{\sqrt I}=\sqrt I$. Since $\sqrt{I+J}$ contains both $\sqrt I$ and $\sqrt J$, it contains their [sum of ideals](sum-of-ideals.md). Taking [radicals of ideals](radical-of-an-ideal.md) gives $\sqrt{\sqrt I+\sqrt J}\subseteq\sqrt{I+J}$. The reverse inclusion follows from $I+J\subseteq\sqrt I+\sqrt J$.

In particular, $\sqrt I+\sqrt J=R$ implies $I+J=R$. One can also see this directly: write $1=a+b$ with $a^m\in I$ and $b^n\in J$, then expand $(a+b)^{m+n-1}$ by the [binomial theorem](binomial-theorem.md). Each term belongs to $I$ or $J$, so $1\in I+J$.
