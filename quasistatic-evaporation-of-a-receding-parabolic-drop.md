# Quasistatic evaporation of a receding parabolic drop

↑ **Parent:** [Thin-film equation](thin-film-equation.md)

For a symmetric two-dimensional thin drop with uniform evaporative loss $\epsilon$, a freely receding [contact line](contact-line.md) and fixed leading outer [contact angle](contact-angle.md) $\theta$, quasistatic capillary balance makes the height quadratic. Its volume per transverse length is $2\theta s^2/3$. Since $h_t+(h\overline u)_x=-\epsilon$ and the contact-line flux vanishes, volume loss is $-2\epsilon s$. On slow time $T=\epsilon t$, this gives the stated constant recession speed.

Substitute that speed into $h_t$ and integrate flux from the centre, obtaining $h\overline u=-\epsilon x(s^2-x^2)/(4s^2)$. Division by height gives the displayed inward [velocity](velocity.md). With a pinned width instead, the same uniform-loss model gives $\theta_t=-3\epsilon/s$ and outward [velocity](velocity.md) $\overline u=\epsilon x/(s\theta)$. Thus the contact-line assumption alone reverses solute transport. A pinned perimeter can produce a [coffee-ring effect](coffee-ring-effect.md); the receding fixed-angle outer model predicts central transport instead. A strictly no-slip sharp moving [contact line](contact-line.md) is singular, so the finite outer angle is a leading quasistatic approximation requiring microscopic regularization at finite speed.
