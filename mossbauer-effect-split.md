# Mössbauer effect

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear physics](nuclear-physics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mössbauer_effect)

A nucleus bound in a solid can emit or absorb a [photon](photon.md) without exciting lattice vibrations, transferring momentum to the solid as a whole. The resulting recoil energy is negligible compared with the isolated-nucleus recoil, making very narrow resonant emission and absorption lines observable. The [Pound-Rebka experiment](pound-rebka-experiment.md) uses this effect to resolve the small frequency shift from [gravitational redshift](gravitational-redshift.md).

The striking feature is the combination of a large [photon](photon.md) [energy](energy-split.md) and a tiny [natural linewidth](natural-linewidth.md): an [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md) can act as an extraordinarily selective [energy](energy-split.md) filter. The [Mössbauer effect](mossbauer-effect-split.md) supplies a narrow shared [emission line](emission-line.md) and [absorption line](absorption-line.md) even when [nuclear recoil](nuclear-recoil.md) would separate the corresponding free-nucleus lines. A slow mechanical motion can then tune their overlap through the [Doppler effect](doppler-effect.md). The [Pound-Rebka experiment](pound-rebka-experiment.md) is an application to [general relativity](general-relativity-split.md); the discovery experiment below concerns the recoil-free process itself.
