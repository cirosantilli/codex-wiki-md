# Phase problem

↑ **Parent:** [X-ray crystallographic structure inference](x-ray-crystallographic-structure-inference.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_problem)

The [phase problem](phase-problem.md) is the loss of the [wave phases](phase-waves.md) of the [crystal basis structure factors](crystal-basis-structure-factor.md) when an experiment records their squared magnitudes. Write $F_{hkl}=|F_{hkl}|e^{i\phi_{hkl}}$. A [Fourier series](fourier-series-split.md) reconstruction of a periodic [electron density](electron-density.md) uses both $|F_{hkl}|$ and $\phi_{hkl}$, whereas an ordinary [wave intensity](intensity-physics.md) measurement supplies only $|F_{hkl}|^2$. Estimating the missing phases therefore needs additional constraints or measurements.[Online Dictionary of Crystallography by International Union of Crystallography; Definition: missing structure-factor phases prevent reconstruction of atomic positions; Methods of solution: estimates are tested against observations](online-dictionary-of-crystallography-by-international-union-of-crystallography/phase-problem.md)

There is also an unavoidable choice of origin. Translating all motif positions by the same [fractional crystal coordinate](fractional-crystal-coordinate.md) vector $t$ multiplies $F_{hkl}$ by $e^{2\pi i(h,k,l)\cdot t}$ without changing $|F_{hkl}|^2$. The physical arrangement is unchanged. A reconstructed coordinate list must therefore distinguish a coordinate convention from information actually recovered from the measurements.
