# Cardinality

↑ **Parent:** [Cardinal number](cardinal-number-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinality)

The cardinality $|X|$ of a [set](set-split.md) $X$ expresses its size up to [bijection](bijection.md). Two [sets](set-split.md) have the same cardinality exactly when a [bijection](bijection.md) pairs their members. For a [finite set](finite-set.md), cardinality is the ordinary number of [set elements](element-of-a-set.md). In the presence of the [axiom of choice](axiom-of-choice.md), every [set](set-split.md) can be assigned the least [ordinal](ordinal-split.md) in its [bijection](bijection.md) class as its [cardinal number](cardinal-number-split.md).

An [injective function](injective-function.md) $A\to B$ shows that $A$ is no larger than $B$: different members of $A$ receive different partners in $B$. If there are [injections](injective-function.md) in both directions, the [Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein theorem](cantor-schroder-bernstein-theorem.md) supplies a [bijection](bijection.md). Infinite [cardinality](cardinality.md) records this pairing structure rather than a geometric dimension or density. That is why a line of [natural numbers](natural-number.md) and a grid of natural-number pairs can have the same size, while an interval of [real numbers](real-number.md) has larger [cardinality](cardinality.md).
