<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) adjustment from part (i) can be written

$$
\boxed{\mathbf T=\mathbf T_0(t)+q(\mathbf x,t)\mathbf1,\qquad
q=-\rho\xi+C(t).}
$$

Indeed, since $\operatorname{div}(q\mathbf1)=\nabla q$, the difference of the two [Cauchy momentum equations](../../../../../cauchy-momentum-equation.md) is

$$
\nabla q=\rho\ddot{\mathbf x}_0=-\rho\nabla\xi.
$$

Thus $q+\rho\xi$ is constant in space on each connected component. The function $C(t)$ is fixed by any prescribed [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) or surface [traction](../../../../../traction.md) datum. In terms of the usual compressive [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) multiplier, $p=p_0-q=p_0+\rho\xi-C(t)$. The plus sign in $q\mathbf1$ therefore does not identify $q$ with the compressive multiplier $p$ itself.
