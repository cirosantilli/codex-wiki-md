<h1 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For a twice continuously differentiable [scalar potential](../../../../../scalar-potential.md), mixed second [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) commute. Taking the spatial [curl](../../../../../curl.md) of the equation from part (i) gives

$$
\boxed{\nabla\times\ddot{\mathbf x}
=-\nabla\times\nabla\xi=\mathbf0.}
$$

For example, its first component is $-\partial_2\partial_3\xi+\partial_3\partial_2\xi=0$. The [curl](../../../../../curl.md) acts on the spatial representation of the [material acceleration](../../../../../material-acceleration.md), not directly on its expression in reference coordinates.

This is a necessary local condition. It is sufficient locally, and sufficient globally on a [simply connected domain](../../../../../simply-connected-domain.md) under the usual smoothness assumptions. On a domain with holes, a global [scalar potential](../../../../../scalar-potential.md) additionally requires every closed-loop [line integral](../../../../../line-integral.md) of the [material acceleration](../../../../../material-acceleration.md) to vanish. The homogeneous motion in part (c) admits an explicit global [scalar potential](../../../../../scalar-potential.md), so this topological qualification causes no obstruction there.
