## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Write the [first strain invariant](../continuum-mechanics.md#first-strain-invariant) in components of the [deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient):

$$
I_1=\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf F\mathbf F^T)
=\sum_{i,I}F_{iI}^2.
$$

Here lower-case and upper-case indices refer to the [current configuration](../continuum-mechanics.md#current-configuration) and [reference configuration](../continuum-mechanics.md#reference-configuration), respectively. Differentiating gives $\partial I_1/\partial F_{iI}=2F_{iI}$. The [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) therefore gives

$$
\frac{\partial W}{\partial F_{iI}}=2w'(I_1)F_{iI},
\qquad
\partial_{\mathbf F}W=2w'(I_1)\mathbf F.
$$

Substitution into the given [hyperelastic material](../elasticity-physics.md#hyperelastic-material) law, with its indicated [matrix transpose](../vector-space.md#transpose), yields

$$
\boxed{\mathbf T
=\mathbf F(\partial_{\mathbf F}W)^T-p\mathbf1
=2w'(I_1)\mathbf F\mathbf F^T-p\mathbf1
=2\frac{\partial w}{\partial I_1}\mathbf B-p\mathbf1.}
$$

The [left Cauchy-Green deformation tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#left-cauchy-green-deformation-tensor) is $\mathbf B=\mathbf F\mathbf F^T$. The scalar $p$ is the [Lagrange multiplier](../mathematical-optimization.md#lagrange-multiplier) enforcing [incompressibility](../fluid-mechanics.md#incompressible-flow), rather than a function fixed by the [strain energy density](../elasticity-physics.md#strain-energy-density) alone. The [identity matrix](../vector-space.md#identity-matrix) $\mathbf1$ makes this an isotropic [constraint reaction in hyperelastic stress](../elasticity-physics.md#constraint-reaction-in-hyperelastic-stress).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Use separate moving [orthonormal bases](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-basis) in the reference and current [cylindrical coordinate systems](../multivariable-calculus.md#cylindrical-coordinate-system). For [pure torsion of an incompressible cylinder](../elasticity-physics.md#pure-torsion-of-an-incompressible-cylinder), the [deformation map](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-map) is

$$
\mathbf x(R,\Theta,Z)=R\mathbf e_r(\Theta+\tau Z)+Z\mathbf e_z.
$$

The [unit vectors](../vector-space.md#unit-vector) depend on the angle: $\partial_\theta\mathbf e_r=\mathbf e_\theta$ and $\partial_\theta\mathbf e_\theta=-\mathbf e_r$. Consequently

$$
\partial_R\mathbf x=\mathbf e_r,\qquad
\partial_\Theta\mathbf x=R\mathbf e_\theta,\qquad
\partial_Z\mathbf x=\tau R\mathbf e_\theta+\mathbf e_z.
$$

In particular, the last derivative includes the rotation of $\mathbf e_r$ along the cylinder. The material [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) has the reference coordinate scale factor $1/R$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf F
&=(\partial_R\mathbf x)\otimes\mathbf E_R
+\frac1R(\partial_\Theta\mathbf x)\otimes\mathbf E_\Theta
+(\partial_Z\mathbf x)\otimes\mathbf E_Z\\
&=\mathbf e_r\otimes\mathbf E_R
+\mathbf e_\theta\otimes\mathbf E_\Theta
+(\tau r\mathbf e_\theta+\mathbf e_z)\otimes\mathbf E_Z.
\end{aligned}
$$

The [outer products](../vector-space.md#outer-product) specify the images of the three reference [unit vectors](../vector-space.md#unit-vector). Thus, with current basis vectors indexing rows and reference basis vectors indexing columns,

$$
\boxed{\mathbf F=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&\tau r\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Its [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) is one, checking [incompressibility](../fluid-mechanics.md#incompressible-flow). The radius is unchanged, so the current outer radius in part (d) is $a=A$. The formula at the coordinate axis is understood by continuity in [Cartesian coordinates](../linear-algebra.md#cartesian-coordinate-system).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Multiplying the [deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient) by its [matrix transpose](../vector-space.md#transpose) gives the [left Cauchy-Green deformation tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#left-cauchy-green-deformation-tensor) and its [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace):

$$
\mathbf B=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&1+\tau^2r^2&\tau r\\
0&\tau r&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad I_1=3+\tau^2r^2.
$$

The derivative of the [strain energy density](../elasticity-physics.md#strain-energy-density) is

$$
\hat w'(I_1)=\mu_1+\mu_2 I_1^n.
$$

For brevity put $g(r)=\mu_1+\mu_2(3+\tau^2r^2)^n$. Part (a) gives the [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor), in the current [cylindrical coordinate system](../multivariable-calculus.md#cylindrical-coordinate-system) [orthonormal basis](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-basis), as

$$
\boxed{\mathbf T=\begin{pmatrix}
2g-p&0&0\\
0&2g(1+\tau^2r^2)-p&2g\tau r\\
0&2g\tau r&2g-p
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

In particular, the end-face [shear stress](../viscous-fluid-flow.md#shear-stress) is $T_{\theta z}=2g\tau r$. The undetermined [pressure](../pressure.md) $p$ affects the normal [stresses](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) but does not affect this [shear stress](../viscous-fluid-flow.md#shear-stress). No normal [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) conditions have been specified to determine $p$.

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/d/i)

Using $a=A$ and the end-face [shear stress](../viscous-fluid-flow.md#shear-stress) from part (c), the printed [torque](../momentum.md#torque) normalization gives

$$
M(\tau)=2\tau\int_0^A\left[\mu_1+\mu_2(3+\tau^2r^2)^n\right]r^3\,dr.
$$

The [binomial theorem](../binomial-coefficient.md#binomial-theorem) expands the remaining [integral](../integral.md) into powers of $r$:

$$
(3+\tau^2r^2)^n
=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk3^{n-k}\tau^{2k}r^{2k}.
$$

Integrating each term gives an expression valid also at $\tau=0$:

$$
\boxed{M(\tau)=\frac{\mu_1A^4}{2}\tau
+\mu_2\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk
\frac{3^{n-k}A^{2k+4}}{k+2}\tau^{2k+1}.}
$$

For example, $n=1$ gives $M=(\mu_1+3\mu_2)A^4\tau/2+\mu_2A^6\tau^3/3$. The [torque](../momentum.md#torque) is an odd [polynomial](../polynomial.md) in the twist per unit reference length, as expected when reversing the twist reverses the end-face [shear stress](../viscous-fluid-flow.md#shear-stress).

For a physical end-face [torque](../momentum.md#torque) $N$, integration over the azimuth normally gives

$$
N=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^A T_{\theta z}r^2\,dr\,d\theta=2\pi M.
$$

The original PDF explicitly defines $M$ without this $2\pi$, so the boxed answer retains its convention. Since $\mathbf T$ is the [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor), its shear component here is spatial $T_{\theta z}$; the printed capital $Z$ in $T_{\theta Z}$ does not require a change to a [nominal stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#nominal-stress-tensor).

<h4 id="1/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/d/ii)

Only the $k=0$ term of the [polynomial](../polynomial.md) contributes to the limit:

$$
\frac{M(\tau)}\tau
=\frac{A^4}{2}(\mu_1+3^n\mu_2)+O(\tau^2).
$$

Thus the requested [torsional stiffness per unit twist](../elasticity-physics.md#torsional-stiffness-per-unit-twist) is

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{A^4}{2}(\mu_1+3^n\mu_2).}
$$

The small-[strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) [shear modulus](../elasticity-physics.md#shear-modulus) is $G=2\hat w'(3)=2(\mu_1+3^n\mu_2)$. Therefore $\alpha=GA^4/4$, consistently with the usual circular-shaft [torque](../momentum.md#torque) relation after restoring the $2\pi$ factor. If the prescribed end rotation is the [angle](../geometry-and-topology.md#angle) $\varphi=\tau L$, the physical [torsional stiffness](../elasticity-physics.md#torsional-stiffness) is $dN/d\varphi=2\pi\alpha/L$ at zero twist. This also explains why $L$ does not enter the requested $\alpha$.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

The [radial unit vector in spherical coordinates](../multivariable-calculus.md#radial-unit-vector-in-spherical-coordinates) is $\mathbf e_R=\mathbf X/R$, so $\mathbf X\otimes\mathbf X/R^2=\mathbf e_R\otimes\mathbf e_R$. The [identity matrix](../vector-space.md#identity-matrix) is the sum of the three basis [outer products](../vector-space.md#outer-product). Substitution in the given [deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient) gives

$$
\mathbf F=f'(R)\mathbf e_R\otimes\mathbf e_R
+\frac{f(R)}R\left(\mathbf e_\Theta\otimes\mathbf e_\Theta
+\mathbf e_\Phi\otimes\mathbf e_\Phi\right)
=\operatorname{diag}\left(f'(R),\frac{f(R)}R,\frac{f(R)}R\right).
$$

The reference and current radial directions coincide for this [spherically symmetric deformation](../continuum-mechanics.md#spherically-symmetric-deformation). The positive [principal stretches](../continuum-mechanics.md#principal-stretch) are therefore $\lambda_r=f'(R)$ and $\lambda_\theta=\lambda_\phi=f(R)/R$. [Incompressibility](../fluid-mechanics.md#incompressible-flow) imposes

$$
f'(R)\frac{f(R)^2}{R^2}=1,
\qquad \frac{d}{dR}f(R)^3=3R^2.
$$

Integrating and using $f(A)=a$ gives

$$
\boxed{r=f(R)=\left(R^3+a^3-A^3\right)^{1/3},\qquad
b^3=B^3+a^3-A^3.}
$$

In particular $b^3-a^3=B^3-A^3$, expressing conservation of shell [volume](../geometry-and-topology.md#volume). Writing $\lambda=r/R$ also gives $\lambda_r=\lambda^{-2}$ and $\lambda_\theta=\lambda_\phi=\lambda$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

The [Cauchy momentum equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-momentum-equation) in [static elastic equilibrium](../elasticity-physics.md#static-elastic-equilibrium), with zero [body force](../fluid-mechanics.md#body-force), is $\operatorname{div}\mathbf T=\mathbf0$. The [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor) is symmetric. [Material isotropy](../elasticity-physics.md#material-isotropy) and the equal tangential [principal stretches](../continuum-mechanics.md#principal-stretch) imply $t_\theta=t_\phi$, so its radial equation is

$$
\boxed{\frac{dt_r}{dr}+\frac2r(t_r-t_\theta)=0,\qquad
\text{equivalently}\quad
\frac{dt_r}{dr}=\frac2r(t_\theta-t_r).}
$$

All [stresses](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) and spatial derivatives in this equation are measured in the [current configuration](../continuum-mechanics.md#current-configuration).

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Let $W_r,W_\theta,W_\phi$ denote derivatives of the [strain energy density](../elasticity-physics.md#strain-energy-density) with respect to its three [principal stretches](../continuum-mechanics.md#principal-stretch). The [principal stress formula for incompressible hyperelasticity](../elasticity-physics.md#principal-stress-formula-for-incompressible-hyperelasticity) is

$$
t_r=\lambda_rW_r-p,\qquad
t_\theta=\lambda_\theta W_\theta-p,\qquad
t_\phi=\lambda_\phi W_\phi-p.
$$

It follows by applying $\mathbf T=\mathbf F(\partial_{\mathbf F}W)^T-p\mathbf1$ in principal axes. Equal tangential [principal stretches](../continuum-mechanics.md#principal-stretch) and [material isotropy](../elasticity-physics.md#material-isotropy) give $W_\theta=W_\phi$ at $(\lambda^{-2},\lambda,\lambda)$. Differentiating the reduced [strain energy density](../elasticity-physics.md#strain-energy-density) by the [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
h'(\lambda)&=-2\lambda^{-3}W_r+W_\theta+W_\phi\\
&=2\left(W_\theta-\lambda^{-3}W_r\right)
=\frac2\lambda(t_\theta-t_r).
\end{aligned}
$$

The unknown [pressure](../pressure.md) cancels in this difference. Substituting in part (i), and then using the supplied derivative of $\lambda$, gives

$$
\frac{dt_r}{dr}=\frac{\lambda h'(\lambda)}r=\frac{h'(\lambda)}R,
\qquad
\frac{d\lambda}{dr}=\frac{1-\lambda^3}{R}.
$$

Consequently the desired [ordinary differential equation](../differential-equation.md#ordinary-differential-equation) is

$$
\boxed{\frac{dt_r}{d\lambda}=\frac{h'(\lambda)}{1-\lambda^3}.}
$$

For completeness, the supplied derivative follows from $R^3=r^3-(a^3-A^3)$: $dR/dr=r^2/R^2=\lambda^2$, and differentiating $\lambda=r/R$ gives $(1-\lambda^3)/R$. At the undeformed value $\lambda=1$ the division must be interpreted by a limit; the [spherically symmetric deformation](../continuum-mechanics.md#spherically-symmetric-deformation) with $a=A$ has $\lambda\equiv1$ and should be handled directly by the radial equilibrium equation.

<h4 id="2/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/b/iii)

Take the inner surface to be free of [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction), as appropriate to an empty cavity with no [surface tension](../surface-tension.md). At the outer surface the imposed [pressure](../pressure.md) is compressive when positive. The outward [normal vectors](../differential-geometry.md#normal-vector) are $-\mathbf e_r$ at $r=a$ and $+\mathbf e_r$ at $r=b$, giving

$$
t_r(a)=0,\qquad t_r(b)=-P.
$$

Integrating the [ordinary differential equation](../differential-equation.md#ordinary-differential-equation) from part (ii) between the two surfaces gives

$$
-P=\int_{\lambda_a}^{\lambda_b}\frac{h'(\lambda)}{1-\lambda^3}\,d\lambda.
$$

The outer stretch follows from [incompressibility](../fluid-mechanics.md#incompressible-flow):

$$
\lambda_b^3=\frac{b^3}{B^3}
=1+\frac{A^3}{B^3}(\lambda_a^3-1).
$$

Hence an expression entirely in terms of the inner stretch and the reference radii is

$$
\boxed{P(\lambda_a)=
\int_{\lambda_a}^{[1+(A/B)^3(\lambda_a^3-1)]^{1/3}}
\frac{h'(\lambda)}{\lambda^3-1}\,d\lambda.}
$$

Equivalently $P=-\int_{\lambda_b}^{\lambda_a}h'(\lambda)/(\lambda^3-1)\,d\lambda$. For an expanding shell, $1<\lambda_b<\lambda_a$; when $h'>0$ on this range, $P<0$, correctly describing an outward tensile loading. If an inner [pressure](../pressure.md) $P_i$ were present, the same derivation would give $P-P_i$ on the left. Thus the empty-cavity condition specifies the pressure reference needed for the requested answer.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

First let $A\downarrow0$ at fixed positive cavity radius $a$ and fixed reference outer radius $B$. Then

$$
\lambda_a=\frac aA\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
\lambda_b\longrightarrow\left(1+\frac{a^3}{B^3}\right)^{1/3},
$$

and the [pressure](../pressure.md) sustaining a finite cavity is

$$
P(a)=-\int_{(1+a^3/B^3)^{1/3}}^\infty
\frac{h'(\lambda)}{\lambda^3-1}\,d\lambda.
$$

**The onset of cavitation is the additional limit $a\downarrow0$.** The outer stretch then tends to one, and the signed critical [pressure](../pressure.md) is

$$
\boxed{P_{\mathrm{crit}}=-\int_1^\infty
\frac{h'(\lambda)}{\lambda^3-1}\,d\lambda.}
$$

When this [improper integral](../real-analysis.md#improper-integral) is positive and finite before the minus sign, the tensile loading magnitude is $-P_{\mathrm{crit}}>0$. Thus the cavity opens under tension, even though the load variable is named [pressure](../pressure.md). The critical value is independent of the original sphere radius $B$.

For $A=0$ and $a>0$, the [deformation map](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-map) is smooth away from $R=0$ but singular at the original centre: $r=(R^3+a^3)^{1/3}$ approaches $a$, rather than zero, as $R\downarrow0$. This is the [elastic cavitation](../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-cavitation) mechanism; it cannot be represented by a smooth [deformation map](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-map) through the centre.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

<h5 id="2/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/d/i)

The [neo-Hookean solid](../elasticity-physics.md#neo-hookean-solid) corresponds to $\alpha=1$, with positive [shear modulus](../elasticity-physics.md#shear-modulus) $\mu$. Substitution of the [principal stretches](../continuum-mechanics.md#principal-stretch) gives

$$
h(\lambda)=\frac\mu2(\lambda^{-4}+2\lambda^2-3),\qquad
h'(\lambda)=2\mu(\lambda-\lambda^{-5}).
$$

The identity $\lambda^6-1=(\lambda^3-1)(\lambda^3+1)$ simplifies the integrand:

$$
\frac{h'(\lambda)}{\lambda^3-1}
=2\mu\left(\lambda^{-2}+\lambda^{-5}\right).
$$

The two [improper integrals](../real-analysis.md#improper-integral) are elementary:

$$
\boxed{P_{\mathrm{crit}}
=-2\mu\left(\int_1^\infty\lambda^{-2}\,d\lambda
+\int_1^\infty\lambda^{-5}\,d\lambda\right)
=-2\mu\left(1+\frac14\right)=-\frac52\mu.}
$$

Thus the critical tensile [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) magnitude is $5\mu/2$. As a check on the finite-sphere limit, part (c) gives

$$
P(a)=-2\mu\left(\lambda_b^{-1}+\frac14\lambda_b^{-4}\right),\qquad
\lambda_b=(1+a^3/B^3)^{1/3},
$$

which tends to $-5\mu/2$ as the cavity radius tends to zero.

<h4 id="2/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

<h5 id="2/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/d/ii)

For general $\alpha>0$, put $S(\lambda)=\lambda^{-4}+2\lambda^2$. The reduced [strain energy density](../elasticity-physics.md#strain-energy-density) and its derivative are

$$
h(\lambda)=\frac\mu2\left[S(\lambda)^\alpha-3^\alpha\right],\qquad
h'(\lambda)=2\mu\alpha S(\lambda)^{\alpha-1}(\lambda-\lambda^{-5}).
$$

The same factorization as in part (i) yields

$$
\frac{h'(\lambda)}{\lambda^3-1}
=2\mu\alpha S(\lambda)^{\alpha-1}
\left(\lambda^{-2}+\lambda^{-5}\right).
$$

This form removes the apparent singularity at $\lambda=1$: its limit is $4\mu\alpha3^{\alpha-1}$, finite for every fixed $\alpha>0$. Thus only the upper endpoint can obstruct convergence of the [improper integral](../real-analysis.md#improper-integral).

As $\lambda\to\infty$, $S(\lambda)\sim2\lambda^2$, giving

$$
\frac{h'(\lambda)}{\lambda^3-1}
\sim\mu\alpha2^\alpha\lambda^{2\alpha-4}.
$$

For $\mu>0$, the [comparison test](../sequence-and-series.md#direct-comparison-test) with a power [integral](../integral.md) implies convergence exactly when $2\alpha-4<-1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{0<\alpha<\frac32}
$$

is precisely the range with finite critical [pressure](../pressure.md), the [cavitation criterion for power-law strain energy](../elasticity-physics.md#cavitation-criterion-for-power-law-strain-energy). At $\alpha=3/2$ the divergence is logarithmic; for larger $\alpha$ it is a power divergence. These materials require an unbounded tensile loading magnitude in this ideal model. The qualification $\mu>0$ excludes the degenerate zero-energy material.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

The first equation is the local [balance of linear momentum](../continuum-mechanics.md#balance-of-linear-momentum). Use $\mathsf M,\mathsf L,\mathsf T$ for the dimensions of [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass), [length](../physics.md#length), and [time](../classical-mechanics.md#time-in-physics), to distinguish dimensional time from the [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor) $\mathbf T$.

- $\mathbf x(\mathbf X,t)$ is the current position of the particle labelled by reference position $\mathbf X$; both have dimension $\mathsf L$. The [deformation map](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-map) specifies the particle's trajectory.
- $\ddot{\mathbf x}=\partial_t^2\mathbf x|_{\mathbf X}$ is its [material acceleration](../continuum-mechanics.md#material-acceleration), with dimension $\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2}$. The dots follow the particle and are not spatially fixed time derivatives.
- $\rho$ is current [mass density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density), with dimension $\mathsf M\mathsf L^{-3}$. For a homogeneous material with [incompressibility](../fluid-mechanics.md#incompressible-flow), [mass conservation](../continuum-mechanics.md#mass-conservation) makes it constant in space and time.
- $\mathbf b$ is [body force](../fluid-mechanics.md#body-force) per unit [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass), such as gravitational [acceleration](../classical-mechanics.md#acceleration), with dimension $\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2}$. Hence $\rho\mathbf b$ is [force](../force.md) per unit [volume](../geometry-and-topology.md#volume).
- $\mathbf T$ is the [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor), mapping a unit [normal vector](../differential-geometry.md#normal-vector) $\mathbf n$ to surface [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) $\mathbf T\mathbf n$. Each component has dimension $\mathsf M\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf T^{-2}$, or [force](../force.md) per unit [area](../differential-geometry.md#surface-area).
- $\operatorname{div}$ differentiates in the [current configuration](../continuum-mechanics.md#current-configuration), with dimension $\mathsf L^{-1}$. In [Cartesian coordinates](../linear-algebra.md#cartesian-coordinate-system), $(\operatorname{div}\mathbf T)_i=\partial T_{ij}/\partial x_j$. Thus $\operatorname{div}\mathbf T$, $\rho\mathbf b$, and $\rho\ddot{\mathbf x}$ all have dimension $\mathsf M\mathsf L^{-2}\mathsf T^{-2}$.
- The superscript $T$ denotes the dimension-preserving [matrix transpose](../vector-space.md#transpose). The relation $\mathbf T=\mathbf T^T$ expresses [angular momentum conservation](../momentum.md#conservation-of-angular-momentum) in a continuum without body couples or couple [stresses](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress).

Here $t$ has dimension $\mathsf T$. The hypothesis $\partial_t\mathbf b=\mathbf0$ means that the prescribed spatial [body force](../fluid-mechanics.md#body-force) field is the same for every member of the static family and the resulting motion.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

Restore the particle label suppressed in the question, writing the static family as $\mathbf x_0(\mathbf X;\tau)$. At each fixed $\tau$, its [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor) satisfies

$$
\operatorname{div}\mathbf T_0(\tau)+\rho\mathbf b=\mathbf0.
$$

This [dynamic continuation of a static incompressible elastic deformation](../elasticity-physics.md#dynamic-continuation-of-a-static-incompressible-elastic-deformation) sets $\tau=t$ to form a motion. At every time its [deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient) is the same as that of the corresponding static member. For an elastic material the elastic part of the [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor) depends on that [deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient), while [incompressibility](../fluid-mechanics.md#incompressible-flow) leaves an arbitrary scalar [constraint reaction in hyperelastic stress](../elasticity-physics.md#constraint-reaction-in-hyperelastic-stress). Therefore write

$$
\mathbf T_0=\mathbf S(\mathbf F)-p_0\mathbf1,\qquad
\mathbf T=\mathbf S(\mathbf F)-p\mathbf1,
\qquad
\mathbf T-\mathbf T_0=(p_0-p)\mathbf1.
$$

Here $\mathbf1$ is the [identity matrix](../vector-space.md#identity-matrix). Subtracting static [balance of linear momentum](../continuum-mechanics.md#balance-of-linear-momentum) from dynamic [balance of linear momentum](../continuum-mechanics.md#balance-of-linear-momentum) gives

$$
\rho\ddot{\mathbf x}_0=\nabla(p_0-p).
$$

Since the homogeneous incompressible material has constant [mass density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density), define $\xi=(p-p_0)/\rho$. Necessarily

$$
\boxed{\ddot{\mathbf x}_0(\mathbf X,t)
=-\nabla\xi(\mathbf x_0(\mathbf X,t),t).}
$$

Conversely, suppose this [acceleration potential](../continuum-mechanics.md#acceleration-potential) exists. Choose $p=p_0+\rho\xi$. Then

$$
\operatorname{div}\mathbf T+\rho\mathbf b
=\operatorname{div}\mathbf T_0+\rho\mathbf b-\rho\nabla\xi
=\rho\ddot{\mathbf x}_0.
$$

The added isotropic [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) is symmetric, so [angular momentum conservation](../momentum.md#conservation-of-angular-momentum) remains satisfied. This proves both directions for the field equations and the elastic [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation). The surface [tractions](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) for the motion are those of the adjusted [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress); identical prescribed static [tractions](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) would impose an additional boundary restriction on $\xi$.

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

The [pressure](../pressure.md) adjustment from part (i) can be written

$$
\boxed{\mathbf T=\mathbf T_0(t)+q(\mathbf x,t)\mathbf1,\qquad
q=-\rho\xi+C(t).}
$$

Indeed, since $\operatorname{div}(q\mathbf1)=\nabla q$, the difference of the two [Cauchy momentum equations](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-momentum-equation) is

$$
\nabla q=\rho\ddot{\mathbf x}_0=-\rho\nabla\xi.
$$

Thus $q+\rho\xi$ is constant in space on each connected component. The function $C(t)$ is fixed by any prescribed [pressure](../pressure.md) or surface [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) datum. In terms of the usual compressive [pressure](../pressure.md) multiplier, $p=p_0-q=p_0+\rho\xi-C(t)$. The plus sign in $q\mathbf1$ therefore does not identify $q$ with the compressive multiplier $p$ itself.

<h4 id="3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/b/iii)

For a twice continuously differentiable [scalar potential](../classical-field-theory.md#scalar-potential), mixed second [partial derivatives](../multivariable-calculus.md#partial-derivative) commute. Taking the spatial [curl](../multivariable-calculus.md#curl) of the equation from part (i) gives

$$
\boxed{\nabla\times\ddot{\mathbf x}
=-\nabla\times\nabla\xi=\mathbf0.}
$$

For example, its first component is $-\partial_2\partial_3\xi+\partial_3\partial_2\xi=0$. The [curl](../multivariable-calculus.md#curl) acts on the spatial representation of the [material acceleration](../continuum-mechanics.md#material-acceleration), not directly on its expression in reference coordinates.

This is a necessary local condition. It is sufficient locally, and sufficient globally on a [simply connected domain](../complex-analysis.md#simply-connected-domain) under the usual smoothness assumptions. On a domain with holes, a global [scalar potential](../classical-field-theory.md#scalar-potential) additionally requires every closed-loop [line integral](../multivariable-calculus.md#line-integral) of the [material acceleration](../continuum-mechanics.md#material-acceleration) to vanish. The homogeneous motion in part (c) admits an explicit global [scalar potential](../classical-field-theory.md#scalar-potential), so this topological qualification causes no obstruction there.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/c/i)

Take two time derivatives at fixed reference position $\mathbf X$:

$$
\ddot{\mathbf x}=\ddot{\mathbf F}\mathbf X+\ddot{\mathbf c}.
$$

The constraint $\det\mathbf F=1$ ensures that the [deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient) is invertible, so $\mathbf X=\mathbf F^{-1}(\mathbf x-\mathbf c)$. Thus the spatial [material acceleration](../continuum-mechanics.md#material-acceleration) is the affine [vector field](../multivariable-calculus.md#vector-field)

$$
\ddot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf A(t)(\mathbf x-\mathbf c(t))+\ddot{\mathbf c}(t),
\qquad \mathbf A=\ddot{\mathbf F}\mathbf F^{-1}.
$$

Its spatial [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) is $\mathbf A$, and its [curl](../multivariable-calculus.md#curl) is

$$
\nabla\times\ddot{\mathbf x}
=(A_{32}-A_{23},\ A_{13}-A_{31},\ A_{21}-A_{12}).
$$

Part (b)(iii) therefore requires

$$
\boxed{\ddot{\mathbf F}\mathbf F^{-1}
=\left(\ddot{\mathbf F}\mathbf F^{-1}\right)^T.}
$$

Equivalently, multiplying on the left by $\mathbf F^T$ and on the right by $\mathbf F$ gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf F^T\ddot{\mathbf F}=\ddot{\mathbf F}^{\,T}\mathbf F.}
$$

The translation $\mathbf c(t)$ contributes no spatial [curl](../multivariable-calculus.md#curl) and is unrestricted by this condition. A useful equivalent first integral is

$$
\frac d{dt}\left(\mathbf F^T\dot{\mathbf F}-\dot{\mathbf F}^{\,T}\mathbf F\right)=\mathbf0:
$$

the antisymmetric [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) in parentheses must be constant, and need not vanish. The separate constraint $\det\mathbf F=1$ is still required; differentiating it also gives $\operatorname{tr}(\dot{\mathbf F}\mathbf F^{-1})=0$.

The condition is understood in the setting of part (b), where a static family already exists under the prescribed [body force](../fluid-mechanics.md#body-force). For a homogeneous elastic [deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient), the elastic [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) $\mathbf S(\mathbf F)$ is spatially constant. Static [balance of linear momentum](../continuum-mechanics.md#balance-of-linear-momentum) consequently gives $\nabla p_0=\rho\mathbf b$, ensuring that $\mathbf b$ has a [scalar potential](../classical-field-theory.md#scalar-potential). If the static-family assumption were dropped and an arbitrary time-independent [body force](../fluid-mechanics.md#body-force) allowed, the general necessary condition would instead be $\nabla\times\ddot{\mathbf x}=\nabla\times\mathbf b$.

<h4 id="3/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/c/ii)

For this [homogeneous motion](../continuum-mechanics.md#homogeneous-elastic-deformation), suppose the [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) $\mathbf A=\ddot{\mathbf F}\mathbf F^{-1}$ is symmetric. Put $\mathbf y=\mathbf x-\mathbf c(t)$. The [acceleration potential of a homogeneous motion](../continuum-mechanics.md#acceleration-potential-of-a-homogeneous-motion) is

$$
\boxed{\xi(\mathbf x,t)
=-\frac12\mathbf y^T\mathbf A(t)\mathbf y
-\ddot{\mathbf c}(t)\cdot\mathbf y+g(t),}
$$

where $g(t)$ is arbitrary. When taking the spatial [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient), $t$, $\mathbf c$, $\ddot{\mathbf c}$, and $\mathbf A$ are fixed. Symmetry gives

$$
\nabla\xi=-\frac12(\mathbf A+\mathbf A^T)\mathbf y-\ddot{\mathbf c}
=-\mathbf A\mathbf y-\ddot{\mathbf c}
=-\ddot{\mathbf x}.
$$

This verifies the potential directly and also proves sufficiency of the [symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-matrix) condition within the static-family setting. Its dimension is $\mathsf L^2\mathsf T^{-2}$, so the [pressure](../pressure.md) correction $-\rho\xi$ has the dimension of [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress). Combining with part (b)(ii), the corresponding dynamic [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor) is

$$
\mathbf T=\mathbf T_0(t)+
\left[\frac\rho2\mathbf y^T\mathbf A\mathbf y
+\rho\ddot{\mathbf c}\cdot\mathbf y+\widetilde C(t)\right]\mathbf1,
$$

where $\widetilde C(t)=C(t)-\rho g(t)$. Substituting its [divergence](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence) into the [Cauchy momentum equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-momentum-equation) reproduces $\rho[\mathbf A\mathbf y+\ddot{\mathbf c}]$, as required.
