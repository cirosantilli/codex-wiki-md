<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The radial gradient $F=D\mathbf y$ is symmetric positive definite. If it belongs to a well $SO(3)U_i$, write $F=QU_i$. Then $F^TF=U_i^2$, while positive symmetry makes $F$ itself the unique [positive square root of an operator](../../../../positive-square-root-of-an-operator.md) of $F^TF$. Hence $F=U_i$. Likewise a [symmetric positive-definite matrix](../../../../symmetric-positive-definite-matrix.md) in $SO(3)$ must be $I$. Therefore

$$
D\mathbf y(\mathbf x)\in\{I,U_1,\ldots,U_N\}.
$$

For each radius, the tangential stretch $r(R)/R$ must belong to the finite set of eigenvalues of these matrices. The ratio is [continuous](../../../../continuous-function.md) on the [connected](../../../../connected-space.md) radial interval, so it is one constant $v>0$. Thus $r(R)=vR$ and differentiation gives $r'=v$ as well. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathbf y(\mathbf x)=v\mathbf x.}
$$

This proof uses finiteness and positive definiteness of the wells, not isotropy or the [Baker-Ericksen inequalities](../../../../baker-ericksen-inequalities.md). It also covers $R_1=0$: the punctured three-dimensional ball remains [connected](../../../../connected-space.md), and no condition at the excluded center was used.
