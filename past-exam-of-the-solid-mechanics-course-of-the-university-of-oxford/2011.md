<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

A [homogeneous hyperelastic material](../elasticity-physics.md#homogeneous-hyperelastic-material) has a [strain energy density](../elasticity-physics.md#strain-energy-density) per unit reference [volume](../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) $W(F)$ depending on the [deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient), with no explicit dependence on the [reference configuration](../continuum-mechanics.md#reference-configuration). For orientation-preserving gradients $F\in GL^+(3)$, its first [Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#piola-kirchhoff-stress-tensors) is the [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative):

$$
\boxed{\widehat T(F)=D_FW(F),\qquad T_{iA}=\frac{\partial W}{\partial F_{iA}}(F).}
$$

Equivalently the infinitesimal stored-energy change is $DW(F)[H]=T:H$. Reference and spatial indices are distinguished here by upper- and lowercase index letters.

The [material symmetry group](../elasticity-physics.md#material-symmetry-group) may be written $G_W=\{H\in GL^+(3):W(FH)=W(F)\text{ for every }F\in GL^+(3)\}$. A volume-preserving relabelling convention restricts the candidate $H$ to $SL(3)$; the [material isotropy](../elasticity-physics.md#material-isotropy) criterion is the same. The material has **[material isotropy](../elasticity-physics.md#material-isotropy)** when $SO(3)\subseteq G_W$, meaning $W(FQ)=W(F)$ for every proper [rotation](../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) $Q$. If only rotational [symmetries](../physics.md#symmetry-physics) are included in the definition of the group, [material isotropy](../elasticity-physics.md#material-isotropy) means that group equals $SO(3)$. This right-rotation invariance is distinct from [material frame indifference](../continuum-mechanics.md#material-frame-indifference), the left-rotation invariance $W(QF)=W(F)$.

The [polar decomposition in continuum mechanics](../continuum-mechanics.md#polar-decomposition-in-continuum-mechanics) gives the unique factorizations

$$
\boxed{F=RU=VR,\qquad R\in SO(3),\quad U=(F^TF)^{1/2},\quad V=(FF^T)^{1/2},}
$$

where $U,V$ are [positive-definite symmetric matrices](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-positive-definite-matrix). Their common positive [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3$ are the **[principal stretches](../continuum-mechanics.md#principal-stretch)**, equivalently the [singular values](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) of $F$. They describe the local stretch magnitudes after separating out a rigid [rotation](../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Define the invariants by the [characteristic polynomial](../linear-operator-theory.md#characteristic-polynomial)

$$
\det(tI-B)=t^3-I_Bt^2+II_Bt-III_B.
$$

Because $B=FF^T$ has positive [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $\lambda_j^2$, its invariants are the [elementary symmetric functions](../polynomial.md#elementary-symmetric-polynomial) in those [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue):

$$
I_B=\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2+\lambda_3^2,\qquad
II_B=\lambda_1^2\lambda_2^2+\lambda_1^2\lambda_3^2+\lambda_2^2\lambda_3^2,\qquad
III_B=\lambda_1^2\lambda_2^2\lambda_3^2.
$$

The [Frobenius norm](../compact-operator.md#frobenius-norm) gives $|F|^2=\operatorname{tr}(F^TF)=\sum_j\lambda_j^2$. Write $J=\det F>0$. Since $\operatorname{cof}F=JF^{-T}$, its [singular values](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) are $J/\lambda_j$, so

$$
|\operatorname{cof}F|^2=J^2\sum_j\lambda_j^{-2}
=\lambda_2^2\lambda_3^2+\lambda_1^2\lambda_3^2+\lambda_1^2\lambda_2^2.
$$

Finally $\det B=\det F\det F^T=J^2$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{I_B=|F|^2,\qquad II_B=|\operatorname{cof}F|^2,\qquad III_B=(\det F)^2.}
$$

These identities also give $I_B=\operatorname{tr}B$ and $II_B=((\operatorname{tr}B)^2-\operatorname{tr}(B^2))/2$.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Take a [singular value decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-decomposition) $F=Q_1DQ_2^T$, with $D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)$ positive and $Q_1,Q_2\in SO(3)$. Such a choice is possible because $\det F>0$. Left [material frame indifference](../continuum-mechanics.md#material-frame-indifference) removes $Q_1$, and right [material isotropy](../elasticity-physics.md#material-isotropy) removes $Q_2$, giving $W(F)=W(D)$.

Moreover $W(D)$ is unchanged when the positive diagonal entries are permuted. Every [permutation](../combinatorics.md#permutation) can be implemented by a signed [permutation matrix](../vector-space.md#permutation-matrix) of [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) one; the signs disappear in conjugating a [diagonal matrix](../linear-algebra.md#diagonal-matrix). Thus the energy depends only on the unordered [principal stretches](../continuum-mechanics.md#principal-stretch).

The three invariants determine precisely this unordered triple: the roots of $t^3-I_Bt^2+II_Bt-III_B$ are $\lambda_1^2,\lambda_2^2,\lambda_3^2$, and their positive [square roots](../algebra.md#square-root) give the stretches. Consequently, on the set of admissible invariant triples, define $h(I_B,II_B,III_B)$ to be $W(D)$. The preceding argument proves that this is well defined and that

$$
\boxed{W(F)=h(I_B,II_B,III_B).}
$$

Not every positive triple is realizable by three positive roots. Since the requested statement only asserts existence of a [function](../function.md) on $(0,\infty)^3$, extend $h$ arbitrarily on the nonrealizable triples; no choice there affects the formula for a [deformation in continuum mechanics](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-mechanics). Smooth invariant representations, where needed for differentiating the [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation), are understood locally on the realizable set.

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

Retain the [material frame indifference](../continuum-mechanics.md#material-frame-indifference) assumption used in part (c), in addition to [material isotropy](../elasticity-physics.md#material-isotropy). Put $p=u_{,1}$, $q=u_{,2}$ and $s=p^2+q^2$. For this [antiplane shear](../elasticity-physics.md#antiplane-shear),

$$
F=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\p&q&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&p\\0&1&q\\p&q&1+s\end{pmatrix},\qquad J=1.
$$

The invariant identities give $I_B=II_B=3+s$ and $III_B=1$. Hence the restriction of the [strain energy density](../elasticity-physics.md#strain-energy-density) is

$$
\boxed{W(Dy)=w(|Du|^2),\qquad w(s)=h(3+s,3+s,1).}
$$

[Antiplane energy does not determine the full stress](../elasticity-physics.md#antiplane-energy-does-not-determine-the-full-stress): the restriction gives one [scalar](../vector-space.md#scalar) energy along antiplane gradients. It does not determine the full [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) derivative with respect to every entry of $F$.

Indeed, using the [isotropic hyperelastic stress invariant representation](../elasticity-physics.md#isotropic-hyperelastic-stress-invariant-representation) and differentiating the energy, with $h_j$ denoting the corresponding [partial derivatives](../multivariable-calculus.md#partial-derivative), gives

$$
DW=2h_1F+2h_2(I_BI-B)F+2III_Bh_3F^{-T}.
$$

Using $S=J^{-1}TF^T$, the general objective [isotropic](../continuum-mechanics.md#isotropy) [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor) is

$$
\boxed{S=\frac2J\left[h_1B+h_2(I_BB-B^2)+III_Bh_3I\right].}
$$

All [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) are evaluated at the current invariant triple. The extra terms generally remain nonzero on antiplane gradients, so **the printed assertion that the full [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) must be $\beta B$ does not follow**.

A stress-free, objective [isotropic](../continuum-mechanics.md#isotropy) counterexample is the compressible [neo-Hookean solid](../elasticity-physics.md#neo-hookean-solid) $W(F)=\frac\mu2(|F|^2-3)-\mu\log J$, with $\mu>0$. Its derivative is $T=\mu F-\mu F^{-T}$, so on antiplane gradients

$$
S=\mu(B-I).
$$

Take $u(x_1,x_2)=x_1$. Then $B_{22}=1$ but $S_{22}=0$, whereas $B_{13}=1$ and $S_{13}=\mu$. The diagonal entry would force $\beta=0$, contradicting the shear entry. Thus this is not a matter of choosing the right [scalar](../vector-space.md#scalar) coefficient.

For the additional one-invariant constitutive assumption $W(F)=\widehat w(I_B)$, the stronger formula does hold, with

$$
\boxed{\beta(I_B,III_B)=\frac{2\widehat w'(I_B)}{\sqrt{III_B}}.}
$$

This is a valid special case, rather than a consequence of [material isotropy](../elasticity-physics.md#material-isotropy) alone. For the general restricted energy, the shear entries are still fixed: $S_{31}=2w'(s)u_{,1}$ and $S_{32}=2w'(s)u_{,2}$, since $w'=h_1+h_2$ along $(3+s,3+s,1)$. The full [normal stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#normal-stress) needs the constitutive information that the printed claim omits. If [material isotropy](../elasticity-physics.md#material-isotropy) alone is interpreted without [material frame indifference](../continuum-mechanics.md#material-frame-indifference), even the first reduction to $|Du|^2$ needs that additional [material frame indifference](../continuum-mechanics.md#material-frame-indifference) assumption.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

In the classical smooth setting, a [deformation in continuum mechanics](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-mechanics) is a sufficiently regular, [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function) map $y:\Omega\to\mathbb R^3$ with $\det Dy>0$. [Injectivity](../algebra.md#injective-function) excludes material interpenetration, and a positive [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) preserves orientation and local [volume](../geometry-and-topology.md#volume). We use $C^1$ regularity for these kinematics and additional differentiability where stress-divergence identities are applied.

[Cauchy's stress theorem](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-s-stress-theorem) states that the contact [force](../force.md) on an oriented surface element at a current point depends on the point and its [unit normal](../differential-geometry.md#unit-normal), rather than on the shape of the chosen subbody. Thus the contact [force](../force.md) on the [boundary](../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) of a current subbody $v$ has the form $\int_{\partial v}t(z,n)\,da$. Static [force](../force.md) balance, for a [body force](../fluid-mechanics.md#body-force) $b$ per unit current [volume](../geometry-and-topology.md#volume), is

$$
\int_{\partial v}t(z,n)\,da+\int_vb(z)\,dv=0
$$

for every subbody. A shrinking pillbox gives $t(z,-n)=-t(z,n)$; a shrinking tetrahedron then gives linear dependence on the normal, $t(z,n)=S(z)n$. Applying the [divergence theorem](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence-theorem) and using arbitrary subbodies gives

$$
\boxed{t(z,n)=S(z)n,\qquad\operatorname{div}S+b=0.}
$$

If [body force](../fluid-mechanics.md#body-force) is specified per unit mass instead, replace $b$ by $\rho b$. [Symmetry](../physics.md#symmetry-physics) of $S$ follows additionally from [conservation of angular momentum](../momentum.md#conservation-of-angular-momentum) in the absence of body couples; it is not a consequence of [force](../force.md) balance alone.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

The surface element relation is [Nanson's formula](../continuum-mechanics.md#nanson-s-formula). The [force](../force.md) on a corresponding pair of current and reference surface elements must agree:

$$
t\,da=(S\circ y)n\,da=(S\circ y)\operatorname{cof}F\,N\,dA=T N\,dA.
$$

Therefore the first [Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#piola-kirchhoff-stress-tensors) is

$$
\boxed{T=(S\circ y)\operatorname{cof}F=J(S\circ y)F^{-T}.}
$$

It measures [force](../force.md) per reference [area](../differential-geometry.md#surface-area), with a current [force](../force.md) direction and a reference normal direction.

In components, differentiate $T_{iA}=S_{ij}(y(x))(\operatorname{cof}F)_{jA}$. The [Piola identity](../continuum-mechanics.md#piola-identity) says $\partial_A(\operatorname{cof}Dy)_{jA}=0$. One proof writes the [cofactor matrix](../linear-algebra.md#cofactor-matrix) as $\frac12\varepsilon_{jkl}\varepsilon_{ABC}y_{k,B}y_{l,C}$; differentiating in $A$ produces symmetric second [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) contracted against antisymmetric epsilon symbols, hence zero. The [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) consequently gives

$$
\partial_AT_{iA}=\frac{\partial S_{ij}}{\partial y_k}(y)F_{kA}(\operatorname{cof}F)_{jA}
=J\frac{\partial S_{ij}}{\partial y_j}(y),
$$

since $F_{kA}(\operatorname{cof}F)_{jA}=J\delta_{kj}$. Thus, with the coordinate composition made explicit,

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Div}_xT=J(\operatorname{div}_yS)\circ y.}
$$

The distinction between reference [divergence](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence) and current [divergence](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence) is essential to this identity.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

For $R=|x|>0$, $DR=\theta$ and $D\theta=(I-\theta\otimes\theta)/R$. Differentiating the [spherically symmetric deformation](../continuum-mechanics.md#spherically-symmetric-deformation) gives

$$
\boxed{F=r'(R)\theta\otimes\theta+\frac{r(R)}R(I-\theta\otimes\theta).}
$$

For an orientation-preserving [spherically symmetric deformation](../continuum-mechanics.md#spherically-symmetric-deformation) with positive radius, $r>0$ and $r'>0$. The radial direction and its perpendicular plane are [eigenspaces](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) of this positive [symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-matrix), so

$$
\boxed{\lambda_1=r'(R),\qquad\lambda_2=\lambda_3=r(R)/R.}
$$

At a positive [diagonal matrix](../linear-algebra.md#diagonal-matrix) $D$, every diagonal [rotation](../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) with two minus signs commutes with $D$. The supplied equivariance relation therefore forces $\widehat T(D)$ to be a [diagonal matrix](../linear-algebra.md#diagonal-matrix): each off-diagonal entry changes sign under one such [rotation](../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) while $D$ stays fixed. Varying a positive diagonal entry shows that the diagonal entries of $\widehat T(D)$ are $\Phi_{,j}$. [Permutation](../combinatorics.md#permutation) [symmetry](../physics.md#symmetry-physics) of $\Phi$ implies $\Phi_{,2}=\Phi_{,3}$ when $\lambda_2=\lambda_3$.

Choose a [rotation](../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) whose first column is $\theta$. Applying equivariance to $F=QDQ^T$ now gives

$$
\boxed{T=\Phi_{,1}\theta\otimes\theta+\Phi_{,2}(I-\theta\otimes\theta),}
$$

with the [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) evaluated at $(r',r/R,r/R)$. Thus the [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) has one radial and one repeated tangential principal value, independently of the choice of tangent basis.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

The [Green-Lagrange strain tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#green-lagrange-strain-tensor) has [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $(\lambda_j^2-1)/2$. Its [Frobenius norm](../compact-operator.md#frobenius-norm) and trace are invariant under the orthogonal principal-axis change, so

$$
\boxed{W(F)=\frac\mu4\sum_{j=1}^3(\lambda_j^2-1)^2+\frac\lambda8\left(\sum_{j=1}^3\lambda_j^2-3\right)^2.}
$$

For the homogeneous dilation $F=\rho I$, put $K=\mu+3\lambda/2$. Then $E=(\rho^2-1)I/2$. Differentiating the energy through $E$ gives the [second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#second-piola-kirchhoff-stress-tensor) $2\mu E+\lambda\operatorname{tr}(E)I=K(\rho^2-1)I$, and multiplication by $F$ gives the [first Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#first-piola-kirchhoff-stress-tensor). Thus

$$
\boxed{T=\rho K(\rho^2-1)I,\qquad S=J^{-1}TF^T=\frac{K(\rho^2-1)}\rho I.}
$$

The normals of the reference and deformed spheres point in the same radial direction. As $\rho\downarrow0$, the reference [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) magnitude $|T N|$ tends to zero, while the current [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) magnitude $|S n|$ tends to infinity, provided $K>0$, as for physically stable [Lamé parameters](../elasticity-physics.md#lame-parameter). The negative signs of the [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) coefficients for $0<\rho<1$ indicate compression.

There is no contradiction: the current [area](../differential-geometry.md#surface-area) is $\rho^2$ times the reference [area](../differential-geometry.md#surface-area), so the same [force](../force.md) divided by the vanishing current [area](../differential-geometry.md#surface-area) has the larger [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress). Indeed $T N=\rho^2S n$. The limiting map at $\rho=0$ is not itself an admissible [deformation in continuum mechanics](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-mechanics); the statement concerns the approach through positive $\rho$. A degenerate choice $K=0$ would not have the asserted growth without bound.

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

The [displacement field](../elasticity-physics.md#displacement-field-mechanics) is $u(x)=y(x)-x$, and its [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) is $Du=Dy-I$. [Linear elasticity](../elasticity-physics.md#linear-elasticity) expands about an undeformed stress-free reference state, taking $y=x+\epsilon v$ with $|Du|\ll1$ and external loads of the same small order. A rigid translation need not itself be small; it is the [deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient) that controls the [infinitesimal strain tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#infinitesimal-strain-tensor).

For $W\in C^2$ with $DW(I)=0$, a [Taylor series](../calculus.md#taylor-series) gives

$$
W(I+H)=W(I)+\frac12\mathbb A_{iA jB}H_{iA}H_{jB}+o(|H|^2),\qquad
\mathbb A_{iA jB}=\frac{\partial^2W}{\partial F_{iA}\partial F_{jB}}(I).
$$

The constant $W(I)$ can be set to zero. The [tensor](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor) $\mathbb A$ is the [elasticity tensor](../elasticity-physics.md#elasticity-tensor). Commutation of continuous [mixed partial derivatives](../multivariable-calculus.md#mixed-partial-derivative) proves its major [symmetry](../physics.md#symmetry-physics):

$$
\boxed{\mathbb A_{iA jB}=\mathbb A_{jB iA}.}
$$

[Material frame indifference](../continuum-mechanics.md#material-frame-indifference) at a stress-free reference also makes the quadratic energy depend only on the [infinitesimal strain tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#infinitesimal-strain-tensor) $e=(H+H^T)/2$, leading to the usual minor [symmetries](../physics.md#symmetry-physics) after spatial and reference indices are identified.

In this scaling, an external [force](../force.md) of order $\epsilon$ may be evaluated on the reference geometry: its change due to the order-$\epsilon$ displacement contributes only at order $\epsilon^2$. Thus the leading forces are fixed [dead loads](../elasticity-physics.md#dead-loading). Likewise $F=I+O(\epsilon)$, $J=1+O(\epsilon)$ and $S=O(\epsilon)$, so the Piola transformation gives $T=J S F^{-T}=S+O(\epsilon^2)$. Hence **Cauchy and first Piola stresses agree to linear order**. This reasoning uses the usual small-load, stress-free linearization; an order-one prestress or leading follower-load effect requires a different expansion.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Let the [boundary](../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) split into a displacement part $\Gamma_D$ and a [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) part $\Gamma_N$. The linearized stored [elastic energy](../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-energy) functional is

$$
\boxed{I^{\mathrm{lin}}[u]=\frac12\int_\Omega\mathbb A_{iA jB}u_{i,A}u_{j,B}\,dx.}
$$

Its corresponding total potential subtracts the work of fixed body and surface loads:

$$
\Pi[u]=I^{\mathrm{lin}}[u]-\int_\Omega f\cdot u\,dx-\int_{\Gamma_N}g\cdot u\,dA.
$$

The problem in the [calculus of variations](../calculus-of-variations.md) is to minimize $\Pi$ among admissible displacements with $u=u_D$ on $\Gamma_D$. For a stable quadratic [elastic energy](../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-energy) this is the usual energy minimization formulation. Its stationarity condition suffices to derive equilibrium.

Vary by $u+sv$, with $v=0$ on $\Gamma_D$. Major [symmetry](../physics.md#symmetry-physics) combines the two differentiated quadratic terms, giving

$$
\int_\Omega S^{\mathrm{lin}}:Dv\,dx=\int_\Omega f\cdot v\,dx+\int_{\Gamma_N}g\cdot v\,dA,
\qquad S^{\mathrm{lin}}_{iA}=\mathbb A_{iA jB}u_{j,B}.
$$

[Integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts), first with test functions compactly supported in the interior, gives $-\operatorname{Div}S^{\mathrm{lin}}=f$. Then arbitrary traces on $\Gamma_N$ give the natural [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) condition. The complete [boundary value problem](../boundary-value-problem.md) is

$$
\boxed{-\operatorname{Div}S^{\mathrm{lin}}=f\text{ in }\Omega,\qquad
S^{\mathrm{lin}}N=g\text{ on }\Gamma_N,\qquad u=u_D\text{ on }\Gamma_D.}
$$

For a [homogeneous hyperelastic material](../elasticity-physics.md#homogeneous-hyperelastic-material) the [elasticity tensor](../elasticity-physics.md#elasticity-tensor) is constant. In [isotropic](../continuum-mechanics.md#isotropy) [linear elasticity](../elasticity-physics.md#linear-elasticity) the [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) is $S^{\mathrm{lin}}=\lambda\operatorname{div}u\,I+\mu(Du+Du^T)$, giving the familiar [Navier-Cauchy equation](../elasticity-physics.md#navier-cauchy-equation) $-\mu\Delta u-(\lambda+\mu)\nabla\operatorname{div}u=f$.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Taking traces of the [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) gives

$$
\lambda\operatorname{tr}e_\lambda=\frac13\left(\operatorname{tr}S_\lambda-2\mu\operatorname{tr}e_\lambda\right).
$$

The assumed finite limits of [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) and [strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) make the right side bounded. Therefore $\operatorname{tr}e_\lambda=O(\lambda^{-1})$, and the limiting [strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) satisfies $\operatorname{tr}e=0$. The permitted interchange of [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) and limits gives $\operatorname{tr}e=\operatorname{div}u$. Define

$$
q=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda\operatorname{tr}e_\lambda=\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}S,\qquad p=-q.
$$

Then the limit [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) is $S=-pI+2\mu e$. Passing to the limit in [force](../force.md) balance and using $2\operatorname{Div}e=\Delta u+\nabla\operatorname{div}u$ gives

$$
\boxed{-\mu\Delta u+\nabla p=f,\qquad\operatorname{div}u=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.}
$$

The [pressure](../pressure.md) acts as the [Lagrange multiplier](../mathematical-optimization.md#lagrange-multiplier) enforcing the [volume](../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) constraint. The [differential equations](../differential-equation.md) determine it up to an additive constant, while a specified absolute limit [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) can fix that constant.

Nonlinear [elastic incompressibility](../continuum-mechanics.md#elastic-incompressibility) is $\det Dy=1$. Expanding the [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) for $Dy=I+Du$ gives $\det(I+Du)=1+\operatorname{div}u+O(|Du|^2)$, so its first-order form is precisely $\operatorname{div}u=0$. The volumetric energy term $\lambda(\operatorname{div}u)^2/2$ becomes increasingly expensive as $\lambda$ grows, while the fixed positive $\mu$ leaves [shear strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#shear-strain) possible. Thus **the infinite-bulk-stiffness limit is the [incompressible limit of isotropic linear elasticity](../elasticity-physics.md#incompressible-limit-of-isotropic-linear-elasticity)**, with the same equilibrium operator as the steady [Stokes equations](../stokes-flow.md#stokes-equation).
