## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2008](2008.md)

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

By the [spectral theorem](../hilbert-space.md#spectral-theorem) a [symmetric positive-definite matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-positive-definite-matrix) $C$ has $C=Q\operatorname{diag}(c_i)Q^{\mathsf T}$, with $Q$ [orthogonal](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) and $c_i>0$. Define $S=Q\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{c_i})Q^{\mathsf T}$. It is [symmetric](../set-theory.md#symmetric-relation) [positive-definite](../bilinear-form.md#positive-definite-bilinear-form) and $S^2=C$. For [uniqueness](../mathematical-logic.md#uniqueness), any [symmetric](../set-theory.md#symmetric-relation) [positive-definite](../bilinear-form.md#positive-definite-bilinear-form) square root $T$ commutes with $C=T^2$, hence preserves each [eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) of $C$. On its $c$-[eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace), $T$ is [symmetric](../set-theory.md#symmetric-relation) [positive-definite](../bilinear-form.md#positive-definite-bilinear-form) and all its [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) have square $c$, so all equal $\sqrt c$. Therefore $T=S$, including repeated [eigenspaces](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace).

For $\det F>0$, put

$$
\boxed{U=(F^{\mathsf T}F)^{1/2},\quad R=FU^{-1},\quad V=RUR^{\mathsf T}=(FF^{\mathsf T})^{1/2}.}
$$

Then $R^{\mathsf T}R=I$ and $\det R=\det F/\det U>0$, so $R\in SO(3)$; also $F=RU=VR$. Any other [right polar decomposition](../continuum-mechanics.md#polar-decomposition-in-continuum-mechanics) has $F^{\mathsf T}F=U^2$, so [uniqueness](../mathematical-logic.md#uniqueness) of the positive root fixes $U$ and then $R$. The left factor is consequently fixed too.

The principal stretches $v_i$ are the positive [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) of $U$, equivalently the [singular values](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) of $F$. For a [unit vector](../vector-space.md#unit-vector) $N=\sum_i c_iN_i$ in an orthonormal [eigenbasis](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenbasis) of $U$,

$$
|FN|^2=N^{\mathsf T}U^2N=\sum_i v_i^2c_i^2\leq v_{\max}^2\sum_i c_i^2=v_{\max}^2.
$$

Thus $\boxed{\max_{|N|=1}|FN|=v_{\max}}$, attained precisely by the [unit vectors](../vector-space.md#unit-vector) in the [eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) of $U$ belonging to $v_{\max}$. With a simple largest [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) these are its two opposite unit [eigenvectors](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector); degeneracy allows every unit combination within that [eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace).

For the [simple shear](../continuum-mechanics.md#simple-shear),

$$
F=\begin{pmatrix}1&\gamma&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad C=F^{\mathsf T}F=\begin{pmatrix}1&\gamma&0\\\gamma&1+\gamma^2&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

A line with nonzero direction $N$ keeps its length exactly when

$$
|FN|^2-|N|^2=\gamma N_2(2N_1+\gamma N_2)=0.
$$

Hence $\boxed{N_2=0\quad\text{or}\quad2N_1+\gamma N_2=0}$, with $N_3$ arbitrary. Every segment in the cube [parallel](../vector-space.md#linear-dependence) to one of these directions preserves length; there is no restriction on its starting point other than remaining in the cube.

The two in-[plane](../geometry-and-topology.md#plane) stretches are $v_\pm=(\sqrt{4+\gamma^2}\pm\gamma)/2$, and the third is one. A maximizing direction for $\gamma>0$ is $N\parallel(1,v_+,0)$, since the $C$ [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) equation gives $N_2/N_1=(v_+^2-1)/\gamma=v_+$. Therefore $\boxed{N\to\pm(1,1,0)/\sqrt2}$ as $\gamma\downarrow0$. At exactly zero shear all directions tie; the limit refers to the selected maximizing direction for positive shear. These are the [length-preserving directions and principal directions of simple shear](../continuum-mechanics.md#length-preserving-directions-and-principal-directions-of-simple-shear).

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2008](2008.md)

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

[Cauchy's stress hypothesis](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-s-stress-hypothesis) says that [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) across a cut depends locally on its point, time and oriented normal, rather than on the global shape of the cut. Here all areas and normals are referential: $E$ is a fixed material region in reference coordinates, $\rho_R$ its mass [density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density) per reference volume, $v$ the current particle velocity, $b$ [body force](../fluid-mechanics.md#body-force) per unit mass, and $s_R$ force per unit reference area. The time derivative on the left is total material momentum rate; the two right terms are contact and [body forces](../fluid-mechanics.md#body-force).

A shrinking pillbox gives $s_R(-N)=-s_R(N)$. In a shrinking tetrahedron the volume contributions scale as its linear size cubed, whereas face forces scale as size squared. Their leading balance expresses [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) on an arbitrary face as the normal-component-weighted sum of [tractions](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) on three coordinate faces. Defining those three [tractions](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) as tensor columns gives $\boxed{s_R(N)=T_RN}$. Applying the [divergence theorem](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence-theorem) to [momentum balance](../momentum.md#momentum-conservation) and localizing over arbitrary $E$ then yields

$$
\boxed{\rho_R\dot v=\operatorname{Div}T_R+\rho_Rb.}
$$

The referential tensor is the [first Piola-Kirchhoff stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#first-piola-kirchhoff-stress-tensor) and need not be [symmetric](../set-theory.md#symmetric-relation); it should not be silently identified with the spatial [Cauchy stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor).

[Incompressibility](../fluid-mechanics.md#incompressible-flow) is $\boxed{\det F=1}$. For a [homogeneous hyperelastic material](../elasticity-physics.md#homogeneous-hyperelastic-material) the referential [constitutive relation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) becomes

$$
\boxed{T_R=DW(F)-p\operatorname{cof}F=DW(F)-pF^{-\mathsf T}\quad(\det F=1),}
$$

where the undetermined scalar [pressure](../pressure.md) $p$ enforces the [constraint](../classical-mechanics.md#constraint-mechanics). Homogeneity removes explicit reference-position dependence from $W$, not the possible spatial variation of that [pressure](../pressure.md).

For the [neo-Hookean](../elasticity-physics.md#neo-hookean-solid) energy, $DW=2\alpha F$. For the positive diagonal ansatz the nominal principal stresses are $t_i=2\alpha v_i-p/v_i$. Since $F$ is constant, [equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system) requires $\operatorname{cof}F\nabla p=0$, so $p$ is constant. Stress-free endfaces give $\boxed{p=2\alpha v_3^2>0}$. The side normals include $(0,-1,0)$ and $\pm(\sqrt3/2,1/2,0)$, so their prescribed [tractions](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) force $\boxed{t_1=t_2=T}$.

For fixed positive $p$, the scalar function $2\alpha v-p/v$ is [strictly increasing](../calculus.md#strictly-increasing-function) on $v>0$. Thus equality of the two nominal stresses implies $v_1=v_2=v$. [Incompressibility](../fluid-mechanics.md#incompressible-flow) gives $v_3=v^{-2}$, and substitution gives

$$
\boxed{T=2\alpha(v-v^{-5}),\qquad p=2\alpha v^{-4}.}
$$

The load function has derivative $2\alpha(1+5v^{-6})>0$, tends to minus infinity as $v\downarrow0$ and to infinity as $v\to\infty$. It therefore determines exactly one positive $v$ for every specified load, with $v\geq1$ for outward [tensile](../force.md#tension) $T\geq0$. The resulting constant stress satisfies [equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system) and every prescribed face [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction), proving existence and [uniqueness](../mathematical-logic.md#uniqueness) within the requested positive diagonal class. It does not claim [uniqueness](../mathematical-logic.md#uniqueness) among all possible nonhomogeneous [deformations](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-mechanics). This is the [equibiaxial dead loading of a neo-Hookean prism](../elasticity-physics.md#equibiaxial-dead-loading-of-a-neo-hookean-prism).

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

Put $n=x/R$ and $q=r(R)/R$. Differentiating $y=qx$ uses $\nabla R=n$ and $\nabla q=(r'-q)n/R$. Hence

$$
\boxed{F=qI+(r'-q)n\otimes n.}
$$

Its [radial eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) is $r'$ and its two tangential [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) are $q$. They are positive by hypothesis, so they are also the principal stretches:

$$
\boxed{v_1=r',\qquad v_2=v_3=q.}
$$

For an [isotropic](../continuum-mechanics.md#isotropy) [stored energy](../elasticity-physics.md#strain-energy-density) the [nominal stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#nominal-stress-tensor) has the same radial/tangential [projectors](../vector-space.md#projection-linear-algebra),

$$
P=DW(F)=\Phi_1 n\otimes n+\Phi_2(I-n\otimes n),
$$

where symmetry of $\Phi$ gives equal tangential derivatives. Since $\operatorname{Div}(n\otimes n)=2n/R$ and the scalar coefficients depend only on $R$,

$$
\operatorname{Div}P=\left[\frac{d\Phi_1}{dR}+\frac2R(\Phi_1-\Phi_2)\right]n.
$$

Thus [equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system) is exactly $\boxed{d\Phi_1/dR=2(\Phi_2-\Phi_1)/R}$.

For the particular energy define $\delta=r'q^2=\det F>0$. Its two derivatives are

$$
\Phi_1=\mu+h'(\delta)q^2,\qquad\Phi_2=\mu+h'(\delta)r'q.
$$

Also $q'=(r'-q)/R$. Differentiating the first formula and substituting the [equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system) equation gives

$$
h''(\delta)\delta'q^2+2h'(\delta)qq'=2h'(\delta)q(r'-q)/R=2h'(\delta)qq'.
$$

The last terms cancel. Strict positivity of $h''$ and $q$ yields $\boxed{\delta'=0}$, so $r^2r'=D R^2$ for one positive constant $D$. Integration gives $r^3=DR^3+C$.

The displacement data mean $r(1)=\lambda_1$ and $r(2)=2\lambda_2$, not $r(2)=\lambda_2$. Solving the two scalar equations gives

$$
\boxed{D=\frac{8\lambda_2^3-\lambda_1^3}{7},\qquad C=\frac{8(\lambda_1^3-\lambda_2^3)}7,\qquad r=(DR^3+C)^{1/3}.}
$$

The given inequality makes $D>0$. Since $r^3$ increases from $\lambda_1^3>0$ to $8\lambda_2^3$, it is positive on the whole shell, and $r'=DR^2/r^2>0$. Thus the formula is [smooth](../analysis.md#smooth-function) and [admissible](../representation-theory.md#admissible-representation-of-a-locally-profinite-group) and satisfies [equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system). Conversely every [admissible](../representation-theory.md#admissible-representation-of-a-locally-profinite-group) radial [equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system) had to have this constant [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) and these two integration constants, proving [uniqueness](../mathematical-logic.md#uniqueness). This is a [constant-Jacobian radial equilibrium for a stretch-sum energy](../elasticity-physics.md#constant-jacobian-radial-equilibrium-for-a-stretch-sum-energy).

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

On each half-space the [deformation](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-mechanics) is affine. Continuity on the separating [plane](../geometry-and-topology.md#plane) makes the two affine constants equal and gives $(A-B)t=0$ for every [tangent vector](../tangent-vector.md) $t\perp N$. Decomposing any $x$ into its tangential part and $(x\cdot N)N$ gives

$$
(A-B)x=[(A-B)N](N\cdot x),\qquad\boxed{A-B=a\otimes N,\quad a=(A-B)N.}
$$

This is the [Hadamard jump condition](../continuum-mechanics.md#hadamard-jump-condition). Since $B=A-a\otimes N=A(I-A^{-1}a\otimes N)$, the [matrix determinant lemma](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-determinant-lemma) gives

$$
\det B=\det A(1-N\cdot A^{-1}a).
$$

Both [determinants](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) are positive, hence $\boxed{A^{-1}a\cdot N<1}$.

Write $a_1=\eta_1$, $a_2=\eta_2$, $S=a_1^2+a_2^2$ and $\Delta=a_2^2-a_1^2\ne0$. The two proposed angles have $c=2a_1a_2/S$ and $s=\kappa\Delta/S$, and $c^2+s^2=1$. Therefore the displayed [rotations](../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) lie in $SO(3)$. Direct multiplication gives

$$
RU_2-U_1=\frac\Delta S\begin{pmatrix}-a_2&-\kappa a_2&0\\\kappa a_1&a_1&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}
=\boxed{\frac\Delta S(-a_2,\kappa a_1,0)\otimes(1,\kappa,0).}
$$

This is a [rank-one connection](../continuum-mechanics.md#rank-one-connection) for each sign. With a unit normal one may choose $N=(1,\kappa,0)/\sqrt2$ and jump vector $a=\sqrt2\Delta(-a_2,\kappa a_1,0)/S$. The two [rotations](../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) are distinct because $\Delta\ne0$; the permitted at-most-two result therefore proves these are all solutions. Flipping both $a$ and $N$ changes no connection.

It remains to test the material [plane](../geometry-and-topology.md#plane). Because $y(0)=0$, the affine constants vanish. Let $t=(-p_2,p_1,0)$, so the [plane](../geometry-and-topology.md#plane) is spanned by $t$ and $e_3$. Both [deformation gradients](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient) send $e_3$ to $a_1e_3$. If the [plane](../geometry-and-topology.md#plane) is not the [twin plane](../chemical-substance.md#twin-plane), it cuts the two half-spaces; its two image halves are coplanar exactly when the in-[plane](../geometry-and-topology.md#plane) vectors $Bt$ and $At$ are [parallel](../vector-space.md#linear-dependence). Put $d=(-a_2,\kappa a_1)$ and $n=(1,\kappa)$ in their two-dimensional sections. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det_2(Bt,At)
&=\frac\Delta S\det_2(Bt,d)(n\cdot t)\\
&=\boxed{\frac{\kappa a_1a_2\Delta}{S}(p_1-\kappa p_2)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Its prefactor is nonzero, so [coplanarity](../euclidean-geometry.md#coplanarity) requires $p_1=\kappa p_2$, precisely $p\parallel(1,\kappa,0)\parallel N$. Conversely, on that [twin plane](../chemical-substance.md#twin-plane) $(A-B)x=0$, so the two [affine maps](../geometry-and-topology.md#affine-map) agree and its image is a [plane](../geometry-and-topology.md#plane). Thus $\boxed{\Pi\text{ remains plane}\iff p\parallel N}$. All other allowed [planes](../geometry-and-topology.md#plane) acquire a kink along their intersection with the twin. This is the [planarity test across a cubic-to-tetragonal twin](../chemical-substance.md#planarity-test-across-a-cubic-to-tetragonal-twin).
