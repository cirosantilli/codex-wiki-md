# Cauchy criterion for an improper integral

↑ **Parent:** [Improper integral](improper-integral.md)

For a locally integrable real or complex function, the displayed tail condition is equivalent to ordinary convergence of its [improper integral](improper-integral.md) at infinity. Apply the [Cauchy criterion for a function limit](cauchy-criterion-for-a-function-limit.md) to its truncated integrals, using completeness of the real or complex numbers. The necessity follows by subtracting two nearby-to-limit truncations; sufficiency follows by first taking a convergent sequence of truncations and then bounding all other late truncations against it. The analogous criterion applies at a finite singular endpoint. Bounding the modulus of every tail by its absolute-value integral proves that an [absolutely convergent improper integral](absolutely-convergent-improper-integral.md) converges ordinarily.
