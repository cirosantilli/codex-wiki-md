# Abelianization

↑ **Parent:** [Commutator subgroup](commutator-subgroup.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianization)

The abelianization of a [group](group-split.md) $G$ is the [quotient group](quotient-group.md)

$$
G^{\mathrm{ab}}=G/G',\qquad q:G\longrightarrow G^{\mathrm{ab}},\quad q(g)=gG',
$$

where $G'$ is the [commutator subgroup](commutator-subgroup.md). Its elements are [cosets](coset.md), and its operation is $(gG')(hG')=ghG'$. The result is an [abelian group](abelian-group.md): the two products $gh$ and $hg$ differ by a [group commutator](group-commutator.md), which disappears in the [quotient group](quotient-group.md).

For the [symmetric group](symmetric-group.md) $S_3$, the [sign of a permutation](sign-of-a-permutation.md) realizes $S_3^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong C_2$, as shown in [commutator subgroup of the symmetric group on three letters](commutator-subgroup-of-the-symmetric-group-on-three-letters.md). For the [dihedral group](dihedral-group.md) of a [square](square.md), [abelianization of the square symmetry group](abelianization-of-the-square-symmetry-group.md) gives $C_2\times C_2$: two independent yes-or-no pieces of information survive. An [abelian group](abelian-group.md) already has $G'=\{e\}$, so its [abelianization](abelianization.md) is isomorphic to itself. At the other extreme, a [perfect group](perfect-group.md) has trivial [abelianization](abelianization.md).

Calling this the largest [abelian quotient](abelian-quotient.md) means that every [group homomorphism](group-homomorphism.md) into any [abelian group](abelian-group.md) factors uniquely through $q$. It does not require $G$ to be finite, or compare the sizes of unrelated [abelian groups](abelian-group.md). The [universal property of abelianization](universal-property-of-abelianization.md) makes this precise. It lets us solve all such mapping problems in one simpler [group](group-split.md): any invariant that respects multiplication and takes values in an [abelian group](abelian-group.md) sees only $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$.
