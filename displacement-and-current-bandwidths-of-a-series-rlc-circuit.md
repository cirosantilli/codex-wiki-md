# Displacement and current bandwidths of a series RLC circuit

↑ **Parent:** [Series RLC circuit](series-rlc-circuit.md)

Write $\omega_0^2=1/(LC)$ and $\gamma=R/L$ for a [series RLC circuit](series-rlc-circuit.md). The squared charge response has denominator $D=(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+\gamma^2\omega^2$. Its nonzero [resonance](resonance.md) peak occurs at $\omega_r^2=\omega_0^2-\gamma^2/2$. The two squared half-height frequencies, when both exist, are

$$
\omega_\pm^2=\omega_0^2-\frac{\gamma^2}{2}\pm\gamma\sqrt{\omega_0^2-\frac{\gamma^2}{4}}.
$$

Their difference in [angular frequencies](angular-frequency.md) is only approximately $\gamma$ at large [quality factor](q-factor.md). The [electric current](electric-current.md) instead has $|I|^2=|V|^2/[R^2+(\omega L-1/(\omega C))^2]$. Its exact half-power frequencies satisfy $|\omega L-1/(\omega C)|=R$, whose positive roots differ by $R/L$, independently of [capacitance](capacitance.md). Thus an exact capacitance-independent [bandwidth](bandwidth-signal-processing.md) applies to current or resistor-voltage output; capacitor-voltage output is a low-pass response with a possible resonant peak.
