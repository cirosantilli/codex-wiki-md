<h1 id="section-b/b6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a cold, unmagnetized, collisionless [plasma](../../../../../../plasma-physics.md), take an equilibrium [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) density $n_e$, [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) of [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) $m_e$ and charge $-e$, and approximately stationary ions. Use fields proportional to $e^{-i\omega t}$. The [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) [displacement](../../../../../../displacement.md) satisfies

$$
m_e\ddot{\mathbf x}=-e\mathbf E,
\qquad \mathbf x=\frac{e\mathbf E}{m_e\omega^2}.
$$

Its [electric polarization](../../../../../../polarization-density.md) is $\mathbf P=-n_ee\mathbf x$, hence the [cold collisionless plasma dielectric response](../../../../../../cold-collisionless-plasma-dielectric-response.md)

$$
\boxed{\epsilon_r(\omega)=1-\frac{\omega_p^2}{\omega^2},
\qquad \omega_p^2=\frac{n_ee^2}{\epsilon_0m_e}.}
$$

The negative susceptibility arises from the inertial [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) response, rather than a restoring [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) binding an [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) to a particular atom.

A transverse [electromagnetic wave](../../../../../../electromagnetic-wave-split.md) then obeys

$$
k^2c^2=\omega^2\epsilon_r=\omega^2-\omega_p^2.
$$

For $\omega>\omega_p$, the [plasma](../../../../../../plasma-physics.md) supports propagation, with [refractive index](../../../../../../refractive-index.md) $n_r=\sqrt{1-\omega_p^2/\omega^2}<1$. The [phase velocity](../../../../../../phase-velocity.md) is greater than $c$, while the [group velocity](../../../../../../group-velocity.md) is $c\sqrt{1-\omega_p^2/\omega^2}<c$; their product is $c^2$, and no superluminal [signal](../../../../../../signal.md) is implied. Below the [plasma frequency](../../../../../../plasma-frequency.md), the bulk [wavenumber](../../../../../../wavenumber.md) is imaginary and the field is evanescent. A sufficiently thick [plasma](../../../../../../plasma-physics.md) largely reflects such radiation in the lossless model.

Including an [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) momentum-relaxation rate $\nu_c$ replaces the denominator by $\omega(\omega+i\nu_c)$:

$$
\epsilon_r=1-\frac{\omega_p^2}{\omega(\omega+i\nu_c)}.
$$

Its [imaginary part](../../../../../../imaginary-part.md) describes absorption with the stated [time](../../../../../../time-in-physics.md) convention. Finite [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) also produces [plasma screening](../../../../../../plasma-screening.md) of slowly varying electrostatic fields, with $\lambda_D=\sqrt{\epsilon_0k_BT_e/(n_ee^2)}$. That spatial, longitudinal screening problem is distinct from simply taking $\omega=0$ in the cold transverse-wave formula. Magnetic bias, ion motion and kinetic resonances require more general [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md) responses.
