<h1 id="section-b/b6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For weak fields in a linear isotropic material, [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) is $\mathbf M=\chi_m\mathbf H$, with [magnetic susceptibility](../../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md) $\chi_m$. **[Paramagnetism](../../../../../../paramagnetism.md) has $\chi_m>0$**, while **[diamagnetism](../../../../../../diamagnetism.md) has $\chi_m<0$**. In either case, $\mathbf B=\mu_0(\mathbf H+\mathbf M)=\mu_0(1+\chi_m)\mathbf H$. These weak responses generally disappear when the applied field is removed, unlike hysteretic [ferromagnetism](../../../../../../ferromagnetism.md).

A paramagnet can contain permanent [magnetic moments](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) from unpaired [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) or atomic [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md). Alignment lowers the [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) $-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B$, while thermal disorder opposes it. For independent classical moments of magnitude $\mu$, their weak-field average projection is $\mu^2B/(3k_BT)$, so

$$
\chi_m\simeq\frac{\mu_0n\mu^2}{3k_BT}.
$$

This is [Curie's law](../../../../../../curie-s-law.md); it assumes weak [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) and no important interactions between moments. With quantum [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) $J$, replace $\mu^2$ by $(g\mu_B)^2J(J+1)$. Strong-field [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) saturates instead of growing linearly without limit. Conduction [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) also exhibit [Pauli paramagnetism](../../../../../../pauli-paramagnetism.md): only occupations near the [Fermi surface](../../../../../../fermi-surface.md) readily redistribute between [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) states, giving a nearly temperature-independent susceptibility when $T\ll T_F$.

[Diamagnetism](../../../../../../diamagnetism.md) is an induced orbital response opposing the applied [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md). For bound [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md), a weak field changes orbital motion and induces an opposing [magnetic moment](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md). For an isotropic collection of atoms, the elementary orbital estimate is

$$
\chi_{\rm dia}=-\frac{\mu_0ne^2}{6m_e}\sum_j\langle r_j^2\rangle,
$$

where the sum runs over the [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) of one atom. It is usually small and weakly [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) dependent. Delocalized [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) also have orbital Landau [diamagnetism](../../../../../../diamagnetism.md); for an ideal three-dimensional free [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) [gas](../../../../../../gas.md) with a quadratic isotropic dispersion and [electron spin](../../../../../../electron-spin.md) with $g=2$, $\chi_L=-\chi_P/3$. Both [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) and orbital contributions must be included to determine the total response. [Diamagnetism](../../../../../../diamagnetism.md) is not a consequence of permanent dipoles choosing to align against the field; that orientation would increase their [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md).
