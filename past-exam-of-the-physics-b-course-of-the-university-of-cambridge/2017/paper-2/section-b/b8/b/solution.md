<h1 id="section-b/b8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [gravitational assist](../../../../../../gravitational-assist.md) changes a spacecraft's [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) relative to the Sun by scattering it in the [gravitational field](../../../../../../gravitational-field.md) of a moving planet. In the planet's rest frame, ignore other [forces](../../../../../../force-split.md) during the encounter and take the spacecraft [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) negligible. The incoming and outgoing asymptotic [velocities](../../../../../../velocity.md) have equal magnitude,

$$
|\mathbf u_{\rm in}|=|\mathbf u_{\rm out}|=u_\infty,
$$

because the encounter is conservative, but their directions differ along a hyperbolic [orbit](../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md). If the planet has approximately constant heliocentric [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) $\mathbf V_p$, the heliocentric spacecraft [velocities](../../../../../../velocity.md) are $\mathbf v_{\rm in,out}=\mathbf V_p+\mathbf u_{\rm in,out}$. Its specific [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) change is

$$
\boxed{\Delta\mathcal E=\frac12(v_{\rm out}^2-v_{\rm in}^2)
=\mathbf V_p\cdot(\mathbf u_{\rm out}-\mathbf u_{\rm in}).}
$$

Thus a flyby can increase or decrease heliocentric [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) depending on its geometry. The planet exchanges [momentum](../../../../../../momentum-split.md) and orbital [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) with the spacecraft; its [speed](../../../../../../speed.md) change is extremely small because its [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) is much greater. There is no [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) creation and no increase of asymptotic [speed](../../../../../../speed.md) in the planet's own frame.

For a point-mass planet, [conservation of energy](../../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) and [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) gives the hyperbolic eccentricity and deflection angle

$$
e=1+\frac{r_pu_\infty^2}{GM_p},\qquad
\delta=2\sin^{-1}(1/e),
\qquad \tan\frac\delta2=\frac{GM_p}{b u_\infty^2},
$$

where $r_p$ is [periapsis](../../../../../../periapsis.md) distance and $b$ the [impact parameter](../../../../../../impact-parameter.md). The velocity-vector change has magnitude $2u_\infty\sin(\delta/2)$, so the maximum energy-change magnitude for this fixed deflection is $2V_pu_\infty\sin(\delta/2)$. A small [periapsis](../../../../../../periapsis.md) increases the turning angle, but [collision](../../../../../../collision.md), atmosphere and [tidal forces](../../../../../../tidal-force.md) limit how close a real spacecraft may pass. Multiple encounters allow large changes without expending the propellant that equivalent direct [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) changes would require.
