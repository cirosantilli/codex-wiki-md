<h1 id="section-c/c12/energy-of-incident-particles/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Energy of incident particles](../energy-of-incident-particles.md)

Wall arrivals are not an unbiased sample of the [gas](../../../../../../gas.md): faster atoms arrive more frequently. The normalized [speed distribution](../../../../../../speed-distribution.md) among incident atoms is

$$
f_{\rm hit}(v)=\frac{vf(v)}{\overline v}.
$$

The angular factor in the arrival rate cancels when calculating the [mean](../../../../../../expected-value-split.md) [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md). Thus

$$
\langle K\rangle_{\rm hit}
=\frac m2\frac{\int_0^\infty v^3f(v)\,dv}{\int_0^\infty vf(v)\,dv}
=\frac m2\frac{I_5}{I_3}.
$$

Using $I_5=2I_3/\alpha$ gives

$$
\boxed{\langle K\rangle_{\rm hit}=\frac m\alpha=2k_BT.}
$$

By contrast, an atom chosen randomly from the bulk has [mean](../../../../../../expected-value-split.md) [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) $3k_BT/2$. The arrival weighting raises the normal-direction contribution to $k_BT$ while each tangential direction still contributes $k_BT/2$. This is also the [mean](../../../../../../expected-value-split.md) [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) carried by atoms effusing through a thin aperture from an equilibrated reservoir.
