<h1 id="section-c/c12/pressures-in-heated-connected-vessels/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Pressures in heated connected vessels](../pressures-in-heated-connected-vessels.md)

Denote the heated and cold vessels by subscripts $h,c$. For [argon](../../../../../../argon.md), the opposing effusive [particle](../../../../../../particle-physics.md) rates are equal in the steady state:

$$
n_h\overline v(T_1)=n_c\overline v(T_0).
$$

Because $\overline v\propto\sqrt T$ and $p=nk_BT$,

$$
\boxed{\frac{p_h}{\sqrt{T_1}}=\frac{p_c}{\sqrt{T_0}},
\qquad \frac{p_h}{p_c}=\sqrt{\frac{T_1}{T_0}}.}
$$

This is [thermal transpiration](../../../../../../thermal-transpiration.md) in the free-molecular limit. It fixes a ratio; the initial conditions determine the absolute pressures.

For the stated two identical vessels, take their fixed [volume](../../../../../../volume-thermodynamics.md) to be $V$ and the combined system sealed. Initially it contains $2p_0V/(k_BT_0)$ atoms. Hence

$$
\frac{p_hV}{k_BT_1}+\frac{p_cV}{k_BT_0}=\frac{2p_0V}{k_BT_0}.
$$

Let $q=\sqrt{T_1/T_0}$. Combining this inventory with $p_h=qp_c$ gives the [closed-vessel thermal transpiration](../../../../../../closed-vessel-thermal-transpiration.md) result

$$
\boxed{p_c=\frac{2p_0q}{q+1},\qquad
p_h=\frac{2p_0q^2}{q+1},\qquad
\Delta p=p_h-p_c=\frac{2p_0q(q-1)}{q+1}.}
$$

Heating makes the hot side less dense but at higher [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md). Both expressions reduce to $p_0$ when $T_1=T_0$. For a small imposed [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) rise $\Delta T$, the [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md) difference starts as $p_0\Delta T/(2T_0)$.

If instead the cold vessel is interpreted as an externally maintained [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md) reservoir with $p_c=p_0$, the answer becomes $\Delta p=p_0(q-1)$. That alternative requires the fixed-pressure reservoir assumption; it does not conserve the initial [particle](../../../../../../particle-physics.md) inventory of two sealed equal-volume vessels. Merely describing the vessels as large does not keep their pressures fixed through the redistribution. The pressures in the boxed answer assume the natural closed-vessel interpretation.
