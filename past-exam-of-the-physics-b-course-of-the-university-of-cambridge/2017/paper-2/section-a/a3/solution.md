<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

Let $s(t)$ be the [length](../../../../../length.md) of the free straight segment from the current contact point to the [mass](../../../../../mass.md), and let $\theta$ measure the contact point's [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) from first contact. For an inextensible taut rope, winding through angle $\theta$ uses [length](../../../../../length.md) $R\theta$:

$$
s=L-R\theta.
$$

The point [mass](../../../../../mass.md) follows an [involute](../../../../../involute.md) of the pole's circular cross-section. Its instantaneous [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) is perpendicular to the free rope. To verify the geometry explicitly, use planar polar [unit vectors](../../../../../unit-vector.md) at the contact point:

$$
\mathbf x=R\mathbf e_r(\theta)+s\mathbf e_\theta(\theta),
\qquad \frac{d\mathbf x}{d\theta}
=(R+s')\mathbf e_\theta-s\mathbf e_r=-s\mathbf e_r.
$$

The rope's [tension](../../../../../tension.md) therefore does no [work](../../../../../work.md), and the [speed](../../../../../speed.md) stays at its initial value $v$. The geometry gives $v=s\dot\theta$. Consequently

$$
\frac{ds}{dt}=-R\dot\theta=-\frac{Rv}{s},
\qquad s^2=L^2-2Rvt.
$$

The distance from the pole's axis is $\sqrt{R^2+s^2}$, so a point [mass](../../../../../mass.md) first reaches the pole when $s=0$. The [winding time around a circular pole](../../../../../winding-time-around-a-circular-pole.md) is thus

$$
\boxed{t_{\rm hit}=\frac{L^2}{2Rv}.}
$$

This has units of [time](../../../../../time-in-physics.md). The model assumes a light inextensible rope, no [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) loss at contact and a point [mass](../../../../../mass.md). The required [tension](../../../../../tension.md) $mv^2/s$ diverges at the ideal final contact, so finite strength and size matter very close to impact.
