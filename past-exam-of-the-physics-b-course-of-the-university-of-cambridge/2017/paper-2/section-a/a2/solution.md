<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

Treat the lake as a large [thermal reservoir](../../../../../heat-bath.md) at $T_f=283.15\,\mathrm K$, and take the copper's [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md) $C=50\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$ constant over the cooling interval from $T_i=783.15\,\mathrm K$. Although the actual cooling is irreversible, [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) is a state function, so its change can be calculated along a hypothetical reversible path:

$$
\Delta S_{\rm block}=\int_{T_i}^{T_f}\frac{C}{T}\,dT
=C\ln\left(\frac{T_f}{T_i}\right)
=\boxed{-50.9\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.}
$$

The lake receives $Q=C(T_i-T_f)=2.50\times10^4\,\mathrm J$ at constant [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), giving

$$
\Delta S_{\rm lake}=\frac{Q}{T_f}=88.3\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$

Therefore, neglecting other changes,

$$
\boxed{\Delta S_{\rm Universe}=\Delta S_{\rm block}+\Delta S_{\rm lake}
=37.4\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}>0.}
$$

The positive total is the [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) generated by transferring [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) across a finite [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) difference. The copper's [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) decreases, but the colder lake gains a greater amount.
