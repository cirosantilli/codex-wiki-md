<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

Use $x=\rho\cos\phi$, $y=\rho\sin\phi$ to recognize the [planar quadrupole potential](../../../../../planar-quadrupole-potential.md), $V=A(x^2-y^2)$. Its [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is minus the [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E=(-2Ax,\,2Ay,\,0),}
$$

or, in [cylindrical coordinates](../../../../../cylindrical-coordinate-system.md),

$$
E_\rho=-2A\rho\cos2\phi,\qquad
E_\phi=2A\rho\sin2\phi,\qquad E_z=0.
$$

For $A>0$, arrows point toward the origin along the $x$ axis and away from it along the $y$ axis. The off-axis [electric field lines](../../../../../electric-field-line.md) satisfy

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{E_y}{E_x}=-\frac yx,
\qquad \boxed{xy=\mathrm{constant}.}
$$

The axes themselves are the separating field lines; the origin is a field null. Reversing $A$ reverses every arrow. The field is independent of $z$, and $A$ has units $\mathrm{V\,m^{-2}}$.
