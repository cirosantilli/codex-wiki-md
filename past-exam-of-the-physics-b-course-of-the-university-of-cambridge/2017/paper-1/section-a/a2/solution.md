<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

In steady rigid [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) of the liquid about its vertical axis, [centrifugal acceleration](../../../../../centrifugal-acceleration.md) is $\Omega^2r$ outward. [Hydrostatic pressure](../../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) balance in the rotating frame gives

$$
\frac{\partial p}{\partial r}=\rho\Omega^2r,\qquad
\frac{\partial p}{\partial z}=-\rho g.
$$

The [free surface](../../../../../free-surface.md) is at constant atmospheric [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md), so its slope obeys

$$
0=dp=\rho\Omega^2r\,dr-\rho g\,dz,
\qquad z-z_0=\frac{\Omega^2r^2}{2g}.
$$

Compare this [parabolic free surface of a rigidly rotating liquid](../../../../../parabolic-free-surface-of-a-rigidly-rotating-liquid.md) with $z-z_0=r^2/(4f)$. For [focal length](../../../../../focal-length.md) $f=1\ \mathrm m$,

$$
\boxed{\Omega=\sqrt{\frac{g}{2f}}
\simeq2.21\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}
\simeq21.1\ \mathrm{rev\,min^{-1}}.}
$$

Either sense of [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) gives the same shape. The result assumes the liquid has reached rigid [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) before solidifying and that surface-tension and solidification distortions can be neglected.
