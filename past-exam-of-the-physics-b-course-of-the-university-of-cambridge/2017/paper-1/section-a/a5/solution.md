<h1 id="section-a/a5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A5](../a5.md)

Cold air is denser than room air at approximately the same [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). The [ideal gas law](../../../../../ideal-gas-law.md) gives a fractional [mass density](../../../../../density.md) contrast of order $\Delta\rho/\rho\sim\Delta T/T$. The initial outflow is therefore a [gravity current](../../../../../gravity-current.md) driven by [reduced gravity](../../../../../reduced-gravity-split.md)

$$
g'=g\frac{\Delta\rho}{\rho}\sim g\frac{\Delta T}{T}.
$$

For an illustrative refrigerator height $H\sim1\ \mathrm m$, room [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) $T\sim295\ \mathrm K$ and [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) difference $\Delta T\sim20\ \mathrm K$, this gives $g'\sim0.66\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. Balancing the released [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) with [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) gives $U\sim\sqrt{g'H}\sim0.8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The [buoyancy exchange time of a cold-air enclosure](../../../../../buoyancy-exchange-time-of-a-cold-air-enclosure.md) is consequently

$$
\boxed{t\sim\frac H{\sqrt{g'H}}=\sqrt{\frac H{g'}}
\sim1\text{–}2\ \mathrm s,\quad\text{of order seconds}.}
$$

Using constant [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) over height $H$ gives $\sqrt{2H/g'}\sim1.7\ \mathrm s$, consistent at this level. Warm air enters as cold air leaves, so unrestricted vacuum free fall is not the right [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) model. No dimensions or temperatures are specified in the source, so an order-of-magnitude estimate with explicit representative values is the appropriate result; shelves and mixing can delay complete replacement.
