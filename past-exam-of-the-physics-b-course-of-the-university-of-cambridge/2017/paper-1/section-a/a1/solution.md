<h1 id="section-a/a1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A1](../a1.md)

Write the plate area as $S$ and separation as $d$, so the original [capacitance](../../../../../capacitance.md) is $C=\varepsilon_0S/d$. Neglect edge fields. The greatest [capacitance](../../../../../capacitance.md) occurs when each material occupies half the plate area and spans the full separation. These two regions are [capacitors](../../../../../capacitor.md) in parallel, sharing the same [electric potential difference](../../../../../electric-potential-difference.md):

$$
\boxed{C_{\max}=\frac C2+\frac{\epsilon C}{2}
=\frac{1+\epsilon}{2}C.}
$$

The least [capacitance](../../../../../capacitance.md) occurs when a dielectric layer of thickness $d/2$ covers the whole area, leaving a vacuum layer of the same thickness. The two layers are [capacitors](../../../../../capacitor.md) in series: the normal [electric displacement field](../../../../../electric-displacement-field.md) is continuous and the voltage drops add. Their individual capacitances are $2\epsilon C$ and $2C$, giving

$$
\boxed{C_{\min}=\left(\frac1{2C}+\frac1{2\epsilon C}\right)^{-1}
=\frac{2\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}C.}
$$

The [arithmetic-harmonic bounds for dielectric capacitance](../../../../../arithmetic-harmonic-bounds-for-dielectric-capacitance.md) show that these are genuine extrema over other arrangements of the same two volumes, within the ideal parallel-plate model. A uniform trial [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) in the fixed-voltage energy principle bounds $C$ above by the arithmetic mean of the two permittivities; a uniform trial [electric displacement field](../../../../../electric-displacement-field.md) in the fixed-charge principle bounds it below by their harmonic mean. The two layer arrangements attain those bounds. Both reduce to $C$ when $\epsilon=1$.
