<h1 id="section-a/a1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Initially and finally the block and lake are at the same [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). After the falling block has come to rest, its lost [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../../gravitational-energy.md) has dissipated into [internal energy](../../../../../../internal-energy-split.md) of the lake and surroundings. In the idealization that all $mgh$ is ultimately thermalized in the lake at $T_0=283.15\,\mathrm K$,

$$
Q=mgh=(0.5)(9.81)(200)=981\,\mathrm J.
$$

The block returns to its original thermal state, so its net [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) change is zero. Mechanical [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) carries no thermal [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) that must be subtracted. Thus

$$
\boxed{\Delta S_{\mathrm{Universe}}=\frac{mgh}{T_0}=3.46\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.}
$$

Use of $g=9.80\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ gives the same three-significant-figure answer. Splashing and any temporary heating do not change this final-state balance if their mechanical [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) is eventually dissipated in the same reservoir.
