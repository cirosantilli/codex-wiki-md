<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

Assume an air-filled or evacuated lossless [electromagnetic waveguide](../../../../../electromagnetic-waveguide.md), and a pulse bandwidth narrow enough to use its carrier's local [group velocity](../../../../../group-velocity.md). The longitudinal [dispersion relation](../../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) is

$$
\omega^2=c^2k_z^2+\omega_c^2.
$$

It gives

$$
v_{\mathrm p}=\frac\omega{k_z}=\frac c{\sqrt{1-(f_c/f)^2}},
\qquad
v_{\mathrm g}=\frac{d\omega}{dk_z}=c\sqrt{1-(f_c/f)^2}.
$$

Here $f_c/f=20/40=1/2$, so

$$
\boxed{v_{\mathrm p}=\frac{2c}{\sqrt3}=3.46\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\qquad
v_{\mathrm g}=\frac{\sqrt3c}{2}=2.60\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The [phase velocity](../../../../../phase-velocity.md) exceeds $c$, but the pulse envelope travels at the subluminal [group velocity](../../../../../group-velocity.md); a moving constant-phase surface does not by itself transport information. Their product is $v_{\mathrm p}v_{\mathrm g}=c^2$. A broad pulse can distort because this [waveguide](../../../../../waveguide.md) is dispersive.
