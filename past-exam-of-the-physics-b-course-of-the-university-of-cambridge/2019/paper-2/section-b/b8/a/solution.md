<h1 id="section-b/b8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let the [rotating frame](../../../../../../rotating-reference-frame.md) have origin [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) $\mathbf A_0$ and [angular velocity](../../../../../../angular-velocity.md) $\boldsymbol\Omega(t)$ relative to an [inertial frame](../../../../../../inertial-frame.md). For a [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) referred to the moving axes, the transport rule is

$$
\left(\frac{d\mathbf a}{dt}\right)_{\mathrm I}
=\left(\frac{d\mathbf a}{dt}\right)_{\mathrm R}+\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf a.
$$

Apply it twice to position. With rotating-frame [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) $\mathbf v$ and [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) $\mathbf a_R$,

$$
\mathbf a_I=\mathbf A_0+\mathbf a_R+2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v
+\dot{\boldsymbol\Omega}\times\mathbf r
+\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r).
$$

Thus Newton's equation expressed in the [rotating frame](../../../../../../rotating-reference-frame.md) is

$$
\boxed{m\mathbf a_R=\mathbf F_{\mathrm{real}}-m\mathbf A_0
-2m\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v-m\dot{\boldsymbol\Omega}\times\mathbf r
-m\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r).}
$$

The added terms are translational, Coriolis, Euler and centrifugal [fictitious forces](../../../../../../fictitious-force.md). They reflect the frame's [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md), rather than additional interactions with nearby objects.

The [centrifugal force](../../../../../../centrifugal-force.md) points outward from the [rotation](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) axis and, for constant $\boldsymbol\Omega$, has potential $-m|\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r|^2/2$. The [Coriolis force](../../../../../../coriolis-force.md) is perpendicular to [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md), so does no [work](../../../../../../work.md) on the particle measured in that [rotating frame](../../../../../../rotating-reference-frame.md); it changes direction rather than speed directly. The [Euler force](../../../../../../euler-force.md) is present when the frame's [rotation](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) rate or direction changes.

On Earth, gravity and [centrifugal acceleration](../../../../../../centrifugal-acceleration.md) combine into effective gravity. For slow horizontal motion at latitude $\lambda$, the vertical component of Earth's [rotation](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) gives Coriolis parameter $f=2\Omega\sin\lambda$: an initially northward motion deflects eastward in the northern hemisphere. This supplies a useful local explanation of rotating flows and Foucault [precession](../../../../../../precession.md). A bucket of rotating [liquid](../../../../../../liquid.md) illustrates the [centrifugal potential](../../../../../../centrifugal-potential.md): its steady free surface is an equipotential of $gz-\Omega^2r^2/2$, giving $z=\Omega^2r^2/(2g)+\mathrm{constant}$. These equations require a declared frame; adding a [centrifugal force](../../../../../../centrifugal-force.md) while retaining inertial-frame [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) would count the same kinematic effect twice.
