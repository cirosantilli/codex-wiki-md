<h1 id="section-b/b6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md) responds to an [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) mainly by displacing or orienting bound charges, rather than by sustaining a steady conduction current. The [polarization density](../../../../../../polarization-density.md) $\mathbf P$ is [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) per unit [volume](../../../../../../volume-thermodynamics.md). It creates bound charge

$$
\rho_b=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P,\qquad\sigma_b=\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf n.
$$

Separating these charges from free charge motivates

$$
\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P,\qquad\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho_f.
$$

For an isotropic linear [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md), $\mathbf P=\epsilon_0\chi_e\mathbf E$, so

$$
\boxed{\mathbf D=\epsilon\mathbf E,\qquad\epsilon=\epsilon_0(1+\chi_e)=\epsilon_0\epsilon_r.}
$$

The [electric susceptibility](../../../../../../electric-susceptibility.md) $\chi_e$ describes material polarization and is not the [magnetic susceptibility](../../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md). More generally it is a [tensor](../../../../../../tensor.md) and can depend on [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md), field strength and [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md).

Electronic polarization displaces an [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) cloud relative to its nucleus; ionic polarization displaces positive and negative ions; orientational polarization partially aligns permanent molecular dipoles against thermal disorder. For independent permanent moments of magnitude $p$, weak-field orientational response gives $\chi_e\simeq np^2/(3\epsilon_0k_BT)$ when local-field corrections and interactions are negligible. This explains a common inverse-temperature contribution without asserting that every [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md) follows that law.

At a static interface, $\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf D_2-\mathbf D_1)=\sigma_f$ and the tangential [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is continuous. Bound surface charge is already included through $\mathbf P$. For a homogeneous filled [capacitor](../../../../../../capacitor.md), $C=\epsilon A/d$, and a reversible linear [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md) stores [energy density](../../../../../../energy-density.md) $u=\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf D/2$. At fixed voltage, insertion raises the stored field [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) but the voltage source also supplies [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md); [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) arguments must include that source. At fixed charge the [capacitor energy](../../../../../../capacitor-energy.md) instead decreases on insertion.

At [frequencies](../../../../../../frequency.md) too high for a polarization mechanism to follow the field, its contribution weakens. A complex [permittivity](../../../../../../permittivity-split.md) represents dissipative [dielectric loss](../../../../../../dielectric-loss.md) as well as phase lag; the sign of its imaginary part depends on the time convention. Strong fields can cause nonlinear response or [dielectric breakdown](../../../../../../dielectric-breakdown.md), beyond the reversible linear model.
