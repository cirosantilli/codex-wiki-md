<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

The radial [force](../../../../../force-split.md) is $F_r=-dU/dr=-3Ar^2$. A nontrivial [circular orbit](../../../../../circular-orbit.md) therefore requires $A>0$, so that the force is inward. Balancing its magnitude against the required [centripetal acceleration](../../../../../centripetal-acceleration.md) gives

$$
\frac{mv^2}{r}=3Ar^2,\qquad K=\frac12mv^2=\frac32Ar^3.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\frac{K}{U}=\frac32.}
$$

This is the [circular-orbit energy ratio in a power-law potential](../../../../../circular-orbit-energy-ratio-in-a-power-law-potential.md), $2K=rU'(r)=sU$ for $U=Ar^s$, with $s=3$. It uses the zero of [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) specified by $U=Ar^3$; adding a constant would change the ratio without changing the motion. For $A<0$, the force is outward and no such free circular orbit exists.
