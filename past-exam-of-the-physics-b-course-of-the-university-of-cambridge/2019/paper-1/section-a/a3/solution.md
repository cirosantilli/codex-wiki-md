<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

For [linear elasticity](../../../../../linear-elasticity.md) of an isotropic material, the [isotropic elastic modulus relation](../../../../../isotropic-elastic-modulus-relation.md) gives

$$
B=\frac{E}{3(1-2\nu)},\qquad
G_s=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu)}.
$$

The printed [Young's modulus](../../../../../young-s-modulus.md) is $E=10^{-3}\ \mathrm{GPa}=10^6\ \mathrm{Pa}$, and the printed [Poisson's ratio](../../../../../poisson-s-ratio.md) is $\nu=1.47$. Formal substitution gives

$$
B=-1.72\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa},\qquad
G_s=2.02\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}.
$$

With compressive [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) positive, the linear volume response is $\Delta V/V=-p/B$. Thus a thirty per cent reduction formally requires

$$
\boxed{p=0.30B=-5.15\times10^4\ \mathrm{Pa}=-0.509\ \mathrm{atm},\qquad
G_s=0.202\ \mathrm{MPa}.}
$$

Here $1\ \mathrm{atm}=101325\ \mathrm{Pa}$. The negative pressure is a formal tensile load, not the requested positive isotropic compression.

The original PDF really prints $1.47$. It is incompatible with a stable ordinary isotropic elastic solid: positive [bulk modulus](../../../../../bulk-modulus.md) and [shear modulus](../../../../../shear-modulus.md) require $-1<\nu<1/2$. Consequently there is no physically admissible positive-pressure answer with the stated constants. If $0.47$ was intended, the corresponding linear estimates would instead be

$$
\boxed{p\simeq1.67\times10^6\ \mathrm{Pa}=16.4\ \mathrm{atm},\qquad
G_s\simeq0.340\ \mathrm{MPa}\quad(\nu=0.47\ \text{assumed}).}
$$

That is an explicit alternative, not a correction established by the source. A thirty per cent volume strain is also outside a controlled small-strain approximation; accurate rubber behaviour at that deformation needs a finite-strain constitutive law. The initial volume cancels because the specified change is a fractional one.
