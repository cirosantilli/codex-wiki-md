The units supplied for 130 indicate [specific heat capacity](../../heat.md#specific-heat-capacity), so the block's total [heat capacity](../../heat.md#heat-capacity) is $C=mc=0.5(130)=65\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$. Treat it as constant, and the lake as a [thermal reservoir](../../thermal-equilibrium.md#heat-bath) at $T_0=283.15\,\mathrm K$. The initial block [temperature](../../temperature.md) is $T_i=373.15\,\mathrm K$.

Compute the block's [entropy](../../entropy.md) change using a reversible comparison path, even though the actual cooling is irreversible:

$$
\Delta S_{\mathrm{block}}=\int_{T_i}^{T_0}\frac{C\,dT}{T}
=C\ln\frac{T_0}{T_i}=-17.940\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$

The lake receives $Q=C(T_i-T_0)=5850\,\mathrm J$ at constant [temperature](../../temperature.md), so

$$
\Delta S_{\mathrm{lake}}=\frac Q{T_0}=20.660\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\Delta S_{\mathrm{Universe}}=2.72\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.}
$$

This is [entropy generated by cooling a finite body in a reservoir](../../entropy.md#entropy-generated-by-cooling-a-finite-body-in-a-reservoir). The positive total is consistent with the [Second law of thermodynamics](../../second-law-of-thermodynamics.md), although the cooling block's own [entropy](../../entropy.md) decreases.

<h4 id="section-a/a1/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A1](#section-a/a1)

<h5 id="section-a/a1/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-a/a1/b)

Initially and finally the block and lake are at the same [temperature](../../temperature.md). After the falling block has come to rest, its lost [gravitational potential energy](../../energy.md#gravitational-energy) has dissipated into [internal energy](../../internal-energy.md) of the lake and surroundings. In the idealization that all $mgh$ is ultimately thermalized in the lake at $T_0=283.15\,\mathrm K$,

$$
Q=mgh=(0.5)(9.81)(200)=981\,\mathrm J.
$$

The block returns to its original thermal state, so its net [entropy](../../entropy.md) change is zero. Mechanical [potential energy](../../energy.md#potential-energy) carries no thermal [entropy](../../entropy.md) that must be subtracted. Thus

$$
\boxed{\Delta S_{\mathrm{Universe}}=\frac{mgh}{T_0}=3.46\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.}
$$

Use of $g=9.80\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ gives the same three-significant-figure answer. Splashing and any temporary heating do not change this final-state balance if their mechanical [energy](../../energy.md) is eventually dissipated in the same reservoir.

<h3 id="section-a/a2">A2</h3>

<h4 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](#section-a/a2)

Three [phase coexistence curves](../../thermodynamic-potential.md#phase-coexistence-curve) meet at the [triple point](../../thermodynamic-potential.md#triple-point), where [ice](../../chemical-substance.md#ice), [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) and [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) vapour have equal [chemical potential](../../chemical-potential.md). In the ordinary-pressure region, the low-temperature phase is [ice](../../chemical-substance.md#ice), the high-pressure moderate-temperature phase is [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid), and the low-pressure high-temperature phase is vapour. The [triple point](../../thermodynamic-potential.md#triple-point) is approximately

$$
\boxed{T_t=273.16\,\mathrm K,\qquad p_t=611.7\,\mathrm{Pa}.}
$$

Below $p_t$, warming stable ordinary [ice](../../chemical-substance.md#ice) crosses the [sublimation](../../critical-phenomenon.md#sublimation) boundary directly into vapour; there is no intervening stable [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid). Above $p_t$, [melting](../../critical-phenomenon.md#melting) and then boiling can occur. The [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid)–vapour curve terminates at the [liquid-gas critical point](../../critical-phenomenon.md#liquid-gas-critical-point), approximately $647.1\,\mathrm K$ and $22.06\,\mathrm{MPa}$, beyond which there is no [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid)–vapour phase boundary.

The distinctive feature is the negative slope of the ordinary [ice](../../chemical-substance.md#ice)–[water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) [melting](../../critical-phenomenon.md#melting) line. The [Clapeyron equation](../../thermodynamic-potential.md#clausius-clapeyron-relation) gives

$$
\frac{dp}{dT}=\frac{\Delta s}{\Delta v}.
$$

[Melting](../../critical-phenomenon.md#melting) increases [entropy](../../entropy.md) but reduces [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) because ordinary [ice](../../chemical-substance.md#ice) is less dense than [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water), so $\Delta s>0$ and $\Delta v<0$. Increasing [pressure](../../pressure.md) consequently lowers the [melting](../../critical-phenomenon.md#melting) [temperature](../../temperature.md) locally. [Sublimation](../../critical-phenomenon.md#sublimation) and [vaporization](../../critical-phenomenon.md#vaporization) both increase [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) and [entropy](../../entropy.md), giving positive slopes. The following original schematic shows these signs; it is not a quantitative fit and omits the additional high-pressure [ice](../../chemical-substance.md#ice) polymorphs.

Assume an air-filled or evacuated lossless [electromagnetic waveguide](../../electromagnetic-wave.md#electromagnetic-waveguide), and a pulse bandwidth narrow enough to use its carrier's local [group velocity](../../dispersion-relation.md#group-velocity). The longitudinal [dispersion relation](../../dispersion-relation.md) is

$$
\omega^2=c^2k_z^2+\omega_c^2.
$$

It gives

$$
v_{\mathrm p}=\frac\omega{k_z}=\frac c{\sqrt{1-(f_c/f)^2}},
\qquad
v_{\mathrm g}=\frac{d\omega}{dk_z}=c\sqrt{1-(f_c/f)^2}.
$$

Here $f_c/f=20/40=1/2$, so

$$
\boxed{v_{\mathrm p}=\frac{2c}{\sqrt3}=3.46\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\qquad
v_{\mathrm g}=\frac{\sqrt3c}{2}=2.60\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The [phase velocity](../../dispersion-relation.md#phase-velocity) exceeds $c$, but the pulse envelope travels at the subluminal [group velocity](../../dispersion-relation.md#group-velocity); a moving constant-phase surface does not by itself transport information. Their product is $v_{\mathrm p}v_{\mathrm g}=c^2$. A broad pulse can distort because this [waveguide](../../wave.md#waveguide) is dispersive.

The original PDF calls $\epsilon_r$ a relative [magnetic permeability](../../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism) while also specifying a nonmagnetic [dielectric](../../permittivity.md#dielectric). These descriptions are inconsistent if read literally. The physically consistent interpretation of the notation is [relative permittivity](../../quantum-mechanics.md#relative-permittivity) $\epsilon_r=2$ and relative [magnetic permeability](../../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism) $\mu_r=1$.

Let $a=3\,\mathrm{mm}$ and let $b=6\,\mathrm{mm}$ be the inner radius of the outer conductor. For line charge density $\lambda$, [Gauss's law](../../maxwell-equations.md#gauss-s-law) gives $E_r=\lambda/(2\pi\epsilon r)$, hence

$$
V=\int_a^b E_r\,dr=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon}\ln\frac ba,
\qquad C'=\frac\lambda V=\frac{2\pi\epsilon_0\epsilon_r}{\ln(b/a)}.
$$

The [magnetic field](../../magnetic-field.md) in the [dielectric](../../permittivity.md#dielectric) is $H_\varphi=I/(2\pi r)$. Its [energy](../../energy.md) per unit length gives

$$
\frac12L'I^2=\int_a^b\frac{\mu H_\varphi^2}{2}\,2\pi r\,dr,
\qquad L'=\frac{\mu_0\mu_r}{2\pi}\ln\frac ba.
$$

Using $b/a=2$,

$$
\boxed{C'=1.61\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{F\,m^{-1}}=161\,\mathrm{pF\,m^{-1}},}
$$



$$
\boxed{L'=1.39\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{H\,m^{-1}}=139\,\mathrm{nH\,m^{-1}}.}
$$

These are the [lossless coaxial line parameters](../../electrical-engineering.md#lossless-coaxial-line-parameters), assuming negligible field penetration into the conductors. If the quoted radii instead referred to an outer diameter or if $\mu_r=2$ were intended, the data would need clarification; the stated [dielectric](../../permittivity.md#dielectric) and nonmagnetic assumptions support the interpretation above.

For an ideal [electrical transformer](../../electrical-engineering.md#transformer-electrical-engineering), let $n=N_p/N_s$. The reflected secondary load is $R_{\mathrm{in}}=n^2R_L$. Matching a $4\,\Omega$ load to an $8\,\Omega$ source therefore requires

$$
\boxed{n=\sqrt{\frac84}=\sqrt2.}
$$

With the generator replaced by its internal resistance, its resistance referred to the secondary is

$$
\boxed{R_{\mathrm{out}}=\left(\frac{N_s}{N_p}\right)^2(8\,\Omega)=4\,\Omega.}
$$

This is the source output resistance relevant to matching and maximum [power](../../energy.md#power) transfer.

To find the windings, use the [inductance of a toroidal winding](../../electromagnetism.md#inductance-of-a-toroidal-winding). The [mean](../../expected-value.md) magnetic path is $\ell=2\pi(0.10)\,\mathrm m$, the core [area](../../differential-geometry.md#surface-area) is $A=30\times10^{-4}=0.003\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $\mu=500\mu_0$. Thus

$$
L_p=\frac{\mu_0\mu_rN_p^2A}{\ell},
\qquad N_p=\sqrt{\frac{(0.75)2\pi(0.10)}{\mu_0(500)(0.003)}}=500.
$$

The ideal secondary turn count is $N_s=500/\sqrt2=353.55$, giving the practical nominal design

$$
\boxed{N_p=500,\qquad N_s\simeq354.}
$$

With integer turns the irrational ratio cannot be exact: $500:354$ reflects the $4\,\Omega$ load as $7.98\,\Omega$, an approximately $0.25\%$ resistance mismatch. These turn counts use the stated $0.75\,\mathrm H$ self-inductance; no operating [frequency](../../wave.md#frequency) was specified from which to set a separate minimum [inductance](../../electromagnetism.md#inductance).

The $200\,\Omega$ condition must refer to the unloaded magnetizing reactance, not to the matched loaded input resistance. With $L_p=0.75\,\mathrm H$,

$$
|X_p|=2\pi fL_p\geq200\,\Omega
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f\geq42.4\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

The secondary self-inductance is $L_s=L_p/n^2=0.375\,\mathrm H$, so its corresponding unloaded coil impedance satisfies

$$
\boxed{Z_{s,\mathrm{coil}}=i\omega L_s,\qquad |Z_{s,\mathrm{coil}}|\geq100\,\Omega.}
$$

If output impedance means this transformed coil reactance, $100\,\Omega$ is its minimum magnitude. It is distinct from the $4\,\Omega$ transformed source resistance. This is the [magnetizing reactance and reflected resistance in a transformer](../../electrical-engineering.md#magnetizing-reactance-and-reflected-resistance-in-a-transformer) distinction. A finite magnetizing [inductance](../../electromagnetism.md#inductance) adds a parallel branch, $Z_{\mathrm{in}}=(8\,\Omega)\parallel(i\omega L_p)$, so exact purely resistive matching is an ideal large-reactance approximation; interpreting the loaded input itself as both $8\,\Omega$ and at least $200\,\Omega$ would be impossible.

<h4 id="section-b/b6/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#section-b/b6)

<h5 id="section-b/b6/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#section-b/b6/a)

In a homogeneous linear medium, write $\mathbf D=\epsilon\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf B=\mu\mathbf H$. Without free charge or current, [Maxwell's equations](../../maxwell-equations.md) become

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,\quad
\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t},\quad
\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu\epsilon\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t}.
$$

Taking the [curl](../../multivariable-calculus.md#curl) of [Faraday's law](../../maxwell-equations.md#faraday-s-law-of-induction), using $\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf E)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E)-\nabla^2\mathbf E$, gives

$$
\boxed{\nabla^2\mathbf E-\mu\epsilon\,\partial_t^2\mathbf E=0,
\qquad\nabla^2\mathbf B-\mu\epsilon\,\partial_t^2\mathbf B=0.}
$$

Their plane-wave solutions have $\omega^2=k^2/(\mu\epsilon)$ and speed $v=1/\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}$; in vacuum this is $c=1/\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}$. For a wave $\operatorname{Re}[\mathbf E_0e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)}]$, the [divergence](../../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence) equations give $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf E_0=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf B_0=0$, while [Faraday's law](../../maxwell-equations.md#faraday-s-law-of-induction) gives $\mathbf B_0=\mathbf k\times\mathbf E_0/\omega$. Thus the fields and propagation direction form an [orthogonal](../../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) triad, with $B_0=E_0/v$.

The two independent transverse electric components allow linear, circular and elliptical [polarization of an electromagnetic wave](../../electromagnetic-wave.md#polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave). Their relative amplitudes and phase determine the polarization state. In a lossless nondispersive medium the [energy density](../../statistical-physics.md#energy-density) and [energy](../../energy.md) flux are

$$
u=\frac12\epsilon E^2+\frac{B^2}{2\mu},\qquad
\mathbf S=\mathbf E\times\mathbf H.
$$

A travelling [plane wave](../../wave-function.md#plane-wave) has equal electric and [magnetic energy](../../electromagnetic-wave.md#magnetic-energy) contributions and time-averaged intensity $\langle S\rangle=E_0^2/(2Z)$, where $Z=\sqrt{\mu/\epsilon}$ is its [wave impedance](../../electromagnetic-wave.md#wave-impedance) and $E_0$ denotes peak electric-field amplitude. Reflection and refraction follow by imposing the electromagnetic [boundary conditions](../../differential-equation.md#boundary-condition). Frequency-dependent constitutive response introduces dispersion; conductivity introduces attenuation, so the simple real constant-speed result has a specified domain of validity.

<h4 id="section-b/b6/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#section-b/b6)

<h5 id="section-b/b6/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-b/b6/b)

A [dielectric](../../permittivity.md#dielectric) responds to an [electric field](../../electric-field.md) mainly by displacing or orienting bound charges, rather than by sustaining a steady conduction current. The [polarization density](../../electromagnetism.md#polarization-density) $\mathbf P$ is [electric dipole moment](../../electrostatics.md#electric-dipole-moment) per unit [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics). It creates bound charge

$$
\rho_b=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P,\qquad\sigma_b=\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf n.
$$

Separating these charges from free charge motivates

$$
\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P,\qquad\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho_f.
$$

For an isotropic linear [dielectric](../../permittivity.md#dielectric), $\mathbf P=\epsilon_0\chi_e\mathbf E$, so

$$
\boxed{\mathbf D=\epsilon\mathbf E,\qquad\epsilon=\epsilon_0(1+\chi_e)=\epsilon_0\epsilon_r.}
$$

The [electric susceptibility](../../permittivity.md#electric-susceptibility) $\chi_e$ describes material polarization and is not the [magnetic susceptibility](../../statistical-physics.md#magnetic-susceptibility). More generally it is a [tensor](../../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor) and can depend on [frequency](../../wave.md#frequency), field strength and [temperature](../../temperature.md).

Electronic polarization displaces an [electron](../../physics.md#electron) cloud relative to its nucleus; ionic polarization displaces positive and negative ions; orientational polarization partially aligns permanent molecular dipoles against thermal disorder. For independent permanent moments of magnitude $p$, weak-field orientational response gives $\chi_e\simeq np^2/(3\epsilon_0k_BT)$ when local-field corrections and interactions are negligible. This explains a common inverse-temperature contribution without asserting that every [dielectric](../../permittivity.md#dielectric) follows that law.

At a static interface, $\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf D_2-\mathbf D_1)=\sigma_f$ and the tangential [electric field](../../electric-field.md) is continuous. Bound surface charge is already included through $\mathbf P$. For a homogeneous filled [capacitor](../../electromagnetism.md#capacitor), $C=\epsilon A/d$, and a reversible linear [dielectric](../../permittivity.md#dielectric) stores [energy density](../../statistical-physics.md#energy-density) $u=\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf D/2$. At fixed voltage, insertion raises the stored field [energy](../../energy.md) but the voltage source also supplies [energy](../../energy.md); [force](../../force.md) arguments must include that source. At fixed charge the [capacitor energy](../../electromagnetism.md#capacitor-energy) instead decreases on insertion.

At [frequencies](../../wave.md#frequency) too high for a polarization mechanism to follow the field, its contribution weakens. A complex [permittivity](../../permittivity.md) represents dissipative [dielectric loss](../../permittivity.md#dielectric-loss) as well as phase lag; the sign of its imaginary part depends on the time convention. Strong fields can cause nonlinear response or [dielectric breakdown](../../permittivity.md#dielectric-breakdown), beyond the reversible linear model.

<h4 id="section-b/b6/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#section-b/b6)

<h5 id="section-b/b6/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#section-b/b6/c)

In a homogeneous Ohmic conductor, $\mathbf J=\sigma\mathbf E$. Combining this with [Maxwell's equations](../../maxwell-equations.md) gives

$$
\nabla^2\mathbf E=\mu\sigma\,\partial_t\mathbf E+\mu\epsilon\,\partial_t^2\mathbf E.
$$

For a good conductor, $\sigma\gg\omega\epsilon$, so conduction dominates [displacement current](../../maxwell-equations.md#displacement-current). With time dependence $e^{-i\omega t}$ and depth $z>0$, take $E\propto e^{ikz}$. Then

$$
k^2=i\mu\sigma\omega,\qquad k=\frac{1+i}{\delta},
\qquad
\boxed{\delta=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\mu\sigma\omega}}.}
$$

The field is consequently proportional to $e^{-z/\delta}e^{iz/\delta}$: it both attenuates and changes phase with depth. The [skin depth](../../electric-charge.md#skin-depth) is the distance for amplitude to fall by $e^{-1}$; [power](../../energy.md#power) density falls as $e^{-2z/\delta}$, so its attenuation length is $\delta/2$.

Physically, changing fields induce currents whose [magnetic field](../../magnetic-field.md) opposes penetration. Equivalently, after neglecting [displacement current](../../maxwell-equations.md#displacement-current), [magnetic diffusion](../../magnetohydrodynamics.md#magnetic-diffusion) obeys $\partial_t\mathbf B=(\mu\sigma)^{-1}\nabla^2\mathbf B$. Faster changes have less time to diffuse into the metal. Increasing [frequency](../../wave.md#frequency), conductivity or [magnetic permeability](../../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism) reduces $\delta$, provided the relevant material parameters remain applicable.

For a thick conductor the tangential surface [electrical impedance](../../electrical-circuit.md#electrical-impedance) is $Z_s=(1-i)/(\sigma\delta)$ with the stated time convention and consistent field orientation. Its real part $1/(\sigma\delta)$ represents dissipative resistance per square. Current crowding near the surface raises the AC resistance of a wire when $\delta$ is small compared with its radius; thin strands and laminated magnetic cores reduce suitable eddy-current losses. When $\sigma\not\gg\omega\epsilon$, the full complex propagation constant must replace the good-conductor approximation. In a magnetic metal the [magnetic permeability](../../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism) can itself be nonlinear and [frequency](../../wave.md#frequency) dependent.

<h4 id="section-b/b6/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#section-b/b6)

<h5 id="section-b/b6/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#section-b/b6/d)

A [transmission line](../../electrical-engineering.md#transmission-line) must be treated as a distributed circuit when its propagation time is significant on the [signal](../../electrical-engineering.md#signal) timescale. Let $R',L',G',C'$ denote resistance, [inductance](../../electromagnetism.md#inductance), leakage conductance and [capacitance](../../electromagnetism.md#capacitance) per unit length. Applying [Kirchhoff's voltage law](../../random-walk.md#kirchhoff-cycle-law) and [Kirchhoff's current law](../../random-walk.md#kirchhoff-node-law) to a short length $dx$ gives the [telegrapher's equations](../../electrical-engineering.md#telegrapher-s-equations)

$$
\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}=-R'I-L'\frac{\partial I}{\partial t},\qquad
\frac{\partial I}{\partial x}=-G'V-C'\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}.
$$

For a lossless line, $R'=G'=0$. Differentiating again gives [wave equations](../../wave-equation.md) with

$$
\boxed{v=\frac1{\sqrt{L'C'}},\qquad Z_0=\sqrt{\frac{L'}{C'}}.}
$$

The travelling solutions can be written $V=V_+(t-x/v)+V_-(t+x/v)$ and $I=[V_+(t-x/v)-V_-(t+x/v)]/Z_0$. The minus sign for the backward wave is essential to [power](../../energy.md#power) transport.

At a load $Z_L$, continuity of voltage and current gives the voltage [reflection coefficient](../../partial-differential-equation.md#reflection-coefficient)

$$
\boxed{\Gamma_L=\frac{Z_L-Z_0}{Z_L+Z_0}.}
$$

A matched termination $Z_L=Z_0$ absorbs the incident [signal](../../electrical-engineering.md#signal) without reflection. An open circuit has $\Gamma_L=+1$ and a short circuit has $\Gamma_L=-1$. Superposed incident and reflected waves produce [standing waves](../../mathematical-physics.md#standing-wave). A sinusoidal lossless line of length $\ell$ transforms the termination into

$$
Z_{\mathrm{in}}=Z_0\frac{Z_L+iZ_0\tan(k\ell)}{Z_0+iZ_L\tan(k\ell)}
$$

for the $e^{i\omega t}$ convention. At a quarter [wavelength](../../wave-equation.md#wavelength), $Z_{\mathrm{in}}=Z_0^2/Z_L$, allowing impedance transformation. A coaxial line supports a transverse electromagnetic mode whose ideal $L',C'$ are calculated in A4. Its [energy](../../energy.md) travels in the fields between conductors; the [characteristic impedance](../../electrical-engineering.md#characteristic-impedance) is not the wire's DC resistance. Losses introduce attenuation and make the [characteristic impedance](../../electrical-engineering.md#characteristic-impedance) and propagation constant complex.

**[Magnetization](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetization) and field response.** A magnetic material contains microscopic magnetic moments associated with [electron](../../physics.md#electron) [spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) and orbital motion. The [magnetization](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetization) $\mathbf M$ is [magnetic dipole moment](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-dipole-moment) per unit [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics). In SI the macroscopic fields satisfy

$$
\mathbf B=\mu_0(\mathbf H+\mathbf M).
$$

Here $\mathbf H$ separates free-current excitation from the material response. A uniform isotropic linear material has $\mathbf M=\chi_m\mathbf H$, giving

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B=\mu\mathbf H,\qquad\mu_r=1+\chi_m.}
$$

This constant scalar [magnetic permeability](../../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism) model is useful only within its linear range. Crystals can have a susceptibility [tensor](../../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor); strongly magnetic materials depend on field and magnetic history. For a finite specimen the internal $\mathbf H$ also includes its demagnetizing field. In a uniformly magnetized ellipsoid, $H_{\mathrm{int}}=H_{\mathrm{applied}}-N_dM$ along a [principal axis](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#principal-axis), so sample shape affects the apparent susceptibility.

**Diamagnetic and paramagnetic response.** [Diamagnetism](../../electromagnetism.md#diamagnetism) is a weak negative susceptibility caused by induced orbital response opposing the applied field. It occurs in all matter but can be masked by larger positive contributions. Closed-shell materials such as bismuth or many organic compounds show it clearly. Ordinary [diamagnetism](../../electromagnetism.md#diamagnetism) is often only weakly [temperature](../../temperature.md) dependent; superconducting flux exclusion is a separate collective phenomenon and is not described by an arbitrarily small negative susceptibility.

[Paramagnetism](../../electromagnetism.md#paramagnetism) arises when available magnetic moments partially align with a field. Thermal disorder prevents complete alignment at weak fields. For independent localized moments of magnitude $m$, the classical weak-field result is

$$
M\simeq\frac{nm^2B}{3k_BT},\qquad
\chi_m\simeq\frac{\mu_0nm^2}{3k_BT},
$$

where the second expression uses $B\simeq\mu_0H$ in a weakly magnetic sample. Thus localized moments obey a [Curie law](../../statistical-physics.md#curie-s-law) in this regime. Quantum [angular momentum](../../momentum.md#angular-momentum) changes the coefficient to the appropriate $J(J+1)$ expression and gives saturation at strong field. Conduction [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) instead exhibit [Pauli paramagnetism](../../quantum-ideal-gas-statistics.md#pauli-paramagnetism): only states near the [Fermi surface](../../fermi-liquid.md#fermi-surface) can change [spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) populations, so a degenerate gas has a nearly temperature-independent response. Its orbital [diamagnetism](../../electromagnetism.md#diamagnetism) must also be included for a full metallic susceptibility.

**Collective magnetic order.** [Exchange interactions](../../electromagnetism.md#exchange-interaction) can favor parallel alignment, producing [ferromagnetism](../../electromagnetism.md#ferromagnetism) below the [Curie temperature](../../electromagnetism.md#curie-temperature). Iron, cobalt and nickel are familiar examples. A material may contain regions of spontaneous [magnetization](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetization), or [magnetic domains](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-domain), while having little net external moment because the domains point in different directions. Applied fields move domain walls and rotate moments; at large fields the [magnetization](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetization) approaches saturation. Pinning and [energy](../../energy.md) barriers make this process hysteretic. [Remanence](../../electromagnetism.md#remanent-magnetization) is the [magnetization](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetization) left after removing the driving field, and [coercivity](../../real-analysis.md#coercive-function) measures the reverse field needed to bring the chosen response back to zero.

The [area](../../differential-geometry.md#surface-area) of a closed $B$–$H$ loop is the [energy](../../energy.md) dissipated per unit [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) per quasistatic cycle, $\oint H\,dB$, with positive dissipated magnitude. Consequently the [magnetic permeability](../../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism) is neither one universal constant nor enough information to describe a ferromagnet: differential [magnetic permeability](../../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism) $dB/dH$, saturation and [hysteresis](../../critical-phenomenon.md#hysteresis) all matter. Above the ordering [temperature](../../temperature.md) the spontaneous moment disappears, and a Curie–Weiss law is often an appropriate approximate paramagnetic description away from the critical region.

In [antiferromagnetism](../../electromagnetism.md#antiferromagnetism), ordered opposing sublattices have equal moments and cancel macroscopically in the ideal compensated case. The order disappears above the [Néel temperature](../../electromagnetism.md#neel-temperature). In [ferrimagnetism](../../electromagnetism.md#ferrimagnetism), opposing sublattices have unequal moments and leave a net [magnetization](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetization). Ferrites combine ferrimagnetic response with relatively high electrical resistivity, making them valuable when eddy-current losses would be substantial in a metallic core.

**[Electromagnets](../../magnetic-field.md#electromagnet) and engineering choice.** A coil supplies free-current magnetomotive [force](../../force.md) $NI$. For a simple uniform magnetic path, [Ampère's law](../../maxwell-equations.md#ampere-s-circuital-law) gives $H\ell\simeq NI$ and flux $\Phi\simeq\mu ANI/\ell$. A high-[magnetic permeability](../../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism) soft magnetic core concentrates flux and reduces the current required for a given field. With an air gap $g$,

$$
NI\simeq\frac{B\ell_c}{\mu_0\mu_r}+\frac{Bg}{\mu_0}.
$$

Even a short gap can dominate the [magnetic reluctance](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-reluctance). The approximate attractive [force](../../force.md) across one uniform gap is $F\simeq B^2A/(2\mu_0)$, obtained from the field [energy](../../energy.md), with fringing neglected.

Soft magnetic materials have small [coercivity](../../real-analysis.md#coercive-function) and narrow [hysteresis](../../critical-phenomenon.md#hysteresis) loops; they suit relays, [electrical transformer](../../electrical-engineering.md#transformer-electrical-engineering) cores and controllable [electromagnets](../../magnetic-field.md#electromagnet). Hard magnetic materials retain their [magnetization](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetization) and suit [permanent magnets](../../electromagnetism.md#permanent-magnet). Laminating metallic cores breaks large eddy-current loops; ferrites are useful at higher [frequencies](../../wave.md#frequency) because of their resistivity. Increasing current cannot increase $B$ indefinitely through the linear formula: core saturation, coil heating and leakage limit performance. A good design selects material for the field amplitude, switching [frequency](../../wave.md#frequency) and acceptable loss, rather than simply choosing the largest quoted [magnetic permeability](../../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism).

Let the [rotating frame](../../mathematical-physics.md#rotating-reference-frame) have origin [acceleration](../../classical-mechanics.md#acceleration) $\mathbf A_0$ and [angular velocity](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#angular-velocity) $\boldsymbol\Omega(t)$ relative to an [inertial frame](../../mathematical-physics.md#inertial-frame). For a [vector](../../vector-space.md#vector) referred to the moving axes, the transport rule is

$$
\left(\frac{d\mathbf a}{dt}\right)_{\mathrm I}
=\left(\frac{d\mathbf a}{dt}\right)_{\mathrm R}+\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf a.
$$

Apply it twice to position. With rotating-frame [velocity](../../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) $\mathbf v$ and [acceleration](../../classical-mechanics.md#acceleration) $\mathbf a_R$,

$$
\mathbf a_I=\mathbf A_0+\mathbf a_R+2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v
+\dot{\boldsymbol\Omega}\times\mathbf r
+\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r).
$$

Thus Newton's equation expressed in the [rotating frame](../../mathematical-physics.md#rotating-reference-frame) is

$$
\boxed{m\mathbf a_R=\mathbf F_{\mathrm{real}}-m\mathbf A_0
-2m\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v-m\dot{\boldsymbol\Omega}\times\mathbf r
-m\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r).}
$$

The added terms are translational, Coriolis, Euler and centrifugal [fictitious forces](../../mathematical-physics.md#fictitious-force). They reflect the frame's [acceleration](../../classical-mechanics.md#acceleration), rather than additional interactions with nearby objects.

The [centrifugal force](../../mathematical-physics.md#centrifugal-force) points outward from the [rotation](../../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) axis and, for constant $\boldsymbol\Omega$, has potential $-m|\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r|^2/2$. The [Coriolis force](../../mathematical-physics.md#coriolis-force) is perpendicular to [velocity](../../classical-mechanics.md#velocity), so does no [work](../../energy.md#work) on the particle measured in that [rotating frame](../../mathematical-physics.md#rotating-reference-frame); it changes direction rather than speed directly. The [Euler force](../../mathematical-physics.md#euler-force) is present when the frame's [rotation](../../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) rate or direction changes.

On Earth, gravity and [centrifugal acceleration](../../mathematical-physics.md#centrifugal-acceleration) combine into effective gravity. For slow horizontal motion at latitude $\lambda$, the vertical component of Earth's [rotation](../../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) gives Coriolis parameter $f=2\Omega\sin\lambda$: an initially northward motion deflects eastward in the northern hemisphere. This supplies a useful local explanation of rotating flows and Foucault [precession](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#precession). A bucket of rotating [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) illustrates the [centrifugal potential](../../mathematical-physics.md#centrifugal-potential): its steady free surface is an equipotential of $gz-\Omega^2r^2/2$, giving $z=\Omega^2r^2/(2g)+\mathrm{constant}$. These equations require a declared frame; adding a [centrifugal force](../../mathematical-physics.md#centrifugal-force) while retaining inertial-frame [acceleration](../../classical-mechanics.md#acceleration) would count the same kinematic effect twice.

<h4 id="section-b/b8/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B8](#section-b/b8)

<h5 id="section-b/b8/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-b/b8/b)

For a slender [elastic beam](../../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-beam) undergoing small bending, cross-sections remain approximately plane and perpendicular to the centreline. At distance $z$ from the [neutral axis](../../elasticity-physics.md#neutral-axis), longitudinal [strain](../../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) is $\epsilon_{xx}=-z\kappa$, where $\kappa$ is [curvature](../../differential-geometry.md#curvature). [Linear elasticity](../../elasticity-physics.md#linear-elasticity) gives $\sigma_{xx}=E\epsilon_{xx}$, so the [bending moment](../../elasticity-physics.md#bending-moment) has magnitude

$$
M=EI\kappa,\qquad I=\int_A z^2\,dA.
$$

Here $I$ is the second moment of cross-sectional [area](../../differential-geometry.md#surface-area), not a [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) [moment of inertia](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#moment-of-inertia). For width $b$ and depth $h$ in the bending direction, $I=bh^3/12$. The cubic depth dependence explains why a deep beam resists bending more effectively than the same material spread sideways.

For a uniform beam with transverse [displacement](../../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) $y(x)$, $\kappa\simeq y''$ and bending [energy](../../energy.md) is $\tfrac12\int EI(y'')^2dx$. Balancing transverse load $q(x)$ yields the [Euler-Bernoulli beam equation](../../elasticity-physics.md#euler-bernoulli-beam-equation) $EIy''''=q$ under a consistent sign convention. Supports specify values of deflection, slope, moment or shear; the equation alone does not fix the response.

Now apply a compressive end load $P>0$. A small deflection shortens the axial projection by $\tfrac12\int(y')^2dx$, so the total [potential energy](../../energy.md#potential-energy) relative to the straight configuration is

$$
\Pi[y]=\frac12\int_0^\ell\left[EI(y'')^2-P(y')^2\right]dx.
$$

Its stationary equation is

$$
EIy''''+Py''=0.
$$

For pinned ends, $y(0)=y(\ell)=0$ and the end moments vanish, so $y''(0)=y''(\ell)=0$. The candidate shapes $y=A\sin(n\pi x/\ell)$ require $P=EI(n\pi/\ell)^2$. The first loss of stability is consequently

$$
\boxed{P_{\mathrm{cr}}=\frac{\pi^2EI}{\ell^2}\quad\text{for a pinned–pinned strut}.}
$$

At smaller $P$, the quadratic [energy](../../energy.md) is positive; beyond this threshold the straight configuration no longer minimizes it. End restraint changes the threshold: clamped–clamped gives $4\pi^2EI/\ell^2$ and clamped–free gives $\pi^2EI/(4\ell^2)$, equivalently an effective-length correction. A column buckles in the direction of smaller flexural rigidity. Euler buckling is a geometric instability within elastic response; yielding or crushing can occur first for a short thick strut. Large deflections, shear deformation and imperfections require refinements of the ideal slender-beam model.

<h4 id="section-b/b8/c">c</h4>

<h5 id="section-b/b8/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#section-b/b8/c)

Take a fixed [sphere](../../euclidean-geometry.md#sphere) of radius $R$ in an unbounded incompressible inviscid fluid, with uniform [velocity](../../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) $U\mathbf e_z$ at infinity. Assume [irrotational flow](../../vorticity.md#irrotational-flow), so $\mathbf v=\nabla\phi$ and [incompressibility](../../fluid-mechanics.md#incompressible-flow) gives $\nabla^2\phi=0$. Axisymmetry and the dipolar angular dependence of the far field suggest

$$
\phi=(Ar+B/r^2)\cos\theta.
$$

Matching the far field gives $A=U$. The no-penetration condition $v_r(R)=0$ then gives $B=UR^3/2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\phi=U\left(r+\frac{R^3}{2r^2}\right)\cos\theta,}
$$



$$
v_r=U\left(1-\frac{R^3}{r^3}\right)\cos\theta,\qquad
v_\theta=-U\left(1+\frac{R^3}{2r^3}\right)\sin\theta.
$$

The surface has [stagnation points](../../fluid-mechanics.md#stagnation-point) at its front and rear poles, while the equatorial speed is $3U/2$. Fluid slips tangentially past the [sphere](../../euclidean-geometry.md#sphere); imposing a no-slip condition would contradict this inviscid potential-flow model.

With gravity omitted or the hydrostatic [pressure](../../pressure.md) subtracted, steady Bernoulli gives

$$
p+\frac12\rho v^2=p_\infty+\frac12\rho U^2.
$$

At the surface,

$$
\boxed{p(R,\theta)=p_\infty+\frac12\rho U^2
\left(1-\frac94\sin^2\theta\right).}
$$

The front–rear symmetry makes the [integral](../../integral.md) of [pressure](../../pressure.md) [force](../../force.md) along the flow vanish: terms proportional to $\cos\theta$ integrate to zero over the [sphere](../../euclidean-geometry.md#sphere). This is [D'Alembert's paradox](../../fluid-mechanics.md#d-alembert-s-paradox), not a prediction that real spheres have no [drag](../../fluid-mechanics.md#drag-physics). [Viscosity](../../dynamic-viscosity.md) creates [boundary layers](../../continuum-mechanics.md#boundary-layer); separation and a wake break the fore–aft symmetry. [Potential flow](../../fluid-mechanics.md#potential-flow) remains valuable for the outer [velocity](../../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) field when viscous regions are thin, but it cannot supply their [drag](../../fluid-mechanics.md#drag-physics) by itself.

<h4 id="section-b/b8/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B8](#section-b/b8)

<h5 id="section-b/b8/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#section-b/b8/d)

A spatially uniform external [gravitational acceleration](../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#gravitational-acceleration) moves a body's [centre of mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#center-of-mass) without stretching it. [Tidal forces](../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#tidal-force) arise from the variation of that [acceleration](../../classical-mechanics.md#acceleration) across the body. Let a distant [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) $M$ lie at distance $D$ along [unit vector](../../vector-space.md#unit-vector) $\mathbf n$, and let $|\mathbf r|\ll D$ denote position relative to the body's centre. Subtract the centre's [acceleration](../../classical-mechanics.md#acceleration). To first order,

$$
\boxed{\mathbf a_{\mathrm{tidal}}=\frac{GM}{D^3}
\left[3(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r)\mathbf n-\mathbf r\right].}
$$

The [tidal tensor](../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#tidal-tensor) is $T_{ij}=GM(3n_in_j-\delta_{ij})/D^3$. It stretches along the line to the source with [eigenvalue](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $2GM/D^3$ and compresses in the two perpendicular directions with [eigenvalues](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $-GM/D^3$. Its [trace](../../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) vanishes outside the source, consistently with [Laplace's equation](../../partial-differential-equation.md#laplace-equation) for the [gravitational potential](../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass).

After removal of the constant and uniform-acceleration terms, the tidal potential is

$$
\Phi_{\mathrm{tidal}}=-\frac{GM}{2D^3}
\left[3(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r)^2-r^2\right].
$$

An ideal equilibrium ocean on a [sphere](../../euclidean-geometry.md#sphere) of radius $R$ follows an equipotential of the body's gravity plus this perturbation. For surface gravity $g$, the leading [displacement](../../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) is

$$
\delta h(\psi)=-\frac{\Phi_{\mathrm{tidal}}(R,\psi)}g
=\frac{GMR^2}{2gD^3}(3\cos^2\psi-1),
$$

up to a constant fixing total [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics). The quadrupolar form produces bulges on both the near and far sides; the far-side bulge follows from differential [acceleration](../../classical-mechanics.md#acceleration), without postulating a repulsive gravitational [force](../../force.md).

The Moon and Sun both raise terrestrial [tides](../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#tide), with relative strength set mainly by $M/D^3$, rather than by the total gravitational pull $M/D^2$. Their contributions reinforce near new and full Moon to produce [spring tides](../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#spring-tide), and partly cancel near quadrature to produce [neap tides](../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#neap-tide). Earth's [rotation](../../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) relative to the tidal pattern gives a strong semidiurnal component in the simplest geometry. Real [tide](../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#tide) heights and timing depend on ocean depth, coastlines, basin resonances, [Coriolis acceleration](../../mathematical-physics.md#coriolis-acceleration), friction and the declination of the forcing bodies. [Tidal dissipation](../../planetary-science.md#tidal-dissipation) produces phase lags and [torques](../../momentum.md#torque), exchanging rotational and [orbital angular momentum](../../quantum-mechanics.md#orbital-angular-momentum) and gradually altering the [spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) and orbit. The equilibrium bulge is therefore a useful starting model, not an exact local [tide](../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#tide) forecast.

<h3 id="section-b/b9">B9</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section B](#section-b)

<h4 id="section-b/b9/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B9](#section-b/b9)

**Inertia is a [tensor](../../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor).** For a [rigid body](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md), distances between material points are fixed. Write each position relative to the [centre of mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#center-of-mass) as $\mathbf r$, so its rotational [velocity](../../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) is $\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf r$. Integrating $\mathbf r\times(\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf r)$ gives [angular momentum](../../momentum.md#angular-momentum)

$$
L_i=I_{ij}\omega_j,\qquad
I_{ij}=\int(r^2\delta_{ij}-r_ir_j)\,dm.
$$

The [rotational kinetic energy](../../energy.md#rotational-kinetic-energy) is

$$
T_{\mathrm{rot}}=\frac12\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\mathbf I\boldsymbol\omega.
$$

The [inertia tensor](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#inertia-tensor) is real and symmetric, so [orthogonal](../../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) [principal axes](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#principal-axis) diagonalize it. [Angular momentum](../../momentum.md#angular-momentum) is parallel to [angular velocity](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#angular-velocity) only along a [principal axis](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#principal-axis), or within a subspace of equal principal moments. For translation plus [rotation](../../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) about the [centre of mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#center-of-mass), $T=Mv_{\mathrm{CM}}^2/2+T_{\mathrm{rot}}$. Moving the reference to a parallel axis adds $M(d^2\mathbf1-\mathbf d\mathbf d^{\mathsf T})$ to the [tensor](../../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor), the [tensor](../../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor) form of the parallel-axis theorem.

**Euler's equations.** The external [torque](../../momentum.md#torque) about the [centre of mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#center-of-mass) obeys $\boldsymbol\tau=(d\mathbf L/dt)_{\mathrm{space}}$. In body axes, the transport rule gives

$$
\boldsymbol\tau=\left(\frac{d\mathbf L}{dt}\right)_{\mathrm{body}}+\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf L.
$$

The principal moments are constant in that frame, hence

$$
\boxed{I_1\dot\omega_1+(I_3-I_2)\omega_2\omega_3=\tau_1,}
$$



$$
I_2\dot\omega_2+(I_1-I_3)\omega_3\omega_1=\tau_2,\qquad
I_3\dot\omega_3+(I_2-I_1)\omega_1\omega_2=\tau_3.
$$

These are [Euler's equations](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#euler-s-equations-rigid-body-dynamics). With zero [torque](../../momentum.md#torque), spatial [angular momentum](../../momentum.md#angular-momentum) and [kinetic energy](../../energy.md#kinetic-energy) are conserved, although the body components generally vary. For distinct moments, small perturbations about [rotation](../../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) around the largest or smallest principal moment oscillate, whereas those about the intermediate moment grow. This is the intermediate-axis instability.

**A lamina.** Choose the lamina's plane as $z=0$. Then $I_{xz}=I_{yz}=0$, so the normal is a [principal axis](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#principal-axis), and

$$
\boxed{I_z=\int(x^2+y^2)dm=I_x+I_y.}
$$

This perpendicular-axis theorem applies to a planar [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) distribution; it is not a general identity for a three-dimensional body. Rotate the in-plane axes to diagonalize the remaining symmetric two-by-two block. For a uniform rectangular lamina with sides $a,b$ and [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) $M$, its central principal moments are $I_x=Mb^2/12$, $I_y=Ma^2/12$ and $I_z=M(a^2+b^2)/12$. If $a>b$, then $I_x<I_y<I_z$: torque-free [rotation](../../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) about the long in-plane axis or the normal is stable, while that about the short in-plane axis is unstable. Coincident moments in a square require the degenerate, rather than strict intermediate-axis, case. Under a constraint to rotate only about the normal, the motion reduces to the scalar equation $\tau_z=I_z\dot\omega_z$.

**A [symmetric top](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#symmetric-top) without [torque](../../momentum.md#torque).** Let $I_1=I_2=I_\perp$ and $I_3=I_\parallel$. Euler's equations reduce to

$$
\dot\omega_3=0,\qquad
\dot\omega_1=-\nu\omega_2,\qquad
\dot\omega_2=\nu\omega_1,
\qquad \nu=\frac{I_\parallel-I_\perp}{I_\perp}\omega_3.
$$

Thus $\omega_1+i\omega_2$ rotates at signed body-frame [frequency](../../wave.md#frequency) $\nu$, while the axial component stays fixed. The symmetry axis has constant angle to fixed spatial [angular momentum](../../momentum.md#angular-momentum). Indeed $\mathbf L=I_\perp\boldsymbol\omega+(I_\parallel-I_\perp)\omega_3\mathbf e_3$, so

$$
\dot{\mathbf e}_3=\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf e_3
=\frac{\mathbf L}{I_\perp}\times\mathbf e_3.
$$

It precesses in space about $\mathbf L$ with angular speed $L/I_\perp$. This spatial [precession](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#precession) and the body-frame [frequency](../../wave.md#frequency) $\nu$ describe different motions and should not be identified.

**A heavy top.** If a [symmetric top](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#symmetric-top) is pivoted a distance $\ell$ from its [centre of mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#center-of-mass), gravity gives [torque](../../momentum.md#torque) $\mathbf r_{\mathrm{CM}}\times M\mathbf g$. Here the relevant moments are about the fixed pivot. The axial [angular momentum](../../momentum.md#angular-momentum) and the vertical angular-momentum component are conserved, because gravity supplies neither an axial [torque](../../momentum.md#torque) nor a [torque](../../momentum.md#torque) about the vertical. Using tilt $\theta$, [precession](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#precession) angle $\phi$ and [spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) angle $\psi$, its [Lagrangian](../../calculus-of-variations.md#lagrangian) is

$$
\mathcal L=\frac12I_\perp(\dot\theta^2+\dot\phi^2\sin^2\theta)
+\frac12I_\parallel(\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta)^2-Mg\ell\cos\theta.
$$

Eliminating the two conserved cyclic momenta leaves one-dimensional motion in an effective tilt potential; bounded oscillation in tilt is [nutation](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#nutation). Steady [precession](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#precession) at $\dot\phi=\Omega$ and fixed tilt satisfies

$$
I_\perp\Omega^2\cos\theta-L_3\Omega+Mg\ell=0.
$$

For a rapidly spinning top on the slow branch, the first term is small and

$$
\boxed{\Omega\simeq\frac{Mg\ell}{L_3}=\frac{Mg\ell}{I_\parallel\omega_3}.}
$$

Gravity then changes the direction of [angular momentum](../../momentum.md#angular-momentum) rather than simply tipping the top straight down. The approximation fails near conditions where [nutation](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#nutation) or the fast-precession branch matters; the full equations retain those possibilities.

**[Pressure](../../pressure.md) and its physical terms.** Solving the molar [Van der Waals equation](../../pressure.md#van-der-waals-equation) for [pressure](../../pressure.md) gives

$$
\boxed{P=\frac{RT}{V-b}-\frac a{V^2},\qquad V>b.}
$$

The excluded-volume parameter $b$ reduces the [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) available for translational motion, raising the kinetic [pressure](../../pressure.md) relative to $RT/V$. The attraction parameter $a$ lowers the [pressure](../../pressure.md) because attractive interactions reduce the momentum transferred to the boundary. This mean-field model treats $a,b$ as temperature-independent material constants. For one mole, $V$ is the sample [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) with the molar amount already fixed; $b$ has units of that [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) and $a$ of [pressure](../../pressure.md) times [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) squared.

**[volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) dependence of [entropy](../../entropy.md).** The [Helmholtz free energy](../../thermodynamic-potential.md#helmholtz-free-energy) has differential $dF=-S\,dT-P\,dV$. Equality of mixed [derivatives](../../calculus.md#derivative) yields the [Maxwell relation](../../thermodynamics.md#maxwell-relations)

$$
\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial V}\right)_T
=\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_V.
$$

For $a=0$ this [derivative](../../calculus.md#derivative) is $R/(V-b)$, giving

$$
\boxed{S(V,T)-S(V_*,T)=R\ln\frac{V-b}{V_*-b},\qquad V,V_*>b.}
$$

Equivalently, $S=R\ln(V-b)+f(T)$ with a common reference [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) understood inside the [logarithm](../../calculus.md#logarithm). The [equation of state](../../pressure.md#equation-of-state) fixes this [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) dependence, not the otherwise unspecified function of [temperature](../../temperature.md).

**Excess [entropy](../../entropy.md) at fixed [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) and [temperature](../../temperature.md).** The [ideal gas](../../ideal-gas.md) has $(\partial S_{\mathrm{id}}/\partial V)_T=R/V$. Therefore

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\Delta S^{\mathrm{ex}}}{\partial V}\right)_T
=R\left(\frac1{V-b}-\frac1V\right).
$$

Integrate from infinite [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics), where the [entropy](../../entropy.md) difference is specified to vanish:

$$
\Delta S^{\mathrm{ex}}(V,T)
=\int_\infty^V R\left(\frac1{v-b}-\frac1v\right)dv
=\boxed{R\ln\left(1-\frac bV\right).}
$$

It is negative because excluding configurations reduces the accessible state count. It tends to zero as $V\to\infty$ and has leading dilute limit $-Rb/V$. This is the [entropy excess of a van der Waals gas](../../pressure.md#entropy-excess-of-a-van-der-waals-gas).

**Effect of attraction.** Restoring a temperature-independent $a$ changes the [pressure](../../pressure.md) by $-a/V^2$, but not its [derivative](../../calculus.md#derivative) with respect to [temperature](../../temperature.md). With the same infinite-volume [entropy](../../entropy.md) reference, it therefore does not change $\Delta S^{\mathrm{ex}}$ at fixed $V,T$. This can also be seen from

$$
F_{\mathrm{ex}}=-RT\ln(1-b/V)-\frac aV,
\qquad S_{\mathrm{ex}}=-\left(\frac{\partial F_{\mathrm{ex}}}{\partial T}\right)_V.
$$

The attraction affects [internal energy](../../internal-energy.md) and the equilibrium [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics), so it can affect [entropy](../../entropy.md) indirectly when different state points are compared. If $a$ were [temperature](../../temperature.md) dependent, its [derivative](../../calculus.md#derivative) would contribute directly to the [entropy](../../entropy.md); that is outside the stipulated constant-parameter model.

**Coexistence isotherm.** Below the [Van der Waals critical point](../../pressure.md#van-der-waals-critical-point), the formal homogeneous isotherm has a local minimum and maximum. Its middle branch has $\partial P/\partial V>0$, hence negative [isothermal compressibility](../../thermodynamics.md#isothermal-compressibility), and is mechanically unstable. Portions of the negative-slope branches can be [metastable](../../critical-phenomenon.md#metastability) rather than globally stable. The equilibrium isotherm replaces the loop between [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) $V_l$ and vapour [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) $V_g$ by a constant saturation [pressure](../../pressure.md) $P_s$. The two [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) are fixed by

$$
P(V_l,T)=P(V_g,T)=P_s,
\qquad
\boxed{\int_{V_l}^{V_g}[P(V,T)-P_s]dV=0.}
$$

The [Maxwell equal-area construction](../../thermodynamic-potential.md#maxwell-construction) expresses equality of the two phases' chemical potentials. Intermediate total [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) are mixtures: changing the vapour fraction changes total [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) at the same [pressure](../../pressure.md), rather than following the unstable homogeneous curve. The [spinodals](../../critical-phenomenon.md#spinodal) are the turning points of the formal isotherm; they are distinct from the coexistence endpoints.

**Dense-phase [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) at low [temperature](../../temperature.md).** At a general subcritical [temperature](../../temperature.md), the two coexistence equations above must be solved together. For this [equation of state](../../pressure.md#equation-of-state) the [area](../../differential-geometry.md#surface-area) condition is explicitly

$$
RT\ln\frac{V_g-b}{V_l-b}
+a\left(\frac1{V_g}-\frac1{V_l}\right)
=P_s(V_g-V_l).
$$

This supplies an exact implicit determination of the [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) even when a simple closed form is unavailable.

For the asymptotically low-temperature limit, put $\tau=RTb/a\ll1$. The [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) is dense, $V_l\simeq b$, while the vapour is dilute, $P_sV_g\simeq RT$ and $V_g\gg V_l$. The [area](../../differential-geometry.md#surface-area) condition then gives

$$
\ln\frac{V_g}{V_l-b}\simeq1+\frac{a}{RTV_l}.
$$

Thus the vapour [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) grows exponentially in $1/\tau$, and the saturation [pressure](../../pressure.md) is exponentially small. It is consequently consistent to neglect $P_s$ in the [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) equation to algebraic order in $\tau$:

$$
0\simeq\frac{RT}{V_l-b}-\frac a{V_l^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad RTV_l^2-aV_l+ab\simeq0.
$$

Choose the root near $b$, not the large-volume zero-pressure root:

$$
\boxed{V_l\simeq\frac a{2RT}\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4RTb}{a}}\right)
=\frac{2b}{1+\sqrt{1-4\tau}}.}
$$

Expanding gives the leading requested dense-phase behavior,

$$
\boxed{V_l=b+\frac{RTb^2}{a}+O(b\tau^2),\qquad T\to0\ \text{within the model}.}
$$

This is the [low-temperature liquid volume of a van der Waals gas](../../pressure.md#low-temperature-liquid-volume-of-a-van-der-waals-gas). Setting $P_s=0$ is an asymptotic approximation, not an exact coexistence condition at every [temperature](../../temperature.md) below $T_c$. The figure illustrates generic subcritical coexistence; its finite-temperature [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) [volume](../../thermodynamics.md#volume-thermodynamics) is determined by the full equal-area equations. The van der Waals [liquid](../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) also ceases to be a realistic description at sufficiently low physical [temperature](../../temperature.md), where other phases or quantum effects can matter.

**Two equivalent second-law statements.** The [Clausius statement of the second law](../../second-law-of-thermodynamics.md#clausius-statement-of-the-second-law) forbids a cyclic device whose sole net effect is transferring [heat](../../heat.md) from a colder reservoir to a hotter one. A [refrigerator](../../thermodynamics.md#refrigerator) can make that transfer when supplied with [work](../../energy.md#work), so the sole-effect qualification matters. The [Kelvin-Planck statement of the second law](../../second-law-of-thermodynamics.md#kelvin-planck-statement-of-the-second-law) forbids a cyclic device whose sole net effect is drawing [heat](../../heat.md) from a single reservoir and converting all of it into [work](../../energy.md#work). A [heat engine](../../thermodynamics.md#heat-engine) can produce [work](../../energy.md#work) when it also rejects [heat](../../heat.md) to a colder reservoir.

The two statements are equivalent by combining a supposed violating device with an ordinary reversible engine or [refrigerator](../../thermodynamics.md#refrigerator): one can cancel one reservoir exchange and obtain a violation of the other statement. The following original schematic shows the forbidden sole effects, with arrows indicating [energy](../../energy.md) flow.

**Microscopic fluctuations.** The second law is a macroscopic statistical law. A finite fluctuation can transfer a small [heat](../../heat.md) amount $Q$ from cold to hot, with reservoir [entropy](../../entropy.md) change $Q/T_h-Q/T_c<0$. Such an individual event does not imply that a cyclic engine can extract systematic useful [work](../../energy.md#work) from equilibrium. Entropy-decreasing trajectories become exceedingly improbable when the [entropy](../../entropy.md) involved is large compared with $k_B$. Under suitable microscopic reversibility conditions, fluctuation relations quantify the relative [probabilities](../../probability-theory.md#probability) of positive and negative [entropy](../../entropy.md) production and give nonnegative [mean](../../expected-value.md) total [entropy](../../entropy.md) production. Thus a deterministic no-fluctuation reading has a limit at small scales or short times; the statistical second law remains applicable.

**Maximum heating rate.** Interpret the quoted $0.5\,\mathrm{kW}$ as the maximum work-input rate $\dot W_{\max}$ to an ideal reversible [heat pump](../../thermodynamics.md#heat-pump). This interpretation is needed to obtain a heating rate larger than its electrical input; the wording does not explicitly distinguish input [power](../../energy.md#power) from delivered [heat](../../heat.md). A reversible cycle satisfies

$$
\frac{\dot Q_h}{T_h}=\frac{\dot Q_c}{T_c},\qquad
\dot W=\dot Q_h-\dot Q_c.
$$

Therefore the heating [coefficient of performance](../../thermodynamics.md#coefficient-of-performance) is

$$
\mathrm{COP}_h=\frac{\dot Q_h}{\dot W}=\frac{T_h}{T_h-T_c}.
$$

With $T_h=293\,\mathrm K$ and $T_c=273\,\mathrm K$,

$$
\boxed{\dot Q_{h,\max}=(0.5)\frac{293}{20}=7.325\,\mathrm{kW}\simeq7.33\,\mathrm{kW}.}
$$

The additional [heat](../../heat.md) comes from the outdoor reservoir; it is not produced by converting [work](../../energy.md#work) into more [energy](../../energy.md) than was supplied.

**Lowest outdoor [temperature](../../temperature.md).** Let $\Delta T=T_h-T_c>0$. The largest available heating rate is $\dot W_{\max}T_h/\Delta T$, while the house loses $\lambda\Delta T$. Equilibrium can be maintained when

$$
\lambda\Delta T\leq\frac{\dot W_{\max}T_h}{\Delta T},
\qquad
\Delta T^2\leq\frac{\dot W_{\max}T_h}{\lambda}.
$$

At the limiting outdoor [temperature](../../temperature.md),

$$
\Delta T_{\max}=\sqrt{\frac{(0.5\,\mathrm{kW})(293\,\mathrm K)}{0.1\,\mathrm{kW\,K^{-1}}}}
=38.275\,\mathrm K.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{T_{c,\min}=254.725\,\mathrm K\simeq255\,\mathrm K
\quad( -18.4\,{}^\circ\mathrm C).}
$$

This is the [indoor-temperature limit of a Carnot heat pump](../../thermodynamics.md#indoor-temperature-limit-of-a-carnot-heat-pump). It assumes the ideal pump retains its stated input-power capability over the operating range. Real [heat](../../heat.md) exchangers require finite [temperature](../../temperature.md) differences and incur irreversibility, so the reversible result is an ideal upper performance bound.

**Conventional heater.** At this limiting outdoor [temperature](../../temperature.md), the required delivered heating [power](../../energy.md#power) is simply the house's [heat](../../heat.md) loss:

$$
\boxed{\dot Q_{\mathrm{gas}}=\lambda\Delta T_{\max}=3.83\,\mathrm{kW}.}
$$

This is [heat](../../heat.md) delivered to the house. The fuel-input [power](../../energy.md#power) would be $3.83/\eta\,\mathrm{kW}$ for heater efficiency $\eta$. At the earlier outdoor [temperature](../../temperature.md) of $273\,\mathrm K$, the required delivered [heat](../../heat.md) would instead be $0.1(293-273)=2.00\,\mathrm{kW}$; the final question's reference [temperature](../../temperature.md) must be kept clear.

If $0.5\,\mathrm{kW}$ is read literally as a maximum delivered [heat](../../heat.md) rate rather than a work-input rating, the alternative answers are $\dot Q_{h,\max}=0.5\,\mathrm{kW}$, $T_{c,\min}=293-0.5/0.1=288\,\mathrm K$, and a conventional heater delivering $0.5\,\mathrm{kW}$ at that limit. Such a pump could not balance the stated house loss at $273\,\mathrm K$. Both readings follow consistently once the ambiguous [power](../../energy.md#power) rating is defined; the Carnot calculation above uses the work-input reading.

**Configuration count.** Choose the end-to-end direction as positive and let $N_R,N_L$ count rightward and leftward links. Then

$$
N_R+N_L=N,\qquad a(N_R-N_L)=L,
\qquad N_R=\frac{N+L/a}{2},\quad N_L=\frac{N-L/a}{2}.
$$

A configuration is determined by choosing the positions of its leftward links, so

$$
\boxed{\Omega(L)=\frac{N!}{N_R!N_L!}.}
$$

The counts must be nonnegative integers; in particular, allowed signed extensions occur in steps of $2a$. If distance means $|L|$ with neither direction selected, there are two corresponding sets for $L>0$, adding a factor of two. That constant does not affect the [tension](../../force.md#tension). There is no excluded-volume penalty in this one-dimensional idealized count.

**[Entropy](../../entropy.md) from the multiplicity.** Put $x=L/(Na)$, so $|x|<1$. Applying the leading [Stirling's approximation](../../real-analysis.md#stirling-formula) to all three large factorials gives

$$
S=k_B\ln\Omega\simeq k_B[N\ln N-N_R\ln N_R-N_L\ln N_L]
$$



$$
\boxed{S(x)\simeq Nk_B\ln2-\frac{Nk_B}{2}
\left[(1+x)\ln(1+x)+(1-x)\ln(1-x)\right].}
$$

Terms subleading in $N$, including logarithmic factorial prefactors, have been omitted consistently. Expanding near zero extension gives

$$
S(L)\simeq Nk_B\ln2-\frac{k_BL^2}{2Na^2}
+O\left(Nk_Bx^4\right).
$$

Stretching reduces the number of available configurations and hence the [entropy](../../entropy.md).

**[Tension](../../force.md#tension) and the range of [Hooke's law](../../elasticity-physics.md#hooke-s-law).** The model assigns no extension-dependent [internal energy](../../internal-energy.md). At fixed [temperature](../../temperature.md), the externally applied [tension](../../force.md#tension) is $\mathcal F=(\partial F/\partial L)_T=-T(\partial S/\partial L)_T$. Differentiating the full leading [entropy](../../entropy.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathcal F=\frac{k_BT}{2a}\ln\frac{1+x}{1-x}
=\frac{k_BT}{a}\operatorname{artanh}x.}
$$

It is the external [force](../../force.md) needed to oppose the chain's restoring [entropic force](../../force.md#entropic-force). Only when $|L|\ll Na$ can one put $\operatorname{artanh}x\simeq x$, yielding

$$
\boxed{\mathcal F\simeq\frac{k_BT}{Na^2}L,\qquad |L|\ll Na.}
$$

The paper's displayed linear law therefore requires this small-extension approximation, which is not explicitly stated in its premise $L<L_0$. It is not the exact [force](../../force.md) law up to full extension. The entropic spring constant grows with [temperature](../../temperature.md) because stronger thermal disorder makes a given reduction in configurational [entropy](../../entropy.md) cost more free [energy](../../energy.md). This is the [one-dimensional entropic chain force-extension relation](../../mathematical-biology.md#one-dimensional-entropic-chain-force-extension-relation).

**A sparse number of backward links.** Write $q=N_L\ll N$, so $N_R=N-q$ and $L=a(N-2q)$. Exactly,

$$
\Omega=\frac{N(N-1)\cdots(N-q+1)}{q!}
=\frac{N^q}{q!}\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}\left(1-\frac jN\right).
$$

For fixed $q$ and large $N$, each numerator factor approaches $N$, giving

$$
\boxed{\Omega\simeq\frac{N^{N_L}}{N_L!}.}
$$

More precisely, the [logarithm](../../calculus.md#logarithm) of the product is $-q(q-1)/(2N)+O(q^3/N^2)$. A small relative error in the multiplicity requires $q^2/N\ll1$; merely $q/N\ll1$ need not make the omitted product close to one. The weaker sparse-link condition does suffice for the leading [entropy](../../entropy.md) density and leading high-extension [tension](../../force.md#tension). These are [sparse backward links near full chain extension](../../mathematical-biology.md#sparse-backward-links-near-full-chain-extension).

**[Tension](../../force.md#tension) in that limit.** In the overlap regime $1\ll q\ll N$, use Stirling also for $q!$:

$$
S\simeq k_B[q\ln N-q\ln q+q],\qquad
\frac{dq}{dL}=-\frac1{2a}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathcal F\simeq\frac{k_BT}{2a}\ln\frac N{N_L}.}
$$

The full leading [entropy](../../entropy.md) would give $(k_BT/(2a))\ln[(N-N_L)/N_L]$, which has the same dominant [logarithm](../../calculus.md#logarithm) when $N_L/N\to0$.

**Approaching full extension.** The continuum [force](../../force.md) law diverges logarithmically as $N_L\to0$, or $L\to Na$. One must not literally apply Stirling to a zero factorial: at the discrete endpoint there is exactly one directed configuration, $\Omega=1$, and its [entropy](../../entropy.md) is zero. A continuous fixed-extension [derivative](../../calculus.md#derivative) is not defined at that endpoint.

An independent force-ensemble calculation makes the limiting physical statement precise. For applied [tension](../../force.md#tension) $\mathcal F$, a link has [partition function](../../canonical-ensemble.md#canonical-partition-function) $z=2\cosh(\beta\mathcal Fa)$, giving the exact [mean](../../expected-value.md) extension

$$
\boxed{\langle L\rangle=Na\tanh(\beta\mathcal Fa).}
$$

It approaches $Na$ only as $\mathcal F\to\infty$ at positive [temperature](../../temperature.md), and inversion reproduces the logarithmic [force](../../force.md) law for [mean](../../expected-value.md) extension. In a real chain, bond extension or failure eventually replaces the ideal inextensible-link model.

**[Temperature](../../temperature.md) as an equilibrium relation.** Classical thermodynamics begins with the [zeroth law of thermodynamics](../../thermodynamics.md#zeroth-law-of-thermodynamics): [thermal equilibrium](../../thermal-equilibrium.md) is transitive. A [thermometer](../../temperature.md#thermometer) can therefore assign a common empirical [temperature](../../temperature.md) to systems in mutual [thermal equilibrium](../../thermal-equilibrium.md). Different [thermometer](../../temperature.md#thermometer) properties initially give different monotone empirical scales; a resistance reading by itself does not define the absolute scale.

The [Second law of thermodynamics](../../second-law-of-thermodynamics.md) provides a substance-independent thermodynamic scale. A reversible engine operating between reservoirs has a [heat](../../heat.md) ratio depending only on their [temperatures](../../temperature.md). One chooses absolute [temperature](../../temperature.md) so that $Q_h/Q_c=T_h/T_c$, with $Q_h,Q_c$ the positive [heat](../../heat.md) magnitudes. Its reversible efficiency is $1-T_c/T_h$. Equivalently, reversible [heat](../../heat.md) defines [entropy](../../entropy.md) through $dS=\delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}}/T$. For an equilibrium simple compressible system,

$$
dU=T\,dS-p\,dV+\mu\,dN,
\qquad
\boxed{\frac1T=\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial U}\right)_{V,N}.}
$$

Classical thermodynamics uses this relation without needing a microscopic account of [entropy](../../entropy.md). A dilute-gas [thermometer](../../temperature.md#thermometer) realizes an empirical scale approaching the thermodynamic scale after corrections for nonideal behavior.

**Statistical meaning of the same [derivative](../../calculus.md#derivative).** In the microcanonical description, an equilibrium [energy](../../energy.md) shell containing $\Omega(U,V,N)$ accessible states has $S=k_B\ln\Omega$. Its statistical [temperature](../../temperature.md) is consequently

$$
\boxed{\frac1T=k_B\left(\frac{\partial\ln\Omega}{\partial U}\right)_{V,N}.}
$$

To see why this is an equilibrium [temperature](../../temperature.md), let two weakly coupled systems exchange a small [energy](../../energy.md) while their total [energy](../../energy.md) stays fixed. The combined multiplicity is $\Omega_A(U_A)\Omega_B(U_{\mathrm{tot}}-U_A)$. Maximizing its [logarithm](../../calculus.md#logarithm) gives

$$
\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_A}{\partial U_A}
=\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_B}{\partial U_B},
$$

which is equality of the two inverse [temperatures](../../temperature.md). Stability requires the total [entropy](../../entropy.md) maximum, not only stationarity. The overwhelmingly dominant [macrostate](../../statistical-physics.md#macrostate) in a large system therefore realizes the [thermodynamic equilibrium](../../thermodynamics.md#thermodynamic-equilibrium) condition.

A large reservoir gives a small subsystem the canonical [probabilities](../../probability-theory.md#probability) $p_i\propto e^{-\beta E_i}$, with $\beta=1/(k_BT)$. In that ensemble, $F=-k_BT\ln Z$ and $S=-k_B\sum_i p_i\ln p_i$ agree with macroscopic thermodynamic relations in their usual equilibrium regime. The equipartition identity for a classical dilute monatomic gas, $\langle p^2/(2m)\rangle=3k_BT/2$, is a useful operational connection between microscopic motion and [temperature](../../temperature.md), but is not a universal definition: quantum modes need not obey equipartition, and a system away from equilibrium need not have one [temperature](../../temperature.md) at all.

For finite systems one must specify the [entropy](../../entropy.md) convention and [energy](../../energy.md) resolution; surface and fluctuation corrections can distinguish definitions that agree in the ordinary [thermodynamic limit](../../statistical-physics.md#thermodynamic-limit). Bounded-energy population-inverted systems can also have a negative [entropy](../../entropy.md) [derivative](../../calculus.md#derivative) and hence a negative statistical [temperature](../../temperature.md). That possibility lies outside an ordinary positive-temperature ideal-gas [thermometer](../../temperature.md#thermometer); it does not [mean](../../expected-value.md) [temperature](../../temperature.md) is merely an arbitrary average [kinetic energy](../../energy.md#kinetic-energy).

**Obtaining the [energy](../../energy.md)–[temperature](../../temperature.md) conversion.** The [Boltzmann constant](../../thermodynamics.md#boltzmann-constant) connects [entropy](../../entropy.md) measured in $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$ with the dimensionless [logarithm](../../calculus.md#logarithm) of a state count. Historically it could be determined from the [ideal gas law](../../ideal-gas.md#ideal-gas-law) $pV=Nk_BT$: measure the [molar gas constant](../../ideal-gas.md#molar-gas-constant) $R$ and independently determine the [Avogadro constant](../../physics.md#avogadro-constant) $N_A$, then obtain $k_B=R/N_A$. The independent particle-number determination matters; using $R$ alone would not fix [energy](../../energy.md) per particle.

An electrical method uses [Johnson-Nyquist noise](../../electrical-engineering.md#thermal-electrical-noise). A resistor of resistance $R_e$ at known [temperature](../../temperature.md) has, in the classical range $hf\ll k_BT$, a one-sided open-circuit voltage-noise spectrum $S_V(f)=4k_BTR_e$. Over a calibrated flat bandwidth $\Delta f$,

$$
\boxed{k_B=\frac{\langle v^2\rangle}{4TR_e\Delta f}.}
$$

A practical measurement uses an equilibrium resistor, a calibrated low-noise amplifier and a known [transfer function](../../control-theory.md#transfer-function), subtracts or independently characterizes amplifier [noise](../../electrical-engineering.md#noise-signal-processing), and replaces a nominal bandwidth by the actual [effective noise bandwidth](../../electrical-engineering.md#effective-noise-bandwidth). Varying [temperature](../../temperature.md) and fitting the mean-square voltage is more informative than treating one amplified voltage sample as the thermal [variance](../../variance.md). Other primary methods relate [temperature](../../temperature.md) to sound speed in a dilute gas or to calibrated particle [diffusion](../../thermodynamics.md#diffusion) and [mobility](../../critical-phenomenon.md#order-parameter-mobility).

There is a historically relevant unit-definition qualification: by the date of this exam, the revised SI had already taken effect on 20 May 2019. The [kelvin](../../temperature.md#kelvin) is now defined by fixing

$$
\boxed{k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}\quad\text{exactly}.}
$$

The same experiments now realize or compare thermodynamic [temperatures](../../temperature.md) using this fixed constant, whereas historically they measured the conversion constant on an independently defined [temperature](../../temperature.md) scale. The physical distinction between equilibrium [temperature](../../temperature.md) and microscopic state counting is unchanged.[BIPM, SI definitions effective from 20 May 2019 and fixed [Boltzmann constant](../../thermodynamics.md#boltzmann-constant)](https://www.bipm.org/en/measurement-units/)
