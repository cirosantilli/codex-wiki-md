<h4 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-a/1)

The reversible [isothermal ideal-gas work](../../second-law-of-thermodynamics.md#isothermal-ideal-gas-work) is $Q=RT_0\ln2$; the heat would not be fixed by endpoints alone without the process assumption.

Full derivation: [solution](paper-2a.md#section-a/1/solution).

↑ **Parent:** [Section A](#section-a)

<h4 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-a/2)

Pairing adjacent contributions from the [Biot-Savart law](../../electromagnetism.md#biot-savart-law) gives magnitude $\mu_0IN(N+1)/(4\pi a)$, directed opposite to the first wire's field at the observation point.

Full derivation: [solution](paper-2a.md#section-a/2/solution).

↑ **Parent:** [Section A](#section-a)

<h4 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h4>

The lowest [transverse electric mode](../../electromagnetic-wave.md#transverse-electric-mode) has [cutoff frequency](../../electrical-engineering.md#cutoff-frequency) $c/(2a)\simeq15.0\,\mathrm{GHz}$; propagation requires frequency strictly above cutoff.

Full derivation: [solution](paper-2a.md#section-a/3/solution).

<h3 id="section-a/4">4</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section A](#section-a)

<h4 id="section-a/4/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#section-a/4)

Two distinct reversible adiabats would form a one-reservoir cycle with an isotherm, contradicting the [Kelvin-Planck statement of the second law](../../second-law-of-thermodynamics.md#kelvin-planck-statement-of-the-second-law).

Full derivation: [solution](paper-2a.md#section-a/4/solution).

↑ **Parent:** [Section A](#section-a)

The [trihydrogen cation](../../chemistry.md#trihydrogen-cation) has one breathing [normal mode](../../wave-equation.md#normal-mode) and two degenerate in-plane distortion [normal modes](../../wave-equation.md#normal-mode). Rigid translations and rotations do not count as vibrations.

## Section B

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2B](paper-2b.md)

<h3 id="section-b/b6">B6</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section B](#section-b)

<h4 id="section-b/b6/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#section-b/b6)

For [steady fields in two leaky dielectric layers](../../permittivity.md#steady-fields-in-two-leaky-dielectric-layers), set $\mathcal D=\sigma_2d_1+\sigma_1d_2$. Then $E_1=\sigma_2V/\mathcal D$, $E_2=\sigma_1V/\mathcal D$, $J=\sigma_1\sigma_2V/\mathcal D$. The interfacial free and total charges are $(\epsilon_2\sigma_1-\epsilon_1\sigma_2)V/\mathcal D$ and $\epsilon_0(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)V/\mathcal D$, respectively.

Full derivation: [solution](paper-2a.md#section-b/b6/solution).

<h3 id="section-b/b7">B7</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section B](#section-b)

<h4 id="section-b/b7/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#section-b/b7)

During partial entry, resistive [magnetic braking](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-braking) gives $v=g\tau(1-e^{-t/\tau})$ with $\tau=mR/(B_0a)^2$. The lossless inductive limit gives $x=g(1-\cos\omega t)/\omega^2$, $v=g\sin\omega t/\omega$, with $\omega=B_0a/\sqrt{mL}$. Complete immersion changes the equations; the solution below distinguishes reversal from full entry.

Full derivation: [solution](paper-2a.md#section-b/b7/solution).

<h3 id="section-b/b8">B8</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section B](#section-b)

<h4 id="section-b/b8/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B8](#section-b/b8)

The [parallel-plate transmission line](../../electrical-engineering.md#parallel-plate-transmission-line) has per-length $C=\epsilon b/a$ and $L=\mu a/b$. The lossless [telegrapher's equations](../../electrical-engineering.md#telegrapher-s-equations) imply wave speed $1/\sqrt{LC}=1/\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}$, independent of the much slower [electron drift velocity](../../electric-charge.md#electron-drift-velocity).

Full derivation: [solution](paper-2a.md#section-b/b8/solution).

<h3 id="section-b/b9">B9</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section B](#section-b)

<h4 id="section-b/b9/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B9](#section-b/b9)

The [image charge for a grounded conducting sphere](../../electric-field.md#image-charge-for-a-grounded-conducting-sphere) is $-qa/d$ at radius $a^2/d$. With symmetric real charges, their exact force-balance equation has a solution close to $d/a=8$. Holding potential $V$ adds force $qaV/d^2$ to the grounded result, approximately $qV/(64a)$ at that separation.

Full derivation: [solution](paper-2a.md#section-b/b9/solution).

## Section C

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2B](paper-2b.md)

<h3 id="section-c/c10">C10</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section C](#section-c)

<h4 id="section-c/c10/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C10](#section-c/c10)

[Bernoulli's theorem](../../euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid.md#bernoulli-equation) and [conservation of mass](../../continuum-mechanics.md#mass-conservation) give $t=r_1^2/(2r)$. Ignoring weight, the stated stagnant-rear-pressure model gives zero resultant at $r_2=\sqrt2r_1$. Including the leading film-weight estimate gives a cancellation radius near $2gr_1^2/v^2$. The printed inequality does not establish negligible force at every larger radius; the shared derivation explains this source qualification.

Full derivation: [solution](paper-2a.md#section-c/c10/solution).

<h3 id="section-c/c11">C11</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section C](#section-c)

<h4 id="section-c/c11/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C11](#section-c/c11)

[Rolling without slipping](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#rolling-without-slipping) gives $\omega^2=g/[(L-r)(1+2R^2/(5r^2))]$. With the supplied dimensions, $T\simeq80.5\,\mathrm s$ and the maximum centre-of-mass speed for a $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ rise is $0.0349\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

Full derivation: [solution](paper-2a.md#section-c/c11/solution).

<h3 id="section-c/c12">C12</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section C](#section-c)

<h4 id="section-c/c12/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C12](#section-c/c12)

The [torsional wave equation in a circular shaft](../../wave-equation.md#torsional-wave-equation-in-a-circular-shaft) has speed $\sqrt{G/\rho}$. The given shaft has torque $100\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, twist $9.95\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$, strain energy $0.497\,\mathrm J$ and kinetic energy $181\,\mathrm J$. The respective transit-time estimates are $786\,\mathrm W$ and $286\,\mathrm{kW}$; these must not be confused with independent bounds on the actual $30\,\mathrm{kW}$ stress-work flux.

Full derivation: [solution](paper-2a.md#section-c/c12/solution).

<h3 id="section-c/c13">C13</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section C](#section-c)

<h4 id="section-c/c13/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C13](#section-c/c13)

Stable circular power-law orbits require $nB>0$ and $n>-2$, excluding $n=0$. [Bertrand's theorem](../../mathematical-physics.md#bertrand-s-theorem) selects $n=-1,2$ when every bound orbit must close. For $n=2+\epsilon$, the near-circular ellipse orientation precesses at $-\epsilon\Omega/8$ to first order.

Full derivation: [solution](paper-2a.md#section-c/c13/solution).
