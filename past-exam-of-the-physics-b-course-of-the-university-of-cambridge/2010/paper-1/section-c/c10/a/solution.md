<h1 id="section-c/c10/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At fixed positive [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md), the extrapolated term $C_{mp}\ln(T/T_0)$ tends to **$-\infty$** as $T\to0^+$. That prediction is **not physically realistic** for an equilibrium sample. The classical ideal-gas description with constant [heat capacity](../../../../../../heat-capacity.md) cannot extend unchanged to absolute zero. A real substance generally undergoes phase changes, and [quantum statistics](../../../../../../quantum-ideal-gas-statistics-split.md) and thermal freeze-out change its [heat capacity](../../../../../../heat-capacity.md) and [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md). For a nondegenerate equilibrium [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md), the third law gives [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) approaching zero; residual ground-state degeneracy can give a finite residual value rather than this negative [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md). This is a breakdown of the extrapolation, not a violation of the third law by a real gas.
