<h1 id="section-c/c10/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At fixed positive [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), $-R\ln(p/p_0)$ tends to **$+\infty$** as $p\to0^+$. This is a **physically meaningful unbounded-volume limit** for a fixed mole number: $V_m=RT/p\to\infty$, so the number of accessible translational configurations grows without bound. For an [ideal gas](../../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) the [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) difference is $R\ln(V_{m,2}/V_{m,1})$ at fixed [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). No finite vessel reaches an infinite [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md), and $p=0$ at finite [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) is not a finite-volume one-mole state. The [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md) is approached by dilution; it does not conflict with the third law, which concerns a different [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) limit.
