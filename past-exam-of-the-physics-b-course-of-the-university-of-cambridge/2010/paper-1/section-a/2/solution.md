<h1 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Use local east, north and up axes. At latitude $\lambda$, $\boldsymbol\Omega=(0,\Omega\cos\lambda,\Omega\sin\lambda)$ and eastward [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) is $(v,0,0)$. The [Coriolis acceleration](../../../../../coriolis-acceleration.md) is

$$
-2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v=(0,-2\Omega v\sin\lambda,2\Omega v\cos\lambda).
$$

Its horizontal part is southward, so the lift must acquire a northward component: **bank left**, with the north wing lower. For the requested local Coriolis correction, let the vertical lift balance $mg$. The [Coriolis bank angle for eastward flight](../../../../../coriolis-bank-angle-for-eastward-flight.md) is

$$
\tan\theta=\frac{2\Omega v\sin\lambda}{g},\qquad
v=\frac{800}{3.6}=222.22\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\qquad
\boxed{\theta\simeq0.00310\,\mathrm{rad}=0.178^\circ.}
$$

Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ and $\Omega=7.2921\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Including the upward Coriolis term replaces $g$ by $g-2\Omega v\cos\lambda$ and changes the answer by only about $0.1\%$. This is the horizontal Coriolis compensation, not a full calculation of a curved constant-latitude flight path. The rotation rate follows Earth's sidereal period.[Orbital parameters: sidereal rotation period 23.9345 hours](../../../../../earth-fact-sheet-by-national-aeronautics-and-space-administration.md)
