<h1 id="section-a/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For a [plane wave](../../../../../plane-wave.md) propagating along $\hat{\mathbf k}$ in [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md), [Maxwell equations](../../../../../maxwell-equations-split.md) give $\mathbf B=\hat{\mathbf k}\times\mathbf E/c$, with $\mathbf E\perp\hat{\mathbf k}$. Hence

$$
\mathbf S=\frac{\mathbf E\times\mathbf B}{\mu_0}
=\frac{E^2}{\mu_0c}\hat{\mathbf k}
=\epsilon_0cE^2\hat{\mathbf k}.
$$

Taking $E=E_0\cos(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)$, the period average of the squared [cosine](../../../../../cosine.md) is $1/2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\left\langle|\mathbf S|\right\rangle=\frac12\epsilon_0cE_0^2.}
$$

The fields are in phase, so the travelling-wave [energy flux](../../../../../energy-flux.md) never reverses. The peak [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) convention is essential; using a root-mean-square [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) would remove the factor $1/2$.
