<h1 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

For a fixed linear circuit, [self-inductance](../../../../../self-inductance.md) relates the [magnetic flux linkage](../../../../../magnetic-flux-linkage.md) produced by its own current to that current: $\Lambda=LI$, with $\Lambda=\sum_{\rm turns}\Phi_B$. The self-induced [electromotive force](../../../../../electromotive-force.md) is $\mathcal E=-d\Lambda/dt=-L\,dI/dt$ when $L$ is constant. Its unit is the henry, $1\,\mathrm H=1\,\mathrm{Wb/A}$.

[mutual inductance](../../../../../mutual-inductance.md) relates the linkage in one circuit to current in another: $\Lambda_2=M_{21}I_1$ and $\Lambda_1=M_{12}I_2$. For stationary reciprocal linear media, $M_{12}=M_{21}=M$ with consistently oriented turns, and $\mathcal E_2=-M\,dI_1/dt$. Orientation fixes the sign. The coupled magnetic [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is $W=\tfrac12L_1I_1^2+\tfrac12L_2I_2^2+MI_1I_2$, so positivity requires $M^2\leq L_1L_2$. Moving coils or nonlinear cores require differentiation of the full linkage rather than treating the [inductance](../../../../../inductance.md) as a constant.
