<h1 id="section-b/b8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity-split.md) of a [fluid](../../../../../../fluid.md) [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) field is $\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf v$. A [vortex line](../../../../../../vortex-line.md) is an integral curve tangent to that vector, not generally a particle path or [streamline](../../../../../../streamline.md). Since $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\omega=0$, [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity-split.md) flux through successive cross-sections of a [vortex tube](../../../../../../vortex-tube.md) is equal; a regular line cannot simply end inside the [fluid](../../../../../../fluid.md). By Stokes's theorem, the tube strength is the [circulation](../../../../../../circulation-physics.md)

$$
\Gamma=\int_S\boldsymbol\omega\cdot d\mathbf A=\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf v\cdot d\mathbf l.
$$

For an inviscid [barotropic fluid](../../../../../../barotropic-fluid.md) with conservative body [force](../../../../../../force-split.md), [Kelvin's circulation theorem](../../../../../../kelvin-s-circulation-theorem.md) conserves [circulation](../../../../../../circulation-physics.md) around a material circuit. Under those assumptions [vortex tubes](../../../../../../vortex-tube.md) move with the [fluid](../../../../../../fluid.md). In three-dimensional incompressible ideal [flow](../../../../../../flow.md),

$$
\frac{D\boldsymbol\omega}{Dt}=(\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\nabla)\mathbf v;
$$

stretching a material tube raises its [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity-split.md) as its cross-section shrinks. In two-dimensional [incompressible flow](../../../../../../incompressible-flow.md) there is no such stretching, so scalar [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity-split.md) is materially conserved. Constant [viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) adds a diffusion term $\nu\nabla^2\boldsymbol\omega$; [baroclinicity](../../../../../../baroclinity.md) and boundaries can also generate [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity-split.md).

An ideal line vortex has $v_\phi=\Gamma/(2\pi r)$ and zero ordinary [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity-split.md) for $r>0$, with its [circulation](../../../../../../circulation-physics.md) concentrated in the singular core. Circular trajectories therefore do not imply nonzero local [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity-split.md) everywhere. A finite physical core removes the [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) singularity.
