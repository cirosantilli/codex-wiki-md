<h1 id="section-b/b8/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md) is $\mathrm{Re}=\rho UL/\eta=UL/\nu$, comparing inertial [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) $\rho U^2$ with viscous [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) $\eta U/L$. Nondimensionalizing an incompressible Newtonian [flow](../../../../../../flow.md) with [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) $U$, [length](../../../../../../length.md) $L$ and [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md) $\rho U^2$ gives

$$
\partial_{t^*}\mathbf v^*+\mathbf v^*\cdot\nabla^*\mathbf v^*
=-\nabla^*p^*+\frac1{\mathrm{Re}}\nabla^{*2}\mathbf v^*.
$$

At small [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md) inertia is negligible and a creeping-flow balance applies. At large [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md) the outer [flow](../../../../../../flow.md) may be approximately inviscid, but thin no-slip [boundary layers](../../../../../../boundary-layer.md) can still govern drag, separation and dissipation. A high [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md) permits turbulent motion without guaranteeing it; transition depends on geometry, disturbances and roughness.

Dynamically similar model experiments need matching geometry and relevant dimensionless parameters, including [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md). If gravity, compressibility or surface tension matters, Froude, Mach or capillary-related parameters must also be matched. Testing linear pressure-flow or force-speed relations while varying $U$, $L$, [viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) and [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) is more informative than assigning one universal transition value to all apparatus.
