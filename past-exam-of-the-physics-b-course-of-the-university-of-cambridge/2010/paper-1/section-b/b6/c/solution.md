<h1 id="section-b/b6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In [paramagnetism](../../../../../../paramagnetism.md), a material develops [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) along a weak applied field, with positive susceptibility; in [diamagnetism](../../../../../../diamagnetism.md), it develops an induced moment opposing it, with negative susceptibility. In a simple localized-moment paramagnet, Zeeman alignment competes with thermal disorder. For independent two-state moments $\pm\mu$,

$$
Z_1=2\cosh\frac{\mu B}{k_BT},\qquad
M=n\mu\tanh\frac{\mu B}{k_BT},\qquad
\chi_H\simeq\frac{\mu_0n\mu^2}{k_BT}
$$

when the field is weak and demagnetizing corrections are negligible. Stronger fields saturate the moment; interactions and conduction-electron [Pauli paramagnetism](../../../../../../pauli-paramagnetism.md) can depart from this [Curie law](../../../../../../curie-s-law.md).

Ordinary [diamagnetism](../../../../../../diamagnetism.md) comes from a field-induced change of orbital motion, including in closed-shell matter without permanent atomic moments. Its weak susceptibility is often nearly [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) independent away from a transition, and it contributes a background even when another magnetic response dominates. A nonuniform field tends to attract a paramagnetic specimen toward stronger field and repel a diamagnetic specimen in the weak linear regime. Calibrate the holder contribution and field [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) and measure a [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md)/field series to distinguish induced response from retained [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md). Ferromagnetic [hysteresis](../../../../../../hysteresis.md) and superconducting flux expulsion require different collective explanations.
