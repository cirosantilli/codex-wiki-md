<h1 id="section-b/b6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Since $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$, a [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) can locally be represented by a [magnetic vector potential](../../../../../../magnetic-vector-potential.md),

$$
\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A.
$$

It is not unique: $\mathbf A\to\mathbf A+\nabla\chi$ leaves its [curl](../../../../../../curl.md) unchanged. In magnetostatics, choose the [Coulomb gauge](../../../../../../coulomb-gauge.md) $\nabla\cdot\mathbf A=0$. Ampère's law then gives $\nabla^2\mathbf A=-\mu_0\mathbf J$ in [vacuum](../../../../../../vacuum.md). For a localized steady current distribution with the usual decay condition,

$$
\mathbf A(\mathbf r)=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int\frac{\mathbf J(\mathbf r')}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}\,d^3r'.
$$

Its [curl](../../../../../../curl.md) gives the Biot–Savart field. For a uniform field, one convenient choice is $\mathbf A=\tfrac12\mathbf B\times\mathbf r$. Stokes's theorem relates $\oint_C\mathbf A\cdot d\mathbf l$ to [magnetic flux](../../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) through a spanning surface. In time-dependent electrodynamics, $\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi-\partial_t\mathbf A$; changing $\mathbf A$ by $\nabla\chi$ requires $\phi\to\phi-\partial_t\chi$ to preserve the [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md). Gauge freedom simplifies calculation without changing the electromagnetic observables.
