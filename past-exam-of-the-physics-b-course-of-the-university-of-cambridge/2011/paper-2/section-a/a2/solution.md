<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

For a [uniformly magnetized ring with a narrow gap](../../../../../uniformly-magnetized-ring-with-a-narrow-gap.md), take the local direction of [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) as positive around the [magnetic circuit](../../../../../magnetic-circuit.md). Neglect leakage and fringing in the narrow gap, and take the cross section as uniform. [Continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) of [magnetic flux](../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) gives $H_g=H_r+M$. With no free current, [Ampère's law](../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) gives $lH_r+aH_g=0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{H_g=\frac{lM}{l+a}\simeq M,\qquad H_r=-\frac{aM}{l+a}\simeq-\frac al M.}
$$

The rod's field is a [demagnetizing field](../../../../../demagnetizing-field.md) opposite its [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md); its magnitude is $aM/(l+a)$. The gap field points with the [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) and $B_g=\mu_0H_g$.
