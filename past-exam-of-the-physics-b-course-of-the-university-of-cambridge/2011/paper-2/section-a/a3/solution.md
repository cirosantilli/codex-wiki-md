<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

Put the positive plate at $z=d/2$ and the negative plate at $z=-d/2$. Reflection through $z=0$, combined with reversal of the two plate charges, makes the tangential fields cancel at every point of the mid-plane. Thus **$E_x=E_y=0$** at the corner as well as inside the [capacitor](../../../../../capacitor.md).

For the [uniform-charge estimate at a capacitor corner](../../../../../uniform-charge-estimate-at-a-capacitor-corner.md), assemble four identical square pairs around their common corner. Their normal fields there are equal by rotation [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) and add to the central field $\sigma/\epsilon_0$ of the larger plate pair. With the bulk parallel-plate relation $\sigma\simeq\epsilon_0V/d$, the conventional corner estimate is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E_{\rm corner}\simeq\left(0,0,-\frac{V}{4d}\right)\quad\text{(uniform charge sheets).}}
$$

This quarter-field result uses prescribed uniform surface charge, not [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) alone for finite equipotential conductors. Real conducting plates redistribute charge near their edges; $d\ll a$ justifies the bulk relation away from the edges but does not remove the local corner fringe. For that exact conductor problem only the zero tangential components follow from the stated [reflection symmetry](../../../../../reflection-symmetry.md); its normal coefficient requires a fringing-field boundary-value solution. The standard parallel-plate formula explicitly neglects edge effects.[Section 8.1, parallel-plate field and the approximation of negligible edge effects](../../../../../university-physics-volume-2-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others.md)
