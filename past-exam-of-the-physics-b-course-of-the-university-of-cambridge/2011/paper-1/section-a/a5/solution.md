<h1 id="section-a/a5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A5](../a5.md)

Use a local horizontal plane, constant gravity and first order in Earth's angular [speed](../../../../../speed.md). Let $x$ point along the initial horizontal shot, $y$ to the gunner's left and $z$ upward. At the North pole, $\boldsymbol\Omega=\Omega\hat{\mathbf z}$. Maximum unperturbed range at equal launch and landing heights requires a $45^\circ$ elevation, giving

$$
v_x=\frac{u}{\sqrt2},\qquad t_f=\frac{\sqrt2u}{g},\qquad R_0=\frac{u^2}{g}.
$$

The leading [Coriolis acceleration](../../../../../coriolis-acceleration.md) is $\ddot y=-2\Omega v_x$, while vertical motion has no first-order Coriolis correction. With zero initial lateral [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) and [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md),

$$
y(t_f)=-\Omega v_x t_f^2,\qquad
\boxed{|y(t_f)|=\frac{\sqrt2\Omega u^3}{g^2}\simeq1.34\times10^2\,\mathrm m.}
$$

Here $u=500\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $g\simeq9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ and $\Omega\simeq7.2921\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. The miss is **to the right of the aiming direction**, independent of its azimuth at the pole. The flight time is about $72.1\,\mathrm s$, so $\Omega t_f\simeq0.0053\ll1$. This [polar projectile Coriolis deflection](../../../../../polar-projectile-coriolis-deflection.md) uses the usual flat, uniform-gravity approximation; planetary [curvature](../../../../../curvature.md) and variation of gravity are beyond its accuracy. Using the polar gravity rather than $9.81$ changes the estimate by less than one percent. Earth's sidereal rotation period, rather than the solar day, sets $\Omega$.[Orbital parameters: sidereal rotation period 23.9345 hours; bulk parameters: polar surface acceleration](../../../../../earth-fact-sheet-by-national-aeronautics-and-space-administration.md)
