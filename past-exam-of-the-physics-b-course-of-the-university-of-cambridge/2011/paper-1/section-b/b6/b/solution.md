<h1 id="section-b/b6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

An [electric dipole](../../../../../../electric-dipole.md) has equal and opposite charges separated by a small vector from negative to positive, with [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) $\mathbf p=q\mathbf d$. More generally, a localized neutral distribution has $\mathbf p=\int\mathbf r'\rho(\mathbf r')\,d^3r'$, independent of origin. At distances large compared with its size, the leading nonzero term in the [multipole expansion](../../../../../../electric-multipole-expansion.md) is

$$
\phi(\mathbf r)=\frac{\mathbf p\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2},\qquad
\mathbf E(\mathbf r)=\frac{3(\mathbf p\cdot\hat{\mathbf r})\hat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf p}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3},\quad r>0.
$$

The field points along $\mathbf p$ on either axial side and opposite $\mathbf p$ in the equatorial plane; its axial magnitude is twice the equatorial magnitude at equal [radius](../../../../../../radius.md). Near the charges the finite-charge expression must replace the far-field approximation.

A permanent dipole in an externally imposed slowly varying electrostatic field has interaction [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) $-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E$, [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) $\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf p\times\mathbf E$ and [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) $\nabla(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E)$ for fixed $\mathbf p$. A uniform field aligns it but gives no net [force](../../../../../../force-split.md). For a linearly induced dipole $\mathbf p=\alpha\mathbf E$, the formation-inclusive [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) is $-\alpha E^2/2$, not $-\alpha E^2$: the moment itself grows while the field is applied. Dipole [density](../../../../../../density.md) gives [electric polarization](../../../../../../polarization-density.md), whose bound charges are $\rho_b=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$ and $\sigma_b=\mathbf P\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}$.
