<h1 id="section-b/b6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Gauss's law](../../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) connects [electric flux](../../../../../../electric-flux.md) through a closed surface to its enclosed charge:

$$
\oint_{\partial V}\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf A=\frac{Q_{\rm enc}}{\epsilon_0},\qquad
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}.
$$

The differential form follows from the [divergence theorem](../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md). The law is exact; extracting a field magnitude by simple division additionally needs [symmetry](../../../../../../symmetry-physics.md). For [spherical symmetry](../../../../../../spherical-symmetry.md), $E_r(r)=Q(r)/(4\pi\epsilon_0r^2)$; a uniformly charged ball therefore has a field linear in $r$ inside and an inverse-square field outside. An infinite line of linear [charge density](../../../../../../charge-density.md) $\Lambda$ gives $E_r=\Lambda/(2\pi\epsilon_0r)$ by a cylindrical surface. A uniformly charged infinite sheet gives $E=\sigma/(2\epsilon_0)$ on either side by a pillbox.

An electrostatic conductor has zero interior field and constant potential; excess charge occupies its surface. A pillbox gives $E_{n,\rm out}=\sigma/\epsilon_0$ when the inside is conducting. More generally, $\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot(\mathbf E_2-\mathbf E_1)=\sigma/\epsilon_0$. For [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md) matter, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho_{\rm free}$ separates bound polarization charge from free charge. [Gauss's law](../../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) alone does not establish a uniform field for an arbitrary finite plate or recover a detailed nonsymmetric charge distribution from one flux measurement.
