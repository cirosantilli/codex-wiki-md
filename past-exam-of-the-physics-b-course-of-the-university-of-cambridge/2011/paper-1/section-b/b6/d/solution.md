<h1 id="section-b/b6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

In charge- and current-free [vacuum](../../../../../../vacuum.md), [Maxwell equations](../../../../../../maxwell-equations-split.md) reduce to

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,\qquad
\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\partial_t\mathbf B,\qquad
\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\epsilon_0\partial_t\mathbf E.
$$

Taking a [curl](../../../../../../curl.md) and using $\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf E=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E)-\nabla^2\mathbf E$ gives

$$
\nabla^2\mathbf E=\frac1{c^2}\partial_t^2\mathbf E,\qquad
c=(\mu_0\epsilon_0)^{-1/2},
$$

and the same wave equation for $\mathbf B$. For a [plane wave](../../../../../../plane-wave.md) $\mathbf E=\operatorname{Re}[\mathbf E_0e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)}]$, $\omega=ck$, $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf E_0=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf B_0=0$ and $\mathbf B_0=\hat{\mathbf k}\times\mathbf E_0/c$. Thus the fields are transverse, mutually perpendicular and in phase; polarization describes the electric-field trajectory at a fixed point.

The [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) [density](../../../../../../density.md) and [Poynting vector](../../../../../../poynting-vector.md) are

$$
u=\frac{\epsilon_0E^2}{2}+\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}=\epsilon_0E^2,\qquad
\mathbf S=\frac{\mathbf E\times\mathbf B}{\mu_0}=cu\hat{\mathbf k}.
$$

For a linearly polarized sinusoid with peak electric [amplitude](../../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) $E_0$, the mean [intensity](../../../../../../intensity-physics.md) is $\epsilon_0cE_0^2/2$. Electromagnetic momentum [density](../../../../../../density.md) is $\mathbf S/c^2$; normal absorption produces [radiation pressure](../../../../../../radiation-pressure.md) $\langle S\rangle/c$, while ideal retroreflection doubles it. Linear [superposition](../../../../../../superposition-principle.md) permits interference and standing waves, whose local electric and [magnetic fields](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) need not have the traveling-plane-wave equality point by point.
