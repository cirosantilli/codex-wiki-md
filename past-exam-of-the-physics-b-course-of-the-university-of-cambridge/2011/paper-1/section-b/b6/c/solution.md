<h1 id="section-b/b6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a fixed circuit, [Faraday's law of induction](../../../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) reads

$$
\oint_C\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf l=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_S\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf A,
\qquad \nabla\times\mathbf E=-\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t}.
$$

Surface orientation and contour direction obey the right-hand rule. The negative sign is [Lenz's law](../../../../../../lenz-s-law.md): a passive induced current acts against the flux change, consistently with [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) conservation. The induced [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) generally has nonzero [curl](../../../../../../curl.md) and cannot be represented by one single-valued [electrostatic potential](../../../../../../electric-potential.md).

For a material circuit moving with local [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) $\mathbf u$, the measured [electromotive force](../../../../../../electromotive-force.md) includes the magnetic [Lorentz force](../../../../../../lorentz-force.md) on its carriers:

$$
\mathcal E=\oint_{C(t)}(\mathbf E+\mathbf u\times\mathbf B)\cdot d\mathbf l
=-\frac{d\Phi_B[C(t)]}{dt}.
$$

This distinguishes transformer induction in a stationary coil from motional induction in a changing circuit. A straight sliding rod of [length](../../../../../../length.md) $l$ moving perpendicular to itself and to uniform $B$ has $|\mathcal E|=Blv$. With circuit resistance $R$, the electrical power $(Blv)^2/R$ equals the external mechanical power needed to oppose magnetic drag. An ideal transformer gives $V_2/V_1=N_2/N_1$ in magnitude from common [flux linkage](../../../../../../magnetic-flux-linkage.md); real leakage, resistance and core losses limit that simple result. [pickup coils](../../../../../../pickup-coil.md) measure flux change rather than an absolute static [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md), so integration and a reference state are needed to recover a field history.
