<h1 id="section-b/b8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Kepler's laws of planetary motion](../../../../../../kepler-s-laws-of-planetary-motion.md) state that, in the relative two-body description, bound trajectories are [ellipses](../../../../../../ellipse.md) with the attracting body at a focus, the [radius](../../../../../../radius.md) vector sweeps equal [areas](../../../../../../surface-area.md) in equal times, and the square of the period is proportional to the cube of the [semimajor axis](../../../../../../semi-major-axis.md) for a fixed total [mass](../../../../../../mass.md). Their Newtonian derivation is especially informative. With relative coordinate $\mathbf r$ and $\mu_g=G(M+m)$, the inverse-square [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) is $-\mu_g\mathbf r/r^3$. Its zero [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) conserves $\mathbf h=\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r}$, fixing a plane and the [areal velocity](../../../../../../areal-velocity.md) $h/2$.

For $u=1/r$, the [orbit](../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) equation is $u''+u=\mu_g/h^2$ as a function of polar angle. Therefore

$$
r=\frac{h^2/\mu_g}{1+e\cos(\phi-\phi_0)}.
$$

Bound negative-energy motion has $0\leq e<1$ and semilatus rectum $h^2/\mu_g=a(1-e^2)$. Dividing [ellipse](../../../../../../ellipse.md) [area](../../../../../../surface-area.md) by [areal velocity](../../../../../../areal-velocity.md) gives

$$
\boxed{T^2=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{G(M+m)}.}
$$

For a planet much lighter than its star, replace $M+m$ by $M$. Zero-energy and positive-energy encounters are parabolic and hyperbolic rather than periodic [ellipses](../../../../../../ellipse.md). Perturbations from other bodies and departures from point-mass Newtonian gravity can precess or otherwise modify the [orbit](../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md); Kepler's laws are a two-body approximation, not an exact rule for every observed planetary path.
