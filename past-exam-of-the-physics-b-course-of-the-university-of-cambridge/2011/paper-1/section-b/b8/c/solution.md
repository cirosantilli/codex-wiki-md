<h1 id="section-b/b8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [tidal force](../../../../../../tidal-force.md) measures the difference of [gravitational acceleration](../../../../../../gravitational-acceleration.md) across an extended object. A freely falling [reference frame](../../../../../../reference-frame.md) removes a spatially uniform [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md), but not its [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md). For a distant point [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) $M$ at distance $R$ and direction $\hat{\mathbf n}$ from the reference centre, a small internal [displacement](../../../../../../displacement.md) $\mathbf x$ gives

$$
\delta\mathbf g=\frac{GM}{R^3}\left[3\hat{\mathbf n}(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf x)-\mathbf x\right],\qquad |\mathbf x|\ll R.
$$

The [tidal tensor](../../../../../../tidal-tensor.md) has [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $2GM/R^3$ radially and $-GM/R^3$ in each transverse direction: radial stretching and transverse squeezing. Its trace vanishes in the source-free region, consistent with the gravitational Gauss law. An associated relative potential is $\Phi_{\rm tide}=-GM[3(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf x)^2-x^2]/(2R^3)$.

The scale is $GMl/R^3$ across size $l$, not the centre's absolute gravity $GM/R^2$. This distinction explains how a more distant massive object can exert a large [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) but a modest tide. Opposite-side stretching supports the elementary two-bulge picture; real ocean tides also involve rotation, basin geometry and [fluid](../../../../../../fluid.md) dynamics, so the static [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) alone does not predict coastal heights or timing. The same [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) stresses satellites and can disrupt a self-gravitating body when it competes with the body's own gravitational restoring [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md).
