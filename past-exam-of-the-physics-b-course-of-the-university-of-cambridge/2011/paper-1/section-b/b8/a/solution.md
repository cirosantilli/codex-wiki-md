↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [galilean transformation](../../../../../../galilean-transformation.md) relates [inertial frames](../../../../../../inertial-frame.md) in uniform relative translation. For $\mathbf V$ the [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) of the primed origin,

$$
\mathbf r'=\mathbf r-\mathbf Vt,\qquad t'=t,\qquad
\mathbf v'=\mathbf v-\mathbf V,\qquad \mathbf a'=\mathbf a.
$$

Absolute time and invariant [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) make [Newton's second law](../../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) retain its form for the usual nonrelativistic interactions. For a particle of [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) $m$, $\mathbf p'=\mathbf p-m\mathbf V$ and $K'=K-\mathbf V\cdot\mathbf p+mV^2/2$. Momentum and [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) are frame dependent, although an isolated system's conservation laws hold in either [inertial frame](../../../../../../inertial-frame.md). Relative [velocities](../../../../../../velocity.md) and collision predictions are consistent between frames. A rotating or accelerating frame instead requires inertial-force terms. [Maxwell equations](../../../../../../maxwell-equations-split.md) with a fixed [light](../../../../../../light.md) [speed](../../../../../../speed.md) are not Galilean invariant; [Lorentz transformations](../../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) replace these relations when relativistic effects matter.
