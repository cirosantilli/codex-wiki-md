<h1 id="section-b/b8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let a frame's origin have inertial acceleration $\mathbf a_0$ and its axes rotate with [angular velocity](../../../../../../angular-velocity.md) $\boldsymbol\Omega(t)$. For position $\mathbf r$ and [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) $\mathbf v'$ measured in that frame, Newton's law becomes

$$
m\mathbf a'=\mathbf F-m\mathbf a_0
-2m\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v'
-m\dot{\boldsymbol\Omega}\times\mathbf r
-m\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r).
$$

The extra terms are translational, Coriolis, Euler and centrifugal inertial [forces](../../../../../../force-split.md). [Centrifugal force](../../../../../../centrifugal-force.md) points away from the rotation axis; [Coriolis force](../../../../../../coriolis-force.md) deflects moving bodies sideways and does no instantaneous [work](../../../../../../work.md) on their relative motion, since it is perpendicular to $\mathbf v'$. The Euler term appears when the rotation rate or axis changes.

For constant $\boldsymbol\Omega$, [centrifugal force](../../../../../../centrifugal-force.md) has potential $U_{\rm cf}=-m|\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r|^2/2$. It can be combined with a conservative real potential to find rotating-frame equilibria. The Coriolis term remains [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) dependent. These [forces](../../../../../../force-split.md) arise from describing acceleration in a noninertial frame; they are not additional interactions between bodies. They are essential for trajectories on rotating apparatus and for large-scale terrestrial motions. **Frame-dependent terms must be included consistently with the chosen coordinates.**
