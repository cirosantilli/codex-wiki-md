<h1 id="section-b/b6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Write [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) as [magnetic dipole moment](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) per volume. In weak-field linear response, $\mathbf M=\chi\mathbf H$ and $\mathbf B=\mu_0(\mathbf H+\mathbf M)$.

**[Paramagnetism](../../../../../../paramagnetism.md)** has positive susceptibility: permanent spin or orbital moments tend to align with a field against thermal disorder. Independent localized moments in the classical weak-field regime give $\chi\propto1/T$, the [Curie law](../../../../../../curie-s-law.md). Conduction-electron [Pauli paramagnetism](../../../../../../pauli-paramagnetism.md) has a different origin and need not follow that [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) law.

**[Diamagnetism](../../../../../../diamagnetism.md)** is the induced magnetic response opposing the applied field, giving negative susceptibility. Changes in orbital motion underlie this response; it exists even without permanent moments. It is often relatively weak and only weakly [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) dependent, although particular quantum systems have stronger or more complicated behaviour.

**[Ferromagnetism](../../../../../../ferromagnetism.md)** arises from interactions favouring parallel magnetic order. Below the [Curie temperature](../../../../../../curie-temperature.md) a domain can have spontaneous [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) even at zero applied field. A sample can nevertheless have nearly zero net moment if differently oriented domains cancel. Domain rearrangement and rotation produce a nonlinear [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) curve, saturation, [remanence](../../../../../../remanent-magnetization.md) and [coercivity](../../../../../../coercive-function.md); [hysteresis](../../../../../../hysteresis.md) dissipates [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) in a field cycle. Above the ordering [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) the spontaneous order disappears, and a Curie-Weiss susceptibility is a useful mean-field approximation. **The signs of weak-field susceptibility distinguish para- and [diamagnetism](../../../../../../diamagnetism.md); spontaneous order and domain dynamics distinguish [ferromagnetism](../../../../../../ferromagnetism.md) from independent-moment alignment.**
