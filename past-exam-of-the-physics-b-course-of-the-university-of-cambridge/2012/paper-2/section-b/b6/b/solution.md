↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Polarization density](../../../../../../polarization-density.md) $\mathbf P$ is [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) per unit volume. Displacing positive and negative [bound charges](../../../../../../bound-charge.md) leaves the bulk nearly neutral locally but can produce uncompensated charge where polarization varies or terminates. For a [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md) occupying a volume with outward unit normal $\mathbf n$,

$$
\boxed{\rho_b=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P,\qquad \sigma_b=\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf n.}
$$

The [divergence theorem](../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) makes their total sum zero for a body described entirely by bound dipoles. Uniform polarization gives no interior volume charge but generally gives opposite surface charges on different faces.

In a linear isotropic [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md), $\mathbf P=\epsilon_0\chi_e\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P=\epsilon\mathbf E$. [Gauss's law](../../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) becomes $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho_f$, separating free from [bound charge](../../../../../../bound-charge.md). Across a static interface, tangential [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is continuous, while the normal [displacement](../../../../../../displacement.md) jumps by the free surface charge. Polarization charges alter the internal field, as in the [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md) cylinder calculation. Molecular distortion and orientation contribute differently, and response can depend on [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) and frequency. **[Bound charge](../../../../../../bound-charge.md) is a redistribution of material charge, not an additional free-charge reservoir.**
