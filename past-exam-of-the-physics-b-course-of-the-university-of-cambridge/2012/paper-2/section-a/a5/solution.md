<h1 id="section-a/a5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A5](../a5.md)

Let $N$ be circumferential [tension](../../../../../tension.md). A small arc subtending $d\theta$ has inward resultant $N\,d\theta$ and [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $\rho A r\,d\theta$. Its [centripetal acceleration](../../../../../centripetal-acceleration.md) gives $N=\rho A\omega^2r^2$. Circumferential [stress](../../../../../stress.md) is $N/A$ and the [strain](../../../../../strain.md) is $\Delta(2\pi r)/(2\pi r)=\Delta r/r$. Thus the [centrifugal strain of a thin elastic hoop](../../../../../centrifugal-strain-of-a-thin-elastic-hoop.md) is

$$
\boxed{\frac{\Delta r}{r}\simeq\frac{\rho\omega^2r^2}{E}.}
$$

Use the unstrained radius and cross-section at this order. The approximation requires $\rho\omega^2r^2/E\ll1$, a slender hoop and linear elastic response; the area cancels.
