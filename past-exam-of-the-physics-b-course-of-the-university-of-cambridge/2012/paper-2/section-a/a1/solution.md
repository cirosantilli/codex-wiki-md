<h1 id="section-a/a1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A1](../a1.md)

For the one-mole convention, **$b$ is the excluded [molar volume](../../../../../molar-volume.md)**: finite molecular size reduces the volume available for translational motion from $V$ to $V-b$. It is an effective [excluded volume](../../../../../excluded-volume.md), not simply the sum of the bare molecular volumes. **$a/V^2$ corrects for attractive [intermolecular forces](../../../../../intermolecular-force.md)**, which reduce the [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) exerted on the wall. The attraction per molecule scales with [number density](../../../../../number-density.md), and the number of affected molecules also scales with [density](../../../../../density.md), producing the density-squared correction. The [Van der Waals equation](../../../../../van-der-waals-equation.md) consequently raises the measured [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) to the corresponding repulsive-gas value before multiplying by the available volume. Both corrections vanish relative to the ideal-gas terms at sufficiently low [density](../../../../../density.md).
