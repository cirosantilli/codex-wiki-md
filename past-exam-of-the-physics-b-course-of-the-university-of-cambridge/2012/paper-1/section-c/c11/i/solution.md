<h1 id="section-c/c11/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For an attractive [inverse-square force](../../../../../../inverse-square-force.md), write $V(r)=-\kappa/r$ with $\kappa>0$, and take nonzero conserved [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) $J$. The [conic section](../../../../../../conic-section.md) orbit has

$$
r=\frac{J^2/(m\kappa)}{1+e\cos\psi},\qquad
e^2=1+\frac{2EJ^2}{m\kappa^2}.
$$

The physically allowed cases are **[ellipse](../../../../../../ellipse.md) for $-m\kappa^2/(2J^2)\leq E<0$, [parabola](../../../../../../parabola.md) for $E=0$, and [hyperbola](../../../../../../hyperbola.md) for $E>0$**. The minimum allowed [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) gives the circular special case $e=0$. Here $E$ is measured relative to infinity. Zero [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) instead gives radial collision or escape trajectories, interpreted as degenerate limits rather than regular closed [ellipses](../../../../../../ellipse.md).
