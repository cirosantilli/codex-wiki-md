<h1 id="section-a/a1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A1](../a1.md)

Use absolute [thermodynamic temperatures](../../../../../thermodynamic-temperature.md) and assume a constant-parameter [Arrhenius equation](../../../../../arrhenius-equation.md), with composition held fixed so that [reaction rate](../../../../../reaction-rate.md) is proportional to the [rate constant](../../../../../rate-constant.md). Write $R(T)=A e^{-\beta/T}$, where $\beta=E_a/\mathcal R$. [two-temperature Arrhenius extrapolation](../../../../../two-temperature-arrhenius-extrapolation.md) eliminates the unknown [Arrhenius prefactor](../../../../../pre-exponential-factor.md) and gives

$$
\beta=\frac{\ln10}{1/273.15-1/293.15}=9218.85\,\mathrm K,\qquad
\ln\frac{R(313.15)}{R_0}=\ln10\,\frac{1/273.15-1/313.15}{1/273.15-1/293.15}.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{R(40\,{}^\circ\mathrm C)=10^{2(293.15)/(313.15)}R_0\simeq74.5R_0.}
$$

A tenfold increase for one $20\,\mathrm K$ interval does not imply another tenfold increase for the next interval: the [Arrhenius plot](../../../../../arrhenius-plot.md) is linear in inverse [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). Two measured rates alone do not specify a unique extrapolation without a [rate law](../../../../../rate-law.md) for its [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) dependence; the result above states that modelling assumption explicitly.
