<h1 id="section-a/a5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A5](../a5.md)

A stationary person relative to the rim has [centripetal acceleration](../../../../../centripetal-acceleration.md) $a\omega^2=g$. If $u$ is a signed walking [speed](../../../../../speed.md) relative to the rim, the inertial tangential [speed](../../../../../speed.md) is $a\omega+u$, so the inward normal reaction satisfies

$$
\frac Nm=\frac{(a\omega+u)^2}{a}=g+2\omega u+\frac{u^2}{a}.
$$

Consequently the support [force](../../../../../force-split.md) per [mass](../../../../../mass.md), and hence apparent weight, is **$4g$ for $u=+a\omega$ and zero for $u=-a\omega$**. In the latter case the walker has zero inertial [speed](../../../../../speed.md) and needs no centripetal support; ordinary weight-bearing contact cannot be assumed to persist without a restraint. For [artificial gravity in a rotating ring](../../../../../artificial-gravity-in-a-rotating-ring.md), the [centrifugal force](../../../../../centrifugal-force.md) and the [Coriolis force](../../../../../coriolis-force.md) alone are insufficient: the walker also has relative-path [centripetal acceleration](../../../../../centripetal-acceleration.md) $u^2/a$. Omitting it would give the wrong apparent weights at these large walking [speeds](../../../../../speed.md).
