<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

Let $A$ be the initial plan [area](../../../../../surface-area.md) and take compression normal to the sheet. Smooth plates exert no lateral traction, so the uniaxial [stress](../../../../../stress.md) is $\sigma_z=-F/A$ and the two lateral [strains](../../../../../strain.md) are $\varepsilon_x=\varepsilon_y=\nu F/(EA)$. To first order in [strain](../../../../../strain.md),

$$
\Delta A=A(\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y)=\frac{2\nu F}{E}=\frac{2(0.48)(200)}{50\times10^6}=\boxed{3.84\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}=3.84\,\mathrm{mm^2}.}
$$

This [area expansion under uniaxial compression](../../../../../area-expansion-under-uniaxial-compression.md) is the increase of the sheet's plan [area](../../../../../surface-area.md), equivalently the [area](../../../../../surface-area.md) of either broad face. Counting the sum of both broad faces would give twice this change; edge [area](../../../../../surface-area.md) would require thickness information. The cancellation of $A$ is a small-strain linear-elastic result, not a statement that arbitrarily large compressions need no geometry data.
