<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

For a lossless [transmission line](../../../../../transmission-line.md) with [characteristic impedance](../../../../../characteristic-impedance.md) $Z_0$ and resistive termination $R_L$, the voltage [reflection coefficient](../../../../../reflection-coefficient.md) is

$$
\Gamma=\frac{R_L-Z_0}{R_L+Z_0}.
$$

The reflected pulse is inverted and has half the incident [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md), so $\Gamma=-1/2$. Substituting the termination gives

$$
\frac{25-Z_0}{25+Z_0}=-\frac12,\qquad \boxed{Z_0=75\ \Omega.}
$$

The echo delay is a round trip from the observation point to the termination and back, not a one-way travel time. Thus the propagation [speed](../../../../../speed.md) is

$$
v=\frac{2(1\ \mathrm m)}{10\ \mathrm{ns}}=2.0\times10^8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.
$$

For a homogeneous, nonmagnetic [coaxial cable](../../../../../coaxial-cable.md) dielectric, $v=c/\sqrt{\epsilon_r}$, giving **$\epsilon_r\simeq2.25$**. More generally the measured [speed](../../../../../speed.md) fixes $\epsilon_r\mu_r=(c/v)^2$; identifying this with [relative permittivity](../../../../../relative-permittivity.md) assumes $\mu_r\simeq1$ and negligible dispersion or attenuation of the pulse.
