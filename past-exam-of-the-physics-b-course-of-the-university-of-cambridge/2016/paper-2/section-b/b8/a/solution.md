<h1 id="section-b/b8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A horizontal [pressure gradient force](../../../../../../pressure-gradient-force.md) accelerates air from higher toward lower [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md). In a rotating frame the [Coriolis force](../../../../../../coriolis-force.md) deflects the flow. Away from strong friction, at large spatial scales and small [Rossby number](../../../../../../rossby-number.md), a slowly changing flow can reach [geostrophic balance](../../../../../../geostrophic-balance.md):

$$
f\widehat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u_g=-\frac1\rho\nabla_h p,\qquad \boxed{\mathbf u_g=\frac1{\rho f}\widehat{\mathbf z}\times\nabla_h p},
$$

where $f=2\Omega\sin\varphi$ is the [Coriolis parameter](../../../../../../coriolis-parameter.md). The [geostrophic wind](../../../../../../geostrophic-wind.md) runs along [atmospheric isobars](../../../../../../atmospheric-isobar.md), with speed $|\nabla_h p|/(\rho|f|)$. Closely spaced [atmospheric isobars](../../../../../../atmospheric-isobar.md) therefore indicate stronger [geostrophic wind](../../../../../../geostrophic-wind.md) when [density](../../../../../../density.md) and latitude are comparable. In the northern hemisphere lower [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md) lies to the left of this wind, producing counterclockwise circulation around a low; the sense reverses in the southern hemisphere.

A curved flow also has [centripetal acceleration](../../../../../../centripetal-acceleration.md). For cyclonic circular flow in the northern hemisphere, the [gradient wind](../../../../../../gradient-wind.md) relation is

$$
\frac{v^2}{r}+fv=\frac1\rho\frac{dp}{dr}.
$$

Near the ground, friction reduces wind speed and weakens its Coriolis contribution, so air crosses [atmospheric isobars](../../../../../../atmospheric-isobar.md) toward lower [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md). The simple geostrophic formula fails near the equator, where $f$ becomes small, and where rapid changes or tight curvature make neglected acceleration important. Vertically, large-scale motion is often approximated by [hydrostatic balance](../../../../../../hydrostatic-balance.md), $dp/dz=-\rho g$.

[Bernoulli's principle](../../../../../../bernoulli-s-principle.md) can relate speed changes to [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md) changes along an appropriate inviscid streamline, but its simple dynamic-pressure relation is not a replacement for the rotating force balance governing a broad atmospheric pressure map.
