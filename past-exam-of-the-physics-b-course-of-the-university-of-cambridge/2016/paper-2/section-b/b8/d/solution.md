<h1 id="section-b/b8/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For two bodies interacting through a [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) that depends only on their separation, introduce the [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) coordinate and the [two-particle relative coordinate](../../../../../../two-particle-relative-coordinate.md):

$$
\mathbf R=\frac{m_1\mathbf r_1+m_2\mathbf r_2}{M},\qquad\mathbf r=\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2,\qquad M=m_1+m_2.
$$

Their [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) separates exactly:

$$
T=\frac12M\dot{\mathbf R}^2+\frac12\mu\dot{\mathbf r}^2,\qquad \boxed{\mu=\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}.}
$$

The [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) moves uniformly if there is no external [force](../../../../../../force-split.md), while the internal [two-body problem](../../../../../../two-body-problem.md) reduces to one effective particle of [reduced mass](../../../../../../reduced-mass.md) $\mu$:

$$
M\ddot{\mathbf R}=0,\qquad \mu\ddot{\mathbf r}=-\nabla U(r).
$$

The original trajectories follow from $\mathbf r_1=\mathbf R+(m_2/M)\mathbf r$ and $\mathbf r_2=\mathbf R-(m_1/M)\mathbf r$.

For [Newtonian gravity](../../../../../../gravitational-acceleration.md), $U=-Gm_1m_2/r$, so the relative [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) is $\ddot{\mathbf r}=-GM\mathbf r/r^3$. Conserved relative [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) and [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) then give the usual [conic sections](../../../../../../conic-section.md). For a bound orbit of relative semimajor axis $a$,

$$
\boxed{E=-\frac{Gm_1m_2}{2a},\qquad T_{\rm orb}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{a^3}{G(m_1+m_2)}}.}
$$

The two bodies share this [orbital period](../../../../../../orbital-period.md) and orbit their common [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) with semimajor axes in inverse proportion to their [masses](../../../../../../mass.md). If $m_2\gg m_1$, then $\mu\simeq m_1$, recovering the fixed-heavy-centre approximation. Using $M$ as the internal inertial [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) would confuse centre-of-mass translation with relative motion.
