<h1 id="section-b/b8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Coupled oscillators](../../../../../../coupled-oscillator.md) have more than one collective pattern of motion. For two equal [masses](../../../../../../mass.md) $m$, each attached to a fixed support by a spring of stiffness $k$ and linked to each other by stiffness $\kappa$, small displacements obey

$$
m\ddot x_1+b\dot x_1+(k+\kappa)x_1-\kappa x_2=F_1\cos\Omega t,
$$



$$
m\ddot x_2+b\dot x_2+(k+\kappa)x_2-\kappa x_1=F_2\cos\Omega t.
$$

Here equal linear damping $b$ is included to make finite [steady-state responses](../../../../../../steady-state-response.md) at resonance. The [normal mode coordinates](../../../../../../normal-coordinate.md) $q_+=(x_1+x_2)/\sqrt2$ and $q_-=(x_1-x_2)/\sqrt2$ diagonalize these equations. Their natural [angular frequencies](../../../../../../angular-frequency.md) are

$$
\boxed{\omega_+^2=k/m,\qquad \omega_-^2=(k+2\kappa)/m.}
$$

The symmetric [normal mode](../../../../../../normal-mode.md) leaves the coupling spring unextended; the antisymmetric [normal mode](../../../../../../normal-mode.md) stretches it and therefore has the higher [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md). With $F_\pm=(F_1\pm F_2)/\sqrt2$, their complex [steady-state responses](../../../../../../steady-state-response.md) are

$$
\boxed{\widetilde q_\pm=\frac{F_\pm}{m(\omega_\pm^2-\Omega^2)+ib\Omega}.}
$$

Thus the drive's spatial pattern matters as well as its [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md). Equal in-phase [forces](../../../../../../force-split.md) drive only the symmetric [normal mode](../../../../../../normal-mode.md); opposite [forces](../../../../../../force-split.md) drive only the antisymmetric one; driving a single mass generally excites both. Finite damping broadens the [resonance](../../../../../../resonance.md) peaks and produces a phase lag. In the ideal undamped system, a resonant drive with nonzero projection onto a [normal mode](../../../../../../normal-mode.md) produces secular growth rather than a bounded resonant steady state.

**[Figure 2](#section-b/b8/b/image-original-driven-two-mass-response-showing-two-resonances-and-an-intervening-antiresonance). Original driven two-mass response showing two resonances and an intervening antiresonance**.

The displacement of a particular mass adds contributions from both [normal modes](../../../../../../normal-mode.md). They can cancel at an [antiresonance](../../../../../../antiresonance.md). For $F_2=0$ and no damping, $x_1$ vanishes at $\Omega^2=(k+\kappa)/m$, while the partner still moves. This differs from the two nearby resonances, where a mode responds strongly; a small measured response need not mean that no motion or stored [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) exists elsewhere.
