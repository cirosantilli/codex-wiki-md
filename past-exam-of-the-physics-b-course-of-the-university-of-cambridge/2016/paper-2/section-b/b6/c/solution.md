<h1 id="section-b/b6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

[Free charge](../../../../../../free-charge.md) is charge assigned to externally supplied or mobile carriers in a macroscopic description. [Bound charge](../../../../../../bound-charge.md) describes the net charge associated with a material's [polarization density](../../../../../../polarization-density.md) $\mathbf P$. Its bulk and surface densities are

$$
\boxed{\rho_b=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P,\qquad \sigma_b=\mathbf P\cdot\widehat{\mathbf n},}
$$

where the normal points out of the polarized material. Uniform bulk [polarization density](../../../../../../polarization-density.md) has no bulk [bound charge](../../../../../../bound-charge.md), but it can still have surface [bound charge](../../../../../../bound-charge.md).

The [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is sourced by the total [electric charge](../../../../../../electric-charge-split.md). Introducing the [electric displacement field](../../../../../../electric-displacement-field.md) separates the free source in the macroscopic field equation:

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\frac{\rho_f+\rho_b}{\epsilon_0},\qquad \boxed{\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P,\qquad\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho_f.}
$$

This does not mean that [bound charges](../../../../../../bound-charge.md) are physically absent from $\mathbf D$: they are represented through $\mathbf P$. In a linear isotropic dielectric, $\mathbf P=\epsilon_0\chi_e\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf D=\epsilon\mathbf E$, with $\epsilon=\epsilon_0(1+\chi_e)$.

Across a stationary ordinary dielectric interface, electrostatics gives continuity of tangential [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md), while a free sheet charge gives

$$
\boxed{\widehat{\mathbf n}\times(\mathbf E_2-\mathbf E_1)=0,\qquad \widehat{\mathbf n}\cdot(\mathbf D_2-\mathbf D_1)=\sigma_f.}
$$

The normal [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) need not be continuous. For a fully filled [parallel-plate capacitor](../../../../../../parallel-plate-capacitor.md) at fixed free plate charge, polarization reduces the internal [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) by $\epsilon_r$ and increases [capacitance](../../../../../../capacitance.md) by the same factor. If a battery instead holds the [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) fixed, extra free plate charge flows from the battery; the constraint must be stated before comparing stored [energies](../../../../../../energy-split.md).[Chapter 10, §§10–1 to 10–4: dielectric capacitor, polarization charge and the field equations](../../../../../../the-feynman-lectures-on-physics-volume-ii-by-richard-feynman-and-others.md)
