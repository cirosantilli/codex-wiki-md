<h1 id="section-b/b6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [electric dipole](../../../../../../electric-dipole.md) consists of equal and opposite [electric charges](../../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) separated by a displacement $\mathbf d$. Its [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) is $\mathbf p=q\mathbf d$, directed from negative to positive [electric charge](../../../../../../electric-charge-split.md). At distances large compared with the separation, its leading [electric potential](../../../../../../electric-potential.md) and [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) are

$$
\boxed{\phi(\mathbf r)=\frac{\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r}}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2},\qquad \mathbf E=\frac{3(\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})\widehat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf p}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3}.}
$$

There is no monopole term because the net [electric charge](../../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) is zero. The axial [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) points along $\mathbf p$ with magnitude $2p/(4\pi\epsilon_0r^3)$, while the equatorial [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) points oppositely with half that magnitude.

A uniform applied [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) exerts zero net [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) on an ideal fixed [electric dipole](../../../../../../electric-dipole.md), but it exerts [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) $\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf p\times\mathbf E$. Its orientation-dependent [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) is $U=-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E$, so alignment with the [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is stable. A nonuniform applied [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) can also exert a net [force](../../../../../../force-split.md), $\mathbf F=\nabla(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E)$ for a fixed permanent [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md).

A permanent [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) exists without an applied [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md). An induced [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) instead develops in response to the local [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md); in an isotropic linear model $\mathbf p=\alpha\mathbf E$. Creating that induced moment requires accounting for the work of polarization, so its mechanical [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) in a specified applied [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is $-\alpha E^2/2$, rather than $-\alpha E^2$. In matter, the dipole moment per unit volume is the [polarization density](../../../../../../polarization-density.md) $\mathbf P$.
