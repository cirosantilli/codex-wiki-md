<h1 id="section-b/b6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Mutual inductance](../../../../../../mutual-inductance.md) expresses how a changing [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md) in one circuit induces an [electromotive force](../../../../../../electromotive-force.md) in another. Choose winding orientations and define their linked [magnetic fluxes](../../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) by

$$
\Lambda_1=L_1I_1+MI_2,\qquad \Lambda_2=MI_1+L_2I_2.
$$

For a reciprocal linear magnetic system, the same $M$ appears in both expressions. [Faraday's law of induction](../../../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) gives $\mathcal E_2=-M\,dI_1/dt$ when the secondary [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md) does not itself change. The sign depends on winding orientation and is consistent with [Lenz's law](../../../../../../lenz-s-law.md).

The stored [magnetic energy](../../../../../../magnetic-energy.md) is

$$
U=\frac12L_1I_1^2+MI_1I_2+\frac12L_2I_2^2.
$$

Its nonnegativity for arbitrary [electric currents](../../../../../../electric-current.md) requires $M^2\leq L_1L_2$. Strong coupling makes $|M|$ approach $\sqrt{L_1L_2}$; flux leakage makes it smaller.

An ideal [electrical transformer](../../../../../../transformer-electrical-engineering.md) has two windings linked by the same time-varying core [magnetic flux](../../../../../../magnetic-flux.md). The induced winding [voltages](../../../../../../voltage.md) are proportional to their turn counts. For appropriately chosen terminal polarities,

$$
\boxed{\frac{V_2}{V_1}=\frac{N_2}{N_1},\qquad V_1I_1=V_2I_2,\qquad \frac{I_2}{I_1}=\frac{N_1}{N_2}.}
$$

The [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md) convention in the [power](../../../../../../power.md) relation takes primary power into the device and secondary power out to the load. Consequently a load [electrical impedance](../../../../../../electrical-impedance.md) $Z_L$ appears at the primary as $Z_{\rm in}=(N_1/N_2)^2Z_L$. A larger secondary [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) therefore comes with a smaller available secondary [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md), rather than extra [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md).

A steady direct [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md) produces no sustained transformer action after its switching transient. Practical departures from the ideal include winding [electrical resistance](../../../../../../electrical-resistance.md), leakage [magnetic flux](../../../../../../magnetic-flux.md), finite magnetizing [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md), core saturation, [eddy currents](../../../../../../eddy-current.md) and [magnetic hysteresis](../../../../../../magnetic-hysteresis.md) losses.
