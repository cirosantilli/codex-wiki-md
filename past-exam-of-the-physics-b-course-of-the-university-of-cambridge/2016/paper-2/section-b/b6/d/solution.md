<h1 id="section-b/b6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A [permanent magnet](../../../../../../permanent-magnet.md) retains a substantial [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) without an applied coil [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md). In a ferromagnetic material this reflects microscopic magnetic moments, [magnetic domains](../../../../../../magnetic-domain.md) and their resistance to rearrangement. The history-dependent relation between applied field and [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) gives [magnetic hysteresis](../../../../../../magnetic-hysteresis.md), remanence and coercivity. A soft core used in an [electromagnet](../../../../../../electromagnet.md) or [electrical transformer](../../../../../../transformer-electrical-engineering.md) is chosen for easy magnetization and small hysteretic loss; a permanent-magnet material must resist demagnetization.[Chapter 36, especially §36–3 and Figures 36–8 to 36–9: magnetization curves, remanence and hysteresis loss](../../../../../../the-feynman-lectures-on-physics-volume-ii-by-richard-feynman-and-others.md)

The macroscopic fields obey

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B=\mu_0(\mathbf H+\mathbf M),\qquad \nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J_f,\qquad\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0}
$$

in [magnetostatics](../../../../../../magnetostatics.md). The [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) can be represented by equivalent [bound currents](../../../../../../bound-current.md),

$$
\mathbf J_b=\nabla\times\mathbf M,\qquad \mathbf K_b=\mathbf M\times\widehat{\mathbf n}.
$$

Thus both permanent magnetic matter and free coil [electric currents](../../../../../../electric-current.md) generate a [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md). Its [magnetic field lines](../../../../../../magnetic-field-line.md) form continuous loops; apparent north and south poles are not isolated magnetic charges.

In a long ideal [solenoid](../../../../../../solenoid.md), $H\simeq nI$. A linear core would give $B\simeq\mu_0\mu_r nI$, until saturation or other nonlinear effects invalidate a constant [magnetic permeability](../../../../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md). In a finite magnetic circuit, air gaps and demagnetizing fields affect the field strongly: because the gap has low [magnetic permeability](../../../../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md), it often needs most of the available magnetomotive force. A [permanent magnet](../../../../../../permanent-magnet.md) can have a nonzero internal $\mathbf H$ even when $\mathbf J_f=0$, because its boundaries create a demagnetizing field. Hence setting $\mathbf H=0$ merely because there is no free coil [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md) is generally incorrect.
