<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

Take the initial thrower and receiver positions to be $(-a,0)$ and $(a,0)$, with counterclockwise [angular velocity](../../../../../angular-velocity.md) $\omega$. Interpret $v$ as the launch [speed](../../../../../speed.md) relative to the thrower. The ball inherits the thrower's tangential [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md), so in an [inertial frame](../../../../../inertial-frame.md) its initial horizontal [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) is $(v,-a\omega)$. The flight time across the diameter is $t_f\simeq2a/v$. During that time the receiver moves approximately $+a\omega t_f$ sideways while the ball moves $-a\omega t_f$. Thus the [transverse miss on a rotating platform](../../../../../transverse-miss-on-a-rotating-platform.md) is

$$
\boxed{d\simeq2a\omega t_f=\frac{4a^2\omega}{v}.}
$$

The ball passes behind the receiver in the sense of rotation. Equivalently, in the rotating frame its transverse [Coriolis acceleration](../../../../../coriolis-acceleration.md) has magnitude $2\omega v$ to leading order; integrating over $t_f$ gives $d=\tfrac12(2\omega v)t_f^2$. Terms neglected here are higher order in $a\omega/v$. Specifying an initially radial launch [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) in the inertial frame instead would define a different throw and would lose the inherited tangential [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md).
