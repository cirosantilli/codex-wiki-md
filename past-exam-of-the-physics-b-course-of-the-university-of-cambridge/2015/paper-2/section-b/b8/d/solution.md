<h1 id="section-b/b8/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For steady, inviscid, [incompressible flow](../../../../../../incompressible-flow.md), Euler's equation integrated along a [streamline](../../../../../../streamline.md) gives

$$
\boxed{p+\tfrac12\rho v^2+\rho gz=\text{constant along that streamline}.}
$$

This is [Bernoulli's equation](../../../../../../bernoulli-equation.md). The constant is common to different [streamlines](../../../../../../streamline.md) if the flow is also irrotational; incompressibility alone does not imply that. Viscous loss or work by a pump requires additional terms.

A [Pitot-static tube](../../../../../../pitot-static-tube.md) compares [stagnation pressure](../../../../../../stagnation-pressure.md) $p_0$ at a forward-facing opening with nearby static [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md) $p$. For equal height, the stagnation point has $v=0$, so

$$
\boxed{v=\sqrt{2(p_0-p)/\rho}.}
$$

This measures flow speed when compressibility corrections are negligible.

In a flowmeter using the [Venturi effect](../../../../../../venturi-effect.md), continuity gives $A_1v_1=A_2v_2$. For a horizontal constriction with $A_2<A_1$, Bernoulli gives $p_1-p_2=\rho(v_2^2-v_1^2)/2$, hence

$$
\boxed{v_1=\sqrt{\frac{2(p_1-p_2)}{\rho[(A_1/A_2)^2-1]}}.}
$$

The fluid accelerates and static [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md) falls in the throat. This is a second practical application of the conversion between [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md) and [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md); real instruments need calibration for flow losses.
