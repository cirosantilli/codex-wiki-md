<h1 id="section-b/b6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

An [inductor](../../../../../../inductor.md) stores magnetic energy and opposes changes of current through [electromagnetic induction](../../../../../../electromagnetic-induction.md). For a fixed linear device, the flux linkage is $\Psi=N\Phi=LI$, the self-induced [electromotive force](../../../../../../electromotive-force.md) is $-L\,dI/dt$, and the passive terminal voltage is $V=L\,dI/dt$. Hence

$$
\boxed{U=\int_0^I LI'\,dI'=\tfrac12LI^2,\qquad |X_L|=\omega L.}
$$

The voltage leads a sinusoidal current by a quarter cycle. A long solenoid has $B\simeq\mu NI/l$ and **$L\simeq\mu N^2A/l$**; its energy agrees with the volume integral of $B^2/(2\mu)$.

For two coupled coils, $\Psi_1=L_1I_1+\mathcal M I_2$, $\Psi_2=\mathcal M I_1+L_2I_2$, with signs set by coil orientations. Reciprocity gives the same [mutual inductance](../../../../../../mutual-inductance.md) in both equations. For the [energy stored in a nonlinear inductor](../../../../../../energy-stored-in-a-nonlinear-inductor.md), $U=\int I\,d\Psi$ replaces the simple quadratic formula, and [hysteresis](../../../../../../hysteresis.md) can dissipate energy. Real coils also have resistance, core loss and parasitic capacitance, so an ideal [inductance](../../../../../../inductance.md) model has a finite useful frequency range.[Section 4.1.2: inductance and magnetostatic energy](../../../../../../electromagnetism-by-david-tong.md)
