<h1 id="section-b/b6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Reflected and transmitted waves arise because the fields must satisfy the electromagnetic [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md). With no free surface charge or current, tangential $E,H$ and normal $D,B$ are continuous. At normal incidence between lossless media, define the real [wave impedance](../../../../../../wave-impedance.md) $Z_j=\sqrt{\mu_j/\epsilon_j}$. Writing $r=E_r/E_i$ and $t=E_t/E_i$ in a common transverse electric-field direction gives

$$
1+r=t,\qquad\frac{1-r}{Z_1}=\frac{t}{Z_2},\qquad\boxed{r=\frac{Z_2-Z_1}{Z_2+Z_1},\quad t=\frac{2Z_2}{Z_1+Z_2}.}
$$

For the [power normalization of Fresnel amplitudes](../../../../../../power-normalization-of-fresnel-amplitudes.md), power fractions are **$R=|r|^2$** and **$T=(Z_1/Z_2)|t|^2$**, so $R+T=1$. An electric-field amplitude coefficient alone is not a transmitted power fraction.

At oblique incidence, phase matching along the interface gives [Snell's law](../../../../../../snell-s-law.md), $n_1\sin\theta_i=n_2\sin\theta_t$. Applying the same [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) separately to the two linear [polarizations](../../../../../../polarization-waves.md) gives the [Fresnel equations](../../../../../../fresnel-equations.md). For nonmagnetic dielectrics the parallel-polarized reflection vanishes at $\tan\theta_B=n_2/n_1$. Above the critical angle into a lower-index lossless medium, the transmitted field is evanescent and reflection is total. A perfect conductor instead has zero tangential [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) and unit reflected power; finite conductivity introduces penetration and absorption.[Chapter 7: reflection and transmission at dielectric interfaces](../../../../../../electromagnetism-by-david-tong.md)
