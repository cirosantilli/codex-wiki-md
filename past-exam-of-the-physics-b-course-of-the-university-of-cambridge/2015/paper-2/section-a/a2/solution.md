<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

For the [image force on a line charge above a conducting plane](../../../../../image-force-on-a-line-charge-above-a-conducting-plane.md), with no imposed external [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md), use the [method of images](../../../../../method-of-images.md): replace the plane by a parallel image line of charge $-\lambda$ at height $-h$. The image is $2h$ from the real line, and its field at the real line has magnitude $\lambda/(4\pi\epsilon_0h)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{\mathbf F}{\text{length}}=-\frac{\lambda^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0h}\,\hat{\mathbf z}.}
$$

Here $\hat{\mathbf z}$ points upward from the plane. **The force is attractive and normal to the conductor**, for either sign of $\lambda$. The image reproduces the induced surface charge field; it is not an extra physical charge, and no factor $1/2$ is inserted in the force on the actual line.
