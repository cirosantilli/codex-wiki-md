<h1 id="section-a/a4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A4](../a4.md)

For a thick good conductor in the normal [skin effect](../../../../../skin-effect.md) regime, the [skin-effect surface resistance](../../../../../skin-effect-surface-resistance.md) is determined by

$$
\delta=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\mu\sigma\omega}},\qquad R_s=\frac1{\sigma\delta}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu\omega}{2\sigma}}.
$$

Identical guide geometry then gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{R_{\rm steel}}{R_{\rm gold}}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{\rm steel}\sigma_{\rm gold}}{\mu_{\rm gold}\sigma_{\rm steel}}}\simeq\sqrt{\frac{4.1\times10^7}{1.5\times10^6}}=5.23,}
$$

where the numerical ratio assumes both coatings are nonmagnetic, with $\mu\simeq\mu_0$. A magnetic stainless-steel grade would require its [magnetic permeability](../../../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md); conductivity alone does not fix that alternative.

Using $\mu_0\simeq4\pi\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{H\,m^{-1}}$, the gold [skin depth](../../../../../skin-depth.md) at the stated frequency is

$$
\boxed{\delta_{\rm gold}\simeq7.86\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm m=0.79\,\mathrm{\mu m}.}
$$

The [metal coating thickness relative to skin depth](../../../../../metal-coating-thickness-relative-to-skin-depth.md) sets the useful scale. Thus a layer of order **$1\,\mathrm{\mu m}$** begins to be useful, and a few [skin depths](../../../../../skin-depth.md), roughly **$2$–$3\,\mathrm{\mu m}$**, substantially screen the poorer substrate. This is a thickness scale, not a specified attenuation threshold. It assumes a continuous coating thicker than the relevant microscopic surface scale.[Chapter 7: wave propagation in conductors and skin depth](../../../../../electromagnetism-by-david-tong.md)[Vacuum permeability in the then-current SI convention](../../../../../codata-recommended-values-of-the-fundamental-physical-constants-2014-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology.md)
