<h1 id="section-c/c8/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Use $Q=CV$ for the magnitude of charge on either plate. The fully filled [capacitance](../../../../../../capacitance.md) is $C_f=2\pi\epsilon_0\epsilon L/\ln(b/a)$, while the initial value is given in part (c). At the same fixed [electric potential difference](../../../../../../electric-potential-difference.md),

$$
\boxed{\Delta Q=V(C_f-C_i)
=\frac{2\pi\epsilon_0(\epsilon-1)V(L-x)}{\ln(b/a)}.}
$$

For $\epsilon>1$ and positive $V$, this is an increase in the positive plate's [electric charge](../../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) magnitude, supplied by the voltage source; the negative plate acquires an equal opposite change. The net free charge of the two-plate capacitor remains zero. Thus “charge on the capacitor” has its usual circuit meaning, not the algebraic sum over both plates.
