<h1 id="section-c/c8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In a linear isotropic [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md) of [relative permittivity](../../../../../../relative-permittivity.md) $\epsilon$, the [electric displacement field](../../../../../../electric-displacement-field.md) and [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) satisfy

$$
\boxed{\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\epsilon\mathbf E,\qquad
\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho_f,\qquad\nabla\times\mathbf E=0.}
$$

Here $\rho_f$ denotes the free [charge density](../../../../../../charge-density.md) called $\rho$ in the question. For spatially constant $\epsilon$, these also give $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\rho_f/(\epsilon_0\epsilon)$. Equivalently, $\mathbf E=-\nabla\varphi$ and $\nabla^2\varphi=-\rho_f/(\epsilon_0\epsilon)$. The [electric displacement field](../../../../../../electric-displacement-field.md) has units $\mathrm{C\,m^{-2}}$ and the [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) has units $\mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}$.

The local equations determine the fields once their geometry and [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) are specified; a density value alone does not determine a unique vector field without that information.
