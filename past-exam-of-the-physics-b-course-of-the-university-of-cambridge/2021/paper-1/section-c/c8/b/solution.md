<h1 id="section-c/c8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $\mathbf n$ point from medium 1 to medium 2. Integrate $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho_f$ over a thin [Gaussian pillbox](../../../../../../gaussian-pillbox.md) crossing the interface. As its thickness tends to zero, the side contribution vanishes and any free [surface charge density](../../../../../../surface-charge-density.md) $\sigma_f$ gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf D_2-\mathbf D_1)=\sigma_f.}
$$

Apply $\oint\mathbf E\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=0$ to a thin rectangular loop spanning the same interface. Its short sides vanish in the limit, so the tangential [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is continuous:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf n\times(\mathbf E_2-\mathbf E_1)=0.}
$$

For a clean dielectric interface with no free [surface charge density](../../../../../../surface-charge-density.md), these [electrostatic interface boundary conditions](../../../../../../electrostatic-interface-boundary-conditions.md) reduce to $D_{1n}=D_{2n}$ and $E_{1t}=E_{2t}$. In particular $\epsilon_1E_{1n}=\epsilon_2E_{2n}$; the normal [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) itself need not be continuous. Bound polarization charge is already incorporated into $\mathbf D$.
