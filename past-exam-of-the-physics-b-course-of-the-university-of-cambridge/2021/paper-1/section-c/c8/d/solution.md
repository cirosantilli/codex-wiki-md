<h1 id="section-c/c8/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let increasing overlap $x$ mean that the [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md) moves farther into the capacitor. Its [capacitance](../../../../../../capacitance.md) derivative is

$$
\frac{dC}{dx}=\frac{2\pi\epsilon_0(\epsilon-1)}{\ln(b/a)}.
$$

At fixed [electric potential difference](../../../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) $V$, the [capacitor energy](../../../../../../capacitor-energy.md) rises by $dU_{\rm field}=V^2dC/2$, but the voltage source supplies electrical [work](../../../../../../work.md) $V\,dQ=V^2dC$. The difference is available as mechanical [work](../../../../../../work.md) $F_xdx$:

$$
F_xdx=V\,dQ-dU_{\rm field}=\frac12V^2dC.
$$

The [dielectric insertion force at fixed voltage](../../../../../../dielectric-insertion-force-at-fixed-voltage.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{F_x=\frac12V^2\frac{dC}{dx}
=\frac{\pi\epsilon_0(\epsilon-1)V^2}{\ln(b/a)}.}
$$

For the usual $\epsilon>1$, it pulls the dielectric inward, increasing $x$; its magnitude is constant within the long-overlap approximation. In the original longitudinal diagram this is to the left. The units are newtons, since $V^2dC/dx$ is energy per length. Differentiating the field energy alone with a minus sign at fixed $V$ would miss the source contribution; the correct [fixed-voltage capacitor energy balance](../../../../../../fixed-voltage-capacitor-energy-balance.md) is essential.
