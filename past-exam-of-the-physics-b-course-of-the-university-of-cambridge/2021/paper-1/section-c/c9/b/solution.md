<h1 id="section-c/c9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Parameterize the loop by $\mathbf r'=(a\cos\phi,a\sin\phi,0)$ and choose positive [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md) counterclockwise as seen from $+z$. At the observation point $\mathbf r=(0,0,z)$,

$$
d\boldsymbol\ell'=(-a\sin\phi,a\cos\phi,0)\,d\phi,\qquad
\mathbf r-\mathbf r'=(-a\cos\phi,-a\sin\phi,z).
$$

The numerator of the [Biot-Savart law](../../../../../../biot-savart-law.md) is

$$
d\boldsymbol\ell'\times(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')
=(az\cos\phi,az\sin\phi,a^2)\,d\phi.
$$

The distance $\sqrt{a^2+z^2}$ is constant round the loop. The transverse terms integrate to zero, while the axial term integrates to $2\pi a^2$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B(0,0,z)=\frac{\mu_0a^2I}{2(a^2+z^2)^{3/2}}\widehat{\mathbf z}.}
$$

For use below, define the undisturbed [magnetic field intensity](../../../../../../magnetic-field-intensity.md) $H_0(z)=B_0(z)/\mu_0=a^2I/[2(a^2+z^2)^{3/2}]$. This distinguishes the applied field from the internal field after adding the magnetic sample.
