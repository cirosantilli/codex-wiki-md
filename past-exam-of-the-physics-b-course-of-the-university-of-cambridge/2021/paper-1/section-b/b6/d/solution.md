<h1 id="section-b/b6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use a [Hohmann transfer orbit](../../../../../../hohmann-transfer-orbit.md). At the initial radius $r_0$, make a short prograde burn parallel to the existing [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md). The increased speed changes the [circular Kepler orbit](../../../../../../circular-kepler-orbit.md) into an [elliptic orbit](../../../../../../elliptic-orbit.md) whose [periapsis](../../../../../../periapsis.md) is the burn point and whose [apoapsis](../../../../../../apoapsis.md) lies on the desired larger circle. Coast for half the transfer [orbital period](../../../../../../orbital-period.md) to [apoapsis](../../../../../../apoapsis.md).

At that point the transfer [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) is again tangent to the target circle, but its magnitude is smaller than the circular speed there. A second short prograde burn raises it to the required circular speed. Thus **both burns increase the instantaneous speed and preserve its direction at the burn**, even though the final circular speed is lower than the original one. During the coast the spacecraft loses [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) while gaining gravitational [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md).
