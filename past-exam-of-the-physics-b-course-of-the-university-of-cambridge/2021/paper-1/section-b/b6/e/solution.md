<h1 id="section-b/b6/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The original [circular Kepler orbit](../../../../../../circular-kepler-orbit.md) has $v_0^2=GM/r_0$. For the transfer [ellipse](../../../../../../ellipse.md), set $r_p=r_0$, $r_a=3r_0$ in part (c):

$$
J_t^2=\frac32GMm^2r_0,\qquad
v_p=\frac{J_t}{mr_0}=\sqrt{\frac32}\,v_0.
$$

The first required speed increase is $\Delta v_1=(\sqrt{3/2}-1)v_0$. Under the question's negligible-mass-change fuel model, fuel use is proportional to the magnitude of the [impulse](../../../../../../impulse.md). Since the full fuel supply corresponds to $mv_0$,

$$
\boxed{f_1=\frac{m\Delta v_1}{mv_0}=\sqrt{\frac32}-1
=0.2247\simeq22.5\%.}
$$

This is the stated “about a quarter” of the original fuel supply. It is an instance of the [Hohmann transfer impulse fractions](../../../../../../hohmann-transfer-impulse-fractions.md); no variable-mass rocket model is required under the stipulated assumptions.
