↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the two-body approximation with a dominant central mass, [Kepler's laws of planetary motion](../../../../../../kepler-s-laws-of-planetary-motion.md) are:

- [Kepler's first law](../../../../../../kepler-s-first-law.md): a bound planetary [orbit](../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) is an [ellipse](../../../../../../ellipse.md) with the central attracting body at one focus.
- [Kepler's second law](../../../../../../kepler-s-second-law.md): the line from that focus to the planet sweeps equal areas in equal times, equivalent to constant [areal velocity](../../../../../../areal-velocity.md).
- [Kepler's third law](../../../../../../kepler-s-third-law.md): the squared [orbital period](../../../../../../orbital-period.md) is proportional to the cube of the [semi-major axis](../../../../../../semi-major-axis.md), $T^2=4\pi^2a^3/(GM)$ when the orbiting mass is negligible.

The first law includes a [circular Kepler orbit](../../../../../../circular-kepler-orbit.md) as a special case. The third-law constant is the same for bodies orbiting the same dominant mass.
