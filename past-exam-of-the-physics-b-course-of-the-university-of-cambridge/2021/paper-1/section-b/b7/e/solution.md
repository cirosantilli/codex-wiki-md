<h1 id="section-b/b7/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

As $R\to0$, the short joining fibre is very stiff and the lower [normal mode](../../../../../../normal-mode.md) has $\phi_1\simeq\phi_2$. The spheres therefore behave as one torsional load with combined [moment of inertia](../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) $2I$, suspended by the upper fibre of [torsion constant](../../../../../../torsion-constant.md) $K_t/\ell_1$. Its [angular frequency](../../../../../../angular-frequency.md) satisfies

$$
\boxed{\omega_-^2\to\frac{K_t/\ell_1}{2I}=\frac{\omega_0^2}{2}.}
$$

The high-frequency opposite-twist mode costs increasingly large elastic [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) and moves to arbitrarily high frequency in the massless-fibre model.

As $R\to\infty$, the joining fibre is very compliant. In the higher [normal mode](../../../../../../normal-mode.md) the lower sphere scarcely moves: the amplitude ratio tends to $A_2/A_1\simeq-1/R\to0$. The upper sphere approaches the original single [torsion pendulum](../../../../../../torsion-pendulum.md), giving

$$
\boxed{\omega_+^2\to\omega_0^2.}
$$

The other mode is then a slow twist of the almost freely suspended lower sphere. These interpretations assume the formal limits do not invalidate the thin massless-fibre approximation.
