<h1 id="section-c/c9/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Using $Z_{\rm rot}\simeq CT=C/(k_B\beta)$ gives

$$
\ln Z_{\rm rot}=\ln\left(\frac{C}{k_B\beta}\right).
$$

The internal-energy identity from (b), followed by [differentiation](../../../../../../differentiation.md) with respect to [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) at fixed [moment of inertia](../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md), yields

$$
\boxed{U_{\rm rot}\simeq\frac1\beta=k_BT,\qquad C_{V,\rm rot}=\left(\frac{\partial U_{\rm rot}}{\partial T}\right)_I\simeq k_B.}
$$

For one mole of independent rotors, the rotational [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) is $RT$ and the rotational molar [heat capacity](../../../../../../heat-capacity.md) is $R\simeq8.31\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$.

This agrees with the [equipartition theorem](../../../../../../equipartition-theorem.md). The given spectrum describes a linear rotor with two independent rotational [degrees of freedom](../../../../../../degree-of-freedom.md), each contributing $k_BT/2$ to the high-temperature mean [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md). Rotation about the molecular axis is not an additional [degree of freedom](../../../../../../degree-of-freedom.md) of this ideal linear-rotor model, so the result is $k_B$ rather than $3k_B/2$ per rotor. These are rotational contributions alone; translational and vibrational [heat capacities](../../../../../../heat-capacity.md) have not been included. At low [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) the discrete excitation gap freezes rotational excitation out, so the rotational [heat capacity](../../../../../../heat-capacity.md) tends to zero instead of remaining at its classical value.
