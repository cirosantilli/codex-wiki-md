<h1 id="section-c/c8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The relation describes the slope of the [phase coexistence curve](../../../../../../phase-coexistence-curve.md) of a pure substance in the pressure-temperature plane:

$$
\boxed{\frac{dp}{dT}=\frac{L}{T\Delta V}.}
$$

Here $p(T)$ is the common equilibrium [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md) of the two phases, $T$ is their common absolute [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) in kelvins, and the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is taken along their coexistence curve. Choose a direction of transformation from phase 1 to phase 2. Then $L$ is the reversible [latent heat](../../../../../../latent-heat.md) for that transformation and $\Delta V=V_2-V_1$ is the corresponding [volume](../../../../../../volume.md) change, using the same [amount of substance](../../../../../../amount-of-substance.md) for both quantities.

In the molar convention used below, $L=h_{m,2}-h_{m,1}$ has units $\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}$ and $\Delta V=v_{m,2}-v_{m,1}$ has units $\mathrm{m^3\,mol^{-1}}$, so the right-hand side has units $\mathrm{Pa\,K^{-1}}$. For vaporization, both $L$ and $\Delta V$ are positive. The displayed general relation is also called the [Clapeyron equation](../../../../../../clausius-clapeyron-relation.md); the ideal-vapor approximation used in (c) gives its usual Clausius form.
