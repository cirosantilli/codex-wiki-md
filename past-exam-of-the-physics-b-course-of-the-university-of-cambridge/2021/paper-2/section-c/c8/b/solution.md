<h1 id="section-c/c8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At coexistence, the two phases of a pure substance have equal [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md), or equivalently equal molar [Gibbs free energy](../../../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md):

$$
\mu_1(T,p)=\mu_2(T,p).
$$

For either phase, $d\mu=-s_m\,dT+v_m\,dp$. Move an infinitesimal distance along the coexistence curve, where the equality must remain true. Subtracting the two differentials gives

$$
0=-\left(s_{m,2}-s_{m,1}\right)dT+\left(v_{m,2}-v_{m,1}\right)dp,
\qquad \frac{dp}{dT}=\frac{\Delta s_m}{\Delta v_m}.
$$

A reversible transformation at this common [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) absorbs [latent heat](../../../../../../latent-heat.md) $L=T\Delta s_m$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\frac{dp}{dT}=\frac{\Delta s_m}{\Delta v_m}=\frac{L}{T\Delta v_m}.}
$$

The derivation assumes [thermodynamic equilibrium](../../../../../../thermodynamic-equilibrium.md) between two bulk phases of a single pure substance, with the same [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) and [pressure](../../../../../../pressure-split.md), and only ordinary pressure-volume work. Surface-curvature shifts, additional external-field work and composition changes are omitted. It applies to a first-order [phase boundary](../../../../../../phase-boundary.md) where the [latent heat](../../../../../../latent-heat.md) and [volume](../../../../../../volume.md) discontinuity are defined. No ideal-gas assumption and no constant-$L$ assumption are needed for this differential relation. At the critical endpoint both discontinuities vanish, so their ratio requires a limiting treatment rather than direct substitution of two zeros.
