<h1 id="section-b/b6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For [torque-free precession of a symmetric top](../../../../../../torque-free-precession-of-a-symmetric-top.md), take principal [moments of inertia](../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) $I_1=I_2=I_\perp$ and $I_3=I_\parallel$, with no external [torque](../../../../../../torque.md). In an [inertial frame](../../../../../../inertial-frame.md) moving with its [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md), its [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) $\mathbf L$ is constant in an [inertial frame](../../../../../../inertial-frame.md), and its [rotational kinetic energy](../../../../../../rotational-kinetic-energy.md) is conserved.

In the principal body axes, [Euler equations for a torque-free rigid body](../../../../../../euler-equations-for-a-torque-free-rigid-body.md) reduce to

$$
I_\perp\dot\omega_1+(I_\parallel-I_\perp)\omega_2\omega_3=0,\qquad
I_\perp\dot\omega_2-(I_\parallel-I_\perp)\omega_3\omega_1=0,\qquad
I_\parallel\dot\omega_3=0.
$$

Therefore $\omega_3$ is constant and

$$
\omega_1+i\omega_2=\omega_\perp e^{i(\Omega_bt+\alpha)},\qquad
\boxed{\Omega_b=\frac{I_\parallel-I_\perp}{I_\perp}\,\omega_3.}
$$

Thus the transverse [angular velocity](../../../../../../angular-velocity.md) rotates uniformly in the body frame; its signed rate reverses between oblate and prolate inertia tensors. This body-frame wobble rate should be distinguished from the space-frame [precession](../../../../../../precession.md) of the symmetry axis.

Let $\mathbf e_3$ be the body's symmetry axis. Since

$$
\mathbf L=I_\perp\boldsymbol\omega+(I_\parallel-I_\perp)\omega_3\mathbf e_3,
$$

its angle $\theta$ to $\mathbf L$ is constant, with $\cos\theta=I_\parallel\omega_3/L$, and

$$
\dot{\mathbf e}_3=\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf e_3
=\frac{\mathbf L}{I_\perp}\times\mathbf e_3.
$$

Hence the symmetry axis describes a cone about fixed $\mathbf L$ at the space-frame [precession](../../../../../../precession.md) rate

$$
\boxed{\dot\phi=\frac L{I_\perp}.}
$$

There is no [nutation](../../../../../../nutation.md) of this angle for torque-free motion of an exactly [symmetric top](../../../../../../symmetric-top.md). In the usual [Euler angles](../../../../../../euler-angles.md), $\omega_3=\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta$, so $\dot\psi=-\Omega_b$. Both rates describe the same motion in different axes. A freely tossed axisymmetric disc illustrates the effect; a top supported at a point under gravity is a different, externally torqued problem.
