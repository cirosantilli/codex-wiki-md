<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

The [third law of thermodynamics](../../../../../third-law-of-thermodynamics.md), in its perfect-crystal form, states that the [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) of a pure, perfectly ordered [crystal](../../../../../crystal.md) with a unique [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) tends to zero as $T\to0$. Its more general entropy-difference formulation says that the [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) difference between equilibrium states related by a finite change of a regular external control parameter tends to zero at [absolute zero](../../../../../absolute-zero.md):

$$
\lim_{T\to0}\left[S(T,X_2)-S(T,X_1)\right]=0.
$$

The common limiting [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) is independent of $X$. Ground-state degeneracy or frozen disorder can produce [residual entropy](../../../../../residual-entropy.md), so zero [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) is not an unconditional assertion about every sample.

In the usual regular thermodynamic setting, [absolute zero](../../../../../absolute-zero.md) cannot be attained from positive [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) by a finite sequence of cooling operations with finite resources. An entropy-temperature plot shows why a finite reversible adiabatic step cannot reach the common zero-temperature endpoint from a state with positive [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) above that endpoint: an [isentropic process](../../../../../isentropic-process.md) follows a horizontal line, while all the regular [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) curves meet only at $T=0$.

<a id="section-a/a2/image-entropy-curves-and-a-cooling-staircase-approaching-absolute-zero"></a>
![](../../../../../past-exam-of-the-physics-b-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2021/paper-2-a2-zero-temperature.png)

**[Figure 1](#section-a/a2/image-entropy-curves-and-a-cooling-staircase-approaching-absolute-zero). Entropy curves and a cooling staircase approaching absolute zero**.

The figure illustrates this with two possible settings having $S-S_0=a_1T$ and $S-S_0=a_2T$, where $0<a_1<a_2$. First change the setting isothermally from the upper to the lower curve, rejecting [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md); then change it reversibly and adiabatically back to the upper curve. [Entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) conservation on the latter step gives

$$
a_1T_n=a_2T_{n+1},\qquad T_{n+1}=\frac{a_1}{a_2}T_n,
\qquad T_n=T_0\left(\frac{a_1}{a_2}\right)^n>0
$$

for every finite $n$. The staircase approaches the origin but reaches it only after infinitely many such stages. Irreversibility generates [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) and cannot improve on the reversible endpoint.

This is a thermodynamic illustration of [unattainability of absolute zero](../../../../../unattainability-of-absolute-zero.md), not a claim that every cooling apparatus has this particular linear [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) law. Its conclusion presumes regular equilibrium states and bounded control parameters; dynamical statements about finite cooling time additionally require physical assumptions about the available interactions and cooling rates.
