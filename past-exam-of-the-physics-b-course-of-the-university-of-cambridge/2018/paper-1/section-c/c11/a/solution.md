<h1 id="section-c/c11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a [point mass](../../../../../../point-mass.md), $\mathbf g(\mathbf R)=-GM\mathbf R/R^3$. Along the outward radial direction, $g_R=-GM/R^2$ and

$$
\frac{dg_R}{dR}=\frac{2GM}{R^3}.
$$

Thus a small radial displacement $\mathbf a=a\widehat{\mathbf R}$ has differential acceleration

$$
\boxed{\mathbf T(\mathbf a)\simeq\frac{2GM}{R^3}\mathbf a.}
$$

The farther point falls inward less strongly, so its acceleration relative to the reference point points outward. The formula is first order in $|a|/R$.
