<h1 id="section-b/b7/general-loop-and-terrestrial-field/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [General loop and terrestrial field](../general-loop-and-terrestrial-field.md)

**Why the shape is immaterial far away.** Partition an arbitrary small planar loop into small rectangular loops with the same oriented current. Currents on internal shared edges cancel, leaving only the original boundary. Their [magnetic dipole moments](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) add to $\mathbf m=I\mathcal A\mathbf n$, with $\mathcal A$ the enclosed area. Far enough away, the leading fields add as dipoles at the same position. The general [magnetic dipole field](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-field.md) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r^3}
\left[3(\mathbf m\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})\widehat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf m\right].}
$$

Higher spatial multipoles contain shape-dependent corrections. The axial and transverse results above are the special directions of this expression.

**The aligned terrestrial model.** Take the moment to point toward geographic south. At northern latitude $\lambda$, the [magnetic dipole inclination versus latitude](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-inclination-versus-latitude.md) gives local northward and downward components

$$
B_N=B_{\rm eq}\cos\lambda,\qquad B_D=2B_{\rm eq}\sin\lambda.
$$

Using the specified $\lambda=53^\circ$ and $B_{\rm eq}=35\ \mathrm{\mu T}$,

$$
B_N\simeq21.1\ \mathrm{\mu T},\qquad B_D\simeq55.9\ \mathrm{\mu T},
$$



$$
\boxed{|\mathbf B|=B_{\rm eq}\sqrt{1+3\sin^2\lambda}
\simeq59.7\ \mathrm{\mu T},\quad
\iota=\arctan(2\tan\lambda)\simeq69.4^\circ.}
$$

The direction is geographic north and $69.4^\circ$ below horizontal, with no eastward component in this ideal model. These values use the latitude printed in the paper.
