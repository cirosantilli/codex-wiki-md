<h1 id="section-a/a5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A5](../a5.md)

Apply equal compressive [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) $p$ in all directions; here $\sigma$ denotes [Poisson's ratio](../../../../../poisson-s-ratio.md). The three principal normal [stresses](../../../../../stress.md) are $-p$. For an isotropic linearly elastic material, [Hooke's law](../../../../../hooke-s-law.md) gives

$$
\epsilon_x=\frac{-p-\sigma(-p-p)}{E}
=-\frac{p(1-2\sigma)}E,
$$

and similarly for $y,z$. At small [strain](../../../../../strain.md), the fractional volume change is the sum of these normal [strains](../../../../../strain.md):

$$
\frac{\Delta V}{V}=\epsilon_x+\epsilon_y+\epsilon_z
=-\frac{3p(1-2\sigma)}E.
$$

The [bulk modulus](../../../../../bulk-modulus.md) is $B=-p/(\Delta V/V)$, giving the [isotropic elastic modulus relation](../../../../../isotropic-elastic-modulus-relation.md),

$$
\boxed{B=\frac{E}{3(1-2\sigma)}.}
$$

The result assumes [linear elasticity](../../../../../linear-elasticity.md) and fixed isotropic moduli. In the incompressible limit $\sigma\to1/2$ with finite [Young's modulus](../../../../../young-s-modulus.md), the bulk modulus tends to infinity.
