<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

Let the particle have [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $m$, and assume its [force](../../../../../force-split.md) is derived from a time-independent [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) $V(x)$ during the stated interval. Differentiation gives

$$
\dot x=nAt^{n-1},\qquad \ddot x=n(n-1)At^{n-2}.
$$

For $n\ne0$, choose a branch on which $t=(x/A)^{1/n}$ is real. [Conservation of energy](../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) is the quickest integration of [Newton's second law](../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md):

$$
\frac12m\dot x^2+V(x)=E,
\qquad
\boxed{V(x)=E-\frac{mn^2A^2}{2}\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{2-2/n}.}
$$

The additive constant $E$ is arbitrary. For instance, the displayed expression applies directly for $t>0$, $A\ne0$ and $x/A>0$, without raising a possibly negative $A$ to a fractional power. Its [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) gives $-V'(x)=mn(n-1)At^{n-2}$ along the trajectory, as required. The second term has units of [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) because $A$ has units $\mathrm{m\,s^{-n}}$.

For $n=1$, the [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) is constant, so the [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) is constant on the visited interval; the expression above reduces to such a constant. For $n=0$, or $A=0$, the particle is stationary: the trajectory only fixes $V'$ to vanish at its position and does not determine the [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) elsewhere. These exceptional cases cannot determine a unique nonconstant function.
