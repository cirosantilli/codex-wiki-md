<h1 id="section-c/c10/reversible-adiabats/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Reversible adiabats](../reversible-adiabats.md)

Over the plotted range, approximate the [surface tension](../../../../../../surface-tension-split.md) by $\gamma(T)=\gamma_0-\beta T$ with constant positive $\beta=-\gamma'$, and take $C_A=70\,\mathrm{mJ\,K^{-1}}=0.070\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$. A reversible adiabat has

$$
0=dS=\frac{C_A}{T}\,dT+\beta\,dA.
$$

Integrating gives

$$
\boxed{C_A\ln\left(\frac{T}{T_*}\right)+\beta A=\text{constant},
\qquad \beta\simeq1.3\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-2}K^{-1}}.}
$$

The arbitrary fixed reference [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) $T_*$ makes the [logarithm](../../../../../../logarithm.md) dimensionless; changing it only shifts the integration constant. This is the dimensionally precise version of writing $C_A\ln T+\beta A$ as constant. Each term has units of [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md). Between two states,

$$
\boxed{T_f=T_i\exp\left(-\frac{\beta\Delta A}{C_A}\right).}
$$

If the graph had substantial [curvature](../../../../../../curvature.md), the [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) differential would still hold, but this particular constant-$\beta$ exponential would no longer follow.
