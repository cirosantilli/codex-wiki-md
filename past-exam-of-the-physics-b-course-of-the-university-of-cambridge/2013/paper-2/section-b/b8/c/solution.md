<h1 id="section-b/b8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For an attractive [inverse-square potential](../../../../../../inverse-square-potential.md), let the [reduced mass](../../../../../../reduced-mass.md) be $\mu$, potential $-k/r$, and nonzero orbital [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) $\ell$. Conservation of [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) makes the orbit planar. The orbit equation is

$$
\boxed{r=\frac{\ell^2/(\mu k)}{1+e\cos\varphi},\qquad
e^2=1+\frac{2E\ell^2}{\mu k^2}.}
$$

Positive relative [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) gives $e>1$ and an unbound [hyperbolic Kepler orbit](../../../../../../hyperbolic-kepler-orbit.md), arriving from and returning to infinity. Its asymptotes satisfy $\cos\varphi_\infty=-1/e$. At infinity $E=\mu v_\infty^2/2$ and $\ell=\mu b v_\infty$ for [impact parameter](../../../../../../impact-parameter.md) $b$, so the deflection angle is

$$
\boxed{\chi=2\arcsin(1/e)
=2\arctan\frac{k}{\mu b v_\infty^2}.}
$$

This describes gravitational encounters in the Newtonian two-body approximation. The body speeds up near [periapsis](../../../../../../periapsis.md) while total [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) remains positive. It is distinct from an elliptic bound orbit and from the zero-energy parabolic limit; repulsive Coulomb scattering also gives hyperbolae but uses a different orientation of the force and orbit.
