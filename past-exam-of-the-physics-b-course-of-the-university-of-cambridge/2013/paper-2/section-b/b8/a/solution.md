<h1 id="section-b/b8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Generalized coordinates](../../../../../../generalized-coordinate.md) incorporate [holonomic constraints](../../../../../../holonomic-constraint.md) without solving separately for every constraint force. For conservative mechanics use $L=T-V$. The [principle of stationary action](../../../../../../principle-of-stationary-action.md) makes the [action](../../../../../../action.md) $\int L\,dt$ stationary for variations vanishing at the endpoints. [Integration by parts](../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}=0.}
$$

The [canonical momentum](../../../../../../canonical-momentum.md) is $p_i=\partial L/\partial\dot q_i$. A [cyclic coordinate](../../../../../../cyclic-coordinate.md) gives conserved $p_i$; absent explicit time dependence, $\sum_i p_i\dot q_i-L$ is conserved. These are symmetry–conservation relationships, not assumptions that all momenta are constant. Nonconservative generalized forces enter the right side.

For example, a pendulum has $L=m\ell^2\dot\theta^2/2+mg\ell\cos\theta$, giving $m\ell^2\ddot\theta+mg\ell\sin\theta=0$. The method extends directly to coupled coordinates and velocity-dependent interactions when the correct [Lagrangian](../../../../../../lagrangian.md) is used; $T-V$ with a position-only potential is not the most general form.
