<h1 id="section-b/b8/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For a homogeneous isotropic solid with small displacement $\mathbf u$, [linear elasticity](../../../../../../linear-elasticity.md) gives $\sigma_{ij}=\lambda_L(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u)\delta_{ij}+2\mu e_{ij}$. [Momentum](../../../../../../momentum-split.md) balance becomes

$$
\rho\,\partial_t^2\mathbf u=(\lambda_L+\mu)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u)+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf u.
$$

A [plane wave](../../../../../../plane-wave.md) with displacement parallel to its [wavevector](../../../../../../wavevector.md) is longitudinal, whereas perpendicular displacement is transverse. Writing $K=\lambda_L+2\mu/3$ for the [bulk modulus](../../../../../../bulk-modulus.md) and $\mu$ for the [shear modulus](../../../../../../shear-modulus.md), their bulk speeds are

$$
\boxed{c_P=\sqrt{\frac{K+4\mu/3}{\rho}},\qquad c_S=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\rho}}.}
$$

There are two independent transverse polarizations. The [bulk modulus](../../../../../../bulk-modulus.md) supplies resistance to compression; shear rigidity supplies transverse restoring [stress](../../../../../../stress.md). An ordinary fluid has no static shear rigidity and cannot sustain these propagating bulk shear waves. A thin rod with freely contracting sides instead has longitudinal [speed](../../../../../../speed.md) $\sqrt{Y/\rho}$, because its lateral [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) differ from a bulk [plane wave](../../../../../../plane-wave.md). [Reflection](../../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md), [refraction](../../../../../../refraction.md) and mode conversion occur at interfaces; a free surface also supports [Rayleigh waves](../../../../../../rayleigh-wave.md).
