↑ **Parent:** [B7](../b7.md)

[Magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) $\mathbf M$ is [magnetic dipole moment](../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) per unit volume. In SI units,

$$
\mathbf B=\mu_0(\mathbf H+\mathbf M),\qquad
\mathbf M=\chi\mathbf H,\qquad \mu=\mu_0(1+\chi)
$$

in a linear isotropic response. The internal field matters: for a uniformly magnetized [ellipsoid](../../../../../ellipsoid.md) it is $\mathbf H_{\rm int}=\mathbf H_{\rm applied}-\mathbf N_d\mathbf M$. Sample shape therefore affects an apparent [magnetic susceptibility](../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md).

**[Diamagnetism](../../../../../diamagnetism.md)** opposes the applied field, giving small negative [magnetic susceptibility](../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md) from the induced orbital response. It is often only weakly [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) dependent. **[Paramagnetism](../../../../../paramagnetism.md)** aligns pre-existing moments and gives positive [magnetic susceptibility](../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md); independent localized moments obey approximately $\chi=C/T$ in weak fields before saturating. [Pauli paramagnetism](../../../../../pauli-paramagnetism.md) of conduction electrons is a different, weakly temperature-dependent mechanism.

**[Ferromagnetism](../../../../../ferromagnetism.md)** involves collective exchange-driven alignment and spontaneous [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) below the [Curie temperature](../../../../../curie-temperature.md). A specimen can nevertheless have nearly zero total moment because [magnetic domains](../../../../../magnetic-domain.md) point in different directions. Field-driven domain-wall motion and [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) change the net [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md). [Antiferromagnets](../../../../../antiferromagnetism.md) have compensating ordered sublattices below a [Néel temperature](../../../../../neel-temperature.md); [ferrimagnets](../../../../../ferrimagnetism.md) have oppositely ordered but unequal sublattice moments and retain a net moment. Ferrites are useful examples of electrically resistive ferrimagnetic core materials.

Ferromagnetic response is nonlinear and history dependent. A [hysteresis](../../../../../hysteresis.md) loop displays [remanent magnetization](../../../../../remanent-magnetization.md), [magnetic coercivity](../../../../../magnetic-coercivity.md) and saturation. A single constant [magnetic permeability](../../../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md) cannot describe the entire loop: differential [magnetic permeability](../../../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md) describes its local slope. The quasistatic [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) loss per unit volume and cycle is the loop [area](../../../../../surface-area.md) $\oint H\,dB$. [Soft magnetic materials](../../../../../soft-magnetic-material.md) have low [magnetic coercivity](../../../../../magnetic-coercivity.md) and usually high useful [magnetic permeability](../../../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md); [hard magnetic materials](../../../../../hard-magnetic-material.md) retain [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) and suit permanent magnets.

**An [electromagnet](../../../../../electromagnet.md) normally uses a soft, high-permeability core** to guide flux generated by a coil. For a uniform-section core with length $\ell_c$ and air gap $g$, neglecting leakage and fringing,

$$
NI\simeq H_c\ell_c+\frac{Bg}{\mu_0},\qquad
\boxed{B\simeq\frac{\mu_0NI}{g+\ell_c/\mu_r}.}
$$

An air gap can dominate the [magnetic reluctance](../../../../../magnetic-reluctance.md) even when the core [magnetic permeability](../../../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md) is large. Core saturation limits the improvement with current, while winding resistance causes heating. For alternating operation, laminations suppress eddy-current loops in metallic cores; high-resistivity ferrites also reduce those losses. Low [hysteresis](../../../../../hysteresis.md) loss is important in repeatedly cycled cores, whereas high [magnetic coercivity](../../../../../magnetic-coercivity.md) is valuable in a permanent magnet. Useful design therefore depends on the full magnetic response, electrical losses and operating [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md), rather than the largest quoted [magnetic permeability](../../../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md) alone.
