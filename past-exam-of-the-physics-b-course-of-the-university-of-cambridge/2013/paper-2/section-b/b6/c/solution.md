<h1 id="section-b/b6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**A current in one circuit links [magnetic flux](../../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) through another.** It produces [magnetic flux linkage](../../../../../../magnetic-flux-linkage.md) through another. With fixed geometry and a linear reciprocal medium, choose consistent winding orientations and write

$$
\Lambda_1=L_1I_1+MI_2,\qquad \Lambda_2=MI_1+L_2I_2.
$$

The shared coefficient is **[mutual inductance](../../../../../../mutual-inductance.md)**; reversal of one winding reverses its sign. [Faraday's law of induction](../../../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) gives the mutual induced emf $\mathcal E_2=-M\dot I_1$ for fixed $M$. The minus sign expresses [Lenz's law](../../../../../../lenz-s-law.md).

The [magnetic energy](../../../../../../magnetic-energy.md) is

$$
W=\tfrac12L_1I_1^2+MI_1I_2+\tfrac12L_2I_2^2.
$$

Nonnegative [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) for every current pair requires $|M|\leq\sqrt{L_1L_2}$, motivating the coupling coefficient $M/\sqrt{L_1L_2}$. Transformers use strong common flux to exchange [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) between circuits; leakage flux reduces coupling. A shared core increases linkage, but saturation, [hysteresis](../../../../../../hysteresis.md) and eddy-current losses limit the linear description. A moving geometry also contributes $-I_1\dot M$ to the mutual emf.
