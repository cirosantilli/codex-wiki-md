<h1 id="section-a/a4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A4](../a4.md)

Use the [rotating reference frame](../../../../../rotating-reference-frame.md) equilibrium model, in which the [centrifugal potential](../../../../../centrifugal-potential.md) [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) of a particle of [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $m_i$ is $U_i(r)=-m_i\omega^2r^2/2$. The [Boltzmann distribution](../../../../../boltzmann-distribution.md) then gives

$$
\frac{n_i(r)}{n_i(a)}=
\exp\left[\frac{m_i\omega^2(r^2-a^2)}{2k_BT}\right].
$$

Define the concentration ratio as $c=n_1/n_2$. At the axis,

$$
\boxed{c_{\rm axis}=c_{\rm wall}
\exp\left[\frac{(m_2-m_1)\omega^2a^2}{2k_BT}\right].}
$$

Thus the axis favours the lighter species. The expression assumes a dilute classical mixture at one [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), equilibrium in the [rotating reference frame](../../../../../rotating-reference-frame.md), and sampling sufficiently close to $r=0$.
