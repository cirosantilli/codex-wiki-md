<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

In the ideal [semidiurnal tide](../../../../../semidiurnal-tide.md) model, successive high tides occur after the [Earth](../../../../../earth.md)–[Moon](../../../../../moon.md) relative angle advances by $\pi$. Since the motions have the same sense, subtract their [angular speeds](../../../../../angular-speed.md):

$$
\boxed{\Delta t=\frac{\pi}{\Omega_E-\Omega_M}
=\frac1{2(T_E^{-1}-T_M^{-1})}.}
$$

The lunar [orbital period](../../../../../orbital-period.md) is sidereal, so the consistent terrestrial [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) period is $T_E=23.9345\,\mathrm h$. With $T_M=27.3\times24\,\mathrm h$, this gives **about 12 h 25 min**. The sidereal terrestrial period is distinct from the 24-hour solar day.[Orbital parameters, sidereal rotation period 23.9345 h and length of day 24 h](../../../../../earth-fact-sheet-by-national-aeronautics-and-space-administration.md)

If the [Earth](../../../../../earth.md)'s [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) is approximated as one revolution in 24 hours, the same calculation instead gives **12 h 27 min**. That small difference is the day convention, not a change of sign in the relative [angular speed](../../../../../angular-speed.md). Actual coastal [tide](../../../../../tide.md) times also depend on ocean dynamics and the solar [tide](../../../../../tide.md).
