<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

For the [uniform magnetic field in an off-axis cylindrical cavity](../../../../../uniform-magnetic-field-in-an-off-axis-cylindrical-cavity.md), let $\mathbf b$ point from the conductor's axis to the hole's axis and let the current flow in $+z$. Its uniform [current density](../../../../../current-density.md) in the metal is

$$
\mathbf J=\frac{I}{\pi(R^2-a^2)}\hat{\mathbf z}.
$$

Superpose a complete cylinder carrying $\mathbf J$ and a cylinder occupying the hole carrying $-\mathbf J$. Inside a uniformly filled cylinder, [Ampère's law](../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) gives $\mathbf B=(\mu_0/2)\mathbf J\times\mathbf r$. For any point in the cavity,

$$
\mathbf B=\frac{\mu_0}{2}\bigl[\mathbf J\times\mathbf r-\mathbf J\times(\mathbf r-\mathbf b)\bigr]
=\boxed{\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi(R^2-a^2)}\,\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf b.}
$$

There is no remaining position dependence, so the field is uniform. Its magnitude is $\mu_0Ib/[2\pi(R^2-a^2)]$, perpendicular to the axis-offset [vector](../../../../../vector.md) in the cross-sectional plane. Reversing the current reverses the field.
