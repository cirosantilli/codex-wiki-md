<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

Put [line charges](../../../../../line-charge.md) $+\lambda$ and $-\lambda$ on the two axes. For $d\gg a_1,a_2$, the surface potentials differ by

$$
V_1-V_2\simeq\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}
\left(\ln\frac d{a_1}+\ln\frac d{a_2}\right).
$$

Thus the [capacitance per unit length](../../../../../capacitance-per-unit-length.md) in vacuum is

$$
\boxed{C'=\frac{2\pi\epsilon_0}{\ln[d^2/(a_1a_2)]}
=\frac{\pi\epsilon_0}{\ln[d/\sqrt{a_1a_2}]}.}
$$

The two logarithms belong to one potential difference, not two independent [capacitances](../../../../../capacitance.md) in parallel. A homogeneous [dielectric](../../../../../dielectric.md) replaces $\epsilon_0$ by its absolute [permittivity](../../../../../permittivity-split.md); finite radii and proximity corrections are neglected.
