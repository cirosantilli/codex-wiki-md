<h1 id="section-a/a1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A1](../a1.md)

For [hydrostatic volume strain from Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio](../../../../../hydrostatic-volume-strain-from-young-s-modulus-and-poisson-s-ratio.md) in isotropic [linear elasticity](../../../../../linear-elasticity.md), the [bulk modulus](../../../../../bulk-modulus.md) is $K=E/[3(1-2\nu)]=5.56\,\mathrm{GPa}$. Hydrostatic compression therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\Delta V=-\frac{pV}{K}=-1.82\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}\simeq-18.2\,\mathrm{cm^3}.}
$$

Here $p=101325\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is the [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) increase, and the negative sign denotes contraction. Equivalently each normal [strain](../../../../../strain.md) is $-(1-2\nu)p/E$ and the three [strains](../../../../../strain.md) add. This is the small-strain isotropic model; concrete's microstructure is represented only through the supplied elastic constants.
