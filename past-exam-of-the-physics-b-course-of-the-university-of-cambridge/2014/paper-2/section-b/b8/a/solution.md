<h1 id="section-b/b8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An isotropic linear elastic solid has two independent elastic constants. For infinitesimal [strain](../../../../../../strain.md),

$$
\sigma_{ij}=2G\epsilon_{ij}+\lambda\epsilon_{kk}\delta_{ij},
\qquad K=\lambda+\frac23G.
$$

The [shear modulus](../../../../../../shear-modulus.md) $G$ governs shape change and the [bulk modulus](../../../../../../bulk-modulus.md) $K$ governs volume change. [Young's modulus](../../../../../../young-s-modulus.md) $E$ and [Poisson's ratio](../../../../../../poisson-s-ratio.md) $\nu$ are equivalent parameters:

$$
G=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu)},\qquad K=\frac{E}{3(1-2\nu)}.
$$

Positive [strain](../../../../../../strain.md) [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) requires $G,K>0$, hence $-1<\nu<1/2$; $\nu\to1/2$ is the incompressible limit. Small-amplitude bulk waves have speeds $c_P=\sqrt{(K+4G/3)/\rho}$ and $c_S=\sqrt{G/\rho}$. **Isotropy removes directional dependence, not the distinction between compression and shear.** These laws apply within the elastic small-strain regime, not after yielding or fracture.
