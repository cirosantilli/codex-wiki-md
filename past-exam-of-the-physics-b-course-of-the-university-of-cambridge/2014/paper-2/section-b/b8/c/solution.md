<h1 id="section-b/b8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For two bodies interacting through $U(\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2)$, define

$$
\mathbf R=\frac{m_1\mathbf r_1+m_2\mathbf r_2}{M},\quad
\mathbf r=\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2,\quad
M=m_1+m_2,\quad\mu=\frac{m_1m_2}{M}.
$$

Their [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) separates exactly:

$$
T=\frac12M\dot{\mathbf R}^{\,2}+\frac12\mu\dot{\mathbf r}^{\,2}.
$$

Without external forces, the [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) moves uniformly while $\mu\ddot{\mathbf r}=-\nabla U$. **The relative orbit is a one-body problem with [reduced mass](../../../../../../reduced-mass.md) $\mu$**, not generally either individual [mass](../../../../../../mass.md). For $m_2\gg m_1$, $\mu\simeq m_1$; for equal masses $m$, $\mu=m/2$. If the interaction is central, relative [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) is conserved and the relative motion is planar.
