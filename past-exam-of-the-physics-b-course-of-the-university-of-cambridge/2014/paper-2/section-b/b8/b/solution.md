<h1 id="section-b/b8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a rigid body about a chosen origin,

$$
I_{ij}=\int\rho(\mathbf r)(r^2\delta_{ij}-r_ir_j)\,dV,
\qquad \mathbf L=\mathbf I\boldsymbol\omega,
\qquad T_{\rm rot}=\frac12\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\mathbf I\boldsymbol\omega.
$$

The [inertia tensor](../../../../../../inertia-tensor.md) is real and symmetric, so [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) [principal axes](../../../../../../principal-axis.md) diagonalize it. [Angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) is parallel to [angular velocity](../../../../../../angular-velocity.md) for rotation about a [principal axis](../../../../../../principal-axis.md), but need not be parallel in a general direction. Its [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are the [principal moments of inertia](../../../../../../principal-moment-of-inertia.md).

If the origin is translated from the [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) by $\mathbf a$, the parallel-axis theorem becomes $I_{ij}=I_{ij}^{\rm CM}+M(a^2\delta_{ij}-a_ia_j)$. In body axes, Euler's equation is $\boldsymbol\tau=(d\mathbf L/dt)_{\rm body}+\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf L$. Thus **the full [tensor](../../../../../../tensor.md) is needed whenever the rotation is not confined to one fixed [principal axis](../../../../../../principal-axis.md)**.
