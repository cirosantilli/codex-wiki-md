<h1 id="section-b/b8/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[Dynamic viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) measures resistance to a [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) gradient. In Newtonian simple shear, $\tau_{xy}=\eta\,\partial u_x/\partial y$; positive [viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) dissipates mechanical [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) into [heat](../../../../../../heat-split.md). [Kinematic viscosity](../../../../../../kinematic-viscosity.md) is $\nu=\eta/\rho$. For an incompressible fluid of constant [viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md),

$$
\rho\left(\partial_t\mathbf u+\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\mathbf u\right)
=-\nabla p+\eta\nabla^2\mathbf u+\mathbf f.
$$

Scaling [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) by $U$ and length by $L$ gives the [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md) $\mathrm{Re}=\rho UL/\eta=UL/\nu$, the ratio of inertial to viscous terms. At small [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md), creeping-flow approximations neglect inertia; at large [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md), inertia dominates much of the bulk but [viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) can remain crucial in boundary layers. **A large [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md) permits instability and [turbulence](../../../../../../turbulence-split.md); it does not by itself specify a universal transition threshold.** Geometry and disturbances also matter.
