In [magnetostatics](../../../../../../magnetostatics.md), [Ampère's circuital law](../../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) in vacuum is

$$
\oint_C\mathbf B\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=\mu_0I_{\rm enc},
\qquad\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf J.
$$

It is especially useful when symmetry makes the tangential field constant on an [Amperian loop](../../../../../../amperian-loop.md). It gives $B=\mu_0I/(2\pi r)$ for an infinite straight wire, $B\simeq\mu_0nI$ inside a long [solenoid](../../../../../../solenoid.md), and $B\simeq\mu NI/(2\pi r)$ in an ideal [toroid](../../../../../../toroid.md). The long-solenoid and toroidal expressions neglect fringing.

For time-dependent fields, [displacement current](../../../../../../displacement-current.md) is essential:

$$
\oint_C\mathbf B\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell
=\mu_0I_{\rm enc}+\mu_0\epsilon_0\frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}.
$$

In matter, the corresponding macroscopic form is $\oint\mathbf H\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=I_{\rm free}+d\!\int\mathbf D\cdot d\mathbf A/dt$. **The enclosed [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md) alone is sufficient only in the magnetostatic limit.**
