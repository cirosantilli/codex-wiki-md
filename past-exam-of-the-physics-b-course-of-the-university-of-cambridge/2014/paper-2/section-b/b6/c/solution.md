<h1 id="section-b/b6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Because $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$, a [magnetic vector potential](../../../../../../magnetic-vector-potential.md) can be introduced with $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$. It is not unique: $\mathbf A+\nabla\chi$ gives the same field. In [magnetostatics](../../../../../../magnetostatics.md), the [Coulomb gauge](../../../../../../coulomb-gauge.md) $\nabla\cdot\mathbf A=0$ gives $\nabla^2\mathbf A=-\mu_0\mathbf J$ in vacuum. For localized [electric currents](../../../../../../electric-current.md),

$$
\mathbf A(\mathbf r)=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int
\frac{\mathbf J(\mathbf r')}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}\,dV'.
$$

A [magnetic scalar potential](../../../../../../magnetic-scalar-potential.md) is more restricted. In a simply connected current-free region of constant permeability, write $\mathbf B=-\nabla\psi$; then $\nabla^2\psi=0$. Alternatively define the scalar potential for $\mathbf H$, differing by a constant permeability factor. **A single-valued scalar potential generally cannot cover a domain encircling a net [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md)**, even where the local curl vanishes. The [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) potential does not require the field itself to be curl-free.
