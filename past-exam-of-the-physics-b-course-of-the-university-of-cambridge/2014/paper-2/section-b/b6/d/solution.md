<h1 id="section-b/b6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Guided electromagnetic waves propagate along a structure whose boundaries select transverse field patterns. At a perfect conductor the tangential [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) vanishes. In a hollow rectangular guide, TE modes have $E_z=0$ and TM modes have $H_z=0$. Their longitudinal [wavenumber](../../../../../../wavenumber.md) obeys

$$
k_z^2=\frac{\omega^2}{v^2}-k_\perp^2,
\qquad k_\perp^2=\left(\frac{m\pi}{a}\right)^2+
\left(\frac{n\pi}{b}\right)^2.
$$

Below the [waveguide cutoff frequency](../../../../../../waveguide-cutoff-frequency.md) $\omega_c=vk_\perp$, that mode is evanescent rather than freely propagating. TM modes require both indices positive; TE modes may have one zero but not both. For $a>b$, the lowest cutoff is the TE$_{10}$ mode, with $f_c=v/(2a)$. A hollow single-conductor guide has no TEM mode, whereas a two-conductor transmission line can support one without this geometrical cutoff.[§13.4, mode boundary conditions, index restrictions and lowest-cutoff discussion](../../../../../../electromagnetic-fields-and-energy-by-hermann-a-haus-and-james-r-melcher/rectangular-waveguide-modes.md)

For a propagating TE or TM mode, $v_p=v/\sqrt{1-\omega_c^2/\omega^2}$ and $v_g=v\sqrt{1-\omega_c^2/\omega^2}$. The [phase velocity](../../../../../../phase-velocity.md) can exceed $v$ without superluminal information transfer. Frequency-dependent group delay can broaden pulses; excitation of several modes adds intermodal [dispersion](../../../../../../wave-dispersion.md).
