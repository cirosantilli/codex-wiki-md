<h1 id="section-b/b7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](../b7.md)

[Electromagnetic induction](../../../../../electromagnetic-induction.md) produces an [electromotive force](../../../../../electromotive-force.md) when [magnetic flux linkage](../../../../../magnetic-flux-linkage.md) changes. For an oriented moving loop,

$$
\mathcal E=\oint(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell
=-\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}.
$$

For a stationary loop this reduces to [Faraday's law of induction](../../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md), $\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\partial_t\mathbf B$. Motion in a static field can instead supply the motional term. [Lenz's law](../../../../../lenz-s-law.md) gives the minus sign: the induced [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) opposes the flux change, with the required electrical [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) supplied by mechanical [work](../../../../../work.md) or the field source.

For linear fixed-geometry circuits, flux linkages satisfy $\Lambda_1=L_1I_1+MI_2$ and $\Lambda_2=MI_1+L_2I_2$. [Self-inductance](../../../../../self-inductance.md) gives $\mathcal E_1=-L_1\dot I_1$; [mutual inductance](../../../../../mutual-inductance.md) gives the additional term $-M\dot I_2$. Reciprocity gives the same mutual coefficient for both circuits, with its sign determined by winding orientation. The stored field [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is

$$
W_B=\frac12L_1I_1^2+\frac12L_2I_2^2+MI_1I_2,
\qquad |M|\le\sqrt{L_1L_2}.
$$

The bound follows from nonnegative [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) for arbitrary [electric currents](../../../../../electric-current.md). In a linear medium the equivalent field expression is $W_B=\tfrac12\int\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf H\,dV$.

An ideal [electrical transformer](../../../../../transformer-electrical-engineering.md) links both windings with one changing core flux, giving $V_1/V_2=N_1/N_2$ for corresponding voltage magnitudes. A loaded secondary drives an opposing magnetomotive force and increases primary [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md); ideally $I_1/I_2=N_2/N_1$ and input and output powers agree. **A [electrical transformer](../../../../../transformer-electrical-engineering.md) exchanges voltage for [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md), not for extra [power](../../../../../power.md).** Real devices have winding resistance, leakage flux, eddy-current and [hysteresis](../../../../../hysteresis.md) losses, and finite magnetizing [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md). A steady DC input gives no continuing secondary voltage after the switching transient and may cause excessive primary [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) or core saturation.
