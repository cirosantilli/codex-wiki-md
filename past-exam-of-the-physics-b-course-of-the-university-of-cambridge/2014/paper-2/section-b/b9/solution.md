<h1 id="section-b/b9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B9](../b9.md)

A [central potential](../../../../../central-potential.md) conserves [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) and confines a nonradial orbit to a plane. With [reduced mass](../../../../../reduced-mass.md) $\mu$, the radial motion obeys

$$
E=\frac12\mu\dot r^2+U_{\rm eff}(r),\qquad
U_{\rm eff}=U(r)+\frac{L^2}{2\mu r^2}.
$$

Turning points satisfy $E=U_{\rm eff}$. A radially bounded allowed interval gives bound motion, whereas an interval reaching infinity permits escape. The [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) sign alone classifies these only with suitable assumptions about $U(\infty)$ and barriers. Circular orbits satisfy $U_{\rm eff}'=0$ and are radially stable when $U_{\rm eff}''>0$. Bound orbits in a general [central potential](../../../../../central-potential.md) need not close: successive periapses can precess.

For attractive inverse-square gravity, $U=-\kappa/r$ and

$$
r=\frac{p}{1+e\cos\phi},\quad p=\frac{L^2}{\mu\kappa},\quad
 e^2=1+\frac{2EL^2}{\mu\kappa^2}.
$$

Ellipses have $E<0$, $e<1$, and the attracting centre at a focus; parabolic and hyperbolic escape have $e=1$ and $e>1$. Conservation of [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) gives equal swept areas in equal times. The inverse-square law additionally yields $T^2=4\pi^2a^3/[G(m_1+m_2)]$. These are Kepler's laws; a harmonic [central potential](../../../../../central-potential.md) is another example with closed bound orbits, while generic central laws produce [precession](../../../../../precession.md).

In a [gravitational assist](../../../../../gravitational-assist.md), a spacecraft's asymptotic [speed](../../../../../speed.md) relative to a planet is approximately unchanged but its direction turns. Transforming to the frame in which the planet has [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) $\mathbf V$ gives

$$
\Delta\varepsilon=\mathbf V\cdot(\mathbf u_{\rm out}-\mathbf u_{\rm in}).
$$

The spacecraft can gain or lose orbital [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md); the planet supplies the compensating [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md), with a very small [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) change because of its large [mass](../../../../../mass.md).

Repulsive Coulomb scattering instead has $U=\kappa_C/r$. For incident relative [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) $E$ and [impact parameter](../../../../../impact-parameter.md) $b$, the hyperbolic orbit gives

$$
b=\frac{\kappa_C}{2E}\cot\frac\theta2,\qquad
\boxed{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\left(\frac{\kappa_C}{4E}\right)^2
\csc^4\frac\theta2.}
$$

This [Rutherford scattering](../../../../../rutherford-scattering.md) formula follows by equating the incident annulus $2\pi b\,db$ to the scattered solid-angle ring. It assumes classical elastic scattering by a point Coulomb centre; screening, finite nuclear size or quantum effects can change the applicable regime.
