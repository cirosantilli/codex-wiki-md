<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

Use $N$ turns of mean radius $a\simeq1.05\,\mathrm{mm}$, including the wire radius, on a light nonmagnetic rod. With wire radius $r_w$, length $l$ and [density](../../../../../density.md) $\rho$, neglecting helical pitch gives

$$
m_B=NI\pi a^2,\qquad M=2\pi aN\rho\pi r_w^2,
\qquad J=M\left(\frac{l^2}{12}+\frac{a^2}{2}\right).
$$

The last expression is the transverse central [moment of inertia](../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) of the uniformly distributed turns. The [magnetic torque](../../../../../surface-magnetic-torque-on-an-accretion-disk.md) is $-m_BB_h\sin\theta$, so the [small-angle magnetic dipole oscillation](../../../../../small-angle-magnetic-dipole-oscillation.md) obeys $J\ddot\theta+m_BB_h\theta=0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\rho r_w^2}{IaB_h}
\left(\frac{l^2}{12}+\frac{a^2}{2}\right)}\simeq4.0\,\mathrm s.}
$$

The number of turns cancels. Taking the turn radius as $1.00\,\mathrm{mm}$ instead gives $4.04\,\mathrm s$, consistent at the accuracy of the thin-winding model. The rod's inertia, damping and lead-wire [torque](../../../../../torque.md) are neglected.
