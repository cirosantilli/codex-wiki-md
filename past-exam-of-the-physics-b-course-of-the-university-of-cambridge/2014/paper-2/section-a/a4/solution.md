<h1 id="section-a/a4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A4](../a4.md)

For slow gyroscopic [precession](../../../../../precession.md), the gravitational [torque](../../../../../torque.md) turns the spin [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md): $Mg\ell\sin\theta=\Omega I_3\omega\sin\theta$, with $I_3=MR^2/2$. Thus

$$
\Omega\simeq\frac{2g\ell}{R^2\omega},\qquad
\boxed{T_p\simeq\frac{\pi R^2\omega}{g\ell}
=1.61\,\mathrm s,\qquad\omega=40\pi\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}.}
$$

The inclination cancels at this order. More precisely, [uniform precession of a heavy symmetric top](../../../../../uniform-precession-of-a-heavy-symmetric-top.md) satisfies

$$
\frac{I_1}{M}\cos\theta\,\Omega^2
-\frac{I_3}{M}\omega\Omega+g\ell=0,
\qquad \frac{I_1}{M}=\frac{R^2}{4}+\ell^2.
$$

Its slow root gives $T_p\simeq1.57\,\mathrm s$. **About $1.6\,\mathrm s$** is the slow-precession answer; arbitrary initial conditions can also produce [nutation](../../../../../nutation.md) rather than exact steady [precession](../../../../../precession.md).
