<h1 id="section-a/a5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A5](../a5.md)

Assume uniform charge per unit [length](../../../../../length.md) and the same angular [speed](../../../../../speed.md) $\Omega$. A charge element at distance $r$ has $\mathbf v=\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r$, so the [Biot-Savart law](../../../../../biot-savart-law.md) gives the [centre field of a rigidly rotating charged loop](../../../../../centre-field-of-a-rigidly-rotating-charged-loop.md) as $dB=\mu_0\Omega\,dq/(4\pi r)$. For one square side, with $b=a/2$,

$$
\int_{-b}^{b}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+b^2}}=2\ln(1+\sqrt2).
$$

Using $dq=Q\,dx/(4a)$ on each side gives

$$
B_{\square}=\frac{\mu_0Q\Omega}{2\pi a}\ln(1+\sqrt2),\qquad
B_{\circ}=\frac{\mu_0Q\Omega}{2\pi a}.
$$

Thus **$B_{\square}/B_{\circ}=\ln(1+\sqrt2)\simeq0.8814$**: the circular field is larger by $1/\ln(1+\sqrt2)\simeq1.135$. This is a rigidly rotating charge distribution, not a current constrained to the sides of a stationary square.
