<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

An [electron](../../../../../electron.md) has [electric charge](../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) $-e$, so its gain of [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) through an accelerating [electric potential difference](../../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) of magnitude $V$ is $eV$. Starting from rest, [conservation of energy](../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) gives

$$
\frac12m_ev^2=eV,\qquad
v=\sqrt{\frac{2eV}{m_e}}.
$$

With $e=1.60218\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm C$, [electron mass](../../../../../electron-mass.md) $m_e=9.10938\times10^{-31}\ \mathrm{kg}$, and $V=150\ \mathrm V$,

$$
\boxed{v=7.26\times10^6\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

This is a speed; the [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) points along the accelerating path, opposite the [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md). Here $v/c\simeq0.0242$, where $c$ is the [speed of light](../../../../../speed-of-light.md), so the classical [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) approximation is accurate. As a check, [special relativity](../../../../../special-relativity-split.md) gives $\gamma=1+eV/(m_ec^2)$ and $v=c\sqrt{1-\gamma^{-2}}\simeq7.262\times10^6\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, agreeing to the quoted precision.
