<h1 id="section-c/c9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The starting point is [Gauss's law for magnetism](../../../../../../gauss-s-law-for-magnetism.md), $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$: a [flux tube](../../../../../../flux-tube.md) carries the same [magnetic flux](../../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) through successive transverse sections. To identify that tube approximately with the physical core, one must also make a negligible-leakage approximation. The large core [magnetic permeability](../../../../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md) makes the core route much less reluctant than competing paths through the surrounding air; the small gap further reduces the incentive for a long external return path. The gentle taper, $s\gg R-r$, lets the field follow the changing section with only small transverse corrections.

For two cuts and the intervening lateral [surface](../../../../../../topological-surface-split.md), the [divergence theorem](../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) gives $\Phi_2-\Phi_1=-\Phi_{\rm side}$. Neglecting lateral leakage gives

$$
\boxed{\Phi\simeq B(x)A(x)\simeq\text{constant}.}
$$

The normal [magnetic flux density](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) is continuous at each core–air interface by part (a), so this same [magnetic flux](../../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) crosses the gap. To use the gap's geometrical area $\pi r^2$ in the later formulas, the [magnetic fringing](../../../../../../magnetic-fringing.md) must also be negligible, normally requiring $h\ll r$. This is the small-gap interpretation of the paper's approximation. Large [magnetic permeability](../../../../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md) alone does not eliminate leakage or [magnetic fringing](../../../../../../magnetic-fringing.md) for arbitrary gap geometry.
