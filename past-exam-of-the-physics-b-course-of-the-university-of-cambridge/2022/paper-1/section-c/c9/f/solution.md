<h1 id="section-c/c9/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Set $\alpha=r/R<1$. Comparing with the untapered expression in part (d), using the same $N,I,\mu,h,L$ and large radius $R$,

$$
\frac{H_{\rm gap,taper}}{H_{\rm gap,untaper}}
=\frac{\mu h+L}{\mu h+L\alpha^2+2s\alpha}.
$$

The stated limit $s/L\ll\alpha$ makes $2s\alpha\ll L\alpha^2$, so

$$
\boxed{\frac{H_{\rm gap,taper}}{H_{\rm gap,untaper}}\simeq
\frac{\mu h+L}{\mu h+L\alpha^2}>1.}
$$

The taper concentrates [magnetic flux](../../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) into a smaller pole-face area, increasing the gap [magnetic field intensity](../../../../../../magnetic-field-intensity.md) and [magnetic flux density](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) for the same winding [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md). If $\mu h\ll L\alpha^2$, the enhancement approaches $1/\alpha^2=(R/r)^2$. If instead $\mu h\gg L$, the gap dominates the [magnetic reluctance](../../../../../../magnetic-reluctance.md) and the enhancement approaches unity: both fields approach $NI/h$.

The benefit is therefore strongest when the untapered core's contribution is significant, while the taper lengths contribute little. The same-core-length convention is the comparison defined by parts (d) and (e); if one instead fixes the full iron path length, the untapered comparator has length $L+2s$, a difference negligible in the stated limit. A smaller gap area can increase the field even though the total [magnetic flux](../../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) need not increase. [Magnetic saturation](../../../../../../magnetic-saturation.md) and [magnetic fringing](../../../../../../magnetic-fringing.md), excluded from this model, limit the ideal concentration.
