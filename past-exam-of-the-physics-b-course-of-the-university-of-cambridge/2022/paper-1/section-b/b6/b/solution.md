<h1 id="section-b/b6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

One application is the slow steady [precession](../../../../../../precession.md) of a rapidly spinning [gyroscope](../../../../../../gyroscope.md) supported at one point. Let its [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) be a distance $\ell$ from the pivot along its spin axis, at angle $\alpha$ to the upward vertical. Write $J\simeq I_1\omega$ for its dominant spin [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md). In a [reference frame](../../../../../../reference-frame.md) precessing steadily about the vertical with [angular velocity](../../../../../../angular-velocity.md) $\boldsymbol\Omega_p$, the spin [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) is approximately stationary, so

$$
\mathbf G\simeq\boldsymbol\Omega_p\times\mathbf J.
$$

The gravitational [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) has magnitude $mg\ell\sin\alpha$. The [cross product](../../../../../../cross-product.md) on the right has magnitude $\Omega_pJ\sin\alpha$, giving, for a nonvertical axis,

$$
\boxed{\Omega_p\simeq\frac{mg\ell}{I_1\omega}.}
$$

Its direction follows from the [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) equation. This is the slow [precession](../../../../../../precession.md) approximation: the additional [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) associated with [precession](../../../../../../precession.md) is small compared with the spin contribution.
