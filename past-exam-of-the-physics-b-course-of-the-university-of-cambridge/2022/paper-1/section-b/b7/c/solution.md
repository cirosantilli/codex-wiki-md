<h1 id="section-b/b7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

By vertical [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) balance and reflection [symmetry](../../../../../../symmetry-physics.md), each support exerts an upward [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) $W/2$. Use the paper's convention in which the sagging [bending moment](../../../../../../bending-moment.md) is negative. For a cut at $0<x<L/2$, the left support's moment about that cut gives

$$
B(x)=-\frac W2x.
$$

For $L/2<x<L$, include the central downward [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) when taking moments of all loads to the left:

$$
B(x)=-\frac W2x+W\left(x-\frac L2\right)=-\frac W2(L-x).
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{B(x)=\begin{cases}-Wx/2,&0<x<L/2,\\-W(L-x)/2,&L/2<x<L.\end{cases}}
$$

The [bending moment](../../../../../../bending-moment.md) is continuous at the load, with $B(L/2)=-WL/4$, and vanishes at both freely rotating supports. The jump is in its first [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md), reflecting the concentrated transverse [force](../../../../../../force-split.md).
