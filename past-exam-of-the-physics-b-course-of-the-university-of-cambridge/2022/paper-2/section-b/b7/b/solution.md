<h1 id="section-b/b7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

An [electrical insulator](../../../../../../electrical-insulator.md) has no freely conducting bulk charge in the ideal static description, but an applied [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) can displace bound positive and negative charge relative to one another. The resulting [electric dipole moments](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) per unit [volume](../../../../../../volume.md) define the [polarization density](../../../../../../polarization-density.md) $\mathbf P$. Distortion of electronic charge clouds, relative displacement of [ions](../../../../../../ion.md), and partial alignment of permanent molecular [electric dipoles](../../../../../../electric-dipole.md) can all contribute. Thermal agitation opposes the last mechanism.

For a weak field in a linear [isotropic](../../../../../../isotropy.md) [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md), the constitutive law is

$$
\mathbf P=\epsilon_0\chi_e\mathbf E,\qquad
\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P=\epsilon\mathbf E,\qquad
\epsilon=\epsilon_0(1+\chi_e)=\epsilon_0\epsilon_r.
$$

Here $\chi_e$ is the [electric susceptibility](../../../../../../electric-susceptibility.md), $\epsilon$ is the [permittivity](../../../../../../permittivity-split.md), and $\mathbf D$ is the [electric displacement field](../../../../../../electric-displacement-field.md). In an [anisotropic](../../../../../../anisotropy.md) material, $\chi_e$ is a [tensor](../../../../../../tensor.md) and $\mathbf P$ need not be parallel to $\mathbf E$. These response laws describe the macroscopic field, not necessarily the microscopic field acting on one [molecule](../../../../../../molecule-split.md).

Spatial variation and surface termination of the [polarization density](../../../../../../polarization-density.md) give [bound charge and bound current](../../../../../../bound-charge-and-bound-current.md) through the bound charge densities

$$
\rho_b=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P,\qquad \sigma_b=\mathbf P\cdot\hat{\mathbf n},
$$

where $\hat{\mathbf n}$ is outward from the material. Thus uniform [polarization density](../../../../../../polarization-density.md) has no bulk bound charge, but generally has opposite bound charges on opposing surfaces. [Gauss's law](../../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) can be rewritten as

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho_{\rm free}.
$$

With no [free surface](../../../../../../free-surface.md) charge, the normal component of $\mathbf D$ is continuous across an interface; the tangential [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is continuous in [electrostatics](../../../../../../electrostatics-split.md).

For example, fill a [parallel-plate capacitor](../../../../../../parallel-plate-capacitor.md) of plate area $A$ and gap $d$ with a uniform [dielectric](../../../../../../dielectric.md), neglecting fringing. At fixed free plate charge $Q$, $D=Q/A$, so

$$
E=\frac{Q}{\epsilon A},\qquad V=Ed,\qquad
\boxed{C=\frac{\epsilon A}{d}=\epsilon_rC_0.}
$$

The induced bound surface charges oppose the original field, reducing the [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) and potential difference at fixed $Q$. At fixed applied potential difference, the field remains $V/d$ and the battery instead supplies more free charge. The [electric displacement field](../../../../../../electric-displacement-field.md) separates these free-charge and bound-charge roles.
