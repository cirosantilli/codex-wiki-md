<h1 id="section-b/b7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [method of images](../../../../../../method-of-images.md) solves certain [electrostatics](../../../../../../electrostatics-split.md) problems by replacing a conductor by fictitious [electric charges](../../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) outside the region where the solution is required. The replacement must satisfy the actual boundary values of the [electric potential](../../../../../../electric-potential.md) as well as the prescribed sources. The [uniqueness of Poisson equation](../../../../../../uniqueness-of-poisson-equation.md) then establishes that the constructed [electric potential](../../../../../../electric-potential.md) equals the physical one in that region. The image locations are mathematical devices; they are not real charges inside the metal.

For a [point charge](../../../../../../point-charge.md) $q$ at $(0,0,a)$ above an infinite grounded conducting plane $z=0$, put an image charge $-q$ at $(0,0,-a)$. In a [cylindrical coordinate system](../../../../../../cylindrical-coordinate-system.md) with coordinates $(s,z)$ the potential in the physical region is

$$
\phi(s,z)=\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\frac1{\sqrt{s^2+(z-a)^2}}-\frac1{\sqrt{s^2+(z+a)^2}}\right],\qquad z>0.
$$

This vanishes at $z=0$, has the required singularity at the real charge, and tends to zero at infinity. With [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) $\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi$, the induced [surface charge density](../../../../../../surface-charge-density.md) follows from the normal field immediately above the conductor:

$$
\sigma(s)=\epsilon_0E_z(s,0^+)=-\frac{qa}{2\pi(s^2+a^2)^{3/2}},\qquad
\int_0^\infty\sigma(s)\,2\pi s\,ds=-q.
$$

The [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) on $q$ is the field of the image evaluated at the real charge:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf F=-\frac{q^2}{16\pi\epsilon_0a^2}\hat{\mathbf z}.}
$$

The [electrostatic energy](../../../../../../electrostatic-energy.md) relative to infinite separation is $U=\tfrac12q\phi_{\rm image}=-q^2/(16\pi\epsilon_0a)$. The factor $1/2$ accounts for the fact that the induced charge changes as the real charge is assembled; treating the image as an independently fixed physical charge would give the wrong [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md).

A second standard geometry is a grounded conducting sphere of radius $b$ with a charge $q$ a distance $d>b$ from its centre. Its exterior field is generated by an image $q'=-qb/d$ at distance $b^2/d$ from the centre on the line towards the real charge. On the sphere the distances to the real and image charges are in the ratio $d/b$, making their potentials cancel. For an isolated neutral sphere, an additional central image $+qb/d$ makes the total induced charge zero and leaves the surface an [equipotential](../../../../../../equipotential.md). The construction is powerful for suitable symmetric boundaries, but arbitrary boundaries do not in general admit a finite image system.
