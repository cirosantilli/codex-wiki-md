<h1 id="section-c/c9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Each independent cavity mode is a [quantum harmonic oscillator](../../../../../../quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md) of [angular frequency](../../../../../../angular-frequency.md) $\omega$. Its occupation numbers $n=0,1,\ldots$ represent the numbers of [photons](../../../../../../photon.md) in that mode. At [thermal equilibrium](../../../../../../thermal-equilibrium-split.md), [photons](../../../../../../photon.md) can be absorbed and emitted by the walls, so their number is not conserved and the [photon chemical potential](../../../../../../photon-chemical-potential.md) is zero. The [Boltzmann distribution](../../../../../../boltzmann-distribution.md) of the oscillator excitations then gives

$$
\langle n\rangle=\frac1{e^{\hbar\omega/(k_BT)}-1},\qquad
\bar U=\hbar\omega\langle n\rangle.
$$

The expression supplied in the paper is the thermal excitation [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md). The oscillator's temperature-independent [zero-point energy](../../../../../../zero-point-energy.md) is excluded from the emitted [thermal radiation](../../../../../../thermal-radiation.md).

Putting $\epsilon=\hbar\omega$ and $d\epsilon=\hbar\,d\omega$ into the mode count from (a) gives the number of modes per unit cavity [volume](../../../../../../volume.md):

$$
\frac{dN}{L^3}=\frac{\omega^2}{\pi^2c^3}\,d\omega.
$$

Multiplying by the mean thermal [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) in each mode yields [Planck's law](../../../../../../planck-s-law.md) in the angular-frequency convention:

$$
\boxed{u(T,\omega)\,d\omega=\frac{\omega^2\,d\omega}{\pi^2c^3}\frac{\hbar\omega}{e^{\hbar\omega/(k_BT)}-1}.}
$$

The two [photon polarizations](../../../../../../photon-polarization.md) are already included in the [density of states](../../../../../../density-of-states.md); no further factor of two is needed. $u(T,\omega)d\omega$ has units $\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}$, whereas $u(T,\omega)$ has units $\mathrm{J\,s\,m^{-3}}$.
