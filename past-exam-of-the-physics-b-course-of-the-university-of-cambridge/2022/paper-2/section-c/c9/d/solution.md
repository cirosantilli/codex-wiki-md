<h1 id="section-c/c9/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let the spacecraft radius be $a$, and assume that [heat](../../../../../../heat-split.md) redistribution keeps its whole surface at a common [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) $T$. Neglect background radiation, and let $R\gg r,a$. The Sun's [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md), from the [Stefan–Boltzmann law](../../../../../../stefan-boltzmann-law.md), is

$$
L_S=4\pi r^2\sigma T_S^4.
$$

At distance $R$, the incident [irradiance](../../../../../../irradiance.md) is $F_S=L_S/(4\pi R^2)=\sigma T_S^4(r/R)^2$. The spacecraft intercepts radiation over its projected disc, area $\pi a^2$, but emits from its whole spherical area $4\pi a^2$. The [radiative equilibrium](../../../../../../radiative-equilibrium.md) condition is therefore

$$
\pi a^2\sigma T_S^4\frac{r^2}{R^2}=4\pi a^2\sigma T^4.
$$

Cancelling the common factors gives

$$
\boxed{T=T_S\sqrt{\frac r{2R}}\ \propto\ T_S\sqrt{\frac rR}.}
$$

The radius of the spacecraft cancels. The factor $1/\sqrt2$ follows from the ratio of absorbing projected area to emitting surface area. Uniform surface [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) is the intended additional idealization behind a single equilibrium surface [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). A poorly conducting spacecraft would have position-dependent [temperatures](../../../../../../temperature-split.md); for example, a locally reradiating patch facing the Sun would instead have $T_{\rm subsolar}=T_S\sqrt{r/R}$, and a different maximum-temperature constraint.
