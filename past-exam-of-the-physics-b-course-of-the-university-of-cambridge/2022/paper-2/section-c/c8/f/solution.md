<h1 id="section-c/c8/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

For a diatomic [ideal gas](../../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) in the usual regime with three translational and two rotational quadratic [degrees of freedom](../../../../../../degree-of-freedom.md), but frozen vibrational excitation, the [equipartition theorem](../../../../../../equipartition-theorem.md) gives

$$
C_V=\frac52R_g,\qquad C_p=\frac72R_g,\qquad \gamma=\frac75.
$$

The [Brayton cycle](../../../../../../brayton-cycle.md) efficiency is therefore

$$
\boxed{\eta=1-10^{-2/7}=0.4821\simeq48.2\%.}
$$

The constant-pressure expansion has $T_3/T_2=V_3/V_2=2$, while the compression has $T_2/T_1=10^{2/7}=1.93070$. The same relations give $T_4/T_1=2$. Thus, in units of the minimum [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md),

$$
(T_1,T_2,T_3,T_4)=T_1(1,1.93070,3.86140,2).
$$

The maximum and minimum [temperatures](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) are $T_3$ and $T_1$. A [Carnot cycle](../../../../../../carnot-cycle.md) between those extremes has

$$
\boxed{\eta_{\rm Carnot}=1-\frac{T_1}{T_3}=1-\frac1{2\,10^{2/7}}=0.7410\simeq74.1\%.}
$$

The difference is about $25.9$ percentage points. In the [Brayton cycle](../../../../../../brayton-cycle.md), [heat](../../../../../../heat-split.md) is supplied and rejected over ranges of [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md); the whole input is not supplied at $T_3$, nor is the whole rejection made at $T_1$. This explains the lower efficiency even for mechanically ideal stages. Absolute state [temperatures](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) are not supplied, so $\gamma=7/5$ is an explicit standard diatomic-gas approximation rather than a conclusion about all possible rotational and vibrational excitation regimes.
