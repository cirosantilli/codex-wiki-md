<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**The research challenge is to retain causal control while measuring appearance under the coupled spectral, spatial and temporal variations of actual scenes.** Simply replacing a flat patch with a photograph does not guarantee that either the stimulus or the task is ecologically informative.

A display's RGB values do not specify the spectrum reaching a participant's [cone cells](../../../../cone-cell.md). Primary spectra, viewing geometry, calibration, [luminance](../../../../luminance.md) range and the observer's [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md) and pigment sensitivities matter. A photograph can clip highlights, alter white balance and compress dynamic range; a display cannot necessarily reproduce the original spectrum, [brightness](../../../../brightness.md) or angular [light](../../../../light.md) field. A researcher studying spectral constancy should measure [radiance](../../../../radiance.md) and [illuminants](../../../../illuminant.md) or specify the limits of a display simulation, rather than treating an uncalibrated image as physical ground truth.

Real materials also vary in more than diffuse [reflectance](../../../../reflectance.md). Shape, roughness, specularity, [translucency](../../../../translucency.md) and surrounding illumination jointly determine an image. Changing one rendering parameter can unintentionally change highlight area, contrast and apparent shape. Conversely, holding an image statistic constant can produce an unnatural stimulus. Experiments need independent manipulation of candidate causes and a clear statement of which physical scene properties are represented by the apparatus.

[James M. Kraft](../../../../james-m-kraft.md) and [David H. Brainard](../../../../david-h-brainard.md) demonstrated a productive compromise with a chamber of actual surfaces. They independently controlled illumination and surface reflectances and used a projection colorimeter to let observers adjust a test patch to appear achromatic. Manipulations could hold candidate image statistics approximately fixed while changing the illumination. The results rejected explanations based solely on local adaptation, the image mean or the most intense region.[PNAS 96, 307–312; chamber, independent manipulations and achromatic settings](../../../../mechanisms-of-color-constancy-under-nearly-natural-viewing-by-james-m-kraft-and-david-h-brainard.md) The achievement was not realism alone: it was enough realism combined with manipulations that made competing mechanisms disagree. These results do not show that local adaptation never contributes.

For material appearance, [Roland W. Fleming](../../../../roland-w-fleming.md) and colleagues used rendered objects illuminated by natural illumination distributions and systematically altered those distributions. [Reflectance](../../../../reflectance.md) matching was more reliable under realistic illumination statistics than under some altered statistics.[J Vis 3, 347–368; reflectance matching and illumination-statistics manipulations](../../../../real-world-illumination-and-the-perception-of-surface-reflectance-properties-by-roland-w-fleming-and-others.md) This makes illumination an experimental variable, not merely a nuisance background. It also warns that a model tested only under a small set of studio [lights](../../../../light.md) can exploit cues unavailable or misleading elsewhere.

The dependent variable needs equal care. Selecting the same paint, matching apparent [color](../../../../color.md), adjusting an achromatic point, detecting an [illuminant](../../../../illuminant.md) change and naming a material do not measure the same outcome. A person may correctly infer that two patches are the same material while still noticing an appearance difference. Instructions, response format, adaptation time and available context can alter the task. Eye and head movements change retinal exposure, so fixation constraints should be chosen deliberately and their cost to natural viewing acknowledged.

A strong program therefore combines calibrated simplified stimuli, controlled real scenes, validated rendering and tests on previously unseen objects and illumination. It should sample more than one observer and record relevant visual variation, report both accuracy and uncertainty, and test model generalization across scenes rather than only across random frames of the same scene. Proposed interventions can vary geometry while preserving spectral input, or alter illumination while suppressing a particular cue. Their outcomes must be measured, not assumed. **The goal is interpretable generalization: experimental control sufficient to distinguish mechanisms and scene diversity sufficient to test whether those mechanisms remain useful.**
