# Leaking Atwood machine

↑ **Parent:** [Atwood machine](atwood-machine.md)

Two initially equal buckets of total [mass](mass.md) $M_0$ share an ideal [Atwood machine](atwood-machine.md). If one loses sand at constant rate $\alpha$ and the sand leaves with zero initial velocity relative to that bucket, the leakage supplies no rocket thrust. The remaining masses satisfy $(M+M_0)\dot v=g(M_0-M)$ for upward velocity of the leaking side. Using $\dot M=-\alpha$ and integrating from rest gives $v=(g/\alpha)[M-M_0-2M_0\log((M+M_0)/(2M_0))]$.

If the initial sand fraction is $\epsilon$, emptying occurs at $M=(1-\epsilon)M_0$. This gives the displayed speed, which tends to zero for $\epsilon\to0$ and to $(gM_0/\alpha)(2\log2-1)$ for $\epsilon\to1^-$. The latter is a finite limiting result, not a physical claim about an exactly massless empty bucket. The derivation is an application of a [variable-mass system](variable-mass-system.md), rather than of $d(Mv)/dt=F$ for a closed body.
