# Radiation-pressure laser power measurement

↑ **Parent:** [Radiation pressure](radiation-pressure.md)

A 2013 experiment by [Paul A. Williams](paul-a-williams.md) and collaborators mounted a 25-mm dielectric mirror on a commercial [force-restoration balance](force-restoration-balance.md). Removing its pan-weight compensation spring allowed horizontal [force](force-split.md) sensing with a horizontal [laser](laser-split.md) path. They measured mirror [optical reflectance](reflectance.md) and varied incidence from about $7^\circ$ to $45^\circ$. A 1071-nm [laser](laser-split.md), capable of 530 W, was switched at 0.1 Hz. Digitized [mass](mass.md)-equivalent readings were converted to [force](force-split.md) using $g$; [quadrature demodulation](quadrature-demodulation.md) isolated the switched signal from drift. [Williams and collaborators, pp. 2–3, Figures 1–3 and equation (2): preparation, apparatus and readout](use-of-radiation-pressure-for-measurement-of-high-power-laser-emission-by-paul-a-williams-and-others.md)

[Measurement calibration](measurement-calibration.md) used a 20-g [mass](mass.md), with checks down to 1 mg. The optical [forces](force-split.md) lay below that checked range, limiting the accuracy claim. An angle scan tested the predicted [cosine](cosine.md) dependence at fixed intercepted [power](power.md). Replacing the mirror with a [carbon nanotube](carbon-nanotube.md) coated absorber increased absorbed [power](power.md) while reducing the predicted optical [force](force-split.md); no modulation peak was detected. This bounded gas-heating artifacts under those conditions, rather than proving their universal absence. The fitted slope was about 7% below $2/c$. Inferring [laser](laser-split.md) [power](power.md) requires calibrated [force](force-split.md), [optical reflectance](reflectance.md), [radiometric absorptance](radiometric-absorptance.md) and [angle of incidence](angle-of-incidence.md), with [measurement uncertainty](measurement-uncertainty.md) for each. [Williams and collaborators, pp. 2–4, Figures 3, 5–7: calibration limits, angle and absorber controls, and reported slope discrepancy](use-of-radiation-pressure-for-measurement-of-high-power-laser-emission-by-paul-a-williams-and-others.md)
