# Schwinger correction to the electron magnetic moment

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum electrodynamics](quantum-electrodynamics.md)

The first [QED](quantum-electrodynamics.md) [radiative correction](radiative-correction.md) predicts a positive [anomalous magnetic moment](anomalous-magnetic-moment.md). In [Julian Seymour Schwinger](julian-seymour-schwinger.md)'s 1948 paper On Quantum-Electrodynamics and the Magnetic Moment of the Electron, the extra spin moment is $\delta\mu/\mu=\alpha/(2\pi)$. His $e^2/(\hbar c)$ uses the unrationalized charge convention; in rationalized [natural units](natural-units.md) the same [fine-structure constant](fine-structure-constant.md) is $\alpha=e^2/(4\pi)$. He explicitly connected the result to [mass](mass.md) and [charge renormalization](charge-renormalization.md).[p. 416: free-electron mass and experimental charge; additional spin magnetic moment](https://physics.umd.edu/grt/taj/624c/Schwinger_AnomalousMoment.pdf)

In the [electron spin g-factor](electron-spin-g-factor.md) convention used here,

$$
a_e^{(1)}=\frac{\alpha}{2\pi},\qquad
g_s=2\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}+O(\alpha^2)\right).
$$

For the deliberately rounded teaching input $\alpha=1/137$, $a_e^{(1)}\simeq0.0011617149$ and $g_s^{(1)}\simeq2.0023234298$. The correction is about $0.116\%$ of the [Dirac equation](dirac-equation.md) spin moment. These digits describe the stipulated calculation, not the precision of a measured [fine-structure constant](fine-structure-constant.md) or a current experimental comparison.
