# Raman shift

↑ **Parent:** [Raman scattering](raman-scattering.md)

The signed [Raman shift](raman-shift.md) subtracts scattered from incident [spectroscopic wavenumber](spectroscopic-wavenumber.md):

$$
\Delta\widetilde\nu=\frac{\nu_0-\nu_s}{c}
=\frac{1}{\lambda_0}-\frac{1}{\lambda_s}
=\frac{E_f-E_i}{hc}.
$$

Here $c$ is the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md), and both [wavelengths](wavelength.md) are in [vacuum](vacuum.md). Positive shifts denote [Stokes Raman scattering](stokes-raman-scattering.md); negative shifts denote [Anti-Stokes Raman scattering](anti-stokes-raman-scattering.md). Some plots reverse the horizontal direction or report separate unsigned magnitudes, so check the convention. [Spectroscopic wavenumber](spectroscopic-wavenumber.md) has no $2\pi$ factor, unlike the common angular [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) $k=2\pi/\lambda$. A shift of $1000\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ means $100000\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, not a [frequency](frequency.md) of $1000\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

For a one-quantum change of [molecular vibration](molecular-vibration.md) with ordinary [frequency](frequency.md) $\nu_v$, the magnitude is $\widetilde\nu_v=\nu_v/c$. This makes [Raman spectroscopy](raman-spectroscopy.md) attractive: the same molecular [energy](energy-split.md) spacing can be read using different visible excitation [wavelengths](wavelength.md). Equal and opposite [Raman shifts](raman-shift.md) are not equal and opposite changes measured in nanometres, because [wavelength](wavelength.md) is the reciprocal of [spectroscopic wavenumber](spectroscopic-wavenumber.md).
