# Bacteria

↑ **Parent:** [Prokaryote](prokaryote.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria)

[Bacteria](bacteria.md) are a major evolutionary lineage of [organisms](organism.md) with [prokaryotic](prokaryote.md) cellular organization. [Escherichia coli](escherichia-coli.md) and [cyanobacteria](cyanobacteria.md) illustrate its variety: the same broad cellular plan supports different kinds of [metabolism](metabolism.md). Most [bacteria](bacteria.md) have a [cell wall](cell-wall.md) containing [peptidoglycan](peptidoglycan.md); [archaea](archaea.md) do not have [peptidoglycan](peptidoglycan.md) walls. A [cell wall](cell-wall.md) and a [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md) are distinct structures, and some [bacteria](bacteria.md) lack a [cell wall](cell-wall.md). See [OpenStax Biology 2e, 22.2: bacterial envelopes](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/22-2-structure-of-prokaryotes-bacteria-and-archaea).

The usefulness of recognizing [bacteria](bacteria.md) extends beyond naming: it identifies a shared evolutionary background for comparing [genes](gene.md), [proteins](protein.md) and cellular mechanisms. A familiar shape or the absence of a [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md) alone cannot distinguish [bacteria](bacteria.md) from [archaea](archaea.md); molecular comparisons, including [ribosomal RNA](ribosomal-rna.md), supply additional evidence.
