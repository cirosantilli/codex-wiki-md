# Electrostatic acceleration energy gain

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatic accelerator](electrostatic-accelerator.md)

For a fixed [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $q$, a time-independent [electric potential](electric-potential.md) $\Phi$ and negligible collisions or radiation losses, conservation of [energy](energy-split.md) gives

$$
K_f-K_i=-\Delta(q\Phi)=q(\Phi_i-\Phi_f)=qV.
$$

Here $V$ is the signed potential drop along the trajectory. A [proton](proton.md) starting nearly at rest at $+V$ and arriving at ground gains $eV$: a chosen $500\,\mathrm{kV}$ gives $500\,\mathrm{keV}$, by the definition of the [electronvolt](electronvolt.md). Several intermediate electrodes can distribute that drop along a tube, but the gains telescope to the same endpoint difference. Passing through more electrodes does not multiply the available [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) beyond $qV$.

This is the distinction from a [cyclotron](cyclotron.md). In a static [electric field](electric-field-split.md), a closed trip gives zero net [work](work.md). A [cyclotron](cyclotron.md) changes the gap [electric field](electric-field-split.md) between successive visits, so correctly timed particles repeatedly gain [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md). Its limit involves the [cyclotron resonance condition](cyclotron-resonance-condition.md) and [relativistic detuning of a cyclotron](relativistic-detuning-of-a-cyclotron.md); a single-pass [electrostatic accelerator](electrostatic-accelerator.md) instead requires the full accelerating [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) to be sustained at once. Neither machine's beam energy by itself specifies the [energy](energy-split.md) released by a [nuclear reaction](nuclear-reaction.md).
