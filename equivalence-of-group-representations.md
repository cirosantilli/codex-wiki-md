# Equivalence of group representations

↑ **Parent:** [Group representation](group-representation.md)

Two finite-dimensional [group representations](group-representation.md) $D,E$ over the same [field](field.md) are equivalent if there is one invertible linear map $S$ intertwining all their group actions, equivalently $E(g)=S^{-1}D(g)S$ for every $g$. The same change of basis must work simultaneously for all group elements. The [cyclic property of the trace](cyclic-property-of-the-trace.md) implies that [equivalent representations](equivalence-of-group-representations.md) have identical [characters](character-of-a-representation.md): $\operatorname{tr}(S^{-1}D(g)S)=\operatorname{tr}D(g)$. Consequently a difference at a single group element proves inequivalence. This implication does not need the group to be finite, and the reverse implication is not asserted for arbitrary groups or fields.
