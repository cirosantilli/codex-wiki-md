# Coordinate vector

↑ **Parent:** [Ordered basis](ordered-basis.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_vector)

For an [ordered basis](ordered-basis.md) $\mathcal B=(b_1,\ldots,b_n)$, the coordinate vector $[v]_{\mathcal B}=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)^T$ records the unique [coefficients](coefficient.md) satisfying $v=\sum_i a_i b_i$. [Change of basis](change-of-basis.md) changes this representation while preserving the underlying [vector](vector.md).

For $b_1=(1,0)^T$, $b_2=(1,1)^T$, the [vector](vector.md) $v=(3,1)^T$ has [coordinate vector](coordinate-vector.md) $(2,1)^T$, since $2b_1+b_2=v$. These are the same coefficients extracted by the [dual basis](dual-basis.md) in its worked example. The [vector](vector.md) is independent of the chosen [basis](basis.md); its list of [coordinates](coordinate-linear-algebra.md) is not. In an [orthonormal eigenbasis](orthonormal-eigenbasis.md), the [coordinates](coordinate-linear-algebra.md) additionally separate the action of a [self-adjoint operator](self-adjoint-operator.md) into independent [scalar multiplications](scalar-multiplication.md).
