# Discrete probability distribution

↑ **Parent:** [Probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_probability_distribution)

A [discrete probability distribution](discrete-probability-distribution-split.md) is a [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) concentrated on a finite or [countable set](countable-set.md) of possible values $S$: a [random variable](random-variable-split.md) $X$ with this law satisfies $\mathbb P(X\in S)=1$. Its [probability mass function](probability-mass-function.md) assigns a [probability](probability.md) to each value:

$$
p_X(x)=\mathbb P(X=x),\qquad p_X(x)\geq0,\qquad \sum_{x\in S}p_X(x)=1.
$$

Thus the [probability](probability.md) of an event is obtained by adding masses, rather than integrating a [probability density function](probability-density-function.md). The possible values need not be equally spaced, or even integers. When the relevant moments are finite, its [expected value](expected-value-split.md) and [variance](variance-split.md) are

$$
\mu=\mathbb E X=\sum_{x\in S}xp_X(x),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(X)=\sum_{x\in S}(x-\mu)^2p_X(x)=\mathbb E[X^2]-\mu^2.
$$

For a constructed example, assign values $0,2,5$ masses $0.5,0.3,0.2$. They sum to one; $\mathbb P(X\geq2)=0.3+0.2=0.5$, $\mathbb E X=0(0.5)+2(0.3)+5(0.2)=1.6$, and $\mathbb E[X^2]=4(0.3)+25(0.2)=6.2$. Hence the [variance](variance-split.md) is $6.2-1.6^2=3.64$. A [mean](expected-value-split.md) can lie between possible values: no outcome here equals $1.6$.

The useful organizing question is what an experiment records. One yes/no [indicator random variable](indicator-random-variable.md) gives a [Bernoulli distribution](bernoulli-distribution.md); a success count in a fixed number of [independent](independent-random-variables.md) attempts gives a [binomial distribution](binomial-distribution.md); the attempt number of the first success gives a [geometric distribution](geometric-distribution.md). A [Poisson distribution](poisson-distribution.md) describes an event count under different assumptions. Naming the quantity first helps prevent selecting a familiar formula for the wrong experiment.
