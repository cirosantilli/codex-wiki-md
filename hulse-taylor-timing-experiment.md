# Hulse-Taylor timing experiment

↑ **Parent:** [PSR B1913+16](psr-b1913-plus-16.md)

The surprise was a [pulsar](pulsar.md) whose apparent [period](period-of-a-function.md) would not stay fixed. [Russell Alan Hulse](russell-alan-hulse.md)'s Arecibo search used a multichannel receiver, [pulsar dedispersion](pulsar-dedispersion.md) and computer searches over [period](period-of-a-function.md) and pulse width. Follow-up at higher time resolution retained the changing [period](period-of-a-function.md), excluding inadequate sampling as its explanation. The observed variation exceeded the expected terrestrial [Doppler shift](doppler-effect.md); a predicted reversal from decreasing to increasing [period](period-of-a-function.md) appeared on 16 September 1974.[The Discovery of the Binary Pulsar, lecture delivered 8 December 1993, pp. 53–58 and 62–65, Figures 3–4 and 8–10](the-discovery-of-the-binary-pulsar-by-russell-alan-hulse.md)

The 1975 analysis fitted an eccentric [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md) to [Doppler shifts](doppler-effect.md) inferred from about 200 five-minute [period](period-of-a-function.md) measurements over ten days. That discovery fit was not yet the later precision arrival-time experiment.[L51–L52, section II, Tables 1–2, Figure 1 and equations 1–4](discovery-of-a-pulsar-in-a-binary-system-by-russell-alan-hulse-and-joseph-hooton-taylor-jr.md) An orbit explains both signs of the apparent [period](period-of-a-function.md) change without requiring the [stellar rotation](stellar-rotation.md) to alternate between speeding up and slowing down.
