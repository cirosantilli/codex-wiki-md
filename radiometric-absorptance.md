# Radiometric absorptance

↑ **Parent:** [Radiometry](radiometry-split.md)

[Radiometric absorptance](radiometric-absorptance.md) is the fraction of incident [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) converted to [heat](heat-split.md) in the specified receiver: $P_{\rm abs}=aP_{\rm in}$, with $0\leq a\leq1$ for a passive receiver. Specify the entrance boundary, [wavelength](wavelength.md), incidence [angle](angle.md) and receiver [temperature](temperature-split.md). Radiation reflected or transmitted out of that receiver is not absorbed [power](power.md). A cavity can improve [radiometric absorptance](radiometric-absorptance.md) by giving [light](light.md) repeated opportunities for [wave absorption](wave-absorption.md); a dark coating alone does not establish $a=1$.

For broadband [light](light.md), [spectral radiant flux](spectral-radiant-flux.md) matters: $P_{\rm abs}=\int a(\lambda)\Phi_{e,\lambda}(\lambda)\,d\lambda$. A single effective [radiometric absorptance](radiometric-absorptance.md) therefore depends on the incident [optical spectrum](optical-spectrum.md) as well as on the receiver. If the stated incident boundary lies before a window of known power transmission $\tau_w$, the receiver absorbs $a\tau_wP_{\rm before}$ when other optical losses are absent. Window loss and receiver [radiometric absorptance](radiometric-absorptance.md) are separate corrections.
