# Induced metric

↑ **Parent:** [Riemannian manifold](riemannian-manifold.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_metric)

An [immersion](immersion.md) $F:M\to N$ into a [Riemannian manifold](riemannian-manifold.md) $(N,h)$ inherits the [Riemannian metric](riemannian-metric.md) $g=F^*h$: measure two [tangent vectors](tangent-vector.md) after mapping them into the surrounding [tangent space](tangent-space.md). This is a [pullback of a Riemannian metric](pullback-of-a-riemannian-metric.md), with

$$
g_p(u,v)=h_{F(p)}(dF_pu,dF_pv).
$$

The injective derivative of an [immersion](immersion.md) makes this [inner product](inner-product.md) [positive-definite](positive-definite-bilinear-form.md). For a parametrized [surface](topological-surface.md) in [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md), its coefficients are simply the [scalar products](dot-product.md) of the coordinate tangent vectors: the [first fundamental form](first-fundamental-form.md).

For the [circular cylinder](circular-cylinder.md) $F(\theta,z)=(R\cos\theta,R\sin\theta,z)$, the tangent vectors $F_\theta$ and $F_z$ are [orthogonal](orthogonal-vectors.md), with squared [norms](norm.md) $R^2$ and $1$. Consequently

$$
ds^2=R^2\,d\theta^2+dz^2.
$$

The unrolled coordinate $x=R\theta$ gives $ds^2=dx^2+dz^2$. This calculation explains [intrinsic flatness of a circular cylinder](intrinsic-flatness-of-a-circular-cylinder.md) and makes [Riemannian distance on a circular cylinder](riemannian-distance-on-a-circular-cylinder.md) a planar problem with repeated endpoint lifts.

Preserving a [Riemannian metric](riemannian-metric.md) locally preserves the [arc length](arc-length.md) of every mapped [curve](curve.md), but it need not preserve distances measured by unrestricted paths in the surrounding space. The [great-circle distance](great-circle-distance.md) between two sphere points, for example, exceeds their straight chord [length](length.md) unless they coincide. [Peter Petersen, Riemannian Geometry, §1.1: induced metrics and Riemannian immersions](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~petersen/RG.pdf).
