<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

**[Phosphorylation](../../../../phosphorylation.md) converts ABA perception into regulated ion transport, coordinates calcium-dependent and calcium-independent inputs, and makes [stomatal closure](../../../../stomatal-closure.md) reversible.** Its role is distributed across a network of kinases, phosphatases and transport targets. ABA does not close the pore by directly supplying a mechanical force.

The opening and closing machinery is a pair of [guard cells](../../../../guard-cell.md) surrounding a [stoma](../../../../stoma.md). Accumulation of osmotically active solutes favors water entry and increased [turgor pressure](../../../../turgor-pressure.md); solute loss favors water exit and reduced aperture. During water stress, [abscisic acid](../../../../abscisic-acid.md) shifts the balance toward anion and potassium loss. Changes in transport can precede slower changes in [gene expression](../../../../gene-expression.md), allowing the existing guard-cell machinery to respond rapidly.

The core ABA switch involves inhibition of an inhibitor. Without the activating ABA signal, selected clade-A [protein phosphatase 2C](../../../../protein-phosphatase-2c.md) enzymes, including [ABI1](../../../../abi1.md), restrain ABA-responsive [SnRK2 kinases](../../../../snrk2-kinase.md). ABA binding to a [PYR-PYL-RCAR ABA receptor](../../../../pyr-pyl-rcar-aba-receptor.md) promotes inhibition of these phosphatases. This permits activating kinase phosphorylation to persist and supports activation of [OST1](../../../../ost1.md), also called [SnRK2.6](../../../../ost1.md). Thus a hormone-dependent change in phosphatase activity changes the phosphorylation state and catalytic activity of downstream signaling proteins. The effect is specific to the relevant receptor, phosphatase and kinase combinations; it is not a claim that all phosphatases inhibit closure or all phosphorylation promotes it.

Biochemical reconstitution supports this causal ordering: defined receptor, phosphatase and kinase components can reconstruct ABA-dependent regulation without requiring an intact plant. This separates a sufficient core perception-and-relay module from the many parallel processes present in a stressed cell. However, a reconstructed molecular or transcriptional readout alone does not prove that the full mechanical response of a [stoma](../../../../stoma.md) has been rebuilt.[pp. 660–664, reconstituted ABA signaling components](../../../../in-vitro-reconstitution-of-an-abscisic-acid-signalling-pathway-by-hiroaki-fujii-and-others.md)

A crucial transport target is [SLAC1](../../../../slac1.md). [OST1](../../../../ost1.md)-dependent [phosphorylation](../../../../phosphorylation.md) activates this slow anion channel, allowing anions to leave the [guard cell](../../../../guard-cell.md). Anion efflux depolarizes the [membrane potential](../../../../membrane-potential-split.md), favoring outward potassium current through [GORK](../../../../gork.md). Loss of these solutes reduces the osmotic drive retaining water, so water exits and [turgor pressure](../../../../turgor-pressure.md) falls. Activation of an anion channel can therefore initiate a larger electrical and osmotic response without the kinase phosphorylating every ion that moves. Suppression of opening-associated transport further reinforces the shift toward closure.

Original qualitative pathway schematic. Arrows indicate promotion; blunt-ended connections indicate inhibition. The core switch is separated from calcium-dependent convergence and the final electrical and osmotic consequences. It does not imply that every ABA response requires a detectable calcium elevation.

Channel activation experiments provide an important bridge between the core ABA switch and physiology. Coexpression and electrophysiology showed that [OST1](../../../../ost1.md) activates [SLAC1](../../../../slac1.md), whereas phosphatase action antagonizes activation; kinase activity and channel regulatory regions are therefore functionally important. These findings are stronger than simple colocalization. Together with guard-cell genetic and current measurements, they support a route from a phosphorylation-dependent channel change to altered aperture. A heterologous cell nevertheless lacks the complete transport and mechanical environment of a real [guard cell](../../../../guard-cell.md).[Figures 1–4, channel currents and kinase–phosphatase regulation](../../../../activity-of-guard-cell-anion-channel-slac1-is-controlled-by-drought-stress-signaling-kinase-phosphatase-pair-by-dietmar-geiger-and-others.md)

Calcium-dependent signaling converges on this machinery. [Calcium-dependent protein kinases](../../../../calcium-dependent-protein-kinase.md) can activate [SLAC1](../../../../slac1.md), and different kinases have different calcium sensitivities. Consequently, [phosphorylation](../../../../phosphorylation.md) can combine hormone-dependent release of the phosphatase brake with a [calcium signature](../../../../calcium-signature.md). This is a convergence network, not a mandatory linear sequence in which ABA must always cause a measurable global calcium rise before any closure can occur.[pp. 8023–8028, kinase-specific calcium dependence and anion-channel regulation](../../../../guard-cell-anion-channel-slac1-is-regulated-by-cdpk-protein-kinases-with-distinct-ca2-plus-affinities-by-dietmar-geiger-and-others.md) Regulation of plant [NADPH oxidases](../../../../nadph-oxidase.md) and [reactive oxygen species](../../../../reactive-oxygen-species.md) can further couple kinase activity, [redox signalling](../../../../redox-signalling.md) and calcium entry. The physiological contribution of these branches varies with conditions.

Reversal is as important as activation. Phosphatases, calcium clearance and renewed opening-associated transport reset the system when conditions change. Phosphorylation can alter activity, interactions and localization without waiting for protein replacement. **The outcome is a tunable balance of kinase and phosphatase activities that connects ABA-dependent sensing to solute flux, water movement and pore mechanics, while integrating additional environmental inputs.**
