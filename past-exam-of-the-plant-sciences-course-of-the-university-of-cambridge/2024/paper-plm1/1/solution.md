**We understand several calcium sensors and downstream responses, but do not yet possess a general, predictive account of how a particular [calcium signature](../../../../calcium-signature.md) selects a particular plant response.** The unresolved problem is the causal transformation from a multidimensional signal to an outcome in its cellular context. It is too strong to say that nothing is known about [calcium signal decoding](../../../../calcium-signal-decoding.md).

A [calcium signature](../../../../calcium-signature.md) includes the amplitude, duration, frequency and spatial distribution of increases in free [calcium ion](../../../../calcium-ion.md) concentration. The nucleus, cytosol and regions close to [ion channels](../../../../ion-channel.md) can experience different signals. A stimulus may therefore change several candidate information-bearing variables simultaneously. Two treatments producing different oscillation frequencies might also differ in pulse height, total calcium exposure or the number of responding cells. A correlation between frequency and [gene expression](../../../../gene-expression.md) does not establish that frequency is the variable actually decoded. Nor need each stimulus have one unique [calcium signature](../../../../calcium-signature.md): different stimuli can recruit overlapping machinery, while the same stimulus can produce different responses in different tissues.

There are plausible molecular decoders. [Calmodulin](../../../../calmodulin.md) changes its interactions with targets after calcium binding. A [calcium-dependent protein kinase](../../../../calcium-dependent-protein-kinase.md) combines a calcium sensor with a catalytic domain, directly connecting calcium binding to target [phosphorylation](../../../../phosphorylation.md). [Calcineurin B-like proteins](../../../../calcineurin-b-like-protein.md) recruit [CBL-interacting protein kinases](../../../../cbl-interacting-protein-kinase.md), providing combinations of sensor, kinase and subcellular localization. In symbiotic signaling, nuclear oscillations feed into [CCaMK](../../../../calcium-and-calmodulin-dependent-protein-kinase.md) and downstream developmental regulation. These mechanisms explain how calcium can influence an output; identifying one does not automatically explain discrimination among different waveforms.

[Calcium sensor kinetics](../../../../calcium-sensor-kinetics.md) provide one possible mechanism. For a deliberately simplified sensor with active fraction $a$, free calcium concentration $C(t)$ and positive rate constants, consider

$$
\frac{da}{dt}=k_{\mathrm{on}}C(t)(1-a)-k_{\mathrm{off}}a.
$$

When calcium returns to a negligible value between pulses, $a$ decays approximately as $\exp(-k_{\mathrm{off}}t)$. Pulses separated by much less than $1/k_{\mathrm{off}}$ can therefore accumulate sensor activation; widely separated pulses allow recovery. This is an illustrative kinetic model, not a fitted description of every plant sensor. Real decoders may have several calcium-binding sites, autoinhibition, target-dependent affinity and persistent [phosphorylation](../../../../phosphorylation.md). Crucially, simple integration of closely spaced pulses does not by itself prove a dedicated frequency code independent of pulse amplitude and duration.

There is experimental evidence that waveform matters. Imposed guard-cell calcium patterns can produce different stomatal outcomes, including a distinction between immediate changes and a maintained response. This goes beyond merely observing that a closing [stoma](../../../../stoma.md) has calcium oscillations. Nevertheless, it establishes a response relationship in a particular experimental system; assigning that relationship to one endogenous decoder requires additional molecular evidence.[pp. 1053–1057, imposed oscillation parameters and stomatal responses](../../../../a-defined-range-of-guard-cell-calcium-oscillation-parameters-encodes-stomatal-movements-by-g-j-allen-and-others.md) Likewise, an activated [CCaMK](../../../../calcium-and-calmodulin-dependent-protein-kinase.md) can trigger spontaneous [nodulation](../../../../nodulation.md), bypassing upstream requirements. Such bypass experiments place kinase activity downstream of early symbiotic signaling, but do not identify which feature of the native calcium trace the wild-type kinase reads.[pp. 1153–1156, spontaneous nodule development after kinase deregulation](../../../../deregulation-of-a-ca2-plus-calmodulin-dependent-kinase-leads-to-spontaneous-nodule-development-by-leila-tirichine-and-others.md)

Measurement presents a separate limitation. A [calcium reporter](../../../../calcium-reporter.md) has its own affinity, response time and dynamic range. Slow reporters can merge brief pulses; saturation can erase amplitude differences. Probe expression can buffer calcium, and population measurements average heterogeneous cells. A calcium signal close to a channel may activate a nearby sensor without creating a large whole-cell change. Comparing different reporters or compartments without calibration can therefore create apparent biological differences that partly reflect measurement.

Finally, decoding depends on state. Sensor abundance, targets, prior [phosphorylation](../../../../phosphorylation.md), hormones and parallel [redox signalling](../../../../redox-signalling.md) all modify the response to a given [calcium signature](../../../../calcium-signature.md). Sensors can also regulate channels and calcium removal, feeding back on signal production. A mutant that changes both waveform and output has not cleanly separated encoding from decoding. A decisive study would manipulate relevant waveform features while controlling the others, measure sensor activation and physiological response in the same cells, and test whether selective loss and restoration of decoder function abolish and restore discrimination. **The central gap is thus a quantitative causal map linking local calcium dynamics, decoder kinetics and cellular state, rather than a lack of candidate calcium-binding proteins.**
