**Epigenetic information affects [plant](../../../../plant.md) [genome evolution](../../../../genome-evolution.md) both by regulating the behavior of DNA sequences and by supplying heritable regulatory variation on which selection can act.** It is therefore neither an alternative to genetic evolution nor merely a passive consequence of it. Changes in [DNA methylation](../../../../dna-methylation.md), [chromatin](../../../../chromatin.md) and small-RNA targeting influence which sequences transpose, recombine, mutate or remain expressed, while the DNA sequence helps determine where those [epigenetic states](../../../../epigenetic-state.md) are established.

An [epigenetic state](../../../../epigenetic-state.md) is not simply a measurement of expression at one instant. For evolutionary purposes, one must ask whether a difference persists through cell division, survives reproduction and changes a [phenotype](../../../../phenotype.md) relevant to reproductive success. An [epiallele](../../../../epiallele.md) can differ in regulatory state without a change in the local nucleotide sequence, but many epigenetic differences are caused by genetic variation elsewhere. A newly inserted repeat, for example, may recruit silencing to a nearby gene. The insertion is a genetic difference, while the resulting methylation is part of its mechanism. Demonstrating an independent epigenetic contribution therefore requires more than finding different methylomes in genetically different varieties.

A major connection is control of [transposable elements](../../../../transposable-element.md). Mobilization can increase copy number, interrupt coding sequences, change [gene regulation](../../../../regulation-of-gene-expression.md) and promote rearrangements between repeated sequences. [plants](../../../../plant.md) silence many such elements through [DNA methylation](../../../../dna-methylation.md), [H3K9me2](../../../../h3k9-dimethylation.md) and [RNA-directed DNA methylation](../../../../rna-directed-dna-methylation.md). This protects genome integrity but also permits a genome to tolerate a reservoir of potentially mobile DNA. The physical accessibility of that DNA matters: [DDM1](../../../../ddm1.md) helps methyltransferases reach nucleosomal [heterochromatin](../../../../heterochromatin.md), showing how [chromatin organization](../../../../chromatin-organization.md) contributes to the containment of repetitive sequences.[experimental analysis of heterochromatic methylation and transposon regulation](../../../../the-arabidopsis-nucleosome-remodeler-ddm1-allows-dna-methyltransferases-to-access-h1-containing-heterochromatin-by-assaf-zemach-and-others.md)

Silencing is not permanently absolute. Failure or reprogramming of maintenance can expose an element to [transcription](../../../../transcription-biology.md), and a successful new insertion can create a lasting sequence change even if silencing is restored later. Reactivation in a somatic cell is not automatically evolutionarily transmitted: an insertion must reach a reproductive lineage to contribute directly to inherited genome change. In [Arabidopsis thaliana](../../../../arabidopsis-thaliana.md) pollen, transposon reactivation in the vegetative nucleus was distinguished from the protected sperm lineage; the associated small RNAs illustrated how one cell type can contribute information relevant to silencing in another.[Summary and comparison of pollen vegetative nuclei with sperm](../../../../epigenetic-reprogramming-and-small-rna-silencing-of-transposable-elements-in-pollen-by-r-keith-slotkin-and-others.md) This is regulated reprogramming, not indiscriminate activation of the inherited genome.

Transposon control also changes the material available for regulatory evolution. An insertion may carry a promoter, enhancer or transcription-factor binding site, while its epigenetic silencing can repress an adjacent gene or define a [chromatin](../../../../chromatin.md) boundary. If selection favors part of the inserted sequence, its regulatory contribution can be retained while other element functions decay. Conversely, inappropriate spread of silencing can be harmful. The evolutionary outcome depends on insertion position, expression effects, mobility and host regulation; it is not true that all transposon activity is beneficial or that all methylation near genes is repressive. In particular, gene-body [CG methylation](../../../../cg-methylation.md) and promoter-associated silencing should not be treated as interchangeable states.

Epigenetic information also influences sequence evolution through mutation and recombination. [hydrolytic deamination](../../../../hydrolytic-deamination.md) of methylated cytosine can generate a thymine mismatch and, if unrepaired, a C-to-T substitution. A regulatory mark can thus change the [probability](../../../../probability.md) of a subsequent genetic mutation; the resulting base substitution is no longer an [epimutation](../../../../epimutation.md). [chromatin](../../../../chromatin.md) state also helps organize where meiotic breaks and crossovers occur. Repeat-rich [heterochromatin](../../../../heterochromatin.md) often exchanges less readily than gene-rich chromosome arms, altering the degree to which selection acts on linked variants. These effects are contextual rather than a rule that every methylated cytosine or every marked region has the same evolutionary fate.

There is also a genuinely heritable state component. Mutation-accumulation lines of [Arabidopsis thaliana](../../../../arabidopsis-thaliana.md) showed spontaneous methylation differences across generations, with individual sites more labile than many larger methylated regions and with recurrent changes at some sites.[comparison of lines descended from a common ancestor; regional stability and recurrent epimutations](../../../../spontaneous-epigenetic-variation-in-the-arabidopsis-thaliana-methylome-by-claude-becker-and-others.md) Thus an epigenetic variant's evolutionary importance depends jointly on its phenotypic effect, transmission [probability](../../../../probability.md) and reversion rate. A rapidly reversible state may support plasticity but fail to behave like a stable DNA allele over many generations.

Experiments with [epigenetic recombinant inbred lines](../../../../epigenetic-recombinant-inbred-line.md) provide evidence that some methylation differences can contribute to quantitative [phenotypes](../../../../phenotype.md). Mapping in a comparatively uniform genetic background identified epigenetic loci associated with flowering time and root length.[epigenetic quantitative-trait mapping and selection in epiRILs](../../../../mapping-the-epigenetic-basis-of-complex-traits-by-sandra-cortijo-and-others.md) This establishes a possible substrate for selection, not a claim that most natural adaptation is sequence-independent. Residual sequence variation, new transposon insertions, genetic control of methylation and environment-dependent effects must still be distinguished in natural populations.

Hybridization and [allopolyploidy](../../../../allopolyploidy.md) create further interactions. Divergent regulatory systems and multiple parental copies must operate together, and changes in [chromatin](../../../../chromatin.md) can alter dosage or expression before any gene is lost. In [nucleolar dominance](../../../../nucleolar-dominance.md), one parental set of ribosomal RNA genes is epigenetically silenced. The involvement of [HDA6](../../../../hda6.md), [DNA methylation](../../../../dna-methylation.md) and histone-state changes shows that unequal parental expression can arise without deleting the silent set.[Abstract and perturbation of parental rRNA-gene silencing](../../../../erasure-of-histone-acetylation-by-arabidopsis-hda6-mediates-large-scale-gene-silencing-in-nucleolar-dominance-by-keith-earley-and-others.md) Such expression differences can influence which duplicate functions remain useful, although silencing alone does not prove the later direction of gene loss or subgenome evolution.

**Epigenetic information therefore shapes [genome evolution](../../../../genome-evolution.md) as a regulator of genome activity, a modifier of genetic change and sometimes an inherited source of phenotypic variation.** Its effects must be analyzed together with sequence, reproduction and selection, without assuming that environmentally induced changes are necessarily adaptive or permanently inherited.
