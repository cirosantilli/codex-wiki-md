↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

**Both lineages sustain major [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) reservoirs and ongoing uptake, but their relative contribution depends on biome, area, turnover, disturbance and the [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) pool being counted. High [photosynthesis](../../../../photosynthesis.md), a large existing stock and durable net [carbon sequestration](../../../../carbon-sequestration.md) are different quantities.** [gymnosperms](../../../../gymnosperm.md) dominate many coniferous boreal and some temperate forests; [angiosperms](../../../../angiosperm.md) dominate much tropical and temperate broadleaf forest and also contribute grasslands, savannas, wetlands and other vegetation. Comparing only the number of [species](../../../../species.md), or only the average growth rate of a tree, cannot rank their global contribution.

Begin with a [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) budget for a stated area and period. [gross primary production](../../../../gross-primary-production.md) is total photosynthetic uptake. Subtracting [autotrophic respiration](../../../../autotrophic-respiration.md) gives [net primary production](../../../../net-primary-production.md), and subtracting [heterotrophic respiration](../../../../heterotrophic-respiration.md) gives [net ecosystem production](../../../../net-ecosystem-production.md):

$$
\mathrm{NPP}=\mathrm{GPP}-R_a,\qquad
\mathrm{NEP}=\mathrm{GPP}-R_a-R_h.
$$

For an ecosystem boundary that excludes exported products, its change in [carbon storage](../../../../carbon-storage.md) also includes disturbance and lateral transfers:

$$
\boxed{\frac{\Delta C}{\Delta t}=\mathrm{NEP}-F_{\mathrm{fire}}-F_{\mathrm{harvest}}-F_{\mathrm{export}}+F_{\mathrm{import}}.}
$$

Each flux must use the same units and period, and the terms must be defined to avoid counting the same loss twice. For an invented annual example, $\mathrm{GPP}=12$, $R_a=6$, $R_h=5$ and combustion losses of 2, all in tonnes of [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) per hectare per year, give a net loss of 1 despite positive [net ecosystem production](../../../../net-ecosystem-production.md). [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) transferred from a living tree to dead wood by mortality is initially an internal transfer, not an instantaneous atmospheric emission; subsequent decomposition or combustion determines release.

Existing [carbon storage](../../../../carbon-storage.md) includes living stems and [root](../../../../root-botany.md) systems, dead material and soil. A slow-growing forest can contain a large stock accumulated over long periods, while a young stand with a smaller stock can have a high current [carbon sequestration](../../../../carbon-sequestration.md) rate. Conversely, some old forests continue accumulating [carbon](../../../../carbon.md); age alone does not determine the sign of the flux. Converting a [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) quantity to [carbon dioxide](../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) mass requires the factor $44/12$, not a change in the biological [carbon sequestration](../../../../carbon-sequestration.md) rate. A comparison of global totals must sum area-weighted pool sizes and fluxes across regions, rather than extrapolate one hectare or infer taxonomic shares directly from biome averages.

[gymnosperm](../../../../gymnosperm.md) forests illustrate the importance of persistence and environment. Many conifers retain foliage, operate in short growing seasons and invest in durable tissues. Long-lived stems can store [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) for decades to centuries; cold or waterlogged conditions can slow decomposition and support large soil or organic-layer stocks. These soil stocks are properties of the whole [ecosystem](../../../../ecosystem.md), including [root](../../../../root-botany.md) systems, microbes, hydrology and often [bryophytes](../../../../bryophyte.md), and cannot all be attributed to conifer physiology. Slow annual production can coexist with large accumulated storage. The conservative features of many conifers also carry costs, and neither evergreen foliage nor slow growth is universal within [gymnosperms](../../../../gymnosperm.md).

[angiosperms](../../../../angiosperm.md) occupy a wider range of growth forms and strongly influence highly productive warm forests as well as seasonal and open systems. Large tropical trees maintain substantial woody stocks, while root turnover, litter inputs and soil protection contribute additional storage. In grasslands, much persistent [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) can be belowground despite a modest aboveground standing crop. Many [angiosperms](../../../../angiosperm.md) have vessel-based [xylem](../../../../xylem.md) with high transport capacity, supporting rapid resource acquisition under favorable conditions; many [gymnosperms](../../../../gymnosperm.md) rely on [tracheids](../../../../tracheid.md). These are mechanistic differences, not a universal hydraulic ranking: anatomy varies within both lineages, and drought resistance depends on [phenotypic traits](../../../../phenotypic-trait.md) of the entire transport pathway. Likewise, fast-growing angiosperm tissue can turn over rapidly, so high production is not equivalent to long residence time.

Inventory-based global forest budgets demonstrate substantial uptake in several forest regions, while also showing why land-use losses must be included when calculating a net forest sink.[Science 333, pp. 988–993; forest inventory synthesis and separate accounting of land-use emissions and regrowth](../../../../a-large-and-persistent-carbon-sink-in-the-world-s-forests-by-yude-pan-and-others.md) Such estimates are generally organized by regions and forest histories, not a clean experimental split between seed-plant lineages. A rigorous comparison would combine forest composition and area with repeated biomass inventories, belowground and soil measurements, [eddy covariance](../../../../eddy-covariance.md) and remote sensing. Allometric uncertainty, unmeasured deep soil and incomplete disturbance histories limit apparent precision. [eddy covariance](../../../../eddy-covariance.md) measures atmospheric exchange for a footprint; it does not independently assign every flux to a taxonomic group or automatically measure exported [carbon](../../../../carbon.md).

Elevated [carbon dioxide](../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) can stimulate [photosynthesis](../../../../photosynthesis.md) and increase [water-use efficiency](../../../../water-use-efficiency.md) through changes in uptake and [stomatal conductance](../../../../stomatal-conductance.md), creating opportunities for growth in both groups. But storage responses depend on nutrient supply, water, allocation and respiratory losses. In a deciduous forest [free-air carbon dioxide enrichment](../../../../free-air-carbon-dioxide-enrichment.md) experiment, stimulation of production weakened as nitrogen limitation developed.[PNAS 107, pp. 19368–19373; eleven-year enrichment experiment and changing nitrogen limitation](../../../../co2-enhancement-of-forest-productivity-constrained-by-limited-nitrogen-availability-by-richard-j-norby-and-others.md) In a mature forest enrichment experiment, a comprehensive [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) budget found that much additional uptake returned through respiratory fluxes rather than accumulating as new storage.[Nature 580, pp. 227–231; ecosystem carbon budget after four years of enrichment](../../../../the-fate-of-carbon-in-a-mature-forest-under-carbon-dioxide-enrichment-by-mingkai-jiang-and-others.md) These are counterexamples to equating a leaf-level [fertilization](../../../../fertilization.md) response with a durable whole-ecosystem sink. Replicated ambient plots, nutrient measurements and tracking of belowground allocation are essential controls.

Climate warming can lengthen northern growing seasons and permit recruitment where low temperature previously limited growth. Yet warming can also increase atmospheric [vapour-pressure deficit](../../../../vapour-pressure-deficit.md), soil drying, respiration, fire and insect damage. Thawing [permafrost](../../../../permafrost.md) can expose older soil [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) to decomposition; that loss is not automatically balanced by new tree growth. Forest expansion across bright snow-covered surfaces can lower [albedo](../../../../albedo.md), partly offsetting the climatic cooling from additional [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) storage.[Nature 408, pp. 187–190; modeled carbon and snow-albedo effects of boreal forestation](../../../../offset-of-the-potential-carbon-sink-from-boreal-forestation-by-decreases-in-surface-albedo-by-richard-a-betts.md) This is a distinction between [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) accounting and the full climate effect, not an argument that stored [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) has no value.

Warm angiosperm-dominated forests face heat, drying and disturbance as well. Hydraulic damage can impair water transport in both major seed-plant lineages, and comparative hydraulic measurements show that forests in wet climates are not necessarily protected by large safety margins.[Nature 491, pp. 752–755; comparison of experienced xylem pressures and hydraulic damage thresholds](../../../../global-convergence-in-the-vulnerability-of-forests-to-drought-by-brendan-choat-and-others.md) Long-term tropical plot records found different timings of weakening uptake in African and Amazonian forests, with mortality an important contributor.[Nature 579, pp. 80–87; repeated inventories, tree growth and mortality, and contrasting regional sink histories](../../../../asynchronous-carbon-sink-saturation-in-african-and-amazonian-tropical-forests-by-wannes-hubau-and-others.md) A declining sink can remain a sink; a trend toward zero uptake should not be reported as an observed net source without the corresponding budget. Nor can these plot results be generalized to every angiosperm ecosystem.

Future distributions will depend on recruitment and dispersal as well as adult tolerance. Replacement of conifers by broadleaf [species](../../../../species.md) can alter growth, litter, fire and surface reflectance, but neither the direction nor the total [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) consequence follows from lineage identity alone. Management should therefore compare protection of existing stocks, avoided disturbance, regrowth and planting under explicit baselines, over an appropriate time horizon. Harvested products, their decay and any claimed fossil-fuel substitution require separate accounting rather than assuming every harvested tonne remains sequestered. **Gymnosperm forests are especially consequential through extensive long-lived woody and cold-soil reservoirs, while angiosperms support large warm-forest stocks, substantial production and diverse belowground stores; the durability of both contributions depends on the balance of uptake, turnover and climate-sensitive losses.**
