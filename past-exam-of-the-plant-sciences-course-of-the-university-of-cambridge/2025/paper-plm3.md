**The habitat occupied by the conspicuous [plant](../../organism.md#plant) is only part of the life cycle: successful reproduction also requires suitable conditions for its other generation, dispersal and [fertilization](../../biology.md#fertilization).** In [alternation of generations](../../biology.md#alternation-of-generations), a multicellular [haploid](../../genetics.md#haploid) [gametophyte](../../biology.md#gametophyte) alternates with a multicellular [diploid](../../genetics.md#diploid) [sporophyte](../../biology.md#sporophyte). The transitions in [ploidy](../../genetics.md#ploidy) occur at [meiosis](../../genetics.md#meiosis) and [fertilization](../../biology.md#fertilization); growth within either generation is by [mitosis](../../cell-cycle.md#mitosis):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{sporophyte }(2n)&\xrightarrow{\text{meiosis}}\text{spores }(n)\xrightarrow{\text{mitosis}}\text{gametophyte }(n),\\
\text{gametophyte }(n)&\xrightarrow{\text{mitosis}}\text{gametes }(n)\xrightarrow{\text{fertilization}}\text{zygote }(2n)\xrightarrow{\text{mitosis}}\text{sporophyte }(2n).
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus [spores](../../biology.md#spore) disperse and initiate a generation, whereas [gametes](../../biology.md#gamete) fuse. A [plant](../../organism.md#plant) [gametophyte](../../biology.md#gametophyte) normally produces [gametes](../../biology.md#gamete) by [mitosis](../../cell-cycle.md#mitosis), not by another reduction division. Retention and nourishment of the young [plant embryo](../../organism.md#plant-embryo) on the maternal [gametophyte](../../biology.md#gametophyte) distinguishes the land-plant reproductive arrangement from simply releasing a naked [zygote](../../biology.md#zygote) into the environment.

In [bryophytes](../../organism.md#bryophyte), the persistent, usually most conspicuous generation is the [gametophyte](../../biology.md#gametophyte). It bears [antheridia](../../biology.md#antheridium) producing motile [sperm](../../biology.md#sperm) and [archegonia](../../biology.md#archegonium) containing eggs. Following [fertilization](../../biology.md#fertilization), the [sporophyte](../../biology.md#sporophyte) remains attached to the maternal [gametophyte](../../biology.md#gametophyte) and obtains resources through that association. Although some [sporophytes](../../biology.md#sporophyte) photosynthesize, this does not make them nutritionally independent. The unbranched reproductive axis terminates in a single [sporangium](../../biology.md#sporangium); elevating it can improve [spore dispersal](../../biology.md#spore-dispersal) without requiring the whole [gametophyte](../../biology.md#gametophyte) to grow tall.

This organization helps explain both constraints and successes on land. [bryophyte](../../organism.md#bryophyte) [gametophytes](../../biology.md#gametophyte) lack the true [xylem](../../organism.md#xylem) and [phloem](../../organism.md#phloem) of [vascular plants](../../organism.md#vascular-plant), and often depend strongly on surface water and short transport distances. [rhizoids](../../organism.md#rhizoid) provide attachment and can contribute to uptake, but are not anatomically equivalent to a [root](../../organism.md#root-botany). Their small stature and limited control of water loss restrict sustained activity during drying. However, many tolerate drying and resume activity after rehydration: [desiccation tolerance](../../organism.md#desiccation-tolerance) and avoidance of drying are different strategies. Periodic metabolic inactivity can permit persistence in exposed habitats even where continuous growth would be impossible.

Reproduction imposes an additional bottleneck. Motile [sperm](../../biology.md#sperm) need a water connection to reach an egg, so successful vegetative survival on a dry surface does not imply that [fertilization](../../biology.md#fertilization) can occur there at any time. Rain, dew and protected microsites can provide intermittent opportunities. Separation of male and female [gametophytes](../../biology.md#gametophyte) can add a spatial limitation. Resistant [spore](../../biology.md#spore) walls containing [sporopollenin](../../biology.md#sporopollenin) protect a dispersal stage, but a dispersed [spore](../../biology.md#spore) must still germinate and establish, and the resulting [gametophyte](../../biology.md#gametophyte) must reproduce. Some [mosses](../../organism.md#moss) and [hornworts](../../organism.md#hornwort) have [stomata](../../organism.md#stoma) on their [sporophytes](../../biology.md#sporophyte); [liverworts](../../organism.md#liverwort) lack true [stomata](../../organism.md#stoma), and their air pores are not equivalent regulated valves. It is therefore misleading to assign all these lineages one identical water-control mechanism.

Early land-plant [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution) was not a ladder in which living [mosses](../../organism.md#moss) became living [ferns](../../organism.md#fern). The [Rhynie chert](../../biology.md#rhynie-chert) preserves reproductive bodies interpreted as [gametophytes](../../biology.md#gametophyte) with axial growth, conducting tissues and [stomata](../../organism.md#stoma), sometimes sharing structural characters with their associated [sporophytes](../../biology.md#sporophyte). These fossils document combinations unlike those of modern [bryophytes](../../organism.md#bryophyte) and caution against projecting a tiny modern fern [gametophyte](../../biology.md#gametophyte) onto every early lineage.[International Journal of Plant Sciences 154, pp. 35–58; anatomical evidence for gametophytes and proposed associations with sporophytes](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-gametophyte-generation-of-some-early-devonian-land-plants-by-winfried-remy-and-others) Identification depends on reproductive structures and anatomy; resemblance alone cannot demonstrate that two isolated fossil bodies belonged to one life cycle. Branching [sporophytes](../../biology.md#sporophyte) bearing several [sporangia](../../biology.md#sporangium) increased reproductive output per [fertilization](../../biology.md#fertilization) event, while the [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution) of effective transport and support permitted greater independence from the maternal generation.

In most familiar [ferns](../../organism.md#fern), the large leafy individual is an independently growing [sporophyte](../../biology.md#sporophyte), with a [root](../../organism.md#root-botany) system, [xylem](../../organism.md#xylem), [phloem](../../organism.md#phloem), a [plant cuticle](../../organism.md#plant-cuticle) and [stomata](../../organism.md#stoma). These features support water acquisition, long-distance transport and regulated exchange, allowing larger stature and access to a broader range of terrestrial environments. The [sporophyte](../../biology.md#sporophyte) produces [spores](../../biology.md#spore) in [sporangia](../../biology.md#sporangium), often clustered into [sori](../../organism.md#sorus). In a typical [homosporous](../../biology.md#homospory) fern, a [spore](../../biology.md#spore) grows into a small free-living [gametophyte](../../biology.md#gametophyte), commonly a photosynthetic [prothallus](../../biology.md#prothallus). The young [sporophyte](../../biology.md#sporophyte) initially develops on it but becomes independent. Therefore [sporophyte](../../biology.md#sporophyte) dominance reduces one kind of dependence without eliminating the ecological requirements of the other generation.

The fern [gametophyte](../../biology.md#gametophyte) commonly lacks the hydraulic and water-conservation capacities of the mature [sporophyte](../../biology.md#sporophyte); establishment, competition, drying and the water requirement for [sperm](../../biology.md#sperm) movement can limit recruitment. Yet it need not be uniformly more drought-sensitive. Experimental drying and rehydration of tropical fern [gametophytes](../../biology.md#gametophyte) revealed substantial variation in recovery, with differences associated with habitat and repeated drying cycles.[New Phytologist 176, pp. 708–717; controlled desiccation cycles and chlorophyll-fluorescence recovery](../../biology-bibliography.md#ecological-and-evolutionary-consequences-of-desiccation-tolerance-in-tropical-fern-gametophytes-by-james-e-watkins-jr-and-others) Such assays need hydrated controls and checks of subsequent growth or reproduction: recovery of [chlorophyll fluorescence](../../biology.md#chlorophyll-fluorescence) alone is not completion of the life cycle. The two generations can exploit different microsites, so habitat limitation is not simply the intersection of two identical environmental requirements.

There are important alternatives within the fern life cycle. [heterosporous](../../biology.md#heterospory) water ferns produce distinct [microspores](../../biology.md#microspore) and [megaspores](../../biology.md#megaspore), with reduced male and female [gametophytes](../../biology.md#gametophyte) developing within the [spore](../../biology.md#spore) walls. This offers protection and changes provisioning, but it does not supply the delivery mechanism of a [pollen tube](../../biology.md#pollen-tube) that can remove dependence on an external water film. Nor does [adaptation](../../biology.md#adaptation) to an aquatic habitat demonstrate freedom from water for [fertilization](../../biology.md#fertilization). Differences among [homospory](../../biology.md#homospory), [heterospory](../../biology.md#heterospory), mating systems and vegetative persistence mean that a single generalized prothallus cannot describe every fern.

Developmental genetics confirms that the two generations are distinct developmental programs rather than automatic visual consequences of chromosome number. Removing [KNOX2](../../molecular-genetics.md#knox2) regulators in a moss produced gametophyte-like bodies from [diploid](../../genetics.md#diploid) embryos without intervening [meiosis](../../genetics.md#meiosis).[Science 339, pp. 1067–1070; KNOX2 deletion and development of diploid gametophyte bodies](../../biology-bibliography.md#knox2-genes-regulate-the-haploid-to-diploid-morphological-transition-in-land-plants-by-keiko-sakakibara-and-others) This does not reconstruct the historical ancestor, but demonstrates a mechanism by which the deployment of generations can evolve. **Across these lineages, increasing [sporophyte](../../biology.md#sporophyte) autonomy broadened terrestrial opportunities, while establishment of the [gametophyte](../../biology.md#gametophyte) and water-dependent [fertilization](../../biology.md#fertilization) continued to filter where complete sexual life cycles could occur.**

**Maximum [likelihood](../../maximum-likelihood-estimation.md#likelihood-function) and Bayesian phylogenetics can use the same model of sequence [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution), but they answer different inferential questions: optimization of the [likelihood](../../maximum-likelihood-estimation.md#likelihood-function) versus a posterior distribution over trees and other unknowns.** Let $D$ be a [sequence alignment](../../molecular-genetics.md#sequence-alignment), $T$ a [phylogenetic tree](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-tree) topology, $\mathbf b$ its branch lengths in expected substitutions per site, and $\theta$ the parameters of a [nucleotide substitution model](../../biology.md#nucleotide-substitution-model). Both methods can use

$$
L(T,\mathbf b,\theta)=P(D\mid T,\mathbf b,\theta).
$$

The [likelihood function](../../maximum-likelihood-estimation.md#likelihood-function) sums over unobserved ancestral states, rather than choosing one ancestral sequence and treating it as observed. For conditionally independent sites it is a product of site likelihoods; transitions along a branch can be described by the [matrix exponential](../../linear-operator-theory.md#matrix-exponential) $e^{Qb}$ of a [continuous-time Markov chain](../../continuous-time-markov-chain.md) rate matrix $Q$. The [phylogenetic pruning algorithm](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-pruning-algorithm) computes the ancestral sums efficiently.[Journal of Molecular Evolution 17, pp. 368–376; likelihood calculation by summing over ancestral states](../../biology-bibliography.md#evolutionary-trees-from-dna-sequences-a-maximum-likelihood-approach-by-joseph-felsenstein)

With [maximum likelihood estimation](../../maximum-likelihood-estimation.md), one seeks

$$
(\widehat T,\widehat{\mathbf b},\widehat\theta)\in\arg\max_{T,\mathbf b,\theta}L(T,\mathbf b,\theta).
$$

In practice, the enormous discrete tree space requires searches and repeated optimization of continuous parameters; heuristic failure to find the best tree is a computational error distinct from statistical uncertainty. A tree's optimized [likelihood](../../maximum-likelihood-estimation.md#likelihood-function) is not the [probability](../../probability-theory.md#probability) that it is correct. [bootstrap](../../statistical-modelling.md#bootstrapping-statistics) analyses resample alignment columns and repeat inference to assess support under the resampling procedure; their frequencies are not automatically [posterior probabilities](../../bayesian-statistics.md#posterior-probability). Independence assumptions matter, because linked sites and heterogeneous loci can make simple column resampling an imperfect representation of replication.

In [Bayesian phylogenetic inference](../../biology.md#bayesian-phylogenetic-inference),

$$
p(T,\mathbf b,\theta\mid D)=\frac{P(D\mid T,\mathbf b,\theta)\,\pi(T,\mathbf b,\theta)}{P(D)}.
$$

The [prior distribution](../../bayesian-statistics.md#prior-probability) supplies weights on topologies, branch lengths and model parameters. Integrating over nuisance parameters gives

$$
P(T\mid D)\propto\int L(T,\mathbf b,\theta)\pi(T,\mathbf b,\theta)\,d\mathbf b\,d\theta.
$$

Thus a very narrow [likelihood](../../maximum-likelihood-estimation.md#likelihood-function) peak can lose posterior support to a broader region with a slightly lower maximum. [maximum a posteriori estimation](../../bayesian-statistics.md#maximum-a-posteriori-estimation) is another optimization and does not replace this integration. [Markov chain Monte Carlo](../../bayesian-statistics.md#markov-chain-monte-carlo) provides dependent samples approximating the [posterior distribution](../../bayesian-statistics.md#bayesian-posterior); a clade's [posterior probability](../../bayesian-statistics.md#posterior-probability) is the posterior mass of trees containing that clade. The early likelihood-based Bayesian formulation explicitly distinguished these probabilities from bootstrap support.[Journal of Molecular Evolution 43, pp. 304–311; posterior probabilities of trees and comparison with bootstrap proportions](../../biology-bibliography.md#probability-distribution-of-molecular-evolutionary-trees-a-new-method-of-phylogenetic-inference-by-bruce-rannala-and-ziheng-yang)

Neither approach is protected against systematic error. Poor [sequence alignment](../../molecular-genetics.md#sequence-alignment), incorrect [orthology](../../molecular-genetics.md#orthology) assignments, compositionally heterogeneous sequences, and an inadequate substitution process can support a wrong tree with high apparent confidence. A [gene tree](../../biology.md#gene-tree) can differ from a [species tree](../../biology.md#species-tree) because of [incomplete lineage sorting](../../biology.md#incomplete-lineage-sorting) or [introgression](../../biology.md#introgression); extra sequence from that [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) does not automatically fix the mismatch. Model comparison, biological examination of the data and sensitivity to locus sampling are needed in both approaches. In Bayesian analyses, agreement among short chains is not proof of convergence: independent runs, adequate mixing between topologies and an appropriate [effective sample size of a Markov chain](../../bayesian-statistics.md#effective-sample-size-of-a-markov-chain) should be checked. A [credible interval](../../bayesian-statistics.md#credible-interval) and a [confidence interval](../../statistical-inference.md#confidence-interval) also have different interpretations even if their numerical endpoints happen to resemble one another.

Time inference adds a particularly strong role for assumptions. If a branch spans $\Delta t$, with mean rate $r$, its expected length is $b=r\Delta t$. Without an external absolute-time anchor, scaling every time by $c$ and every rate by $1/c$ [leaves](../../organism.md#leaf) the sequence [likelihood](../../maximum-likelihood-estimation.md#likelihood-function) unchanged. Even a very long alignment can identify branch lengths accurately without identifying an absolute calendar scale. For example, a lineage length $b=0.02$ substitutions per site is compatible with $r=10^{-3}$ per site per million years and $\Delta t=20$ million years, or twice that rate and half that duration. This is an [identifiability](../../statistical-model.md#identifiability) issue, not merely a shortage of sampled bases.

In [Bayesian divergence-time inference](../../biology.md#bayesian-divergence-time-inference), a schematic joint analysis is

$$
p(T,\mathbf t,\mathbf r,\theta\mid D,F)\propto
P(D\mid T,\mathbf t,\mathbf r,\theta)\,
\pi(T,\mathbf t,\mathbf r,\theta\mid F),
$$

where $F$ is fossil information already used to construct the conditional prior. An alternative generative analysis includes a fossil-sampling [likelihood](../../maximum-likelihood-estimation.md#likelihood-function) explicitly; the same fossil evidence must not be counted twice. A [strict molecular clock](../../biology.md#strict-molecular-clock) equates rates across branches; a [relaxed molecular clock](../../biology.md#relaxed-molecular-clock) allows variation, whose distribution and correlations also require specification. A narrow rate prior favors particular absolute ages, and a strong rate-smoothing assumption can redistribute inferred time among branches. A [birth-death process](../../continuous-time-markov-chain.md#birth-death-process) prior on the tree encodes assumptions about lineage origination, extinction and sampling; it is not a neutral choice just because it is mathematically convenient.

[fossil calibration](../../biology.md#fossil-calibration) must separate a fossil's geological age from the age of the lineage split it constrains. A confidently placed crown fossil gives a minimum age for the relevant crown, whereas a stem fossil does not automatically set that crown minimum. Uncertain placement and the incompleteness of the record complicate upper bounds. [hard calibration bounds](../../biology.md#hard-calibration-bound) assign zero probability outside the permitted range; enough sequence cannot overcome a zero prior. [soft calibration bounds](../../biology.md#soft-calibration-bound) allow small tail probabilities, exposing rather than eliminating the possibility of conflict. Multiple-calibration analyses demonstrate that changing the treatment of bounds changes inferred times and uncertainty.[Molecular Biology and Evolution 23, pp. 212–226; hard versus soft bounds and the divergence-time prior](../../biology-bibliography.md#bayesian-estimation-of-species-divergence-times-under-a-molecular-clock-using-multiple-fossil-calibrations-with-soft-bounds-by-ziheng-yang-and-bruce-rannala)

Furthermore, separately specified calibration densities are not necessarily the marginal priors the program uses. The rule that an ancestor must be older than its descendant truncates their joint possibilities. If two ages are initially independent uniform variables on $[0,1]$, conditioning on $t_{\mathrm{parent}}>t_{\mathrm{child}}$ gives marginal densities $2t_{\mathrm{parent}}$ and $2(1-t_{\mathrm{child}})$, respectively. Neither remains uniform. Combining tree-process priors, calibration densities and ordering constraints can therefore introduce substantial information unintentionally.[Sections on node ordering and the realized joint prior; recommendation to sample the prior with a fixed likelihood](../../biology-bibliography.md#conceptual-issues-in-bayesian-divergence-time-estimation-by-bruce-rannala)

The practical response is to run the analysis without sequence information to inspect the effective [prior distribution](../../bayesian-statistics.md#prior-probability), then compare it with the [posterior distribution](../../bayesian-statistics.md#bayesian-posterior). Repeat under defensible calibration tails, rate models, tree priors and fossil placements; report which ages change and whether the data sharpen relative ages more than absolute ages. Examine [prior predictive checks](../../bayesian-statistics.md#prior-predictive-check) and [posterior predictive checks](../../bayesian-statistics.md#posterior-predictive-check), and keep numerical mixing diagnostics separate from biological model adequacy. **Priors can legitimately supply the time information missing from sequences, but narrow posterior ages are persuasive only when that information and its sensitivity are explicit.**

**Ancestral state reconstruction turns a difference between living [flowers](../../organism.md#flower) into a hypothesis about a particular evolutionary change; experiments must then establish the molecular cause.** Without the historical step, two similar [flowers](../../organism.md#flower) may be mistaken for a retained ancestral condition, and a molecular difference may be assigned to the wrong evolutionary branch. [floral symmetry](../../organism.md#floral-symmetry) provides a particularly instructive example because a return to radial appearance need not restore ancestral developmental identity.

Start by sampling the focal lineage and informative relatives, estimating a rooted [phylogenetic tree](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-tree), and recording the [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) at comparable developmental stages. For [floral symmetry](../../organism.md#floral-symmetry), distinguish the symmetry of the whole [flower](../../organism.md#flower), the shapes and identities of individual [petals](../../organism.md#petal), and the arrangement of [stamens](../../organism.md#stamen). A radial outline can be produced by several different organ-level changes. Coding every such [flower](../../organism.md#flower) as simply “radial” is a useful coarse hypothesis, but it can hide the very developmental mechanism one wishes to explain. Assess within-species variation and incomplete observations rather than forcing all uncertainty into a single state.

An explicit evolutionary model can assign transition rates between [radial floral symmetry](../../organism.md#radial-floral-symmetry) and [bilateral floral symmetry](../../organism.md#bilateral-floral-symmetry). With state-change matrix $Q$, branch transition probabilities are $P(t)=e^{Qt}$ under a [continuous-time Markov chain](../../continuous-time-markov-chain.md). For an ancestral node $v$, the likelihood-based weight of state $s$ combines the descendant observations, the rest of the tree and an assumed root-state distribution. In a Bayesian treatment,

$$
P(S_v=s\mid D)=\sum_T\int P(S_v=s\mid D,T,\eta)\,p(T,\eta\mid D)\,d\eta,
$$

where $\eta$ includes transition rates and branch parameters, and $D$ denotes the comparative data used in the joint analysis. This expresses the need to carry phylogenetic and model uncertainty forward. A [maximum parsimony](../../biology.md#maximum-parsimony) reconstruction instead minimizes state changes; it does not automatically justify equal rates or identify the most probable historical path. [stochastic character mapping](../../biology.md#stochastic-character-mapping) can sample possible histories, including unobserved changes within branches, rather than equating endpoint differences with exactly one event.

The resulting reconstruction identifies where to look. If a lineage with radial [flowers](../../organism.md#flower) is nested within a group inferred to have had bilateral [flowers](../../organism.md#flower), the natural molecular question is how a derived loss or transformation of asymmetry occurred. Candidate mechanisms include loss of a dorsal regulator, contraction or expansion of its expression domain, changes in downstream targets, or compensating changes elsewhere in a [gene regulatory network](../../mathematical-biology.md#gene-regulatory-network). If several radial lineages evolved independently, their reconstructed branches provide replicates for testing repeated use of the same [gene](../../genetics.md#gene). They do not justify treating every living [species](../../biology.md#species) in one derived clade as an independent origin.

Mutational work in [Antirrhinum](../../organism.md#antirrhinum) identified [CYCLOIDEA](../../molecular-genetics.md#cycloidea) as a regulator of dorsal floral growth and organ patterning.[Nature 383, pp. 794–799; mutant analysis and spatial expression of cyc during floral development](../../biology-bibliography.md#origin-of-floral-asymmetry-in-antirrhinum-by-da-luo-and-others) This makes related regulators plausible candidates for symmetry [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution) elsewhere, but does not prove that every bilateral [flower](../../organism.md#flower) uses precisely the same deployment. A [gene tree](../../biology.md#gene-tree) is needed to distinguish corresponding copies from products of [gene duplication](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-duplication); expression of an arbitrarily chosen member of the family would be an inadequate test.

The legume [Cadia](../../organism.md#cadia) illustrates the payoff. Its [flowers](../../organism.md#flower) look radially symmetric, although its placement among relatives supports derivation from a lineage with bilateral [flowers](../../organism.md#flower). A simple “loss of asymmetry” account might predict removal of the dorsal identity program. Instead, the expression domain of a [CYCLOIDEA](../../molecular-genetics.md#cycloidea)-like regulator was expanded into lateral and ventral parts of the corolla, supporting the interpretation that petals had acquired dorsal identity around the [flower](../../organism.md#flower).[PNAS 103, pp. 12017–12020; phylogenetic context, petal comparisons and expanded expression of a LegCYC copy](../../biology-bibliography.md#an-apparent-reversal-in-floral-symmetry-in-the-legume-cadia-is-a-homeotic-transformation-by-helene-l-citerne-and-others) Thus a coarse character reconstruction can describe a return to radial symmetry while finer developmental characters indicate a new organization. The ancestral comparison generates competing molecular hypotheses; the expression data discriminate between their predictions.

Expression association remains weaker than a complete causal demonstration. To test the [Cadia](../../organism.md#cadia) model, compare spatial [gene expression](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-expression) at matched developmental stages in the derived lineage and a close bilateral relative. Use validated probes or [RNA sequencing](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-sequencing) with controls for cross-detection among duplicated copies, cellular composition and developmental timing. Reducing the expanded regulator specifically in lateral and ventral primordia should alter those organ identities if the proposed deployment is necessary. Conversely, expanding the corresponding regulator in a bilateral relative tests sufficiency. Include unmodified siblings, independent edited lines, a restoration construct and assays of other developmental functions, because a global perturbation may change floral shape indirectly through poor growth.

One can next distinguish [cis-regulatory evolution](../../molecular-genetics.md#cis-regulatory-evolution) from altered trans-acting inputs. Candidate [enhancers](../../molecular-genetics.md#enhancer) can be tested with matched reporters and endogenous sequence replacement; mapping an expression difference to a regulatory interval is more informative than comparing total transcript levels in whole [flowers](../../organism.md#flower). A reporter's behavior outside its native genomic context establishes regulatory potential, not the necessity of that element. If a coding change is implicated, reconstruct ancestral sequences on the relevant [gene tree](../../biology.md#gene-tree), synthesize plausible alternatives at uncertain sites, and compare their activity in the same [genetic background](../../genetics.md#genetic-background). Introducing derived substitutions into an ancestral sequence and reversing them in the derived sequence tests historical contingency and [epistasis](../../genetics.md#epistasis). A single best ancestral sequence should not be treated as an observed fossil protein.

Several limits govern interpretation. Reconstruction depends on the sampled relatives, rooting, character coding and the transition model; unequal gain and loss rates can change the inferred direction. Extinct intermediate lineages may be missing. A shift reconstructed on a long branch need not be simultaneous with every sequence substitution on that branch, and a high ratio of nonsynonymous to synonymous change does not itself identify a symmetry mechanism. Finally, explaining developmental origin is different from explaining adaptive significance. Pollinator behavior, [pollen](../../biology.md#pollen) transfer and lifetime [reproductive success](../../biology.md#reproductive-success) require direct field or experimental tests, with floral display and reward controlled, before a symmetry shift can be called a pollination [adaptation](../../biology.md#adaptation). **The strongest account connects a probabilistic historical transition to a specific molecular change and an experimentally established developmental effect, while keeping appearance, mechanism and selective advantage distinct.**

**The innovation is the integration of male and female reproductive organs on one developmentally coordinated, usually determinate shoot. Its origin is best understood as a reorganization of older reproductive programs, although the exact extinct precursor remains uncertain.** A [bisexual flower](../../organism.md#bisexual-flower) bears both [stamens](../../organism.md#stamen) and [carpels](../../organism.md#carpel); this differs from [monoecy](../../biology.md#monoecy), in which separate male and female structures occur on one individual. Many living [angiosperms](../../organism.md#angiosperm) have unisexual [flowers](../../organism.md#flower), so bisexuality is an inferred ancestral feature and a recurrently modified condition, not a definition satisfied by every living member.

The starting materials already existed in ancestral [seed plants](../../organism.md#seed-plant): [microsporangia](../../biology.md#microsporangium) producing the male lineage, [ovules](../../organism.md#ovule) retaining the female lineage, protective structures and reproductive [meristems](../../organism.md#meristem). Living [gymnosperms](../../organism.md#gymnosperm) provide comparisons of these functions, but no extant cone can simply be declared the direct ancestor of the [flower](../../organism.md#flower). A bisexual organization must establish male and female organ identities in distinct positions, integrate their maturation and regulate the growth of a common reproductive axis. Enclosure of [ovules](../../organism.md#ovule) within [carpels](../../organism.md#carpel), a receptive surface for [pollen](../../biology.md#pollen), and the elaboration of the [perianth](../../organism.md#perianth) are related innovations, but their historical order cannot be inferred merely by listing their present-day functions.

Developmental genetics shows how a common axis can generate different reproductive organs. In the [ABC model of flower development](../../biology.md#abc-model-of-flower-development), overlapping regulatory activities specify different organ types. For the reproductive regions, B- and C-class activity together, with suitable cofactors, specifies [stamen](../../organism.md#stamen) identity, while C-class activity without B-class activity specifies [carpel](../../organism.md#carpel) identity. In [Arabidopsis thaliana](../../biology.md#arabidopsis-thaliana), the relevant programs involve [APETALA3](../../molecular-genetics.md#apetala3), [PISTILLATA](../../molecular-genetics.md#pistillata) and [AGAMOUS](../../molecular-genetics.md#agamous), with [SEPALLATA](../../molecular-genetics.md#sepallata) activities required for normal specification of the inner organs. Higher-order loss of [SEPALLATA](../../molecular-genetics.md#sepallata) function transformed their identities, providing experimental evidence that the spatial code depends on additional regulatory partners.[Nature 405, pp. 200–203; sepallata multiple mutants and floral organ identity](../../biology-bibliography.md#b-and-c-floral-organ-identity-functions-require-sepallata-mads-box-genes-by-soraya-pelaz-and-others) The simple A/B/C lettering summarizes functional experiments in model [plants](../../organism.md#plant); its exact domains and functions are not universal across all [flowers](../../organism.md#flower).

Many of these regulators are [MADS-box transcription factors](../../molecular-genetics.md#mads-box-transcription-factor) acting in interacting complexes. [gene duplication](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-duplication), divergence of [protein](../../protein.md) interactions, changes in target [genes](../../genetics.md#gene) and [cis-regulatory evolution](../../molecular-genetics.md#cis-regulatory-evolution) can therefore generate new combinations and spatial boundaries without creating male and female development from nothing. Comparative work found related B-class activity in [gymnosperms](../../organism.md#gymnosperm), including experimental effects of a [Gnetum](../../organism.md#gnetum) regulator expressed in [Arabidopsis thaliana](../../biology.md#arabidopsis-thaliana).[The Plant Journal 31, pp. 457–475; heterologous expression and effects on floral B function](../../biology-bibliography.md#on-the-origin-of-class-b-floral-homeotic-genes-functional-substitution-and-dominant-inhibition-in-arabidopsis-by-expression-of-an-orthologue-from-the-gymnosperm-gnetum-by-kai-uwe-winter-and-others) This supports continuity in reproductive regulatory machinery. Heterologous activity does not show that a gymnosperm [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) had the same entire function in its native ancestor, or that the modern angiosperm network was already assembled.

One plausible developmental route is spatial subdivision of a previously unisexual axis. In an [out-of-male hypothesis](../../biology.md#out-of-male-hypothesis), restriction of B-class activity within an originally male reproductive structure allows a distal region with C-class but no B-class activity to express female identity, while the proximal region retains male identity. Conversely, recruitment of male activity into part of an initially female structure is developmentally conceivable. These are comparative models of changing organ identities; contemporary homeotic transformations demonstrate accessibility of a developmental outcome, not the sequence of events that actually occurred in an extinct lineage.

The [mostly male theory](../../biology.md#mostly-male-theory) makes a more specific historical proposal: an ancestral predominantly male structure acquired ectopic ovules and ultimately a bisexual organization. Its arguments combine morphology, fossils and hypotheses about reproductive regulators, including the history of [LEAFY](../../molecular-genetics.md#leafy) and [NEEDLY](../../molecular-genetics.md#needly) homologues.[The Botanical Review 66, pp. 143–171; proposed origin of bisexuality and links between gene histories and reproductive morphology](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-mostly-male-theory-of-flower-evolutionary-origins-from-genes-to-fossils-by-michael-w-frohlich-and-david-s-parker) It should not be equated with every model involving a shift in B-class expression. Nor does the loss or retention of one duplicated regulator uniquely determine the ancestral cone morphology: expression domains can evolve, [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) histories can be difficult to resolve, and the relevant intermediates may be extinct. Another possibility is condensation and reorganization of an axis bearing separate reproductive units. Fossil anatomy is needed to discriminate organ-level and whole-axis homologies.

[ancestral state reconstruction](../../biology.md#ancestral-state-reconstruction) across living [angiosperms](../../organism.md#angiosperm) supports a bisexual ancestral [flower](../../organism.md#flower), while leaving uncertainty about several details of its organization.[Nature Communications 8, article 16047; reconstruction of the crown-angiosperm flower and uncertainty across floral characters](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-ancestral-flower-of-angiosperms-and-its-early-diversification-by-herve-sauquet-and-others) This concerns the last common ancestor of living [angiosperms](../../organism.md#angiosperm), not necessarily the very first transitional reproductive shoot on their stem lineage. Different characters need not have arisen simultaneously. Fossils can constrain plausible combinations and their ordering, whereas developmental experiments reveal which reorganizations can work. Neither line of evidence alone establishes a complete historical sequence.

The adaptive opportunities of integration are substantial but conditional. A shared [perianth](../../organism.md#perianth) or floral reward can attract a visitor that both removes [pollen](../../biology.md#pollen) and deposits it on receptive [stigma](../../organism.md#stigma-botany) surfaces. Spatially coordinated organs can make transfer more precise and reduce the construction cost of maintaining separate displays. Bisexuality also permits flexible allocation between male and female reproduction as resources or mating opportunities change. When cross-pollination is unreliable, some self-compatible [flowers](../../organism.md#flower) can obtain [reproductive assurance](../../biology.md#reproductive-assurance) through self-fertilization. That benefit depends on compatibility and timing, however: bearing both organ types does not itself guarantee selfing.

Integration also creates conflicts. Transfer of a [plant](../../organism.md#plant)'s own [pollen](../../biology.md#pollen) to its receptive surfaces can reduce outcross [pollen](../../biology.md#pollen) export, block opportunities for outcrossing, or produce [inbreeding depression](../../population-genetics.md#inbreeding-depression). [herkogamy](../../organism.md#herkogamy) separates organs spatially, [dichogamy](../../organism.md#dichogamy) separates their maturation in time, and [self-incompatibility](../../biology.md#self-incompatibility) can reject genetically unsuitable [pollen](../../biology.md#pollen). These mechanisms show how the benefits of a shared shoot can be retained while limiting self-fertilization; they need not all have accompanied its initial origin. Later transitions to separate sexes can be favored in other ecological or genetic settings.

Tests of adaptive significance should compare [pollen](../../biology.md#pollen) export, receipt, [seed](../../organism.md#seed) set and offspring performance, rather than count visits alone. Manipulate organ position or timing while controlling display size and reward; compare cross-pollination and autonomous selfing treatments, with emasculation and hand-pollination controls where appropriate. A diversification analysis must also account for shared ancestry and other innovations: the coincidence of bisexual [flowers](../../organism.md#flower) and the success of [angiosperms](../../organism.md#angiosperm) does not isolate bisexuality as the cause of their [species richness](../../ecology.md#species-richness). **A defensible account combines inherited reproductive regulatory modules, new spatial coordination and conditional reproductive advantages, while treating the precise precursor and causal role in diversification as hypotheses.**

**Variation in [plant](../../organism.md#plant) strategies helps explain why different [species](../../biology.md#species) contribute different functions, and why a diverse community can sustain performance across conditions. The evidence must connect [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) differences to demographic consequences and then to measured ecosystem processes.** A [life-history trade-off](../../ecology.md#life-history-trade-off) is a limitation on jointly improving components such as growth, survival and reproduction under specified conditions. A [plant functional trait](../../ecology.md#plant-functional-trait) is a measurable characteristic affecting or indicating performance; it is not necessarily a direct measure of lifetime [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness).

Resource allocation supplies a mechanistic starting point. If an individual acquires an amount $A$ and allocates fraction $a$ to reproduction and the remainder to growth, then $R=aA$ and $G=(1-a)A$. At fixed $A$, increasing one allocation decreases the other. But individuals that acquire more can grow more and reproduce more simultaneously, so a positive observed growth–reproduction correlation does not disprove an allocation constraint. Conversely, a negative correlation can be caused by different environments rather than a genetically fixed constraint. Common-garden comparisons, manipulated resource supply and experimental alteration of reproductive demand can distinguish acquisition, allocation and environmental effects. Lifetime costs should include later survival and reproduction, rather than only current mass.

One strong comparative pattern is the [leaf economics spectrum](../../ecology.md#leaf-economics-spectrum). Relatively inexpensive, short-lived [leaves](../../organism.md#leaf) tend to have high mass-based [photosynthesis](../../biology.md#photosynthesis) and nutrient concentrations, whereas costly, persistent [leaves](../../organism.md#leaf) provide slower returns over longer periods. [specific leaf area](../../ecology.md#specific-leaf-area) and [leaf mass per area](../../ecology.md#leaf-mass-per-area) quantify opposite sides of the area–construction-mass relationship. A large global dataset found coordinated variation in [leaf](../../organism.md#leaf) construction, physiology and longevity across habitats, while also showing overlap among broad [plant](../../organism.md#plant) types.[Nature 428, pp. 821–827; global leaf-trait relationships and overlap among functional types](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-worldwide-leaf-economics-spectrum-by-ian-j-wright-and-others) This supports a recurring acquisition–conservation axis, not a rule that every high-photosynthesis [species](../../biology.md#species) must grow fastest in every habitat. Mass-based and area-based measurements must be distinguished, and leaf-level rates cannot by themselves determine whole-plant performance.

Demographic evidence is equally important. Comparisons of tropical trees link differences in growth and mortality to [phenotypic traits](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) including [wood density](../../ecology.md#wood-density), but a single [leaf](../../organism.md#leaf) or wood measurement does not explain every [species](../../biology.md#species)' strategy.[Ecology 91, pp. 3664–3674; trait associations with demographic variation in a tropical forest](../../biology-bibliography.md#functional-traits-and-the-growth-mortality-trade-off-in-tropical-trees-by-s-joseph-wright-and-others) Low construction costs can permit rapid acquisition in favorable conditions while providing less persistence under damage or resource scarcity. Direct demographic measurements are needed because a [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) may be correlated with several different mechanisms. Likewise, at a fixed reproductive budget, a large [seed](../../organism.md#seed) can carry more reserves but fewer [seeds](../../organism.md#seed) can be produced; its possible establishment advantage must be weighed against dispersal and the number of sites reached.

There is no single universal fast–slow line. [plant](../../organism.md#plant) height, [seed](../../organism.md#seed) size, [leaf](../../organism.md#leaf) construction, belowground deployment and phenology can vary along partly independent dimensions. A global analysis of [plant](../../organism.md#plant) form and function identified broad size-related and leaf-economics dimensions rather than one [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) that orders all [plant](../../organism.md#plant) strategies.[Nature 529, pp. 167–171; multidimensional variation in major plant traits](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-global-spectrum-of-plant-form-and-function-by-sandra-diaz-and-others) Patterns also vary within [species](../../biology.md#species) through [phenotypic plasticity](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-plasticity) and inherited differences. Closely related [species](../../biology.md#species) are not independent experimental replicates, and environmental sorting can generate [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) correlations. Analyses should incorporate shared ancestry, sampling across environments and uncertainty in the relation between an easily measured [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) and the underlying physiological mechanism.

These differences can affect coexistence. A [species](../../biology.md#species) efficient at acquiring a limiting resource when abundant may be less able to persist when it is scarce, and [species](../../biology.md#species) can differ in rooting depth, season of activity or response to disturbance. Such differences can reduce direct competition and allow complementary resource use. But a trade-off alone does not establish stable coexistence: the relevant population dynamics must allow each [species](../../biology.md#species) to recover when rare under the proposed mechanism. [niche differentiation](../../ecology.md#niche-differentiation) can stabilize coexistence, while large differences in competitive ability may still exclude a weaker [species](../../biology.md#species). [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) variation is therefore a source of testable mechanisms, not automatic proof that greater diversity must arise.

At the ecosystem level, [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) effects can operate through dominance and through interactions. The [mass-ratio hypothesis](../../ecology.md#mass-ratio-hypothesis) emphasizes that abundant [species](../../biology.md#species)' [phenotypic traits](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) strongly influence a community's processes, so high productivity may reflect dominance by a productive [species](../../biology.md#species). [complementarity effects](../../ecology.md#complementarity-effect) can arise when [species](../../biology.md#species) use resources differently or facilitate one another, increasing their combined performance. A [species richness](../../ecology.md#species-richness) experiment can separate an arithmetic selection component from an average relative-yield component. Let $M_i>0$ be [species](../../biology.md#species) $i$'s monoculture yield under comparable conditions, $p_i$ its initial mixture proportion, $Y_i$ its mixture yield and $\Delta RY_i=Y_i/M_i-p_i$, with $\sum_i p_i=1$. Then

$$
\Delta Y=\sum_iY_i-\sum_ip_iM_i
=N\,\overline{\Delta RY}\,\overline M+N\,\operatorname{Cov}(\Delta RY,M),
$$

where the [covariance](../../variance.md#covariance) uses denominator $N$. The first term is the [complementarity effect](../../ecology.md#complementarity-effect); the second is the [selection effect in biodiversity experiments](../../ecology.md#selection-effect-in-biodiversity-experiments).[Nature 412, pp. 72–76; additive partition of net biodiversity effects](../../biology-bibliography.md#partitioning-selection-and-complementarity-in-biodiversity-experiments-by-michel-loreau-and-andy-hector) A positive first term does not uniquely identify belowground resource partitioning: facilitation, changed enemies or other processes can also raise relative yield. Nor does this “selection effect” mean evolutionary [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection).

A compelling experiment manipulates [species richness](../../ecology.md#species-richness) and composition in replicated, randomized plots, holds initial total density or planting design comparable, and includes the appropriate monocultures. Multiple mixtures at each [species richness](../../ecology.md#species-richness) help separate [species](../../biology.md#species) identity from [species richness](../../ecology.md#species-richness). Measuring soil resources, seasonal production and root deployment can then test the proposed interaction, rather than merely report an effect on final standing biomass. [transgressive overyielding](../../ecology.md#transgressive-overyielding), in which a mixture exceeds its best monoculture comparator, is stronger evidence against a simple best-species sampling explanation, although it does not itself identify the physiological cause. Field diversity–productivity correlations alone can be confounded by soil fertility, management or reverse causation.

[phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) variation also matters through time. [species](../../biology.md#species) with different responses to drought, temperature or seasonal conditions need not fluctuate together. For total community biomass $B=\sum_iB_i$,

$$
\operatorname{Var}(B)=\sum_i\operatorname{Var}(B_i)+2\sum_{i<j}\operatorname{Cov}(B_i,B_j).
$$

Less synchronized fluctuations can stabilize total production even if individual populations remain variable. A decade-long grassland [species richness](../../ecology.md#species-richness) experiment found greater community-level productivity and temporal stability in more diverse treatments.[Nature 441, pp. 629–632; manipulated prairie diversity and temporal variation in productivity](../../biology-bibliography.md#biodiversity-and-ecosystem-stability-in-a-decade-long-grassland-experiment-by-david-tilman-and-others) A stability metric such as $\mu_B/\sigma_B$ can increase through a higher mean as well as a lower variability, so these contributions should be examined separately. [response diversity](../../ecology.md#response-diversity) is especially relevant when [species](../../biology.md#species) with similar effects on a function differ in their vulnerability to change.

Finally, one function cannot stand for all ecosystem functioning. Rapid biomass production, long-term [carbon storage](../../ecology.md#carbon-storage), nutrient retention, decomposition and resistance to disturbance can depend on different [phenotypic traits](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) or involve conflicts. [ecosystem multifunctionality](../../ecology.md#ecosystem-multifunctionality) can therefore require [species](../../biology.md#species) that contribute differently across functions, places and years; apparent redundancy for one measured function need not persist under a new climate or when another function is considered. **Trade-off evidence supports a mechanistic explanation for the roles of [biodiversity](../../ecology.md#biodiversity), but the inference is strongest when trait–performance relationships, causal diversity manipulations and the particular ecosystem functions are all measured.**

**Both lineages sustain major [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) reservoirs and ongoing uptake, but their relative contribution depends on biome, area, turnover, disturbance and the [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) pool being counted. High [photosynthesis](../../biology.md#photosynthesis), a large existing stock and durable net [carbon sequestration](../../ecology.md#carbon-sequestration) are different quantities.** [gymnosperms](../../organism.md#gymnosperm) dominate many coniferous boreal and some temperate forests; [angiosperms](../../organism.md#angiosperm) dominate much tropical and temperate broadleaf forest and also contribute grasslands, savannas, wetlands and other vegetation. Comparing only the number of [species](../../biology.md#species), or only the average growth rate of a tree, cannot rank their global contribution.

Begin with a [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) budget for a stated area and period. [gross primary production](../../biology.md#gross-primary-production) is total photosynthetic uptake. Subtracting [autotrophic respiration](../../biology.md#autotrophic-respiration) gives [net primary production](../../ecology.md#net-primary-production), and subtracting [heterotrophic respiration](../../biology.md#heterotrophic-respiration) gives [net ecosystem production](../../ecology.md#net-ecosystem-production):

$$
\mathrm{NPP}=\mathrm{GPP}-R_a,\qquad
\mathrm{NEP}=\mathrm{GPP}-R_a-R_h.
$$

For an ecosystem boundary that excludes exported products, its change in [carbon storage](../../ecology.md#carbon-storage) also includes disturbance and lateral transfers:

$$
\boxed{\frac{\Delta C}{\Delta t}=\mathrm{NEP}-F_{\mathrm{fire}}-F_{\mathrm{harvest}}-F_{\mathrm{export}}+F_{\mathrm{import}}.}
$$

Each flux must use the same units and period, and the terms must be defined to avoid counting the same loss twice. For an invented annual example, $\mathrm{GPP}=12$, $R_a=6$, $R_h=5$ and combustion losses of 2, all in tonnes of [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) per hectare per year, give a net loss of 1 despite positive [net ecosystem production](../../ecology.md#net-ecosystem-production). [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) transferred from a living tree to dead wood by mortality is initially an internal transfer, not an instantaneous atmospheric emission; subsequent decomposition or combustion determines release.

Existing [carbon storage](../../ecology.md#carbon-storage) includes living stems and [root](../../organism.md#root-botany) systems, dead material and soil. A slow-growing forest can contain a large stock accumulated over long periods, while a young stand with a smaller stock can have a high current [carbon sequestration](../../ecology.md#carbon-sequestration) rate. Conversely, some old forests continue accumulating [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon); age alone does not determine the sign of the flux. Converting a [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) quantity to [carbon dioxide](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) mass requires the factor $44/12$, not a change in the biological [carbon sequestration](../../ecology.md#carbon-sequestration) rate. A comparison of global totals must sum area-weighted pool sizes and fluxes across regions, rather than extrapolate one hectare or infer taxonomic shares directly from biome averages.

[gymnosperm](../../organism.md#gymnosperm) forests illustrate the importance of persistence and environment. Many conifers retain foliage, operate in short growing seasons and invest in durable tissues. Long-lived stems can store [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) for decades to centuries; cold or waterlogged conditions can slow decomposition and support large soil or organic-layer stocks. These soil stocks are properties of the whole [ecosystem](../../ecology.md#ecosystem), including [root](../../organism.md#root-botany) systems, microbes, hydrology and often [bryophytes](../../organism.md#bryophyte), and cannot all be attributed to conifer physiology. Slow annual production can coexist with large accumulated storage. The conservative features of many conifers also carry costs, and neither evergreen foliage nor slow growth is universal within [gymnosperms](../../organism.md#gymnosperm).

[angiosperms](../../organism.md#angiosperm) occupy a wider range of growth forms and strongly influence highly productive warm forests as well as seasonal and open systems. Large tropical trees maintain substantial woody stocks, while root turnover, litter inputs and soil protection contribute additional storage. In grasslands, much persistent [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) can be belowground despite a modest aboveground standing crop. Many [angiosperms](../../organism.md#angiosperm) have vessel-based [xylem](../../organism.md#xylem) with high transport capacity, supporting rapid resource acquisition under favorable conditions; many [gymnosperms](../../organism.md#gymnosperm) rely on [tracheids](../../organism.md#tracheid). These are mechanistic differences, not a universal hydraulic ranking: anatomy varies within both lineages, and drought resistance depends on [phenotypic traits](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) of the entire transport pathway. Likewise, fast-growing angiosperm tissue can turn over rapidly, so high production is not equivalent to long residence time.

Inventory-based global forest budgets demonstrate substantial uptake in several forest regions, while also showing why land-use losses must be included when calculating a net forest sink.[Science 333, pp. 988–993; forest inventory synthesis and separate accounting of land-use emissions and regrowth](../../biology-bibliography.md#a-large-and-persistent-carbon-sink-in-the-world-s-forests-by-yude-pan-and-others) Such estimates are generally organized by regions and forest histories, not a clean experimental split between seed-plant lineages. A rigorous comparison would combine forest composition and area with repeated biomass inventories, belowground and soil measurements, [eddy covariance](../../ecology.md#eddy-covariance) and remote sensing. Allometric uncertainty, unmeasured deep soil and incomplete disturbance histories limit apparent precision. [eddy covariance](../../ecology.md#eddy-covariance) measures atmospheric exchange for a footprint; it does not independently assign every flux to a taxonomic group or automatically measure exported [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon).

Elevated [carbon dioxide](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) can stimulate [photosynthesis](../../biology.md#photosynthesis) and increase [water-use efficiency](../../ecology.md#water-use-efficiency) through changes in uptake and [stomatal conductance](../../biology.md#stomatal-conductance), creating opportunities for growth in both groups. But storage responses depend on nutrient supply, water, allocation and respiratory losses. In a deciduous forest [free-air carbon dioxide enrichment](../../ecology.md#free-air-carbon-dioxide-enrichment) experiment, stimulation of production weakened as nitrogen limitation developed.[PNAS 107, pp. 19368–19373; eleven-year enrichment experiment and changing nitrogen limitation](../../biology-bibliography.md#co2-enhancement-of-forest-productivity-constrained-by-limited-nitrogen-availability-by-richard-j-norby-and-others) In a mature forest enrichment experiment, a comprehensive [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) budget found that much additional uptake returned through respiratory fluxes rather than accumulating as new storage.[Nature 580, pp. 227–231; ecosystem carbon budget after four years of enrichment](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-fate-of-carbon-in-a-mature-forest-under-carbon-dioxide-enrichment-by-mingkai-jiang-and-others) These are counterexamples to equating a leaf-level [fertilization](../../biology.md#fertilization) response with a durable whole-ecosystem sink. Replicated ambient plots, nutrient measurements and tracking of belowground allocation are essential controls.

Climate warming can lengthen northern growing seasons and permit recruitment where low temperature previously limited growth. Yet warming can also increase atmospheric [vapour-pressure deficit](../../ecology.md#vapour-pressure-deficit), soil drying, respiration, fire and insect damage. Thawing [permafrost](../../geophysics.md#permafrost) can expose older soil [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) to decomposition; that loss is not automatically balanced by new tree growth. Forest expansion across bright snow-covered surfaces can lower [albedo](../../geophysics.md#albedo), partly offsetting the climatic cooling from additional [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) storage.[Nature 408, pp. 187–190; modeled carbon and snow-albedo effects of boreal forestation](../../biology-bibliography.md#offset-of-the-potential-carbon-sink-from-boreal-forestation-by-decreases-in-surface-albedo-by-richard-a-betts) This is a distinction between [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) accounting and the full climate effect, not an argument that stored [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) has no value.

Warm angiosperm-dominated forests face heat, drying and disturbance as well. Hydraulic damage can impair water transport in both major seed-plant lineages, and comparative hydraulic measurements show that forests in wet climates are not necessarily protected by large safety margins.[Nature 491, pp. 752–755; comparison of experienced xylem pressures and hydraulic damage thresholds](../../biology-bibliography.md#global-convergence-in-the-vulnerability-of-forests-to-drought-by-brendan-choat-and-others) Long-term tropical plot records found different timings of weakening uptake in African and Amazonian forests, with mortality an important contributor.[Nature 579, pp. 80–87; repeated inventories, tree growth and mortality, and contrasting regional sink histories](../../biology-bibliography.md#asynchronous-carbon-sink-saturation-in-african-and-amazonian-tropical-forests-by-wannes-hubau-and-others) A declining sink can remain a sink; a trend toward zero uptake should not be reported as an observed net source without the corresponding budget. Nor can these plot results be generalized to every angiosperm ecosystem.

Future distributions will depend on recruitment and dispersal as well as adult tolerance. Replacement of conifers by broadleaf [species](../../biology.md#species) can alter growth, litter, fire and surface reflectance, but neither the direction nor the total [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) consequence follows from lineage identity alone. Management should therefore compare protection of existing stocks, avoided disturbance, regrowth and planting under explicit baselines, over an appropriate time horizon. Harvested products, their decay and any claimed fossil-fuel substitution require separate accounting rather than assuming every harvested tonne remains sequestered. **Gymnosperm forests are especially consequential through extensive long-lived woody and cold-soil reservoirs, while angiosperms support large warm-forest stocks, substantial production and diverse belowground stores; the durability of both contributions depends on the balance of uptake, turnover and climate-sensitive losses.**
