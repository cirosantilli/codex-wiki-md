# Ion

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry-split.md)

An [ion](ion.md) is a [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) or a group of [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) with a nonzero net [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md). A positive [ion](ion.md) is a [cation](cation.md); a negative [ion](ion.md) is an [anion](anion.md). The whole entity carries the [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md): a [hydronium](hydronium.md) [ion](ion.md) is a group of four [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) with net charge $+e$. This definition agrees with [IUPAC Gold Book: ion](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/I03158).

For an ordinary [ion](ion.md) containing only [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md) and [electrons](electron.md), let $N_p$ count all its [protons](proton.md) and $N_e$ all its [electrons](electron.md). Each [proton](proton.md) contributes the [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md) $+e$, each [electron](electron.md) contributes $-e$, and [neutrons](neutron-split.md) contribute zero. Therefore

$$
q=(N_p-N_e)e=ze,\qquad z=N_p-N_e.
$$

The signed integer $z$ is the net [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) number. Removing an [electron](electron.md) increases $z$ by one; adding an [electron](electron.md) decreases $z$ by one. Changing the [electron](electron.md) count leaves the [atomic number](atomic-number.md), and therefore the identity of the [chemical element](chemical-element.md), unchanged.

For example, a neutral [sodium](sodium.md) [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) has $11$ [protons](proton.md) and $11$ [electrons](electron.md). Its [cation](cation.md) $\mathrm{Na}^+$ still has $11$ [protons](proton.md), but only $10$ [electrons](electron.md), so $q=(11-10)e=+e$. A neutral [chlorine](chlorine.md) [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) has $17$ [protons](proton.md) and $17$ [electrons](electron.md); its [anion](anion.md) $\mathrm{Cl}^-$ has $17$ [protons](proton.md) and $18$ [electrons](electron.md), so $q=(17-18)e=-e$. These are particle inventories, not pictures of [atomic orbitals](atomic-orbital.md). See [2.6 Ionic and Molecular Compounds](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/2-6-ionic-and-molecular-compounds.md) (§2.6: ions and ionic compounds).

![](ion-charge-bookkeeping.png)

**[Figure 7](#image-ion-electric-charge-bookkeeping-neutral-sodium-and-chlorine-chemical-atoms-compared-with-their-cation-and-anion). Ion electric charge bookkeeping: neutral sodium and chlorine chemical atoms compared with their cation and anion**. Every colored dot counts one [proton](proton.md) or one [electron](electron.md). Rows are counting arrays, not spatial models of [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md) or [electron shells](electron-shell.md). The net [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) are $0,+e,0,-e$.

One [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md) [formula unit](formula-unit.md) accounts for one $\mathrm{Na}^+$ [cation](cation.md) and one $\mathrm{Cl}^-$ [anion](anion.md), whose net [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) sum to zero. Ordinary solid [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md) is an extended arrangement of [ions](ion.md), rather than discrete $\mathrm{NaCl}$ [molecules](molecule-split.md). This is a useful distinction: a neutral material can contain charged constituents, and its [chemical formula](chemical-formula.md) tells us their ratio without claiming that each pair is a separate [molecule](molecule-split.md).

[Ion](ion.md) bookkeeping connects [chemistry](chemistry-split.md) to [electric current](electric-current.md): moving [ions](ion.md) can transport [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md), while stationary [ions](ion.md) cannot supply a transport [current density](current-density.md). In [electrochemistry](electrochemistry-split.md), both signs of [ions](ion.md) can contribute to the same [conventional current](conventional-current.md). An [ion](ion.md) opposing a fixed [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) is called a [counterion](counterion.md).
