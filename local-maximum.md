# Local maximum

↑ **Parent:** [Critical point](critical-point.md)

A [real-valued function](real-valued-function.md) $f:D\to\mathbb R$, with $D\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, has a local maximum at $x_*$ if some $\delta>0$ satisfies

$$
f(x)\leq f(x_*)\qquad\text{for all }x\in D\text{ with }\|x-x_*\|<\delta.
$$

Only a [neighbourhood](neighbourhood-mathematics.md) within the [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) is compared. Equality is allowed; a [strict local maximum](strict-local-maximum.md) requires $f(x)<f(x_*)$ for every other point in that [neighbourhood](neighbourhood-mathematics.md). A [constant function](constant-function.md) therefore has a [local maximum](local-maximum.md) and a [local minimum](local-minimum.md) at every input, with neither strict. This is a local form of an [extremum of a real-valued function](maximum-and-minimum.md).

For $f(x)=x^4-2x^2$, zero is a [strict local maximum](strict-local-maximum.md) because $f(x)=x^2(x^2-2)<0=f(0)$ when $0<|x|<\sqrt2$. It is not a [global maximum](global-maximum.md): values far away grow without bound. This distinction makes a [local maximum](local-maximum.md) useful for describing a nearby peak without claiming that it is the highest value anywhere.

Negating the [function](function-split.md) exchanges [local maxima](local-maximum.md) and [local minima](local-minimum.md), including their strict versions. At a smooth [interior](interior-topology.md) [local maximum](local-maximum.md), the [critical point](critical-point.md) argument forces zero [gradient](gradient.md); for a [function](function-split.md) twice continuously [differentiable](differentiable-function.md) nearby, its [Hessian matrix](hessian-matrix.md) is [negative semidefinite](negative-semidefinite-matrix.md). These necessary conditions alone do not establish a [local maximum](local-maximum.md).
