<h1 id="young-s-modulus">Young's modulus</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Linear elasticity](linear-elasticity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young's_modulus)

Young's modulus is the ratio of axial [stress](stress.md) to axial [strain](strain.md) in uniaxial [linear elasticity](linear-elasticity.md). For an isotropic plate, the bending rigidity is $E t^3/[12(1-\nu^2)]$, with thickness $t$ and [Poisson's ratio](poisson-s-ratio.md) $\nu$.

A [tensile modulus experiment](tensile-modulus-experiment.md) asks how much a specimen stretches under an axial [force](force-split.md), while allowing its sides to contract freely. For initial cross-sectional [area](surface-area.md) $A_0$ and an [extensometer](extensometer.md) spanning initial [gauge length](gauge-length.md) $L_g$, define [engineering stress](engineering-stress.md) $\sigma=F/A_0$ and [engineering strain](engineering-strain.md) $\varepsilon=\Delta L_g/L_g$. In the proportional range,

$$
E=\frac{d\sigma}{d\varepsilon}=\frac{L_g}{A_0}\frac{dF}{d(\Delta L_g)}.
$$

The ratio $\sigma/\varepsilon$ agrees with this [derivative](derivative.md) when the line passes through the unloaded origin. A fitted offset should not be mistaken for a material property. [Young's modulus](young-s-modulus.md) has units of [pressure](pressure-split.md): $1\ \mathrm{Pa}=1\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$. By contrast, the [spring constant](spring-constant.md) of a uniform tensile specimen of length $L$ is $k_s=EA_0/L$, with units $\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$. Two rods made of the same material can therefore have very different [spring constants](spring-constant.md) while sharing [Young's modulus](young-s-modulus.md).

The normalization removes a useful geometrical ambiguity: doubling $L$ doubles the extension at a given [force](force-split.md), whereas doubling $A_0$ halves it. For a round specimen $A_0=\pi d^2/4$; for a rectangular one $A_0=wt$, using initial diameter $d$, width $w$ and thickness $t$. Diameter error matters twice at first order: $\delta A_0/A_0\simeq2\delta d/d$. Specify material direction, [temperature](temperature-split.md) and loading rate when comparing [Young's modulus](young-s-modulus.md). Lateral restraint changes the [stress](stress.md) state and can prevent this experiment from measuring the uniaxial modulus.

The name commemorates [Thomas Young](thomas-young.md), whose formulation of a measure of [elasticity](elasticity-physics-split.md) is recorded by the Royal Society. This attribution alone does not establish priority for every modern definition or experimental method.[Royal Society person record NA7947, Activity: formulation of a measure of elasticity called Young's Modulus](young-thomas-1773-1829-physician-physicist-and-egyptologist-by-royal-society.md)
