**No.** Let $S$ be the displayed infinite axiom set. Any finite subset $S_0\subseteq S$ mentions only finitely many of the two infinitude requirements. Choose $N\geq1$ at least as large as every index mentioned. A finite [equivalence relation](../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) with exactly $N$ singleton classes and $N$ doubleton classes satisfies $S_0$, but is not a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of the [complete theory](../../../../../complete-theory.md).

If a finite axiomatization existed, let $\sigma$ be the conjunction of its axioms. Then $S\models\sigma$, so the [compactness theorem](../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) gives a finite $S_0\subseteq S$ with $S_0\models\sigma$. Every [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of $S_0$ would then satisfy the full [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md), contradicting the finite [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) above. This is the [finite subaxiomatization from an equivalent finite theory](../../../../../finite-subaxiomatization-from-an-equivalent-finite-theory.md) argument: **finite axiomatizability would force one finite fragment of the explicit axioms to suffice**.
