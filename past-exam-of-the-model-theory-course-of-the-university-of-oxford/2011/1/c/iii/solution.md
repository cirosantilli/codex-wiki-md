<h1 id="1/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For the additive [first-order structure](../../../../../first-order-structure.md), let

$$
\varphi_n(x):=\exists y\,(x=\underbrace{y+\cdots+y}_{n\text{ terms}}),\qquad n\geq1.
$$

It defines $n\mathbb Z$. These sets are pairwise distinct: if $m>n>0$, the integer $n$ belongs to $n\mathbb Z$ but not to $m\mathbb Z$. Thus there are infinitely many inequivalent one-variable [first-order formulas](../../../../../first-order-formula.md) modulo the [complete theory](../../../../../complete-theory.md) of the additive integers. The [Ryll-Nardzewski theorem](../../../../../ryll-nardzewski-theorem.md) implies **that [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) is not aleph-zero-categorical**.

For the multiplicative [first-order structure](../../../../../first-order-structure.md) define divisibility by $d(y,x):=\exists z(yz=x)$. For $k\geq1$, the [first-order formula](../../../../../first-order-formula.md)

$$
\psi_k(x):=\exists y_1\cdots y_{2k}\left(\bigwedge_{i<j}y_i\ne y_j\ \land\ \bigwedge_{i=1}^{2k}d(y_i,x)\right)
$$

asserts the existence of at least $2k$ distinct integer divisors. The integer $2^{k-1}$ has exactly $2k$ such divisors, namely $\pm2^j$ for $0\leq j\leq k-1$, so it satisfies $\psi_k$ but not any $\psi_\ell$ with $\ell>k$. Again there are infinitely many inequivalent one-variable [first-order formulas](../../../../../first-order-formula.md). The [Ryll-Nardzewski theorem](../../../../../ryll-nardzewski-theorem.md) therefore implies **the [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) of the multiplicative integers is not aleph-zero-categorical**.
