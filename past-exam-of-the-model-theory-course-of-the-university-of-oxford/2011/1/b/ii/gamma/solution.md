**No.** Use the [prime-sign automorphism of the multiplicative integers](../../../../../../prime-sign-automorphism-of-the-multiplicative-integers.md)

$$
s(0)=0,\qquad s(n)=(-1)^{v_2(n)}n\quad(n\ne0),
$$

where $v_2(n)$ is the exponent of two in $|n|$, a [P-adic valuation](../../../../../../p-adic-valuation.md). For nonzero integers, $v_2(mn)=v_2(m)+v_2(n)$, so $s(mn)=s(m)s(n)$; the identity also holds when a factor is zero. The exponent of two is unchanged by changing the sign, hence $s(s(n))=n$. Thus $s$ is a multiplicative [structure automorphism](../../../../../../automorphism-of-a-first-order-structure.md).

It fixes one and sends two to minus two. The addition-graph point $(1,1,2)$ is sent to $(1,1,-2)$, outside the graph of addition. By [automorphism invariance of parameter-free definability](../../../../../../automorphism-invariance-of-parameter-free-definability.md), **addition is not definable in the multiplicative reduct**.
