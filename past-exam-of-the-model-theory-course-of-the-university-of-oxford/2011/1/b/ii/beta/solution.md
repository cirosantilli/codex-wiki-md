**Yes.** One is the unique identity element of multiplication, defined by

$$
\boxed{\forall y\,(x\cdot y=y).}
$$

The integer one satisfies the [first-order formula](../../../../../../first-order-formula.md), and testing at the actual integer one forces any satisfying $x$ to equal one. Thus this is a parameter-free [universal formula](../../../../../../universal-formula.md) even though the language has no named identity.
