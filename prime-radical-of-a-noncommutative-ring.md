# Prime radical of a noncommutative ring

↑ **Parent:** [Prime ideal of a noncommutative ring](prime-ideal-of-a-noncommutative-ring.md)

The prime radical is the intersection of all [prime ideals of a noncommutative ring](prime-ideal-of-a-noncommutative-ring.md). For a nonzero [ring](ring.md) satisfying the [ascending chain condition](ascending-chain-condition.md) on two-sided [ideals](ideal.md), there exist [prime ideals of a noncommutative ring](prime-ideal-of-a-noncommutative-ring.md) $P_1,\ldots,P_r$ with $P_1\cdots P_r=0$. Indeed, a maximal counterexample among two-sided [ideals](ideal.md) could not itself be a [prime ideal of a noncommutative ring](prime-ideal-of-a-noncommutative-ring.md), and two larger witness [ideals](ideal.md) would contradict its maximality. Every [prime ideal of a noncommutative ring](prime-ideal-of-a-noncommutative-ring.md) contains one $P_i$, so the intersection equals $\bigcap_iP_i$, and its $r$th power is zero. This nilpotence assertion fails without a chain condition.
