# Bosonic coincidence enhancement in two orthogonal orbitals

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic exchange symmetry](bosonic-exchange-symmetry.md)

Place one noninteracting spin-zero [boson](boson.md) in each of two orthonormal orbitals $a,b$. Its normalized symmetric spatial [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) is $[a(\mathbf r_1)b(\mathbf r_2)+b(\mathbf r_1)a(\mathbf r_2)]/\sqrt2$. At coincident positions the [probability density](probability-density.md) is $2|a(\mathbf r)|^2|b(\mathbf r)|^2$, twice the labelled distinguishable-product value. Coincidence at an exact point is understood as a density or the limit of small detector volumes. If both particles occupy the same orbital, the normalized product is already symmetric and this comparison gives factor one instead; the factor two is not a universal property of every two-boson state.
