# Natural selection

↑ **Parent:** [Evolution](evolution.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection)

[Natural selection](natural-selection.md) involves systematic differences in expected [reproductive success](reproductive-success.md) associated with variation in [phenotype](phenotype.md). For a continuing evolutionary response, the relevant variation must be inherited: offspring must tend to preserve the differences being favored. Actual offspring counts also fluctuate by chance, producing [genetic drift](genetic-drift.md). Survival matters insofar as it affects contribution to future generations. [Genotype fitness](genotype-fitness.md) is therefore contextual, depending on environment and the life cycle being counted, rather than simply measuring strength or longevity. See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e: population evolution](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/19-1-population-evolution).

In chapter IV of his 1859 book, Darwin explained [natural selection](natural-selection.md) through preservation of favorable variations and greater chances of survival and reproduction. See [Darwin, On the Origin of Species, first edition, chapter IV](https://darwin-online.org.uk/converted/published/1859_Origin_F373/1859_Origin_F373.html). The beautiful consequence is cumulative change without foresight: inherited differences are filtered repeatedly by present conditions. [Natural selection](natural-selection.md) neither generates a needed [mutation](mutation.md) on demand nor guarantees progress toward a predetermined goal.
