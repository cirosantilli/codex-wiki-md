# A univalent function has nonzero derivative

↑ **Parent:** [Univalent function](univalent-function.md)

For an [injective](injective-function.md) [holomorphic function](holomorphic-function.md) $f$ on an [open subset](open-set.md) of $\mathbb C$, $f'(z)$ is nowhere zero. If $f(z)-f(z_0)=(z-z_0)^m h(z)$ with $h(z_0)\ne0$, choose a small [circle](circle.md) where both $h$ and $mh+(z-z_0)h'$ have no zeros. The [argument principle](argument-principle.md) counts $m$ zeros of $f(z)-f(z_0)-w$ inside that [circle](circle.md) for sufficiently small $w\ne0$: deforming $w$ to zero cannot change the count. All these zeros are simple, since $f'$ only vanishes at $z_0$ inside the [circle](circle.md) and $z_0$ is not a zero when $w\ne0$. Injectivity forces $m=1$, and hence $f'(z_0)\ne0$.
