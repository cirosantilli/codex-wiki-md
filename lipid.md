# Lipid

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical substance](chemical-substance.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid)

[Lipids](lipid.md) are a chemically diverse family of [chemical substances](chemical-substance.md), conventionally associated with biological origin and solubility in [nonpolar](nonpolar-molecule.md) [solvents](solvent.md). They include [triglycerides](triglyceride.md), [phospholipids](phospholipid.md), and [cholesterol](cholesterol.md). The name does not specify one [molecular formula](molecular-formula.md), one repeating [monomer](monomer.md), or one kind of [polymer](polymer.md). Some [lipids](lipid.md) have both [hydrophilic](hydrophilicity.md) and [hydrophobic](hydrophobicity.md) regions rather than being uniformly [nonpolar](nonpolar-molecule.md). See [IUPAC Gold Book: lipids](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/L03571).

Structure explains the different jobs. A [triglyceride](triglyceride.md) has three [fatty acid](fatty-acid.md)-derived [acyl groups](acyl-group.md) attached to [glycerol](glycerol.md) and serves as an [energy](energy.md) store. A typical [glycerophospholipid](glycerophospholipid.md) instead combines two such chains with a [phosphate](phosphate.md)-containing head, making it an [amphiphile](amphiphile.md) suited to a [lipid bilayer](lipid-bilayer.md). [Cholesterol](cholesterol.md) has a different structure and also contributes to [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md) properties. Thus a [lipid](lipid.md) is a chemical building material, while a [lipid bilayer](lipid-bilayer.md) is an organization of many [molecules](molecule.md). See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 3.3: lipid structures and functions](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/3-3-lipids).
