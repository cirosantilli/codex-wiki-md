# Symmetric top with a quadratic alignment potential

↑ **Parent:** [Symmetric top](symmetric-top.md)

Consider a [symmetric top](symmetric-top.md) with fixed vertex $Q$, axial [unit vector](unit-vector.md) $\mathbf e$, [principal moments of inertia](principal-moment-of-inertia.md) $A,A,C$, and centre of mass at $h\mathbf e$. Let a [force](force-split.md) $\kappa\ell(\mathbf k-\mathbf e)$ act at $\ell\mathbf e$, where $\mathbf k$ is the upward vertical [unit vector](unit-vector.md). Together with [gravity](gravity.md), it produces [torque](torque.md) $(\kappa\ell^2-mgh)\mathbf e\times\mathbf k$ and [potential energy](potential-energy.md) $mgh\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf e+\kappa\ell^2(1-\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf e)$.

The [axial angular velocity of a symmetric top](axial-angular-velocity-of-a-symmetric-top.md) $n$, vertical [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf L$ and total [mechanical energy](mechanical-energy.md) are conserved. For steady [precession](precession.md), $\dot{\mathbf e}=\Omega\mathbf k\times\mathbf e$ and $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf e=\cos\alpha$. Substituting $\mathbf e\times\ddot{\mathbf e}=\Omega^2\cos\alpha\,\mathbf e\times\mathbf k$ into $A\mathbf e\times\ddot{\mathbf e}+Cn\dot{\mathbf e}=(\kappa\ell^2-mgh)\mathbf e\times\mathbf k$ gives the condition in the title for nonvertical inclination. If the total [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) itself is $\Omega\mathbf k$, then $n=\Omega\cos\alpha$, and the condition becomes $\kappa\ell^2=mgh+(A-C)\Omega^2\cos\alpha$.
