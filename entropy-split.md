# Entropy

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy)

Entropy is an [extensive quantity](extensive-quantity.md) and a [state function](state-function.md) of an equilibrium system, measured in $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$. Differences between equilibrium states are calculated along a [reversible thermodynamic process](reversible-thermodynamic-process.md):

$$
S_2-S_1=\int_1^2\frac{\delta Q_{\rm rev}}{T}.
$$

Here [heat](heat-split.md) entering the system is positive and $T$ is its absolute [thermodynamic temperature](thermodynamic-temperature.md). The reversible path used for calculation need not be the path actually taken. Dividing the actual [heat](heat-split.md) by a single [temperature](temperature-split.md) generally fails for an irreversible process.

For example, put two rigid bodies, each with constant [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) $C=100\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$, at $600\,\mathrm K$ and $300\,\mathrm K$ in an otherwise [isolated system](isolated-system.md). Assume each body has a uniform internal [temperature](temperature-split.md), no [phase transition](phase-transition.md) occurs, and no [work](work.md) is extracted. Their final [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md) is at $450\,\mathrm K$, because the [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md) lost by one equals that gained by the other. Using [entropy from heat capacity](entropy-from-heat-capacity.md),

$$
\Delta S_h=C\log(450/600)=-28.77\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}},\qquad
\Delta S_c=C\log(450/300)=40.55\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}},
$$

so $\Delta S_{\rm tot}=C\log(9/8)=11.78\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$. One body's [entropy](entropy-split.md) decreases while the total increases. [MIT, Entropy changes and the hot-brick problem](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node41.html)

**[Figure 1](#image-thermal-equilibration-of-two-finite-bodies-conserves-energy-and-increases-total-entropy). Thermal equilibration of two finite bodies conserves energy and increases total entropy**.

In [statistical mechanics](statistical-mechanics.md), [Boltzmann's entropy formula](boltzmann-s-entropy-formula.md) counts equally likely [microstates](microstate.md) compatible with a [macrostate](macrostate.md); [Gibbs entropy](gibbs-entropy.md) handles a specified [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) over [microstates](microstate.md). If the number of equally likely [microstates](microstate.md) doubles, [entropy](entropy-split.md) increases by $k_B\log2$. The [logarithm](logarithm.md) makes multiplicative counts additive for independent systems. These are precise statements about states and probabilities, not a universal measure of visible messiness. Calling [entropy](entropy-split.md) merely "disorder" obscures the thermal calculation above and hides which [microstates](microstate.md) are being counted. [MIT, Statistical entropy and randomness](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node57.html)

The usefulness of [entropy](entropy-split.md) is that measurable [heat capacities](heat-capacity.md) determine its changes, and the [Second law of thermodynamics](second-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) turns those changes into constraints on processes. It also connects macroscopic measurements to microscopic descriptions without requiring us to track every particle.
