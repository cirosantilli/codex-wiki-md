# Bounded slope integrands force strong minima to be global

↑ **Parent:** [Weierstrass necessary condition](weierstrass-necessary-condition.md)

Suppose the [Weierstrass necessary condition](weierstrass-necessary-condition.md) applies to a strong local minimum of this fixed-endpoint integral. It requires the tangent to $g$ at $u'(x)$ to lie below the entire graph for almost every $x$. A line of nonzero slope is unbounded above in one direction, while $g$ is bounded; hence this supporting line has slope zero. Its contact value is then a [global minimum](global-minimum.md) of $g$. The resulting [function](function-split.md) minimizes the integral globally, since its integrand is almost everywhere equal to the global lower bound. Thus for a bounded differentiable slope-only integrand a strong local minimum can exist only at global energy level. Weak local minima can behave differently because their competitors have nearby derivatives.
