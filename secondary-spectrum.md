# Secondary spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Achromatic lens](achromatic-lens.md)

The [secondary spectrum](secondary-spectrum.md) is residual [chromatic aberration](chromatic-aberration.md) after an [achromatic lens](achromatic-lens.md) brings two selected [wavelengths](wavelength.md) to the same [optical focus](focus-optics.md). For example, equal red and blue [focal lengths](focal-length.md) can coexist with a different green [focal length](focal-length.md). The [Abbe number](abbe-number.md) fixes one F–C relative difference, not the complete shape of [optical dispersion](dispersion-optics.md) between or beyond those [wavelengths](wavelength.md). The perfectly flat sum in the [achromatic doublet power balance](achromatic-doublet-power-balance.md) diagram deliberately omits this residual effect. [Nikon, ED glass and secondary spectrum](https://imaging.nikon.com/sport-optics/guide/fieldscopes/choosing/choosing_03/).
