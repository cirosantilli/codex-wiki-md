# Linear independence

↑ **Parent:** [Vector space](vector-space-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_independence)

In a [vector space](vector-space-split.md) $V$ over a specified [field](field.md) $F$, a finite family of [vectors](vector.md) $(v_1,\ldots,v_n)$ is [linearly independent](linear-independence.md) when, for [coefficients](coefficient.md) $a_i\in F$,

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n a_i v_i=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_1=\cdots=a_n=0.
$$

Here the left-hand zero is the [zero vector](zero-vector.md), while the right-hand zeros belong to the [field](field.md) $F$. Thus [linear independence](linear-independence.md) excludes every nontrivial [linear relation](linear-relation.md). For an infinite family, require this condition for every finite subfamily.[MIT 18.06SC, Strang lecture summary 9, p. 1: linear independence](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06sc-linear-algebra-fall-2011/0bbc30e3f1d7933ea07a2d2e9ab050d9_MIT18_06SCF11_Ses1.9sum.pdf)

For $v_1=(1,1)$ and $v_2=(1,-1)$ over the [real numbers](real-number.md), the [linear relation](linear-relation.md) $av_1+bv_2=0$ gives $a+b=0$ and $a-b=0$. Adding and subtracting yields $2a=2b=0$, hence $a=b=0$. This verifies [linear independence](linear-independence.md) directly and explains why the [linear combination](linear-combination.md) giving $(3,1)$ has exactly the [coefficients](coefficient.md) $(2,1)$.

A [linearly independent](linear-independence.md) family cannot contain the [zero vector](zero-vector.md): a [coefficient](coefficient.md) of $1$ on that entry and $0$ on every other entry would be a nontrivial [linear relation](linear-relation.md). The empty family is [linearly independent](linear-independence.md), because its empty sum is the [zero vector](zero-vector.md) and there are no [coefficients](coefficient.md) that could be nonzero. [Linear independence](linear-independence.md) identifies a collection without redundant [vectors](vector.md); combined with a [spanning set](spanning-set.md), it gives a [basis](basis.md). The [uniqueness of linear combination coefficients](uniqueness-of-linear-combination-coefficients.md) makes its representations unambiguous.
