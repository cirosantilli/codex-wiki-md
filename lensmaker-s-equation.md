<h1 id="lensmaker-s-equation">Lensmaker's equation</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Thin lens](thin-lens.md)

The [lensmaker's equation](lensmaker-s-equation.md) connects [focal length](focal-length.md) to the [refractive index](refractive-index.md) and shape of a [thin lens](thin-lens.md). For a [thin lens](thin-lens.md) in air with surfaces shaped as parts of [spheres](sphere.md), [refractive index](refractive-index.md) $n$, and incident [light](light.md) travelling to the right,

$$
\frac1f=(n-1)\left(\frac1{R_a}-\frac1{R_b}\right).
$$

Each signed [radius of curvature](radius-of-curvature.md) is positive when its centre of [curvature](curvature.md) lies to the right of that surface, and negative when it lies to the left. A symmetric biconvex [thin lens](thin-lens.md) with $n=1.5$, $R_a=1\,\mathrm m$, and $R_b=-1\,\mathrm m$ therefore has $1/f=0.5(1+1)\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}=1\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, giving $f=1\,\mathrm m$ and [optical power](optical-power.md) $+1\,\mathrm D$. This is an ideal shape example consistent with the [thin lens](thin-lens.md) diagrams, not a specification of their finite thickness.

In a uniform surrounding medium of [refractive index](refractive-index.md) $n_s$ on both sides, replace $n-1$ by $n/n_s-1$. Thus the same shape reverses its focusing sign if its [refractive index](refractive-index.md) is lower than its surroundings: for $n=1.5$ and $n_s=2$, the same radii give $1/f=-0.5\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, or $f=-2\,\mathrm m$. Positive [focal length](focal-length.md) therefore cannot be inferred from convex shape in every environment. A proof outline applies [Snell's law](snell-s-law.md) with the [small-angle approximation](small-angle-approximation.md) at each surface, adds their direction changes, and neglects the distance between them. [Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics I, §27-3, equations 27.10–27.12](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_27.html).
