# Terminal velocity

↑ **Parent:** [Velocity](velocity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_velocity)

[Terminal velocity](terminal-velocity.md) is a constant limiting [velocity](velocity.md) approached when a [velocity](velocity.md)-dependent resistance balances the other applied [forces](force.md). A falling body may keep moving while its net [force](force.md) and [acceleration](acceleration.md) tend to zero. For downward motion in a stationary medium, its limiting [speed](speed.md) is set by balancing [gravitational weight](weight-physics.md) against [drag force](drag-physics.md), including [buoyancy](buoyancy.md) if appreciable. The balance, rather than a universal numerical value, is the defining idea. [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, section 6.4: drag and terminal speed](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/6-4-drag-force-and-terminal-speed)

The [terminal velocity under linear drag](terminal-velocity-under-linear-drag.md) gives a simple example with an explicit approach to the limit. Real falling objects need an appropriate resistance model: [linear drag](linear-drag.md) is useful for slow motion of a small sphere in [Stokes flow](stokes-flow-split.md), whereas many faster falling objects require [quadratic drag](quadratic-drag.md). Changing [mass](mass.md) also changes the question: the existing [accreting raindrop with quadratic drag](accreting-raindrop-with-quadratic-drag.md) has a [speed-radius attractor of an accreting raindrop](speed-radius-attractor-of-an-accreting-raindrop.md), rather than the fixed-[mass](mass.md) balance used below.
