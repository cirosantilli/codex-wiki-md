# Manifold criterion for a suspension

↑ **Parent:** [Suspension (topology)](suspension-topology.md)

For a nonempty [compact](compact-space.md) [topological manifold](topological-manifold.md) $X^d$ without boundary, its [suspension](suspension-topology.md) is a topological [manifold](topological-manifold.md) without boundary exactly when $X$ is homeomorphic to $S^d$. The forward implication is a [local homology from a link](local-homology-from-a-link.md) calculation at a suspension vertex: $\widetilde H_j(X)$ is $\mathbb Z$ in degree $d$ and zero elsewhere. For $d\geq2$, put a coordinate ball between two nested cone neighbourhoods. The inclusion of their punctures is a [homotopy equivalence](homotopy-equivalence.md) but factors through the [simply connected](simply-connected-space.md) punctured ball, forcing $\pi_1(X)=0$. Thus $X$ is a [homotopy sphere](homotopy-sphere.md), and the [topological generalized Poincare theorem](topological-generalized-poincare-theorem.md) gives the result. The dimensions zero and one follow from the finite-set and [circle](circle.md) classifications. Conversely $S(S^d)\cong S^{d+1}$. If manifolds with boundary are allowed, the interval and its disk suspension show why this formulation needs the boundary restriction.
