# Ellipsoid rotational diffusion microscopy experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Rotational diffusion](rotational-diffusion.md)

In 2006, [Yilong Han](yilong-han.md) and colleagues dispersed plastic [ellipsoids](ellipsoid.md) in [water](water.md) inside a thin glass cell, using a very dilute suspension to limit particle interactions. A camera on an [optical microscope](optical-microscope.md) recorded 30 frames per second. Image analysis extracted centre positions and long-axis [angles](angle.md), with reported angular resolution about $1^\circ$. Their shape supplied the observable axis. Reflected interference colours calibrated cell thickness; nearly constant particle interference colour checked vertical wandering. One tracked particle had dimensions $2.4\times0.3\times0.3\,\mu\mathrm m$ in an $846\,\mathrm{nm}$ gap. Angular displacement [variance](variance-split.md) grew linearly with lag; displacement resolved along the particle's axes and in the laboratory showed how turning washes out translational [anisotropy](anisotropy.md). These are observations of confined particles: walls alter drag, and slight out-of-plane tumbling affected positional resolution. They do not establish the bulk [sphere](sphere.md) formula. The paper's Figure 1A–B shows simulations; Figure 1F shows a measured trajectory.[Han et al., Brownian Motion of an Ellipsoid, Science 314 (2006), pp. 626–628; Figures 1D–F and 2A–B: apparatus, angular readout, dilution and height checks, angular variance and translational crossover](brownian-motion-of-an-ellipsoid-by-yilong-han-and-others.md)

For interpreting such images, the [director](director-liquid-crystals.md) distinction is essential: identical ends give an [angle](angle.md) modulo $\pi$. A continuously unwrapped trace can follow small successive turns, but it does not make the ends physically distinguishable. The invariant planar axis [autocorrelation](autocorrelation.md) is $\langle\cos(2\Delta\phi)\rangle$, and its decay determines $D_r$ through $e^{-4D_rt}$. Separately measuring position and [rigid body orientation](rigid-body-orientation.md) is what makes the connection between translational [anisotropy](anisotropy.md) and loss of angular memory experimentally accessible.
