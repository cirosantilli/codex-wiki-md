# Zero-based geometric sum of exponential variables

↑ **Parent:** [Compound geometric distribution](compound-geometric-distribution.md)

If independent summands have [exponential distribution](exponential-distribution.md) of mean $\mu$ and the independent count has a zero-based [geometric distribution](geometric-distribution.md) of parameter $p$, their [random sum of independent claims](random-sum-of-independent-claims.md) has an atom $p$ at zero and an exponential positive component of rate $p/\mu$. Its [Laplace transform of a nonnegative random variable](laplace-transform-of-a-nonnegative-random-variable.md) is $p/[1-(1-p)/(1+\mu s)]=p+(1-p)p/(p+\mu s)$. Thus $\mathbb P(S>x)=(1-p)e^{-px/\mu}$ for $x>0$. Unlike the [geometric sum of exponential variables](geometric-sum-of-exponential-variables.md) with a positive count, this law is not purely exponential.
