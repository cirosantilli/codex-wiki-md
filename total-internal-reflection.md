# Total internal reflection

↑ **Parent:** [Snell law for electromagnetic waves](snell-law-for-electromagnetic-waves.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_internal_reflection)

[Total internal reflection](total-internal-reflection.md) occurs when a [wave](wave-split.md) travels from higher to lower [refractive index](refractive-index.md) above the [critical angle for total internal reflection](critical-angle-for-total-internal-reflection.md), leaving no propagating transmitted [plane electromagnetic wave](plane-electromagnetic-wave.md) normal to the interface.

At an infinite planar interface, [total internal reflection](total-internal-reflection.md) returns all incident normal [power](power.md) in the reflected [plane electromagnetic wave](plane-electromagnetic-wave.md). Assume homogeneous [dielectrics](dielectric.md) with [isotropy](isotropy.md), [linearity](linearity.md), no [dielectric loss](dielectric-loss.md), [magnetic permeability](permeability-electromagnetism.md) $\mu_0$, and positive real [refractive indices](refractive-index.md) $n_1>n_2$. Above the [critical angle for total internal reflection](critical-angle-for-total-internal-reflection.md), both [TE polarization](transverse-electric-polarization.md) and [TM polarization](transverse-magnetic-polarization.md) have unit [optical reflectance](reflectance.md) despite the external [evanescent wave](evanescent-wave.md).[Basic Electromagnetic and Wave Optics, Total Internal Reflection and Evanescent Waves, equations (153)–(158)](interactive-optics-by-sander-konijnenberg-aurele-j-l-adam-and-h-paul-urbach.md)

This makes [total internal reflection](total-internal-reflection.md) useful for guiding [light](light.md) in an [optical fiber](optical-fiber.md): suitable internal incidence [angles](angle.md) keep [energy](energy-split.md) inside successive boundaries. Its more surprising feature is that perfect [optical reflectance](reflectance.md) coexists with an external [electric field](electric-field-split.md). The [evanescent field in total internal reflection](evanescent-field-in-total-internal-reflection.md) can interact with a nearby object, as the [frustrated total internal reflection](frustrated-total-internal-reflection.md) experiment demonstrates.

For an original idealized example, choose $n_1=1.5$, $n_2=1$ at [vacuum](vacuum.md) [wavelength](wavelength.md) $\lambda_0=633\,\mathrm{nm}$. At [angle of incidence](angle-of-incidence.md) $40^\circ$, [Snell law for electromagnetic waves](snell-law-for-electromagnetic-waves.md) gives an [angle of refraction](angle-of-refraction.md) $\arcsin(1.5\sin40^\circ)\approx74.6186^\circ$, so a transmitted [light ray](light-ray.md) still emerges. At $\theta_c\approx41.8103^\circ$, its direction becomes tangent to the boundary. At $50^\circ$, the proposed [sine](sine.md) of a real [angle of refraction](angle-of-refraction.md) would be $1.5\sin50^\circ\approx1.14907>1$: the transmitted [wavevector](wavevector.md) must instead have an imaginary normal component. These are predictions for the stipulated media, not measured properties of a particular block.

A real semicircular-block experiment isolates this threshold. The University of Maryland laboratory instructions place a [laser](laser-split.md), motor-driven rotation stage and [photodetector](photodetector.md) together; an [aperture](aperture.md) limits the detector voltage. The semicircular block is centered on the rotation axis, with the [photodetector](photodetector.md) aligned along its flat face. The instructions identify a strong grazing-beam signal and a weaker signal just before the [critical angle for total internal reflection](critical-angle-for-total-internal-reflection.md). The readout is detector voltage versus stage [angle](angle.md). Retracing the incident [laser](laser-split.md) beam identifies [normal incidence](normal-incidence.md) for alignment.[pp. 1–2, alignment procedure and Total Internal Reflection apparatus and detector instructions](snell%E2%80%99s-law-and-total-internal-reflection-laboratory-instructions-university-of-maryland-department-of-physics.md)

The semicircular shape supplies a geometric control: a [light ray](light-ray.md) passing radially through the curved face enters at [normal incidence](normal-incidence.md), avoiding additional entrance [refraction](refraction.md). The weaker signal near the flat face locates a limiting [angle of refraction](angle-of-refraction.md); a vanishing remote-detector signal alone would not establish that the external [electric field](electric-field-split.md) vanishes. Beam width and alignment limit angular resolution; no measured threshold or decay depth is supplied.[p. 1, beam-position uncertainty prompt; p. 2, semicircular-block geometry and near-critical signal](snell%E2%80%99s-law-and-total-internal-reflection-laboratory-instructions-university-of-maryland-department-of-physics.md)
