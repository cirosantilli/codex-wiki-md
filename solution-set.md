# Solution set

↑ **Parent:** [Set](set-split.md)

The [solution set](solution-set.md) of a mathematical condition $P$ in a specified [domain](domain-mathematical-analysis.md) $D$ collects every allowed value that satisfies it:

$$
S=\{x\in D:P(x)\}.
$$

It describes the answer without committing to the steps used to find it. For the [equation](equation.md) $x^2=1$ over the [real numbers](real-number.md), the [solution set](solution-set.md) is $\{-1,1\}$; restricting to positive [real numbers](real-number.md) leaves $\{1\}$. For $2x=1$, the [solution set](solution-set.md) is $\{1/2\}$ over the [rational numbers](rational-number.md) and the [empty set](empty-set.md) over the [integers](integer-split.md). The domain is part of the question.

An [equation](equation.md) can have one solution, several solutions, no solutions, or an entire domain of solutions. Over the [real numbers](real-number.md), $2x+3=9$ gives the [singleton set](singleton-mathematics.md) $\{3\}$, $x^2+1=0$ gives the [empty set](empty-set.md), and the [mathematical identity](identity-mathematics.md) $(x+1)^2=x^2+2x+1$ gives the whole [real line](real-line.md). Changing that second equation's domain to the [complex numbers](complex-number.md) gives $\{-i,i\}$. The elementary definition by values that satisfy an [equation](equation.md) and the classification by [solution sets](solution-set.md) appear in [College Algebra 2e, §2.2: Solving Linear Equations in One Variable](https://openstax.org/books/college-algebra-2e/pages/2-2-linear-equations-in-one-variable).

**[Figure 1](#image-three-equivalent-equations-have-the-same-singleton-set-of-solutions-3-even-though-their-displayed-function-values-differ). Three equivalent equations have the same singleton set of solutions $\{3\}$, even though their displayed function values differ**.

Requiring several conditions simultaneously takes the [intersection](set-intersection.md) of their [solution sets](solution-set.md). The squared [equation](equation.md) $x=(x-2)^2$ has [solution set](solution-set.md) $\{1,4\}$. Adding the restriction $x\geq2$ gives

$$
\{1,4\}\cap[2,\infty)=\{4\},
$$

which is also the [solution set](solution-set.md) of $\sqrt{x}=x-2$. The [extraneous solution](extraneous-solution.md) example explains why the sign restriction is needed. Requiring at least one of several conditions instead takes their [union](set-union.md). This turns logical combinations of constraints into reusable operations on [sets](set-split.md).

For several [variables](variable-mathematics.md), a solution is an ordered assignment, usually represented by a [tuple](finite-tuple.md). The real [equation](equation.md) $x+y=1$ has [solution set](solution-set.md) $\{(t,1-t):t\in\mathbb R\}$, a [straight line](straight-line.md) in the [Euclidean plane](euclidean-plane.md) with coordinates $(x,y)$. Adding $x-y=0$ intersects that [straight line](straight-line.md) with another, leaving the single [tuple](finite-tuple.md) $(1/2,1/2)$. A [system of linear inequalities](system-of-linear-inequalities.md) has no solution exactly when the [intersection](set-intersection.md) of its individual [solution sets](solution-set.md) is the [empty set](empty-set.md). For [optimization constraints](optimization-constraint.md), this common [solution set](solution-set.md) is the [feasible region](feasible-region.md).

Because a [solution set](solution-set.md) is a [set](set-split.md), the order in which its members are listed is irrelevant. The [multiple root](multiple-root.md) of $(x-1)^2=0$ is still the one solution $1$; its [multiplicity of a root](multiplicity-of-a-root.md) is additional polynomial information, rather than a second occurrence inside the [solution set](solution-set.md). [Equivalent equations](equivalent-equations.md) preserve the [solution set](solution-set.md) on the stated domain, even if their algebraic forms differ.
