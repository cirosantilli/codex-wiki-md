# Adenosine diphosphate

↑ **Parent:** [Nucleotide](nucleotide.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenosine_diphosphate)

[ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) contains the same [adenosine](adenosine.md) unit as [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md), with two [phosphate](phosphate.md) groups instead of three. One [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) converted by the stated [hydrolysis](hydrolysis.md) gives one [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) and one [inorganic phosphate](inorganic-phosphate.md): $3=2+1$ accounts for the [phosphate](phosphate.md) groups while [adenine](adenine.md) and [ribose](ribose.md) are retained. [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) is therefore a reusable material carrier, rather than [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) with its entire structure discarded.

Regeneration reverses the net [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md):

$$
\mathrm{ADP}+\mathrm P_i\longrightarrow\mathrm{ATP}+\mathrm{H_2O}.
$$

At the same stipulated reference conditions its $\Delta G^{\circ\prime}$ is $+30.5\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, the negative of the [hydrolysis](hydrolysis.md) value. It is an [endergonic reaction](endergonic-reaction.md) and requires [energy](energy-split.md) input through [energy coupling](energy-coupling.md); a [catalyst](catalyst.md) alone cannot supply that input. Recycling the material carrier does not recycle the expended driving [energy](energy-split.md). [Cellular respiration](cellular-respiration.md) and [light-dependent reactions](light-dependent-reactions.md) supply routes for regeneration. See [Cooper, The Cell: ATP generation and energy coupling](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9903/).

[ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) is a [nucleotide](nucleotide.md) with two [phosphate](phosphate.md) groups. Formation of [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) from [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) and [phosphate](phosphate.md) couples to [energy](energy-split.md) supply in [light-dependent reactions](light-dependent-reactions.md); the [Calvin cycle](calvin-cycle.md) returns [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) after using [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md). [OpenStax Biology 2e, 8.3: recycling ATP and ADP](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/8-3-using-light-energy-to-make-organic-molecules).
