# Astrophysical disk

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysics](astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysical_disk)

An astrophysical disk is a flattened rotating distribution of gas, dust, stars, or other matter whose orbital motion strongly influences its structure and evolution.

## Axisymmetric waves of a homogeneous incompressible disk

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)

In a thin constant-density disk with [free surfaces](fluid-mechanics.md#free-surface) $z=\pm H$, neglect slow radial gradients and let [perturbations](asymptotic-expansion.md#perturbation) vary as $e^{ikr-i\omega t}$. With $h=p'/\rho$, the local inviscid equations are $-i\omega u-2\Omega v=-ikh$, $-i\omega v+\kappa^2u/(2\Omega)=0$, $-i\omega w=-h_z$ and $iku+w_z=0$. Eliminating [velocity](classical-mechanics.md#velocity) gives $h_{zz}=k^2\omega^2h/(\omega^2-\kappa^2)$. This local [WKB approximation](analysis.md#wkb-approximation) assumes $H/r\ll1$ and $|kr|\gg1$ and has both surface and [inertial wave](geophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#inertial-wave) branches.

// Target: astrophysics.bigb

### Energy identity for free-surface disk waves

↑ **Parent:** [Axisymmetric waves of a homogeneous incompressible disk](#axisymmetric-waves-of-a-homogeneous-incompressible-disk)

For radial and vertical [Lagrangian fluid displacements](elasticity-physics.md#lagrangian-fluid-displacement) of a local axisymmetric mode, integrate the momentum equations and use [incompressible flow](fluid-mechanics.md#incompressible-flow) plus the [free-surface pressure condition of an incompressible disk](#free-surface-pressure-condition-of-an-incompressible-disk). The result is

$$
\omega^2\int_{-H}^H(|\xi_r|^2+|\xi_z|^2)dz
=\kappa^2\int_{-H}^H|\xi_r|^2dz+\Omega_z^2H\bigl(|\xi_z(H)|^2+|\xi_z(-H)|^2\bigr).
$$

For nonnegative $\kappa^2$ and positive $\Omega_z^2$, every nontrivial oscillatory mode has real nonnegative $\omega^2$. The surface term is the gravitational restoring energy of the displaced vacuum interfaces.

### Inertial modes of an incompressible disk

↑ **Parent:** [Axisymmetric waves of a homogeneous incompressible disk](#axisymmetric-waves-of-a-homogeneous-incompressible-disk)

For $0<\omega^2<\kappa^2$, set $S=kH\omega/\sqrt{\kappa^2-\omega^2}$. Even-pressure modes satisfy $\omega^2=-\Omega_z^2S\tan S$, while odd-pressure modes satisfy $\omega^2=\Omega_z^2S\cot S$. In the case $\Omega_z>\kappa>0$, the branches approach $\kappa$ as $kH\to0$. For each fixed vertical order $n\geq1$ and large $kH$, $S\to n\pi/2$ and $\omega\sim\kappa n\pi/(2kH)$. Even orders have even [pressure](pressure.md) and odd orders odd [pressure](pressure.md). The limiting [boundary condition](differential-equation.md#boundary-condition) is zero normal derivative of [pressure](pressure.md) at both interfaces.

// Target: astrophysics.bigb

### Surface modes of an incompressible disk

↑ **Parent:** [Axisymmetric waves of a homogeneous incompressible disk](#axisymmetric-waves-of-a-homogeneous-incompressible-disk)

For $\omega^2>\kappa^2$, set $Q=kH\omega/\sqrt{\omega^2-\kappa^2}$. Even-pressure and odd-pressure modes have $p'\propto\cosh(Qz/H)$ and $\sinh(Qz/H)$ and obey $\omega^2=\Omega_z^2Q\tanh Q$ and $\omega^2=\Omega_z^2Q\coth Q$, respectively. At large $kH$, both become free-surface gravity waves with

$$
\omega^2\sim\tfrac12\left[\kappa^2+\sqrt{\kappa^4+4\Omega_z^4(kH)^2}\right]=\Omega_z^2kH+\tfrac12\kappa^2+O((kH)^{-1}).
$$

Their opposite-parity splitting is exponentially small as the two interfaces decouple.

// Target: astrophysics.bigb

### Free-surface pressure condition of an incompressible disk

↑ **Parent:** [Axisymmetric waves of a homogeneous incompressible disk](#axisymmetric-waves-of-a-homogeneous-incompressible-disk)

Vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) gives $p_0=\rho\Omega_z^2(H^2-z^2)/2$. At a vacuum interface, the [Lagrangian pressure perturbation](fluid-mechanics.md#lagrangian-pressure-perturbation) vanishes: $p'+\xi_z\partial_zp_0=0$. The vertical equation gives $\xi_z=p'_z/(\rho\omega^2)$, hence

$$
p'-\frac{\Omega_z^2}{\omega^2}zp'_z=0\quad\text{at }z=\pm H.
$$

The moving interface makes this different from setting the [Eulerian perturbation of a fluid variable](fluid-mechanics.md#eulerian-perturbation-of-a-fluid-variable) $p'$ to zero on the original surface.

## Kuzmin disk

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)

A Kuzmin disk is an infinitesimally thin axisymmetric [astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md) whose [relative potential](galaxy.md#relative-potential) is $\psi=Gm/\sqrt{R^2+(|z|+b)^2}$, with $m,b>0$. Away from the disk this is a point-mass vacuum field. The jump of its vertical derivative is $-2Gmb/(R^2+b^2)^{3/2}$, so the [Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#poisson-equation-for-newtonian-gravity), written $\nabla^2\psi=-4\pi G\rho$, gives the displayed [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk). Integrating $2\pi R\Sigma$ gives total [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) $m$.

### Kuzmin reflection method

↑ **Parent:** [Kuzmin disk](#kuzmin-disk)

Reflect a vacuum [gravitational potential](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass) in the upper half-space evenly across $z=0$. For the convention in which [gravitational acceleration](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#gravitational-acceleration) is $\nabla\psi$, the derivative jump is $2\partial_z\psi(R,0^+)=-4\pi G\Sigma(R)$. Thus a point mass $m$ below the plane produces a [Kuzmin disk](#kuzmin-disk), and superposing point masses with weight $\mu(b)\,db$ produces $\Sigma(R)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^\infty b\mu(b)(R^2+b^2)^{-3/2}\,db$. Positive weights give a nonnegative [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) wherever the integral converges.

#### Scale-free Kuzmin superposition

↑ **Parent:** [Kuzmin reflection method](#kuzmin-reflection-method)

For $B>0$ and $0<\beta<1$, the unreferenced [relative potential](galaxy.md#relative-potential) of the [Kuzmin reflection method](#kuzmin-reflection-method) diverges at large $b$. Referencing each component to the origin before integrating gives the finite potential difference

$$
\psi(R,z)=GB\int_0^\infty b^\beta\left[\frac1{\sqrt{R^2+(|z|+b)^2}}-\frac1b\right]db.
$$

At small $b$ the subtraction is integrable because $\beta>0$; at large $b$ the difference is $O(b^{-2})$, integrable because $\beta<1$. Its plane force is $\partial_R\psi=-CR^{\beta-1}$, where $C=(GB/2)B((1+\beta)/2,1-\beta/2)$. A [circular speed](galaxy.md#circular-speed) $V=KR^{\beta/2}$ fixes $C=K^2$, giving

$$
\Sigma(R)=\frac{K^2R^{\beta-1}}{2\pi G}\frac{\Gamma(1+\beta/2)\Gamma((1-\beta)/2)}{\Gamma((1+\beta)/2)\Gamma(1-\beta/2)}.
$$

The [beta function](complex-analysis.md#beta-function) substitutions for the force and [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) have different exponents. Its central mass is locally finite, but its total mass diverges at large radius; a potential zero at infinity cannot be imposed. The force and density approach those of the [Mestel disk](#mestel-disk) as $\beta\downarrow0$.

## Disk warp

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)

A disk warp is a radial change of the orbital plane of successive disk annuli. In the small-tilt approximation, a complex tilt $W$ records its magnitude and azimuth. Pressure and internal torques communicate the tilt through [bending waves of a Keplerian disk](#bending-wave-of-a-keplerian-disk) in the inviscid thin-disk regime. A flat tilted disk has constant $W$ even though its absolute orientation differs from the reference equatorial plane.

### Inviscid warp equations of a nearly Keplerian disk

↑ **Parent:** [Disk warp](#disk-warp)

Here $G$ is the complex internal horizontal torque, $P$ the vertically integrated pressure, and $\omega_1,\omega_2$ the approximate [nodal precession](planetary-science.md#nodal-precession) and [apsidal precession](planetary-science.md#apsidal-precession) rates. In the exactly Keplerian case, local plane waves obey $\omega^2=k^2P/(4\Sigma)=k^2c_s^2/4$, so tilt disturbances propagate at $c_s/2$. The secular thin-disk approximation requires wavelengths large relative to the height while the local approximation requires them small relative to the orbital radius.

#### Stationary bending warp of a Kerr disk

↑ **Parent:** [Inviscid warp equations of a nearly Keplerian disk](#inviscid-warp-equations-of-a-nearly-keplerian-disk)

For $a>0$, $\Sigma\propto R^{-3/4}$, constant [disk aspect ratio](#disk-aspect-ratio) $\epsilon=H/R$, and $r=Rc^2/(GM)$, the inviscid steady [disk warp](#disk-warp) equation reduces to $W_{xx}+W=0$ in the displayed coordinate. The vanishing inner torque gives $W_x(x_{\mathrm{in}})=0$; the outer tilt gives $W(0)=W_\infty$. The displayed solution holds when $\cos x_{\mathrm{in}}\ne0$. If $x_{\mathrm{in}}=\pi$, it is $W=W_\infty\cos x$, with an aligned node at $r=2^{4/5}r_{\mathrm{in}}$ and opposite signed inner tilt. For negative spin the corresponding equation is hyperbolic, giving $W=W_\infty\cosh(x_{\mathrm{in}}-x)/\cosh x_{\mathrm{in}}$ with $x=4\sqrt{24|a|}\,r^{-5/4}/(5\epsilon)$.

## Hankel representation of thin-disk gravity

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)

For an axisymmetric reflection-symmetric [thin disk](#thin-disk), the [Laplace equation in cylindrical coordinates](partial-differential-equation.md#laplace-equation-in-cylindrical-coordinates) separates into regular radial [Bessel functions](bessel-function.md) and vertical decaying exponentials. The [divergence theorem](multivariable-calculus.md#divergence-theorem) imposes $\Phi_z^+-\Phi_z^-=4\pi G\Sigma$. [Hankel transform](fourier-transform.md#hankel-transform) inversion gives $S(k)=-2\pi G\int_0^\infty\Sigma(R)J_0(kR)R\,dR$. Its radial derivative gives the [galaxy rotation curve](galaxy.md#galaxy-rotation-curve). Infinite scale-free models may require subtracting an additive divergent constant.

## Angular-momentum-conserving self-gravitating disc radius

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)

Let a disc contain fraction $f_d$ of total halo mass, take its edge gravity to be $v_d^2=C_dGM_d/R_d$, and equate its edge specific angular momentum to $qR_hv_{\rm vir}$. With $v_{\rm vir}^2=GM_h/R_h$, these conditions give $R_h/R_d=C_df_d/q^2$. The structural coefficient $C_d$ is essential: disc mass alone does not uniquely determine its edge rotation curve.

## Protoplanetary disk

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)

A protoplanetary disk is a gas-and-solid [astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md) around a young star in which [planet formation](planetary-science.md#planet-formation) occurs. Its evolution combines [accretion](astrophysics.md#accretion), radiation, gas dynamics and the growth of solid material. A [minimum-mass solar nebula](planetary-science.md#minimum-mass-solar-nebula) is one reconstruction of the material needed to form the Solar System.

### Protoplanetary disk dispersal

↑ **Parent:** [Protoplanetary disk](#protoplanetary-disk)

Removal of disk gas by [accretion](astrophysics.md#accretion) onto the host [star](stellar-astrophysics.md#star), winds and [photoevaporation](astrophysics.md#photoevaporation). The finite availability of gas limits which solid cores can acquire massive envelopes. The disk's evolutionary state matters more than a single universal dispersal time.

### Snow line

↑ **Parent:** [Protoplanetary disk](#protoplanetary-disk)

A transition beyond which a specified volatile can predominantly condense into solid grains under local [temperature](temperature.md) and [pressure](pressure.md). Without qualification it often means the water-ice transition. Its location evolves with disk heating, cooling and composition; it is not a universal fixed orbital radius.

## Mestel disk

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestel_disk)

A Mestel disk is a thin axisymmetric [astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md) with [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) inversely proportional to radius. Its [galaxy rotation curve](galaxy.md#galaxy-rotation-curve) is exactly flat, $v_c=v_M$, and $v_c^2=GM(R)/R$ despite its nonspherical geometry. In the [Legendre expansion of thin-disk gravity](#legendre-expansion-of-thin-disk-gravity), all positive-degree interior and exterior terms cancel. Its total mass is infinite, so only potential differences, rather than a potential zero at infinity, are defined.

### Rotating Mestel disk distribution function

↑ **Parent:** [Mestel disk](#mestel-disk)

In the midplane [Mestel disk](#mestel-disk) potential $\Phi=v_c^2\log(R/R_0)$, take specific [energy](energy.md) $E=(v_R^2+v_\phi^2)/2+\Phi$ and $L_z=Rv_\phi$. A prograde [galactic distribution function](galaxy.md#galactic-distribution-function) $f=A L_z^qe^{-E/s}$ for $L_z>0$, with $s>0$, has [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) proportional to $R^{q-v_c^2/s}$. Matching $\Sigma=\Sigma_0R_0/R$ gives $q=v_c^2/s-1>-1$ and

$$
A=\frac{\Sigma_0R_0^{-q}}{\sqrt\pi\,2^{q/2}s^{(q+2)/2}\Gamma((q+1)/2)}.
$$

The denominator is the product of the full radial Gaussian integral and the positive-azimuthal half-line moment. Here $s$ is a squared-velocity scale, not a [velocity dispersion](galaxy.md#velocity-dispersion) itself. Changing the potential's additive constant changes $A$. With positive exponent $e^{\varepsilon/s}$, $\varepsilon$ must instead be the negative of the specific total energy.

### Velocity moments of an isotropic Mestel disk

↑ **Parent:** [Mestel disk](#mestel-disk)

The planar [Mestel disk](#mestel-disk) has $F(E)\propto e^{-E/v_0^2}$, giving independent centered [Gaussian distributions](probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution) for $v_R,v_\phi$ with [velocity dispersions](galaxy.md#velocity-dispersion) $\sigma_R=\sigma_\phi=v_0$. Its [streaming velocity](galaxy.md#streaming-velocity) is zero. A [maximally prograde stellar distribution](galaxy.md#maximally-prograde-stellar-distribution) instead has mean $\sqrt{2/\pi}v_0$ and azimuthal [variance](variance.md) $v_0^2(1-2/\pi)$, while all second raw moments remain unchanged.

### Mestel disk potential-density pair

↑ **Parent:** [Mestel disk](#mestel-disk)

The reflection-symmetric [Newtonian gravitational potential](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#newtonian-gravitational-potential) $\Phi=v_0^2\log[(\sqrt{R^2+z^2}+|z|)/R_*]$ is harmonic off the plane. Its normal-derivative jump is $2v_0^2/R$, so the [Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#poisson-equation-for-newtonian-gravity) gives [surface density of a disk](#surface-density-of-a-disk) $S=v_0^2/(2\pi GR)$ and volume [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) $\rho=S\delta(z)$. The midplane [circular speed](galaxy.md#circular-speed) is constant. The scale-free model has infinite total [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) and does not use a finite-mass boundary condition at infinity.

#### Boundary ambiguity of a flat disk rotation curve

↑ **Parent:** [Mestel disk potential-density pair](#mestel-disk-potential-density-pair)

The midplane [galaxy rotation curve](galaxy.md#galaxy-rotation-curve) fixes the radial derivative of the [Newtonian gravitational potential](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#newtonian-gravitational-potential), not every exterior boundary condition. Adding $a|z|$ leaves that radial derivative unchanged but adds a uniform sheet of [surface density of a disk](#surface-density-of-a-disk) $a/(2\pi G)$. The pure scale-free [Mestel disk](#mestel-disk) excludes such extra sources.

## Legendre expansion of thin-disk gravity

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)

For a thin axisymmetric [astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md), angular integration of the Newtonian kernel eliminates odd [Legendre polynomials](linear-ordinary-differential-equation.md#legendre-polynomial). Define $I_n=\int_0^R\Sigma(s)s^{n+1}ds$ and $J_n=\int_R^\infty\Sigma(s)s^{-n}ds$. The even coefficient is $\alpha_n=\pi[n!/(2^n((n/2)!)^2)]^2$. In differentiating the paired inner and outer contributions, their moving-boundary terms cancel. The resulting [galaxy rotation curve](galaxy.md#galaxy-rotation-curve) includes an inward inner-ring contribution and an outward exterior-ring contribution. For smooth [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk), the force at the disk is interpreted with a symmetric [Cauchy principal value](complex-analysis.md#cauchy-principal-value) or a vanishing-thickness regularization.

## Radiation-supported vertical disk

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)

A [radiation-supported vertical disk](#radiation-supported-vertical-disk) has vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) supplied mainly by [radiation pressure](pressure.md#radiation-pressure). In a constant-[opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity), optically thick layer in gravity $-\Omega^2z$, [radiative diffusion](astrophysics.md#radiative-diffusion) gives $F=c\Omega^2z/\kappa$. This statement applies at a fixed radius and does not resolve the optically thin surface.

### Constant viscosity in radiation-supported vertical balance

↑ **Parent:** [Radiation-supported vertical disk](#radiation-supported-vertical-disk)

Combining $F=c\Omega^2z/\kappa$ with viscous heating $dF/dz=(9/4)\mu\Omega^2$ gives a height-independent [dynamic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) $\mu=4c/(9\kappa)$ under constant [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity). This exact limiting relation differs from an approximate vertically averaged [alpha disk](#alpha-disk) closure.

## Keplerian disk

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)

A Keplerian disk rotates with angular frequency $\Omega_K=\sqrt{GM/r^3}$ set mainly by a central mass, so $\Omega_K\propto r^{-3/2}$.

### Corotation radius

↑ **Parent:** [Keplerian disk](#keplerian-disk)

The radius where [Keplerian rotation](#keplerian-disk) matches the spin of the central star.

## Thin disk

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thin_disk)

A thin disk has vertical semi-thickness $H$ much smaller than cylindrical radius $r$. Vertical gravity is then approximately linear in height and orbital dynamics is nearly planar.

### Passive disk

↑ **Parent:** [Thin disk](#thin-disk)

A passive [astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md) receives most of its heat from external irradiation rather than internal [viscous dissipation](stokes-flow.md#viscous-dissipation). For a stellar disk, a rough criterion is $GM\dot M/R_*\ll L_*$. The actual local requirement compares absorbed stellar flux with viscous flux; incidence angle, shadowing and absorption can invalidate a luminosity comparison alone.

#### Irradiation grazing angle of a disk

↑ **Parent:** [Passive disk](#passive-disk)

For a thin illuminated surface $z=z_s(R)$ and a central point source, the radial light ray has slope $z_s/R$ whereas the surface tangent has slope $z_s'$. Their angle difference is the grazing angle. The incident flux is $L_*\alpha/(4\pi R^2)$ to leading order. This requires a positive grazing angle and an unobstructed ray; a negative formal flux instead signals failure of direct illumination, not negative heating.

### Mean projected area of a randomly oriented thin disc

↑ **Parent:** [Thin disk](#thin-disk)

A thin circular disc presents projected area $\pi R^2|\cos i|$ to a sightline. Isotropic normals have a uniform distribution of $\cos i$ on $[-1,1]$, giving mean absolute cosine one half. The resulting interception cross-section is therefore half the face-on area; no extra orientation weighting is needed after this average is used in an incidence calculation.

### Thin-disk expansion of the stellar gravitational potential

↑ **Parent:** [Thin disk](#thin-disk)

At fixed [cylindrical radius](multivariable-calculus.md#cylindrical-radius) $R$ with $|z|\ll R$, the central [gravitational potential](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass) is harmonic in height. The [vertical epicyclic frequency](#vertical-epicyclic-frequency) equals $\Omega_K$ in this spherical potential.

### Razor-thin disk approximation

↑ **Parent:** [Thin disk](#thin-disk)

The razor-thin disk approximation replaces a disk's vertical density profile by $\rho(x,y,z)=\Sigma(x,y)\delta(z)$. It is useful for horizontal dynamics on scales much longer than the disk thickness.

#### Sharp-edge force singularity of a thin disk

↑ **Parent:** [Razor-thin disk approximation](#razor-thin-disk-approximation)

At an abruptly truncated [razor-thin disk](#razor-thin-disk-approximation) with nonzero edge [surface density of a disk](#surface-density-of-a-disk), the midplane radial force diverges logarithmically. Locally, mass occupies a half-plane and the inward force contains a nonzero angular factor multiplying $G\Sigma_{\rm edge}\int_\epsilon^\ell ds/s$. The opposite exterior half-plane is absent, so its cancellation does not occur. Finite thickness or a taper taking the edge density to zero regularizes the force. Therefore a finite-edge [galaxy rotation curve](galaxy.md#galaxy-rotation-curve) requires more than the ideal scale-free [Mestel disk](#mestel-disk) normalization.

#### Barotropic closure of a razor-thin disk

↑ **Parent:** [Razor-thin disk approximation](#razor-thin-disk-approximation)

A two-dimensional disk equation with [pressure](pressure.md) acceleration $-\Sigma^{-1}\nabla P$ requires an equation of state for vertically integrated [pressure](pressure.md) $P$ in terms of [surface density of a disk](#surface-density-of-a-disk) $\Sigma$. A [barotropic fluid](fluid-mechanics.md#barotropic-fluid) closure is $P=P(\Sigma)$, with linear enthalpy perturbation $h'=c_s^2\Sigma'/\Sigma_0$. Merely giving $P=P(\rho)$ for a volume density does not close the two-dimensional equations unless a vertical conversion $\rho(\Sigma)$ is specified. Positive $c_s^2$ gives ordinary [pressure](pressure.md) restoration of an [inertial-acoustic wave](gravitational-instability-of-an-astrophysical-disk.md#inertial-acoustic-wave).

#### Razor-thin disk Poisson kernel

↑ **Parent:** [Razor-thin disk approximation](#razor-thin-disk-approximation)

For a horizontal Fourier mode of [wavenumber](wave-equation.md#wavenumber) $k>0$, the midplane gravitational potential of a razor-thin disk is

$$
\widehat\Phi_m(\mathbf k)=-\frac{2\pi G}{k}\widehat\Sigma(\mathbf k).
$$

##### Thin-disk potential-density inversion

↑ **Parent:** [Razor-thin disk Poisson kernel](#razor-thin-disk-poisson-kernel)

For an isolated [razor-thin disk](#razor-thin-disk-approximation), define the two-dimensional [Fourier transform](fourier-transform.md) by $\widetilde g(\mathbf k)=\int e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}g(\mathbf x)d^2x$. At $k>0$, the [razor-thin disk Poisson kernel](#razor-thin-disk-poisson-kernel) gives the displayed inversion. Since the transform of $1/|\mathbf x|$ is $2\pi/k$, inversion also gives $\Sigma=(4\pi^2G)^{-1}(1/|\mathbf x|)*\Delta_2\Phi$. The forward relation is $\Phi=-G(1/|\mathbf x|)*\Sigma$. An additive potential constant is irrelevant; a uniform infinite sheet and scale-free divergent potentials require separate zero-mode or potential-difference treatment rather than an arbitrary decaying boundary condition.

##### Off-plane razor-thin Poisson kernel

↑ **Parent:** [Razor-thin disk Poisson kernel](#razor-thin-disk-poisson-kernel)

For $k>0$, horizontal [Fourier transform](fourier-transform.md) gives $(\partial_z^2-k^2)\widetilde\Phi=4\pi G\widetilde\Sigma\delta(z)$. Decay and [continuity](calculus.md#continuous-function) give an exponential on each side; its derivative jump fixes the coefficient. The uniform mode needs a separate linear vertical solution and a potential reference.

##### Positive-kernel comparison of thin-disk fields

↑ **Parent:** [Razor-thin disk Poisson kernel](#razor-thin-disk-poisson-kernel)

The vertical field of a disk has kernel $Gz/(|\mathbf R-\mathbf R'|^2+z^2)^{3/2}>0$. The [triangle inequality](topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) therefore bounds a signed-[surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) field by the field of its absolute [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk). If that [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) is everywhere smaller than a nonnegative comparison [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk), their vertical fields satisfy the displayed strict comparison, provided the integrals exist and the strict bound holds on positive measure. This is useful when comparing [gravity-equivalent magnetic surface density](electromagnetism.md#gravity-equivalent-magnetic-surface-density) with actual disk mass.

### Surface density of a disk

↑ **Parent:** [Thin disk](#thin-disk)

The surface density of a disk is its volume density integrated vertically, $\Sigma=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\rho\,dz$.

### Vertical structure of an astrophysical disk

↑ **Parent:** [Thin disk](#thin-disk)

The vertical structure of a thin disk is set by the balance among vertical gravity, pressure, self-gravity, magnetic forces, and motions of gas and solids.

#### Vertical mass coordinate of a disk

↑ **Parent:** [Vertical structure of an astrophysical disk](#vertical-structure-of-an-astrophysical-disk)

In a reflection-symmetric [astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md), the vertical mass coordinate measures [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) per unit area above the midplane. It satisfies $dm=\rho\,dz$, so $\partial_m=\rho^{-1}\partial_z$ inside the material. If the total [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) is $\Sigma$, the upper face is $m=\Sigma/2$; the normalized coordinate $\zeta=2m/\Sigma$ ranges from zero to one. Integrals weighted by [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) become ordinary integrals in $m$, which is especially useful for [radiative diffusion](astrophysics.md#radiative-diffusion) and the [density-weighted mean kinematic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md#density-weighted-mean-kinematic-viscosity).

#### Isothermal vertical structure in stellar gravity

↑ **Parent:** [Vertical structure of an astrophysical disk](#vertical-structure-of-an-astrophysical-disk)

For a [thin disk](#thin-disk) in the harmonic vertical [gravitational potential](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass) $\Omega_K^2z^2/2$, [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) and $p=c_s^2\rho$ with height-independent [isothermal sound speed](compressible-flow.md#isothermal-sound-speed) give the displayed Gaussian density. For perturbations with [adiabatic index](ideal-gas.md#heat-capacity-ratio) $\gamma$, the [Brunt–Väisälä frequency](gravity-wave.md#buoyancy-frequency) is $N^2=(1-1/\gamma)\Omega_K^2z^2/H^2$. An isothermal background can therefore support buoyancy for adiabatic perturbations; isothermal perturbations instead have $\gamma=1$ and zero buoyancy frequency.

##### Vertically integrated pressure of an isothermal disk

↑ **Parent:** [Isothermal vertical structure in stellar gravity](#isothermal-vertical-structure-in-stellar-gravity)

A locally isothermal disk in vertically harmonic external gravity has [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) $c_s^2\rho_z=-\Omega_z^2z\rho$, hence $\rho=\rho_0e^{-z^2/(2H^2)}$ with $H=c_s/\Omega_z$. Because $c_s$ is independent of height, integrating $p=c_s^2\rho$ gives the displayed pressure. The harmonic-gravity approximation has the accuracy of the [thin disk](#thin-disk) expansion.

##### Hermite vertical mode of an isothermal disk

↑ **Parent:** [Isothermal vertical structure in stellar gravity](#isothermal-vertical-structure-in-stellar-gravity)

In an inviscid locally isothermal [Keplerian disk](#keplerian-disk), a radial [normal mode](wave-equation.md#normal-mode) of [wavenumber](wave-equation.md#wavenumber) $k$ has pressure variable $\xi$ proportional to a [Probabilists' Hermite polynomial](orthogonal-polynomial.md#probabilists-hermite-polynomial) in $x=z/H$. Gaussian-weighted finite energy excludes the exponentially growing vertical branch. Polynomial termination gives $n=0,1,\ldots$ and the local dispersion relation $(\widehat\omega^2-n\Omega_K^2)(\widehat\omega^2-\Omega_K^2)=k^2c_s^2\widehat\omega^2$, where $\widehat\omega=\omega-m\Omega_K$. At singular values introduced by eliminating velocity components, the original equations must also be checked. The $n=0$ propagating branch is an [inertial-acoustic wave](gravitational-instability-of-an-astrophysical-disk.md#inertial-acoustic-wave); $n=1$ has vertically uniform vertical velocity and odd pressure perturbation, permitting bending disturbances.

###### Bending wave of a Keplerian disk

↑ **Parent:** [Hermite vertical mode of an isothermal disk](#hermite-vertical-mode-of-an-isothermal-disk)

For the $n=1$, $m=1$ [Hermite vertical mode of an isothermal disk](#hermite-vertical-mode-of-an-isothermal-disk), the local dispersion relation is $[(\omega-\Omega_K)^2-\Omega_K^2]^2=k^2c_s^2(\omega-\Omega_K)^2$. Its low inertial-frame frequency branches satisfy $\omega\simeq\pm kc_s/2$. These [waves](wave.md) transport a changing tilt of successive annuli, or a warp, with [group velocity](dispersion-relation.md#group-velocity) $\pm c_s/2$. The vertical velocity is approximately independent of height. A rigid tilt is the zero-wavenumber, zero-pattern-frequency limit; the gas still executes vertical orbital motion at frequency $\Omega_K$ in its rotating frame.

#### Constant gas-radiation ratio vertical disc structure

↑ **Parent:** [Vertical structure of an astrophysical disk](#vertical-structure-of-an-astrophysical-disk)

For constant positive $\lambda=p_r/p_g$ and molecular gas constant $\mathcal R=k/(\mu_m m_p)$, the gas–radiation [equation of state](pressure.md#equation-of-state) gives $\rho=4\sigma T^3/(3c\lambda\mathcal R)$. Vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) in harmonic central gravity therefore gives $T=T_0(1-z^2/H^2)$ with $T_0=\Omega^2H^2/[8(1+\lambda)\mathcal R]$. The [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) and [pressure](pressure.md) powers are three and four. Integrating both faces yields $\Sigma=(32/35)\rho_0H$ and $\int p\,dz=\Sigma\Omega^2H^2/9$. With constant [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity), [radiative diffusion](astrophysics.md#radiative-diffusion) produces a linear flux and height-independent [dynamic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) $\mu=4c\lambda/[9\kappa(1+\lambda)]$. This is an interior formal profile; its low-density surface requires a photospheric treatment beyond diffusion.

#### Vertical dynamical timescale of a disk

↑ **Parent:** [Vertical structure of an astrophysical disk](#vertical-structure-of-an-astrophysical-disk)

The time for a vertical [pressure](pressure.md) or gravity response. A [sound-crossing time](compressible-flow.md#sound-crossing-time) and a small vertical oscillation period have this scale in a [thin disk](#thin-disk). It need not equal a damping time.

#### Polytropic vertical structure in stellar gravity

↑ **Parent:** [Vertical structure of an astrophysical disk](#vertical-structure-of-an-astrophysical-disk)

In harmonic stellar gravity, an [ideal gas](ideal-gas.md) with a [polytropic equation of state](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#polytropic-equation-of-state) of index $n>0$ has a quadratic [temperature](temperature.md) profile and finite semi-thickness $H^2=2(n+1)\mathcal R T_0/\Omega^2$. This differs from the [polytropic vertical structure of a self-gravitating disk](gravitational-instability-of-an-astrophysical-disk.md#polytropic-vertical-structure-of-a-self-gravitating-disk).

##### Zero-radiative-flux obstruction for a polytropic ideal gas

↑ **Parent:** [Polytropic vertical structure in stellar gravity](#polytropic-vertical-structure-in-stellar-gravity)

For positive finite [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity) and nonvacuum [ideal gas](ideal-gas.md) satisfying [radiative diffusion](astrophysics.md#radiative-diffusion), zero flux implies $dT/dz=0$. Write $P=\mathcal R\rho T$, where $\mathcal R=k_B/(\mu_m m_p)$ for constant [mean molecular weight](ideal-gas.md#mean-molecular-weight) $\mu_m$ in proton-mass units. If a [polytropic equation of state](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#polytropic-equation-of-state) $P=K\rho^\gamma$ with constant $K>0$ is also imposed, then $T=(K/\mathcal R)\rho^{\gamma-1}$. For $\gamma\ne1$, constant [temperature](temperature.md) would force constant density, contradicting vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) $P'=-\rho\Omega^2z$ on an open interval away from the midplane. The familiar finite polytropic slab for $\gamma>1$ is therefore a solution of the hydrostatic/adiabatic closure, not of this finite-opacity zero-radiative-flux system. For $\gamma=1$, the consistent isothermal profile is $\rho=\rho_m\exp[-\Omega^2z^2/(2\mathcal R T_m)]$, with no finite surface.

#### Disk scale height

↑ **Parent:** [Vertical structure of an astrophysical disk](#vertical-structure-of-an-astrophysical-disk)

The disk scale height is the characteristic vertical thickness over which density or pressure decreases. Vertical hydrostatic balance in a thin Keplerian gas disk gives $H\simeq c_s/\Omega_K$.

##### Disk aspect ratio

↑ **Parent:** [Disk scale height](#disk-scale-height)

The aspect ratio of an [astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md) is its vertical thickness scale divided by cylindrical radius. A [thin disk](#thin-disk) has $H/r\ll1$. In gas-supported vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) with harmonic external gravity $-\Omega_z^2z$, $H/r\sim c_s/(r\Omega_z)$, where $\Omega_z$ is the [vertical epicyclic frequency](#vertical-epicyclic-frequency) and $c_s$ is the [sound speed](compressible-flow.md#speed-of-sound). In a centrally dominated [Keplerian disk](#keplerian-disk), $\Omega_z=\Omega$, so this compares [sound speed](compressible-flow.md#speed-of-sound) with orbital speed. If disk self-gravity supplies the vertical restoring force, the relation $H\sim c_s/\Omega$ requires replacement. A polytropic free-surface height and an isothermal scale height differ by model-dependent constants even though their aspect ratios have the same basic role.

###### Disk flaring

↑ **Parent:** [Disk aspect ratio](#disk-aspect-ratio)

A disk flares when its [disk aspect ratio](#disk-aspect-ratio) increases outward. This is stronger than the statement that its height increases: a disk with $H\propto r$ has constant aspect ratio and is not flaring by this definition. The radial [temperature](temperature.md) and [surface density of a disk](#surface-density-of-a-disk) profiles determine flaring in a specified vertical equilibrium model.

###### Flaring passive disk

↑ **Parent:** [Disk flaring](#disk-flaring)

Assume a [passive disk](#passive-disk) has local [isothermal sound speed](compressible-flow.md#isothermal-sound-speed) squared $c_s^2=aT$, [disk scale height](#disk-scale-height) $H=c_s/\Omega_K$, and irradiated height $z_s=fH$ with constant $f$. Its [irradiation grazing angle of a disk](#irradiation-grazing-angle-of-a-disk) and [Stefan–Boltzmann law](heat.md#stefan-boltzmann-law) give $q'=Cq^8/R^3$ for $q=z_s/R$, where $C=4\pi\sigma(GM/a)^4/(L_*f^8)$. Integrating gives $q^{-7}=R_0^2/R^2+B$, with $R_0^2=7C/2$. Where the first term dominates and the [thin disk](#thin-disk) assumptions hold, $q\simeq(R/R_0)^{2/7}$, $H\propto R^{9/7}$ and $T\propto R^{-3/7}$. The law cannot be extrapolated into a geometrically thick region using the same grazing-angle approximation.

#### Vertical epicyclic frequency

↑ **Parent:** [Vertical structure of an astrophysical disk](#vertical-structure-of-an-astrophysical-disk)

The vertical epicyclic frequency is the frequency of a small vertical oscillation about a circular orbit. In an axisymmetric potential, $\Omega_z^2=\partial_z^2\Phi$ at the midplane; in a spherical potential it equals the circular orbital frequency.

#### Radiation-to-gas pressure ratio

↑ **Parent:** [Vertical structure of an astrophysical disk](#vertical-structure-of-an-astrophysical-disk)

The radiation-to-gas pressure ratio compares [radiation pressure](pressure.md#radiation-pressure) with [gas pressure](pressure.md#gas-pressure). It is distinct from [plasma beta](#plasma-beta), which compares gas pressure with [magnetic pressure](magnetohydrodynamics.md#magnetic-pressure). Both are sometimes denoted by $\beta$, so the convention must be stated.

##### Constant-pressure-ratio vertical disk model

↑ **Parent:** [Radiation-to-gas pressure ratio](#radiation-to-gas-pressure-ratio)

Assume a [thin disk](#thin-disk) has height-independent [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity) $\kappa_{\rm op}$ and [radiation-to-gas pressure ratio](#radiation-to-gas-pressure-ratio) $\beta$, with vertical gravity $-\Omega_z^2z$. [Hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) and [radiative diffusion](astrophysics.md#radiative-diffusion) imply

$$
F_z=\frac\beta{1+\beta}\frac{c\Omega_z^2}{\kappa_{\rm op}}z.
$$

Balancing its derivative against viscous heating $\rho\nu q^2\Omega^2$ fixes the [dynamic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md)

$$
\rho\nu=\frac\beta{1+\beta}\frac{c}{q^2\kappa_{\rm op}}\frac{\Omega_z^2}{\Omega^2}.
$$

It is independent of height under these assumptions. For a [spherically symmetric potential](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#spherically-symmetric-potential), $\Omega_z=\Omega$. Integrating $\rho\nu$ through the full surface-to-surface thickness $2H$ gives $\bar\nu\Sigma=2H\rho\nu$, which determines the thickness from the radial [accretion rate](#accretion-rate) solution.

#### Dust settling in an astrophysical disk

↑ **Parent:** [Vertical structure of an astrophysical disk](#vertical-structure-of-an-astrophysical-disk)

Dust settling is the vertical drift of solid particles toward a disk's midplane under gravity and [drag force](fluid-mechanics.md#drag-physics). In the strong-drag limit, a particle with [aerodynamic stopping time](fluid-mechanics.md#aerodynamic-stopping-time) $\tau$ settles through a linear vertical gravitational field at rate $\Omega_z^2\tau$.

#### Homologous vertical motion of an astrophysical disk

↑ **Parent:** [Vertical structure of an astrophysical disk](#vertical-structure-of-an-astrophysical-disk)

In homologous vertical motion, every height scales by one time-dependent thickness $H(t)$, so $z=H(t)\zeta$ and the vertical velocity is $u_z=\dot H\zeta$. A fixed dimensionless density profile then expands and contracts without changing shape.

##### Slow cooling of an astrophysical disk

↑ **Parent:** [Homologous vertical motion of an astrophysical disk](#homologous-vertical-motion-of-an-astrophysical-disk)

A laminar column can contract through nearly [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) when its cooling time greatly exceeds its [vertical dynamical timescale of a disk](#vertical-dynamical-timescale-of-a-disk). For cooling $AT^{1+\beta}$ and fixed column mass, a [similarity solution](partial-differential-equation.md#similarity-solution) has $\rho\propto\eta^{-1}$, $T\propto\eta^2$, $P\propto\eta$, and vertical velocity $u_z=\dot\eta z/\eta$. The coefficient $C$ also depends on the initial [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) normalization.

##### Vertical breathing mode of an astrophysical disk

↑ **Parent:** [Homologous vertical motion of an astrophysical disk](#homologous-vertical-motion-of-an-astrophysical-disk)

The vertical breathing mode is a coherent oscillation of a disk's thickness. For an adiabatic gas with pressure proportional to $H^{1-\gamma}$ in a harmonic vertical potential, $\omega_H=\sqrt{\gamma+1}\,\Omega_z$.

## Accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accretion_disk)

An accretion disk transports mass inward and angular momentum outward through stresses, while dissipating orbital energy as heat.

### Accretion corona

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion disk](#accretion-disk)

A hot, comparatively optically thin [plasma](physics.md#plasma-physics) region associated with an [accretion](astrophysics.md#accretion) flow. Thermal or nonthermal electrons can transfer [energy](energy.md) to softer seed [photons](quantum-mechanics.md#photon) through [inverse Compton scattering](mathematical-physics.md#inverse-compton-scattering), producing a harder radiation component. Its geometry, heating and coupling to the cooler disc are model dependent; a hard [radiation spectrum](electromagnetic-radiation.md#radiation-spectrum) alone does not uniquely determine these properties.

### Accretion-disk tests of strong-field gravity

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion disk](#accretion-disk)

Radiating material close to a compact object probes relativistic orbital motion, [gravitational redshift](general-relativity.md#gravitational-redshift) and light propagation. A thin disk's inferred inner edge can be compared with the predicted [innermost stable circular orbit](astrophysical-black-hole.md#innermost-stable-circular-orbit), while iron-line transfer and thermal-continuum fitting probe complementary aspects of the same geometry. Agreement tests a combined spacetime-and-disk model. A [black-hole spin](astrophysical-black-hole.md#black-hole-spin) measurement made after imposing a [Kerr metric](general-relativity.md#kerr-metric) is not alone an independent verification of that [metric tensor](general-relativity.md#metric-tensor); geometry tests must vary gravitational predictions and astrophysical nuisance parameters together.

#### Thermal-continuum fitting of an accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion-disk tests of strong-field gravity](#accretion-disk-tests-of-strong-field-gravity)

A geometrically thin, optically thick disk has a radially varying effective temperature. Relativistic transport and an atmosphere model convert its thermal spectrum into an inferred emitting area and inner radius. Black-hole [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass), distance, inclination and spectral hardening are important external constraints. Selecting thermal-state observations with moderate [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity) limits contamination and the breakdown of the thin-disk approximation. Relating the fitted inner radius to the [innermost stable circular orbit](astrophysical-black-hole.md#innermost-stable-circular-orbit) remains a physical modeling assumption.

#### Relativistic iron-line spectroscopy

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion-disk tests of strong-field gravity](#accretion-disk-tests-of-strong-field-gravity)

An irradiated [accretion disk](#accretion-disk) can produce iron [fluorescence](fluorescence.md) with a known local [energy](energy.md) scale. [relativistic Doppler effect](wave.md#relativistic-doppler-effect), [gravitational redshift](general-relativity.md#gravitational-redshift), beaming and light bending redistribute those [photons](quantum-mechanics.md#photon) into a broadened asymmetric line. The red wing is sensitive to the deepest emitting radii, while inclination and orbital motion influence the profile. Coronal illumination, ionization, [absorption](radiometry.md#absorption-electromagnetic-radiation) and the continuum model must be controlled before inferring an inner radius or testing a spacetime geometry.

### Quadratic-polytrope disk with constant aspect ratio

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion disk](#accretion-disk)

For $p=K\rho^2$, a point-mass [gravitational potential](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass) and a zero-density free surface at $z=\pm\epsilon r$, the [barotropic cylindrical rotation theorem](fluid-mechanics.md#barotropic-cylindrical-rotation-theorem) gives $\Omega^2=GM/(r^3\sqrt{1+\epsilon^2})$. Vertical balance gives $\rho=GM[1/\sqrt{r^2+z^2}-1/(r\sqrt{1+\epsilon^2})]/(2K)$. Integrating over the finite height produces the displayed constant [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk). This is a local radial equilibrium family; infinite radial extent would have infinite mass.

### Thin-accretion-disk truncation error

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion disk](#accretion-disk)

For smooth radial variation on scale $r$, $c_s\sim\Omega H$, $\nu\sim\alpha\Omega H^2$, $u_r\sim\nu/r$ and $u_z\sim Hu_r/r$. The exact point-mass vertical gravity differs from $\Omega_K^2z$ by relative order $(H/r)^2$. Radial [pressure](pressure.md) support changes the rotation and dominant shear heating by the same order. Meridional viscous forces, radial radiative-flux divergence, thermal [advection](fluid-mechanics.md#advection) and compressional work are at most order $(H/r)^2$ relative to their retained vertical terms for $\alpha\lesssim1$. These estimates require secular disk evolution and exclude sharp radial [boundary layers](continuum-mechanics.md#boundary-layer) or independently rapid vertical motion.

### Wind-powered mass loss from a steady accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion disk](#accretion-disk)

For a [Keplerian disk](#keplerian-disk), let $S$ be the total loss through both faces per unit projected area and $\dot M>0$ the inward [accretion rate](#accretion-rate). Annular [mass conservation](continuum-mechanics.md#mass-conservation) gives $\dot M'=2\pi RS$. A circular orbit has [specific orbital energy](celestial-mechanics.md#specific-orbital-energy) $-GM/(2R)$, so a marginally escaping wind requires additional energy $GM/(2R)$ per unit lost mass. Equating this sink to a fraction $f$ of the two-face [viscous dissipation](stokes-flow.md#viscous-dissipation) $(9/4)\nu\Sigma\Omega^2$ gives the displayed relation. If the wind carries the local [specific angular momentum](celestial-mechanics.md#specific-angular-momentum) $h=\sqrt{GMR}$, [conservation of angular momentum](momentum.md#conservation-of-angular-momentum) gives $(\dot Mh-\mathcal G)'=h\dot M'$, with [viscous torque in an accretion disk](#viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk) $\mathcal G=3\pi\nu\Sigma h$. Eliminating the torque yields $R^2\dot M''+\tfrac32R\dot M'=\tfrac32f\dot M$.

### Accretion-disk boundary layer

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion disk](#accretion-disk)

An accretion-disk boundary layer connects nearly [Keplerian rotation](#keplerian-disk) to a stellar surface rotating at a different angular speed. For a slowly rotating star, dissipation of the incoming orbital [kinetic energy](energy.md#kinetic-energy) gives $L_{\rm BL}\simeq GM\dot M/(2r_*)$, comparable to the [standard thin-disk luminosity](#standard-thin-disk-luminosity). If the emitting area scales as $r_*H$, its [blackbody](astrophysics.md#blackbody) [effective temperature](stellar-structure.md#effective-temperature) scales as $T_{\rm BL}\sim(r_*/H)^{1/4}T_{\rm in}$. The coefficient depends on geometry, and stellar rotation reduces the available energy.

### Accretion flow

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion disk](#accretion-disk)

An accretion flow carries matter inward through a gravitationally bound system. In a disk, angular-momentum transport permits the inward drift while stresses carry angular momentum outward.

#### Magnetically channelled accretion

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion flow](#accretion-flow)

In magnetically channelled accretion, a strong [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) guides an [accretion flow](#accretion-flow) along [flux tubes](magnetic-field.md#flux-tube). The component of the [Lorentz force density](relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lorentz-force-density) parallel to the [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) vanishes, so the longitudinal flow can be treated hydrodynamically while the field controls the transverse geometry. A [magnetic dipole field](magnetic-field.md#magnetic-dipole-field) near a [neutron star](stellar-astrophysics.md#neutron-star) channels matter toward small polar caps.

##### Accretion column

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetically channelled accretion](#magnetically-channelled-accretion)

An [accretion column](#accretion-column) carries an inward [accretion flow](#accretion-flow) onto a localized region of a star. In the strong-field slender approximation, the [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) controls the [flux-tube area](magnetic-field.md#flux-tube-area) while longitudinal pressure and gravity determine acceleration. The signed outward-coordinate velocity is negative; the inward speed and [mass accretion rate](astrophysical-black-hole.md#mass-accretion-rate) are usually defined positive.

##### Magnetic spin equilibrium

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetically channelled accretion](#magnetically-channelled-accretion)

The spin at which the net material and [magnetic torque](#surface-magnetic-torque-on-an-accretion-disk) on an accreting star vanishes. The [corotation radius](#corotation-radius) is typically comparable to the [magnetospheric truncation radius](#magnetospheric-truncation-radius), but the precise torque balance depends on the coupling and any outflow.

###### Minimum magnetic-equilibrium spin period

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic spin equilibrium](#magnetic-spin-equilibrium)

With [magnetic spin equilibrium](#magnetic-spin-equilibrium) at the [magnetospheric truncation radius](#magnetospheric-truncation-radius), $T\propto R_M^{3/2}M^{-1/2}\propto B_*^{6/7}R_*^{18/7}\dot M^{-3/7}$ at fixed [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass). Inserting the [surface accretion Eddington rate](astrophysical-black-hole.md#surface-accretion-eddington-rate) changes the radius power to $15/7$. The relation assumes the boundary remains outside the star; very weak fields instead encounter surface-accretion and [mass-shedding limits](stellar-astrophysics.md#mass-shedding-limit).

##### Propeller effect

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetically channelled accretion](#magnetically-channelled-accretion)

A rapidly spinning magnetosphere adds [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum) to inflowing matter outside the [corotation radius](#corotation-radius), inhibiting accretion and potentially ejecting gas. The centrifugal onset condition does not by itself ensure that every parcel escapes to infinity.

##### Magnetospheric truncation radius

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetically channelled accretion](#magnetically-channelled-accretion)

The inner radius at which a stellar [magnetic dipole field](magnetic-field.md#magnetic-dipole-field) disrupts a surrounding [accretion disk](#accretion-disk). In a [free fall](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#free-fall) [pressure](pressure.md)-balance estimate with fixed aspect ratio $\epsilon=H/r$, $r_m\sim(\epsilon^2\mu^4/(GM\dot M^2))^{1/7}$. Geometry and inflow structure change its numerical coefficient.

###### Spherical magnetospheric pressure balance

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetospheric truncation radius](#magnetospheric-truncation-radius)

A dipole's [magnetic pressure](magnetohydrodynamics.md#magnetic-pressure) scales as $B_*^2R_*^6/(\mu_0r^6)$. Spherical [free fall](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#free-fall) with $\rho=\dot M/(4\pi r^2v)$ and $v\propto(GM/r)^{1/2}$ has [ram pressure](fluid-mechanics.md#ram-pressure) proportional to $\dot M(GM)^{1/2}r^{-5/2}$. Equating them gives the seventh-root scaling. Disc geometry changes the coefficient; the estimate ceases to describe an exterior magnetosphere if the resulting radius lies inside the star.

##### Transonic accretion in a dipolar flux tube

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetically channelled accretion](#magnetically-channelled-accretion)

The [dipolar flux-tube area](magnetic-field.md#dipolar-flux-tube-area) has $A(r)=A_\star(r/R_\star)^3$. Applying [transonic accretion in a power-law tube](compressible-flow.md#transonic-accretion-in-a-power-law-tube) with $n=3$ and a [polytropic equation of state](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#polytropic-equation-of-state) gives

$$
c_s^2=\frac{2c_0^2}{7-5\gamma},
\qquad
r_s=\frac{GM(7-5\gamma)}{6c_0^2},
\qquad
\rho_s=\rho_0\left(\frac2{7-5\gamma}\right)^{1/(\gamma-1)}.
$$

Here $c_0$ and $\rho_0$ are the reservoir [sound speed](compressible-flow.md#speed-of-sound) and [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density), and $c_s$ is the [sound speed](compressible-flow.md#speed-of-sound) at the [sonic point](compressible-flow.md#sonic-point). The [mass accretion rate](astrophysical-black-hole.md#mass-accretion-rate) is

$$
\dot M=A_\star\left(\frac{r_s}{R_\star}\right)^3\rho_s c_s.
$$

For two equal polar caps of surface angular radius $\theta_\star\ll1$, $A_\star\simeq2\pi R_\star^2\theta_\star^2$. One loaded cap gives half the total rate. The finite-radius [transonic branch](compressible-flow.md#transonic-branch) exists for $1<\gamma<7/5$, must cross outside the star ($r_s>R_\star$), and requires $\theta_\star^2r_s/R_\star\ll1$ for the sonic region to remain a narrow [flux tube](magnetic-field.md#flux-tube).

###### Isothermal dipolar accretion equation

↑ **Parent:** [Transonic accretion in a dipolar flux tube](#transonic-accretion-in-a-dipolar-flux-tube)

A narrow [dipolar flux-tube area](magnetic-field.md#dipolar-flux-tube-area) $A(z)\propto z^3$ gives $A\rho v=\dot M$ for inward speed $v>0$. The [isothermal magnetic Bernoulli integral](ideal-magnetohydrodynamics.md#isothermal-magnetic-bernoulli-integral) in $\Phi=-GM/z$ then gives $(v-c_s^2/v)v\prime=3c_s^2/z-GM/z^2$. A smooth [transonic branch](compressible-flow.md#transonic-branch) crosses at $z_s=GM/(3c_s^2)$ with $v_s=c_s$, $\rho_s=e^{5/2}\rho_\infty$ and $\dot M=\rho_sc_sA(z_s)$. The crossing must lie in the domain; subsonic branches need not attain it.

###### Critical adiabatic index for dipolar accretion

↑ **Parent:** [Transonic accretion in a dipolar flux tube](#transonic-accretion-in-a-dipolar-flux-tube)

For [transonic accretion in a dipolar flux tube](#transonic-accretion-in-a-dipolar-flux-tube), the [Bernoulli equation](euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid.md#bernoulli-equation) at the [sonic point](compressible-flow.md#sonic-point) contains $c_s^2[1/(\gamma-1)-5/2]$. A positive reservoir energy requires $\gamma<7/5$. Compression in the $r^3$ [dipolar flux-tube area](magnetic-field.md#dipolar-flux-tube-area) raises the [specific enthalpy](enthalpy.md#specific-enthalpy) more rapidly than in the $r^2$ geometry of spherical [Bondi accretion](astrophysical-black-hole.md#bondi-accretion), whose corresponding upper index is $5/3$.

### Accretion rate

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion disk](#accretion-disk)

The accretion rate is the mass crossing an inward-oriented cylindrical surface per unit time. A common convention takes $\dot M>0$ for inward flow, so the outward radial mass flux is $-\dot M$.

### Hyperaccreting black-hole disk

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion disk](#accretion-disk)

A hyperaccreting black-hole disk has an accretion rate so large that photon cooling can be trapped and neutrino emission can dominate the cooling of its hot dense inner region. Such disks are models for the central engines of some gamma-ray bursts.

#### Neutrino-dominated accretion flow

↑ **Parent:** [Hyperaccreting black-hole disk](#hyperaccreting-black-hole-disk)

A neutrino-dominated accretion flow is a hyperaccreting disk whose inner plasma cools primarily through neutrino-producing weak interactions.

### Viscous evolution of an accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion disk](#accretion-disk)

Viscous disk evolution combines mass conservation with radial transport of angular momentum by stress. In a steady Keplerian disk, the sum of advective and stress-carried angular-momentum fluxes is independent of radius.

#### Compact self-similar disk with density-linear viscosity

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

The [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](#keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation) with the displayed [kinematic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md#kinematic-viscosity) has two such nontrivial finite-support [self-similar solutions](partial-differential-equation.md#similarity-solution): $a=1$, $R=Ct^{2/5}$, and $a=5/4$, $R=Ct^{1/2}$. Both have $\sigma=(15At)^{-1}$ for $A,C,t>0$. Matching powers of radius eliminates the third power only when $(5/2-2a)(2-2a)=0$. The first solution conserves disk [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum); the second conserves disk [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) but requires an inner torque.

##### Nonaccreting similarity disk supplied by an inner torque

↑ **Parent:** [Compact self-similar disk with density-linear viscosity](#compact-self-similar-disk-with-density-linear-viscosity)

The $a=5/4$ [compact self-similar disk with density-linear viscosity](#compact-self-similar-disk-with-density-linear-viscosity) has outward drift $r/(2t)$ but zero limiting mass flux at the origin. Its [viscous torque in an accretion disk](#viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk) has a positive finite inner limit for $t>0$, equal to $dJ/dt$. Thus disk mass conservation coexists with growth of [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum); an external central torque supplies that growth.

##### Accretion trajectories in a compact viscous similarity disk

↑ **Parent:** [Compact self-similar disk with density-linear viscosity](#compact-self-similar-disk-with-density-linear-viscosity)

For the $a=1$ [compact self-similar disk with density-linear viscosity](#compact-self-similar-disk-with-density-linear-viscosity), radial drift is $\dot r=r/(2t)-R/(10t)$. An integrating factor gives the displayed trajectories. From $0<r_i<R(t_i)$, the arrival time at the origin is $t_i[1-r_i/R(t_i)]^{-10}$, finite for every interior point. The outer-edge trajectory has zero [surface density of a disk](#surface-density-of-a-disk) and carries no mass.

#### Wind-loaded Keplerian disk evolution

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

Subtract local [specific angular momentum](celestial-mechanics.md#specific-angular-momentum) times [mass conservation](continuum-mechanics.md#mass-conservation) from [conservation of angular momentum](momentum.md#conservation-of-angular-momentum) including wind losses S,T. With outward radial mass flux $\mathcal F$ and outward [viscous torque in an accretion disk](#viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk) $\mathcal G=-2\pi\bar\nu\Sigma r^3\Omega'$, this gives $\mathcal Fh'+\mathcal G'=2\pi r(Sh-T)$. For [Keplerian rotation](#keplerian-disk) and $T=qSh$, substitution in mass conservation yields the displayed equation. A wind with q=1 removes local angular momentum but supplies no additional drift term; q\>1 can drive accretion even with zero viscosity.

##### Inward transport by an angular-momentum-enhanced disk wind

↑ **Parent:** [Wind-loaded Keplerian disk evolution](#wind-loaded-keplerian-disk-evolution)

For zero viscosity, $S=f(r)\Sigma$ and constant $q>1$, set $\beta=2(q-1)$. On intervals where f\>0, the [wind-loaded Keplerian disk evolution](#wind-loaded-keplerian-disk-evolution) equation becomes $\Sigma_t=\beta rf\Sigma_r+[\beta(2f+rf')-f]\Sigma$. Multiplication by $fr^{2-1/\beta}$ and the displayed travel-time coordinate reduce it to constant-speed [advection](fluid-mechanics.md#advection). Its profile is $g'=F(x'+t)$ and its characteristics move inward with $dr/dt=-\beta rf$. The weight accounts for mass loss as well as radial compression.

#### Tracer diffusion in a power-law accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

In a steady [Keplerian accretion disk](#keplerian-accretion-disk) with $\Sigma=\Sigma_0R^{-2}$, $\nu=\nu_0R^2$, and tracer diffusivity $k=\zeta\nu$, the trace [passive scalar](scalar-transport.md#passive-scalar) concentration obeys the displayed constant-coefficient [advection-diffusion equation](diffusion-equation.md#advection-diffusion-equation) in logarithmic radius. Contaminant mass is proportional to $C\,dY$, because $2\pi R\sigma\,dR=2\pi\Sigma_0 C\,dY$. A radial impulse $C_0\delta(R-R_0)$ therefore has logarithmic amplitude $C_0/R_0$ and evolves by the [Gaussian heat kernel](diffusion-equation.md#gaussian-heat-kernel) centered at $\ln R_0-vt$. The full-line solution idealizes radii far from the disk boundaries.

#### Compact self-similar disk with square-root density viscosity

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

For $\nu=\nu_0(\Sigma/\Sigma_0)^{1/2}(R/R_0)^{9/4}$, the variables $x=\sqrt{R/R_0}$, $S=(\Sigma/\Sigma_0)x^3$ and $\tau=3\nu_0t/(4R_0^2)$ reduce the source-free [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](#keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation) to $S_\tau=(xS^{3/2})_{xx}$. The displayed [self-similar solution](partial-differential-equation.md#similarity-solution) has an outer edge $R_e=36R_0\tau$ and a surface-density cusp proportional to $R^{-3/2}$ at the origin. The positive-part cutoff is essential: beyond the edge, $S^{3/2}$ is the cube of the absolute value, so continuing the squared polynomial without truncation does not solve the same equation.

The disk mass is $8\pi\Sigma_0R_0^2\tau^{-1/2}$ and its [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum) is $12\pi\Sigma_0R_0^2\sqrt{GMR_0}$, independent of time. It therefore describes accretion with outward spreading and conserved disk angular momentum, under the formal zero-torque condition at the origin. The inward mass flux changes sign at $R=9R_0\tau/4$, so the outer portion moves outward while the inner portion accretes.

#### Viscous stability of an accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

Within an instantaneous vertically averaged transport closure, the local [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](#keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation) has effective diffusion coefficient $3\partial_\Sigma(\bar\nu\Sigma)$. A positive coefficient smooths short radial [Fourier modes](fourier-analysis.md#fourier-mode) in the allowed thin-disc wavelength range; a negative coefficient gives the [negative-diffusion criterion for viscous disk instability](#negative-diffusion-criterion-for-viscous-disk-instability). A zero coefficient is linearly degenerate and does not establish strict decay. This local response criterion alone does not settle thermal, self-gravitating or boundary-driven stability.

#### Vertically averaged viscous disk equations

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

In an axisymmetric [thin disc](#thin-disk) with no vertical mass or [torque](momentum.md#torque) flux, integrate mass and [conservation of angular momentum](momentum.md#conservation-of-angular-momentum) over height. With fixed height-independent $l=r^2\Omega$, the equations are $\partial_t\Sigma+r^{-1}\partial_r(r\Sigma v_r)=0$ and $\partial_t(\Sigma l)+r^{-1}\partial_r(r\Sigma v_rl-r^3\bar\nu\Sigma\Omega')=0$. Their difference gives $r\Sigma v_rl'=(r^3\bar\nu\Sigma\Omega')'$. The [density-weighted viscosity of a disk](dynamic-viscosity.md#density-weighted-viscosity-of-a-disk) supplies the integrated stress.

##### Effective viscosity defined by disk stress

↑ **Parent:** [Vertically averaged viscous disk equations](#vertically-averaged-viscous-disk-equations)

For cylindrical rotation independent of height, write $W_{r\phi}=\int T_{r\phi}dz$. Define the effective [kinematic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md#kinematic-viscosity) by $W_{r\phi}=\bar\nu\Sigma r\Omega'$, where $\Sigma$ is the [surface density of a disk](#surface-density-of-a-disk). For Keplerian rotation, $\bar\nu=-2W_{r\phi}/(3\Sigma\Omega)$, and the outward [viscous torque in an accretion disk](#viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk) is $-2\pi r^2W_{r\phi}=3\pi\bar\nu\Sigma\sqrt{GM r}$. A [Newtonian fluid stress tensor](viscous-fluid-flow.md#newtonian-fluid-stress-tensor) gives the [density-weighted viscosity of a disk](dynamic-viscosity.md#density-weighted-viscosity-of-a-disk); the stress definition can also parameterize an effective turbulent or magnetic transport stress.

##### Keplerian viscous diffusion equation

↑ **Parent:** [Vertically averaged viscous disk equations](#vertically-averaged-viscous-disk-equations)

Use $l=\sqrt{GM_*r}$ and $\Omega'=-(3/2)\Omega/r$ in the [vertically averaged viscous disk equations](#vertically-averaged-viscous-disk-equations). The radial drift is $v_r=-3(\Sigma\sqrt r)^{-1}\partial_r(\sqrt r\bar\nu\Sigma)$. [Conservation of mass](continuum-mechanics.md#mass-conservation) gives this nonlinear [diffusion equation](diffusion-equation.md). It assumes the central rotation law remains fixed and excludes wind/surface [torque](momentum.md#torque) terms.

###### Keplerian disk evolution with a ring source

↑ **Parent:** [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](#keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation)

For an axisymmetric [thin disc](#thin-disk) supplied at $R_0$ with the local [Keplerian rotation](#keplerian-disk) specific angular momentum, the displayed source augments the [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](#keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation). The radial velocity remains $v_R=-3(\Sigma R^{1/2})^{-1}\partial_R(\nu\Sigma R^{1/2})$: subtracting local specific angular momentum times [conservation of mass](continuum-mechanics.md#mass-conservation) from [conservation of angular momentum](momentum.md#conservation-of-angular-momentum) cancels the source term. The normalization of the [Dirac delta function](distribution-theory.md#dirac-delta-function) makes the integrated supplied mass per unit time equal to $\dot M(t)$.

###### Square-root-radius diffusion transform

↑ **Parent:** [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](#keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation)

For a steady zero-mass-flux background and constant [viscous transport response exponent](#viscous-transport-response-exponent), this change removes the first derivative in the radial diffusion operator. If $\Sigma_0\propto r^{-p}$, the [kinematic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md#kinematic-viscosity) scales as $r^{p-1/2}$, so the transformed diffusivity scales as $x^{2p-3}$. It is constant at $p=3/2$.

###### Heat-equation transform for linear-radius disk viscosity

↑ **Parent:** [Square-root-radius diffusion transform](#square-root-radius-diffusion-transform)

For source-free [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](#keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation), the variables $y=\sqrt r$ and $g=r^{3/2}\Sigma$ give $g_t=(3\nu_0/4)g_{yy}$ when $\bar\nu=\nu_0r$. Rescaling y gives the displayed [heat equation](diffusion-equation.md#heat-equation). Initial and radial boundary conditions must also be transformed; the reduction does not specify them.

###### Constant-mass disk with viscosity proportional to radius

↑ **Parent:** [Heat-equation transform for linear-radius disk viscosity](#heat-equation-transform-for-linear-radius-disk-viscosity)

With $x=2\sqrt{r/(3A)}$ and $g=Ar^{3/2}\Sigma$, the half-line [heat equation](diffusion-equation.md#heat-equation) solution $g=Ct^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/(4t)}$ has $g_x(0)=0$ but $g(0)>0$. It gives $\Sigma\propto r^{-3/2}t^{-1/2}e^{-r/(3At)}$, constant disk mass and [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum) proportional to $t^{1/2}$. Its radial [velocity](classical-mechanics.md#velocity) is $r/t$, and a central [torque](momentum.md#torque) proportional to $t^{-1/2}$ supplies the increasing [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum). It is an outward-spreading, torque-driven disk, not a zero-torque accreting solution.

###### Accreting similarity disk with viscosity proportional to radius

↑ **Parent:** [Heat-equation transform for linear-radius disk viscosity](#heat-equation-transform-for-linear-radius-disk-viscosity)

For $\bar\nu=Ar$ with $A>0$, use $x=2\sqrt{r/(3A)}$ and $g=Ar^{3/2}\Sigma$. The [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](#keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation) becomes $g_t=g_{xx}$. The [zero-torque inner boundary condition](#zero-torque-inner-boundary-condition) is $g(0,t)=0$, and the positive solution $g=Cxt^{-3/2}e^{-x^2/(4t)}$ gives $\Sigma\propto r^{-1}t^{-3/2}e^{-r/(3At)}$. Its mass is proportional to $t^{-1/2}$, [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum) is constant and inward mass flow at the origin is proportional to $t^{-3/2}$. Its radial [velocity](classical-mechanics.md#velocity) is $r/t-3A/2$, exhibiting inner accretion and outer spreading.

###### General steady viscous flux in a Keplerian disk

↑ **Parent:** [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](#keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation)

Two integrations of the steady [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](#keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation) give this general transported quantity. $A$ fixes the [mass accretion rate](astrophysical-black-hole.md#mass-accretion-rate), while $B$ fixes the additive [angular-momentum flux](momentum.md#angular-momentum-flux) or inner [torque](momentum.md#torque). A [zero-torque inner boundary condition](#zero-torque-inner-boundary-condition) gives $B=-A\sqrt{r_{\rm in}}$; a [nonaccreting constant-torque disk](#nonaccreting-constant-torque-disk) has $A=0$.

###### Two-boundary mass split of a ring-fed disk

↑ **Parent:** [General steady viscous flux in a Keplerian disk](#general-steady-viscous-flux-in-a-keplerian-disk)

For steady injection into a [Keplerian disk](#keplerian-disk) between two zero-[torque](momentum.md#torque) boundaries, [conservation of mass](continuum-mechanics.md#mass-conservation) gives $\dot M=\dot M_{\rm in}+\dot M_{\rm out}$. [Conservation of angular momentum](momentum.md#conservation-of-angular-momentum) gives $\dot M h_0=\dot M_{\rm in}h_{\rm in}+\dot M_{\rm out}h_{\rm out}$, where $h(R)=\sqrt{GMR}$. Eliminating the outward mass flux gives the displayed inward fraction. This fraction does not depend on the viscosity law: the two boundary fluxes must carry away the injected mass and angular momentum. Equivalently, continuity of the [viscous torque in an accretion disk](#viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk) joins $\nu\Sigma\sqrt R=\dot M_{\rm in}(\sqrt R-\sqrt{R_{\rm in}})/(3\pi)$ inside the source to $\dot M_{\rm out}(\sqrt{R_{\rm out}}-\sqrt R)/(3\pi)$ outside.

#### Angular-momentum integrability of a Keplerian disk

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

A [Keplerian disk](#keplerian-disk) with [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) $\Sigma\sim r^{-p}$ at infinity has finite outer [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) for $p>2$ and finite outer [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum) for $p>5/2$, since the relevant integrals are $\int\Sigma r\,dr$ and $\int\Sigma r^{3/2}dr$. The borderline $p=5/2$ has logarithmically divergent angular momentum even though its mass converges.

#### Inverse-radius viscous disk similarity

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

For a [Keplerian accretion disk](#keplerian-accretion-disk) with $\bar\nu\propto r^{5/2}$, dimensionless $g=\bar\nu\Sigma/(\bar\nu_0\Sigma_0)$ satisfies $g_\tau=4x^{5/2}g_{xx}+6x^{3/2}g_x$. In $y=x^{-1/2}$ this is $g_\tau=y g_{yy}$. The ansatz $g=G(y/\tau)$ gives $G=A+B e^{-y/\tau}$; finite disk [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) selects $A=0$. Its nonzero profile has logarithmically infinite disk [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum) on an infinite radial domain.

#### Steady viscous disk supplied by a radial source

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

For a [Keplerian disk](#keplerian-disk) with evolution $\partial_t\Sigma=(2\pi r)^{-1}\partial_r\mathcal F+S$ and $\mathcal F=6\pi r^{1/2}\partial_r(r^{1/2}\bar\nu\Sigma)$, take zero viscous [torque](momentum.md#torque) at $r_1>0$ and zero flux outside the source region at $r_2>r_1$. The [steady state](dynamical-systems.md#steady-state) gives $\mathcal F'=-2\pi rS$, and its outer condition gives the displayed flux. Integrating once more from the [zero-torque inner boundary condition](#zero-torque-inner-boundary-condition) yields

$$
\bar\nu(r)\Sigma(r)=\frac{1}{3\sqrt r}\int_{r_1}^{r}\frac{du}{\sqrt u}\int_u^{r_2}vS(v)\,dv.
$$

The equation determines the viscosity-density product, so a thermodynamic viscosity closure can be imposed afterward. For nonnegative integrable $S$, it is nonnegative and the inner [accretion rate](#accretion-rate) is exactly the total supplied mass per unit time.

##### Outer-edge source of a viscous disk

↑ **Parent:** [Steady viscous disk supplied by a radial source](#steady-viscous-disk-supplied-by-a-radial-source)

A narrow [surface density of a disk](#surface-density-of-a-disk) source approaching the outer edge from inside, with $S=S_0\delta(r-r_2)$ normalized to have full weight, gives $\dot M=2\pi r_2S_0$ and the displayed steady interior profile. This follows by replacing the inner source integral by $r_2S_0$ and integrating $u^{-1/2}$. The flux jumps from $\dot M$ just inside to zero outside the injecting edge. Thus a zero exterior flux is consistent, whereas a continuous zero interior boundary trace is not. A half-weight convention for a symmetric endpoint [Dirac delta function](distribution-theory.md#dirac-delta-function) represents a different total injected mass; it must not be silently mixed with the full-weight boundary source.

#### Steady accretion disk with arbitrary rotation law

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

For inward [accretion rate](#accretion-rate) $\dot M>0$, the steady [conservation of angular momentum](momentum.md#conservation-of-angular-momentum) equation is $\mathcal G=\dot M(h-h_{\rm in})$ when the inner torque vanishes. The [viscous torque in an accretion disk](#viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk) is $\mathcal G=2\pi qh\bar\nu\Sigma$, where $h=r^2\Omega$ and $q$ is the [orbital shear parameter](gravitational-instability-of-an-astrophysical-disk.md#orbital-shear-parameter). Thus

$$
\bar\nu\Sigma=\frac{\dot M}{2\pi q}\left(1-\frac{h_{\rm in}}h\right).
$$

The familiar [Keplerian accretion disk](#keplerian-accretion-disk) follows when $q=3/2$ and $h\propto r^{1/2}$. The general form also applies to the non-Keplerian [Paczyński-Wiita circular orbit](astrophysical-black-hole.md#paczynski-wiita-circular-orbit) rotation law.

#### Viscous timescale

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

The viscous timescale is the radial diffusion time $t_\nu\sim R^2/\nu$ associated with a kinematic viscosity $\nu$ on length scale $R$.

#### Viscous torque in an accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

For vertically integrated kinematic viscosity $\bar\nu$, the viscous torque across a cylinder of radius $r$ is

$$
\mathcal G=-2\pi\bar\nu\Sigma r^3\frac{d\Omega}{dr}.
$$

##### Torque transport of energy in a steady accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous torque in an accretion disk](#viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk)

For a steady [Keplerian accretion disk](#keplerian-accretion-disk) with inward [accretion rate](#accretion-rate) $\dot M$ and vanishing inner [torque](momentum.md#torque), the outward angular-momentum torque is $\mathcal G=\dot M(\sqrt{GMr}-\sqrt{GMr_{\rm in}})$. It transports [energy](energy.md) outward at rate $\Omega\mathcal G$. The binding-energy acquisition rate between radii $b$ and $a$ is $B_{a,b}=GM\dot M(1/a-1/b)/2$. Integrating the vertical [viscous dissipation](stokes-flow.md#viscous-dissipation) and then the annulus gives

$$
D_{a,b}=GM\dot M\left[\frac32\left(\frac1a-\frac1b\right)-\sqrt{r_{\rm in}}(a^{-3/2}-b^{-3/2})\right].
$$

Since $\Omega\mathcal G=GM\dot M[r^{-1}-\sqrt{r_{\rm in}}r^{-3/2}]$, subtraction proves the displayed energy balance. Far from the inner edge $D_{a,b}\simeq3B_{a,b}$, whereas the boundary torque powers vanish for the complete disk and $D=B=GM\dot M/(2r_{\rm in})$. Local dissipation is therefore not supplied solely by local changes in orbital binding energy.

##### Nonaccreting constant-torque disk

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous torque in an accretion disk](#viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk)

A steady [Keplerian disk](#keplerian-disk) can transport [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum) by viscous stress with zero mass flux. The radial drift vanishes, but the [torque](momentum.md#torque) is constant and nonzero. An inner source and outer sink must supply its boundary angular-momentum exchange. This is distinct from a decretion flow, which transports mass outward.

##### Outer angular-momentum flux of a viscous disk

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous torque in an accretion disk](#viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk)

Integrating the [Keplerian accretion disk](#keplerian-accretion-disk) diffusion equation gives $dJ/dt=6\pi\sqrt{GM_\star}[r^{3/2}\partial_r(\bar\nu\Sigma)]_{\rm inner}^{\rm outer}$ when the angular-momentum integral and boundary limits are legitimate. An inner [zero-torque inner boundary condition](#zero-torque-inner-boundary-condition) alone does not eliminate the outer [angular-momentum flux](momentum.md#angular-momentum-flux). For divergent total $J$, use finite-radius cutoffs before making a conservation claim.

##### Zero-torque inner boundary condition

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous torque in an accretion disk](#viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk)

The zero-torque inner boundary condition sets the viscous torque to zero where nearly circular disk flow joins a plunging or stress-free region.

###### Formal viscous inspiral time in Schwarzschild spacetime

↑ **Parent:** [Zero-torque inner boundary condition](#zero-torque-inner-boundary-condition)

For constant [kinematic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md#kinematic-viscosity) $\nu$, use the phenomenological [viscous torque in an accretion disk](#viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk), [specific angular momentum](celestial-mechanics.md#specific-angular-momentum) $h=rc\sqrt{m/(r-3m)}$, and $h_0=\sqrt{12}mc$ at the [innermost stable circular orbit](astrophysical-black-hole.md#innermost-stable-circular-orbit). Neglect radial kinetic motion in the clock relation and neglect differential light travel time. The [Schwarzschild time](schwarzschild-spacetime.md#schwarzschild-time) to drift formally from $r_1\geq6m$ to $6m$ is the displayed expression, with

$$
\mathcal T(X)=3X^2+6X+6\log(X-1)-8(X-1)^{3/2}-24\sqrt{X-1}+8.
$$

Indeed $\mathcal T(2)=0$ and $\mathcal T'(X)=6X[X-2\sqrt{X-1}]/(X-1)\geq0$ for $X\geq2$. The [zero-torque inner boundary condition](#zero-torque-inner-boundary-condition) makes the formal drift speed diverge as $r\downarrow6m$, so the nearly circular approximation must give way to the [plunging region of a black-hole accretion disk](#plunging-region-of-a-black-hole-accretion-disk) sufficiently close to that orbit. This formula describes the stipulated approximation, not an exact relativistic flow solution.

###### Plunging region of a black-hole accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Zero-torque inner boundary condition](#zero-torque-inner-boundary-condition)

The plunging region begins near the [innermost stable circular orbit](astrophysical-black-hole.md#innermost-stable-circular-orbit), where nearly circular motion becomes unstable and radial inflow becomes rapid. If the inflow time is shorter than the stress-transport time, the gas approximately conserves its [specific angular momentum](celestial-mechanics.md#specific-angular-momentum) there.

###### Weak viscous torque in a thin-disk plunging region

↑ **Parent:** [Plunging region of a black-hole accretion disk](#plunging-region-of-a-black-hole-accretion-disk)

A [thin disk](#thin-disk) with [alpha disk](#alpha-disk) viscosity $\nu\sim\alpha H^2\Omega$ has a [viscous timescale](#viscous-timescale) much longer than its dynamical infall time in the [plunging region of a black-hole accretion disk](#plunging-region-of-a-black-hole-accretion-disk). At fixed nonzero [accretion rate](#accretion-rate), rapid inflow reduces its [surface density of a disk](#surface-density-of-a-disk). The resulting [viscous torque in an accretion disk](#viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk) is smaller by the displayed factor than in the slowly drifting circular disk. This estimate concerns radii outside the narrow matching layers.

#### Surface magnetic torque on an accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

A magnetic field crossing the two faces of an accretion disk exerts a surface torque through its azimuthal and vertical Maxwell stress. This torque can remove angular momentum without relying on radial internal stress.

#### Keplerian accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

In a steady Keplerian accretion disk with zero inner torque,

$$
\bar\nu\Sigma=\frac{\dot M}{3\pi}\left[1-\left(\frac{r_{\rm in}}r\right)^{1/2}\right].
$$

##### Steady Keplerian accretion disk with an inner torque

↑ **Parent:** [Keplerian accretion disk](#keplerian-accretion-disk)

For a positive inner [magnetic torque](#surface-magnetic-torque-on-an-accretion-disk) $T_m$, define $\lambda=T_m/(\dot M h_{\rm in})$. Integrating steady [conservation of angular momentum](momentum.md#conservation-of-angular-momentum) gives $\mathcal G=T_m+\dot M(h-h_{\rm in})$. With [Keplerian rotation](#keplerian-disk), this determines the [surface density of a disk](#surface-density-of-a-disk) in the displayed formula.

###### Torque-dominated accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Steady Keplerian accretion disk with an inner torque](#steady-keplerian-accretion-disk-with-an-inner-torque)

When the applied inner torque dominates the advective [angular-momentum flux](momentum.md#angular-momentum-flux), the constant-[kinematic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md#kinematic-viscosity) inner profile resembles a [decretion disk](#decretion-disk). This resemblance does not alone imply outward mass transport.

#### Viscous instability of an accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)

The [negative-diffusion criterion for viscous disk instability](#negative-diffusion-criterion-for-viscous-disk-instability) is $\partial_\Sigma(\bar\nu\Sigma)<0$. A [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) enhancement then transports angular momentum less effectively and grows. Freezing the [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](#keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation) locally gives growth proportional to the square of radial [wavenumber](wave-equation.md#wavenumber), within the thin-disc transport range; wavelengths must still be sufficiently long compared with the [disk scale height](#disk-scale-height) for that closure. The formal short-wave limit is a [backward heat equation](diffusion-equation.md#backward-heat-equation).

##### Negative-diffusion criterion for viscous disk instability

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous instability of an accretion disk](#viscous-instability-of-an-accretion-disk)

Freezing coefficients locally in the [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](#keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation) gives $\Sigma_{1,t}\simeq3\partial_\Sigma(\bar\nu\Sigma)\Sigma_{1,rr}$. A radial [Fourier mode](fourier-analysis.md#fourier-mode) grows at rate $-3\partial_\Sigma(\bar\nu\Sigma)k_r^2$. Negative response reverses ordinary smoothing and gives a [backward heat equation](diffusion-equation.md#backward-heat-equation), within the [wavelength](wave-equation.md#wavelength) range where the [thin disc](#thin-disk) transport closure applies.

##### Viscous transport response exponent

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous instability of an accretion disk](#viscous-instability-of-an-accretion-disk)

The response to a small [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) perturbation is $\delta(\bar\nu\Sigma)=q\bar\nu\delta\Sigma$. For an instantaneous law $\bar\nu\propto r^a\Sigma^b$, $q=b+1$. This exponent is not the orbital shear exponent or a satellite-to-star mass ratio, which also often use the letter $q$.

##### Radiation-pressure viscous instability

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous instability of an accretion disk](#viscous-instability-of-an-accretion-disk)

In a characteristic vertical model of a radiation-pressure-dominated [alpha disk](#alpha-disk) with constant [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity), $\mu\sim\alpha p/\Omega\sim c/\kappa$ and $p\sim\Sigma\Omega^2H$ imply $H\propto\Sigma^{-1}$ and $\bar\nu\Sigma\propto\Sigma^{-1}$. The resulting $d(\bar\nu\Sigma)/d\Sigma<0$ gives a [viscous instability of an accretion disk](#viscous-instability-of-an-accretion-disk), distinct from a [thermal instability of a radiation-pressure-dominated alpha disk](#thermal-instability-of-a-radiation-pressure-dominated-alpha-disk).

##### Viscous spreading of a narrow planetary ring

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous instability of an accretion disk](#viscous-instability-of-an-accretion-disk)

In a local Keplerian sheet, slow viscous drift gives $\partial_t\Sigma=3\partial_x^2(\nu\Sigma)$. For $\nu=A\Sigma^2$, the compact profile $\Sigma=\sigma\sqrt{1-x^2/w^2}$ preserves its shape, conserves $\sigma w$, and spreads with $w\propto t^{1/4}$ at late times.

#### Decretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decretion_disk)

A decretion disk is fed near its inner edge and transports mass outward. Rapidly rotating Be stars can sustain gaseous decretion disks whose outer material is removed by a companion, radiation, or a wind.

##### Be star

↑ **Parent:** [Decretion disk](#decretion-disk)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be_star)

A Be star is a rapidly rotating B-type star showing Balmer emission from a circumstellar gaseous decretion disk.

### Alpha disk

↑ **Parent:** [Accretion disk](#accretion-disk)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_disk)

The alpha-disk prescription writes turbulent kinematic viscosity as $\nu=\alpha c_sH\simeq\alpha c_s^2/\Omega$, with dimensionless $\alpha\lesssim1$. It reflects eddies no larger than the disk thickness and no faster than the sound speed.

#### Gas-pressure viscosity closure with mixed pressure support

↑ **Parent:** [Alpha disk](#alpha-disk)

Let $\mathcal R=k/(\mu_m m_H)$, take constant [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity) $\kappa$, and prescribe the [dynamic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) $\mu=\alpha p_g/\Omega=\alpha\mathcal R\rho T/\Omega$ in a [Keplerian disk](#keplerian-disk). The pressure supporting the disk may contain both [gas pressure](pressure.md#gas-pressure) and [radiation pressure](pressure.md#radiation-pressure). Combining [viscous dissipation](stokes-flow.md#viscous-dissipation) and [radiative diffusion](astrophysics.md#radiative-diffusion) gives $\partial_m^2T^4+A\Omega T=0$ in the [vertical mass coordinate of a disk](#vertical-mass-coordinate-of-a-disk), where $A=27\alpha\kappa\mathcal R/(16\sigma)$. Set $\zeta=2m/\Sigma$, $T=T_*t(\zeta)$ and $T_*^3=A\Omega\Sigma^2/4$. With a [radiative-zero disk surface](#radiative-zero-disk-surface), $t$ satisfies $(t^4)''+t=0$, $t'(0)=t(1)=0$. Writing $I_t=\int_0^1t\,d\zeta$, the [density-weighted mean kinematic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md#density-weighted-mean-kinematic-viscosity) is $\bar\nu=\alpha\mathcal R T_*I_t/\Omega$, giving the displayed formula. This calculation does not assume gas-pressure domination: the closure depends on gas pressure while hydrostatic support can be mixed.

#### Homologous vertical structure of a constant-opacity alpha disk

↑ **Parent:** [Alpha disk](#alpha-disk)

Let $\mathcal R=k/(\mu_m m_p)$ and choose $h^6=3\alpha\kappa\Sigma^2\mathcal R^4/(16\sigma\Omega^5)$. Set $z=h\zeta$, $\rho=(\Sigma/h)D$, $p=\Sigma\Omega^2hP$, $T=(\Omega^2h^2/\mathcal R)\Theta$ and $F=\alpha\Sigma\Omega^3h^2Q$. Vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium), [viscous dissipation](stokes-flow.md#viscous-dissipation), [radiative diffusion](astrophysics.md#radiative-diffusion) and the [ideal gas](ideal-gas.md) law reduce to $P'=-D\zeta$, $Q'=9P/4$, $\Theta'=-DQ/\Theta^3$, $P=D\Theta$. Midplane [symmetry](physics.md#symmetry-physics) gives $Q(0)=0$; a regular radiative-zero surface has $P=\Theta=0$ at a universal $\zeta_s$, with $2\int_0^{\zeta_s}D\,d\zeta=1$. All physical parameters have disappeared. Uniqueness of this normalized boundary-value problem makes every physical profile a rescaling of one dimensionless solution.

##### Radiative-zero disk surface

↑ **Parent:** [Homologous vertical structure of a constant-opacity alpha disk](#homologous-vertical-structure-of-a-constant-opacity-alpha-disk)

For a constant-opacity, gas-pressure-supported radiative disk surface at $z=H$, imposing $p=T=0$ does not impose $F=0$. If the outgoing surface [flux](mathematical-physics.md#flux) is finite and positive, dividing hydrostatic balance by the [temperature](temperature.md) gradient gives $dp/dT\sim16\sigma\Omega^2HT^3/(3\kappa F_s)$. Consequently $p\propto T^4$, $\rho\propto T^3$, and the diffusion equation gives $T\propto H-z$. Thus the [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) also vanishes regularly while the surface radiates a finite [flux](mathematical-physics.md#flux). This ideal boundary replaces the finite-temperature photospheric transition; its accuracy is not controlled solely by the disk aspect ratio.

##### Constant-opacity gas-pressure disk viscosity law

↑ **Parent:** [Homologous vertical structure of a constant-opacity alpha disk](#homologous-vertical-structure-of-a-constant-opacity-alpha-disk)

The [density-weighted mean kinematic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md#density-weighted-mean-kinematic-viscosity) of the universal vertical profile is $\bar\nu=\alpha\Omega h^2\int_{-\zeta_s}^{\zeta_s}P\,d\zeta$. Substitution of the height scale gives $\bar\nu=C\alpha^{4/3}(\kappa/\sigma)^{1/3}\mathcal R^{4/3}\Sigma^{2/3}\Omega^{-2/3}$. For [Keplerian rotation](#keplerian-disk), $\Omega^{-2/3}=(GM)^{-1/3}r$. The positive numerical constant depends only on the dimensionless vertical profile and the convention for $\alpha$.

#### Gas-pressure alpha disk with inverse-density opacity

↑ **Parent:** [Alpha disk](#alpha-disk)

For an optically thick [alpha disk](#alpha-disk) dominated by [gas pressure](pressure.md#gas-pressure), vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) gives $H\propto T^{1/2}/\Omega$ and $\rho\propto\Sigma\Omega T^{-1/2}$. The specified [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity) therefore scales as $\kappa\propto\Sigma^{-3/2}\Omega^{-3/2}T^2$. Viscous heating scales as $Q^+\propto\alpha\Sigma T\Omega$, while [radiative diffusion](astrophysics.md#radiative-diffusion) gives $Q^-\propto T^4/(\kappa\Sigma)\propto\Sigma^{1/2}\Omega^{3/2}T^2$. Their balance gives $T\propto\alpha\Sigma^{1/2}\Omega^{-1/2}$, hence $\nu\propto\alpha^2\Sigma^{1/2}\Omega^{-3/2}$. Fixed $\alpha$ and [Keplerian rotation](#keplerian-disk) yield the displayed law.

The local [thermal stability of an accretion disk](#thermal-stability-of-an-accretion-disk) follows because, at fixed surface density and radius, cooling has logarithmic temperature slope two while heating has slope one. The [viscous stability of an accretion disk](#viscous-stability-of-an-accretion-disk) follows from $\partial_\Sigma(\nu\Sigma)>0$ on the thermal-equilibrium branch. Both conclusions apply within this pressure, opacity and transport closure.

#### Vertically averaged alpha closure for a mixed-pressure layer

↑ **Parent:** [Alpha disk](#alpha-disk)

For the [constant gas-radiation ratio vertical disc structure](#constant-gas-radiation-ratio-vertical-disc-structure), the integrated alpha prescription $\bar\nu\Sigma=\int\mu\,dz=(\alpha/\Omega)\int p\,dz$ gives the displayed mean [kinematic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md#kinematic-viscosity) and $\lambda/(1+\lambda)=\alpha H\Omega\kappa\Sigma/(8c)$. Uniform [dynamic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) and nonuniform [pressure](pressure.md) are compatible because this prescription is an integrated equality. Imposing the corresponding pointwise equality would not preserve the exact vertical solution.

##### Gas-pressure branch of a mixed-pressure alpha disk

↑ **Parent:** [Vertically averaged alpha closure for a mixed-pressure layer](#vertically-averaged-alpha-closure-for-a-mixed-pressure-layer)

For small positive radiation-to-gas ratio, constant [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity), fixed alpha and molecular constants, eliminate $\lambda$ between $\lambda\propto H\Omega\Sigma$ and $\lambda\Sigma\propto H^7\Omega^6$. This gives $H^6\propto\Sigma^2\Omega^{-5}$ and $\bar\nu\propto\Sigma^{2/3}\Omega^{-2/3}$. [Keplerian rotation](#keplerian-disk) supplies the displayed radial power. Since $\bar\nu\Sigma\propto\Sigma^{5/3}$, its derivative with respect to [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) is positive and local viscous perturbations diffuse rather than amplify.

#### Pointwise alpha-prescription obstruction in a radiation-supported layer

↑ **Parent:** [Alpha disk](#alpha-disk)

In the exact constant-[opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity), pure-[radiation pressure](pressure.md#radiation-pressure) diffusion model, [dynamic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) is height-independent. Imposing an additional exact pointwise relation $\mu\propto p(z)$ forces constant pressure, incompatible with nonzero vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) away from the midplane. The characteristic-pressure [alpha disk](#alpha-disk) prescription can still be used for order-of-magnitude scalings; it is not an exact simultaneous vertical solution.

#### Accretion-disk self-gravity

↑ **Parent:** [Alpha disk](#alpha-disk)

Accretion-disk self-gravity becomes important when the disk's own gravitational field is comparable with the central object's restoring field. The outer disk can then become gravitationally unstable, fragment, and form stars.

<h4 id="shakura-sunyaev-thin-disk">Shakura--Sunyaev thin disk</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Alpha disk](#alpha-disk)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakura--Sunyaev_thin_disk)

The Shakura--Sunyaev thin disk is a geometrically thin, optically thick, radiatively efficient accretion-disk model whose turbulent stress is parametrized by the alpha prescription.

##### Multitemperature blackbody disk

↑ **Parent:** [Shakura--Sunyaev thin disk](#shakura-sunyaev-thin-disk)

A multitemperature blackbody disk is an optically thick disk approximated as concentric blackbody annuli of different effective temperatures. If $T\propto R^{-3/4}$, its intermediate-frequency spectrum satisfies $S_\nu\propto\nu^{1/3}$.

###### One-third spectrum of a multitemperature disk

↑ **Parent:** [Multitemperature blackbody disk](#multitemperature-blackbody-disk)

For a [blackbody](astrophysics.md#blackbody) disk with $T\propto r^{-3/4}$, the substitution $x=h\nu/(k_BT)$ in $F_\nu\propto\nu^3\int r(e^x-1)^{-1}dr$ produces $F_\nu\propto\nu^{1/3}\int x^{5/3}(e^x-1)^{-1}dx$. When the inner and outer temperatures make the limits approximately zero and infinity, that convergent integral is independent of [frequency](wave.md#frequency). The law applies between the outer-edge turnover and the hot inner-disk cutoff.

###### Rayleigh-Jeans spectrum of a finite blackbody disk

↑ **Parent:** [Multitemperature blackbody disk](#multitemperature-blackbody-disk)

For a finite [multitemperature blackbody disk](#multitemperature-blackbody-disk), the low-[frequency](wave.md#frequency) [Rayleigh-Jeans law](astrophysics.md#rayleigh-jeans-law) gives $F_\nu\propto\nu^2\int rT(r)\,dr$. If $T\propto r^{-3/4}$ away from the inner edge, outer annuli dominate because the weight per logarithmic radial interval grows as $r^{5/4}$. A measure-zero zero-temperature inner edge does not invalidate the leading law when $rT(r)$ is integrable.

<h5 id="pressure-opacity-zones-of-a-shakura-sunyaev-thin-disk">Pressure-opacity zones of a Shakura--Sunyaev thin disk</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Shakura--Sunyaev thin disk](#shakura-sunyaev-thin-disk)

The standard thin-disk solution separates approximately into an inner radiation-pressure and electron-scattering zone, a middle gas-pressure and electron-scattering zone, and an outer gas-pressure and free-free-opacity zone.

###### Radiation-supported inner-disk height

↑ **Parent:** [Pressure-opacity zones of a Shakura--Sunyaev thin disk](#pressure-opacity-zones-of-a-shakura-sunyaev-thin-disk)

The inner [Shakura--Sunyaev thin disk](#shakura-sunyaev-thin-disk) is supported mainly by [radiation pressure](pressure.md#radiation-pressure) and has nearly constant [electron-scattering opacity](stellar-structure.md#electron-scattering-opacity). Equating [radiative diffusion](astrophysics.md#radiative-diffusion) with local dissipation and imposing vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) gives the characteristic half-thickness

$$
H(R)\simeq\frac{3\kappa_{\rm es}\dot M}{8\pi c}\left(1-\sqrt{R_{\rm in}/R}\right).
$$

The radiation-supported height approaches a plateau away from the inner boundary. The formal zero at that boundary is outside the reliable radiation-dominated approximation. At an [Eddington accretion rate](astrophysical-black-hole.md#eddington-accretion-rate) defined using efficiency $0.1$, this height can make the inner flow only marginally thin.

##### Subsonic radial drift in a thin disk

↑ **Parent:** [Shakura--Sunyaev thin disk](#shakura-sunyaev-thin-disk)

In a steady thin alpha disk, $|u_R|/c_s$ is of order $\alpha H/R\ll1$, whereas $u_\phi/c_s$ is of order $R/H\gg1$. Matter therefore completes many nearly circular orbits during its slow viscous drift inward.

##### Standard thin-disk dissipation flux

↑ **Parent:** [Shakura--Sunyaev thin disk](#shakura-sunyaev-thin-disk)

For a steady Keplerian disk with a zero-torque inner edge $R_{\rm in}$, the flux summed over both faces is

$$
F_{\rm diss}(R)=\frac{3GM\dot M}{4\pi R^3}
\left[1-\left(\frac{R_{\rm in}}R\right)^{1/2}\right].
$$

###### Effective-temperature profile of a zero-torque disk

↑ **Parent:** [Standard thin-disk dissipation flux](#standard-thin-disk-dissipation-flux)

A [Keplerian accretion disk](#keplerian-accretion-disk) with a [zero-torque inner boundary condition](#zero-torque-inner-boundary-condition) and local [blackbody](astrophysics.md#blackbody) cooling has $T_{\rm eff}^4=3GM\dot M(1-\sqrt{r_{\rm in}/r})/(8\pi\sigma r^3)$. The [Stefan–Boltzmann law](heat.md#stefan-boltzmann-law) uses the flux through one face; the [standard thin-disk dissipation flux](#standard-thin-disk-dissipation-flux) includes both faces. The scale $T_{\rm in}=[3GM\dot M/(8\pi\sigma r_{\rm in}^3)]^{1/4}$ is a normalization, not the actual temperature at the stress-free inner edge.

###### Maximum effective temperature of a zero-torque disk

↑ **Parent:** [Effective-temperature profile of a zero-torque disk](#effective-temperature-profile-of-a-zero-torque-disk)

For the [effective-temperature profile of a zero-torque disk](#effective-temperature-profile-of-a-zero-torque-disk), differentiation of $y^{-3}(1-y^{-1/2})$ with $y=r/r_{\rm in}$ gives $y_{\max}=49/36$. Thus $T_{\max}=(6/7)^{3/2}7^{-1/4}T_{\rm in}\simeq0.488T_{\rm in}$. This distinguishes a genuine maximum [effective temperature](stellar-structure.md#effective-temperature) from the normalization used in a power-law approximation.

###### Standard thin-disk luminosity

↑ **Parent:** [Standard thin-disk dissipation flux](#standard-thin-disk-dissipation-flux)

Integrating the [standard thin-disk dissipation flux](#standard-thin-disk-dissipation-flux) gives

$$
L_{\rm thin}=\frac{GM\dot M}{2R_{\rm in}}
=\frac12\dot M R_{\rm in}^2\Omega_{K,\rm in}^2.
$$

Half of the Newtonian potential-energy decrease remains as orbital kinetic energy until the inner edge.

#### Magnetorotational instability

↑ **Parent:** [Alpha disk](#alpha-disk)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetorotational_instability)

The magnetorotational instability destabilizes a magnetized differentially rotating flow when angular velocity decreases outward. Magnetic tension couples neighboring fluid elements, allowing the inner one to lose angular momentum and fall inward while the outer one gains angular momentum and moves outward.

##### Equal-diffusivity magnetorotational channel

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetorotational instability](#magnetorotational-instability)

For $0<A<2\Omega$ and equal [kinematic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md#kinematic-viscosity) and [magnetic diffusivity](magnetohydrodynamics.md#magnetic-diffusivity), a vertical wavevector admits $\mathbf v=(V,V,0)$ and $\mathbf b=(i\omega_AV/A,-i\omega_AV/A,0)$ in Alfvén-speed units. Substitution into the [magnetorotational instability](#magnetorotational-instability) equations gives the displayed frequency and growth rate. In a [Keplerian disk](#keplerian-disk), growth of this polarization requires $B_z^2/(\mu_0\rho)>5\nu\Omega/4$. This is a bound for this selected channel and its wavelength, not a universal threshold for every MRI mode in an unbounded domain.

###### Stress-maximizing magnetorotational channel polarization

↑ **Parent:** [Equal-diffusivity magnetorotational channel](#equal-diffusivity-magnetorotational-channel)

For complex velocity amplitudes $v_x=v_y=V$ and magnetic amplitudes $b_x=-b_y=B$ in Alfvén-speed units, phase averaging gives the displayed stress. For fixed horizontal velocity energy, equal radial and azimuthal components maximize their positive [Reynolds stress](turbulence.md#reynolds-stress). Opposite equal magnetic components maximize the outward tension contribution of the [Maxwell stress tensor](electromagnetism.md#maxwell-stress-tensor), since $-B_xB_y$ is then positive. These conclusions follow from $2|ab|\leq |a|^2+|b|^2$. The vertical channel transports angular momentum outward and extracts energy from decreasing angular velocity.

##### Spring model of magnetorotational instability

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetorotational instability](#magnetorotational-instability)

For an incompressible vertical-field mode, frozen-in [magnetic tension](magnetohydrodynamics.md#magnetic-tension) supplies a spring of frequency $\omega_A$. Its displacement satisfies $\ddot\xi_x-2\Omega\dot\xi_y-4\Omega A\xi_x=-\omega_A^2\xi_x$ and $\ddot\xi_y+2\Omega\dot\xi_x=-\omega_A^2\xi_y$. Their [determinant](multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) gives the [ideal magnetorotational dispersion relation](#ideal-magnetorotational-dispersion-relation). A weak spring transfers [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum) from the faster inner element to the slower outer element, causing inward and outward migration that increase their separation when angular velocity decreases outward. A sufficiently strong spring instead supplies a restoring force.

##### Horizontally invariant magnetized shearing-sheet equations

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetorotational instability](#magnetorotational-instability)

For [ideal magnetohydrodynamics](ideal-magnetohydrodynamics.md) at constant [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) $\rho$, a [shearing sheet](gravitational-instability-of-an-astrophysical-disk.md#shearing-sheet) with velocity $-q\Omega x\mathbf e_y+\mathbf v_h(z,t)$ and [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) $\mathbf B_h(z,t)+B_z\mathbf e_z$ obeys

$$
\partial_tv_x-2\Omega v_y=\frac{B_z}{\mu_0\rho}\partial_zB_x,\qquad
\partial_tv_y+(2-q)\Omega v_x=\frac{B_z}{\mu_0\rho}\partial_zB_y,
$$



$$
\partial_tB_x=B_z\partial_zv_x,\qquad
\partial_tB_y+q\Omega B_x=B_z\partial_zv_y.
$$

Horizontal invariance and vanishing vertical velocity eliminate nonlinear [advection](fluid-mechanics.md#advection). These equations retain [Coriolis acceleration](mathematical-physics.md#coriolis-acceleration), orbital shear and [magnetic tension](magnetohydrodynamics.md#magnetic-tension). The vertical pressure adjusts to balance gravity and [magnetic pressure](magnetohydrodynamics.md#magnetic-pressure).

###### Magnetic bending in an incompressible disk

↑ **Parent:** [Horizontally invariant magnetized shearing-sheet equations](#horizontally-invariant-magnetized-shearing-sheet-equations)

In the [steady state](dynamical-systems.md#steady-state) of the [horizontally invariant magnetized shearing-sheet equations](#horizontally-invariant-magnetized-shearing-sheet-equations), let $B_x(\pm H)=\pm B_+$ and $B_y(\pm H)=0$. For $q>0$ and $B_z\ne0$, the symmetric equilibrium has

$$
B_x=B_+\frac{\sin(Kz)}{\sin(KH)},\quad B_y=v_x=0,\quad
v_y=-\frac{B_z}{2\mu_0\rho\Omega}\frac{dB_x}{dz},\qquad
K^2=\frac{2q\mu_0\rho\Omega^2}{B_z^2}.
$$

This follows by combining azimuthal induction with radial [magnetic tension](magnetohydrodynamics.md#magnetic-tension) balance to obtain $B_x''+K^2B_x=0$. Nonzero $B_+$ requires $\sin(KH)\ne0$; even homogeneous additions at $\cos(KH)=0$ are excluded by imposing midplane symmetry.

###### Nonmonotonic magnetic bending

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic bending in an incompressible disk](#magnetic-bending-in-an-incompressible-disk)

For nonzero imposed surface inclination, [magnetic bending in an incompressible disk](#magnetic-bending-in-an-incompressible-disk) is nonmonotonic when $KH>\pi/2$: the first maximum of $|B_x|$ occurs at $z=\pi/(2K)<H$, followed by a decrease. This is exactly the [finite-thickness magnetorotational instability criterion](#finite-thickness-magnetorotational-instability-criterion) for the same slab. The zero-inclination equilibrium can still be unstable, so nonmonotonic bending is a property of the forced profile, rather than a condition on every possible unstable equilibrium.

##### Ideal magnetorotational dispersion relation

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetorotational instability](#magnetorotational-instability)

For a vertical [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) in a homogeneous [shearing sheet](gravitational-instability-of-an-astrophysical-disk.md#shearing-sheet), a horizontal [normal mode](wave-equation.md#normal-mode) with growth rate $s$ and vertical [Alfvén frequency](ideal-magnetohydrodynamics.md#alfven-frequency) $\omega_a=kv_{Az}$ satisfies

$$
(s^2+\omega_a^2)(s^2+\omega_a^2-2q\Omega^2)+4\Omega^2s^2=0.
$$

The two roots for $s^2$ are real. For $q>0$, a growing magnetic mode exists precisely when $0<\omega_a^2<2q\Omega^2$. [Magnetic tension](magnetohydrodynamics.md#magnetic-tension) is strong enough to couple radially displaced elements, yet weak enough to allow their separation to increase. [Ogilvie's lecture on magnetorotational instability](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/gio10/dad14.pdf) derives this relation and its finite-thickness boundary conditions.

###### Maximum growth rate of ideal magnetorotational instability

↑ **Parent:** [Ideal magnetorotational dispersion relation](#ideal-magnetorotational-dispersion-relation)

For local cylindrical shear $q=-d\ln\Omega/d\ln r$ with $0<q<2$, maximizing the positive squared-growth root of the [ideal magnetorotational dispersion relation](#ideal-magnetorotational-dispersion-relation) gives the displayed formulas. For a Keplerian disk these are $s_{\max}=3\Omega/4$ and $\omega_A^2=15\Omega^2/16$. Field strength selects the fastest wavelength, while the maximum ideal rate is set by shear. Finite domains and magnetic diffusion can exclude that wavelength.

###### Oblique axisymmetric magnetorotational dispersion relation

↑ **Parent:** [Ideal magnetorotational dispersion relation](#ideal-magnetorotational-dispersion-relation)

In the local incompressible weak-field limit, take an axisymmetric [wavevector](elasticity-physics.md#wavevector) $(k_r,0,k_z)$ and $\omega_A=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf B/\sqrt{\mu_0\rho}$. Eliminating the pressure projects the radial force by $k_z^2/k^2$. This yields the displayed dispersion relation, where $\kappa$ is the [radial epicyclic frequency](#radial-epicyclic-frequency). For a hydrodynamically stable rotation profile with outward-decreasing angular velocity, instability requires $0<\omega_A^2<-(k_z^2/k^2)d\Omega^2/d\ln r$. A purely toroidal field has no axisymmetric local field-line-bending frequency and therefore no ordinary weak-field MRI in this approximation.

###### Radially stratified magnetorotational dispersion relation

↑ **Parent:** [Ideal magnetorotational dispersion relation](#ideal-magnetorotational-dispersion-relation)

For an [axisymmetric vertical mode of a shearing sheet](gravitational-instability-of-an-astrophysical-disk.md#axisymmetric-vertical-mode-of-a-shearing-sheet) with a vertical [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md), [radial buoyancy frequency](gravity-wave.md#radial-buoyancy-frequency) $N_r$, and $A=k^2v_A^2>0$, the nonstationary amplitudes obey $\sigma^4+(2A+N_r^2+\Omega^2)\sigma^2+A(A+N_r^2-3\Omega^2)=0$. The two roots for $\sigma^2$ are real. A growing mode exists exactly when $A+N_r^2<3\Omega^2$. If the advected [buoyancy displacement variable](fluid-mechanics.md#buoyancy-displacement-variable) is retained as an independent amplitude, the full characteristic polynomial also has a stationary factor $\sigma$.

###### Maximum growth rate of radially stratified magnetorotational instability

↑ **Parent:** [Radially stratified magnetorotational dispersion relation](#radially-stratified-magnetorotational-dispersion-relation)

For $-5\Omega^2<N_r^2<3\Omega^2$ and a continuum of vertical [wavenumbers](wave-equation.md#wavenumber), the growing branch of the [radially stratified magnetorotational dispersion relation](#radially-stratified-magnetorotational-dispersion-relation) is maximized at $k^2v_A^2=\Omega^2-(N_r^2+\Omega^2)^2/(16\Omega^2)$, with $\sigma_{\max}=(3\Omega^2-N_r^2)/(4\Omega)$. For $N_r^2\leq-5\Omega^2$ the optimum moves to the $k\to0$ boundary and the limiting rate is $\sqrt{-N_r^2-\Omega^2}$. For $N_r^2\geq3\Omega^2$ there is no exponential instability. Boundaries that discretize the allowed [wavenumbers](wave-equation.md#wavenumber) can alter the actual maximum.

###### Finite-thickness magnetorotational instability criterion

↑ **Parent:** [Ideal magnetorotational dispersion relation](#ideal-magnetorotational-dispersion-relation)

If the horizontal [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) perturbations vanish at $z=\pm H$, their allowed [vertical wavenumbers](partial-differential-equation.md#vertical-wavenumber) are $k_m=m\pi/(2H)$, $m=1,2,\ldots$. The lowest [wavenumber](wave-equation.md#wavenumber) is unstable exactly when

$$
0<\frac{\pi^2B_z^2}{8q\mu_0\rho H^2\Omega^2}<1.
$$

If that magnetic mode is stable, all higher magnetic modes are stable as well. A weak field admits longer unstable wavelengths that fit inside the slab; sufficiently strong [magnetic tension](magnetohydrodynamics.md#magnetic-tension) stabilizes every allowed wavelength.

###### Bounded vertical modes of a sech-squared magnetized disk

↑ **Parent:** [Finite-thickness magnetorotational instability criterion](#finite-thickness-magnetorotational-instability-criterion)

For density proportional to $\operatorname{sech}^2(z/H)$, the magnetic vertical equation $F''+k^2\operatorname{sech}^2(z/H)F=0$ becomes the [Legendre differential equation](linear-ordinary-differential-equation.md#legendre-differential-equation) under $\xi=\tanh(z/H)$. Bounded solutions at both surfaces are [Legendre polynomials](linear-ordinary-differential-equation.md#legendre-polynomial), with $k_n=\sqrt{n(n+1)}/H$. The $n=0$ constant function has no magnetically coupled finite wavenumber. The smallest MRI-relevant eigenvalue is $k_1^2=2/H^2$, which makes sufficiently strong [magnetic tension](magnetohydrodynamics.md#magnetic-tension) stabilize all allowed modes.

###### Fastest discrete mode of a stratified magnetorotational instability

↑ **Parent:** [Bounded vertical modes of a sech-squared magnetized disk](#bounded-vertical-modes-of-a-sech-squared-magnetized-disk)

The growing root of the [ideal magnetorotational dispersion relation](#ideal-magnetorotational-dispersion-relation) is maximized continuously at $a=v_A^2k^2/\Omega^2=15/16$, with growth $3\Omega/4$. Discrete vertical modes instead have $a_n=2n(n+1)/\beta$. The fastest one is found by comparing admissible integers on either side of the continuous maximum, rather than rounding a vertical wavenumber blindly. At $\beta=24$, $n=3$ gives $s=\Omega\sqrt{(\sqrt{17}-3)/2}$.

##### Critical wavelength of the magnetorotational instability

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetorotational instability](#magnetorotational-instability)

For a vertical magnetic field in a Keplerian disk, axisymmetric vertical MRI modes are unstable when $k^2v_A^2<3\Omega^2$. The shortest unstable wavelength is $\lambda_{\rm crit}=2\pi v_A/(\sqrt3\Omega)$.

#### Thermal stability of an accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Alpha disk](#alpha-disk)

A local thermal equilibrium is stable when a small temperature increase raises cooling faster than heating at fixed surface density. The standard total-pressure alpha prescription makes a radiation-pressure-dominated, Thomson-opacity disk thermally unstable.

##### Hydrogen-ionization disk instability

↑ **Parent:** [Thermal stability of an accretion disk](#thermal-stability-of-an-accretion-disk)

Partial [hydrogen](chemical-substance.md#hydrogen) [ionization](atomic-physics.md#ionization) changes disk cooling and permits cold and hot stable thermal branches separated by unstable equilibria. A [dwarf nova](stellar-astrophysics.md#dwarf-nova) can cycle between a cold matter-storing disk and a hot, rapidly accreting disk. The cycle is described by the evolution of local [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) and [temperature](temperature.md), coupled to [viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk).

###### Accretion-disk thermal S-curve

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen-ionization disk instability](#hydrogen-ionization-disk-instability)

At fixed disk radius, balancing local heating and cooling gives thermal equilibria parameterized by [surface density](#surface-density-of-a-disk) and [temperature](temperature.md). In the [hydrogen-ionization disk instability](#hydrogen-ionization-disk-instability), this locus has cold and hot stable branches joined by unstable equilibria. Crossing the turning points drives the state changes of a [dwarf nova](stellar-astrophysics.md#dwarf-nova). [Thermal stability of an accretion disk](#thermal-stability-of-an-accretion-disk) tests the local heating and cooling response, while [viscous evolution of an accretion disk](#viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk) changes the surface density.

##### Thermal instability from negative hydrogen ion opacity

↑ **Parent:** [Thermal stability of an accretion disk](#thermal-stability-of-an-accretion-disk)

For a gas-supported [alpha disk](#alpha-disk) with $\kappa\propto\rho^{1/3}T^{10}$, compare rates at fixed [surface density of a disk](#surface-density-of-a-disk), radius, $\alpha$ and composition on the [thermal timescale of an accretion disk](#thermal-timescale-of-an-accretion-disk). Assume that the [vertical dynamical timescale of a disk](#vertical-dynamical-timescale-of-a-disk) is shorter than that thermal time, and that the [viscous timescale](#viscous-timescale) is longer; the separation is controlled for $\alpha\ll1$ and small [disk aspect ratio](#disk-aspect-ratio). Vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) gives $H\propto T^{1/2}$, $\rho\propto T^{-1/2}$, so $\kappa\propto T^{59/6}$. Viscous heating satisfies $Q^+\propto T$ and [radiative cooling](heat.md#radiative-cooling) satisfies $Q^-\propto T^4/(\kappa\Sigma)\propto T^{-35/6}$. At equilibrium a positive [temperature](temperature.md) perturbation changes the net heating by $(41/6)(Q_0/T)\delta T>0$. With positive effective [heat capacity](heat.md#heat-capacity), it grows rather than relaxes. This is a local thermal statement and uses the fixed-column, rather than fixed-volume-density, comparison.

##### Thermal timescale of an accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Thermal stability of an accretion disk](#thermal-stability-of-an-accretion-disk)

The thermal timescale compares the internal energy stored in a disc annulus with its local heating or cooling. In a gas-pressure-supported [alpha disk](#alpha-disk), $t_{\rm th}\sim(\alpha\Omega_K)^{-1}$, whereas the [viscous timescale](#viscous-timescale) is longer by approximately $(R/H)^2$. A local thermal perturbation is therefore studied at fixed [surface density of a disk](#surface-density-of-a-disk), rather than fixed steady-state [mass accretion rate](astrophysical-black-hole.md#mass-accretion-rate).

##### Thermal instability of a radiation-pressure-dominated alpha disk

↑ **Parent:** [Thermal stability of an accretion disk](#thermal-stability-of-an-accretion-disk)

At fixed surface density, a total-pressure alpha disk supported by radiation pressure has viscous heating $Q^+\propto T_c^8$ but radiative cooling $Q^-\propto T_c^4$ when electron scattering supplies a nearly constant opacity. Heating therefore wins after a positive temperature perturbation.

## Radial epicyclic frequency

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radial_epicyclic_frequency)

The radial epicyclic frequency describes small radial oscillations about a circular orbit. For specific angular momentum $h(r)$, $\kappa_r^2=r^{-3}d(h^2)/dr$.

### Radial stability of an axisymmetric circular orbit

↑ **Parent:** [Radial epicyclic frequency](#radial-epicyclic-frequency)

At fixed [specific angular momentum](celestial-mechanics.md#specific-angular-momentum) $j$, perturb the [effective potential](mathematical-physics.md#effective-potential) $\Phi(r)+j^2/(2r^2)$ about a [circular orbit](classical-mechanics.md#circular-orbit). Linear radial motion has $\ddot x+\kappa^2x=0$, where $\kappa^2=\Phi^{\prime\prime}+3\Phi^\prime/r=r^{-3}d(j_c^2)/dr$. Positive $\kappa^2$ gives stable oscillations; axial symmetry alone does not force that sign.

### Rayleigh centrifugal stability criterion

↑ **Parent:** [Radial epicyclic frequency](#radial-epicyclic-frequency)

For inviscid cylindrical rotation without a [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md), an axisymmetric radial interchange conserves each parcel's [specific angular momentum](celestial-mechanics.md#specific-angular-momentum) $l=r^2\Omega$. Its restoring coefficient is $\kappa^2=r^{-3}d(l^2)/dr$. Increasing $l^2$ gives centrifugal stability; decreasing $l^2$ gives an exponentially growing interchange mode when radial displacement is allowed by the boundaries. A vanishing derivative is marginal, not a strictly restoring condition.

// Target: astrophysics.bigb

### Epicyclic frequency bounds for monotone spherical density

↑ **Parent:** [Radial epicyclic frequency](#radial-epicyclic-frequency)

For a nonnegative, radially nonincreasing spherical [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density), $\Omega^2=GM(r)/r^3$ and $\kappa^2=\Omega^2+4\pi G\rho(r)$. Since $\rho(r)$ does not exceed the mean enclosed density, the [radial epicyclic frequency](#radial-epicyclic-frequency) lies between $\Omega$ and $2\Omega$. General axisymmetric disks need not satisfy this bound.

### Kepler-harmonic epicyclic precession

↑ **Parent:** [Radial epicyclic frequency](#radial-epicyclic-frequency)

For $\Phi=-GM/r+\alpha\omega^2r^2/2$, nearly circular motion has $\Omega^2=GM/r^3+\alpha\omega^2$ and $\kappa^2=GM/r^3+4\alpha\omega^2$. Their generally nonrational ratio makes small-eccentricity orbits precess even if $\alpha$ was added slowly. This illustrates [adiabatic actions do not preserve orbital closure](hamiltonian-mechanics.md#adiabatic-actions-do-not-preserve-orbital-closure).

### Epicyclic motion

↑ **Parent:** [Radial epicyclic frequency](#radial-epicyclic-frequency)

Epicyclic motion is the small oscillation of a displaced fluid element or particle about a circular orbit. Its natural radial frequency is the [radial epicyclic frequency](#radial-epicyclic-frequency).

#### Axisymmetric incompressible epicyclic wave

↑ **Parent:** [Epicyclic motion](#epicyclic-motion)

For $k_y=0$ and $k_z\ne0$, [pressure](pressure.md) elimination gives $\dot v_x=2\Omega\chi v_y-\nu k^2v_x$ and $\dot v_y=-2(\Omega-A)v_x-\nu k^2v_y$, where $\chi=k_z^2/k^2$. The inviscid frequency is the [radial epicyclic frequency](#radial-epicyclic-frequency) times $\sqrt\chi$. With $w=v_x/\sqrt\chi$, $(\Omega-A)|w|^2+\Omega|v_y|^2$ is conserved when [viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) vanishes. In a Keplerian flow it bounds kinetic-energy amplification by four, whereas with zero rotation the same system has the algebraic [lift-up effect](hydrodynamic-stability.md#lift-up-effect).

// Target: turbulence.bigb

#### Velocity-kick epicycle

↑ **Parent:** [Epicyclic motion](#epicyclic-motion)

A small planar [velocity](classical-mechanics.md#velocity) kick on a stellar [circular orbit](classical-mechanics.md#circular-orbit) produces a local [epicyclic motion](#epicyclic-motion) in the source's [rotating reference frame](mathematical-physics.md#rotating-reference-frame). With outward $x$, forward $y$, and initial displacements zero, [Hill equations](celestial-mechanics.md#hill-equations) give $x/(\gamma a)=2\cos\theta(1-\cos nt)+\sin\theta\sin nt$ and $y/(\gamma a)=\cos\theta(4\sin nt-3nt)+2\sin\theta(\cos nt-1)$. Tangential kicks combine an epicycle with [Keplerian shear](planetary-science.md#keplerian-shear); a radial kick gives a closed leading-order [epicyclic ellipse](#epicyclic-ellipse).

#### Epicyclic azimuthal advance

↑ **Parent:** [Epicyclic motion](#epicyclic-motion)

The azimuthal advance over one complete small radial oscillation is $\Delta\psi=2\pi\Omega/\kappa$. The oscillatory azimuthal correction averages to zero at first order. This angle compares successive radial turning points of the same type and describes [apsidal precession](planetary-science.md#apsidal-precession) when it differs from a full revolution.

#### Epicyclic ellipse

↑ **Parent:** [Epicyclic motion](#epicyclic-motion)

In a Cartesian frame rotating with an [epicyclic guiding center](#epicyclic-guiding-center), a small planar oscillation obeys $x=X\cos(\kappa t+\chi)$ and $y=-(2\Omega X/\kappa)\sin(\kappa t+\chi)$. The ellipse is elongated azimuthally when the [radial epicyclic frequency](#radial-epicyclic-frequency) satisfies $\kappa<2\Omega$. [Conservation of angular momentum](momentum.md#conservation-of-angular-momentum) makes the motion around the guiding centre retrograde.

#### Epicyclic guiding center

↑ **Parent:** [Epicyclic motion](#epicyclic-motion)

The epicyclic guiding center is the circular-orbit location about which a particle performs [epicyclic motion](#epicyclic-motion). In a [Keplerian shearing sheet](gravitational-instability-of-an-astrophysical-disk.md#keplerian-shearing-sheet), the conserved [canonical momentum](hamiltonian-mechanics.md#canonical-momentum) is $p_y=\dot y+2\Omega x=\Omega x_0/2$. The guiding center therefore labels the particle's first-order [specific angular momentum](celestial-mechanics.md#specific-angular-momentum) offset, even while its instantaneous radial coordinate oscillates.

#### Epicyclic energy

↑ **Parent:** [Epicyclic motion](#epicyclic-motion)

For [epicyclic motion](#epicyclic-motion) in a [Keplerian shearing sheet](gravitational-instability-of-an-astrophysical-disk.md#keplerian-shearing-sheet), write $x-x_0=\operatorname{Re}(Ae^{-i\Omega t})$. The energy of the radial [harmonic oscillator](classical-mechanics.md#simple-harmonic-motion) per unit mass is $E_{\rm epi}=\Omega^2|A|^2/2$. The full horizontal [Jacobi energy in a shearing sheet](gravitational-instability-of-an-astrophysical-disk.md#jacobi-energy-in-a-shearing-sheet) also includes the negative guiding-center term $-3\Omega^2x_0^2/8$.

## Plasma beta

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_beta)

Plasma beta is the ratio of gas pressure to magnetic pressure, $\beta=P_g/P_m$.

## Sub-Keplerian rotation

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)

Sub-Keplerian rotation has angular speed below that of a circular [Keplerian orbit](celestial-mechanics.md#kepler-orbit) at the same radius. In a gaseous disk, an outwardly decreasing pressure supplies some radial support and makes the gas sub-Keplerian.

### Radial drift of solids in a gas disk

↑ **Parent:** [Sub-Keplerian rotation](#sub-keplerian-rotation)

For test particles with linear [drag](fluid-mechanics.md#drag-physics), negligible radial gas flow and negligible dust feedback, [Sub-Keplerian rotation](#sub-keplerian-rotation) removes solid-particle [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum). The displayed inward [velocity](classical-mechanics.md#velocity) uses [Stokes number](fluid-mechanics.md#stokes-number) $\mathrm{St}=\Omega_Kt_s$ and the [pressure-gradient parameter of a protoplanetary disk](#pressure-gradient-parameter-of-a-protoplanetary-disk). Drift is fastest at $\mathrm{St}=1$; the physical particle size at that point depends on the local gas and drag law.

#### Dust trap

↑ **Parent:** [Radial drift of solids in a gas disk](#radial-drift-of-solids-in-a-gas-disk)

A region that concentrates solids by opposing their loss through radial drift. Near a radial gas-[pressure](pressure.md) maximum, the pressure-support drift changes sign and carries particles toward the maximum from both sides. Gas advection and turbulent diffusion can limit retention.

### Pressure-gradient parameter of a protoplanetary disk

↑ **Parent:** [Sub-Keplerian rotation](#sub-keplerian-rotation)

A convention for the small fractional reduction of azimuthal gas speed due to radial [pressure](pressure.md) support. With $c_s^2=P_g/\rho_g$, radial balance gives $v_{g\phi}^2=v_K^2(1-2\eta)$ and hence $v_{g\phi}\simeq v_K(1-\eta)$. Other authors absorb the factor of two into the definition; the convention must accompany a drift formula.

## Streaming instability

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_instability)

The streaming instability is an instability of mutually coupled gas and solid particles in a disk. Relative drift and drag can concentrate the particles into dense structures.

### Streaming instability resonance

↑ **Parent:** [Streaming instability](#streaming-instability)

A streaming-instability resonance occurs when a gas wave has frequency $\omega=\mathbf k\mathbin\cdot\Delta\mathbf u$, matching the wave phase to the particle drift. The resonant forcing then produces algebraic particle-density growth in the test-particle limit.
