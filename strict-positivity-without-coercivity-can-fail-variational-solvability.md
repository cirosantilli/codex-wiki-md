# Strict positivity without coercivity can fail variational solvability

↑ **Parent:** [Coercive operator](coercive-operator.md)

On [l2 sequence space](l2-sequence-space.md) $\ell^2$, let $(Lu)_n=u_n/n$ and $f_n=1/n$. This bounded [self-adjoint operator](self-adjoint-operator.md) has $\langle Lu,u\rangle>0$ for nonzero $u$, but the equation $Lu=f$ requires $u_n=1$, which is not in $\ell^2$. Taking the first $N$ coordinates equal to one makes $I(u)=-\sum_{n=1}^N1/n\to-\infty$. Thus strict positivity in isolation does not give existence in that [Hilbert space](hilbert-space-split.md); a [coercive bilinear form](coercive-bilinear-form.md) and a continuous forcing functional in its norm are essential hypotheses for the usual minimization proof.
