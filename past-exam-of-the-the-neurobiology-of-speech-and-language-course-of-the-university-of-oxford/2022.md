↑ **Parent:** [2022](2022.md)

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**[Phonology](../README.md#phonology) and [syntax](../README.md#syntax) usually show a left-hemisphere bias, whereas semantic processing depends on a more bilateral system with task-dependent asymmetries. None is confined absolutely to one [cerebral hemisphere](../neuroscience.md#cerebral-hemisphere), and lateralisation of an experimental contrast is not equivalent to localisation of a representation.** [Phonology](../README.md#phonology) concerns structured sublexical units, [semantics](../README.md#semantics) concerns meaning, and [syntax](../README.md#syntax) concerns relations that organise words into sentences. These processes interact, so tasks must isolate the relevant contrast rather than merely require [language](../README.md#language).

Historical lesion observations motivated left [language](../README.md#language) dominance, but their anatomical interpretation needs care. Modern [MRI](../astrophysical-disk.md#magnetorotational-instability) of [Paul Broca](../neuroscientist.md#paul-broca)'s preserved cases showed damage extending beyond the conventional cortical region named after him. Severe impaired [speech](../README.md#speech) after a left lesion therefore supports lateralisation without proving that a small superficial patch alone constitutes the [speech](../README.md#speech) system. Large vascular lesions can also damage connections between surviving regions.[Brain 130, 1432–1441; high-resolution MRI of preserved historical brains](../psychology-bibliography.md#paul-broca-s-historic-cases-by-nina-f-dronkers-and-others)

For phonological processing, [Robert J. Zatorre](../neuroscientist.md#robert-j-zatorre) and colleagues used [PET](../electrochemistry.md#photoinduced-electron-transfer) while participants judged different attributes of the same [speech](../README.md#speech) material. Phonetic discrimination increased left inferior-frontal activity, whereas pitch discrimination recruited right prefrontal regions; acoustically matched [speech](../README.md#speech) engaged secondary auditory cortices bilaterally. Holding the signal relatively constant while changing the decision supports functional differentiation beyond a contrast between unrelated stimuli. It does not show that all phonological representations reside in inferior frontal cortex: articulatory recoding, [attention](../psychology.md#attention) and decision demands can contribute to the phonetic contrast.[Science 256, 846–849; ten-volunteer PET, matched syllables and phonetic versus pitch instructions](../psychology-bibliography.md#lateralization-of-phonetic-and-pitch-discrimination-in-speech-processing-by-robert-j-zatorre-and-others)

The distinction matters because [speech perception](../neuroscience.md#speech-perception) begins with bilateral auditory analysis, whereas selecting, sequencing or rehearsing phonological forms can have a stronger left bias. Impairment of repetition after damage to a [dorsal language pathway](../neuroscience.md#dorsal-language-pathway) and comprehension after other damage constrain different transformations. A deficit in producing a [phoneme](../README.md#phoneme) is not automatically a deficit in recognising it. Familiarity with a sound pattern and its linguistic role can also alter the task, so acoustic speed or frequency alone cannot define [phonology](../README.md#phonology).

Semantic processing illustrates why a leftward verbal-task result can conceal bilateral representational resources. In [Gorana Pobric](../neuroscientist.md#gorana-pobric)'s [rTMS](../neuroscience.md#repetitive-transcranial-magnetic-stimulation) experiment, perturbing either [temporal pole](../neuroscience.md#temporal-pole) slowed [semantic associations](../README.md#semantic-association) for words and pictures while a perceptual comparison was spared; stimulation of a control site did not produce the same semantic effect. This supports a contribution from both [anterior temporal lobes](../neuroscience.md#anterior-temporal-lobe) across input formats. It does not require identical response profiles throughout both lobes or imply that semantic retrieval and selection are entirely bilateral.[Neuropsychologia 48, 1336–1342; left/right temporal-pole rTMS, verbal/pictorial semantic association and perceptual control](../psychology-bibliography.md#amodal-semantic-representations-depend-on-both-anterior-temporal-lobes-by-gorana-pobric-and-others)

Verbal naming places additional demands on [lexical retrieval](../neuroscience.md#lexical-retrieval) and [speech production](../neuroscience.md#speech-production). Consequently, stronger left activity in naming than in passive picture viewing can reflect access and output as well as conceptual knowledge. Conversely, right-sided involvement in nonverbal meaning does not establish a separate nonlinguistic semantic store. Comparisons should separate [semantic association](../README.md#semantic-association) from perceptual similarity and control both response requirements and difficulty. Lesion and perturbation results help adjudicate whether an activated region contributes to the task.

For [syntax](../README.md#syntax), lesion studies and functional imaging converge on a predominantly left frontotemporal network, rather than a single grammar centre. [Evelina Fedorenko](../neuroscientist.md#evelina-fedorenko)'s individual-localisation study showed language-selective and domain-general regions next to one another within the anatomical territory called [Broca's area](../neuroscience.md#broca-s-area). Averaging across people can merge them. Thus, an inferior-frontal response to a hard sentence may reflect [language processing](../neuroscience.md#language-processing), [domain-general control](../psychology.md#domain-general-control) or both; independent [functional localizers](../neuroscience.md#functional-localizer) and nonlinguistic controls improve interpretation.[Current Biology 22, 2059–2062; individual functional localization and cross-domain response profiles](../psychology-bibliography.md#language-selective-and-domain-general-regions-lie-side-by-side-within-broca-s-area-by-evelina-fedorenko-and-others)

[Corianne Rogalsky](../neuroscientist.md#corianne-rogalsky)'s [lesion study](../neuroscience.md#lesion-study) compared [sentence-picture matching](../neuroscience.md#sentence-picture-matching) and plausibility judgments in 66 patients. The distribution of syntactic-comprehension impairment implicated a wider left-hemisphere system and challenged a simple equation between [Broca's area](../neuroscience.md#broca-s-area) damage and agrammatic comprehension. Convergence across tasks is useful because picture matching also demands visual analysis and response selection, while plausibility can recruit semantic expectations. Neither behavioural label makes the task syntactically pure.[Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience 30, 234–255; 66-patient sentence-picture matching, plausibility judgment and lesion mapping](../psychology-bibliography.md#the-neurobiology-of-agrammatic-sentence-comprehension-by-corianne-rogalsky-and-others)

[Sentence comprehension](../neuroscience.md#sentence-comprehension) in Japanese and [Japanese Sign Language](../README.md#japanese-sign-language) also showed left-dominant frontal and temporoparietal responses across sensory conditions. This directly addresses hemispheric comparisons, although comprehension versus nonword detection is not a pure assay of [syntax](../README.md#syntax).[Brain 128, 1407–1417; JSL/Japanese comprehension versus sentential non-word detection across Deaf/CODA/hearing groups](../psychology-bibliography.md#sign-and-speech-amodal-commonality-in-left-hemisphere-dominance-for-comprehension-of-sentences-by-kuniyoshi-l-sakai-and-others)

A quantitative [laterality index](../neuroscience.md#laterality-index) can summarise an explicitly defined response. For nonnegative left and right measurements $L,R$ with $L+R>0$,

$$
\mathrm{LI}=\frac{L-R}{L+R}.
$$

It ranges from $-1$ to $1$, but depends on baseline, threshold and region selection. Responses $(20,10)$ and $(10,5)$ have the same index $1/3$ despite different total responses. If left activity falls from $20$ to $10$ while right activity remains $10$, the index becomes zero without any right recruitment. These are illustrative values, not imaging observations. Negative contrasts need a separately justified convention.

The most convincing evidence therefore combines within-person process contrasts, unilateral perturbations or lesions, and converging measurements of behaviour and neural responses. It also describes individual variation instead of assuming that every participant has the population-average pattern. **The justified conclusion is graded functional asymmetry within interacting networks: strongest left biases for many phonological and syntactic operations, bilateral conceptual contributions, and substantial dependence on task and representation level.**

**[Basal ganglia](../neuroscience.md#basal-ganglia) abnormalities plausibly contribute to some developmental [speech](../README.md#speech) and [language](../README.md#language) disorders, especially through motor sequencing and [learning](../animal-behaviour.md#learning), but the evidence does not establish a single basal-ganglia cause across diagnoses.** A causal account must specify the disorder, the affected computation and how an alteration in a circuit produces that impairment. [Childhood apraxia of speech](../neuroscience.md#childhood-apraxia-of-speech), [developmental language disorder](../animal-behaviour.md#developmental-language-disorder) and [stuttering](../neuroscience.md#stuttering) are different [phenotypes](../genetics.md#phenotype); difficulty with [speech](../README.md#speech) output does not by itself identify a grammatical deficit.

The [basal ganglia](../neuroscience.md#basal-ganglia) participate in loops with [cerebral cortex](../neuroscience.md#cerebral-cortex) and [thalamus](../neuroscience.md#thalamus). Such circuits could support selecting, initiating and [learning](../animal-behaviour.md#learning) sequences, including the rapid articulatory transitions needed for [speech](../README.md#speech). A plausible mechanism is impaired acquisition or reliable initiation of a learned motor sequence, rather than storage of every word or syntactic rule in the [striatum](../neuroscience.md#striatum). This gives testable predictions about skill [learning](../animal-behaviour.md#learning), [nonword repetition](../neuroscience.md#nonword-repetition) and sensitivity to external timing cues. General plausibility, however, does not establish which circuit is responsible in an individual child.

The KE family supplied a particularly informative genetic case. [Cecilia S. L. Lai](../biologist.md#cecilia-s-l-lai) and colleagues identified an [FOXP2](../genetics.md#foxp2) alteration segregating with severe [speech](../README.md#speech) and [language](../README.md#language) impairment, together with an independent case in which a chromosome translocation disrupted the same [gene](../genetics.md#gene). The [phenotype](../genetics.md#phenotype) included major problems in coordinated orofacial sequencing alongside broader [language](../README.md#language) difficulties. This strengthens a causal connection between the [gene](../genetics.md#gene) and development of the [phenotype](../genetics.md#phenotype). It does not isolate the anatomical route: a [transcription factor](../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-factor) can alter several developing systems, and the family is not a representative sample of every [developmental language disorder](../animal-behaviour.md#developmental-language-disorder).[Nature 413, 519–523; KE-family cosegregation and independent chromosome-translocation case](../psychology-bibliography.md#a-forkhead-domain-gene-is-mutated-in-a-severe-speech-and-language-disorder-by-cecilia-s-l-lai-and-others)

[Kate Watkins](../neuroscientist.md#kate-watkins)'s [MRI](../astrophysical-disk.md#magnetorotational-instability) study compared affected family members, unaffected relatives and controls. Voxel-based morphometry identified abnormalities in multiple motor- and speech-related regions; targeted volumetry confirmed reduced [caudate](../neuroscience.md#caudate-nucleus) volume bilaterally. Relationships with [oral praxis](../animal-behaviour.md#oral-praxis) and [nonword repetition](../neuroscience.md#nonword-repetition) made the [caudate](../neuroscience.md#caudate-nucleus) result functionally relevant. The unaffected relatives provide a valuable comparison, but an anatomical [correlation](../variance.md#pearson-correlation-coefficient) cannot establish that [caudate](../neuroscience.md#caudate-nucleus) reduction alone mediates the [gene](../genetics.md#gene)'s effects. Multiple abnormalities and correlated behavioural measures make that inference underdetermined.[Brain 125, 465–478; affected/unaffected KE-family members, matched controls, VBM and caudate volumetry](../psychology-bibliography.md#mri-analysis-of-an-inherited-speech-and-language-disorder-structural-brain-abnormalities-by-kate-watkins-and-others)

Animal experiments move beyond an association between a human scan and a symptom. [Matthias Groszer](../neuroscientist.md#matthias-groszer) and colleagues studied mice with a corresponding alteration of the [FOXP2](../genetics.md#foxp2) orthologue. Heterozygotes had relatively preserved baseline motor abilities but impaired motor-skill [learning](../animal-behaviour.md#learning), with abnormal plasticity in striatal and cerebellar circuits. The distinction between baseline performance and improvement with practice supports a learning-related mechanism. Yet a mouse motor task is not human [syntax](../README.md#syntax) or learned [speech](../README.md#speech), and the cerebellar findings themselves argue against an exclusively basal-ganglia interpretation.[Current Biology 18, 354–362; heterozygote motor-skill learning and striatal/cerebellar plasticity](../psychology-bibliography.md#impaired-synaptic-plasticity-and-motor-learning-in-mice-with-a-point-mutation-implicated-in-human-speech-deficits-by-matthias-groszer-and-others)

[Stuttering](../neuroscience.md#stuttering) offers a partially independent line of evidence. [Soo-Eun Chang](../neuroscientist.md#soo-eun-chang)'s study combined resting-state [fMRI](../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#functional-magnetic-resonance-imaging) and probabilistic [tractography](../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#tractography) in children aged 3–9. Differences involved basal-ganglia–thalamocortical and auditory–motor networks, including weaker connectivity between [putamen](../neuroscience.md#putamen) and supplementary motor regions. Studying young children reduces the extent to which decades of compensatory speaking strategies can explain the result. Nevertheless, the measurements were taken after symptoms began. Correlated activity at rest is not direct synaptic coupling, and [tractography](../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#tractography) does not demonstrate the direction or necessity of a physiological connection.[Brain 136, 3709–3726; children aged 3–9, resting-state fMRI and probabilistic tractography](../psychology-bibliography.md#neural-network-connectivity-differences-in-children-who-stutter-by-soo-eun-chang-and-others)

Improved fluency with an external pace would be compatible with compensation for impaired internal timing, but it would not uniquely identify a basal-ganglia abnormality. External cues also change [attention](../psychology.md#attention), anticipation, auditory feedback and task structure. Similarly, a group difference in a procedural-learning task can result from poor motor execution, [working memory](../neuroscience.md#working-memory) or comprehension of instructions. The critical comparison separates [learning](../animal-behaviour.md#learning) rate from initial accuracy and uses nonverbal tasks, matched feedback and retained skill after a delay.

Several causal alternatives remain. A shared developmental influence may alter both [caudate](../neuroscience.md#caudate-nucleus) structure and cortical [language](../README.md#language) function. Reduced [language](../README.md#language) practice may itself change network measurements. A cortical or white-matter abnormality may produce downstream striatal differences. An experimentally supported gene-to-phenotype relationship does not automatically identify every intermediate step in a proposed gene-to-basal-ganglia-to-language chain.

A stronger programme would combine prospective measurements before or close to onset, repeated follow-up of persistence and recovery, detailed [speech](../README.md#speech) versus [language](../README.md#language) phenotyping, and independent motor-learning measures. Analyses should control age, [hearing](../neuroscience.md#hearing), motion, general motor ability and broader development, and assess multiple network regions. Selective perturbations in appropriate models can test mechanisms, while their behavioural scope must remain explicit. **The strongest claim is that distributed corticostriatal [learning](../animal-behaviour.md#learning) circuits are one important contributor in some [phenotypes](../genetics.md#phenotype); neither a universal basal-ganglia lesion model nor a language-specific function of [FOXP2](../genetics.md#foxp2) follows from the available evidence.**

**[Primary progressive aphasia](../neuroscience.md#primary-progressive-aphasia) has helped dissociate syntactic impairment from loss of word meaning, phonological memory and motor [speech](../README.md#speech), and has implicated a left frontotemporal network and its dorsal connections. Its contribution is substantial, but degeneration is distributed and evolving, so it does not yield a one-region map of [syntax](../README.md#syntax).** [PPA](../neuroscience.md#primary-progressive-aphasia) is a syndrome in which progressive [language](../README.md#language) impairment is initially the principal clinical problem; the variant label should follow a detailed assessment rather than substitute for one.

In [Maria Luisa Gorno-Tempini](../neurology.md#maria-luisa-gorno-tempini)'s 31-patient study, the nonfluent group combined [speech](../README.md#speech) apraxia and complex-syntax difficulties with left inferior-frontal and insular [atrophy](../biology.md#atrophy). Semantic impairment was associated with anterior temporal damage, while a logopenic pattern involved left posterior temporal and inferior parietal regions. Comparing variants showed that slow [speech](../README.md#speech), reduced [sentence comprehension](../neuroscience.md#sentence-comprehension) and impaired naming need not share a single cause. The study established useful contrasts, not perfectly isolated syndromes or a unique pathology for each [language](../README.md#language) deficit.[Annals of Neurology 55, 335–346; 31 patients, detailed language assessment and variant-specific VBM](../psychology-bibliography.md#cognition-and-anatomy-in-three-variants-of-primary-progressive-aphasia-by-maria-luisa-gorno-tempini-and-others)

The central methodological gain is to test [syntax](../README.md#syntax) rather than infer it from fluency. A person who cannot execute a well-planned utterance may still comprehend grammatical relations. Conversely, fluent production may conceal impaired thematic-role assignment. Compare short, reversible sentences whose meaning cannot be guessed from ordinary plausibility, vary grammatical construction while matching word knowledge, and measure both comprehension and production. Long sentences alone are insufficient because [phonological working memory](../neuroscience.md#phonological-working-memory) and [attention](../psychology.md#attention) also determine performance.

For example, an illustrative active/passive pair can reverse which noun is the agent while preserving familiar words. A [sentence-picture matching](../neuroscience.md#sentence-picture-matching) task can offer pictures with the same participants but reversed roles. Failure on the noncanonical construction after single-word comprehension is verified is more informative about syntactic dependencies than failure to repeat a long unfamiliar sentence. These proposed controls do not make visual choice, memory demands or response selection disappear; they make the alternatives measurable.

[Stephen M. Wilson](../neuroscientist.md#stephen-m-wilson)'s 2010 [fMRI](../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#functional-magnetic-resonance-imaging) study examined eight patients with [nonfluent variant primary progressive aphasia](../neuroscience.md#nonfluent-variant-primary-progressive-aphasia) during [sentence comprehension](../neuroscience.md#sentence-comprehension). Unlike controls, patients did not show the usual complex-versus-simple modulation in atrophic posterior inferior-frontal cortex, while a mid-superior-temporal region retained complexity modulation. A more anterior frontal region was recruited, but that recruitment did not support successful syntactic processing. This separates the presence of activation from normal computational responsiveness. The small, heterogeneous sample and altered structure limit inferences from a missing contrast.[Journal of Neuroscience 30, 16845–16854; sentence-picture matching, syntactic complexity and response-time modulation in eight patients](../psychology-bibliography.md#neural-correlates-of-syntactic-processing-in-the-nonfluent-variant-of-primary-progressive-aphasia-by-stephen-m-wilson-and-others)

The subsequent tract study added a network constraint. In 27 patients, damage measured with diffusion imaging in the left superior longitudinal/arcuate system was associated with impaired syntactic comprehension and production, beyond the cortical-atrophy contribution included in the analysis. Comparable associations were not found for the ventral tracts examined. This supports the importance of dorsal frontotemporal communication under those tasks. It does not show that ventral connections never contribute to sentence meaning or that a diffusion-derived tract is exclusively syntactic.[Neuron 72, 397–403; 27 PPA patients, syntax measures, DTI and cortical-atrophy adjustment](../psychology-bibliography.md#syntactic-processing-depends-on-dorsal-language-tracts-by-stephen-m-wilson-and-others)

These findings help distinguish a network-disconnection account from a narrowly local account: surviving frontal cortex may fail because temporal inputs or long-range interactions are disrupted. They also motivate looking for separable grammatical computations rather than a single complexity variable. Hierarchical dependencies, maintaining a noun's features, assigning roles and selecting an overt response need not have identical neural requirements.

Converging evidence outside [PPA](../neuroscience.md#primary-progressive-aphasia) is necessary. [Corianne Rogalsky](../neuroscientist.md#corianne-rogalsky)'s [stroke](../neurology.md#stroke) lesion work relates comprehension to a distributed left system, and her concurrent-articulation experiments show how working-memory demands can contribute to inferior-frontal syntactic contrasts. These observations caution against treating frontal disease, reduced rehearsal and damaged [syntax](../README.md#syntax) as interchangeable. They do not erase the [PPA](../neuroscience.md#primary-progressive-aphasia) findings; they sharpen the processes the imaging contrast must explain.[2018, sentence-picture matching and plausibility tasks with lesion-symptom mapping](../psychology-bibliography.md#the-neurobiology-of-agrammatic-sentence-comprehension-by-corianne-rogalsky-and-others)[Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 2, 14; concurrent articulation and finger tapping during syntactic contrasts](../psychology-bibliography.md#broca-s-area-sentence-comprehension-and-working-memory-by-corianne-rogalsky-and-others)

[PPA](../neuroscience.md#primary-progressive-aphasia) also has characteristic limitations. [Atrophy](../biology.md#atrophy) covaries across connected areas and worsens with disease severity; a regional [correlation](../variance.md#pearson-correlation-coefficient) can reflect a broader disease gradient. Clinical groups may be defined partly by the impairment under investigation, so rediscovering that difference is not independent confirmation. Grey-matter volume and [fractional anisotropy](../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#fractional-anisotropy) are indirect measurements, while altered [perfusion](../physiology.md#perfusion) or tissue loss affects the observed [fMRI](../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#functional-magnetic-resonance-imaging) signal. Statistical adjustment for one anatomical variable cannot guarantee complete separation of these causes.

Longitudinal within-person assessments, repeated controls, individual functional localisation and analyses of both structural and functional connections would strengthen the account. A decisive interpretation should predict which construction becomes impaired, which simpler operations remain intact and how the deficit changes with progression. **[PPA](../neuroscience.md#primary-progressive-aphasia) has made [syntax](../README.md#syntax) a tractable network-level target by dissociating components and revealing dorsal connectivity requirements; it supports process-specific, convergent explanations rather than a diagnostic label or isolated grammar centre.**

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**There is no secure, universal [bilingual advantage](../animal-behaviour.md#bilingual-advantage) in domain-general [executive function](../psychology.md#executive-function). There are important benefits of knowing and using two [languages](../README.md#language), some positive task findings and suggestive associations with [cognitive reserve](../neuroscience.md#cognitive-reserve), but these are different claims with different evidential requirements.** [Bilingualism](../animal-behaviour.md#bilingualism) varies with proficiency, acquisition age, frequency of use and the situations in which [languages](../README.md#language) are switched. A binary label can obscure the experience most relevant to a proposed mechanism.

The executive-control [hypothesis](../README.md#hypothesis) begins with competition: using one [language](../README.md#language) while another is available may repeatedly exercise selection and interference control. Transfer to nonlinguistic tasks is plausible, but it is a far-transfer claim, not an automatic consequence of speaking two [languages](../README.md#language). The account should predict which control operation improves and under which switching experiences, rather than count every faster response as evidence of inhibition.

[Ellen Bialystok](../psychologist.md#ellen-bialystok)'s three studies of middle-aged and older adults reported smaller Simon interference costs in bilingual participants, with stronger effects in older groups and demanding conditions. In the [Simon task](../psychology.md#simon-task), the required response depends on a relevant stimulus feature while an irrelevant spatial location can favour the competing response. Comparing congruent and incongruent trials operationalises conflict. These positive results support a conditional association; nonrandom [language](../README.md#language) histories leave socioeconomic, educational and other selection explanations to be tested.[Psychology and Aging 19, 290–303; three studies of middle-aged/older adults, congruency costs and working-memory demands](../psychology-bibliography.md#bilingualism-aging-and-cognitive-control-evidence-from-the-simon-task-by-ellen-bialystok-and-others)

The relevant estimand must be stated. Let $C_g=\overline{RT}_{g,\mathrm{incongruent}}-\overline{RT}_{g,\mathrm{congruent}}$ for group $g$. A conflict advantage requires $C_{\mathrm{bilingual}}<C_{\mathrm{monolingual}}$, which is a group-by-congruency interaction. Illustratively, monolingual means of $(500,550)$ ms and bilingual means of $(450,500)$ ms for congruent/incongruent trials give equal costs of $50$ ms. The bilingual group is faster overall, but this example supplies no selective conflict advantage. Accuracy and [speed-accuracy trade-offs](../psychology.md#speed-accuracy-trade-off) must also be examined. These numbers are a calculation, not observed study data.

Negative studies are therefore informative rather than disposable exceptions. [Kenneth R. Paap](../psychologist.md#kenneth-r-paap) and colleagues tested 15 executive-processing indicators across three studies, including Simon, flanker, antisaccade and switching tasks, and did not find a coherent bilingual benefit. Inconsistent cross-task [correlations](../variance.md#pearson-correlation-coefficient) also challenged treating every task's interference score as a stable measure of one general control faculty. This does not prove an exactly zero effect for every bilingual population, but it contradicts a broad, easily expressed advantage.[Cognitive Psychology 66, 232–258; three studies, 15 executive-processing indicators, cross-task validity](../psychology-bibliography.md#there-is-no-coherent-evidence-for-a-bilingual-advantage-in-executive-processing-by-kenneth-r-paap-and-others)

Child data also constrain generality. [Eneko Antón](../psychologist.md#eneko-anton)'s study used the [Attention Network Test](../psychology.md#attention-network-test) in bilingual and monolingual children with careful [attention](../psychology.md#attention) to age and socioeconomic variables, and found no [bilingual advantage](../animal-behaviour.md#bilingual-advantage) in the principal attentional measures. A reliable conflict effect within participants verifies that a task manipulation worked; it does not guarantee reliable [individual differences](../psychology.md#individual-differences) or a sensitive group contrast. [Measurement reliability](../psychology.md#measurement-reliability) and adequate [statistical power](../probability-and-statistics.md#statistical-power) matter to both positive and negative interpretations.[Frontiers in Psychology 5, 398; child Attention Network Test, socioeconomic and age controls](../psychology-bibliography.md#is-there-a-bilingual-advantage-in-the-ant-task-evidence-from-children-by-eneko-anton-and-others)

Selective reporting can make the literature appear more consistent than the completed research. [Angela de Bruin](../psychologist.md#angela-de-bruin) and colleagues followed conference abstracts into publication and found that results supporting an advantage were more likely to be published than null or opposing results. This is direct evidence about the research pipeline. It does not invalidate every positive experiment or by itself estimate the true average effect. Preregistration, shared outcomes and publication of null results address the identified bias.[Psychological Science 26, 99–107; conference abstracts 1999–2012 and subsequent publication](../psychology-bibliography.md#cognitive-advantage-in-bilingualism-an-example-of-publication-bias-by-angela-de-bruin-and-others)

A different question concerns later clinical symptoms. [Suvarna Alladi](../neurology.md#suvarna-alladi)'s retrospective clinic series contained 648 people with [dementia](../neurology.md#dementia), including 391 bilingual participants, and reported symptom onset about 4.5 years later in the bilingual group after considering several covariates. The result is compatible with [cognitive reserve](../neuroscience.md#cognitive-reserve). It is not evidence that [bilingualism](../animal-behaviour.md#bilingualism) reduced [dementia](../neurology.md#dementia) incidence: all participants already had [dementia](../neurology.md#dementia), and referral, survival, recalled onset and unmeasured life experiences can affect the comparison. Later symptoms also do not necessarily imply less underlying pathology.[Neurology 81, 1938–1944; retrospective specialist-clinic series, 648 patients, 391 bilingual](../psychology-bibliography.md#bilingualism-delays-age-at-onset-of-dementia-independent-of-education-and-immigration-status-by-suvarna-alladi-and-others)

A stronger test would measure [language](../README.md#language) experience continuously, recruit groups from the same communities, prespecify a small set of well-measured outcomes and analyse multiple tasks with an explicit measurement model. Longitudinal studies with baseline cognitive performance reduce selection ambiguity; randomised language-learning interventions with [active controls](../design-of-experiments.md#active-control) test narrower training effects, though short courses do not reproduce lifelong bilingual experience.

Finally, linguistic communication and cultural access are substantive benefits regardless of executive-task results. [Metalinguistic awareness](../animal-behaviour.md#metalinguistic-awareness) and language-specific proficiency are closer to the practised skill than general inhibition, and should be evaluated as separate outcomes. **[Bilingualism](../animal-behaviour.md#bilingualism) is valuable, but the evidence warrants specific, experience- and task-dependent claims rather than a universal cognitive enhancement or a promise of [dementia](../neurology.md#dementia) prevention.**

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [2022](2022.md)

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**[Sign languages](../README.md#sign-language) recruit much of the left-dominant frontotemporal [language](../README.md#language) network while also engaging modality-dependent visual, parietal and motor systems. The evidence supports shared organisation across modalities more strongly than it proves that the same neural populations contain fully amodal phonological, semantic and syntactic codes.** Natural [sign languages](../README.md#sign-language) have conventional sublexical structure and grammar; they are not simply gestures illustrating spoken words.

Brain-lesion evidence provides an important starting point. In [Gregory Hickok](../neuroscientist.md#gregory-hickok)'s study, 19 lifelong signers with unilateral lesions completed single-sign and simple/complex [sentence comprehension](../neuroscience.md#sentence-comprehension) tasks. Left temporal involvement was associated with substantial impairment across tasks; right-lesion and other groups performed well on the simpler conditions, with more difficulty on complex material. The result argues against assigning [language](../README.md#language) to the right hemisphere merely because signs are visual and spatial. Complex-command performance can also reflect memory, spatial processing or general comprehension demands.[Brain and Language 82, 167–178; 19 lifelong signers with unilateral lesions and three comprehension tasks](../psychology-bibliography.md#role-of-the-left-hemisphere-in-sign-language-comprehension-by-gregory-hickok-and-others)

Functional imaging identifies both common and distinct components. [Karen Emmorey](../neuroscientist.md#karen-emmorey) and colleagues compared sign and [speech](../README.md#speech) within native [hearing](../neuroscience.md#hearing) [ASL](../README.md#american-sign-language)–English bilinguals using [PET](../electrochemistry.md#photoinduced-electron-transfer) for picture naming and [fMRI](../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#functional-magnetic-resonance-imaging) for [sentence comprehension](../neuroscience.md#sentence-comprehension). Shared language-related responses coexisted with greater parietal recruitment for signing and different temporal responses for [speech](../README.md#speech). The within-person comparison reduces [confounding](../causal-inference.md#confounding) between [hearing](../neuroscience.md#hearing) status and [language](../README.md#language) modality. It also makes clear that motor output, visual movement and auditory feedback contribute to the total activation pattern.[Frontiers in Psychology 5, 484; within-native-bimodal-bilingual PET naming and fMRI semantic-anomaly detection](../psychology-bibliography.md#how-sensory-motor-systems-impact-the-neural-organization-for-language-direct-contrasts-between-spoken-and-signed-language-by-karen-emmorey-and-others)

An [amodal representation](../neuroscience.md#amodal-representation) is stronger than a common anatomical location: it should capture relevant information independently of how that information enters or leaves the system. A shared inferior-frontal mean response can arise from common control demands, different intermingled populations or the same computation over differently formatted representations. This is the distinction expressed by [regional activation does not identify a neural code](../neuroscience.md#regional-activation-does-not-identify-a-neural-code). Conjunction of significant maps is useful evidence for shared territory, but cannot by itself establish identity of the represented information.

For [phonology](../README.md#phonology), the relevant units need not be sounds. A sign can be distinguished by [handshape](../README.md#handshape), location and movement, just as [speech](../README.md#speech) forms can differ in sublexical features. [Mairéad MacSweeney](../neuroscientist.md#mairead-macsweeney)'s study compared [BSL](../README.md#british-sign-language) location judgments with English rhyme judgments using picture-based tasks. A partly shared left frontoparietal network was modulated by [language](../README.md#language), [hearing](../neuroscience.md#hearing) status and age of first acquisition. Thus sublexical form analysis can share network resources across modalities, while the differences argue against an entirely invariant implementation. Delayed access to a first [language](../README.md#language) must be separated from [deafness](../neuroscience.md#deafness) itself.[NeuroImage 40, 1369–1379; English rhyme versus BSL location judgments, hearing status and acquisition age](../psychology-bibliography.md#phonological-processing-in-deaf-signers-and-the-impact-of-age-of-first-language-acquisition-by-mairead-macsweeney-and-others)

A stronger phonological test would match response selection and difficulty, contrast legal with carefully controlled sublexical combinations, and include nonlinguistic hand-movement judgments. Even successful [cross-modal decoding](../statistical-learning.md#cross-modal-decoding) would need interpretation: sign [handshape](../README.md#handshape) and spoken sound have no automatic one-to-one physical mapping. An abstract combinatorial relation could be shared without every modality-specific feature having an amodal counterpart.

For [semantics](../README.md#semantics), signs and spoken words can access the same conceptual relations while having different forms. Emmorey's within-bilingual comprehension experiment used detection of semantic anomalies and identified overlapping frontotemporal responses with modality-dependent components. This supports common meaning-related processing, although anomaly detection also requires monitoring and a decision. A more specific test would train a decoder on one modality and test concept identity in the other, holding out stimuli and separating meaning from the picture, response or decision used to measure it.[2014, within-bilingual fMRI semantic-anomaly detection and conjunction/direct contrasts](../psychology-bibliography.md#how-sensory-motor-systems-impact-the-neural-organization-for-language-direct-contrasts-between-spoken-and-signed-language-by-karen-emmorey-and-others)

For [syntax](../README.md#syntax), [Kuniyoshi L. Sakai](../neuroscientist.md#kuniyoshi-sakai)'s comparison of [Japanese Sign Language](../README.md#japanese-sign-language) and Japanese sentences found common left-dominant frontal and temporoparietal recruitment for comprehension. Comparing [sentence comprehension](../neuroscience.md#sentence-comprehension) with sentential nonword detection improved specificity beyond passive sign viewing. Different participant groups and potentially different demands nevertheless prevent inferring identical neuronal [syntax](../README.md#syntax) codes. Spatial referent tracking, prosody and grammatical relations may draw on partly overlapping resources rather than a single modality-free operation.[Brain 128, 1407–1417; JSL/Japanese comprehension versus sentential non-word detection across Deaf/CODA/hearing groups](../psychology-bibliography.md#sign-and-speech-amodal-commonality-in-left-hemisphere-dominance-for-comprehension-of-sentences-by-kuniyoshi-l-sakai-and-others)

The best design combines native Deaf signers, [hearing](../neuroscience.md#hearing) native signers and [hearing](../neuroscience.md#hearing) nonsigners where appropriate, with careful measurement of first-language access and proficiency. Nonlinguistic biological motion, matched visual complexity, meaningful versus meaningless sequences and matched response demands help identify what each contrast removes. Individual functional localisation, lesion convergence and cross-modal multivariate tests are stronger together than any activation map alone. A failed decoder does not establish separate representations if measurement noise or anatomical alignment prevents generalisation.

**The clearest conclusion is a [language](../README.md#language) network that can develop for signed or spoken input, with left temporal and inferior-frontal contributions to linguistic processing and modality-sensitive interfaces. Amodal conceptual or combinatorial operations are plausible; exact representational identity for every component remains a stronger, incompletely established claim.**

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [2022](2022.md)

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**Right-hemisphere activity after left-hemisphere damage can be compensatory, inefficiently helpful, maladaptive or incidental. Its role depends on the region, task, residual left network and recovery stage; a group activation map cannot decide among those possibilities.** [Functional compensation](../neuroscience.md#functional-compensation) means that the recruitment supports performance in the damaged system. It need not restore normal performance or reproduce the computation formerly performed on the left.

Longitudinal evidence immediately challenges a uniform interpretation. [Dorothee Saur](../neuroscientist.md#dorothee-saur) and colleagues repeatedly imaged auditory comprehension in 14 patients after left middle-cerebral-artery [stroke](../neurology.md#stroke). Initially weak activation was followed by increased bilateral activity with a prominent right inferior-frontal homologue response, and later by a shift towards left [language](../README.md#language) areas alongside further recovery. The trajectory makes time after [stroke](../neurology.md#stroke) central to interpretation. [Correlation](../variance.md#pearson-correlation-coefficient) between activation and improvement is compatible with a contribution to recovery, but can also reflect restored capacity to engage with the task.[Brain 129, 1371–1384; 14 patients, auditory comprehension and fMRI at three recovery stages](../psychology-bibliography.md#dynamics-of-language-reorganization-after-stroke-by-dorothee-saur-and-others)

A decrease in left dominance is particularly ambiguous. The [laterality index](../neuroscience.md#laterality-index) $(L-R)/(L+R)$ can fall because left activity falls, without right activity increasing. Acute [ischemia](../biology.md#ischemia), [diaschisis](../neurology.md#diaschisis) and altered [neurovascular coupling](../neurology.md#neurovascular-coupling) also change the [fMRI](../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#functional-magnetic-resonance-imaging) signal. Matching the task instruction is insufficient if one patient comprehends the sentences and another only attempts them. Accuracy, response time and an independent physiological assessment should therefore accompany the activation contrast.

A maladaptive account proposes that increased activity in a right homologue interferes with useful remaining left function or promotes an inefficient strategy. [Interhemispheric inhibition](../neuroscience.md#interhemispheric-inhibition) is one candidate mechanism, but cannot be assumed from the fact that the two [cerebral hemispheres](../neuroscience.md#cerebral-hemisphere) change differently. Greater right activation among more severely impaired patients could mean that right activity causes impairment; equally, larger left lesions may both worsen performance and make right support more necessary. Statistical adjustment must account for lesion location and disconnection, not simply lesion volume.

Perturbation supplies a more direct test. [Lutz Winhuisen](../neurology.md#lutz-winhuisen) and colleagues used [PET](../electrochemistry.md#photoinduced-electron-transfer) to select inferior-frontal targets and then disrupted their activity during a semantic task in 11 patients examined within two weeks of [stroke](../neurology.md#stroke). Right-sided stimulation impaired performance in a subset of patients with right IFG activation. This supports a necessary contribution to residual performance in those individuals, even though their [language](../README.md#language) remained less effective than in patients relying more on recovered left function. The result is not evidence that every activated right region is beneficial.[Stroke 36, 1759–1763; 11 patients within two weeks, PET-guided left/right IFG perturbation](../psychology-bibliography.md#role-of-the-contralateral-inferior-frontal-gyrus-in-recovery-of-language-function-in-poststroke-aphasia-by-lutz-winhuisen-and-others)

Evidence sometimes presented for maladaptation also needs qualification. [Margaret A. Naeser](../neuroscientist.md#margaret-a-naeser)'s open study applied repeated right inferior-frontal stimulation to four people with chronic [aphasia](../neuroscience.md#aphasia) and reported later picture-naming improvement. Suppression-associated improvement is compatible with a counterproductive component of right activity. Without a randomised sham comparator, however, practice, expectancy and nonspecific intervention effects remain possible; the result is preliminary, not a general treatment rule. An anterior target in chronic [aphasia](../neuroscience.md#aphasia) also need not have the same function as a different inferior-frontal site early after [stroke](../neurology.md#stroke).[Brain and Language 93, 95–105; four patients 5–11 years poststroke, open protocol and naming follow-up](../psychology-bibliography.md#improved-picture-naming-in-chronic-aphasia-after-tms-to-part-of-right-broca-s-area-by-margaret-a-naeser-and-others)

A proposed mechanistic study would recruit a sufficiently characterised [stroke](../neurology.md#stroke) cohort across prespecified stages, quantify left cortical and tract damage, and measure baseline naming, comprehension and connected [speech](../README.md#speech). Use individual structural imaging and independent functional localisation to identify a right language-related target and a control target. Because an activated voxel can contain mixed functions, site selection should use a relevant contrast, not just the maximum response to any difficult task.

Within each participant, counterbalance a transient perturbation condition with credible sham and an [active control](../design-of-experiments.md#active-control), use masked assessment, parallel stimulus sets and a prespecified primary [language](../README.md#language) outcome. Include nonlinguistic reaction and motor tasks to detect general distraction or response slowing. Session spacing should address carryover, and the analysis should model repeated measurements and interactions with stage and residual left-network integrity. Safety screening and consent are integral to a clinical stimulation study; a [sample size](../probability-and-statistics.md#sample-size) should be justified from variability and the smallest informative effect, not invented from the exam prompt.

If perturbing the right target worsens [language](../README.md#language) selectively relative to both controls, that supports a currently compensatory role. If it improves [language](../README.md#language) selectively and reproducibly, that supports a maladaptive contribution under those conditions. No behavioural change is inconclusive unless the perturbation was effective and the outcome sensitive. The intervention could spread through the network, so neither sign of effect automatically identifies a local code or proves inhibition of the left hemisphere.

Finally, a treatment trial asks a different question from transient necessity. Randomise a longer intervention against sham with matched speech-language therapy, assess untreated items and everyday communication, and follow durability. Repeated imaging can test whether behavioural improvement accompanies a predicted network change, while avoiding circular selection of patients or outcomes on the same data. **The evidence favours conditional, stage- and site-specific right-hemisphere contributions; perturbation with appropriate controls, followed by a separate clinical trial, can distinguish useful [functional compensation](../neuroscience.md#functional-compensation) from harmful recruitment more convincingly than activation alone.**
