<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

**[Sign languages](../../../sign-language.md) recruit much of the left-dominant frontotemporal [language](../../../language.md) network while also engaging modality-dependent visual, parietal and motor systems. The evidence supports shared organisation across modalities more strongly than it proves that the same neural populations contain fully amodal phonological, semantic and syntactic codes.** Natural [sign languages](../../../sign-language.md) have conventional sublexical structure and grammar; they are not simply gestures illustrating spoken words.

Brain-lesion evidence provides an important starting point. In [Gregory Hickok](../../../gregory-hickok.md)'s study, 19 lifelong signers with unilateral lesions completed single-sign and simple/complex [sentence comprehension](../../../sentence-comprehension.md) tasks. Left temporal involvement was associated with substantial impairment across tasks; right-lesion and other groups performed well on the simpler conditions, with more difficulty on complex material. The result argues against assigning [language](../../../language.md) to the right hemisphere merely because signs are visual and spatial. Complex-command performance can also reflect memory, spatial processing or general comprehension demands.[Brain and Language 82, 167–178; 19 lifelong signers with unilateral lesions and three comprehension tasks](../../../role-of-the-left-hemisphere-in-sign-language-comprehension-by-gregory-hickok-and-others.md)

Functional imaging identifies both common and distinct components. [Karen Emmorey](../../../karen-emmorey.md) and colleagues compared sign and [speech](../../../speech.md) within native [hearing](../../../hearing.md) [ASL](../../../american-sign-language.md)–English bilinguals using [PET](../../../photoinduced-electron-transfer.md) for picture naming and [fMRI](../../../functional-magnetic-resonance-imaging.md) for [sentence comprehension](../../../sentence-comprehension.md). Shared language-related responses coexisted with greater parietal recruitment for signing and different temporal responses for [speech](../../../speech.md). The within-person comparison reduces [confounding](../../../confounding.md) between [hearing](../../../hearing.md) status and [language](../../../language.md) modality. It also makes clear that motor output, visual movement and auditory feedback contribute to the total activation pattern.[Frontiers in Psychology 5, 484; within-native-bimodal-bilingual PET naming and fMRI semantic-anomaly detection](../../../how-sensory-motor-systems-impact-the-neural-organization-for-language-direct-contrasts-between-spoken-and-signed-language-by-karen-emmorey-and-others.md)

An [amodal representation](../../../amodal-representation.md) is stronger than a common anatomical location: it should capture relevant information independently of how that information enters or leaves the system. A shared inferior-frontal mean response can arise from common control demands, different intermingled populations or the same computation over differently formatted representations. This is the distinction expressed by [regional activation does not identify a neural code](../../../regional-activation-does-not-identify-a-neural-code.md). Conjunction of significant maps is useful evidence for shared territory, but cannot by itself establish identity of the represented information.

For [phonology](../../../phonology.md), the relevant units need not be sounds. A sign can be distinguished by [handshape](../../../handshape.md), location and movement, just as [speech](../../../speech.md) forms can differ in sublexical features. [Mairéad MacSweeney](../../../mairead-macsweeney.md)'s study compared [BSL](../../../british-sign-language.md) location judgments with English rhyme judgments using picture-based tasks. A partly shared left frontoparietal network was modulated by [language](../../../language.md), [hearing](../../../hearing.md) status and age of first acquisition. Thus sublexical form analysis can share network resources across modalities, while the differences argue against an entirely invariant implementation. Delayed access to a first [language](../../../language.md) must be separated from [deafness](../../../deafness.md) itself.[NeuroImage 40, 1369–1379; English rhyme versus BSL location judgments, hearing status and acquisition age](../../../phonological-processing-in-deaf-signers-and-the-impact-of-age-of-first-language-acquisition-by-mairead-macsweeney-and-others.md)

A stronger phonological test would match response selection and difficulty, contrast legal with carefully controlled sublexical combinations, and include nonlinguistic hand-movement judgments. Even successful [cross-modal decoding](../../../cross-modal-decoding.md) would need interpretation: sign [handshape](../../../handshape.md) and spoken sound have no automatic one-to-one physical mapping. An abstract combinatorial relation could be shared without every modality-specific feature having an amodal counterpart.

For [semantics](../../../semantics.md), signs and spoken words can access the same conceptual relations while having different forms. Emmorey's within-bilingual comprehension experiment used detection of semantic anomalies and identified overlapping frontotemporal responses with modality-dependent components. This supports common meaning-related processing, although anomaly detection also requires monitoring and a decision. A more specific test would train a decoder on one modality and test concept identity in the other, holding out stimuli and separating meaning from the picture, response or decision used to measure it.[2014, within-bilingual fMRI semantic-anomaly detection and conjunction/direct contrasts](../../../how-sensory-motor-systems-impact-the-neural-organization-for-language-direct-contrasts-between-spoken-and-signed-language-by-karen-emmorey-and-others.md)

For [syntax](../../../syntax.md), [Kuniyoshi L. Sakai](../../../kuniyoshi-sakai.md)'s comparison of [Japanese Sign Language](../../../japanese-sign-language.md) and Japanese sentences found common left-dominant frontal and temporoparietal recruitment for comprehension. Comparing [sentence comprehension](../../../sentence-comprehension.md) with sentential nonword detection improved specificity beyond passive sign viewing. Different participant groups and potentially different demands nevertheless prevent inferring identical neuronal [syntax](../../../syntax.md) codes. Spatial referent tracking, prosody and grammatical relations may draw on partly overlapping resources rather than a single modality-free operation.[Brain 128, 1407–1417; JSL/Japanese comprehension versus sentential non-word detection across Deaf/CODA/hearing groups](../../../sign-and-speech-amodal-commonality-in-left-hemisphere-dominance-for-comprehension-of-sentences-by-kuniyoshi-l-sakai-and-others.md)

The best design combines native Deaf signers, [hearing](../../../hearing.md) native signers and [hearing](../../../hearing.md) nonsigners where appropriate, with careful measurement of first-language access and proficiency. Nonlinguistic biological motion, matched visual complexity, meaningful versus meaningless sequences and matched response demands help identify what each contrast removes. Individual functional localisation, lesion convergence and cross-modal multivariate tests are stronger together than any activation map alone. A failed decoder does not establish separate representations if measurement noise or anatomical alignment prevents generalisation.

**The clearest conclusion is a [language](../../../language.md) network that can develop for signed or spoken input, with left temporal and inferior-frontal contributions to linguistic processing and modality-sensitive interfaces. Amodal conceptual or combinatorial operations are plausible; exact representational identity for every component remains a stronger, incompletely established claim.**
