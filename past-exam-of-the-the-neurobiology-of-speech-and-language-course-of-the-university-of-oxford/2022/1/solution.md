**[Phonology](../../../phonology.md) and [syntax](../../../syntax.md) usually show a left-hemisphere bias, whereas semantic processing depends on a more bilateral system with task-dependent asymmetries. None is confined absolutely to one [cerebral hemisphere](../../../cerebral-hemisphere.md), and lateralisation of an experimental contrast is not equivalent to localisation of a representation.** [Phonology](../../../phonology.md) concerns structured sublexical units, [semantics](../../../semantics.md) concerns meaning, and [syntax](../../../syntax.md) concerns relations that organise words into sentences. These processes interact, so tasks must isolate the relevant contrast rather than merely require [language](../../../language.md).

Historical lesion observations motivated left [language](../../../language.md) dominance, but their anatomical interpretation needs care. Modern [MRI](../../../magnetorotational-instability.md) of [Paul Broca](../../../paul-broca.md)'s preserved cases showed damage extending beyond the conventional cortical region named after him. Severe impaired [speech](../../../speech.md) after a left lesion therefore supports lateralisation without proving that a small superficial patch alone constitutes the [speech](../../../speech.md) system. Large vascular lesions can also damage connections between surviving regions.[Brain 130, 1432–1441; high-resolution MRI of preserved historical brains](../../../paul-broca-s-historic-cases-by-nina-f-dronkers-and-others.md)

For phonological processing, [Robert J. Zatorre](../../../robert-j-zatorre.md) and colleagues used [PET](../../../photoinduced-electron-transfer.md) while participants judged different attributes of the same [speech](../../../speech.md) material. Phonetic discrimination increased left inferior-frontal activity, whereas pitch discrimination recruited right prefrontal regions; acoustically matched [speech](../../../speech.md) engaged secondary auditory cortices bilaterally. Holding the signal relatively constant while changing the decision supports functional differentiation beyond a contrast between unrelated stimuli. It does not show that all phonological representations reside in inferior frontal cortex: articulatory recoding, [attention](../../../attention.md) and decision demands can contribute to the phonetic contrast.[Science 256, 846–849; ten-volunteer PET, matched syllables and phonetic versus pitch instructions](../../../lateralization-of-phonetic-and-pitch-discrimination-in-speech-processing-by-robert-j-zatorre-and-others.md)

The distinction matters because [speech perception](../../../speech-perception.md) begins with bilateral auditory analysis, whereas selecting, sequencing or rehearsing phonological forms can have a stronger left bias. Impairment of repetition after damage to a [dorsal language pathway](../../../dorsal-language-pathway.md) and comprehension after other damage constrain different transformations. A deficit in producing a [phoneme](../../../phoneme.md) is not automatically a deficit in recognising it. Familiarity with a sound pattern and its linguistic role can also alter the task, so acoustic speed or frequency alone cannot define [phonology](../../../phonology.md).

Semantic processing illustrates why a leftward verbal-task result can conceal bilateral representational resources. In [Gorana Pobric](../../../gorana-pobric.md)'s [rTMS](../../../repetitive-transcranial-magnetic-stimulation.md) experiment, perturbing either [temporal pole](../../../temporal-pole.md) slowed [semantic associations](../../../semantic-association.md) for words and pictures while a perceptual comparison was spared; stimulation of a control site did not produce the same semantic effect. This supports a contribution from both [anterior temporal lobes](../../../anterior-temporal-lobe.md) across input formats. It does not require identical response profiles throughout both lobes or imply that semantic retrieval and selection are entirely bilateral.[Neuropsychologia 48, 1336–1342; left/right temporal-pole rTMS, verbal/pictorial semantic association and perceptual control](../../../amodal-semantic-representations-depend-on-both-anterior-temporal-lobes-by-gorana-pobric-and-others.md)

Verbal naming places additional demands on [lexical retrieval](../../../lexical-retrieval.md) and [speech production](../../../speech-production.md). Consequently, stronger left activity in naming than in passive picture viewing can reflect access and output as well as conceptual knowledge. Conversely, right-sided involvement in nonverbal meaning does not establish a separate nonlinguistic semantic store. Comparisons should separate [semantic association](../../../semantic-association.md) from perceptual similarity and control both response requirements and difficulty. Lesion and perturbation results help adjudicate whether an activated region contributes to the task.

For [syntax](../../../syntax.md), lesion studies and functional imaging converge on a predominantly left frontotemporal network, rather than a single grammar centre. [Evelina Fedorenko](../../../evelina-fedorenko.md)'s individual-localisation study showed language-selective and domain-general regions next to one another within the anatomical territory called [Broca's area](../../../broca-s-area.md). Averaging across people can merge them. Thus, an inferior-frontal response to a hard sentence may reflect [language processing](../../../language-processing.md), [domain-general control](../../../domain-general-control.md) or both; independent [functional localizers](../../../functional-localizer.md) and nonlinguistic controls improve interpretation.[Current Biology 22, 2059–2062; individual functional localization and cross-domain response profiles](../../../language-selective-and-domain-general-regions-lie-side-by-side-within-broca-s-area-by-evelina-fedorenko-and-others.md)

[Corianne Rogalsky](../../../corianne-rogalsky.md)'s [lesion study](../../../lesion-study.md) compared [sentence-picture matching](../../../sentence-picture-matching.md) and plausibility judgments in 66 patients. The distribution of syntactic-comprehension impairment implicated a wider left-hemisphere system and challenged a simple equation between [Broca's area](../../../broca-s-area.md) damage and agrammatic comprehension. Convergence across tasks is useful because picture matching also demands visual analysis and response selection, while plausibility can recruit semantic expectations. Neither behavioural label makes the task syntactically pure.[Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience 30, 234–255; 66-patient sentence-picture matching, plausibility judgment and lesion mapping](../../../the-neurobiology-of-agrammatic-sentence-comprehension-by-corianne-rogalsky-and-others.md)

[Sentence comprehension](../../../sentence-comprehension.md) in Japanese and [Japanese Sign Language](../../../japanese-sign-language.md) also showed left-dominant frontal and temporoparietal responses across sensory conditions. This directly addresses hemispheric comparisons, although comprehension versus nonword detection is not a pure assay of [syntax](../../../syntax.md).[Brain 128, 1407–1417; JSL/Japanese comprehension versus sentential non-word detection across Deaf/CODA/hearing groups](../../../sign-and-speech-amodal-commonality-in-left-hemisphere-dominance-for-comprehension-of-sentences-by-kuniyoshi-l-sakai-and-others.md)

A quantitative [laterality index](../../../laterality-index.md) can summarise an explicitly defined response. For nonnegative left and right measurements $L,R$ with $L+R>0$,

$$
\mathrm{LI}=\frac{L-R}{L+R}.
$$

It ranges from $-1$ to $1$, but depends on baseline, threshold and region selection. Responses $(20,10)$ and $(10,5)$ have the same index $1/3$ despite different total responses. If left activity falls from $20$ to $10$ while right activity remains $10$, the index becomes zero without any right recruitment. These are illustrative values, not imaging observations. Negative contrasts need a separately justified convention.

The most convincing evidence therefore combines within-person process contrasts, unilateral perturbations or lesions, and converging measurements of behaviour and neural responses. It also describes individual variation instead of assuming that every participant has the population-average pattern. **The justified conclusion is graded functional asymmetry within interacting networks: strongest left biases for many phonological and syntactic operations, bilateral conceptual contributions, and substantial dependence on task and representation level.**
