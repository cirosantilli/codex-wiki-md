# Double pendulum demonstration

↑ **Parent:** [Double pendulum](double-pendulum.md)

The University of Toronto's institutional demonstration “Chaotic Behaviour (Double Pendulum)”, activity Me-020, uses a metal stand with four leveling feet and a pendulum arm carrying a white dot. Preparation includes leveling the stand and correcting any wobble. The instructions call for holding the arm vertically upward and releasing it; the readout is the visible motion of the dot. A further comparison inserts a metal dowel in a groove on the release arm and repeats the release at the same location. Keeping the stand steady and repeating the release position provide practical controls. The page supplies no numerical [angle](angle.md) trace or calibrated timing, and does not explain the dowel's mechanical effect. It therefore documents a qualitative release demonstration, without quantitatively testing the equal-[point mass](point-mass.md) model. Its extended arms also have [moments of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md) absent from massless rods. Irregular visible motion alone does not establish [sensitive dependence on initial conditions](butterfly-effect.md).[Chaotic Behaviour (Double Pendulum), activity Me-020, Description: leveling, vertical release, white-dot readout, dowel insertion and repeated release; Apparatus](chaotic-behaviour-double-pendulum-university-of-toronto-department-of-physics.md)

A separate comparison with [normal modes of a double pendulum](normal-modes-of-a-double-pendulum.md) would require small downward releases and measurement of both [angles](angle.md) over [time](time-in-physics.md). The actual arm [masses](mass.md), [centres of mass](center-of-mass.md) and [moments of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md) would first have to enter the [Lagrangian](lagrangian.md). The upward release in this demonstration explores a different regime from [small oscillations](small-oscillation.md) about the downward [stable equilibrium](stable-equilibrium.md).
