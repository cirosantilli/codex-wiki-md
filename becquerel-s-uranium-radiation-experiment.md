<h1 id="becquerel-s-uranium-radiation-experiment">Becquerel's uranium-radiation experiment</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Radioactive decay](radioactive-decay.md)

In his 2 March 1896 report, [Henri Becquerel](henri-becquerel.md) described [uranium](uranium.md) salt crystals acting on a [photographic plate](photographic-plate.md) through opaque wrapping. A [copper](copper.md) cross weakened the exposure behind it. Plates prepared during intermittent sunshine were left in a drawer and developed on 1 March; unexpectedly strong images prompted deliberate dark tests. He placed a plate and crystals inside two opaque boxes in a drawer; one crystal was separated from the emulsion by glass. After five hours, both produced images. The separation tested transmission without direct chemical contact.[2 March 1896 communication: copper screen, delayed development, deliberate dark exposure and glass-separated crystal](sur-les-radiations-invisibles-emises-par-les-corps-phosphorescents-by-henri-becquerel.md)

The result challenged a dependence on immediate illumination. [Henri Becquerel](henri-becquerel.md) still considered long-lasting invisible [phosphorescence](phosphorescence.md) a possibility, and explicitly withheld a final explanation. The photographic readout established an effect through intervening material; it did not count nuclear transformations or measure a [nuclear decay constant](nuclear-decay-constant.md).[Final two paragraphs: tentative invisible-phosphorescence hypothesis and limits of the inference](sur-les-radiations-invisibles-emises-par-les-corps-phosphorescents-by-henri-becquerel.md)
