# Spin-triplet p-wave pairing

↑ **Parent:** [Helium-3 superfluidity](helium-3-superfluidity.md)

[Spin-triplet p-wave pairing](spin-triplet-p-wave-pairing.md) has pair [spin](spin.md) $S=1$ and orbital [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) $L=1$, allowing multiple [order parameters](order-parameter.md) beyond conventional [BCS theory](bcs-theory.md) with zero orbital [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md). [Lee, 1996 lecture; 1997 reprint p.658](the-extraordinary-phases-of-liquid-3he-by-david-morris-lee.md)

Specifying this symmetry does not determine its microscopic attraction mechanism. A short exchange-symmetry check explains why these quantum numbers fit [fermions](fermion.md). The [spin-one-half triplet states](spin-one-half-triplet-state.md) $|\uparrow\uparrow\rangle$, $(|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle+|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)/\sqrt2$ and $|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle$ are symmetric under exchange. A stipulated orbital factor proportional to relative [momentum](momentum-split.md) $k_x$ changes sign under $\mathbf k\mapsto-\mathbf k$. Multiplying any symmetric [spin](spin.md) function by that odd orbital factor gives an antisymmetric pair [wave function](wave-function-split.md), as required by [Fermi-Dirac statistics](fermi-dirac-statistics.md). By comparison, an even s-wave orbital factor needs the antisymmetric [spin singlet state](spin-singlet-state.md). This checks compatibility with exchange statistics; it neither selects a stable phase nor derives its transition [temperature](temperature-split.md).
