# Continuous function

↑ **Parent:** [Limit of a function](limit-of-a-function.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function)

A [continuous function](continuous-function.md) sends sufficiently nearby [function inputs](function-input.md) to nearby [function outputs](function-output.md). For a [real-valued function](real-valued-function.md) $f:D\to\mathbb R$ and a point $a\in D$ that is an [accumulation point](limit-point.md) of $D$, this means

$$
\lim_{\substack{x\to a\\x\in D}}f(x)=f(a).
$$

The approached value and the assigned value must agree. [Continuity](continuous-function.md) on $D$ means [continuity](continuous-function.md) at every point of the [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md). At an endpoint of an [interval](interval-mathematics.md), only approaches inside that [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) count. The [epsilon-delta definition of continuity](epsilon-delta-definition-of-continuity.md) below also applies at an isolated point, where [continuity](continuous-function.md) holds automatically.[§2.4, Continuity at a Point: definition; Continuity over an Interval: one-sided endpoint conditions](calculus-volume-1-by-gilbert-strang-and-others/calculus-volume-1-%C2%A72-4-continuity.md)

For the [polynomial function](polynomial-function.md) $f(x)=x^2$, approaching $1$ makes the [function output](function-output.md) approach $f(1)=1$. The [continuity bounds for the square function](continuity-bounds-for-the-square-function.md) turn this observation into an explicit guarantee: $|x-1|<0.2$ implies $|x^2-1|<0.6$. This is useful whenever a small error in an [function input](function-input.md) must produce a controlled error in an [function output](function-output.md). The required input tolerance can depend on the point and on the chosen output tolerance.

The [absolute value](absolute-value.md) [function](function-split.md) $g(x)=|x|$ is also a [continuous function](continuous-function.md), including at zero: $|g(x)-g(0)|=|x|$. But its left and right [derivatives](derivative.md) there are $-1$ and $1$, so it is not a [differentiable function](differentiable-function.md) at zero. [Continuity](continuous-function.md) controls values without requiring a [tangent line](tangent-line.md). Conversely, a [differentiable function](differentiable-function.md) is [continuous](continuous-function.md) wherever it is [differentiable](differentiable-function.md): if $f(a+h)-f(a)=h(f'(a)+o(1))$, the difference tends to zero with $h$.

Several useful consequences reuse this one local idea. [Addition](addition.md), [multiplication](multiplication.md) and [function composition](function-composition.md) preserve [continuity](continuous-function.md); a [quotient of functions](quotient-of-functions.md) is [continuous](continuous-function.md) where its denominator is nonzero. On an [interval](interval-mathematics.md), the [intermediate value theorem](intermediate-value-theorem.md) turns [continuity](continuous-function.md) into a guarantee that no intermediate [function output](function-output.md) is skipped. On a [compact set](compact-space.md), the [extreme value theorem](extreme-value-theorem.md) guarantees attained maximum and minimum values. Those extra hypotheses matter: [continuity](continuous-function.md) alone on an unbounded [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) does not provide a global bound or an attained [global maximum](global-maximum.md).

A [rational function](rational-function.md) such as $x\mapsto1/x$ is [continuous](continuous-function.md) on its [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) $\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$. Zero is excluded from that [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md); the missing point prevents a [continuous](continuous-function.md) extension there, but it does not make the existing map [discontinuous](discontinuity-of-a-function.md) at one of its actual inputs. Always state the [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) before judging [continuity](continuous-function.md).
