**A useful [hippocampal episodic memory model](../../../hippocampal-episodic-memory-model.md) assigns complementary computations to [competitive learning](../../../competitive-learning.md), recurrent [autoassociation](../../../autoassociative-memory.md) and feedforward [pattern association](../../../heteroassociative-memory.md).** The event must bind its participants, location and context, so that a partial cue later reinstates the appropriate conjunction. Merely classifying inputs or remembering a location is insufficient to explain an [episodic memory](../../../episodic-memory.md).

A concrete pathway begins with distributed cortical input through the [entorhinal cortex](../../../entorhinal-cortex.md). The [dentate gyrus](../../../dentate-gyrus.md) expands and sparsifies this input: strong [synaptic competition](../../../synaptic-competition.md) means that only a small fraction of [granule cells](../../../granule-cell.md) respond strongly. A winning unit or winning sparse population strengthens its active afferents through constrained [Hebbian learning](../../../hebbian-learning.md). Similar episodes can then recruit less-overlapping outputs, providing [pattern separation](../../../pattern-separation.md) before storage. Separating two nearly identical episodes is useful because a recurrent memory otherwise tends to merge their cues. The desired separation must preserve relevant conjunctions; indiscriminately randomizing the input would make later retrieval impossible.

The sparse code recruits a [hippocampal CA3](../../../hippocampal-ca3.md) ensemble. Rapid, associatively induced [long-term potentiation](../../../long-term-potentiation.md) is a candidate substrate for storing the conjunction, with its induction conditions and persistence requiring experimental constraints. Its recurrent collateral [synapses](../../../synapse.md) allow coactive elements of the event to become associated through [Hebbian learning](../../../hebbian-learning.md). One idealized centred storage rule is

$$
W_{ij}\propto\sum_\mu(\xi_i^\mu-a)(\xi_j^\mu-a),\qquad i\ne j,
$$

where $a$ is mean coding activity and $\xi^\mu$ is an event pattern. With inhibitory control maintaining sparsity, recurrent retrieval iterates $r\leftarrow\phi(Wr+c-\theta)$ from cue $c$. A sufficiently informative partial cue can move activity into the stored event's [basin of attraction](../../../basin-of-attraction.md); recurrent reinstatement supplies missing components. This is [pattern completion](../../../pattern-completion.md). A [Hopfield network](../../../hopfield-network.md) makes the principle precise in a symmetric idealization with an [energy landscape](../../../energy-landscape.md), but biological [CA3](../../../hippocampal-ca3.md) connectivity need not obey the exact symmetry or update assumptions of [asynchronous Hopfield energy descent](../../../asynchronous-hopfield-energy-descent.md). Interference, spurious states and an incorrect initial cue can all produce erroneous completion.

The [hippocampal CA1](../../../hippocampal-ca1.md) and cortical return pathways provide [heteroassociative memory](../../../heteroassociative-memory.md). During the event, [CA3](../../../hippocampal-ca3.md) activity $\xi^\mu$ is paired with a cortical or [CA1](../../../hippocampal-ca1.md) target $z^\mu$, giving an illustrative mapping $A=\sum_\mu z^\mu(\xi^\mu)^{\mathsf T}$. When [CA3](../../../hippocampal-ca3.md) completes the event, $A\widehat\xi$ reinstates its associated output representation. Thus [competitive learning](../../../competitive-learning.md) distinguishes inputs, [autoassociation](../../../autoassociative-memory.md) restores a stored conjunction, and [pattern association](../../../heteroassociative-memory.md) translates the retrieved conjunction into content usable elsewhere. These are separable operations even if a real circuit performs them concurrently. [CA1](../../../hippocampal-ca1.md) also receives direct entorhinal information, so it cannot be treated as a passive relay without testing the contribution of that second input.

There is mechanistically relevant evidence beyond anatomical resemblance. In adult mice, CA3-restricted [NMDA receptor](../../../nmda-receptor.md) deletion spared ordinary acquisition and retrieval of water-maze reference memory with the full cue set, but impaired retrieval when only some cues remained. [CA1](../../../hippocampal-ca1.md) place-related activity also deteriorated under partial-cue conditions. Full-cue performance is an important control against interpreting the deficit as a general inability to swim, see cues or express a learned response. This fits the prediction that recurrent associative plasticity is particularly important for [pattern completion](../../../pattern-completion.md). Nevertheless, the deletion affects receptor-dependent processing throughout the targeted [CA3](../../../hippocampal-ca3.md) cells, and does not isolate only recurrent collateral plasticity or the exact storage rule.[Abstract; pp. 211–218, adult CA3-restricted deletion and full-cue versus partial-cue recall](../../../requirement-for-hippocampal-ca3-nmda-receptors-in-associative-memory-recall-by-kazu-nakazawa-and-others.md)

A separate delayed matching-to-place experiment found impaired rapid acquisition of novel platform locations after CA3-restricted [NMDA receptor](../../../nmda-receptor.md) deletion, with previously experienced locations relatively spared. That extends the interpretation from partial-cue recall to rapid storage of new experiences; it also cautions against assigning [CA3](../../../hippocampal-ca3.md) an exclusively retrieval-related role.[Abstract; pp. 305–315, delayed matching-to-place and novel versus familiar platform locations](../../../hippocampal-ca3-nmda-receptors-are-crucial-for-memory-acquisition-of-one-time-experience-by-kazu-nakazawa-and-others.md)

Simultaneous [dentate gyrus](../../../dentate-gyrus.md) and [hippocampal CA3](../../../hippocampal-ca3.md) recordings offer a more direct input–output comparison. In behaving rats, conflict between local and global spatial reference frames disrupted dentate population patterns, while [CA3](../../../hippocampal-ca3.md) maintained a more coherent representation related to the stored map. Comparing the two populations under the same manipulation supports the complementary [pattern separation](../../../pattern-separation.md) and [pattern completion](../../../pattern-completion.md) computations more specifically than finding different firing rates in different areas. It still does not prove that a particular recalled human episode corresponds to one fixed [CA3](../../../hippocampal-ca3.md) attractor.[Abstract; simultaneous DG/[CA3](../../../hippocampal-ca3.md) recordings during local/global cue conflict](../../../ca3-retrieves-coherent-representations-from-degraded-input-direct-evidence-for-ca3-pattern-completion-and-dentate-gyrus-pattern-separation-by-joshua-p-neunuebel-and-others.md)

**The evidence supports the computational division of labour, but the simplest implementation is incomplete.** The same region can separate strongly changed inputs and complete moderately degraded ones; separation and completion are operating regimes, not immutable labels for every trial. Fast storage must coexist with protection of old memories, and recurrent completion must not overwrite a new but similar event. Novelty, inhibition and [synaptic plasticity](../../../synaptic-plasticity.md) regulation are therefore functional requirements, rather than arbitrary additions. Experimental interventions can affect acquisition, retrieval, exploration and sensory input differently, so their timing and behavioural controls matter.

An unordered fixed-point memory also omits the temporal structure of an episode. The same people in the same place at different times need contextual or sequential representations; an attractor for a bag of features does not distinguish reversed event order. Rehearsal and longer-term cortical learning may change the dependence on the [hippocampus](../../../hippocampus.md). The theory should consequently predict partial-cue success, lure errors, interference and pathway-specific deficits across time, while acknowledging that neither a spatial task nor a correlation in population activity establishes the whole of [episodic memory](../../../episodic-memory.md). **The combined architecture supplies an explicit storage-and-retrieval mechanism, with experimentally supported components and clear remaining requirements.**
