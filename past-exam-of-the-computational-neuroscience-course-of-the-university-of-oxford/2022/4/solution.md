↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

**[Trace learning](../../../trace-learning.md) binds views through temporal continuity; [continuous transformation learning](../../../continuous-transformation-learning.md) binds views through overlap in their active visual features. Both need [competitive learning](../../../competitive-learning.md) to preserve identity selectivity while increasing tolerance to viewpoint.** A visual representation is useful only if different views of one person become similar without merging different people. [View-invariant object recognition](../../../view-invariant-object-recognition.md) requires selectivity as well as tolerance. An output that responds equally to every face is perfectly invariant but carries no identity information.

Consider a model of the [ventral visual stream](../../../ventral-visual-stream.md) consisting of local [feedforward neural networks](../../../feedforward-neural-network.md) with successively larger [receptive fields](../../../receptive-field.md). Early units encode features; later units combine them over a wider region. Within a layer, [synaptic competition](../../../synaptic-competition.md) selects a small number of strongly active outputs and [synaptic normalization](../../../synaptic-normalization.md) controls weight growth. Let $x_j(t)$ be current input activity and $y_i(t)$ the competitive output. A concrete [trace learning](../../../trace-learning.md) implementation is

$$
\bar y_i(t)=\rho\bar y_i(t-1)+(1-\rho)y_i(t),\qquad\Delta w_{ij}=\eta\bar y_i(t-1)x_j(t),\quad0<\rho<1,
$$

followed by [synaptic normalization](../../../synaptic-normalization.md). Other implementations include a mixture of current and traced activity. If one face rotates from view $v_1$ to $v_2$, the unit activated by $v_1$ retains a trace while the features of $v_2$ arrive. Those features strengthen onto the same unit. Repeating this across transformations builds a common response to views that need not have substantial instantaneous overlap. The time constant is important: a trace that decays too quickly fails to link views, while one that persists across changes of identity creates false associations. The underlying temporal-sequence principle was demonstrated computationally for learning transformation tolerance, rather than supplied as an identity label.[Abstract; Neural Computation 3, pp. 194–200, transformation-sequence learning](../../../learning-invariance-from-transformation-sequences-by-peter-foldiak.md)

[Continuous transformation learning](../../../continuous-transformation-learning.md) can use an ordinary current-activity [Hebbian learning](../../../hebbian-learning.md) rule, $\Delta w_{ij}=\eta y_i(t)x_j(t)$, together with competition and normalization. Suppose adjacent views activate overlapping input features. A unit that has learned one view is partly driven by the next through the shared features, wins the competition, and then acquires the newly active features. Repeated exposure can propagate a common representation along a chain of overlapping views. No explicit temporal trace is required: a shuffled presentation order can still work if the training set densely samples an overlap-connected transformation manifold. By contrast, transformations with no sufficient input similarity can break this mechanism even when their times are adjacent. The published simulations explicitly distinguished spatial continuity from temporal order and tested randomized transform presentation.[Abstract and Introduction, pp. 128–131; experiments 3–4 distinguish spatial overlap from temporal ordering](../../../learning-invariant-object-recognition-in-the-visual-system-with-continuous-transformations-by-simon-m-stringer-and-others.md)

A small constructed example makes the distinction concrete. Let three views have active feature sets $\{1,2\}$, $\{2,3\}$ and $\{3,4\}$. Normalized [competitive learning](../../../competitive-learning.md) can let the common feature in each neighbouring pair recruit the same output and strengthen its responses to the new feature. The first and third views need not overlap directly. In contrast, two disjoint views could be linked by [trace learning](../../../trace-learning.md) if presented close together while the first output's trace persists. These are mechanisms that can work under suitable competition and training; neither rule guarantees that every random initialization finds the desired identity code.

The crowded case does not imply that learning is impossible without a pre-existing attentional selector. Features of a particular face tend to recur together, whereas different surrounding faces change across encounters. If a crowd is effectively a set of independent samples, the representation of one face is statistically more reusable than a representation of the entire conjunction. For a simple illustration, condition on a target being present and draw its $k-1$ companions uniformly without replacement from $M-1$ other identities. A particular distractor then co-occurs with the target with [probability](../../../probability.md) $(k-1)/(M-1)$. The target's own visible features recur much more consistently. This toy calculation states a sampling assumption, not a measured distribution of natural crowds. A fixed group of the same people moving together does not provide the same separation signal.

This [statistical separation of objects in clutter](../../../statistical-separation-of-objects-in-clutter.md) has an explicit computational precedent. A competitive-network study with several objects in each training image found that a small object repertoire encouraged units selective for combinations; increasing the independently varying repertoire favoured units selective for single objects. Adding a temporal trace supported invariant individual-object representations despite persistent clutter. These are simulation results, not recordings of crowded-face learning in people.[Abstract; Network 18, pp. 161–187, changing object repertoire and [trace learning](../../../trace-learning.md) with multiple objects](../../../invariant-object-recognition-with-trace-learning-and-multiple-stimuli-present-during-training-by-simon-m-stringer-and-others.md) A related study trained hierarchical competitive networks on multiple rotating three-dimensional objects and obtained view-tolerant representations of individual objects, showing that continuous transformations and changing co-occurrence can work together.[Abstract; [Neural networks](../../../neural-network.md) 21, pp. 888–903, competitive networks and rotating 3D objects](../../../learning-transform-invariant-object-recognition-in-the-visual-system-with-multiple-stimuli-present-during-training-by-simon-m-stringer-and-others.md)

In a real crowd, both mechanisms can nevertheless fail. [Trace learning](../../../trace-learning.md) may bind two people if gaze switches between them within its effective window; eye movements or an [attention](../../../attention.md) signal could reset or gate the trace. [Continuous transformation learning](../../../continuous-transformation-learning.md) may link similar features from different faces, especially when occlusion removes distinctive features. [Attention](../../../attention.md), spatially restricted [receptive fields](../../../receptive-field.md), independent motion and repeated encounters with different companions can improve the evidence available to learning. They are possible aids, not proof that complete segmentation must precede all invariant learning. Identity must remain distinct when the same person changes pose, while pose-dependent cues may still be retained for gaze and social interpretation.

Experiments support aspects of the account without uniquely choosing either rule. Altering the temporal contiguity of visual experience changed position tolerance in [inferior temporal cortex](../../../inferior-temporal-cortex.md) without an explicit supervised identity task, consistent with experience-dependent association across transformations.[Abstract; Science 321, pp. 1502–1507, manipulating temporal contiguity and testing IT position tolerance](../../../unsupervised-natural-experience-rapidly-alters-invariant-object-representation-in-visual-cortex-by-nuo-li-and-others.md) Recordings from identified macaque face patches showed a progression from strongly view-dependent responses through mirror-related view tuning to more view-tolerant identity responses. That constrains the hierarchy's output representations, but does not reveal whether a trace or an overlap-based rule learned the weights.[Results, Figure 4 and Discussion: view tuning, mirror symmetry and identity generalization across face patches](../../../functional-compartmentalization-and-viewpoint-generalization-within-the-macaque-face-processing-system-by-winrich-a-freiwald-and-others.md)

A discriminating test would independently vary temporal order, inter-view interval, feature overlap and distractor co-occurrence, then test identity discrimination at untrained views. **Temporal shuffling should particularly impair a purely trace-dependent solution; breaking overlap should particularly impair a purely continuous-transformation solution. Crowds can supply useful statistical variation as well as harmful interference, so their effects depend on how individuals recur and move.**
