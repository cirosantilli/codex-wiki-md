# Face colouring

↑ **Parent:** [Graph coloring](graph-coloring-split.md)

A face colouring of a [plane graph](plane-graph.md) assigns colours to its [faces of a plane graph](face-of-a-plane-graph.md) so that distinct faces sharing an [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) have different colours. Equivalently it is a [graph colouring](graph-coloring-split.md) of the [dual graph](dual-graph.md), disregarding dual loops that arise when an original bridge is incident to only one face on both sides. Isolated contact at a [vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) does not impose a colour constraint.

For the [dual graph](dual-graph.md) of a plane [tree](tree-graph-theory-split.md), the face-adjacency graph is the original [tree](tree-graph-theory-split.md), because taking the geometric dual twice recovers the original embedding on the sphere. Every nontrivial [tree](tree-graph-theory-split.md) is a [bipartite graph](bipartite-graph-split.md), so its dual requires exactly two face colours, even though that dual has only one vertex and all its [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) are loops. A [graph drawing](graph-drawing.md) must retain the nesting of those loops to preserve the correct face-adjacency graph.
