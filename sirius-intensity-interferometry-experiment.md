# Sirius intensity interferometry experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar intensity interferometry](stellar-intensity-interferometry.md)

In their 1956 [Sirius](sirius.md) report, [Robert Hanbury Brown](robert-hanbury-brown.md) and [Richard Q. Twiss](richard-quentin-twiss.md) used two 1.56-m searchlight reflectors feeding [photomultipliers](photomultiplier.md). The [photocurrent](photocurrent.md) fluctuations passed through 5–45-MHz amplifiers; an adjustable delay aligned arrival times. A linear mixer multiplied the outputs; an integrating motor accumulated their product. A second motor monitored its root-mean-square fluctuation to control gain changes. Separate lamps illuminated capped [photomultipliers](photomultiplier.md) before and after observations to test electronic coupling and drift; blank-sky runs tested correlated [sky background](sky-brightness.md). They fitted a uniformly bright disk and an unknown zero-baseline scale to relative correlations at different [interferometric baselines](interferometric-baseline.md). Broadband response and possible baseline-dependent [systematic errors](systematic-error.md) limited the diameter inference.[pp. 1046–1048: reflectors, delay/correlator, lamp and sky controls, relative-baseline disk fit](a-test-of-a-new-type-of-stellar-interferometer-on-sirius-by-robert-hanbury-brown-and-richard-quentin-twiss.md)

This astronomical test extends the preceding laboratory [Hanbury Brown and Twiss effect](hanbury-brown-and-twiss-effect.md) to two independently collected samples of a [star](star.md). The historical motor reading was an instrument-specific correlation statistic. It should not be identified numerically with the normalized $g^{(2)}$ below without the corresponding [measurement calibration](measurement-calibration.md).
