# General angle quintisection is impossible with straightedge and compass

↑ **Parent:** [Straightedge and compass construction](straightedge-and-compass-construction-split.md)

Consider the constructible angle whose cosine is $5/6$ and sine is $\sqrt{11}/6$. If one fifth of this angle were constructible, its cosine $c$ would satisfy the displayed polynomial, by the identity $\cos5\theta=16\cos^5\theta-20\cos^3\theta+5\cos\theta$. The identity follows by taking the real part of $(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)^5$ and substituting $\sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta$.

The polynomial is irreducible by [Eisenstein's criterion](eisenstein-criterion.md) at five, so $c$ has degree five over the rational numbers. The initial angle field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{11})$ already has degree two, and adjoining any finite collection of [constructible numbers](constructible-number.md) gives total degree over the rational numbers a power of two. Such a field cannot contain the degree-five element $c$, by the [tower law for field extensions](tower-law-for-field-extensions.md). Hence this constructible angle cannot be divided into five equal angles by [straightedge and compass construction](straightedge-and-compass-construction-split.md). Some particular angles can be quintisected; the obstruction disproves a construction valid for all angles.
