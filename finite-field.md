# Finite field

↑ **Parent:** [Algebra](algebra-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_field)

A [finite field](finite-field.md) is a [field](field.md) with finitely many elements: [addition](addition.md), [subtraction](subtraction.md), [multiplication](multiplication.md), and [division](division-mathematics.md) by a nonzero element all stay inside a finite collection. Write $\mathbb F_q$, or $\operatorname{GF}(q)$, for a [finite field](finite-field.md) with $q$ elements. The subscript counts elements; it does not prescribe [modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic.md) modulo $q$. The [field with four elements](field-with-four-elements.md) is the smallest example where that distinction matters.

Every [finite field](finite-field.md) has size $q=p^n$, where $p$ is a [prime number](prime-number.md) and $n$ is a positive [integer](integer.md). To see the counting mechanism, use its [prime field](prime-field.md) $\mathbb F_p$: the [finite field](finite-field.md) is a [finite-dimensional vector space](finite-dimensional-vector-space.md) over $\mathbb F_p$. For a [basis](basis.md) $e_1,\ldots,e_n$, every element has exactly one expression

$$
c_1e_1+\cdots+c_ne_n,\qquad c_i\in\mathbb F_p.
$$

Each [coefficient](coefficient.md) has $p$ choices, so there are $p^n$ expressions. Here $p$ is the [characteristic of a field](characteristic-of-a-field.md) and $n$ is the [dimension of a vector space](dimension-vector-space.md). Thus sizes $4,8,9,16,25$ are possible, whereas $6,10,12$ are not [prime powers](prime-power.md) and cannot be [finite field](finite-field.md) sizes.

Conversely, every [prime power](prime-power.md) occurs, and any two [finite fields](finite-field.md) of the same size admit a [field isomorphism](field-isomorphism.md). These are classification statements, beyond the counting argument. See Conrad's Theorems 1.5, 2.2, and 2.7.[Keith Conrad, Finite Fields, sections 1–2](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/galoistheory/finitefields.pdf) Uniqueness means the [arithmetic](arithmetic-split.md) agrees after relabeling; it does not choose a [basis](basis.md), an [irreducible polynomial](irreducible-polynomial.md) for a [quotient ring](quotient-ring.md) representation, or a particular [field isomorphism](field-isomorphism.md). Even for the [field with four elements](field-with-four-elements.md), exchanging $\alpha$ and $\alpha+1$ preserves the [arithmetic](arithmetic-split.md).

The appeal for [coding theory](coding-theory-split.md) is exact, finite [arithmetic](arithmetic-split.md) with reliable [division](division-mathematics.md): [polynomial interpolation](polynomial-interpolation.md) and [linear algebra](linear-algebra-split.md) work over a finite alphabet. A [Reed-Solomon code](reed-solomon-error-correction.md) represents messages by [polynomials](polynomial-split.md) and adds redundancy by evaluating them at several distinct [finite field](finite-field.md) elements. Forney's MIT notes develop this [arithmetic](arithmetic-split.md) as preparation for such [error-correcting codes](error-correcting-code.md).[G. David Forney Jr., Introduction to finite fields, 2005](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-451-principles-of-digital-communication-ii-spring-2005/8921c013c1d7802bb197572c52442b97_chap7.pdf) In [cryptography](cryptography.md), [finite fields](finite-field.md) likewise supply exact [arithmetic](arithmetic-split.md) on finitely many symbols, with operations that can be implemented using [bits](bit.md).

Historically, Évariste Galois published work on [finite fields](finite-field.md) beyond [prime fields](prime-field.md) in 1830, constructing them by adjoining elements satisfying [irreducible polynomials](irreducible-polynomial.md) over [prime fields](prime-field.md). Conrad's historical account also records E. H. Moore's 1893 terminology, from which the name [Galois field](finite-field.md) and notation $\operatorname{GF}(q)$ descend.[Keith Conrad, Finite Fields, section 5: History](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/math5211s13/handouts/finitefield.pdf)
