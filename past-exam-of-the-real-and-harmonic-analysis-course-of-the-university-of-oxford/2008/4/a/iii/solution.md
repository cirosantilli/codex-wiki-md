<h1 id="4/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For a set $M$ of [finite measure](../../../../../finite-measure.md) apply the preceding result to $f=\mathbf1_M$. Since its local average minus its point value is bounded in absolute value by its mean absolute oscillation, it gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{\lambda(M\cap(x-r,x+r))}{2r}\longrightarrow\mathbf1_M(x)\quad\text{almost everywhere}.}
$$

For a general measurable $M$, apply the same argument to $\mathbf1_{M\cap[-j,j]}\in L^1(\mathbb R)$. At every interior point of $(-j,j)$, sufficiently short averages agree with those of $\mathbf1_M$. The [countable union](../../../../../countable-union.md) of these interiors covers the line, and a [countable union](../../../../../countable-union.md) of null exceptional sets remains null. This proves **the [Lebesgue density theorem](../../../../../lebesgue-s-density-theorem.md) without a finite-[measure](../../../../../measure-split.md) assumption on $M$**. The source's $\chi$ is the indicator of $M$.
