<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose $\mu=\alpha+\sigma=\beta+\tau$ are two such decompositions. Choose measurable supports $S,T$ of the singular parts with $\kappa(S)=\kappa(T)=0$, and put $N=S\cup T$. [Absolute continuity](../../../../../absolutely-continuous-function.md) makes both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ vanish on $N$. On $X\setminus N$ the two singular parts vanish, so for every measurable $A$,

$$
\alpha(A)=\alpha(A\setminus N)=\mu(A\setminus N)=\beta(A\setminus N)=\beta(A).
$$

Subtracting from the [finite measure](../../../../../finite-measure.md) $\mu$ gives $\sigma=\tau$. This proves **uniqueness of the [Lebesgue decomposition](../../../../../lebesgue-decomposition-theorem.md)** without subtracting infinite masses.

For the specified $M$, the restriction to $M$ is singular because $\kappa(M)=0$, and the complementary restriction is absolutely continuous by assumption. Their sum is $\mu$. Uniqueness therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\mu^s=\mu|_M,\qquad\mu^a=\mu|_{X\setminus M}.}
$$
