<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Write $m(dx)=dx/(2\pi)$ for the normalized [Haar measure](../../../../haar-measure.md). The identity $\sin^2(kx)=\tfrac12-\tfrac12\cos(2kx)$ gives $\mu_k=\tfrac12m-\tfrac12\cos(2kx)m$. For any fixed [trigonometric polynomial](../../../../trigonometric-polynomial.md), [orthogonality of complex exponentials](../../../../orthogonality-of-complex-exponentials.md) makes the oscillatory integral zero once $2k$ exceeds its largest frequency. The norms satisfy $\|\mu_k\|=\mu_k(\mathbb T)=1/2$, so the dense-test criterion applies. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mu_k\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\tfrac12m,\qquad d\mu=\frac{dx}{4\pi}.}
$$

In particular the limit has mass one half; treating these [measures](../../../../measure-split.md) as probabilities would introduce a factor-of-two error.
