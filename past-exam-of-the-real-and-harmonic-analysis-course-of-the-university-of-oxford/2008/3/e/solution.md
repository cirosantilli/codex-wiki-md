<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Read the coefficient inside the limit as $\widehat{\mu_k}(n)$; the printed omission of its index would otherwise make that condition independent of the sequence. Weak-star convergence immediately implies convergence of every coefficient by testing $e^{-inx}$. Conversely coefficient convergence gives convergence against every [trigonometric polynomial](../../../../trigonometric-polynomial.md). Since $\|\mu_k\|=1$, the dense-test argument yields **weak-star convergence to $\mu$**.

The limit is necessarily a [probability](../../../../probability.md). Testing the constant function gives $\mu(\mathbb T)=1$. For every nonnegative continuous $f$, $\int f\,d\mu=\lim_k\int f\,d\mu_k\geq0$. A [Radon measure](../../../../radon-measure.md) whose integrals against all such functions are nonnegative is positive, by the positive case of the [Riesz representation theorem](../../../../riesz-representation-theorem.md) (or continuous approximation of Borel indicators). Thus **$\mu$ is nonnegative and has total mass one**.
