<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

From now on $|\mu|(X)<\infty$, so all signed quantities below are finite. Since $|\mu|(E)\geq|\mu(E)|$, the set function

$$
\mu^+(E)=\frac{|\mu|(E)+\mu(E)}2
$$

is nonnegative. [Countable additivity](../../../../../countable-additivity.md) follows from that of the two terms, so $\mu^+$ is a finite [positive measure](../../../../../positive-measure.md).

Set $s=\sup\{|\mu|(M):\mu^+(M)=0\}$. This is finite. Choose measurable $M_j$ with $\mu^+(M_j)=0$ and $|\mu|(M_j)\to s$. Then

$$
M_-=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}M_j
$$

is $\mu^+$-null by [countable subadditivity](../../../../../countable-subadditivity-of-a-measure.md), and [monotonicity](../../../../../monotonic-function.md) gives $|\mu|(M_-)\geq s$. The definition of $s$ gives the reverse inequality. Hence **$M_-$ attains the maximum**, rather than merely approximating it.
