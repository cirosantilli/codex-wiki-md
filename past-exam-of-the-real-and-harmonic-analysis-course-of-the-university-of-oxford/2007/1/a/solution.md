<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [absolute continuity of measures](../../../../absolute-continuity-of-measures.md) condition is

$$
\nu\ll\kappa\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \kappa(E)=0\Longrightarrow\nu(E)=0\qquad(E\in\mathfrak M).
$$

The [Radon-Nikodym theorem](../../../../radon-nikodym-theorem.md) says that if both [positive measures](../../../../positive-measure.md) are [sigma-finite measures](../../../../sigma-finite-measure.md), then this condition is equivalent to the existence of a nonnegative [measurable function](../../../../measurable-function.md) $h$, unique up to equality $\kappa$-[almost everywhere](../../../../almost-everywhere.md), such that

$$
\boxed{\nu(E)=\int_Eh\,d\kappa\quad(E\in\mathfrak M).}
$$

One writes $h=d\nu/d\kappa$, the [Radon-Nikodym derivative](../../../../radon-nikodym-derivative.md). Under these hypotheses $h$ is finite $\kappa$-[almost everywhere](../../../../almost-everywhere.md). If $\nu$ is finite, then $h\in L^1(\kappa)$. A finite [signed measure](../../../../signed-measure.md) absolutely continuous with respect to a sigma-finite [positive measure](../../../../positive-measure.md) likewise has an integrable signed [Radon-Nikodym derivative](../../../../radon-nikodym-derivative.md).

The sigma-finiteness hypothesis on the dominating [measure](../../../../measure-split.md) cannot simply be omitted. On the Borel subsets of $[0,1]$, let $\kappa$ be [counting measure](../../../../counting-measure.md) and $\nu$ [Lebesgue measure](../../../../lebesgue-measure.md). Absolute continuity holds because the only $\kappa$-[null set](../../../../null-set.md) is empty. A [Radon-Nikodym derivative](../../../../radon-nikodym-derivative.md) would satisfy $h(x)=\nu(\{x\})=0$ at every point, contradicting $\nu([0,1])=1$. Here [counting measure](../../../../counting-measure.md) is not sigma-finite.
