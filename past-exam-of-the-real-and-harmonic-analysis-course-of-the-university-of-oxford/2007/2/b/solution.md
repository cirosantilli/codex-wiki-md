<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Caratheodory measurability criterion](../../../../caratheodory-s-criterion.md) yields a complete sigma-algebra $\mathfrak M_\mu^*$, and $\mu$ restricted to it is a countably additive [measure](../../../../measure-split.md). [Completeness](../../../../completeness.md) means that every subset of a measurable [null set](../../../../null-set.md) is measurable.

For the requested algebra proof, $X$ and $\varnothing$ plainly satisfy the criterion. Replacing $A$ by $X\setminus A$ merely exchanges the two terms, proving closure under complements. If $A,B$ are measurable, apply their splitting identities successively to any $E$:

$$
\mu(E)=\mu(E\cap A)+\mu((E\setminus A)\cap B)+\mu(E\setminus(A\cup B)).
$$

Subadditivity applied to the first two pieces implies

$$
\mu(E)\geq\mu(E\cap(A\cup B))+\mu(E\setminus(A\cup B)).
$$

The reverse inequality is outer subadditivity. Hence $A\cup B$ is measurable, proving closure under finite unions and therefore the **[algebra of sets](../../../../algebra-of-sets.md) property**. The argument never subtracts infinite values.
