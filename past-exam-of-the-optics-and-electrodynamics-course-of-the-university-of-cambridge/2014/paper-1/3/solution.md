<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Use metric $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ and common spacetime origins. A [four-vector](../../../../four-vector.md) transforms as $A'=\Lambda A$ under a [Lorentz transformation](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md). A [Lorentz invariant](../../../../lorentz-invariance.md) is a scalar unchanged by that transformation. Since $\Lambda^T\eta\Lambda=\eta$,

$$
\boxed{A'\cdot B'=A^T\Lambda^T\eta\Lambda B=A^T\eta B=A\cdot B.}
$$

The [wave phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) labels the same stage of a physical [oscillation](../../../../oscillation.md) at a [spacetime event](../../../../event-relativity.md), independently of the observer's coordinates. Equal-phase wavefronts and [interference](../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) [phase differences](../../../../phase-difference.md) are physical; fixing a common [phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) origin makes the continuous [phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) a [Lorentz scalar](../../../../lorentz-scalar.md). For a plane wave,

$$
\phi=\omega t-\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r=K_\mu X^\mu,
\qquad K_\mu=\partial_\mu\phi.
$$

The [gradient](../../../../gradient.md) of a scalar is a [covector](../../../../covector.md); raising its index gives the [four-wavevector](../../../../four-wavevector.md) $K^\mu=(\omega/c,\mathbf k)$. Under an $x$ boost,

$$
\boxed{\omega'=\gamma(\omega-vk_x),\quad
k'_x=\gamma(k_x-v\omega/c^2),\quad k'_y=k_y,\quad k'_z=k_z.}
$$

For vacuum [light](../../../../light.md), $k_x=(\omega/c)\cos\theta$, giving

$$
\boxed{\omega'=\gamma\omega(1-\beta\cos\theta).}
$$

A [photon](../../../../photon.md) has [four-momentum](../../../../four-momentum.md) $p^\mu=\hbar K^\mu$, with $K^2=0$. For two [photons](../../../../photon.md) separated by angle $\alpha$,

$$
(p_1+p_2)^2=2\hbar^2K_1\cdot K_2
=\frac{2\hbar^2\omega_1\omega_2}{c^2}(1-\cos\alpha).
$$

In the [centre-of-momentum frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md) the least possible pair [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is $2m_ec^2$, when both final particles are at rest. Thus the threshold has $(p_1+p_2)^2=4m_e^2c^2$; above it, excess [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) supplies their [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\omega_{2,\rm th}=
\frac{2m_e^2c^4}{\hbar^2\omega_1(1-\cos\alpha)}.}
$$

The kinematic equality is a limiting threshold; nonzero production [phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) space requires energies above it. For parallel [photons](../../../../photon.md) no finite threshold is available, whereas head-on incidence minimizes the required [energy](../../../../energy-split.md):

$$
\boxed{E_{2,\min}=\frac{(m_ec^2)^2}{E_1}\quad(\alpha=\pi).}
$$

The [blackbody-peak photon pair-production estimate](../../../../blackbody-peak-photon-pair-production-estimate.md), using the frequency-spectrum blackbody peak, $E_1=2.82144k_BT=7.29\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{eV}$ at $3\,\mathrm K$, gives **$E_{2,\min}\simeq3.58\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{eV}$**, or $358\,\mathrm{TeV}$. If “peak” means the wavelength-spectrum peak, $E_1=4.96511k_BT$ instead gives $2.03\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{eV}$. The spectral convention changes the prefactor, not the conclusion that [photons](../../../../photon.md) of order a few $10^{14}\,\mathrm{eV}$ are needed for this idealized peak-photon threshold.
