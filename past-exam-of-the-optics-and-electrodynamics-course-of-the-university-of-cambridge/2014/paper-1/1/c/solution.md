<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Interpret the motion as **uniform rectilinear motion**: the charge is stationary in an inertial [rest frame](../../../../../rest-frame.md) and has no radiation field. Constant [speed](../../../../../speed.md) alone is insufficient; a curved trajectory has [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) and can radiate even in vacuum.

In a transparent medium, a charge can move faster than the [light](../../../../../light.md) [phase velocity](../../../../../phase-velocity.md) $c/n$ without exceeding $c$. The induced polarization disturbance then adds coherently on a Cherenkov cone, with $\cos\theta_C=1/(n\beta)$. Thus

$$
\beta_{\rm th}=\frac1n,\qquad
\boxed{K_{\rm th}=m_pc^2\left[\frac1{\sqrt{1-n^{-2}}}-1\right]
\simeq321\,\mathrm{MeV}\quad(n=1.5).}
$$

This is the kinematic threshold limit for the specified [refractive index](../../../../../refractive-index.md); appreciable emission requires exceeding it, and dispersion selects which [frequencies](../../../../../frequency.md) satisfy the condition.
