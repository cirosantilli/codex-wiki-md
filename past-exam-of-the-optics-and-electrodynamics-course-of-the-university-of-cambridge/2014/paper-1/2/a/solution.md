<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the magnetic [Aharonov-Bohm effect](../../../../../aharonov-bohm-effect.md), coherent electron paths enclose inaccessible [magnetic flux](../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) even though the field vanishes along the paths. The [magnetic vector potential](../../../../../magnetic-vector-potential.md) contributes relative [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md)

$$
\boxed{\Delta\phi=\frac q\hbar\oint\mathbf A\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell
=\frac{q\Phi_B}{\hbar}\pmod{2\pi}.}
$$

Recombination turns this [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) into a fringe displacement. For an electron $q=-e$, changing [flux](../../../../../flux.md) by $h/e$ gives one full fringe period. Reversing the loop orientation reverses the [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) sign. A gauge change modifies each path's [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) convention but leaves the closed-loop [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) and observed [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) unchanged. The accessible region surrounds excluded [flux](../../../../../flux.md), so a globally trivial gauge cannot remove that [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md).

Original two-path Aharonov-Bohm schematic and the half-period fringe shift from half an electron flux period; paths avoid the flux region**.

A shielded toroidal magnet provides a concrete test: [electron holography](../../../../../electron-holography.md) compared waves passing through its hole and outside it. Superconducting and copper coatings confined the field and excluded electrons from the magnet; the measured relative [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) tested enclosed [flux](../../../../../flux.md) rather than a classical Lorentz deflection along the paths.[Physical Review Letters 56, 792 (1986), primary abstract: shielded toroid and electron-holographic relative phase](../../../../../evidence-for-aharonov-bohm-effect-with-magnetic-field-completely-shielded-from-electron-wave-by-akira-tonomura-and-others.md)
