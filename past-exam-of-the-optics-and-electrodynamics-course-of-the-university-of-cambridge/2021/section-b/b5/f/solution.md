<h1 id="section-b/b5/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The $x$ axis lies along the mean [Poynting vector](../../../../../poynting-vector.md). A two-dimensional [optical camera](../../../../../optical-camera.md) sensor intended to sample many $x$ positions at once would have to lie along the [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) flow, where it receives no normal flux in the ideal geometry; a side-view [optical camera](../../../../../optical-camera.md) also sees no light from an empty nonscattering propagation region. Putting a detector normal to $x$ measures one transverse section instead, and absorbs the light before a downstream detector can sample the same rays.

One could translate a detector to successive $x$ positions or introduce a scattering screen, but that is a different measurement. Moreover the ideal result here is already the constant $I(x)=2I_0$, so it contains no longitudinal [optical coherence](../../../../../optical-coherence.md) structure for the [optical camera](../../../../../optical-camera.md) to resolve. The difficulty is the longitudinal sampling geometry, rather than a hidden $x$-fringe period.
