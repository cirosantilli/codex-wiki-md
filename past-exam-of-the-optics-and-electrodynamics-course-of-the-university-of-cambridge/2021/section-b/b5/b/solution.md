<h1 id="section-b/b5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The given spatial and carrier factors combine into retarded copies of the same random field:

$$
\mathbf E'(\mathbf r,t)=\frac{f(t-t_1)+f(t-t_2)}{\sqrt2}\widehat{\mathbf z},\qquad
t_j=\frac{\mathbf k_j\cdot\mathbf r}{\omega}.
$$

On the $y$ axis, $t_1=ky/(\sqrt2\omega)$ and $t_2=-ky/(\sqrt2\omega)$. Expand the squared electric-field [wave amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md). Each self term contributes $I_0/2$, and the two cross terms together contribute the real part of the full-field correlation. Stationarity and $\Gamma(-\tau)=\Gamma(\tau)^*$ give

$$
\boxed{I(y)=C\langle|\mathbf E'|^2\rangle
=I_0+\operatorname{Re}\Gamma(\tau(y)),\qquad
\tau(y)=\frac{\sqrt2ky}{\omega}.}
$$

In [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md), $k=\omega/c$ and $\tau(y)=\sqrt2y/c$. Reversing the delay convention replaces $\tau$ by $-\tau$ without changing this real-part result. This $I$ is the electric-field [light intensity](../../../../../light-intensity.md) convention of the question. For two nonparallel waves it should not automatically be identified with the magnitude of the net [Poynting vector](../../../../../poynting-vector.md); part (d) gives that geometric conversion explicitly.
