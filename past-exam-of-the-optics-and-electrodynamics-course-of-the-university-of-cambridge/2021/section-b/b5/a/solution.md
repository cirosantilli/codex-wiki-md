<h1 id="section-b/b5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Take $f(t)=a(t)e^{-i\omega t}$ at a fixed reference point, and define the full-field [temporal optical coherence](../../../../../temporal-optical-coherence.md) function by

$$
\boxed{\Gamma(t,\tau)=C\langle f^*(t)f(t+\tau)\rangle
=C\langle a^*(t)a(t+\tau)\rangle e^{-i\omega\tau},\qquad C=\frac{\epsilon_0c}{2}.}
$$

The angle brackets denote an ensemble [expected value](../../../../../expected-value-split.md), or a sufficiently long time average when justified. The factor $C$ puts $\Gamma$ in [light intensity](../../../../../light-intensity.md) units for the chosen peak complex-field convention. The normalized [first-order optical coherence](../../../../../first-order-optical-coherence.md) is $g^{(1)}(\tau)=\Gamma(\tau)/\Gamma(0)$ for a stationary source.

“Independent of absolute time” means the second moment depends on the time difference $\tau$ but not on the starting time $t$: shifting both observation times by the same amount leaves it unchanged. Together with a constant mean this is second-order or [weak stationarity](../../../../../weakly-stationary-process.md); it does not mean that the random [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is constant in time. Put $I_0=\Gamma(0)=C\langle|a|^2\rangle$. The full-field convention is important: an envelope-only correlation omits $e^{-i\omega\tau}$ and must have that carrier factor restored before deriving optical [interference fringes](../../../../../interference-fringe.md).
