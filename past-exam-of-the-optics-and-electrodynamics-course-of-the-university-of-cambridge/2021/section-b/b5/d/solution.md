<h1 id="section-b/b5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For each beam in [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md), its [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) is $\mathbf B_j=\widehat{\mathbf k}_j\times\mathbf E_j/c$. Write the two complex scalar contributions, including the [beam splitter](../../../../../beam-splitter.md) factor, as $u_1=f(t-t_1)/\sqrt2$ and $u_2=f(t-t_2)/\sqrt2$. Since their [electric fields](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) are both along $z$,

$$
\mathbf E'=(u_1+u_2)\widehat{\mathbf z},\qquad
\mathbf B'=\frac1{c\sqrt2}\left[(u_1-u_2)\widehat{\mathbf x}-(u_1+u_2)\widehat{\mathbf y}\right].
$$

The optical-cycle and ensemble-averaged [Poynting vector](../../../../../poynting-vector.md) is

$$
\langle\mathbf S\rangle=\frac1{2\mu_0}\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathbf E'\times\mathbf B'^*\rangle
=\frac C{\sqrt2}\left[\langle|u_1+u_2|^2\rangle\widehat{\mathbf x}
+(\langle|u_1|^2\rangle-\langle|u_2|^2\rangle)\widehat{\mathbf y}\right].
$$

The mean individual [light intensities](../../../../../light-intensity.md) are equal, so

$$
\boxed{\langle\mathbf S\rangle=\frac{I(y)}{\sqrt2}\widehat{\mathbf x}.}
$$

Thus [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) flows along the bisector of the two beam directions, crossing a detector plane normal to $x$. Such a sensor can in principle record the variation across its $y$ coordinate. Its actual normal [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) flux is $I(y)/\sqrt2$ in the normalization used above; this fixed factor does not change [interferometric visibility](../../../../../interferometric-visibility.md).

There is a practical resolution condition in addition to the direction of [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) flow. The [interference fringe spacing](../../../../../interference-fringe-spacing.md) $\lambda/\sqrt2$ is very small, so direct pixels would need spacing no larger than $\lambda/(2\sqrt2)$ as required by the [Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem](../../../../../nyquist-shannon-sampling-theorem.md), or optical magnification and sufficient angular aperture to preserve both incident directions. An ordinary [optical camera](../../../../../optical-camera.md) with much larger effective pixels averages the [interference fringes](../../../../../interference-fringe.md). The Poynting-vector argument establishes the allowed measurement geometry, not guaranteed resolution with any [optical camera](../../../../../optical-camera.md).
