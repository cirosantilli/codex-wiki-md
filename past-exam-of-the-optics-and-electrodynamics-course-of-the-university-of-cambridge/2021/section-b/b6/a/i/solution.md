<h1 id="section-b/b6/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The upper index identifies $P^\alpha$ as a contravariant [Lorentz four-vector](../../../../../../four-vector.md), obeying the rank-one [contravariant tensor](../../../../../../contravariant-tensor.md) transformation law. Its components transform with the coordinate Jacobian,

$$
\boxed{P'^\alpha=\frac{\partial x'^\alpha}{\partial x^\beta}P^\beta.}
$$

It is not invariant component by component. Its contracted square $P_\alpha P^\alpha=m^2c^2$, for metric signature $(+,-,-,-)$, is a [Lorentz invariant](../../../../../../lorentz-invariance.md). This distinguishes a contravariant transformation law from an invariant scalar.
