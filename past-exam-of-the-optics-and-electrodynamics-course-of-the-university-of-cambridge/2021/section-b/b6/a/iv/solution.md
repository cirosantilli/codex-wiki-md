<h1 id="section-b/b6/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The lower derivative index and the upper [electromagnetic four-potential](../../../../../../electromagnetic-four-potential.md) index are fully contracted. For a constant [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md),

$$
\partial'_\alpha A'^\alpha
=(\Lambda^{-1})^\beta{}_{\alpha}\partial_\beta(\Lambda^\alpha{}_{\gamma}A^\gamma)
=\partial_\beta A^\beta.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{\partial_\alpha A^\alpha\text{ is a Lorentz-invariant scalar}.}
$$

This is a statement about Lorentz transformations, not electromagnetic gauge transformations. The quantity is not generally gauge invariant; setting it to zero is the [Lorenz gauge condition](../../../../../../lorenz-gauge-condition.md), which is Lorentz covariant because zero remains zero in every inertial frame.
