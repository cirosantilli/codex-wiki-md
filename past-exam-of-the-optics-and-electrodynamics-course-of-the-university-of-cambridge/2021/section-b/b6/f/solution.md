<h1 id="section-b/b6/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

**Yes: the same physical resonant cavity and its field remain resonant in every inertial description.** A [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) changes the mirror worldlines and the field together. The boundary [phase matching](../../../../../phase-matching.md) and the constructive-interference condition are statements about the same reflection events, and the contracted [wave phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) is a [Lorentz scalar](../../../../../lorentz-scalar.md). A boosted resonant solution therefore still satisfies the moving-mirror [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md).

It would be incorrect merely to insert a Lorentz-contracted length into the formula for two stationary mirrors while leaving both propagation [frequencies](../../../../../frequency.md) unchanged. For example, let the mirrors be separated by $L$ in their [rest frame](../../../../../rest-frame.md) and let $\omega_0L/c=m\pi$, including the usual equal-mirror reflection-phase convention. For an observer boosted at $+v$ along the cavity axis, the cavity moves at $-v$ and its two travelling components have [frequencies](../../../../../frequency.md)

$$
\omega'_+=\gamma\omega_0(1-\beta),\qquad
\omega'_- =\gamma\omega_0(1+\beta).
$$

They need not have one common laboratory [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md). The simultaneous mirror separation is $L'=L/\gamma$, while the forward and return flight times are $L'/(c+v)$ and $L'/(c-v)$. The round-trip interval between events on the same mirror is therefore $2\gamma L/c$.

Along that moving mirror the [wave phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) rate of either wave component is $\omega'-k'v_{\rm mirror}=\omega_0/\gamma$. Its advance over the round trip is

$$
\boxed{\frac{\omega_0}{\gamma}\frac{2\gamma L}{c}=\frac{2\omega_0L}{c}=2\pi m.}
$$

Thus the [resonance](../../../../../resonance.md) condition is unchanged even though the observed travelling [frequencies](../../../../../frequency.md), lengths and flight times all change. For a boost in a general direction the beam directions also undergo [relativistic aberration](../../../../../relativistic-aberration.md); the event-by-event phase-invariance argument still applies. A separate stationary cavity placed in the new frame, or an external laser whose [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) is not transformed with the original setup, is a different physical system.
