<h1 id="section-b/b4/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

The circular [eigenmode](../../../../../normal-mode.md) [orthogonal projection](../../../../../orthogonal-projection.md) matrices obey $P_\pm^2=P_\pm$, $P_+P_-=0$ and $P_++P_-=I$. Define the uniform-medium generator

$$
K=\gamma^+P_++\gamma^-P_-
=\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}\gamma&i\delta\\-i\delta&\gamma\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Expanding its [matrix exponential](../../../../../matrix-exponential.md) by its [power series](../../../../../power-series.md) and using these projection identities gives

$$
\boxed{e^{-Kz}=e^{-\gamma^+z}P_++e^{-\gamma^-z}P_-=\mathcal J(z).}
$$

Equivalently, with $Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\-i&0\end{pmatrix}$, one has $Q^2=I$, $K=\gamma I+\delta Q$ and $e^{-Kz}=e^{-\gamma z}[I\cosh(\delta z)-Q\sinh(\delta z)]$. This is exactly the corrected matrix of part (d). The entries of $K$ have units of inverse length, and the map satisfies $d\mathcal J/dz=-K\mathcal J$, $\mathcal J(0)=I$ and the homogeneous propagation law $\mathcal J(z_1+z_2)=\mathcal J(z_1)\mathcal J(z_2)$.

The extra $\operatorname{sech}^2$ factor in the printed version violates this last law for nonzero $\delta$, so it cannot be generated by one constant $K$ with the stipulated [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md). The constant-$K$ description assumes uniform coefficients and fixed circular [eigenmodes](../../../../../normal-mode.md).
