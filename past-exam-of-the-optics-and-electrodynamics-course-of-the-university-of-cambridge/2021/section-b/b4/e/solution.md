<h1 id="section-b/b4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

When $\gamma^\pm=\alpha^\pm$ are real, $\gamma$ and $\delta$ are real. The two opposite [circular polarizations](../../../../../circular-polarization.md) retain their [wave phases](../../../../../phase-waves.md) but are attenuated at different [wave amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) rates. This [circular dichroism](../../../../../circular-dichroism.md) changes the ellipticity without rotating the principal axis of the [ellipse](../../../../../ellipse.md).

For input $E_0\widehat{\mathbf x}$, the corrected [Jones matrix](../../../../../jones-matrix.md) gives

$$
\mathbf E_{\rm out}=E_0e^{-\gamma z}
\begin{pmatrix}\cosh(\delta z)\\i\sinh(\delta z)\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The common factor $e^{-\gamma z}$ describes the mea [wave amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) attenuation. The $\cosh(\delta z)$ factor reflects the sum of the two transmitted modal [wave amplitudes](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md); $\tanh(\delta z)$ fixes their imbalance and hence the signed ellipticity. The real field has components proportional to $\cosh(\delta z)\cos\omega t$ and $\sinh(\delta z)\sin\omega t$, so

$$
\boxed{\frac{\text{minor semiaxis}}{\text{major semiaxis}}=|\tanh(\delta z)|,\qquad
\frac{I(z)}{I(0)}=e^{-2\gamma z}\cosh(2\delta z)
=\frac{e^{-2\alpha^+z}+e^{-2\alpha^-z}}2.}
$$

The [ellipse](../../../../../ellipse.md)'s principal axis stays along the original input direction. For large differential optical depth it approaches the less attenuated circular [eigenmode](../../../../../normal-mode.md). A passive medium has $\alpha^\pm\geq0$, or $\gamma\geq|\delta|$, so the growing [hyperbolic function](../../../../../hyperbolic-function.md) factor does not imply net optical gain. Using the printed $\operatorname{sech}$ prefactor would give a spurious extra attenuation inconsistent with the modal laws.
