↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Now take $\widehat{\mathbf n}=(0,\sin\psi,\cos\psi)$, with the same angle to the incident $z$ direction. The perpendicular direction is $\widehat{\mathbf x}$ and the in-plane transverse direction is $(0,\cos\psi,-\sin\psi)$. The roles of the two [electric dipole](../../../../../electric-dipole.md) populations interchange: $x$ [electric dipoles](../../../../../electric-dipole.md) radiate at full strength, whereas the observed [wave amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) from a $y$ [electric dipole](../../../../../electric-dipole.md) is reduced by $\cos\psi$.

Thus $I_\perp=CN_1$ and $I_\parallel=CN_2\cos^2\psi$, giving

$$
\boxed{\mathcal P_{yz}(\psi)=\frac{|N_1-N_2\cos^2\psi|}{N_1+N_2\cos^2\psi}.}
$$

This is also obtained by the symmetry $x\leftrightarrow y$, $N_1\leftrightarrow N_2$ in part (a). For $N_1=N_2$, both planes give the familiar [Rayleigh scattering](../../../../../rayleigh-scattering.md) [degree of polarization](../../../../../degree-of-polarization.md) $\sin^2\psi/(1+\cos^2\psi)$: the diffuse signal is unpolarized forward and fully linearly polarized at right angles. At a dark direction with zero denominator, no [degree of polarization](../../../../../degree-of-polarization.md) can be measured.
