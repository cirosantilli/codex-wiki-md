<h1 id="section-a/a1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Work in the dilute, single-scattering limit and add the diffuse scattered [light intensities](../../../../../light-intensity.md) of randomly positioned particles. The incident [unpolarized light](../../../../../unpolarized-light.md) has equal, mutually uncorrelated $x$ and $y$ electric components. Each particle therefore has an induced [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) along its permitted axis, with the same mean squared magnitude proportional to $|\alpha|^2$.

For an observer in direction $\widehat{\mathbf n}=(\sin\psi,0,\cos\psi)$, choose transverse unit directions $\mathbf e_\parallel=(\cos\psi,0,-\sin\psi)$ in the scattering plane and $\mathbf e_\perp=\widehat{\mathbf y}$ perpendicular to it. The [electric dipole radiation](../../../../../electric-dipole-radiation.md) field is proportional to the transverse projection of the [electric dipole](../../../../../electric-dipole.md). Hence an $x$ [electric dipole](../../../../../electric-dipole.md) contributes a [wave amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) factor $\cos\psi$ along $\mathbf e_\parallel$, while a $y$ [electric dipole](../../../../../electric-dipole.md) contributes its full [wave amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) along $\mathbf e_\perp$.

Let $C>0$ collect the common incident [light intensity](../../../../../light-intensity.md), [electric polarizability](../../../../../electric-polarizability.md), distance and [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) factors. Then

$$
I_\parallel=CN_1\cos^2\psi,\qquad I_\perp=CN_2.
$$

The two components are uncorrelated, so the [coherency matrix](../../../../../coherency-matrix.md) is diagonal in this basis. The [degree of polarization](../../../../../degree-of-polarization.md) is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal P_{xz}(\psi)=\frac{|I_\perp-I_\parallel|}{I_\perp+I_\parallel}
=\frac{|N_2-N_1\cos^2\psi|}{N_2+N_1\cos^2\psi}.}
$$

A signed degree of [linear polarization](../../../../../linear-polarization.md), defined positive perpendicular to the scattering plane, has the same expression without the absolute value. The dominant direction is $\widehat{\mathbf y}$ when $N_2>N_1\cos^2\psi$, and $\mathbf e_\parallel$ when the inequality reverses. If the denominator vanishes there is no scattered signal and its [degree of polarization](../../../../../degree-of-polarization.md) is undefined. A coherent exactly forward component, including the transmitted incident beam, is not part of this diffuse-intensity calculation; random particle positions do not by themselves destroy forward [optical coherence](../../../../../optical-coherence.md).
