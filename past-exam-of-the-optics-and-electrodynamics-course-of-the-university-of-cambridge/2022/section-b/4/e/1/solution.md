<h1 id="section-b/4/e/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Keep the [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) fixed in the laboratory and exchange source and observer. The incident [unpolarized light](../../../../../../unpolarized-light.md) now meets $P_2$ first. In fixed transverse laboratory axes the backward glass map is still $R(\theta)$, not $R(-\theta)$: this is [optical nonreciprocity](../../../../../../optical-nonreciprocity.md) of the [Faraday effect](../../../../../../faraday-effect.md). The order of the two [polarizers](../../../../../../polarizer.md) is reversed, so

$$
J_{O\to S}=P_1R(\theta)P_2
=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta-\sin\theta&\cos\theta-\sin\theta\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The reverse [light intensity](../../../../../../light-intensity.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{I_r=\frac{I_0}{2}\cos^2\left(\frac\pi4+\Upsilon LB_\parallel\right).}
$$

For the optimal forward setting $\theta=\pi/4$, it vanishes: the $45^\circ$ input selected by $P_2$ rotates to $90^\circ$ and is blocked by $P_1$. At a general field, $I_r+I_f=I_0/2$. The result assumes equal incident [light intensities](../../../../../../light-intensity.md) in the two experiments. Treating the glass as a reciprocal natural optical rotator would predict cancellation of its laboratory rotation on reversal and would give the wrong answer here.
