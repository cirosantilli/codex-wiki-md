<h1 id="section-b/4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Flint glass is approximately optically isotropic without an applied [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md). A longitudinal [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) changes the motion of its bound [electrons](../../../../../electron.md): the magnetic part of the [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md) acts with opposite sense on the two circularly rotating components of a driven electronic oscillation. Their optical response, and hence their [refractive indices](../../../../../refractive-index.md), split. Equivalently, the transverse [permittivity tensor](../../../../../permittivity-tensor.md) acquires a field-odd antisymmetric contribution whose eigenvectors are opposite [circular polarizations](../../../../../circular-polarization.md).

The resulting [circular birefringence](../../../../../circular-birefringence.md) gives [Faraday rotation](../../../../../faraday-effect.md). For a weak longitudinal bias it is linear in the field:

$$
\boxed{\rho=\Upsilon B_\parallel,\qquad\theta=\Upsilon B_\parallel L.}
$$

Here $\Upsilon$ is the [Verdet constant](../../../../../verdet-constant.md) and $L$ the path length. The [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) breaks [time-reversal symmetry](../../../../../t-symmetry.md), which is why this rotation is nonreciprocal. Reversing the bias reverses the rotation; reversing propagation while keeping the bias fixed does not undo it when physical polarization directions are compared in fixed laboratory axes. A transverse magnetic bias can have different, higher-order effects; the setup here is the longitudinal [Faraday effect](../../../../../faraday-effect.md).
