<h1 id="section-b/5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The prescribed surface [charge density](../../../../../charge-density.md) is that of a [uniformly polarized sphere](../../../../../uniformly-polarized-sphere.md) and has zero total charge because $\int\cos\theta\,d\Omega=0$. Its [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) at $t=0$ is

$$
\mathbf p_0=\int_{r=R}\mathbf r\,\sigma\,dA
=\sigma_0R^3\int\widehat{\mathbf r}\cos\theta\,d\Omega.
$$

The transverse components cancel by [axisymmetry](../../../../../axisymmetric-vector-field.md), while the $z$ component is

$$
p_{0,z}=2\pi\sigma_0R^3\int_0^\pi\cos^2\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta
=\frac{4\pi}3\sigma_0R^3.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathbf p_0=p_0\widehat{\mathbf z},\qquad p_0=\frac{4\pi}3\sigma_0R^3.}
$$

The angular surface distribution is a pure degree-one [spherical harmonic](../../../../../spherical-harmonic.md). Hence its static external [electric potential](../../../../../electric-potential.md) is exactly that of this dipole, with no higher electric [multipole moments](../../../../../multipole-moment.md); it is not an assertion that the potential inside the finite sphere equals a singular point-dipole potential.

For right-handed uniform rotation about $+\widehat{\mathbf x}$, the rigidly attached dipole is

$$
\mathbf p(t)=p_0\left(-\widehat{\mathbf y}\sin\omega t+\widehat{\mathbf z}\cos\omega t\right),\qquad
\ddot{\mathbf p}=-\omega^2\mathbf p.
$$

Consequently $|\ddot{\mathbf p}|^2=p_0^2\omega^4$ is constant. The [electric dipole radiation](../../../../../electric-dipole-radiation.md) formula gives

$$
\boxed{\overline P=\frac{\mu_0p_0^2}{6\pi c}\omega^4
=\frac{8\pi\mu_0\sigma_0^2R^6}{27c}\,\omega^4\equiv K\omega^4.}
$$

There is no extra factor of one-half: the squared sine and cosine contributions of the two perpendicular oscillating components sum to one. This is the leading [electric-dipole approximation for radiation](../../../../../electric-dipole-approximation-for-radiation.md), requiring $\omega R/c\ll1$ and observation in the [radiation zone](../../../../../radiation-zone.md). The stated permanent surface distribution is interpreted as the actual charge producing the given external dipole; adding another dielectric screening factor would contradict the dipole moment the question asks to establish. The [relative permittivity](../../../../../relative-permittivity.md) enters the induced-polarization problem in part (d), not this prescribed total dipole moment.
