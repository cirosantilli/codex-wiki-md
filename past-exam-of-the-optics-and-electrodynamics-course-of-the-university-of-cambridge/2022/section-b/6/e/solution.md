<h1 id="section-b/6/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The information in the original question is insufficient to determine a unique spin-induced change of [charge density](../../../../../charge-density.md). It specifies ideal dielectric filaments with a prescribed excess [linear charge density](../../../../../linear-charge-density.md), but not the [charge carrier](../../../../../charge-carrier.md) species, their [spin](../../../../../spin.md) state, [charge carrier mobility](../../../../../charge-carrier-mobility.md), binding potential or relaxation mechanism. A classical line charge has no spin variable at all. The following conditional conclusions are supported by the fields already calculated.

For a carrier moving with its filament, the local [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) in its instantaneous [rest frame](../../../../../rest-frame.md) is, for the transverse fields here,

$$
\mathbf B_{\rm rest,\perp}=\gamma_u\left(\mathbf B-\frac{\mathbf u\times\mathbf E}{c^2}\right),
$$

where $\mathbf u$ is that carrier's laboratory [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md). Exclude its filament's singular self-field and first retain only the prescribed straight convective motion.

In Case 1 there is a common [rest frame](../../../../../rest-frame.md) in which both filaments and the excess charges are stationary and the interfilament [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) vanishes. Equivalently, in the laboratory $\mathbf B_A=\mathbf v\times\mathbf E_A/c^2$, so B's co-moving charges have $\mathbf B_{A,\rm rest}=0$. Thus uniform common translation alone creates no extra mutual rest-field spin splitting. This does not exclude intrinsic [spin-orbit coupling](../../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md) of electrons moving within their atomic binding potentials; that material physics was never specified.

In Case 2 there is relative motion and no frame in which both source distributions are stationary. Put $E_*=\eta/(2\pi\epsilon_0d)$ as in part (c). At the respective filament axes, the external fields experienced in each carrier distribution's own rest frame are

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B_{A\to B}^{(B\,\rm rest)}
=\frac{\gamma vE_*}{c^2}\widehat{\mathbf y},\qquad
\mathbf B_{B\to A}^{(A\,\rm rest)}
=\frac{\gamma vE_*}{c^2}\widehat{\mathbf y}.}
$$

For the first expression B is at rest in $O$; for the second A is at rest in $O'$ and the field comes from B moving at $-v\widehat{\mathbf x}$. For a carrier with [magnetic dipole moment](../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) $\boldsymbol\mu_s$, the interaction $H_s=-\boldsymbol\mu_s\cdot\mathbf B_{\rm rest}$ distinguishes opposite transverse spin orientations. A field gradient could exert a [spin](../../../../../spin.md)-dependent [force](../../../../../force-split.md) $\nabla(\boldsymbol\mu_s\cdot\mathbf B_{\rm rest})$ on mobile carriers; [spin relaxation](../../../../../spin-relaxation.md) could instead produce a [spin polarization](../../../../../spin-polarization.md). These are possible responses, not consequences of charge density alone. In B's frame the displayed interaction is directly a magnetic-moment coupling to the moving source's field; treating the moving carrier's electrostatic orbital term in another frame also requires consistent binding and acceleration dynamics, including any applicable [Thomas precession](../../../../../thomas-precession.md).

For example, if $\eta>0$ and mobile electron spins can relax, the lower magnetic-energy orientation has the electron's magnetic moment toward $+\widehat{\mathbf y}$ and its [spin](../../../../../spin.md) toward $-\widehat{\mathbf y}$. Oppositely oriented moments would experience opposite gradient forces. The sign changes if the source charge changes sign. None of these statements fixes a net charge redistribution without a confinement and response model, and the stipulated zero transverse size supplies no resolved “sides” on which charge could accumulate.

**Therefore Case 1 has no new mutual spin effect from common bulk motion alone; Case 2 permits motion-induced spin-dependent interactions, but neither case determines an additional charge-density profile from the supplied data.** In particular, the factor $\gamma$ relating proper and laboratory [linear charge densities](../../../../../linear-charge-density.md) in part (b) follows from [length contraction](../../../../../length-contraction.md) and is not an effect of [spin-orbit coupling](../../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md). If the charges remain pinned in an ideal dielectric, their imposed density remains the prescribed density in either case.
