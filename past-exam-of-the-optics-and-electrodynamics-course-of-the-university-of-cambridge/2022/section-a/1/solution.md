<h1 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The emission mechanism is [Cherenkov radiation](../../../../cherenkov-radiation.md). An [electron](../../../../electron.md) moving faster than the [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md) $c/n$ of [light](../../../../light.md) in the material excites [electric polarization](../../../../polarization-density.md) disturbances whose radiation adds coherently on a cone. This does not require the [electron](../../../../electron.md) to exceed the [speed of light](../../../../speed-of-light.md) in [vacuum](../../../../vacuum.md). Taking $n=1.4$ as constant over the detected optical band, the [Cherenkov cone angle](../../../../cherenkov-cone-angle.md) satisfies

$$
\cos\theta_C=\frac{c}{nv}=\frac1{n\beta},\qquad \beta=\frac vc.
$$

Emission requires $\beta>1/n$. The detector accepts $\theta_C<20^\circ$, so

$$
\frac1{n\beta}>\cos20^\circ,\qquad
\boxed{\frac1{1.4}<\beta<\frac1{1.4\cos20^\circ}.}
$$

Numerically,

$$
\boxed{0.71429c<v<0.76013c,\qquad
2.1414\times10^8<v<2.2788\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The lower endpoint has vanishing [Cherenkov radiation](../../../../cherenkov-radiation.md) yield, and the upper endpoint is excluded by the stated strict angular acceptance. Faster [electrons](../../../../electron.md) emit at larger cone angles: at $v\simeq c$, $\theta_C\simeq\cos^{-1}(1/1.4)=44.4^\circ$, outside this detector's acceptance. The angular condition gives the accepted speed interval conditional on rays reaching the detector; no collection geometry or total signal yield is specified.
