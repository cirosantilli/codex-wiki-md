<h1 id="section-b/b5/rayleigh-limit">Rayleigh limit</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

In [Rayleigh scattering](../../../../rayleigh-scattering.md), a small bound system develops an induced [electric dipole moment](../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) $\mathbf p(t)=\operatorname{Re}[\alpha(\omega)\mathbf E_0e^{-i\omega t}]$. Well below its binding resonances, the [electric polarizability](../../../../electric-polarizability.md) is approximately its static value $\alpha(0)$, independent of [frequency](../../../../frequency.md). The mean radiated [power](../../../../power.md) and incident [light intensity](../../../../light-intensity.md) are

$$
\langle P\rangle=\frac{\omega^4|\alpha E_0|^2}{12\pi\epsilon_0c^3},\qquad
\langle S\rangle=\frac12\epsilon_0c|E_0|^2.
$$

Dividing gives the total scattering [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md)

$$
\boxed{\sigma_R=\frac{\omega^4|\alpha(0)|^2}{6\pi\epsilon_0^2c^4}
\propto\omega^4\propto\lambda^{-4}.}
$$

This [Rayleigh scattering wavelength dependence](../../../../rayleigh-scattering-wavelength-dependence.md) comes from the two time derivatives of an oscillating [electric dipole moment](../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md), whose radiated field is proportional to $\omega^2p_0$. It requires the same scatterer with approximately constant low-frequency [electric polarizability](../../../../electric-polarizability.md); resonances and strong [optical dispersion](../../../../dispersion-optics.md) invalidate the simple fourth-power law.
