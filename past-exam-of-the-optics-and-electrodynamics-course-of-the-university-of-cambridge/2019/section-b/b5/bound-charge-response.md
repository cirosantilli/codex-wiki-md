<h1 id="section-b/b5/bound-charge-response">Bound-charge response</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

Put $\omega_0^2=k/m$, where $k$ is the binding spring constant rather than an optical [wavenumber](../../../../wavenumber.md). The steady [complex amplitude](../../../../complex-amplitude.md) solution of the forced [Lorentz oscillator model](../../../../lorentz-oscillator-model.md) is

$$
\mathbf x_0=\frac{q\mathbf E_0}{k-m\omega^2}
=\frac{q\mathbf E_0}{m(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)},\qquad
\alpha(\omega)=\frac{q^2}{m(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)}.
$$

The acceleration [complex amplitude](../../../../complex-amplitude.md) is $-\omega^2\mathbf x_0$. Hence

$$
\langle P\rangle=\frac{q^2\omega^4|\mathbf x_0|^2}{12\pi\epsilon_0c^3},\qquad
\boxed{\sigma(\omega)=\frac{q^4\omega^4}{6\pi\epsilon_0^2c^4(k-m\omega^2)^2}
=\sigma_T\frac{\omega^4}{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2}.}
$$

This is the [scattering cross-section of a harmonically bound charge](../../../../scattering-cross-section-of-a-harmonically-bound-charge.md). The displacement and induced [electric dipole moment](../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) are parallel to the incident [linear polarization](../../../../linear-polarization.md), and integrating their dipole radiation over all directions gives the total [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md) above.

For $\omega\ll\omega_0$, binding dominates and

$$
\boxed{\sigma\simeq\sigma_T(\omega/\omega_0)^4,}
$$

recovering [Rayleigh scattering](../../../../rayleigh-scattering.md) with static [electric polarizability](../../../../electric-polarizability.md) $q^2/k$. For $\omega\gg\omega_0$, inertia dominates: $\mathbf x_0\simeq-q\mathbf E_0/(m\omega^2)$, so its second time derivative becomes frequency independent and

$$
\boxed{\sigma\simeq\sigma_T.}
$$

This is a classical [Thomson scattering](../../../../thomson-scattering.md) regime only while $\hbar\omega\ll mc^2$. Exactly at $\omega=\omega_0$, an undamped driven oscillator has no finite steady-state amplitude; the divergent formula must not be used. Finite [damping](../../../../damping.md) and [radiation reaction](../../../../radiation-reaction.md) regulate the physical resonant response. Coherent contributions from many charges in an extended medium can also require a structure calculation rather than multiplying this single-charge result by a particle count.
