<h1 id="section-b/b4/linear-oscillation">Linear oscillation</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

For the prescribed displacement along $z$, the source [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) is $\mathbf a(t_r)=-a\omega^2\cos(\omega t_r)\widehat{\mathbf z}$. If $\theta$ is the angle from the $z$ axis, $|\mathbf a|^2-(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a)^2=a^2\omega^4\cos^2(\omega t_r)\sin^2\theta$. Averaging $\cos^2$ over one [wave period](../../../../wave-period.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{d\langle P\rangle_{\rm linear}}{d\Omega}
=\frac{q^2a^2\omega^4}{32\pi^2\epsilon_0c^3}\sin^2\theta.}
$$

The [angular distribution of electric dipole radiation](../../../../angular-distribution-of-electric-dipole-radiation.md) has zero intensity on the oscillation axis and maximum intensity in the equatorial plane, with no azimuthal dependence. Its total [power](../../../../power.md) is

$$
\boxed{\langle P\rangle_{\rm linear}=\frac{q^2a^2\omega^4}{12\pi\epsilon_0c^3}.}
$$

The condition $a\omega\ll c$ is the required nonrelativistic condition. The plotted radial coordinate below is angular [power](../../../../power.md), rather than field [wave amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md).
