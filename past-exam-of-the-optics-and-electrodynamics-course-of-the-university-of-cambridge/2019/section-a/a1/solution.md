<h1 id="section-a/a1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A1](../a1.md)

Take $u>0$ to mean that the mirror recedes along the incident [plane electromagnetic wave](../../../../plane-electromagnetic-wave.md). Put $\beta=u/c$, with $|\beta|<1$. The [moving-mirror Doppler shift](../../../../moving-mirror-doppler-shift.md) can be obtained directly by matching [wave phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) on the moving surface. Write the incident [wave phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) as $k_i z-\omega_i t$ and the reflected [wave phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) as $-k_r z-\omega_r t$, where $k_i=\omega_i/c$ and $k_r=\omega_r/c$ are positive [wavenumbers](../../../../wavenumber.md). At the mirror, $z=z_0+ut$, the two phases must have the same time dependence; their constant offset incorporates the reflection [phase shift](../../../../phase-shift.md). Thus

$$
k_i u-\omega_i=-k_r u-\omega_r,\qquad
\omega_i(1-\beta)=\omega_r(1+\beta).
$$

Consequently the observed reflected [angular frequency](../../../../angular-frequency.md) and ordinary [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) are

$$
\boxed{\omega_r=\omega_i\frac{1-u/c}{1+u/c},\qquad
f_r=f_i\frac{1-u/c}{1+u/c}.}
$$

This is a redshift for a receding mirror and a blueshift for an approaching mirror. At small [velocity](../../../../velocity.md), $\omega_r\simeq\omega_i(1-2u/c)$.

Equivalently, the [relativistic Doppler effect](../../../../relativistic-doppler-effect.md) gives $\omega_i'=\gamma(1-\beta)\omega_i$ in the mirror rest [inertial frame](../../../../inertial-frame.md). Reflection there reverses propagation and preserves [frequency](../../../../frequency.md). Transforming the backward-going reflected wave to the laboratory gives another factor $\gamma(1-\beta)$, so $\omega_r=\gamma^2(1-\beta)^2\omega_i=\omega_i(1-\beta)/(1+\beta)$. A single Doppler factor would miss the effect of the return path.
