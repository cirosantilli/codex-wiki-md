<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

An ideal current-carrying wire consists of opposite charge populations, one associated with the stationary lattice and one with drifting carriers. They may cancel in the lattice frame while carrying a net current, as in part (c), up to exchanging the charge signs and current direction. In a carrier-rest description their [densities](../../../../../density.md) no longer cancel because the two populations undergo different [length contractions](../../../../../length-contraction.md). The wire then has both [electric fields](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) and [magnetic fields](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md).

**A neutral steady current in one frame generally becomes a charged steady current in another.** This illustrates how [electric fields](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) and [magnetic fields](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) are parts of one relativistic field rather than separately invariant [forces](../../../../../force-split.md). Real resistive wires also have a driving longitudinal [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) and geometry-dependent surface charges; the infinite uniform filament model isolates the transverse fields of a neutral current and does not describe every field of a complete circuit.
