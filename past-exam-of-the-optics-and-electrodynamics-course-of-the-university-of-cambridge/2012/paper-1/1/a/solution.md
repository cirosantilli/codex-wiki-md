<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) of the reflected beam is perpendicular to the vertical [plane of incidence](../../../../../plane-of-incidence.md), hence parallel to the water surface. At [Brewster's angle](../../../../../brewster-s-angle.md) the reflected [p-polarization](../../../../../polarization-parallel-to-the-plane-of-incidence.md) [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) vanishes; only the [s-polarization](../../../../../polarization-perpendicular-to-the-plane-of-incidence.md) component remains. For incidence from air, approximated by [refractive index](../../../../../refractive-index.md) one,

$$
\tan\theta_B=n_w=1.33,\qquad \theta_B=53.1^\circ.
$$

Incidence is measured from the surface normal, whereas solar elevation is measured from the horizontal. The [Brewster solar elevation](../../../../../brewster-solar-elevation.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{h=90^\circ-\theta_B=\tan^{-1}(1/1.33)=36.9^\circ.}
$$

This assumes an ideally flat, transparent, nonmagnetic interface; ripples, absorption and diffuse light would spoil complete [polarization](../../../../../polarization-waves.md).
