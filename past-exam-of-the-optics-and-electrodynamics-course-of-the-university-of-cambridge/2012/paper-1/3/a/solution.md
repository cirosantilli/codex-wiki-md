<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A transverse static [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) can induce [linear birefringence](../../../../../linear-birefringence.md) through the [electro-optic Kerr effect](../../../../../electro-optic-kerr-effect.md). Define its coefficient by $\Delta n=\lambda_0 K E_{\rm dc}^2$, with the [principal axes](../../../../../principal-axis.md) parallel and perpendicular to the applied field. A [length](../../../../../length.md) $d_K$ then gives $\delta_K=2\pi Kd_KE_{\rm dc}^2$.

For input polarized at $45^\circ$ to those axes, switching from zero retardation to $|\delta_K|=\pi/2$ switches linear to [circular polarization](../../../../../circular-polarization.md). Other input angles generally become elliptical, and axis-aligned input stays linear. The required field magnitude for the lowest-order setting is

$$
\boxed{|E_{\rm dc}|=\frac1{\sqrt{4|K|d_K}}.}
$$

**A Kerr cell is suitable as a switchable [quarter-wave plate](../../../../../quarter-wave-plate.md).** The field can be changed electronically without mechanically rotating the axes; actual switching time depends on the material response and driving circuit. This is induced [linear birefringence](../../../../../linear-birefringence.md), not polarization-selective absorption.[§33–3, final paragraphs: an applied electric field produces birefringence and electrical light switching in a Kerr cell](../../../../../the-feynman-lectures-on-physics-volume-i-by-richard-feynman-and-others.md)
