↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Faraday effect](../../../../../faraday-effect.md) rotates the plane of [linear polarization](../../../../../linear-polarization.md) when light propagates through a material with a [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) component along the ray. In the weak-field transparent regime,

$$
\boxed{\theta=V(\lambda,T)\int\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf l},
$$

with a stated propagation and transverse-basis convention; $V$ is the [Verdet constant](../../../../../verdet-constant.md). For a uniform longitudinal field and sample length $L$, the magnitude is $|VBL|$. A scalar intensity change without a corresponding polarization analysis would not establish this effect.

The microscopic field breaks the equivalence of the two [circular polarization](../../../../../circular-polarization.md) modes: the [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md) and the Zeeman shifts of optical transitions produce different susceptibilities for their opposite senses of rotation. A linearly polarized wave is an equal-amplitude superposition of these two modes. If their real refractive [refractive indices](../../../../../refractive-index.md) are $n_+,n_-$, their relative [wave phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) after the sample is $\Delta\phi=(\omega L/c)(n_+-n_-)$. Recombination rotates the line by

$$
\boxed{\theta=\frac{\Delta\phi}{2}}.
$$

Equal transmitted circular [amplitudes](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) preserve [linear polarization](../../../../../linear-polarization.md). Unequal [absorption](../../../../../absorption-electromagnetic-radiation.md) produces [circular dichroism](../../../../../circular-dichroism.md) and generally [elliptical polarization](../../../../../elliptical-polarization.md) as well, so pure rotation is an approximation appropriate away from strong [absorption](../../../../../absorption-electromagnetic-radiation.md).

A modern motivating experiment sends polarized [laser](../../../../../laser-split.md) light through a [glass](../../../../../glass.md) rod inside a [solenoid](../../../../../solenoid.md), then through a rotatable analyzer onto a [photodiode](../../../../../photodiode.md). Scan the analyzer first to calibrate its angular response. Driving the [solenoid](../../../../../solenoid.md) sinusoidally and recording the detector synchronously separates a small rotation signal from unsynchronized noise; the conversion to angle still requires the analyzer slope and the magnetic-field integral through the rod. A first-party William & Mary laboratory manual specifies this apparatus and measurement procedure.[Faraday Rotation laboratory chapter, pp. 2–7: solenoid, glass rod, analyzer calibration and synchronized readout](../../../../../physics-251-experimental-atomic-physics-fall-2019.md)

Additional controls are equal-magnitude field reversals, zero-field readings before and after each pair, and a blocked-beam pickup test. The odd-in-field rotation is $[\theta_{\rm obs}(B)-\theta_{\rm obs}(-B)]/2$; a fixed analyzer offset cancels. Heating, stressed-glass [birefringence](../../../../../birefringence.md), source drift and field-dependent transmission need separate checks. A single reading near crossed extinction loses the sign because its leading response is quadratic; an analyzer near $45^\circ$ gives a linear small-rotation response.

[Faraday rotation](../../../../../faraday-effect.md) is nonreciprocal. With the magnetic bias held fixed, retracing the path adds rotation in the same laboratory sense rather than undoing it, unlike reciprocal natural [optical activity](../../../../../optical-activity.md). This permits an [optical isolator](../../../../../optical-isolator.md) with a $45^\circ$ Faraday rotator between appropriately oriented [polarizers](../../../../../polarizer.md). One must compare the returned polarization in a consistently defined laboratory basis; merely reversing the sign of $\mathbf B\cdot\widehat{\mathbf k}$ while retaining the old ray-basis convention gives an incorrect cancellation.
