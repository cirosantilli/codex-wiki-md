<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

[wave phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) labels the stage of a physical oscillation at a [spacetime event](../../../../../event-relativity.md). Two observers comparing the same event must identify the same crest, trough or intermediate stage: a change of coordinates cannot change the number of completed cycles between physically specified wavefronts. The continuous [wave phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) can therefore be treated as a [Lorentz scalar](../../../../../lorentz-scalar.md), up to a common choice of [wave phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) origin. This does not assert invariance of the measured [angular frequency](../../../../../angular-frequency.md), which is the rate of change of that [wave phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) along the observer's trajectory.

For [Lorentz invariance of plane-wave phase](../../../../../lorentz-invariance-of-plane-wave-phase.md), write

$$
\Phi=\omega t-\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r+\Phi_0
=K_\mu x^\mu+\Phi_0,\qquad
x^\mu=(ct,\mathbf r),\quad K^\mu=(\omega/c,\mathbf k),
$$

using the metric with signature $(+,-,-,-)$. The [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) $K_\mu=\partial_\mu\Phi$ transforms as a [covector](../../../../../covector.md) because $\Phi$ is a scalar. Raising its index therefore gives the [four-wavevector](../../../../../four-wavevector.md). Explicitly, substituting the inverse boost $t=\gamma(t'+vx'/c^2)$, $x=\gamma(x'+vt')$ gives

$$
\boxed{\omega'=\gamma(\omega-vk_x),\quad
k_x'=\gamma(k_x-v\omega/c^2),\quad k_y'=k_y,\quad k_z'=k_z}.
$$

These are exactly the [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) laws for $(\omega/c,\mathbf k)$. In [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md), $K_\mu K^\mu=0$; the [Doppler effect](../../../../../doppler-effect.md) and [aberration of light](../../../../../relativistic-aberration.md) are two consequences of transforming the same [four-vector](../../../../../four-vector.md).
