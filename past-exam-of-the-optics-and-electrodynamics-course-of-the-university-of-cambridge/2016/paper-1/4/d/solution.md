<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Unpolarized incident light has equal, mutually incoherent $y$- and $z$-polarized intensities. Both components scatter into the same direction $\mathbf v$, so their [coherency matrices](../../../../../coherency-matrix.md) add to

$$
\mathbf J_s=\frac{KI_0}{2}(u_y^2+u_z^2)\begin{pmatrix}u_x^2&u_xu_z\\u_xu_z&u_z^2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

This matrix has rank one when the signal is nonzero. The light is therefore **fully linearly polarized along $\mathbf v$**, with

$$
\boxed{I_{\rm unpol}=\frac{I_a+I_b}{2}=I_R.}
$$

A fixed highly anisotropic needle can act as a polarizing scatterer even for unpolarized input. The equality of total intensities with (c) does not mean the incident states were identical. These conclusions use the leading longitudinal-polarizability approximation; finite transverse response can change the [polarization](../../../../../polarization-waves.md) purity.
