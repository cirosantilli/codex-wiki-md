Now $\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf e=u_y=\sin\theta\sin\phi$, so

$$
\boxed{\frac{I_b}{KI_0}=u_y^2(1-u_y^2)=\sin^2\theta\sin^2\phi\left(1-\sin^2\theta\sin^2\phi\right).}
$$

Whenever nonzero, the scattered light has **the same [linear polarization](../../../../../linear-polarization.md)** along $\mathbf v$ as in (a). Although the incident field points toward the observer, the tilted anisotropic needle can induce a dipole component perpendicular to that direction. An isotropic sphere would instead have an axial radiation zero for this incident [polarization](../../../../../polarization-waves.md).
