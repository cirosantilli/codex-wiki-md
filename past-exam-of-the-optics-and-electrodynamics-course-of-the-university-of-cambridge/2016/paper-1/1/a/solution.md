<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Interpreting the cone as **[Cherenkov radiation](../../../../../cherenkov-radiation.md)**, its angle gives the speed, not directly the [mass](../../../../../mass.md):

$$
\cos\theta=\frac{c}{nv}=\frac1{n\beta},\qquad\gamma=\left(1-\frac1{n^2\cos^2\theta}\right)^{-1/2}.
$$

Combining this with the relativistic [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) $T=(\gamma-1)mc^2$ gives the [Cherenkov mass inference](../../../../../cherenkov-mass-inference.md)

$$
\boxed{m=\frac{T}{c^2\left[\left(1-\frac1{n^2\cos^2\theta}\right)^{-1/2}-1\right]}.}
$$

The material must be transparent at the observed [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md), $n$ is its [phase refractive index](../../../../../phase-refractive-index.md) there, and $n\cos\theta>1$. For $T>0$ this requires $0<\theta<\arccos(1/n)$ for a massive particle above threshold. Radiation of an unspecified other origin would not determine the [mass](../../../../../mass.md) from these data alone.
