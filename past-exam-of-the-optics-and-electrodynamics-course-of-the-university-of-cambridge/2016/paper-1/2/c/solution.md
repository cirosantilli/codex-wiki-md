**A [photonic crystal](../../../../../photonic-crystal.md) has a spatially periodic [dielectric](../../../../../dielectric.md) response on the scale of the optical [wavelength](../../../../../wavelength.md).** For a nonmagnetic, lossless, nondispersive [dielectric](../../../../../dielectric.md), the source-free [Maxwell equations](../../../../../maxwell-equations-split.md) can be written as the eigenproblem

$$
\nabla\times\left[\epsilon_r(\mathbf r)^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf H\right]=\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\mathbf H.
$$

The [Bloch theorem](../../../../../bloch-s-theorem-split.md) gives $\mathbf H_{n\mathbf k}=e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}\mathbf u_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)$ with periodic $\mathbf u$. The allowed frequencies form [photonic band structures](../../../../../photonic-band-structure.md). Coupling of counterpropagating waves near a [Brillouin zone](../../../../../brillouin-zone.md) boundary opens a stop band. A one-dimensional [dielectric](../../../../../dielectric.md) multilayer is a simple example; two-dimensional hole arrays and three-dimensional [dielectric](../../../../../dielectric.md) networks allow more extensive control.

A **complete [photonic band gap](../../../../../photonic-band-gap.md)** excludes bulk propagating modes for every direction and [polarization](../../../../../polarization-waves.md) in a [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) interval. Periodicity alone does not guarantee such a gap: dimensionality, symmetry and refractive-index contrast matter. A missing or altered element can support a localized defect state in the gap, making a cavity; a line defect can guide light. Finite crystals have leakage and wavelength-dependent transmission rather than an infinitely sharp exclusion. Applications include selective mirrors, optical filters, small resonators and integrated waveguides.[Lecture 2: Maxwell eigenproblem; lectures 9–14: Bloch states, one-dimensional gaps and defect localization](../../../../../mathematical-methods-in-nanophotonics-mit-18-369-spring-2016-by-steven-g-johnson.md)
