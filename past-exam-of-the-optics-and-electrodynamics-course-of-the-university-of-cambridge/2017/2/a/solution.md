<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Molecules in clean air are much smaller than visible wavelengths. Their induced [electric dipole moments](../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) undergo [Rayleigh scattering](../../../../rayleigh-scattering.md). Away from molecular resonances, the induced dipole amplitude is approximately $p=\alpha E$, with only weak [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) variation in the [polarizability](../../../../electric-polarizability.md) $\alpha$. The radiation-zone [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) is proportional to $\ddot p$, so the scattered [intensity](../../../../intensity-physics.md) satisfies

$$
\boxed{I_{\rm sc}\propto\omega^4|\alpha|^2 I_{\rm in}\ \simeq\ \lambda^{-4}I_{\rm in}.}
$$

This is the [Rayleigh scattering wavelength dependence](../../../../rayleigh-scattering-wavelength-dependence.md). For equal incident [intensities](../../../../intensity-physics.md), $450\,\mathrm{nm}$ light scatters about $(650/450)^4=4.35$ times as strongly as $650\,\mathrm{nm}$ light. Thus light scattered out of the direct solar beam and into our line of sight is enriched in blue wavelengths.

The dipole mechanism also determines the [Rayleigh scattering angular pattern](../../../../rayleigh-scattering-angular-pattern.md). For [unpolarized light](../../../../unpolarized-light.md), its angular factor is $1+\cos^2\vartheta$; near $90^\circ$ scattering, the scattered light has strong [linear polarization](../../../../linear-polarization.md). The sky's perceived colour depends additionally on the solar spectrum, atmospheric transmission and the eye's spectral response, so the $\lambda^{-4}$ law alone does not predict a violet sky. Over a long near-horizontal path the direct beam loses more short-wavelength light, explaining red sunsets. Large aerosol particles instead produce [Mie scattering](../../../../mie-scattering.md), which is less strongly wavelength-selective and can make haze look whitish.
