<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At an air–glass interface, resolve the incident [unpolarized light](../../../../unpolarized-light.md) into components perpendicular ($s$) and parallel ($p$) to the plane of incidence. The [Fresnel equations](../../../../fresnel-equations.md) give the parallel reflection coefficient

$$
r_p=\frac{n_2\cos\theta_i-n_1\cos\theta_t}{n_2\cos\theta_i+n_1\cos\theta_t},\qquad n_1\sin\theta_i=n_2\sin\theta_t.
$$

At [Brewster's angle](../../../../brewster-s-angle.md), $r_p=0$. Together with [Snell's law](../../../../snell-s-law.md), this gives $\theta_i+\theta_t=\pi/2$ and $\tan\theta_i=n_2/n_1$. Thus, taking the incident medium to be air,

$$
\boxed{\theta_i=\arctan(1.5)=56.31^\circ\text{ from the surface normal}.}
$$

The remaining reflected component has [linear polarization](../../../../linear-polarization.md) **perpendicular to the plane of incidence**. This assumes a transparent, nonmagnetic glass with negligible absorption.
