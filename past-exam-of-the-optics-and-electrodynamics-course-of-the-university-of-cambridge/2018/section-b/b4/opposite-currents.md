<h1 id="section-b/b4/opposite-currents">Opposite currents</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

Keep wire 1's [electrons](../../../../electron.md) moving at $+v$, but let wire 2's [electrons](../../../../electron.md) move at $-v$, so $I_2=-I_1$. The positive charges remain lab-stationary and still feel zero external electrostatic [force](../../../../force-split.md) in the lab. The appropriate rest frame for wire 2's [electrons](../../../../electron.md) now moves at $-v$.

In this frame, wire 1's total line charge is

$$
\lambda_1''=\gamma\left(0-\frac{(-v)I_1}{c^2}\right)
=-\gamma\lambda\frac{v^2}{c^2}<0.
$$

It produces an [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) pointing toward wire 1. The stationary test [electron](../../../../electron.md) is negatively charged, so its electric [force](../../../../force-split.md) points away from wire 1. Transforming back again divides the individual transverse [force](../../../../force-split.md) by $\gamma$, and multiplying by $\lambda/e$ gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{F_x}\ell=+\frac{\lambda^2v^2}{2\pi\epsilon_0c^2d}
=+\frac{\mu_0I^2}{2\pi d},\qquad\text{repulsive}.}
$$

The changed sign arises from the changed transformed charge imbalance, without needing a magnetic-force calculation. More generally the signed result is $F_x/\ell=-\mu_0I_1I_2/(2\pi d)$ for the chosen outward radial direction.
