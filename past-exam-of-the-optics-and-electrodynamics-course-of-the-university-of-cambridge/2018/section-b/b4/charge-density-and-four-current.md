<h1 id="section-b/b4/charge-density-and-four-current">Charge density and four-current</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

Let an object be at rest in $S_0$, with proper [charge density](../../../../charge-density.md) $\rho_0$ and no rest-frame current. In a frame moving at $\mathbf v$ relative to it, the object moves with [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) $-\mathbf v$. A simultaneous volume element is Lorentz-contracted along the motion, $dV=dV_0/\gamma$, while its charge is invariant. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\rho=\gamma\rho_0,\qquad\mathbf J=-\gamma\rho_0\mathbf v,\qquad
\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}.}
$$

The object's [four-velocity](../../../../four-velocity.md) in this frame is $U^\mu=\gamma(c,-\mathbf v)$, so

$$
\boxed{J^\mu=(c\rho,\mathbf J)=\rho_0U^\mu.}
$$

A scalar proper density times a [Lorentz four-vector](../../../../four-vector.md) transforms as a [Lorentz four-vector](../../../../four-vector.md), demonstrating the claim. With the alternative convention that $\mathbf v$ denotes the object's [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) in the observer frame, the spatial component is $+\gamma\rho_0\mathbf v$ instead. Summing the four-currents of different carrier species preserves this transformation law even when the combined current has no rest frame, as for a lab-neutral current-carrying wire.
