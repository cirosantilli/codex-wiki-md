<h1 id="section-b/b4/magnetic-force-between-neutral-wires">Magnetic force between neutral wires</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

A long straight wire carrying current $I_1$ produces field magnitude $B_1=\mu_0|I_1|/(2\pi d)$ at a parallel wire a distance $d$ away. The [magnetic force on a current-carrying wire](../../../../magnetic-force-on-a-current-carrying-wire.md) is $d\mathbf F=I_2d\boldsymbol\ell\times\mathbf B_1$. Hence for equal currents,

$$
\boxed{\frac{|\mathbf F|}{\ell}=\frac{\mu_0I^2}{2\pi d},\qquad\text{attractive for parallel currents}.}
$$

The two forces have equal magnitude and opposite directions. Net electrical neutrality removes a lab-frame external electrostatic [force](../../../../force-split.md) between ideal uniform wires, but does not remove their magnetic interaction.
