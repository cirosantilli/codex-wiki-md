<h1 id="section-b/b2/polarization-dependent-visibility">Polarization-dependent visibility</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

Let the two arms deliver intensities $I_1,I_2$ with unit [Jones vectors](../../../../jones-vector.md) $\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2$. For a perfectly coherent input, the recombined intensity under relative phase $\phi$ is

$$
I(\phi)=I_1+I_2+2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\operatorname{Re}[\mathbf e_1^\dagger\mathbf e_2e^{i\phi}].
$$

Consequently the [polarization overlap in interferometric visibility](../../../../polarization-overlap-in-interferometric-visibility.md) is

$$
\mathcal V=\frac{I_{\max}-I_{\min}}{I_{\max}+I_{\min}}
=\frac{2\sqrt{I_1I_2}}{I_1+I_2}|\mathbf e_1^\dagger\mathbf e_2|.
$$

For equal returned intensities, two [linear polarizations](../../../../linear-polarization.md) at angle $\theta$ have overlap $\cos\theta$ and therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathcal V_{\rm linear,linear}=|\cos\theta|.}
$$

Choose one linear direction as $\mathbf e_L=(1,0)^T$ and a [circular polarization](../../../../circular-polarization.md) as $\mathbf e_C=(1,\pm i)^T/\sqrt2$. Their overlap has magnitude $1/\sqrt2$, giving

$$
\boxed{\mathcal V_{\rm linear,circular}=\frac1{\sqrt2}.}
$$

A non-polarizing [beam splitter](../../../../beam-splitter.md) does not by itself guarantee equal arm losses; if $I_1\ne I_2$, multiply either boxed polarization factor by $2\sqrt{I_1I_2}/(I_1+I_2)$. Perfect temporal coherence does not compensate for polarization or intensity imbalance.
