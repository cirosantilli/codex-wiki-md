<h1 id="section-b/b3/vanishing-field-components">Vanishing field components</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

For a dipole along $z$, axial symmetry removes dependence on azimuth $\phi$. In the [Lorenz gauge](../../../../lorenz-gauge-condition.md), its [magnetic vector potential](../../../../magnetic-vector-potential.md) points along $z$, so it has spherical components $A_r=A_z\cos\theta$, $A_\theta=-A_z\sin\theta$ and $A_\phi=0$. The scalar potential is also independent of $\phi$. Thus $E_\phi=0$, while taking $\nabla\times\mathbf A$ leaves only an azimuthal [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md):

$$
\boxed{E_\phi=B_r=B_\theta=0.}
$$

The generally nonzero components are $E_r,E_\theta,B_\phi$. This statement includes the full dipole near field. In the leading $1/r$ [radiation zone](../../../../radiation-zone.md) term, $E_r$ also vanishes; its nonzero full-field contributions fall faster than $1/r$. Confusing those two levels of approximation would incorrectly suggest four exact zeros everywhere.
