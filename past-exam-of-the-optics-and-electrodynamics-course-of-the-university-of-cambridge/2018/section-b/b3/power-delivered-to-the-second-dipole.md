<h1 id="section-b/b3/power-delivered-to-the-second-dipole">Power delivered to the second dipole</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

Integrating the $\sin^2\theta$ radiation pattern uses $\int\sin^2\theta\,d\Omega=8\pi/3$, so its lossless gain is

$$
G(\theta)=\frac32\sin^2\theta.
$$

The receiver lies on the $x$ axis, broadside to both $z$-directed dipoles. Thus $G_t=G_r=3/2$ and their linear polarizations match. At $f=100\,\mathrm{MHz}$, $\lambda=c/f\simeq3.00\,\mathrm m$, giving

$$
A_{\rm eff}=\frac{\lambda^2}{4\pi}\frac32\simeq1.07\,\mathrm{m^2}.
$$

At separation $r=100\,\mathrm m$, the incident flux is $P_0G_t/(4\pi r^2)$. The [Friis transmission equation](../../../../friis-transmission-equation.md) therefore gives the matched-load [power](../../../../power.md)

$$
\boxed{P_2=P_0G_tG_r\left(\frac{\lambda}{4\pi r}\right)^2
\simeq1.28\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm W=12.8\,\mu\mathrm W.}
$$

The far-field and weak-coupling approximations are well satisfied at a distance of about $33$ wavelengths for an electrically short dipole. Matching refers to the available received [power](../../../../power.md), not interception of all [power](../../../../power.md) in a geometric area equal to the wire's physical size.
