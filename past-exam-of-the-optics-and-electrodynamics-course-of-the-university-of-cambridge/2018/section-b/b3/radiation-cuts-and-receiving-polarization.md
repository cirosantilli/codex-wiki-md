<h1 id="section-b/b3/radiation-cuts-and-receiving-polarization">Radiation cuts and receiving polarization</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

The outgoing radiation field has magnitude $|E_\theta|=|\ddot p(t_r)|\sin\theta/(4\pi\epsilon_0c^2r)$ and $B_\phi=E_\theta/c$. The radial [Poynting vector](../../../../poynting-vector.md), averaged over one cycle, gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{d\langle P\rangle}{d\Omega}=r^2\langle S_r\rangle
=\frac{\omega^4p_0^2}{32\pi^2\epsilon_0c^3}\sin^2\theta.}
$$

Thus the $(x,z)$ cut has a two-lobed $\sin^2\theta$ shape, with zeros along $\pm z$ and maxima along $\pm x$. In the $(x,y)$ plane, $\theta=\pi/2$ throughout and the cut is a circle: there is no azimuthal dependence. At fixed direction the flux falls as $1/r^2$, while [power](../../../../power.md) per solid angle is independent of observation radius.

**[Figure 4](#section-b/b3/image-original-angular-power-cuts-of-a-z-directed-hertzian-dipole-two-lobes-in-xz-and-a-circle-in-xy). Original angular-power cuts of a z-directed Hertzian dipole: two lobes in xz and a circle in xy**.

As a receiver for propagation along $x$, the antenna responds to the incident [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) component along $z$. For linear polarization at angle $\psi$ to $z$, the available load [power](../../../../power.md) is proportional to $\cos^2\psi$: it is maximal for $z$ polarization and zero for $y$ polarization. Equal incident intensity in circular $yz$ polarization gives half the matched-linear [power](../../../../power.md). This polarization response follows from the induced voltage's projection along the dipole axis.
