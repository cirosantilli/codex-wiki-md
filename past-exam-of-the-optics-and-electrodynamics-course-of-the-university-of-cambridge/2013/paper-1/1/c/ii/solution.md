<h1 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a long [solenoid](../../../../../../solenoid.md) at rest, $B=\mu_0nI$, where n is turns per unit axial length and I is the current around a turn. Under a boost along its axis, longitudinal contraction gives $n'=\gamma n$. The circumference of a turn is transverse to the boost and does not contract; [time dilation](../../../../../../time-dilation.md) reduces its charge-circulation rate, measured relative to the moving material of that turn, to $I'=I/\gamma$. Hence

$$
\boxed{B_\parallel'=\mu_0n'I'=\mu_0(\gamma n)(I/\gamma)=B_\parallel.}
$$

Equivalently, the transverse volume-current [density](../../../../../../density.md) of an ideal continuous current sheet is unchanged by an axial boost. The increase in turn [density](../../../../../../density.md) must not be included while leaving the per-turn current unchanged. End effects and the microscopic winding geometry are outside this ideal-solenoid argument.
