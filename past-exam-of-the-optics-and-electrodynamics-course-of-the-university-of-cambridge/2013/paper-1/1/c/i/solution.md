<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use ideal large plates and neglect edge effects. A Lorentz boost normal to the plates does not contract either transverse plate dimension. Charge is invariant, so charge per plate area remains $\sigma'=\sigma$. [Gauss's law](../../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) then gives

$$
\boxed{E_\parallel'=\frac{\sigma'}{\epsilon_0}=\frac\sigma{\epsilon_0}=E_\parallel.}
$$

The plate separation contracts, but the infinite-plate field is [independent](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of separation. It is therefore incorrect to infer a stronger field merely from the shorter gap. The ideal field is parallel to the boost, so there is no induced transverse [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) from this boost.
