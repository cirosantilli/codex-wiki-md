<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Sunlight is approximately unpolarized before its interaction with the environment. For [Rayleigh scattering](../../../../../rayleigh-scattering.md) by small isotropic scatterers, the induced [electric dipoles](../../../../../electric-dipole.md) radiate only their components transverse to the viewing direction. For unpolarized incident [light](../../../../../light.md) scattered through angle $\chi$, the perpendicular and parallel [intensities](../../../../../intensity-physics.md) are proportional to 1 and $\cos^2\chi$. The degree of [linear polarization](../../../../../linear-polarization.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathcal P=\frac{\sin^2\chi}{1+\cos^2\chi}.}
$$

At $90^\circ$ single scattering is fully linearly polarized perpendicular to the [scattering plane](../../../../../scattering-plane.md). This explains the polarized sky away from the [Sun](../../../../../sun.md); multiple scattering and aerosols reduce the ideal contrast.

[Reflection](../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) at a [dielectric](../../../../../dielectric.md) surface treats the two [linear polarizations](../../../../../linear-polarization.md) differently. At Brewster incidence, $\tan\theta_B=n_2/n_1$, the reflected p component vanishes for ideal transparent nonmagnetic media. The remaining reflected glare is s polarized, perpendicular to the incidence plane, approximately horizontal for a horizontal water surface. Away from this angle the [reflection](../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) is generally partially polarized. The transmitted beam also has unequal s/p transmission.

Refraction and internal [reflection](../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) in water droplets likewise have polarization-dependent Fresnel coefficients, producing strong [linear polarization](../../../../../linear-polarization.md) in rainbow [light](../../../../../light.md). These are generic scattering and boundary-condition mechanisms; no biological detection mechanism is required.
