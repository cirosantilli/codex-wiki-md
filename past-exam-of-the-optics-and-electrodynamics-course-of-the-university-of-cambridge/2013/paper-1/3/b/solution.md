<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

With A nearest the observer, the outward order is P then Q. The initially [unpolarized light](../../../../../unpolarized-light.md) becomes x linear, then circular, before reaching the mirror. [Reflection](../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) reverses its [helicity](../../../../../helicity.md). Its return through Q gives y linear, which is blocked by P:

$$
\boxed{J_{\rm return}=PQM QP=-PQ^2P=0.}
$$

Hence **the ideal reflected filter is dark and the target cannot be seen through it**. This is a double-pass polarization rejection effect, not unequal one-pass transmission from its two sides. Real mirrors at oblique incidence, imperfect retardance, finite [bandwidth](../../../../../bandwidth-signal-processing.md) and depolarization can leave leakage.
