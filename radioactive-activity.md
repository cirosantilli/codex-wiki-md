# Radioactive activity

↑ **Parent:** [Radioactive decay](radioactive-decay.md)

With $n$ parent [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md) present, the conditional expected number of transformations in a short interval is approximately $\lambda n\Delta t$. The corresponding [radioactive activity](radioactive-activity.md) is $A=\lambda n$. Averaging over the [finite-population radioactive decay](finite-population-radioactive-decay.md) model gives $\overline A(t)=\lambda\overline N(t)$. Its unit is the [becquerel](becquerel.md).[University Physics Volume 3 by Samuel J. Ling and others; Equations 10.17–10.20 and the following becquerel definition](university-physics-volume-3-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others/10-3-radioactive-decay.md)

For a finite counting interval, depletion matters. Suppose each transformation is recorded independently with [detection efficiency](detection-efficiency.md) $\varepsilon$, with at most one count per transformation and no background. The [expected value](expected-value-split.md) of the count between $t$ and $t+\Delta t$ is

$$
\mathbb E[C]=\varepsilon\overline N(t)(1-e^{-\lambda\Delta t}).
$$

Only when $\lambda\Delta t\ll1$ does this become approximately $\varepsilon\overline A(t)\Delta t$. A count rate therefore needs a detector model before it can be interpreted as the source's [radioactive activity](radioactive-activity.md).
