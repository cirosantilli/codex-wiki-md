# Shear instability

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrodynamic stability](hydrodynamic-stability-split.md)

A shear instability is growth of disturbances that draw kinetic energy from spatial variation of a base-flow velocity. The energy-transfer term involves the mean velocity gradient and perturbation velocity correlations. A velocity gradient alone does not guarantee instability: viscosity, stratification, boundaries and the profile shape matter. [Kelvin-Helmholtz instability](kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md) is a central interfacial example. For a smooth inviscid parallel shear flow, [Rayleigh's inflection-point theorem](rayleigh-s-inflection-point-theorem.md) makes an interior inflection point necessary for an exponentially growing two-dimensional normal mode; it is not sufficient. Applying this local parallel-flow criterion to a slowly varying [boundary layer](boundary-layer.md) is a stability heuristic rather than a complete viscous stability calculation.
