# Chemical reaction

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reaction)

A [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md) interconverts [chemical species](chemical-species.md), as defined by the [IUPAC Gold Book, chemical reaction](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/C01033). The consumed species are [reactants](reactant.md); the formed species are [reaction products](product-chemistry.md). In ordinary [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction.md), [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) are rearranged while each [chemical element](chemical-element.md)'s total atom count is conserved. For a complete [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md), net [electric charge](electric-charge.md) is conserved too.

For example, the balanced [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md)

$$
2\mathrm{H}_2+\mathrm{O}_2\longrightarrow2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}
$$

describes conversion of [molecular hydrogen](molecular-hydrogen.md) and [molecular oxygen](molecular-oxygen.md) into [water](water.md). Both sides contain four [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) and two [oxygen](oxygen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). Three [reactant](reactant.md) [molecules](molecule.md) become two [reaction product](product-chemistry.md) [molecules](molecule.md): atom counts are conserved, but molecule counts need not be. See [Flowers et al., Chemistry 2e, balancing chemical equations](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/4-1-writing-and-balancing-chemical-equations).

The coefficients describe net consumption and formation, without asserting that three [molecules](molecule.md) collide in one step. A balanced [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) supplies quantitative constraints; it does not determine how quickly the [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md) occurs, or whether [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md) prevents complete conversion. Those questions need further information. Keeping these roles distinct makes [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md) useful even when the microscopic details are complicated.
