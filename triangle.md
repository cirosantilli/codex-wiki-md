# Triangle

↑ **Parent:** [Euclidean geometry](euclidean-geometry.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle)

A [triangle](triangle.md) is the [convex hull](convex-hull.md) of three noncollinear [points](point-geometry.md): the three vertices must not lie on one [line](straight-line.md). Its boundary consists of the three joining [line segments](line-segment.md); depending on context, "[triangle](triangle.md)" can mean this boundary or the filled region it encloses. Here a [triangle](triangle.md)'s [area](surface-area.md) refers to the filled region. The noncollinearity assumption excludes a collapsed shape with zero [area](surface-area.md), for which ordinary interior [angles](angle.md) are not defined.

For example, the positions $(0,0)$, $(4,0)$ and $(0,3)$ form a [right triangle](right-triangle.md) with a [right angle](right-angle.md) at the origin. Taking the side of [length](length.md) $4$ as its base gives perpendicular height $3$, so its [area](surface-area.md) is $4\cdot3/2=6$. The height is measured at a [right angle](right-angle.md) to the chosen base; it is not generally the [length](length.md) of another side. In this example it happens to be a side because the [triangle](triangle.md) is a [right triangle](right-triangle.md).

By contrast, side [lengths](length.md) $3,4,6$ define a valid [triangle](triangle.md): each side is shorter than the sum of the other two, as required by the strict [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md). But $3^2+4^2=25\ne36=6^2$, so this is not a [right triangle](right-triangle.md) and the [Pythagorean theorem](pythagorean-theorem.md) cannot calculate its longest side from the other two. The [law of cosines](law-of-cosines.md) instead gives the opposite interior [angle](angle.md) $C$ through

$$
\cos C=\frac{3^2+4^2-6^2}{2\cdot3\cdot4}=-\frac{11}{24},\qquad C\approx117.28^\circ.
$$

This example separates the definition of a [triangle](triangle.md) from the additional assumption of a [right angle](right-angle.md).

Every [triangle](triangle.md) in the [Euclidean plane](euclidean-plane.md) has interior [angles](angle.md) summing to $\pi$ [radians](radian.md), a result proved in [Euclid's Elements](euclid-s-elements.md) I.32.[Euclid, Elements I.32](https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/elements/bookI/propI32.html) It lets two measured [angles](angle.md) determine the third, and underlies the angle-angle criterion for [similar triangles](similar-triangle.md). The qualification "[Euclidean plane](euclidean-plane.md)" matters: the earlier [spherical triangle](spherical-triangle.md) demonstrates a different sum.
