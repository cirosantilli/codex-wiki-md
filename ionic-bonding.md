# Ionic bonding

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionic_bonding)

[Ionic bonding](ionic-bonding.md) binds oppositely charged [ions](ion.md) through attraction governed by [electrostatics](electrostatics.md). In ordinary solid [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md), each [cation](cation.md) interacts with many [anions](anion.md), and also with other [cations](cation.md): attraction and repulsion together contribute to the binding of an extended [ionic crystal](ionic-crystal.md). The [chemical formula](chemical-formula.md) $\mathrm{NaCl}$ specifies equal numbers of the two kinds of [ions](ion.md); it does not divide the [solid](solid.md) into isolated two-atom [molecules](molecule.md).[§7.1, “The Formation of Ionic Compounds”, Figure 7.3](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others.md)

This distinction explains a striking combination of properties: ordinary dry [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md) conducts [electric current](electric-current.md) poorly, while its molten form or a sufficiently concentrated [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) in [water](water.md) conducts well. The [ions](ion.md) already exist in the [solid](solid.md); their ability to move through the sample changes. A [conductivity experiment on salt solutions](conductivity-experiment-on-salt-solutions.md) tests this transport picture, rather than simply asserting that charged constituents must make every material conductive.
