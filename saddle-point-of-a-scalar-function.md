# Saddle point of a scalar function

↑ **Parent:** [Critical point](critical-point.md)

A [saddle point of a scalar function](saddle-point-of-a-scalar-function.md) is a [stationary point](stationary-point.md) with nearby values both greater and less than its value. An indefinite [Hessian matrix](hessian-matrix.md) proves this by choosing directions with opposite signs of the quadratic term. A degenerate [Hessian matrix](hessian-matrix.md) may require higher-order terms: $x^2-y^4$ has a [saddle point of a scalar function](saddle-point-of-a-scalar-function.md) at the origin despite a zero quadratic coefficient in the $y$ direction. This is distinct from a [saddle equilibrium](saddle-equilibrium.md) of a [dynamical system](dynamical-system.md).
