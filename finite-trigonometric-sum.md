# Finite trigonometric sum

↑ **Parent:** [Finite geometric series](finite-geometric-series.md)

Taking the real and imaginary parts of the [finite geometric series](finite-geometric-series.md) $\sum_{m=1}^N e^{im\theta}$ yields

$$
\sum_{m=1}^N\cos(m\theta)=\frac{\sin(N\theta/2)\cos((N+1)\theta/2)}{\sin(\theta/2)},\qquad\sum_{m=1}^N\sin(m\theta)=\frac{\sin(N\theta/2)\sin((N+1)\theta/2)}{\sin(\theta/2)}.
$$

These formulas apply when $\theta\notin2\pi\mathbb Z$. At multiples of $2\pi$, the sums are directly $N$ and $0$. Complex exponentials turn two real trigonometric summations into one [finite geometric series](finite-geometric-series.md).
