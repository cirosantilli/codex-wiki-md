# Percolation critical probability

↑ **Parent:** [Percolation probability](percolation-probability.md)

For the rooted infinite model defining [percolation probability](percolation-probability.md), the percolation critical probability is

$$
p_c=\inf\{p:\theta(p)>0\}.
$$

Below $p_c$, the root has [probability](probability.md) zero of belonging to an [infinite percolation cluster](infinite-percolation-cluster.md). Above $p_c$, that [probability](probability.md) is positive, by the definition and the [monotone coupling of Bernoulli percolation](monotone-coupling-of-bernoulli-percolation.md). The definition alone does not determine behavior at $p_c$, or the numerical value of $p_c$: these require results specific to the [graph](graph-split.md) and the model.

A finite [graph](graph-split.md) with $M$ [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) has $2^M$ open/closed configurations. The [probability](probability.md) of any connectivity [event](event.md) is a finite sum of terms $p^k(1-p)^{M-k}$, hence a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) in $p$. The square's $2p^2-p^4$ is an example. These finite [probabilities](probability.md) vary smoothly, although an infinite-system limit can distinguish a whole interval with $\theta(p)=0$ from one with $\theta(p)>0$. A steep finite connection curve and a mathematically proved [percolation critical probability](percolation-critical-probability.md) are different kinds of information.
