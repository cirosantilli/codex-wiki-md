# Fermi-volume conservation in two hybridized electron bands

↑ **Parent:** [Band hybridization](band-hybridization.md)

For two isotropic electron-like bands with equal spin degeneracy, conserving total [carrier density](carrier-density.md) conserves the sum of their occupied momentum-space volumes. If both uncoupled radii equal $k_0$,

$$
k_{F,-}^3+k_{F,+}^3=2k_0^3.
$$

Only when the upper pocket is empty does this imply a single [Fermi wavevector](fermi-wavevector.md) $k_F=2^{1/3}k_0$; a nonzero mixing matrix element alone does not imply that emptying.

For a counterexample at arbitrarily weak coupling, take $E_a=Ak^2$, $E_b=C+Bk^2$, $A>B>0$, and $C=(A-B)k_0^2$. At the uncoupled [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) $\mu_0=Ak_0^2$, both pockets have radius $k_0$. Let $\mu=\mu_0+\delta\mu$ and $y=k_F^2-k_0^2$. The [characteristic equation](characteristic-equation-of-a-constant-coefficient-differential-equation.md) at the [Fermi level](fermi-level.md) becomes

$$
(\delta\mu-Ay)(\delta\mu-By)=\alpha^2.
$$

Fixed density cancels the first-order chemical-potential shift. Thus $\delta\mu=O(\alpha^2)$ and $k_{F,\mp}=k_0\pm\alpha/(2k_0\sqrt{AB})+O(\alpha^2)$: both pockets remain occupied at small coupling. This expansion assumes fixed $A,B>0$ before taking $\alpha\to0$.
