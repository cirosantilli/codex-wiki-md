# Smith normal form supplies a kernel basis

↑ **Parent:** [Smith normal form](smith-normal-form.md)

Let $M$ be an $m$-by-$n$ [matrix](matrix.md) over a [principal ideal domain](principal-ideal-domain.md) $R$. Choose [Smith normal form](smith-normal-form.md) $PMQ=D$ with exactly $r$ nonzero diagonal entries. Since $P$ and $Q$ are [invertible matrices](invertible-matrix.md), $Mx=0$ is equivalent to $D(Q^{-1}x)=0$. The [integral domain](integral-domain.md) property forces the first $r$ coordinates of $Q^{-1}x$ to vanish, so $Qe_{r+1},\ldots,Qe_n$ are a [basis of a module](basis-of-a-module.md) for the [kernel](kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md). These vectors form part of a [basis of a module](basis-of-a-module.md) of $R^n$, which is stronger than merely finding a nonzero vector in the [kernel](kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) over the [fraction field](field-of-fractions.md).
