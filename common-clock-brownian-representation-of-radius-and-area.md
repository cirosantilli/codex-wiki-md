# Common-clock Brownian representation of radius and area

↑ **Parent:** [Orthogonality of the radial martingale and planar Brownian area](orthogonality-of-the-radial-martingale-and-planar-brownian-area.md)

Jointly time-change the radial and area martingales by the inverse of $A=\int R^2ds$. The bracket matrix becomes $uI_2$, so the [Lévy characterization of multidimensional Brownian motion](levy-characterization-of-multidimensional-brownian-motion.md) gives two independent Brownian coordinates. The clock is strictly increasing; if it were finite at infinity, the radial martingale would converge and $R_t^2=2t+O(1)$ would force its integral to diverge. Thus $R_t^2=2W_{A(t)}+2t$ and $Z_t=B_{A(t)}$ with $W,B$ independent.
