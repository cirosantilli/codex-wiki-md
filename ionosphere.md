# Ionosphere

↑ **Parent:** [Upper atmosphere](upper-atmosphere.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionosphere)

The ionosphere is a region of the [upper atmosphere](upper-atmosphere.md) of [Earth](earth.md) with substantial partial [ionization](ionization.md). Its mobile [electrons](electron.md) and [ions](ion.md) constitute a [plasma](plasma-physics.md) mixed with neutral gas. For [radio communication](radio-communication.md), the crucial question is whether a chosen [radio wave](radio-wave.md) returns toward the ground, is absorbed, or passes through. A [skywave](skywave.md) can connect stations beyond their direct view across the curved surface of [Earth](earth.md), while [wave interference](interference-wave-propagation.md) between a [ground wave](ground-wave.md) and a [skywave](skywave.md) can make reception fade even when the [radio transmitter](radio-transmitter.md) remains steady.

The [ionosphere](ionosphere.md) is useful both as a route for [radio communication](radio-communication.md) and as a remote laboratory: varying the probing [frequency](frequency.md) changes how deeply the [radio wave](radio-wave.md) penetrates the [plasma](plasma-physics.md). The [Appleton-Barnett radio interference experiment](appleton-barnett-radio-interference-experiment.md) turned an unwanted fluctuation in reception into a measurement of the overhead route. [Cold-plasma radio dispersion](cold-plasma-radio-dispersion.md) explains why this is a tunable medium, while [ionospheric virtual height](ionospheric-virtual-height.md) explains why a measured delay is not automatically an altitude.
