# Integral mean bound for an odd square-root transform

↑ **Parent:** [Odd square-root transform of a normalized univalent function](odd-square-root-transform-of-a-normalized-univalent-function.md)

Suppose $h(z)^2=f(z^2)$ is the [odd square-root transform of a normalized univalent function](odd-square-root-transform-of-a-normalized-univalent-function.md). The [Koebe growth theorem](koebe-growth-theorem.md) gives $|h(z)|\leq|z|/(1-|z|^2)$, so its radius-$r$ image has [area](surface-area.md) at most $\pi r^2/(1-r^2)^2$. Write $h=\sum c_nz^n$ and $M(r)=\sum|c_n|^2r^{2n}$, the mean-square boundary [integral](integral-split.md) by the [Parseval identity](parseval-identity.md). The [area formula for a univalent power series](area-formula-for-a-univalent-power-series.md) gives $M'(r)=2\operatorname{Area}(h(r\mathbb D))/(\pi r)\leq2r/(1-r^2)^2$. Integrating from zero proves the bound. The relation $|h(\sqrt r e^{i\theta})|^2=|f(re^{2i\theta})|$ then gives the corresponding first absolute mean bound $r/(1-r)$ for $f$.
