<h1 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $\phi\in V'$ and $v\in V$,

$$
(I'\phi)(v)=\phi(Iv)=\phi(v),
$$

so $I'$ is the [identity map](../../../../../identity-function.md) on the [dual space](../../../../../dual-space-split.md). If $S:U\to V$ and $T:V\to W$, then, for $\psi\in W'$ and $u\in U$,

$$
\bigl((TS)'\psi\bigr)(u)=\psi(T(Su))
=\bigl(S'(T'\psi)\bigr)(u).
$$

Equality on all vectors proves

$$
\boxed{(TS)'=S'T'.}
$$

Apply this to $RR^{-1}=R^{-1}R=I$. It gives both $R'(R^{-1})'=I'$ and $(R^{-1})'R'=I'$. Consequently the [dual map](../../../../../transpose-of-a-linear-map.md) is invertible and

$$
\boxed{(R')^{-1}=(R^{-1})'.}
$$
