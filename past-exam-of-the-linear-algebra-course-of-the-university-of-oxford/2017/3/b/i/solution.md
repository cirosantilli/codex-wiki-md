↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The integral is conjugate-linear in $f$, linear in $g$, and conjugate-symmetric, by the corresponding properties of the integral and of [complex conjugation](../../../../../complex-conjugation.md). Also,

$$
\langle f,f\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x)|^2e^{-x^2}\,dx\geq0.
$$

If a [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $f$ is not zero, there is a real point $x_0$ with $f(x_0)\ne0$: a nonzero [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) cannot vanish at every real point. By [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md), $|f|$ has a positive lower bound on a sufficiently small closed interval about $x_0$. The continuous function $e^{-x^2}$ also has a positive lower bound there. The integral over this interval is strictly positive. Hence

$$
\boxed{\langle f,f\rangle=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ f=0.}
$$

Together with the finiteness allowed in the question, these properties prove that the formula defines an [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) on $V=\mathbb C[x]$.
