<h1 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $e_1,\ldots,e_d$ be the given [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md), with $Te_j=\lambda_je_j$. From the defining identity of the [adjoint operator](../../../../../adjoint-operator.md),

$$
\langle e_i,T^*e_j\rangle
=\langle Te_i,e_j\rangle
=\overline\lambda_i\delta_{ij}.
$$

These coordinates prove $T^*e_j=\overline\lambda_je_j$ without assuming a [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) formula. Therefore

$$
T^*Te_j=|\lambda_j|^2e_j=TT^*e_j
$$

for every [basis](../../../../../basis.md) vector. Thus $T$ is a [normal operator](../../../../../normal-operator.md), and

$$
\boxed{T^*T=TT^*,\qquad
T^*=T\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \lambda_j\in\mathbb R\text{ for every }j.}
$$

The condition of possessing an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) is already part of the hypothesis; real [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) alone would not imply [self-adjointness](../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md) for an arbitrary [linear transformation](../../../../../linear-map.md).
