<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

**No.** Over any [field](../../../../../field.md) $F$, take $V=F^2$, let $T=I$ be the [identity operator](../../../../../identity-operator.md), and choose the [vector subspace](../../../../../vector-subspace.md)

$$
U=\operatorname{span}\{e_1\},\qquad e_1=(1,0),\quad e_2=(0,1).
$$

Both $U$ and the [quotient vector space](../../../../../quotient-vector-space.md) $V/U$ have [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) one. A one-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) has no nonzero [proper vector subspace](../../../../../proper-vector-subspace.md), so neither can have a nonzero proper [invariant subspace](../../../../../invariant-subspace.md). The [induced linear map on a quotient vector space](../../../../../induced-linear-map-on-a-quotient-vector-space.md) is again the [identity operator](../../../../../identity-operator.md).

However, $L=\operatorname{span}\{e_2\}$ is another nonzero [proper vector subspace](../../../../../proper-vector-subspace.md) of $V$, and $T(L)=L$. Hence

$$
\boxed{L\ne U\text{ is also a nonzero proper }T\text{-invariant subspace}.}
$$

The hypotheses do not prevent a [direct sum](../../../../../direct-sum.md) $V=U\oplus L$ whose two summands are [invariant subspaces](../../../../../invariant-subspace.md).
