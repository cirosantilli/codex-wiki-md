<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

For a [vector subspace](../../../../../vector-subspace.md) $U\subseteq V$, its [annihilator of a vector subspace](../../../../../annihilator-of-a-vector-subspace.md) is

$$
\boxed{U^0=\{\varphi\in V':\varphi(u)=0\text{ for every }u\in U\}}.
$$

Let $\dim U=k$ and $\dim V=n$. Extend a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) $u_1,\ldots,u_k$ of $U$ to a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) $u_1,\ldots,u_n$ of $V$, and let $\varepsilon^1,\ldots,\varepsilon^n$ be the [dual basis](../../../../../dual-basis.md). Every [linear functional](../../../../../linear-functional.md) has the unique expression $\varphi=\sum_{j=1}^n\varphi(u_j)\varepsilon^j$. It belongs to $U^0$ precisely when its first $k$ coefficients vanish. Consequently

$$
U^0=\operatorname{span}\{\varepsilon^{k+1},\ldots,\varepsilon^n\},\qquad
\boxed{\dim U+\dim U^0=\dim V}.
$$

The listed [dual basis](../../../../../dual-basis.md) elements are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md), so counting them proves the formula, including $U=0$ and $U=V$.
