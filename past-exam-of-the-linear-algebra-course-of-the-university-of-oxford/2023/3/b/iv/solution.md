<h1 id="3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [iv](../iv.md)

Suppose some exponent $q_j\geq2$. Define

$$
h(x)=\frac{m_T(x)}{f_j(x)^2},\qquad N=f_j(T).
$$

Part (iii) makes $N$ a [normal operator](../../../../../normal-operator.md). For every $v\in V$, put $w=h(T)v$. The [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) identity gives

$$
N^2w=f_j(T)^2h(T)v=m_T(T)v=0.
$$

Part (ii), applied to $N$, forces $Nw=0$. As this holds for every $v$,

$$
0=Nh(T)=\left(\frac{m_T}{f_j}\right)(T).
$$

This contradicts minimality: $m_T/f_j$ is a [monic polynomial](../../../../../monic-polynomial.md) of smaller degree annihilating $T$. Therefore the [minimal polynomial of a normal operator](../../../../../minimal-polynomial-of-a-normal-operator.md) is a [square-free polynomial](../../../../../square-free-polynomial.md) over either of the given [fields](../../../../../field.md):

$$
\boxed{q_1=\cdots=q_r=1,\qquad m_T=f_1\cdots f_r}.
$$

This argument does not require the [irreducible polynomials](../../../../../irreducible-polynomial.md) to be linear, so it also covers the real case.
