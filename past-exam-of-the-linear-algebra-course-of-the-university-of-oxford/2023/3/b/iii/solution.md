<h1 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [iii](../iii.md)

For a [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^d a_jx^j$, the defining identity and uniqueness of the [adjoint operator](../../../../../adjoint-operator.md) give

$$
f(T)^*=\sum_{j=0}^d\overline{a_j}(T^*)^j.
$$

Indeed, the [adjoint operator](../../../../../adjoint-operator.md) of a sum is the sum of the [adjoint operators](../../../../../adjoint-operator.md), the adjoint of $aT$ is $\bar aT^*$, and the adjoint of a product $AB$ is $B^*A^*$. Since the [normal operator](../../../../../normal-operator.md) $T$ commutes with $T^*$, each power of $T$ commutes with each power of $T^*$. Multiplying the two finite sums therefore gives

$$
\boxed{f(T)f(T)^*=f(T)^*f(T)}.
$$

Hence [polynomials of a normal operator are normal](../../../../../polynomials-of-a-normal-operator-are-normal.md), including constant [polynomials](../../../../../polynomial-split.md).
