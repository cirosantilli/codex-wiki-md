At the conventional $5\%$ [significance level](../../../../../significance-level.md), Student 1's [p-value](../../../../../p-value.md) $0.72$ gives **no rejection of equal mean wear**, while Student 2's [p-value](../../../../../p-value.md) $0.009$ gives **rejection of equal mean wear**. The latter also rejects at the $1\%$ [significance level](../../../../../significance-level.md). More generally, compare each [p-value](../../../../../p-value.md) with the prespecified level $\alpha$; no decision threshold was fixed in the data supplied.

Failure to reject does not establish equality, and a [p-value](../../../../../p-value.md) is not the posterior [probability](../../../../../probability.md) that the [null hypothesis](../../../../../null-hypothesis.md) is true. These two-sided [p-values](../../../../../p-value.md) alone also do not identify which material has larger [mean](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) wear.
