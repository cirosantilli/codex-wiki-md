Here $z_\alpha=1.96$, $\sigma(1/2)=1/(2\sqrt n)$ and $\sigma(3/4)=\sqrt3/(4\sqrt n)$. The desired approximate [power](../../../../../power.md) is at least $0.975=\Phi(1.96)$ exactly when the argument of the upper tail in part (i) is at most $-1.96$:

$$
-\frac{\sqrt n}{\sqrt3}+\frac{2(1.96)}{\sqrt3}\leq-1.96.
$$

Rearranging and squaring the positive quantities yields

$$
\boxed{n\geq[1.96(2+\sqrt3)]^2\approx53.5066,\qquad n=54\text{ as the approximate design}.}
$$

This is a [normal approximation](../../../../../normal-approximation.md) to a [sample size](../../../../../sample-size.md) calculation. It is not a guarantee of the exact finite-sample [power](../../../../../power.md) for an arbitrary unrandomized binomial cutoff; the discrete [test size](../../../../../size-of-a-statistical-test.md) and [power](../../../../../power.md) can instead be checked using binomial probabilities.
