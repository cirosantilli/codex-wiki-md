<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a specified [posterior probability](../../../../../posterior-probability.md) $1-\alpha$, an interval $I=(\theta_1,\theta_2)$ is a [highest posterior density interval](../../../../../highest-posterior-density-interval.md) if

$$
\int_{\theta_1}^{\theta_2}\pi(\theta\mid x)\,d\theta=1-\alpha
$$

and every included point has [posterior density](../../../../../posterior-density.md) at least as large as every excluded point, apart from boundary and null-set choices. Equivalently, $I$ is a [level set](../../../../../level-set.md) $\{\theta:\pi(\theta\mid x)>k\}$, with possible choices on its boundary, whose [posterior probability](../../../../../posterior-probability.md) is $1-\alpha$.

This selects the most densely supported values of the [statistical parameter](../../../../../statistical-parameter.md). When the relevant [level set](../../../../../level-set.md) is one interval and has no plateau of positive [probability](../../../../../probability.md), it is the shortest [credible interval](../../../../../credible-interval.md) of that [posterior probability](../../../../../posterior-probability.md). With several separated peaks, the corresponding [highest density region](../../../../../highest-density-region.md) can instead be disconnected, so an interval need not exist. The definition is in the stated parameter coordinate: [posterior density](../../../../../posterior-density.md) ordering can change under a nonlinear change of parameter.
