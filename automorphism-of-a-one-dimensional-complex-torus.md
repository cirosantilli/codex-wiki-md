# Automorphism of a one-dimensional complex torus

↑ **Parent:** [Complex torus](complex-torus.md)

Every identity-preserving [biholomorphism](biholomorphism.md) of $\mathbb C/\Lambda$ lifts to multiplication by a nonzero [complex number](complex-number.md) $\zeta$ satisfying $\zeta\Lambda=\Lambda$. Relative to a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $\Lambda$, multiplication by $\zeta$ is represented by a [unimodular matrix](unimodular-matrix.md) $A\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. Its real [determinant](determinant.md) is $|\zeta|^2$, while complex multiplication preserves orientation, so $\det A=1$ and $|\zeta|=1$. The [Cayley-Hamilton theorem](cayley-hamilton-theorem.md) gives

$$
\zeta^2-\operatorname{tr}(A)\zeta+1=0.
$$

Consequently $2\operatorname{Re}\zeta=\operatorname{tr}(A)\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$, and $\zeta$ is a [root of unity](root-of-unity.md) of order $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, or $6$.
