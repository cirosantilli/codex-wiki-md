<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $Av=\lambda v$ with $v\ne0$. A vanishing [commutator](../../../../commutator.md) gives $A(Bv)=B(Av)=\lambda Bv$, so $Bv$ belongs to the [eigenspace](../../../../eigenspace.md) $E_\lambda$. This is [eigenspace invariance for commuting linear maps](../../../../eigenspace-invariance-for-commuting-linear-maps.md). A [nondegenerate eigenvalue](../../../../simple-eigenvalue.md) has a one-dimensional [eigenspace](../../../../eigenspace.md), so $Bv=\beta v$ for a [scalar](../../../../scalar.md) $\beta$; $\beta=0$ is allowed. Therefore **$v$ is also an [eigenvector](../../../../eigenvector.md) of $B$**. Without the simple-[eigenvalue](../../../../eigenvalue.md) condition, $B$ can mix [vectors](../../../../vector.md) inside a larger [eigenspace](../../../../eigenspace.md), and an arbitrarily chosen [eigenvector](../../../../eigenvector.md) of $A$ need not be an [eigenvector](../../../../eigenvector.md) of $B$.
