<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For [Hermitian matrices](../../../../hermitian-operator.md), $A^\dagger=A$ and $B^\dagger=B$, so

$$
[A,B]^\dagger=B^\dagger A^\dagger-A^\dagger B^\dagger=-[A,B].
$$

The [commutator](../../../../commutator.md) is an [anti-Hermitian matrix](../../../../skew-hermitian-matrix.md). Consequently

$$
\boxed{\alpha=i,\qquad C=-i[A,B]}
$$

makes $C^\dagger=C$. More generally, any nonzero purely imaginary $\alpha$ works. For a nonzero [commutator](../../../../commutator.md) this is also necessary if $C$ is to be [Hermitian](../../../../hermitian-operator.md): comparing the [Hermitian adjoint](../../../../adjoint-operator.md) equations gives $(\overline\alpha+\alpha)C=0$.
