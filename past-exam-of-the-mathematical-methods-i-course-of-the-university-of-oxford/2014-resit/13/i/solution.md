<h1 id="13/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a nonzero real solution use the [Bernoulli power substitution](../../../../bernoulli-power-substitution.md) $u=\sqrt[3]y$. Dividing the [differential equation](../../../../differential-equation-split.md) by $3u^2$ gives $u'+u/3=x/3$. The [integrating factor](../../../../integrating-factor.md) $e^{x/3}$ yields

$$
u=x-3+Ce^{-x/3},\qquad
\boxed{y=(x-3+Ce^{-x/3})^3.}
$$

Direct substitution shows this formula also solves the equation at its zeros. Division temporarily discarded the additional solution $\boxed{y\equiv0}$. Since the real power $y^{2/3}$ is not [locally Lipschitz](../../../../locally-lipschitz-function.md) at zero, the complete real solution family also permits [waiting-time solutions of a sublinear Bernoulli equation](../../../../waiting-time-solutions-of-a-sublinear-bernoulli-equation.md): join a zero interval to a displayed branch at a root of $x-3+Ce^{-x/3}$. At a joining point the branch and its [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) are both zero, so the joined function satisfies the original equation. On each open nonzero interval every solution has the displayed form.
