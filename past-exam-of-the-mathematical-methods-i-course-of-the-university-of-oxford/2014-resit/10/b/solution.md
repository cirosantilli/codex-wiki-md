<h1 id="10/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For positive integer indices, distinct members are [orthogonal](../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) and each has nonzero squared [norm](../../../../norm.md) $\pi$. Thus $\{\sin(nx):n\ge1\}$ is an [orthogonal basis](../../../../orthogonal-basis.md) in the [Hilbert space](../../../../hilbert-space-split.md) of [odd](../../../../odd-function.md) [L2 functions](../../../../square-integrable-function.md) on $(-\pi,\pi)$. The zero-index member must be omitted. More precisely this is a complete [Fourier sine basis](../../../../fourier-sine-basis.md), with generally infinite [L2 norm convergence](../../../../l2-norm-convergence.md), rather than a [Hamel basis](../../../../basis.md) with finite linear combinations. A claim about arbitrary real [functions](../../../../function-split.md) requires suitable integrability and a specified sense of [convergence](../../../../convergence.md).
