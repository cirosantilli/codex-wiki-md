<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Where $y\ne0$ and $x^2\ne1$, the [separable differential equation](../../../separable-differential-equation.md) gives

$$
\frac{y\,dy}{2y^2+1}=\frac{x\,dx}{x^2-1},\qquad
\frac14\log(2y^2+1)=\frac12\log|x^2-1|+C.
$$

The requested point selects the [implicit solution](../../../implicit-solution.md) $2y^2+1=(x^2-1)^2$. The two real branches approaching that point are

$$
\boxed{y=\pm\frac{x}{\sqrt2}\sqrt{x^2-2},\qquad x>\sqrt2.}
$$

There is an important [initial value problem](../../../initial-value-problem.md) qualification: **the differential equation as printed has no classical solution through the specified initial point**. Its right side is undefined at $y=0$, and these branches have an infinite one-sided [derivative](../../../derivative.md) as $x\downarrow\sqrt2$. They satisfy the [differential equation](../../../differential-equation-split.md) on $x>\sqrt2$ and extend continuously to the requested point. Multiplying by the vanishing denominator produces an [implicit differential equation](../../../implicit-differential-equation.md) with a larger domain; it does not repair the original [initial value problem](../../../initial-value-problem.md).
