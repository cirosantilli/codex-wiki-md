<h1 id="11/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $\mu=|b|^2>0$ and $\lambda=a\cdot b$. Expanding the nonnegative squared [Euclidean norm](../../../../euclidean-norm.md) of $c=\mu a-\lambda b$ gives

$$
0\le|c|^2=\mu^2|a|^2-2\mu\lambda^2+\lambda^2|b|^2
=\mu\big(|a|^2|b|^2-(a\cdot b)^2\big).
$$

Dividing by $\mu$ and taking nonnegative [square roots](../../../../square-root.md) proves the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md):

$$
\boxed{|a\cdot b|\le|a|\,|b|.}
$$

Equality holds exactly when $c=0$, equivalently when the nonzero [vectors](../../../../vector.md) $a,b$ are [linearly dependent](../../../../linear-dependence.md).
