<h1 id="12/viii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Viii](../viii.md)

Let $M$ be the [square matrix](../../../../square-matrix.md) whose $j$th column is $A_j$. The [Leibniz formula for determinants](../../../../leibniz-formula-for-determinants.md) is precisely the stated contraction:

$$
S=\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_n}\epsilon_{i_1\cdots i_n}M_{i_1,1}\cdots M_{i_n,n}=\det M.
$$

A [square matrix](../../../../square-matrix.md) has zero [determinant](../../../../determinant.md) exactly when its columns are [linearly dependent](../../../../linear-dependence.md), proving the required equivalence. One direction also follows directly from [multilinearity](../../../../multilinearity.md) and [antisymmetry of a multilinear map](../../../../antisymmetry-of-a-multilinear-map.md): replacing one column by a linear combination of the others leaves only terms with repeated columns, each zero. Conversely, nonzero [determinant](../../../../determinant.md) gives an [inverse matrix](../../../../matrix-inverse-split.md) and hence independent columns.
