<h1 id="8/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For nonzero vectors with angle $\theta$, $|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\sin\theta$ and $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\cos\theta$. Hence $\sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta$ gives [Lagrange's identity](../../../../../lagrange-identity-for-the-cross-product.md)

$$
\boxed{|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|^2=|\mathbf a|^2|\mathbf b|^2-(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)^2.}
$$

If either vector is zero both sides vanish, so this covers every case. Alternatively, expansion of the three [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) components gives the same identity without introducing an angle.
