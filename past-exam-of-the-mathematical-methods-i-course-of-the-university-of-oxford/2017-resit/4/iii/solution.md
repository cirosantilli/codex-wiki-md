<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [hyperbolic functions](../../../../hyperbolic-function.md) satisfy $\cosh^2\theta-\sinh^2\theta=1$, so a useful family is

$$
\boxed{a=\cosh\theta,\qquad b=i\sinh\theta,\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\theta&i\sinh\theta\\-\varepsilon i\sinh\theta&\varepsilon\cosh\theta\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

For real $\theta$ this gives a one-real-parameter family of [complex orthogonal matrices](../../../../complex-orthogonal-matrix.md), which is enough to exhibit the requested hyperbolic parametrization. It does **not** parametrize every matrix in part (ii). For example $a=b=1/\sqrt2$ obeys the constraint but cannot have $a=\cosh\theta$ with real $\theta$, since then $a\ge1$.

Allowing complex $\theta$ gives the full parametrization: $(a-ib)(a+ib)=1$, so choose a [complex logarithm](../../../../complex-logarithm.md) $\theta$ with $e^\theta=a-ib$. Then $a=\cosh\theta$ and $b=i\sinh\theta$. A general complex pair therefore needs a complex parameter; the real-parameter restriction in the question should not be mistaken for a completeness claim.
