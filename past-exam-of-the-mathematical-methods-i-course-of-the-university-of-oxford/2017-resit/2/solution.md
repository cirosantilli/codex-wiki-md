<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

For $x\ne0$, divide by $x^2$ to obtain the [linear ordinary differential equation](../../../linear-ordinary-differential-equation-split.md) $y'+3y/x=x^{-2}$. An [integrating factor](../../../integrating-factor.md) is $x^3$ on either interval not containing zero. Thus $(x^3y)'=x$ and

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\frac1{2x}+\frac{C}{x^3},\qquad x>0\ \text{or}\ x<0.}
$$

Substituting gives $x^2y'+3xy=1$ directly. No classical solution can include $x=0$, since the original equation there would assert $0=1$; constants on the two separate intervals need not agree.
