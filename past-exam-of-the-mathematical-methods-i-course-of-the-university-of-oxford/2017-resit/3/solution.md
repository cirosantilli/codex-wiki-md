<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The real logarithm requires $x>0$. The identically zero function is a solution and must be retained before dividing by $y$. For a nonzero solution, divide by $x$ and make the [Bernoulli differential equation](../../../bernoulli-differential-equation.md) substitution $u=1/y$. This gives

$$
u'-\frac ux=-x\ln x,\qquad \left(\frac ux\right)'=-\ln x.
$$

Integration yields $u/x=-x\ln x+x+C$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{y\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad y(x)=\frac1{Cx+x^2(1-\ln x)},\qquad x>0,}
$$

with each nonzero branch restricted to an interval where its denominator is nonzero. The right-hand side of the first-order equation is locally Lipschitz in $y$ for $x>0$, so a nonzero solution cannot cross the zero solution at a finite positive $x$.
