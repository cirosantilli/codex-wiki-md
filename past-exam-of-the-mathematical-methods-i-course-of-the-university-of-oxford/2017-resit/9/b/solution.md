<h1 id="9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the convention that the [Hermitian inner product](../../../../hermitian-inner-product.md) is conjugate-linear in its first argument and linear in its second. In [orthonormal coordinates](../../../../orthonormal-coordinates.md), $\langle v,w\rangle=\sum_i\overline{v_i}w_i$. It satisfies [conjugate symmetry](../../../../conjugate-symmetry.md), sesquilinearity and positive definiteness. Expanding in the [orthonormal basis](../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) gives

$$
v=\sum_i e_i\langle e_i,v\rangle,\qquad
w=\sum_j e_j\langle e_j,w\rangle.
$$

Applying the [Hermitian inner product](../../../../hermitian-inner-product.md) and $\langle e_i,e_j\rangle=\delta_{ij}$ yields

$$
\boxed{\langle v,w\rangle=\sum_i\overline{\langle e_i,v\rangle}\langle e_i,w\rangle
=\sum_i\langle v,e_i\rangle\langle e_i,w\rangle.}
$$

This identifies exactly where [complex conjugation](../../../../complex-conjugation.md) enters; replacing the first factor by $\langle e_i,v\rangle$ would generally be wrong under this convention.
