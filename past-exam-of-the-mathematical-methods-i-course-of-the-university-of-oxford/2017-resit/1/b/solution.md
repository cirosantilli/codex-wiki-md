<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [root of unity](../../../../root-of-unity.md) satisfies $|z|^3=1$, hence has [modulus](../../../../modulus.md) one. Writing $z=e^{i\phi}$, its [argument](../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) satisfies $3\phi=2\pi k$ for some [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) $k$. The three distinct [complex roots](../../../../complex-root.md) are therefore

$$
\boxed{z=1,\qquad -\frac12+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}i,\qquad -\frac12-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}i.}
$$

They are equally spaced on the [unit circle](../../../../complex-unit-circle.md). Taking $k=0,1,2$ exhausts them, since adding three to $k$ repeats a root and a nonzero [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) of degree three has at most three distinct roots.
