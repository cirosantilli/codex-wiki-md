<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

An [invertible matrix](../../../invertible-matrix.md) is a [square matrix](../../../square-matrix.md) $X$ admitting a [matrix inverse](../../../matrix-inverse-split.md) $X^{-1}$ with $XX^{-1}=X^{-1}X=I$, where $I$ is the [identity matrix](../../../identity-matrix.md). Equivalently, its associated [linear map](../../../linear-map.md) is bijective.

Let $X\mathbf v=\lambda\mathbf v$ with [eigenvector](../../../eigenvector.md) $\mathbf v\ne0$. The [eigenvalue](../../../eigenvalue.md) cannot be zero: otherwise applying $X^{-1}$ gives $\mathbf v=0$. Apply $X^{-1}$ to the [eigenvector](../../../eigenvector.md) equation:

$$
\mathbf v=\lambda X^{-1}\mathbf v,
\qquad\boxed{X^{-1}\mathbf v=\frac1\lambda\mathbf v.}
$$

Thus $1/\lambda$ is an [eigenvalue](../../../eigenvalue.md) of $X^{-1}$ with the same [eigenvector](../../../eigenvector.md).
