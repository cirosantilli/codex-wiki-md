<h1 id="11/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The zero function is a solution on any interval avoiding $x=0$. For a nonzero solution, the [Bernoulli differential equation](../../../../bernoulli-differential-equation.md) substitution $u=1/y$ gives $u'=-y'/y^2$ and

$$
u'-\frac ux=-x.
$$

An [integrating factor](../../../../integrating-factor.md) is $1/x$ on either side of zero. Thus

$$
\left(\frac ux\right)'=-1,\qquad\frac ux=C-x,\qquad u=Cx-x^2.
$$

The full solution set is

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\frac1{Cx-x^2},\qquad\text{or}\qquad y(x)=0.}
$$

Use connected intervals with $x\ne0$ and, in the nonzero family, $Cx-x^2\ne0$. The separate zero solution would be lost by dividing by $y^2$.
