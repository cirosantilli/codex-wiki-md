<h1 id="9/iii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $\Delta=\langle\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf w\rangle=\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf v\times\mathbf w)$. The displayed definitions require $\Delta\ne0$, equivalently [linear independence](../../../../../linear-independence.md) of the original [vectors](../../../../../vector.md). The [vector triple product identity](../../../../../vector-triple-product.md) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf V\times\mathbf W
&=\frac{(\mathbf w\times\mathbf u)\times(\mathbf u\times\mathbf v)}{\Delta^2}\\
&=\frac{\mathbf u\bigl((\mathbf w\times\mathbf u)\cdot\mathbf v\bigr)
-\mathbf v\bigl((\mathbf w\times\mathbf u)\cdot\mathbf u\bigr)}{\Delta^2}
=\frac{\mathbf u}{\Delta}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here the first [scalar triple product](../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md) is $\Delta$ by cyclic permutation, and the second is zero because a [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) is [orthogonal](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) to both its factors. Thus

$$
\boxed{\langle\mathbf U,\mathbf V,\mathbf W\rangle
=\frac{(\mathbf v\times\mathbf w)\cdot\mathbf u}{\Delta^2}
=\frac1\Delta.}
$$

This is the [reciprocal basis involution](../../../../../reciprocal-basis-involution.md) identity. When $\Delta=0$, the proposed [reciprocal basis](../../../../../reciprocal-basis.md) is undefined; for example, three [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) in the $xy$ [plane](../../../../../plane.md) cannot be inserted into the printed quotients.
