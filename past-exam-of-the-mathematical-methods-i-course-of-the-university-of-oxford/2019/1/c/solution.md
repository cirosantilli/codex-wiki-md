<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [complex number](../../../../complex-number.md) $\sqrt3+i$ has [modulus](../../../../modulus.md) two and [principal argument](../../../../principal-argument.md) $\pi/6$. Therefore its [principal complex logarithm](../../../../principal-complex-logarithm.md) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Log}(\sqrt3+i)=\ln2+i\frac\pi6.}
$$

If the source's logarithm means the full multivalued [complex logarithm](../../../../complex-logarithm.md), rather than its principal value, the complete answer is $\ln2+i(\pi/6+2\pi n)$ with $n\in\mathbb Z$. A branch convention is necessary to obtain a unique imaginary part.
