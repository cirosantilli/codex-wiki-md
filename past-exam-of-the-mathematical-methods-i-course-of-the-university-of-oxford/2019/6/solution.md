<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Let $Av=\lambda v$ with $v\ne0$, using the standard complex [Hermitian inner product](../../../hermitian-inner-product.md). Then

$$
v^\dagger Av=\lambda v^\dagger v.
$$

Taking the [complex conjugate](../../../complex-conjugate.md) and using $A^\dagger=-A$ yields $\overline\lambda v^\dagger v=v^\dagger A^\dagger v=-\lambda v^\dagger v$. Since $v^\dagger v>0$,

$$
\boxed{\overline\lambda=-\lambda,\qquad \operatorname{Re}\lambda=0.}
$$

Thus every [eigenvalue](../../../eigenvalue.md) of an [anti-Hermitian matrix](../../../skew-hermitian-matrix.md) lies on the imaginary axis, including the possible zero [eigenvalue](../../../eigenvalue.md); each nonzero [eigenvalue](../../../eigenvalue.md) is purely imaginary.

Now take $Au=\lambda u$ and $Av=\mu v$. On one hand $u^\dagger Av=\mu u^\dagger v$. On the other hand

$$
u^\dagger Av=-(Au)^\dagger v=-\overline\lambda u^\dagger v=\lambda u^\dagger v.
$$

Consequently $(\mu-\lambda)u^\dagger v=0$. Distinct [eigenvalues](../../../eigenvalue.md) therefore imply

$$
\boxed{u^\dagger v=0,}
$$

which is the required orthogonality of the [eigenvectors](../../../eigenvector.md).
