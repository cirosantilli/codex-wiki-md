<h1 id="11/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [11](../11.md)

For a sinusoidal drive of variable positive angular frequency, the average absorbed [power](../../../power.md) is proportional to

$$
\frac{\omega^2}{(\Omega^2-\omega^2)^2+4\gamma^2\omega^2}.
$$

It peaks at $\omega=\Omega$. The [absorbed-power bandwidth of a viscously damped oscillator](../../../absorbed-power-bandwidth-of-a-viscously-damped-oscillator.md) is the separation of its half-power frequencies: solving $|\Omega^2-\omega^2|=2\gamma\omega$ gives

$$
\omega_\pm=\sqrt{\Omega^2+\gamma^2}\pm\gamma,\qquad\boxed{\Delta\omega=2\gamma.}
$$

The usual [quality factor](../../../q-factor.md) is $\boxed{Q=\Omega/(2\gamma)=\Omega/\Delta\omega}$. Large $Q$ means a narrow response band relative to the resonant frequency. The post-drive motion has amplitude envelope $e^{-\gamma t}$, connecting a narrow [bandwidth](../../../bandwidth-signal-processing.md) to a long [ringdown](../../../ringdown.md). For weak damping, the cycle-averaged energy decays as $e^{-2\gamma t}$ and $Q$ is also $2\pi$ times stored energy divided by the energy lost per cycle. The exact instantaneous energy has an oscillatory factor, so this cycle interpretation uses the weak-damping approximation.
