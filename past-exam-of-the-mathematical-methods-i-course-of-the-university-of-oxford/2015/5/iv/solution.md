<h1 id="5/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

If $Au=\lambda u$ and $Av=\mu v$, the [Hermitian matrix](../../../../hermitian-operator.md) identity gives

$$
\lambda\langle u,v\rangle=\langle Au,v\rangle=\langle u,Av\rangle=\mu\langle u,v\rangle,
$$

because both [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md) are real. Thus [eigenvectors](../../../../eigenvector.md) belonging to **distinct eigenvalues are automatically orthogonal**. A [simple spectrum](../../../../simple-spectrum.md) guarantees this for an arbitrary choice of one [eigenvector](../../../../eigenvector.md) per [eigenvalue](../../../../eigenvalue.md).

Within a degenerate [eigenspace](../../../../eigenspace.md), arbitrary independent [eigenvectors](../../../../eigenvector.md) need not be orthogonal. The [Gram-Schmidt process](../../../../gram-schmidt-process.md) always supplies an [orthonormal basis](../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of that [eigenspace](../../../../eigenspace.md). Accordingly the existence of an [orthonormal eigenbasis](../../../../orthonormal-eigenbasis.md) requires no additional condition, whereas automatic orthogonality of arbitrary independent [eigenvectors](../../../../eigenvector.md) is ensured by distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md). Scalar multiples of the same nonzero [eigenvector](../../../../eigenvector.md) are of course not mutually orthogonal.
