<h1 id="9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9](../9.md)

Let $P$ have columns $a,b,c$ and let $G$ have columns $A,B,C$. The cross-product formulas defining the [reciprocal basis](../../../reciprocal-basis.md) give

$$
G^TP=I,\qquad G=P^{-T},
$$

provided the original [scalar triple product](../../../scalar-triple-product.md) $T=\det P$ is nonzero. Taking [determinants](../../../determinant.md) yields the [reciprocal basis involution](../../../reciprocal-basis-involution.md) identity

$$
\boxed{\langle A,B,C\rangle=\det G=\frac1{\det P}=\frac1{\langle a,b,c\rangle}.}
$$

When $T=0$, the displayed reciprocal vectors are undefined, so there is no additional zero-denominator case to evaluate.
