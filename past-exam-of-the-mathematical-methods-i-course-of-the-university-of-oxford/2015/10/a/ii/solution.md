<h1 id="10/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [affine subspace](../../../../../affine-subspace.md) is $S=a+\operatorname{span}(b,c)$. If $b,c$ are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md), their [linear span](../../../../../linear-span.md) is all of $\mathbb R^2$, so **$S$ is the whole plane**. If they are proportional, **$S$ is the line through $a$ in direction $b$**. This line passes through the origin exactly when $a\in\operatorname{span}(b)$. Because $b$ is nonzero, the set cannot be a single point.
