<h1 id="10/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

If $b,c$ are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md), **$S$ is the plane through $a$ with normal $b\times c$**. Its equation is

$$
(x-a)\cdot(b\times c)=0,
$$

so it contains the origin exactly when $a\cdot(b\times c)=0$. If $b,c$ are proportional, **$S$ is the line through $a$ in direction $b$**, containing the origin exactly when $a$ is proportional to $b$. The [linear span](../../../../../linear-span.md) of two nonzero vectors has dimension one or two, so neither a single point nor all of $\mathbb R^3$ occurs.
