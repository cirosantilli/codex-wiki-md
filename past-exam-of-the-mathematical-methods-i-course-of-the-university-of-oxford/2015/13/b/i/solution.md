<h1 id="13/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Because $A$ is a real [symmetric matrix](../../../../../symmetric-matrix.md), differentiating the [quadratic form](../../../../../quadratic-form-split.md) gives

$$
\nabla_v a=(A-\alpha I)v.
$$

Thus the stationary vectors are precisely $\ker(A-\alpha I)$, and at every one of them

$$
\boxed{a=0.}
$$

The zero vector is always stationary. Nonzero stationary vectors exist exactly when $\alpha$ is an [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $A$, and are exactly its nonzero [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) with that [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md). There is no norm constraint here; introducing one would change the stationary-value problem.
