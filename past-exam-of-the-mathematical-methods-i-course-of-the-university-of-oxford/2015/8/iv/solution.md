<h1 id="8/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For an integer $c$, repeated multiplicativity of the [determinant](../../../../determinant.md), with the inverse identity for negative powers, gives

$$
\boxed{\det(A^c)=(\det A)^c\quad(c\in\mathbb Z).}
$$

For an arbitrary real $c$, an invertible real [matrix](../../../../matrix.md) does not by itself define a unique real [matrix power](../../../../matrix-power.md). For example, the scalar matrix $(-1)$ has no real square root. If a [matrix logarithm](../../../../matrix-logarithm.md) $L$ has been chosen with $e^L=A$ and $A^c$ is defined as $e^{cL}$, the [matrix exponential determinant identity](../../../../matrix-exponential-determinant-identity.md) gives the precise formula

$$
\boxed{\det(A^c)=e^{c\operatorname{tr}L}.}
$$

This cannot always be replaced by an independently chosen scalar branch of $(\det A)^c$. For instance, $A=-I_2$ has the complex square root $iI_2$, whose [determinant](../../../../determinant.md) is $-1$, while the principal scalar square root of $\det A=1$ is $+1$. For a positive-definite [symmetric matrix](../../../../symmetric-matrix.md) with its usual positive spectral power, all [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md) are positive and the familiar formula $(\det A)^c$ holds for every real $c$.
