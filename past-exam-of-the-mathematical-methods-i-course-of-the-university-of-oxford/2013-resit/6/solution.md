<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

A real square [orthogonal matrix](../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) satisfies $Q^TQ=I$. A complex square [unitary matrix](../../../unitary-matrix.md) satisfies $U^\dagger U=I$, where $\dagger$ denotes conjugate transpose. A [Hermitian matrix](../../../hermitian-operator.md) satisfies $H^\dagger=H$. The distinction between transpose and conjugate transpose matters for imaginary entries.

For the three displayed matrices, in their source order:

- First: a [unitary matrix](../../../unitary-matrix.md), but neither an [orthogonal matrix](../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) nor a [Hermitian matrix](../../../hermitian-operator.md).
- Second: an [orthogonal matrix](../../../orthogonal-matrix.md), a [unitary matrix](../../../unitary-matrix.md), and a [Hermitian matrix](../../../hermitian-operator.md).
- Third: a [Hermitian matrix](../../../hermitian-operator.md), but neither an [orthogonal matrix](../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) nor a [unitary matrix](../../../unitary-matrix.md).

For the first matrix, $A^\dagger=-A$ and $A^2=-I$, so $A^\dagger A=I$ but $A^\dagger\ne A$. It also fails $A^TA=I$, even if complex orthogonality is considered. The second matrix is real diagonal with entries $1,-1$: its transpose, conjugate transpose and inverse all equal itself. In the third matrix the imaginary off-diagonal entries are conjugate pairs, establishing the [Hermitian matrix](../../../hermitian-operator.md) condition. Its first column has squared norm $1+3=4$, so it is not a [unitary matrix](../../../unitary-matrix.md); it is not real orthogonal, and the complex bilinear first-column square is $1-3=-2\ne1$ as well.
