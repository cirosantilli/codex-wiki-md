<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Squaring first is simpler than expanding the tenth powers. Since $(1-i)^2=-2i$ and $(1+i)^2=2i$, while $i^4=1$, the requested Cartesian forms are

$$
\boxed{\bigl((1-i)^2\bigr)^5=-32i=0-32i,\qquad
\bigl((1+i)^2\bigr)^5=32i=0+32i.}
$$

Thus the [real part](../../../real-part.md) is zero in each case, and the [imaginary parts](../../../imaginary-part.md) are $-32$ and $32$. The results are [complex conjugates](../../../complex-conjugate.md), as also follows because the two inputs are [complex conjugates](../../../complex-conjugate.md) and the fifth-power polynomial has real coefficients.
