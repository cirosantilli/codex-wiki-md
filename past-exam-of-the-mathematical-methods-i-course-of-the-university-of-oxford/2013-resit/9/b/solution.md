<h1 id="9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The denominator for taking reciprocals a second time is $D'=1/D$. The preceding cross-product result therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{\mathbf b'\times\mathbf c'}{D'}=\frac{\mathbf a/D}{1/D}=\mathbf a.}
$$

Cyclic permutation gives $\mathbf b$ and $\mathbf c$. Thus taking the [reciprocal basis](../../../../reciprocal-basis.md) twice returns the original [basis](../../../../basis.md). The matrix statement is the same [reciprocal basis involution](../../../../reciprocal-basis-involution.md): $(B^{-T})^{-T}=B$. Noncoplanarity is essential, since otherwise the defining denominator and the matrix inverse do not exist.
