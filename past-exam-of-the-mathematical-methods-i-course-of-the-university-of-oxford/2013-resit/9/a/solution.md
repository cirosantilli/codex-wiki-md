<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $D=\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)\ne0$ be the [scalar triple product](../../../../scalar-triple-product.md). The [reciprocal basis](../../../../reciprocal-basis.md) satisfies $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf a'=1$ and all mismatched dot products vanish, with cyclic analogues. Apply the given [vector triple product](../../../../vector-triple-product.md) identity to obtain

$$
\mathbf b'\times\mathbf c'
=\frac{(\mathbf c\times\mathbf a)\times(\mathbf a\times\mathbf b)}{D^2}
=\frac{D\mathbf a}{D^2}=\frac{\mathbf a}{D}.
$$

Dotting with $\mathbf a'$ gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf a'\cdot(\mathbf b'\times\mathbf c')=\frac1D.}
$$

The sign is retained: a negatively oriented original [basis](../../../../basis.md) has a negatively oriented [reciprocal basis](../../../../reciprocal-basis.md). Equivalently, if the original vectors are columns of $B$, their reciprocals are columns of $B^{-T}$, whose [determinant](../../../../determinant.md) is $1/\det B$.
