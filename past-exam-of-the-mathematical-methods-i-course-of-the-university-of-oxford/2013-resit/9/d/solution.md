<h1 id="9/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use the preceding coefficient identities with the three prescribed plane equations. They immediately give

$$
\boxed{\mathbf r=s\mathbf a'+t\mathbf b'+u\mathbf c'.}
$$

For example, its dot product with $\mathbf a$ is $s$, because $\mathbf a'\cdot\mathbf a=1$ while the other two terms give zero; the other equations follow cyclically. This verifies existence. If two points satisfied all three equations, their difference would be perpendicular to all three members of a [basis](../../../../basis.md) and therefore zero. Hence the intersection is unique, not merely one possible intersection point.
