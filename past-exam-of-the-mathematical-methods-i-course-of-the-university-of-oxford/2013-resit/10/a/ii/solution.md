<h1 id="10/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

With the same active [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) convention, the $x$ axis remains fixed, while $\mathbf e_y$ moves toward $\mathbf e_z$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{R_x(\beta)=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&\cos\beta&-\sin\beta\\
0&\sin\beta&\cos\beta
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

For instance, a positive quarter-turn maps $\mathbf e_y$ to $\mathbf e_z$ and $\mathbf e_z$ to $-\mathbf e_y$, checking both signs. This is again an [orthogonal matrix](../../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) of [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) one.
