<h1 id="10/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Choose the [unit vector](../../../../unit-vector.md) $\mathbf p=(1,-1,0)/\sqrt2$, which is perpendicular to $\mathbf v$. Then $M\mathbf p=(0,1,-1)/\sqrt2$. Their dot product is $-1/2$, and the [rotation matrix](../../../../rotation-matrix.md) preserves their norms, so

$$
\boxed{\angle(\mathbf p,M\mathbf p)=\arccos(-1/2)=\frac{2\pi}{3}.}
$$

The same unsigned angle holds for every nonzero vector perpendicular to the axis: this is the rotation plane of a three-dimensional proper [rotation](../../../../rotation-mathematics.md). Alternatively, $\operatorname{tr}M=0=1+2\cos\chi$ gives the same rotation angle $\chi=2\pi/3$.
