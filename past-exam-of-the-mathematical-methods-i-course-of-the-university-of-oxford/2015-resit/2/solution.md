<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

[De Moivre's theorem](../../../de-moivre-s-theorem.md) states $(\cos x+i\sin x)^n=\cos(nx)+i\sin(nx)$ for integer $n$. For $n=3$, expanding the cube and taking its real part gives

$$
\cos(3x)=\cos^3x-3\cos x\sin^2x.
$$

Rearranging gives the required identity with

$$
\boxed{a=1,\qquad b=3.}
$$

No division by $\cos x$ is needed, so the proof also covers its zeros.
