<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Let $A_1,A_2,A_3$ denote the three matrices in their printed order. A [Hermitian matrix](../../../hermitian-operator.md) satisfies $A^\dagger=A$, a [unitary matrix](../../../unitary-matrix.md) satisfies $A^\dagger A=I$, and an [orthogonal matrix](../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) is real with $A^TA=I$.

For the first matrix, $A_1^\dagger=A_1$ and $A_1^2=I$. It is therefore **Hermitian and unitary**. It is not real, and even allowing complex entries in the transpose test gives $A_1^TA_1=-I$, so it is not orthogonal.

The second matrix is $A_2=iI$. Thus $A_2^\dagger A_2=(-iI)(iI)=I$, whereas $A_2^\dagger=-A_2$ and $A_2^TA_2=-I$. It is **unitary only** among the listed classes.

The third matrix is real, but direct multiplication gives

$$
A_3^TA_3=\operatorname{diag}\left(1,\frac14,1\right).
$$

It is neither orthogonal nor unitary. Its unequal off-diagonal transpose entries also show $A_3^T\ne A_3$, so it is **none of the three**. The common prefactor $1/2$ applies to the central diagonal entry as well; replacing that entry by one would describe a different matrix.
