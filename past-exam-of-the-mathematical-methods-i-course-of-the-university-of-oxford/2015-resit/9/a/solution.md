<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Set $u=x+y$, so $u'=1+y'=1+u^2/2$. The new equation is a [separable differential equation](../../../../separable-differential-equation.md):

$$
\int\frac{du}{1+u^2/2}=x+C,\qquad\sqrt2\arctan\frac u{\sqrt2}=x+C.
$$

Absorbing the constant scaling into $C$, the real solutions are

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\sqrt2\tan\left(\frac x{\sqrt2}+C\right)-x.}
$$

Each solution is taken on a connected interval avoiding its tangent poles. No constant real solution for $u$ was lost by separation, because $1+u^2/2$ is strictly positive for real $u$.
