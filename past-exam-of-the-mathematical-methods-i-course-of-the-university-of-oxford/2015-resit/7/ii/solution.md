**No, as printed.** Each displayed vector has only two components, so neither is an element of the stated $\mathbb R^3$ without an additional embedding convention. Even if one inserts a zero third component, two vectors span at most a two-dimensional [linear subspace](../../../../vector-subspace.md) and cannot form a [basis](../../../../basis.md) of $\mathbb R^3$. This conclusion does not require guessing a missing third entry.
