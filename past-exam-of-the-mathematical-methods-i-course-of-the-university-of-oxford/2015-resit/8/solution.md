<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

If $Mv=\lambda v$ for a nonzero [eigenvector](../../../eigenvector.md), then $M^2=M$ implies $(\lambda^2-\lambda)v=0$, hence $\lambda\in\{0,1\}$. Conversely, the real-symmetric [spectral theorem](../../../spectral-theorem.md) gives $M=Q\Lambda Q^T$ with orthogonal $Q$. If every diagonal entry of $\Lambda$ is zero or one, then $\Lambda^2=\Lambda$ and therefore $M^2=M$. Thus the necessary and sufficient condition is

$$
\boxed{\text{Every eigenvalue of }M\text{ is either }0\text{ or }1.}
$$

Such a symmetric [idempotent matrix](../../../idempotent-matrix.md) is the [orthogonal projection](../../../orthogonal-projection.md) onto its eigenvalue-one [eigenspace](../../../eigenspace.md), with its eigenvalue-zero [eigenspace](../../../eigenspace.md) as the kernel. Diagonalizability is essential to the sufficiency argument from eigenvalues alone; symmetry supplies it here.
