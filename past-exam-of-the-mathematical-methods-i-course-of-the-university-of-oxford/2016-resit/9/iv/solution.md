<h1 id="9/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

An [orthogonal transformation](../../../../orthogonal-transformation.md) to the [eigenvectors](../../../../eigenvector.md) preserves distance from the centre and writes the surface as $u^2+v^2+3w^2=3$. It is an [ellipsoid](../../../../ellipsoid.md) with [semi-axes](../../../../semi-axis.md) $\sqrt3,\sqrt3,1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{nearest distance from }\mathbf a=1.}
$$

The original $y$ axis is one of the eigen-directions with eigenvalue one, and the centre has $a_y=0$. Consequently $y^2\le3$, attained when the other centred coordinates vanish, so $\boxed{y_{\max}=\sqrt3}$.
