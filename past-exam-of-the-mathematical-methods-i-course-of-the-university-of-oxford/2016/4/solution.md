<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Noncoplanarity makes the [scalar triple product](../../../scalar-triple-product.md) $\Delta$ nonzero, so the three displayed vectors form a [reciprocal basis](../../../reciprocal-basis.md). Their scalar products obey

$$
\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf a=\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf b=\mathbf C\cdot\mathbf c=1,
$$

with all six cross-pairings zero. Therefore, assuming the nonzero $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ needed to define the three given points,

$$
\mathbf X\cdot\frac{\mathbf a}{\alpha}=\mathbf X\cdot\frac{\mathbf b}{\beta}=\mathbf X\cdot\frac{\mathbf c}{\gamma}=1.
$$

Every difference of two of these points is [orthogonal](../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) to $\mathbf X$. The two independent differences span their [plane](../../../plane.md), so **$\mathbf X$ is a normal to the plane**, whose equation is $\mathbf X\cdot\mathbf r=1$. Also $\mathbf X\ne0$, because its scalar product with each specified point is $1$.
