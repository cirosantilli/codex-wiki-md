<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [dimension formula for a sum of subspaces](../../../../dimension-formula-for-a-sum-of-subspaces.md) gives

$$
\dim(\mathcal U+\mathcal V)+\dim(\mathcal U\cap\mathcal V)=4.
$$

Since the sum lies in $\mathbb R^3$ and each plane has [dimension](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) two, the possibilities are

$$
\boxed{(\dim(\mathcal U+\mathcal V),\dim(\mathcal U\cap\mathcal V))=(2,2)\ \text{or}\ (3,1).}
$$

The first case means the planes coincide; two distinct planes through the origin intersect in a line.
