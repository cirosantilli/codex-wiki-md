<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The set is the [kernel](../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) of the [linear map](../../../../linear-map.md) $L(\mathbf r)=\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf r$. It contains zero, and [linearity](../../../../linearity.md) gives $L(\alpha\mathbf r+\beta\mathbf s)=0$ whenever $L(\mathbf r)=L(\mathbf s)=0$, proving closure under [linear combinations](../../../../linear-combination.md). If $\mathbf v\ne0$, it is a plane through the origin; if $\mathbf v=0$, it is all of $\mathbb R^3$. **An arbitrary plane need not be a vector subspace**: for example $z=1$ omits the zero vector. An [affine subspace](../../../../affine-subspace.md) and a vector subspace are not interchangeable.
