<h1 id="8/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Taking [cross products](../../../../cross-product.md) of the two independent spanning vectors gives plane [normal vectors](../../../../normal-vector.md) proportional to $(-4,1,1)$ and $(4,-6,-1)$. Their [cross product](../../../../cross-product.md) is $(5,0,20)$, so the intersection is the line $s\mathbf d$, with $\mathbf d=(1,0,4)$. The closest point to $\mathbf p=(1,1,1)$ is its [orthogonal projection](../../../../orthogonal-projection.md),

$$
\mathbf p_*=\frac{\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf d}{\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf d}\mathbf d
=\left(\frac5{17},0,\frac{20}{17}\right).
$$

The squared distance is $|\mathbf p|^2-(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf d)^2/|\mathbf d|^2=3-25/17$, giving

$$
\boxed{d=\sqrt{\frac{26}{17}}.}
$$

Both plane normals are orthogonal to $\mathbf d$, and $(\mathbf p-\mathbf p_*)\cdot\mathbf d=0$, checking the geometric constraints.
