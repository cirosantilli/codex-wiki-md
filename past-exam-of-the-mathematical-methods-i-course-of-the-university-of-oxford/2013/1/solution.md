<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Rationalizing the denominator gives

$$
\frac1{1+i\sqrt3}=\frac{1-i\sqrt3}{4}.
$$

Hence its [real part](../../../real-part.md), [imaginary part](../../../imaginary-part.md), [complex modulus](../../../complex-modulus.md), and [principal argument](../../../principal-argument.md) are respectively

$$
\boxed{\frac14,\quad-\frac{\sqrt3}{4},\quad\frac12,\quad-\frac\pi3.}
$$

The quadrant matters: the [real part](../../../real-part.md) is positive and the [imaginary part](../../../imaginary-part.md) negative. For the exponential expression, [Euler's formula](../../../euler-s-formula.md) gives $e^{2+i\pi/4}=e^2(1+i)/\sqrt2$, so the same four quantities are

$$
\boxed{\frac{e^2}{\sqrt2},\quad\frac{e^2}{\sqrt2},\quad e^2,\quad\frac\pi4.}
$$

General [complex arguments](../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) are obtained from each stated [principal argument](../../../principal-argument.md) by adding $2\pi k$, $k\in\mathbb Z$.
