<h1 id="12/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The logarithm requires $x>0$ for real-valued solutions. Put $v=xy$. Since $v'=xy'+y$, the equation becomes $v'=v^2\log x$. For a nonzero branch, separation gives

$$
-\frac1v=\int\log x\,dx=x\log x-x+C_0.
$$

Renaming the constant and restoring $y=v/x$ gives

$$
\boxed{y=\frac1{x(C+x-x\log x)}}.
$$

Dividing by $v^2$ would omit the equilibrium $v=0$, so the full solution set also includes $\boxed{y\equiv0}$. The nonzero branches are defined on connected intervals with $x>0$ and $C+x-x\log x\ne0$. A regular solution cannot switch from a nonzero branch to zero at an ordinary point, because the right-hand side is locally Lipschitz in $v$ there. These domain restrictions are part of the full real solution.
