<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Let $A\mathbf v=\lambda\mathbf v$ with $\mathbf v\ne0$ and $A^\dagger=-A$. Taking the conjugate of the scalar $\mathbf v^\dagger A\mathbf v$ gives

$$
\lambda^*\mathbf v^\dagger\mathbf v
=\mathbf v^\dagger A^\dagger\mathbf v
=-\lambda\mathbf v^\dagger\mathbf v.
$$

Since $\mathbf v^\dagger\mathbf v>0$, it follows that

$$
\boxed{\lambda^*=-\lambda,\qquad \operatorname{Re}\lambda=0.}
$$

Thus every nonzero [eigenvalue](../../../eigenvalue.md) of an [anti-Hermitian matrix](../../../skew-hermitian-matrix.md) is purely imaginary; zero is allowed as well.

For $A\mathbf u=\lambda\mathbf u$ and $A\mathbf v=\mu\mathbf v$, compute the same mixed scalar in two ways:

$$
\lambda^*\mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf v
=(A\mathbf u)^\dagger\mathbf v
=\mathbf u^\dagger A^\dagger\mathbf v
=-\mu\mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf v.
$$

Using $\lambda^*=-\lambda$ gives $(\mu-\lambda)\mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf v=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\lambda\ne\mu\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf v=0.}
$$

The relevant orthogonality uses the complex [inner product](../../../inner-product-split.md), not the unconjugated bilinear dot product. Repeated [eigenvalues](../../../eigenvalue.md) do not force every possible pair of eigenvectors within that one eigenspace to be orthogonal.
