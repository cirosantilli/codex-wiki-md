<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

The original [basis](../../../basis.md) matrix has columns $\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2,\mathbf e_3$ and [determinant](../../../determinant.md) $D=3\cdot3-4\cdot4=-7$. Evaluating the defining [cross products](../../../cross-product.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf e_1'=\left(-\frac37,\frac47,0\right),\quad
\mathbf e_2'=\left(\frac47,-\frac37,0\right),\quad
\mathbf e_3'=(0,0,1).}
$$

For instance, $\mathbf e_2\times\mathbf e_3=(3,-4,0)$, which must be divided by the negative denominator $-7$. These vectors satisfy $\mathbf e_i'\cdot\mathbf e_j=\delta_{ij}$, the defining [reciprocal basis](../../../reciprocal-basis.md) relations.

Dotting the desired expansion with the reciprocal vectors yields

$$
\boxed{a_1=\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf e_1'=1,\qquad
 a_2=\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf e_2'=0,\qquad
 a_3=\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf e_3'=2.}
$$

Indeed $\mathbf a=\mathbf e_1+2\mathbf e_3$. The original [basis](../../../basis.md) is not orthonormal, so taking dot products with the original vectors would not give these expansion coefficients.
