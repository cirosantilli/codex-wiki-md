<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $Hv=\lambda v$ with $v\ne0$ and $H^*=H$. Then $v^*Hv=\lambda v^*v$, while taking the [complex conjugate](../../../../complex-conjugate.md) gives $v^*Hv=\overline\lambda v^*v$. Since $v^*v>0$, every [eigenvalue](../../../../eigenvalue.md) of a [Hermitian matrix](../../../../hermitian-operator.md) is real.

If $Hv=\lambda v$ and $Hw=\mu w$, then

$$
\lambda\,v^*w=(Hv)^*w=v^*Hw=\mu\,v^*w.
$$

Distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md) therefore force $v^*w=0$: their [eigenvectors](../../../../eigenvector.md) are orthogonal in the [Hermitian inner product](../../../../hermitian-inner-product.md). A degenerate [eigenvalue](../../../../eigenvalue.md) occurs more than once in the spectrum, counted with multiplicity. For a [Hermitian matrix](../../../../hermitian-operator.md), its [algebraic multiplicity](../../../../algebraic-multiplicity.md) equals its [geometric multiplicity](../../../../geometric-multiplicity.md), so degeneracy gives an [eigenspace](../../../../eigenspace.md) of dimension greater than one; an [orthonormal basis](../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) can be chosen within it.
