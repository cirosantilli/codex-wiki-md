<h1 id="10/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For two nonparallel vectors $v_1,v_2\in\mathbb R^2$, a nontrivial relation $av_1+bv_2=0$ would make one a scalar multiple of the other. They are therefore [linearly independent](../../../../linear-independence.md). Two independent vectors in a two-dimensional [vector space](../../../../vector-space-split.md) form a [basis](../../../../basis.md).

Likewise, three vectors in $\mathbb R^3$ that are not contained in one plane through the origin cannot obey a nontrivial [linear dependence](../../../../linear-dependence.md): such a relation would express one as a combination of the other two, or already make that pair dependent. Thus they are independent and form a [basis](../../../../basis.md) of $\mathbb R^3$. The plane condition here is linear coplanarity with the origin, as required for vectors, rather than affine coplanarity of three arbitrary points.
