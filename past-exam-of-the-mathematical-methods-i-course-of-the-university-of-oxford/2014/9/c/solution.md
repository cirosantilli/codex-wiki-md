<h1 id="9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Put $v=y^2$, so $v'=2yy'$. On an [interval](../../../../interval-mathematics.md) with $x\ne0$, the resulting [first-order linear differential equation](../../../../first-order-linear-differential-equation.md) is $v'-4v/x=2x$. Its [integrating factor](../../../../integrating-factor.md) $x^{-4}$ gives $(v/x^4)'=2/x^3$, hence

$$
\boxed{y^2=Cx^4-x^2,\qquad y=\pm\sqrt{Cx^4-x^2}.}
$$

For real differentiable graphs one needs $C>0$ and $|x|>1/\sqrt C$, with either fixed sign on a connected [interval](../../../../interval-mathematics.md). The points $x=0$ are outside the original equation's domain. At $|x|=1/\sqrt C$, $y=0$ would make the left side zero while the right side is $x\ne0$, so those endpoints are also excluded. The identically zero function is not an additional solution.
