<h1 id="12/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

Multiplicativity of the [determinant](../../../../determinant.md) gives $\langle A,B\rangle=\det A\det B$. Thus **C1 holds**. For $n\ge2$, **C2 fails**: choosing $A=B=I$ and $\lambda=2$ gives $\det(2I)=2^n\ne2$. The same choice $A=B=C=I$ disproves **C3**, since $\det(B+C)=2^n\ne2$. The nonnegativity portion of **C4 holds**, because $\det(A^2)=(\det A)^2\ge0$, but its positive-definiteness clause fails: a nonzero [singular matrix](../../../../singular-matrix.md) that is [symmetric](../../../../symmetric-relation.md), such as $\operatorname{diag}(1,0,\ldots,0)$, has zero self-pairing.

Therefore **this is not an inner product for $n\ge2$**. For the exceptional case $n=1$, the [determinant](../../../../determinant.md) is the scalar entry and the pairing is ordinary multiplication of real numbers, so all four conditions hold. The [determinant pairing is not an inner product](../../../../determinant-pairing-is-not-an-inner-product.md) in higher dimension because it measures volume scaling rather than a bilinear length geometry.
