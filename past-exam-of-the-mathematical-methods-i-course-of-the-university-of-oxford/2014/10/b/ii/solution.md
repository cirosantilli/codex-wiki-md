<h1 id="10/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

First assume $a\ne0$. The first equation is a [plane](../../../../../plane.md), and the second is a [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) centred at $c$. Define its signed normal separation and projected centre by

$$
h=\frac{a\cdot(c-b)}{|a|},\qquad q=c-\frac{a\cdot(c-b)}{|a|^2}a.
$$

The [intersection of a plane and a sphere](../../../../../intersection-of-a-plane-and-a-sphere.md) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{cases}
\text{a circle with centre }q\text{ and radius }\sqrt{r^2-h^2},&|h|<r,\\
\{q\},&|h|=r,\\
\varnothing,&|h|>r.
\end{cases}}
$$

The [circle](../../../../../circle.md) lies in the given [plane](../../../../../plane.md). Its radius follows from the [Pythagorean theorem](../../../../../pythagorean-theorem.md), splitting $x-c=(x-q)+(q-c)$ into [orthogonal](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) components. If $a=0$, the first equation is the identity $0=0$, so the locus is the **entire sphere**, rather than a planar section.
