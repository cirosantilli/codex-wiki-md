<h1 id="11/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [exponential substitution removing a constant first derivative](../../../../../exponential-substitution-removing-a-constant-first-derivative.md), $y=e^xu$, reduces the equation to $u''+\beta^2u=1+\cos x$. For $\beta\ne0$ and $\beta^2\ne1$, solve the homogeneous harmonic equation and use a constant plus a cosine for the particular solution:

$$
\boxed{y=e^x\left[A\cos(\beta x)+B\sin(\beta x)+\frac1{\beta^2}+\frac{\cos x}{\beta^2-1}\right].}
$$

There is a further exceptional case inside the printed condition $\beta^2\ne1$: **$\beta=0$ must be treated separately**. It gives $u''=1+\cos x$, hence

$$
\boxed{y=e^x\left[A+Bx+\frac{x^2}{2}-\cos x\right],\qquad\beta=0.}
$$

This retains the constant forcing's [resonance](../../../../../resonance.md) with the zero-[frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) homogeneous equation. Substitution verifies both branches.
