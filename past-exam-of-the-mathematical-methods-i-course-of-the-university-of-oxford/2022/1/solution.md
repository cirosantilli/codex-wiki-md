<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Let $\Delta=\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)\ne0$. Taking the [scalar product](../../../dot-product.md) with $\mathbf b\times\mathbf c$ in the first expansion kills its other two terms. Taking the [scalar product](../../../dot-product.md) with $\mathbf a$ in the second expansion does the same. Thus the two requested [coordinates](../../../coordinate-linear-algebra.md) are

$$
\boxed{\lambda_1=\frac{\mathbf r\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)}{\Delta},\qquad
\mu_1=\frac{\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf a}{\Delta}.}
$$

The [scalar triple product](../../../scalar-triple-product.md) condition guarantees [linear independence](../../../linear-independence.md) and the existence of these [reciprocal bases](../../../reciprocal-basis.md). The second coordinate is not obtained by merely normalizing the first basis [vector](../../../vector.md): the two expansions use different bases.
