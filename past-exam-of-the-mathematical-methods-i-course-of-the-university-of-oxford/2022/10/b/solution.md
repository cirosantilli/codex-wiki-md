<h1 id="10/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take the [adjoint matrix](../../../../conjugate-transpose.md) of $M^\dagger\mathbf v_i=\lambda_i\mathbf v_i$ to obtain $\mathbf v_i^\dagger M=\lambda_i^*\mathbf v_i^\dagger$. Evaluating $\mathbf v_i^\dagger M\mathbf u_j$ using this equation and $M\mathbf u_j=\lambda_j\mathbf u_j$ gives

$$
(\lambda_j-\lambda_i^*)\mathbf v_i^\dagger\mathbf u_j=0.
$$

Hence $\boxed{\mathbf v_i^\dagger\mathbf u_j=0\text{ if }\lambda_i^*\ne\lambda_j}$. The proof does not assume that $M$ is a [normal matrix](../../../../normal-matrix.md) or [diagonalizable](../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md).

In general the [spectrum](../../../../spectrum-functional-analysis.md) of an [adjoint matrix](../../../../conjugate-transpose.md) is the [complex conjugate](../../../../complex-conjugate.md) of the original [spectrum](../../../../spectrum-functional-analysis.md), not necessarily the same set. The equality of the two sets in the question is an additional assumption, possible for a conjugation-invariant [spectrum](../../../../spectrum-functional-analysis.md); the displayed orthogonality proof only uses the two stated [eigenvalue equations](../../../../eigenvalue-equation.md).
