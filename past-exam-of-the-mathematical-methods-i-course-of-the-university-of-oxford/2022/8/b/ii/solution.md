<h1 id="8/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For nonparallel direction vectors, let $\mathbf n=(\mathbf b\times\mathbf d)/|\mathbf b\times\mathbf d|$. Every joining [vector](../../../../../vector.md) has the same [normal](../../../../../normal-distribution.md) component, $\mathbf R\cdot\mathbf n=(\mathbf a-\mathbf c)\cdot\mathbf n$. Its [norm](../../../../../norm.md) is therefore at least the absolute value of that component. Minimizing $|\mathbf R|^2$ makes it perpendicular to both directions, and hence parallel to $\mathbf n$, attaining the bound:

$$
\boxed{d_{\min}=\left|(\mathbf a-\mathbf c)\cdot\mathbf n\right|.}
$$

The expression printed without absolute values is an oriented separation, not a nonnegative distance. Reversing either line's parametrization can flip its sign. For parallel nonzero directions, this [normal](../../../../../normal-distribution.md) is undefined and the distance is instead $| (\mathbf a-\mathbf c)\times\mathbf b|/|\mathbf b|$.
