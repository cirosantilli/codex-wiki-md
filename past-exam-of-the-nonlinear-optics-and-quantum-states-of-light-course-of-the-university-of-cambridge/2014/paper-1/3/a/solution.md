<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [optical soliton](../../../../../optical-soliton.md) maintains a localized profile because nonlinear refraction compensates linear spreading. A [temporal optical soliton](../../../../../temporal-optical-soliton.md) balances [group velocity dispersion](../../../../../group-velocity-dispersion.md) against [self-phase modulation](../../../../../self-phase-modulation.md); a [spatial optical soliton](../../../../../spatial-optical-soliton.md) balances [diffraction](../../../../../diffraction.md) against [self-focusing](../../../../../self-focusing.md). The relevant coordinate and sign of nonlinearity must be specified.

For anomalous [group velocity dispersion](../../../../../group-velocity-dispersion.md) and positive $\gamma$, a [fundamental temporal bright soliton](../../../../../fundamental-temporal-bright-soliton.md) is

$$
\boxed{A(z,T)=\sqrt{P_s}\operatorname{sech}(T/T_0)
 e^{i\gamma P_s z/2},\qquad \gamma P_sT_0^2=|\beta_2|.}
$$

The identity $(\operatorname{sech}u)''=\operatorname{sech}u-2\operatorname{sech}^3u$ verifies both the width relation and propagation phase. Its [dispersion length](../../../../../dispersion-length.md) $L_D=T_0^2/|\beta_2|$ equals its [nonlinear length](../../../../../nonlinear-length.md) $L_{\rm NL}=1/(\gamma P_s)$. Bright pulses decay to zero background. [dark solitons](../../../../../dark-soliton.md) are [intensity](../../../../../intensity-physics.md) depressions on a nonzero background with a phase change across the dip; black dips reach zero [intensity](../../../../../intensity-physics.md), while grey dips do not. For positive $\gamma$ the corresponding temporal defocusing regime has normal [group velocity dispersion](../../../../../group-velocity-dispersion.md).

A [higher-order temporal soliton](../../../../../higher-order-temporal-soliton.md) has [soliton order](../../../../../soliton-order.md) $N_s=\sqrt{\gamma P_sT_0^2/|\beta_2|}$ equal to an integer greater than one for the special initial $N_s\operatorname{sech}$ profile in normalized units. It breathes through compression and splitting/recombination, rather than keeping one fixed shape. Separated fundamental [solitons](../../../../../soliton.md) can interact and acquire collision shifts. An arbitrary pulse with noninteger $N_s$ is not automatically a pure higher-order [soliton](../../../../../soliton.md); dispersive radiation can carry part of its [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md).

In ideal one-dimensional cubic propagation, dispersion and nonlinearity support stable fundamental bright pulses. Loss, higher-order dispersion, delayed nonlinear response and finite bandwidth change real-fibre propagation. A one-dimensional spatial bright solution also does not guarantee [stability](../../../../../stability-of-a-numerical-method.md) against two-dimensional collapse. Profile preservation, finite-energy background conditions and robustness should be checked rather than inferred from any solitary-looking peak.
