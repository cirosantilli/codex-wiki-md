# Uniqueness of linear combination coefficients

↑ **Parent:** [Linear independence](linear-independence.md)

A family is [linearly independent](linear-independence.md) if and only if every [vector](vector.md) in its [linear span](linear-span.md) has unique [coefficients](coefficient.md) as a [linear combination](linear-combination.md) of that family. For a short proof, subtract two representations to get $\sum_i(a_i-b_i)v_i=0$; [linear independence](linear-independence.md) forces each difference to vanish. Conversely, uniqueness applied to the [zero vector](zero-vector.md) forces every [linear relation](linear-relation.md) to use only zero [coefficients](coefficient.md). This explains the practical role of [linear independence](linear-independence.md): a [coordinate vector](coordinate-vector.md) relative to an [ordered basis](ordered-basis.md) is unambiguous.
