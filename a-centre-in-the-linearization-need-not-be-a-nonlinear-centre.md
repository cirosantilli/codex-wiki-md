# A centre in the linearization need not be a nonlinear centre

↑ **Parent:** [Nonhyperbolic equilibrium](nonhyperbolic-equilibrium.md)

Purely imaginary nonzero [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) make a real planar [linearization](linearization.md) a [centre equilibrium](center-equilibrium.md). They do not control the nonlinear radial drift. For example,

$$
\dot x=-y-x(x^2+y^2),\qquad\dot y=x-y(x^2+y^2)
$$

has the same [linearization](linearization.md) as a uniform rotation. In [polar coordinates](polar-coordinates.md), however, $\dot r=-r^3$ and $\dot\theta=1$. Every sufficiently small nonzero orbit spirals towards the origin, so the origin is an asymptotically stable [weak focus](weak-focus.md), and no nonzero orbit is periodic. Reversing the signs of the cubic terms gives a repelling weak focus. Removing them gives a centre. Thus a nonlinear [centre equilibrium](center-equilibrium.md) requires a further argument, such as a suitable [first integral](first-integral.md); the imaginary pair alone is insufficient.
