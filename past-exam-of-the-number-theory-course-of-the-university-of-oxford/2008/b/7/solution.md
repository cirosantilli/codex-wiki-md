<h1 id="b/7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

Choose a nonzero [integral ideal](../../../../integral-ideal.md) $B$ whose class is $[A]^{-1}$; one can multiply the fractional inverse $A^{-1}$ by a suitable nonzero [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) to obtain such a $B$. By the given bound choose $0\ne\beta\in B$ with $|N(\beta)|\le c_KN(B)$. Put $I_A=(\beta)B^{-1}$. Since $(\beta)\subseteq B$, multiplication by $B^{-1}$ makes $I_A$ an [integral ideal](../../../../integral-ideal.md). Its class is $[B]^{-1}=[A]$, and multiplicativity of [ideal norms](../../../../ideal-norm.md) gives

$$
\boxed{N(I_A)=\frac{|N(\beta)|}{N(B)}\le c_K.}
$$

This proves the class representative bound without accidentally constructing a representative of the inverse class instead.

To determine the [Ideal class group of Q of square root minus thirty-four](../../../../ideal-class-group-of-q-of-square-root-minus-thirty-four.md), write $\eta=\sqrt{-34}$. The given [integral basis](../../../../integral-basis.md) has [discriminant](../../../../discriminant.md) $-136$, so the imaginary-quadratic bound is $4\sqrt{34}/\pi<8$. Every class therefore has a representative of [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) at most seven, and factoring such representatives shows that the [ideal class group](../../../../ideal-class-group.md) is generated by [prime ideals](../../../../prime-ideal.md) of [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) at most seven.

Two ramifies: let $P_2=(2,\eta)$, with $P_2^2=(2)$. Three is inert because $X^2+34\equiv X^2+1$ is irreducible modulo three, so its [prime ideal](../../../../prime-ideal.md) [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) is nine and supplies no small generator. At five and seven the roots are $\pm1$, so the primes of [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) five and seven are conjugate pairs. Set $P_5=(5,\eta-1)$ and $P_7=(7,\eta+1)$. Their conjugates have inverse [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) classes. No new [prime ideal](../../../../prime-ideal.md) has [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) four, since the only prime above two has [residue degree](../../../../residue-degree.md) one; six is not a [prime power](../../../../prime-power.md). Thus $[P_2],[P_5],[P_7]$ generate the [ideal class group](../../../../ideal-class-group.md).

The element [field norms](../../../../field-norm.md) $N(4+\eta)=50$ and $N(1+\eta)=35$, together with reduction at the split primes, give

$$
(4+\eta)=P_2P_5^2,\qquad(1+\eta)=\bar P_5P_7.
$$

The [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) products verify both exponents. Consequently $[P_5]^2=[P_2]$ and $[P_7]=[P_5]$, so the whole [group](../../../../group-split.md) is generated by $[P_5]$ and its order divides four. The class $[P_2]$ is not trivial: a generator would be an element of the [ring of integers](../../../../ring-of-integers.md) $a+b\eta$ of [field norm](../../../../field-norm.md) two, but $a^2+34b^2=2$ has no [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) solution. Since $[P_2]^2=1$, its order is exactly two. Hence $[P_5]$ has order four and

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-34}))\cong C_4.}
$$
