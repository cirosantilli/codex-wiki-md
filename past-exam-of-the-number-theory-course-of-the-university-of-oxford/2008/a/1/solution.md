<h1 id="a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

For an [irreducible polynomial](../../../../irreducible-polynomial.md) $f\in K[X]$, take $L=K[X]/(f)$ and let $\alpha$ be the class of $X$. The quotient is a [field](../../../../field.md), is generated by $\alpha$ over $K$, and satisfies $f(\alpha)=0$. Its degree is $\boxed{[L:K]=\deg f}$, with [basis](../../../../basis.md) $1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{\deg f-1}$.

A [splitting field](../../../../splitting-field.md) is a [field](../../../../field.md) generated over $K$ by all roots of the [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) and in which the [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) is a product of linear factors. Prove existence and the factorial bound together by induction on the degree. Constant nonzero [polynomials](../../../../polynomial-split.md) and linear [polynomials](../../../../polynomial-split.md) require no extension. For a reducible degree-$n$ [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) write $g=hk$ with positive degrees $r,n-r$. Split $h$ by induction, then split $k$ over that [field](../../../../field.md). The resulting [field](../../../../field.md) is generated by all roots of $g$, and its degree over $K$ is at most $r!(n-r)!\le n!$. If $g$ is irreducible, adjoin one root in degree $n$, divide out its linear factor, and split the degree-$(n-1)$ quotient over that extension. The tower formula gives degree at most $n(n-1)!=n!$. This also works in inseparable [characteristic](../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) and proves the required bound without assuming Galoisness.

For the sextic with constant term three, [Eisenstein's criterion](../../../../eisenstein-criterion.md) at three gives degree six. Choose a root $\alpha$, so $\alpha^3$ is a square root of $-3$. Then

$$
\zeta=\frac{1+\alpha^3}{2}
$$

satisfies $\zeta^2-\zeta+1=0$, and is a primitive sixth [root of unity](../../../../root-of-unity.md): $\zeta^3=-1$ and $\zeta\ne-1$. The six distinct roots are $\alpha\zeta^j$ for $0\le j<6$, all in $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. Hence the simple extension is itself the [splitting field](../../../../splitting-field.md) and $\boxed{[L:\mathbb Q]=6}$.

For the constant term two, Eisenstein at two gives $[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]=6$ for a root $\gamma$. Its [splitting field](../../../../splitting-field.md) is $\mathbb Q(\gamma,\zeta_6)$. The supplied noncontainment of a root of $X^2+3$ means that adjoining $\zeta_6=(1+\sqrt{-3})/2$ has degree two. Thus

$$
\boxed{[M:\mathbb Q]=12.}
$$
