<h1 id="b/4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [integral basis](../../../../../integral-basis.md) is a $\mathbb Z$-[basis](../../../../../basis.md) of the [ring of integers](../../../../../ring-of-integers.md) $\mathcal O_K$; it consists of $n$ algebraic integers and is also a $\mathbb Q$-[basis](../../../../../basis.md) of $K$.

The lattice $M$ has finite index $h=[\mathcal O_K:M]>1$. Choose a prime $p\mid h$. The finite [abelian group](../../../../../abelian-group.md) $\mathcal O_K/M$ has an element of order $p$, represented by $\alpha\in\mathcal O_K\setminus M$. Thus

$$
p\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^nc_jw_j\in M.
$$

If all $c_j$ were divisible by $p$, cancellation in the characteristic-zero [field](../../../../../field.md) would put $\alpha$ in $M$, a contradiction. The [discriminant-index formula for an integral lattice](../../../../../discriminant-index-formula-for-an-integral-lattice.md) gives

$$
\Delta^2(M)=h^2\Delta^2(K),
$$

where $\Delta^2(K)$ is an [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md). Hence $p^2\mid\Delta^2(M)$, and the required integral element is $\alpha=p^{-1}\sum_jc_jw_j$.
