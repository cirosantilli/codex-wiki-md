<h1 id="b/6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose a nonzero [integral ideal](../../../../../integral-ideal.md) $B$ whose class is $[A]^{-1}$; one obtains such an [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) by multiplying the [fractional ideal](../../../../../fractional-ideal.md) $A^{-1}$ by a suitable nonzero [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md). The assumed [Minkowski bound for ideal classes](../../../../../minkowski-s-bound.md) gives $0\ne\beta\in B$ with

$$
|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)|\le c_KN(B).
$$

Because $(\beta)\subseteq B$ and nonzero ideals of the [ring of integers](../../../../../ring-of-integers.md) are invertible,

$$
I_A=(\beta)B^{-1}\subseteq\mathcal O_K.
$$

It is an [integral ideal](../../../../../integral-ideal.md) in class $[B]^{-1}=[A]$. By multiplicativity of the [ideal norm](../../../../../ideal-norm.md),

$$
\boxed{N(I_A)=\frac{|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)|}{N(B)}\le c_K.}
$$

The same argument applies if the starting $A$ is a [fractional ideal](../../../../../fractional-ideal.md), since its class is unchanged by clearing denominators.
