<h1 id="b/6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $s=\sqrt{-33}$. The [discriminant](../../../../../discriminant.md) is $-132$, and the bound of part (a) is

$$
c_K=\frac2\pi\sqrt{132}=\frac4\pi\sqrt{33}<8.
$$

Every [ideal class](../../../../../ideal-class.md) therefore has an integral representative of [norm](../../../../../norm.md) at most $7$. The [Kummer-Dedekind theorem](../../../../../kummer-dedekind-theorem.md) gives

$$
(2)=P^2,\quad P=(2,1+s);\qquad
(3)=Q^2,\quad Q=(3,s).
$$

Modulo $5$, $x^2+33=x^2+3$ is irreducible, so there is no [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) of [norm](../../../../../norm.md) $5$. Modulo $7$ it has roots $\pm3$, giving $(7)=R\bar R$, where $R=(7,s+3)$. The [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) of $3+s$ has [norm](../../../../../norm.md) $42$, and reduction in the three residue fields shows that it lies in $P,Q,R$. Equality of norms therefore gives

$$
(3+s)=PQR.
$$

It follows that $[R]=[P][Q]$, because $[P]^2=[Q]^2=1$. The other prime over $7$ has the inverse class, which is the same class here.

By unique factorization of nonzero ideals, all norms at most $7$ now give only the classes $1,[P],[Q],[PQ]$: [norm](../../../../../norm.md) $4$ gives $P^2=(2)$, [norm](../../../../../norm.md) $6$ gives $PQ$, and [norm](../../../../../norm.md) $7$ was handled above. None of $P,Q,PQ$ is principal, since their norms are $2,3,6$ and

$$
a^2+33b^2\notin\{2,3,6\}\qquad(a,b\in\mathbb Z).
$$

Also $[P]\ne[Q]$, since equality would make $PQ$ principal. Thus there are exactly four classes, all of exponent two:

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-33}))\cong C_2\times C_2.}
$$
