<h1 id="a/1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is irreducible by [Eisenstein's criterion](../../../../../../eisenstein-criterion.md) at $2$. Its [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) therefore acts transitively on five roots, so its order is divisible by $5$ by the [orbit-stabilizer theorem](../../../../../../orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md).

To count the real roots, differentiate:

$$
f'(x)=5x(x^3-4).
$$

It is positive on $(-\infty,0)$, negative on $(0,\sqrt[3]4)$, and positive thereafter. The local maximum is $f(0)=2>0$ and the local minimum is $2-6(\sqrt[3]4)^2<0$. The limits at infinity have opposite signs, so there are exactly three real roots and one conjugate pair of nonreal roots. [Complex conjugation](../../../../../../complex-conjugation.md) fixes the three real roots and interchanges the other two; its permutation is a [transposition](../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md). The prime-degree [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) result supplied in the paper now gives

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(f)\cong S_5.}
$$
