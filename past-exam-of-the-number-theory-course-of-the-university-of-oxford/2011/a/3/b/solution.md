<h1 id="a/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A finite [solvable group](../../../../../solvable-group.md) is a [group](../../../../../group-split.md) admitting a finite chain of normal subgroups with abelian successive quotients; equivalently its derived series eventually becomes trivial. The chain

$$
S_4\triangleright A_4\triangleright V_4\triangleright1
$$

has quotients $C_2,C_3,V_4$, all abelian. Thus $S_4$ is solvable. Every [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) of a [solvable group](../../../../../solvable-group.md) is solvable, because intersecting the chain with the [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) gives factors isomorphic to subgroups of the original abelian factors. A [Galois group of a polynomial](../../../../../galois-group-of-a-polynomial.md) of degree at most $4$ embeds in $S_4$ by its action on the roots, so it is solvable even if the [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is reducible or has repeated roots.

For the substitution, the characteristic-zero [solvability by radicals](../../../../../solvability-by-radicals.md) theorem says that a [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) has solvable [Galois group](../../../../../galois-group.md) if and only if its [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md) is contained in a finite tower formed by adjoining successive roots of elements of the preceding [field](../../../../../field.md). First place all roots $\alpha$ of $f$ in such a tower. Adjoin a primitive $m$th [root of unity](../../../../../root-of-unity.md) and, for every nonzero $\alpha$, an $m$th root $\beta_\alpha$. These are radical adjunctions, and all roots of $x^m-\alpha$ are $\beta_\alpha\zeta_m^j$. Zero roots require no adjunction. The resulting radical tower therefore contains the [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md) of $f(x^m)$. The theorem implies

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(f(x^m)/\mathbb Q)\text{ is solvable}.}
$$
