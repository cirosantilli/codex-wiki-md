An [algebraically closed field](../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md) is a [field](../../../../field.md) in which every nonconstant [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) has a root, equivalently every such [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) splits. An [algebraic closure](../../../../algebraic-closure.md) of $K$ is an algebraic extension of $K$ which is algebraically closed.

For the displayed subset $\overline K\subseteq\mathbb C$, if $\alpha,\beta$ are algebraic over $K$, then $K(\alpha,\beta)/K$ is finite. Sums, differences, products and nonzero inverses of its elements are algebraic over $K$, so $\overline K$ is a subfield. If a nonconstant [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) has coefficients in $\overline K$, those finitely many coefficients generate a finite extension $F/K$. It has a root $z\in\mathbb C$ by algebraic closure of $\mathbb C$, and $z$ is algebraic over $F$. Part (i) makes it algebraic over $K$, so $z\in\overline K$. Hence $\overline K$ is algebraically closed and is an algebraic closure of $K$.

Every $z\in\overline K$ lies in the finite subextension $K(z)$, and every element of a finite subextension is algebraic over $K$. Therefore $\overline K$ is exactly the union of all finite subextensions inside $\mathbb C$. The two examples follow.
