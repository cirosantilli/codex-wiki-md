<h1 id="a/3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Identify $\mathbb C$ with the Euclidean plane and start with the points $0,1$. A complex number is a [constructible number](../../../../../constructible-number.md) if a finite sequence of drawing lines through known points and circles with known centres and known distances as radii, and selecting their intersections, produces that point.

Line-line intersections solve linear equations in the current coordinate [field](../../../../../field.md). Line-circle and circle-circle intersections require at most one quadratic equation after eliminating a coordinate. Hence all coordinates obtained lie in a finite tower of real quadratic extensions of $\mathbb Q$. If $\alpha=x+iy$ is constructible, choose a common such real [field](../../../../../field.md) $F$ containing $x,y$. Adjoining $i$ if needed still gives an extension $F(i)/\mathbb Q$ of power-of-two degree. By the [tower law for field extensions](../../../../../tower-law-for-field-extensions.md),

$$
\boxed{[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\text{ is a power of }2.}
$$

A regular heptagon centred at $0$ with one vertex $1$ would have $\xi=e^{2\pi i/7}$ as another constructible vertex. But its cyclotomic degree is $\varphi(7)=6$, which is not a power of two. Thus **the regular heptagon is not constructible**.
