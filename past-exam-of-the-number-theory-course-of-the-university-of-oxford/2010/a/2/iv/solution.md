<h1 id="a/2/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Let $a=\sqrt[n]2$ and $F=\mathbb Q(\xi)$. The roots of $X^n-2$ are $a\xi^j$, so $L=F(a)$. This splitting extension is separable in characteristic zero, hence Galois over $\mathbb Q$. The subextension $F/\mathbb Q$ is itself Galois by part (iii), so restriction gives a surjection

$$
G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)
$$

with normal kernel $H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)$. Surjectivity follows from the extension theorem for embeddings and normality of $L/\mathbb Q$.

For $\sigma\in H$, write $\sigma(a)=a\xi^{j(\sigma)}$. This defines an injective homomorphism $H\to\langle\xi\rangle$: injectivity holds because $L=F(a)$, and multiplicativity holds because $H$ fixes all [roots of unity](../../../../../root-of-unity.md) in $F$. A [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) of a [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) is cyclic. The first isomorphism theorem therefore gives

$$
\boxed{H\text{ cyclic and normal},\qquad G/H\cong(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times.}
$$

There is no need to assume that $|H|=n$; the intersection with the cyclotomic [field](../../../../../field.md) can reduce its order.
