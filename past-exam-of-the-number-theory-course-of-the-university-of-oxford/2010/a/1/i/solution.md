<h1 id="a/1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Any extending [field homomorphism](../../../../../field-homomorphism.md) $j$ is determined by $j(\alpha)$, since every element of $K(\alpha)$ is represented by a [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) in $\alpha$ with coefficients in $K$. Applying $j$ to the minimal-[polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) equation shows $i(m_\alpha)(j(\alpha))=0$. Thus there are at most $m$ choices.

For each listed root $\beta_r$, define $K[x]\to L$ by $\sum a_kx^k\mapsto\sum i(a_k)\beta_r^k$. Its kernel contains $(m_\alpha)$, so it induces a unital map from the [field](../../../../../field.md) $K[x]/(m_\alpha)\cong K(\alpha)$ into $L$. A unital map from a [field](../../../../../field.md) is injective. This gives an extending homomorphism $j_r$ with $j_r(\alpha)=\beta_r$. Distinct roots give distinct maps, proving **exactly $m$ extensions**.
