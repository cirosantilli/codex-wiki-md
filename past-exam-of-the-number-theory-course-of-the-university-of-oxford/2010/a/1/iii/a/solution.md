<h1 id="a/1/iii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

First, the number of embeddings extending a prescribed base embedding is at most the extension degree. Prove this for any finite extension by choosing finitely many algebraic generators and applying part (i) successively. At each step the number of extensions is at most the degree of that simple step; multiplication and the [tower law for field extensions](../../../../../../tower-law-for-field-extensions.md) give the total bound.

If $\widetilde K/K$ is not separable, choose $\alpha$ with inseparable [minimal polynomial](../../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) of degree $e=[K(\alpha):K]$. Its image under $i$ has strictly fewer than $e$ distinct roots in any target [field](../../../../../../field.md): inseparability is preserved under the coefficient embedding. Thus there are $r<e$ embeddings of $K(\alpha)$ extending $i$. Each extends to $\widetilde K$ in at most $[\widetilde K:K(\alpha)]$ ways, giving at most

$$
r[\widetilde K:K(\alpha)]<e[\widetilde K:K(\alpha)]=d.
$$

If instead $i(m_\alpha)$ does not split in $L$, it again has fewer than $e$ distinct roots in $L$, and exactly the same estimate applies. Hence **either obstruction makes the embedding count strictly less than $d$**.
