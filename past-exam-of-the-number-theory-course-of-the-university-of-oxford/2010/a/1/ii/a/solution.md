<h1 id="a/1/ii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An irreducible [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is separable if its roots in a [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md) are distinct, equivalently if it is coprime to its formal [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md). For a general nonzero [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md), the factorwise separability convention means that every irreducible factor is separable. This is the convention under which the printed [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md) implication holds without an extra hypothesis.

If instead separability of an arbitrary [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is defined to require all its roots to be distinct, that implication needs the [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) to be squarefree. For example, $(x-1)^2$ has separable splitting extension $K/K$, but a repeated root. These two conventions agree for irreducible polynomials; repeated factors are the entire distinction here.
