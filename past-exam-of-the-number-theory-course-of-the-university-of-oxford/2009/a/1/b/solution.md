<h1 id="a/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [discriminant](../../../../../discriminant.md) is a [symmetric polynomial](../../../../../symmetric-polynomial.md) in the four roots, so the [Fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-symmetric-polynomials.md) expresses it polynomially in the [elementary symmetric polynomial](../../../../../elementary-symmetric-polynomial.md). For this depressed quartic they are $e_1=e_2=0$, $e_3=-r$, $e_4=s$. Scaling each root by $t$ multiplies the [discriminant](../../../../../discriminant.md) by $t^{12}$, while $r,s$ scale by $t^3,t^4$. Thus only weighted-degree-twelve monomials can appear, giving $\Delta_f=Ar^4+Bs^3$.

For $r=0$, use $f'=4X^3$ and the monic [discriminant](../../../../../discriminant.md) identity $\Delta_f=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_if'(\alpha_i)$. At $n=4$ this gives $4^4(\prod_i\alpha_i)^3=256s^3$, so $B=256$. For $s=0$, the roots are zero together with those of $g=X^3+r$. The cubic's [discriminant](../../../../../discriminant.md) is $-\prod g'(\beta_i)=-27r^2$. The extra squared differences from zero multiply this by $(\prod\beta_i)^2=r^2$, giving $-27r^4$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\Delta_f=256s^3-27r^4.}
$$

These computations are [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) identities, so they extend to parameter values with repeated roots as well.
