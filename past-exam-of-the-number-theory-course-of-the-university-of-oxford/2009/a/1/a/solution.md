<h1 id="a/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [alternating polynomial](../../../../../alternating-polynomial.md) given by the Vandermonde product is alternating: interchanging two variables changes its sign. One proof writes it as a [Vandermonde determinant](../../../../../vandermonde-determinant.md), up to a fixed sign, and interchanging two variables interchanges the corresponding determinant rows. Since [transpositions](../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) generate $S_n$,

$$
\boxed{\sigma(\delta)=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\delta,\qquad\sigma(\Delta)=\Delta.}
$$

Thus every even [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md) fixes $\delta$, while a [transposition](../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) negates the nonzero formal [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md), so it is not invariant under all of $S_n$. This uses $n\ge2$ and [characteristic](../../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) different from two, as in the subsequent rational setting. For $n=1$ both [groups](../../../../../group-split.md) are trivial and the last distinction does not arise.
