<h1 id="a/2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In the distinct-root convention, a [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is separable if it has no repeated roots in its [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md), equivalently $\gcd(f,f')=1$. A common factorwise convention instead requires each distinct irreducible factor to have no repeated roots; repeated occurrences of an already separable factor do not affect the [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md). Under either separability convention, the [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md) is a finite normal separable extension and

$$
\boxed{|\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)|=[L:K].}
$$

For failure in the inseparable case, take $K=\mathbb F_p(t)$ and $f=X^p-t$. It is irreducible, for example by Eisenstein at the prime $t$ in $\mathbb F_p[t]$. Its [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md) is $L=K(t^{1/p})$ of degree $p$, but the [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) has just one distinct root. Every $K$-[automorphism](../../../../../automorphism.md) fixes that root, so the [Galois group](../../../../../galois-group.md) is trivial rather than having order $p$.
