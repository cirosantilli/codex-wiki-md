<h1 id="a/3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let $\alpha=p^{1/p}$ and let $\zeta$ be a primitive $p$th [root of unity](../../../../../root-of-unity.md). The roots are $\alpha\zeta^j$, so $L=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\zeta)$. Eisenstein gives $[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=p$, while the prime cyclotomic extension has degree $[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]=p-1$. Both degrees divide $[L:\mathbb Q]$, and they are coprime. Conversely adjoining $\alpha$ to the [cyclotomic field](../../../../../cyclotomic-field.md) has degree at most $p$. These two bounds give

$$
\boxed{[L:\mathbb Q]=p(p-1).}
$$

Restriction to $E=\mathbb Q(\zeta)$ gives a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $N=\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)$ of order $p$, hence cyclic, with quotient $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\cong C_{p-1}$. Restriction is onto because embeddings of the intermediate [field](../../../../../field.md) extend in the finite Galois [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md). Thus $1\trianglelefteq N\trianglelefteq G$ has abelian factors, proving $\boxed{G\text{ is solvable}}$. For $p=2$ the [cyclotomic field](../../../../../cyclotomic-field.md) is just $\mathbb Q$, and the same argument gives degree two and a [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md).
