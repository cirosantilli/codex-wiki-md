<h1 id="b/6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

For nonzero [ideals](../../../../ideal.md), [unique factorization of ideals in a number field](../../../../unique-factorization-of-ideals-in-a-number-field.md) converts $AB=C^3$ to $v_P(A)+v_P(B)=3v_P(C)$. Coprimality says at most one of the first two exponents is positive, so each is divisible by three. Dividing its exponents by three constructs [ideals](../../../../ideal.md) whose cubes are $A,B$. If a zero [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) is allowed, coprimality forces the other [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) to be the whole [ring](../../../../ring.md) and the conclusion is immediate with the zero [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) as its own cube.

To compute the [Ideal class group of Q of square root minus thirty](../../../../ideal-class-group-of-q-of-square-root-minus-thirty.md), put $\theta=\sqrt{-30}$. The [ring of integers](../../../../ring-of-integers.md) is $\mathbb Z[\theta]$, with [discriminant](../../../../discriminant.md) $-120$. The given [Minkowski bound for ideal classes](../../../../minkowski-s-bound.md) is $2\sqrt{120}/\pi=4\sqrt{30}/\pi<7$. Hence the [ideal class group](../../../../ideal-class-group.md) is generated by [prime ideals](../../../../prime-ideal.md) with [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) below seven. The rational primes two, three and five all ramify, giving unique [prime ideals](../../../../prime-ideal.md) $P_2=(2,\theta)$, $P_3=(3,\theta)$, $P_5=(5,\theta)$ with $P_i^2=(i)$ and [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) $i$. There is no additional [prime ideal](../../../../prime-ideal.md) of [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) four because two ramifies with [residue degree](../../../../residue-degree.md) one; six is not a [prime power](../../../../prime-power.md). Thus these three classes generate and each has order at most two.

As $\theta^2=-30$, [unique factorization of ideals in a number field](../../../../unique-factorization-of-ideals-in-a-number-field.md) gives $(\theta)=P_2P_3P_5$, so their three classes multiply to one. None is principal: a generator of one would be an integral element $a+b\theta$ of [field norm](../../../../field-norm.md) $a^2+30b^2$ equal to two, three or five, and none of those [field norm](../../../../field-norm.md) equations has [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) solutions. The three nontrivial classes are therefore distinct, with the third the product of the first two. Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-30}))\cong C_2\times C_2.}
$$

To rule out [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) solutions, suppose $x^2+30=y^3$. Necessarily $y>0$. If a prime $p\mid30$ divided $y$, then reduction modulo $p$ would force $p\mid x$. Because thirty is squarefree, the valuation of $x^2+30$ at that prime would be exactly one, while that of $y^3$ is a positive multiple of three, a contradiction. Thus $\gcd(y,30)=1$.

If a [prime ideal](../../../../prime-ideal.md) divided both $(x+\theta)$ and $(x-\theta)$, it would divide their difference $(2\theta)$ and their product $(y)^3$. Its underlying rational prime would divide $N(2\theta)=120$, hence divide thirty, and would also divide $y$. This contradicts the gcd just proved, so the two [ideals](../../../../ideal.md) are coprime. Their product is $(y)^3$, so the first part gives $(x+\theta)=A^3$ for an [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) $A$.

In a [ideal class group](../../../../ideal-class-group.md) of exponent two, $[A]^3=1$ forces $[A]=1$. Thus $A=(a+b\theta)$ with $a,b\in\mathbb Z$. Equality of principal [ideals](../../../../ideal.md) gives $x+\theta=\varepsilon(a+b\theta)^3$ for a [unit](../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) $\varepsilon$. The only units are $\pm1$, since $a^2+30b^2=1$ forces $b=0$, $a=\pm1$; both signs are cubes and can be absorbed into the generator. Comparing the coefficient of $\theta$ after cubing now gives

$$
1=3a^2b-30b^3=3b(a^2-10b^2),
$$

which is impossible modulo three. Therefore

$$
\boxed{x^2+30=y^3\text{ has no solutions in }\mathbb Z^2.}
$$
