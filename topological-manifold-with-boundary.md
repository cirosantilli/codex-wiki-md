# Topological manifold with boundary

↑ **Parent:** [Topological manifold](topological-manifold-split.md)

For $n\geq1$, a topological $n$-manifold with boundary is a [Hausdorff](hausdorff-space.md) [second-countable space](second-countable-space.md) locally [homeomorphic](homeomorphism.md) to relatively [open sets](open-set.md) in the [half-space](half-space.md) $\mathbb H^n$. The openness here is in the [subspace topology](subspace-topology.md) of $\mathbb H^n$, not necessarily in $\mathbb R^n$. Its intrinsic boundary $\partial M$ consists of points sent to $x_n=0$; the remaining points have ordinary [coordinate charts](manifold-chart.md) with images open in [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md). The intrinsic boundary is independent of the chosen chart.[Topology I: smooth manifolds, Week 2, Section 1.3, equation (21), Definition 1.21 and Remark 1.24, page 7](topology-i-smooth-manifolds-university-of-toronto-mat1300hf-2012/week-2-course-notes.md)

The [closed interval](closed-real-interval.md) $[0,1]$ illustrates the distinction: its interior points have [open interval](open-interval.md) charts, while $0$ and $1$ have charts modeled on $[0,\varepsilon)\subseteq\mathbb H^1$, using $x$ and $1-x$. Its intrinsic boundary is $\{0,1\}$. The [circle](circle.md) has empty intrinsic boundary even though its [boundary of a set](boundary-of-a-set.md) as a [subset](subset.md) of the [Euclidean plane](euclidean-plane.md) is the whole [circle](circle.md). A [topological manifold](topological-manifold-split.md) without boundary is therefore a special case with $\partial M=\varnothing$, and a [smooth atlas](smooth-atlas.md) on the half-space charts gives a [manifold with boundary](manifold-with-boundary.md) in the smooth sense.
