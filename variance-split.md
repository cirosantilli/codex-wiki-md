# Variance

↑ **Parent:** [Expected value](expected-value.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variance)

The [variance](variance-split.md) measures the mean squared distance of a [random variable](random-variable-split.md) from its [expected value](expected-value.md). For a real [square-integrable](square-integrable-function.md) [random variable](random-variable-split.md) $X$,

$$
\operatorname{var}(X)=\mathbb E[(X-\mathbb EX)^2]
=\mathbb E[X^2]-(\mathbb EX)^2.
$$

The square prevents positive and negative deviations from cancelling. It also gives the [variance](variance-split.md) squared units: if $X$ measures a [length](length.md) in metres, its [variance](variance-split.md) has units of square metres. Its nonnegative square root is the [standard deviation](standard-deviation.md), which has the same units as $X$.

For example, let $X$ take $-1$, $0$ and $1$ with equal [probabilities](probability.md). Its [expected value](expected-value.md) is zero, but its [variance](variance-split.md) is $[(-1)^2+0^2+1^2]/3=2/3$, and its [standard deviation](standard-deviation.md) is $\sqrt{2/3}$. The constant [random variable](random-variable-split.md) zero has the same [expected value](expected-value.md) and zero [variance](variance-split.md): a [mean](expected-value.md) alone does not describe the spread. The [variance](variance-split.md) is zero exactly when $X=\mathbb E X$ [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md).

The [variance](variance-split.md) is useful because squared deviations interact neatly with [linear combinations](linear-combination.md). The [covariance](covariance.md) supplies the cross terms when combining different [random variables](random-variable-split.md), and a [covariance matrix](covariance-matrix.md) organizes those terms for many variables. The characterization of [variance as the minimum mean squared error of a constant](variance-as-the-minimum-mean-squared-error-of-a-constant.md) also explains why the [expected value](expected-value.md) is the best constant prediction under squared error.
