# Sphere-frame flux in a tube

↑ **Parent:** [Moving-boundary lubrication flux](moving-boundary-lubrication-flux.md)

For a [sphere](sphere.md) falling with speed $U$ through a tube of radius $a$, let $Q$ be the downward laboratory [volume flux](volumetric-flow-rate.md). In the [sphere](sphere.md) frame the wall moves upwards and the narrow annulus carries upward flux per unit circumference $q=(\pi a^2U-Q)/(2\pi a)$. [Couette-Poiseuille flow in a thin gap](couette-poiseuille-flow-in-a-thin-gap.md) gives $q=Uh/2-h^3p_z/(12\mu)$. Thus the [pressure](pressure.md) below minus above the [sphere](sphere.md) is $\Delta P=6\mu(2qI_3-UI_2)$, using [lubrication resistance integrals for a nearly occluding sphere](lubrication-resistance-integrals-for-a-nearly-occluding-sphere.md). A nearly piston-like laboratory flux can coexist with a nonzero relative gap flux.
