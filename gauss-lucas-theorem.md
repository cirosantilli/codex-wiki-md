# Gauss-Lucas theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Polynomial](polynomial-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss–Lucas_theorem)

Every [root](root-of-a-root-system.md) of the [derivative](derivative.md) of a nonconstant complex [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) lies in the [convex hull](convex-hull.md) of its roots. If a critical point $\beta$ is itself a root, this is immediate. Otherwise factorization and the [product rule](product-rule.md) give

$$
0=\frac{Q'(\beta)}{Q(\beta)}=\sum_k\frac1{\beta-\alpha_k}.
$$

After taking [complex conjugates](complex-conjugate.md), set $w_k=|\beta-\alpha_k|^{-2}>0$ to obtain $\beta=\sum_kw_k\alpha_k/\sum_kw_k$. This is a [convex combination](convex-combination.md), proving the theorem, with roots counted according to their [multiplicities](multiplicity-mathematics.md). The same formula shows that roots strictly inside an open half-plane or a [convex set](convex-set-split.md) that is [open](open-set.md) keep all critical points there. For a degree-one polynomial the derivative has no zeros and the assertion is vacuous.
