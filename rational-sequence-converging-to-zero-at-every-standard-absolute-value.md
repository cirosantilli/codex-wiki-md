# Rational sequence converging to zero at every standard absolute value

↑ **Parent:** [Weak approximation for inequivalent absolute values](weak-approximation-for-inequivalent-absolute-values.md)

The displayed nonzero rational sequence tends to zero both in the usual real [absolute value](absolute-value.md) and in every [p-adic absolute value](p-adic-absolute-value.md). In the real absolute value, $|y_n|\le1/n!\to0$. For a fixed prime $p$ and $n\ge p$, the denominator is congruent to one modulo $p$, so its [P-adic valuation](p-adic-valuation.md) is zero. Therefore $v_p(y_n)=v_p(n!)\to\infty$, proving $|y_n|_p\to0$.

This is convergence at each fixed place; it is not uniform convergence over all primes. For each particular $n$, new prime divisors of the denominator can compensate the small values at the previously fixed places, consistently with the [product formula](product-formula.md). Thus a product identity for a single rational number does not prohibit pointwise convergence at infinitely many absolute values.
