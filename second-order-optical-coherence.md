# Second-order optical coherence

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum theory of optical coherence](quantum-theory-of-optical-coherence.md)

For a source whose counting correlations are stationary, define the dimensionless correlation

$$
g^{(2)}(\tau)=
\frac{\left\langle\widehat E^{(-)}(t)\widehat E^{(-)}(t+\tau)\widehat E^{(+)}(t+\tau)\widehat E^{(+)}(t)\right\rangle}
{\left\langle\widehat E^{(-)}(t)\widehat E^{(+)}(t)\right\rangle
\left\langle\widehat E^{(-)}(t+\tau)\widehat E^{(+)}(t+\tau)\right\rangle}.
$$

Assume finite moments and nonzero denominators. The numerator is in [normal ordering](normal-ordering.md): two detections remove two [photons](photon.md). The denominator is the product of the two single-count factors, so independent counts have reference value one. This is a diagonal restriction of the four-point second-order field correlation, rather than its complete definition.[2005 individual Nobel lecture, pp. 87–89, equations (18)–(22); Liceo Galilei Verona mirror](one-hundred-years-of-light-quanta-by-roy-jay-glauber.md)

For one selected mode with [number operator](number-operator.md) $N=a^\dagger a$ and $\mu=\langle N\rangle>0$, evaluate both detections at the same time. The [canonical commutation relation](canonical-commutation-relation.md) $aa^\dagger=a^\dagger a+1$ implies $N^2=(a^\dagger)^2a^2+N$, hence

$$
g^{(2)}(0)=\frac{\langle N(N-1)\rangle}{\mu^2}
=1+\frac{\operatorname{Var}N-\mu}{\mu^2}.
$$

The [factorial moment](factorial-moment.md) counts ordered pairs of distinct [photons](photon.md). Using $\langle N^2\rangle$ would include pairing each [photon](photon.md) with itself and would give the wrong coincidence prediction. At $\mu=0$ this normalized expression is undefined, rather than zero.
