# Predation

↑ **Parent:** [Ecology](ecology-split.md)

[Predation](predation.md) is the consumption of [prey](prey.md) by a [predator](predator.md), affecting the demographic rates of both populations.

In the usual animal-feeding sense, a [predator](predator.md) kills and eats its [prey](prey.md). This distinguishes the interaction from feeding on already-dead material and from the prolonged exploitation of a living [host](host-biology.md) in [parasitism](parasitism.md). In the illustrative [food web](food-web.md), a small [fish](fish.md) eating [zooplankton](zooplankton.md) is a [predator](predator.md); a large [fish](fish.md) eating that small [fish](fish.md) makes the same small [fish](fish.md) its [prey](prey.md). These roles belong to a particular interaction. See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 45.6: predation and community interactions](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/45-6-community-ecology).

For a toy count, suppose 50 small [fish](fish.md) begin a day and 8 are eaten by large [fish](fish.md). With births, other deaths and movement explicitly omitted, 42 small [fish](fish.md) remain, and the fraction removed by [predation](predation.md) is $8/50=0.16$. This measures removal of [prey](prey.md), not production of new [predators](predator.md). Food intake can support survival and reproduction, but eight meals do not imply eight new [predators](predator.md); [energy flow](energy-flow-ecology.md) and life-history details intervene. [Lotka-Volterra equations](lotka-volterra-equations.md) provide one idealized account of such [population dynamics](population-dynamics.md), rather than a guarantee that every interacting pair must cycle.
