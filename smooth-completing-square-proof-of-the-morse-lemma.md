# Smooth completing-square proof of the Morse lemma

↑ **Parent:** [Morse lemma](morse-lemma.md)

At a [nondegenerate critical point](nondegenerate-critical-point.md) placed at the origin, integral [Taylor theorem](taylor-theorem.md) gives $f(x)-f(0)=x^TA(x)x$, where $A(x)$ is a smooth symmetric matrix and $A(0)=\tfrac12\operatorname{Hess}f(0)$. A constant linear change makes $A(0)$ diagonal with entries $\pm1$. Successive completion of squares, with the [Schur complement](schur-complement.md) at each step, produces a smooth invertible triangular matrix $B(x)$ such that $x^TA(x)x=\sum_i\epsilon_i(B(x)x)_i^2$. The pivots keep their nonzero signs near zero. The map $x\mapsto B(x)x$ has invertible derivative at zero, so the [inverse function theorem](inverse-function-theorem.md) makes it a coordinate system. The signs are the [Hessian matrix](hessian-matrix.md) signature by [Sylvester's law of inertia](sylvester-s-law-of-inertia.md).
