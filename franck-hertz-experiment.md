# Franck-Hertz experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Excited state](excited-state.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franck–Hertz_experiment)

The Franck-Hertz experiment connects a reproducible loss of [Electron](electron.md) [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) with excitation of a [chemical atom](atom-physics.md). The original mercury-vapor experiments were performed during 1912–1914 while investigating [ionization](ionization.md); they were not designed as a test of the [Bohr model](bohr-model.md). Their later interpretation distinguished internal excitation from removal of an [Electron](electron.md). [KIT physics laboratory, H23 Franck-Hertz-Versuch, Motivation](https://labs.physik.kit.edu/prakt-klass-physik-manuals/P2/Franck_Hertz_Versuch/).

In a modern mercury tube, a heated [cathode](cathode.md) emits [Electrons](electron.md) and a positive perforated grid accelerates them through Hg vapor. A collector slightly below the grid's [electric potential](electric-potential.md) rejects slow [Electrons](electron.md). The oven controls vapor density and hence the [mean free path](mean-free-path.md). Keep heating and retarding settings fixed, sweep the accelerating [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $V$, and record collector [electric current](electric-current.md) $I(V)$. These measured quantities indirectly probe atomic excitation. [Shah, Alam and Anwar, Electron energy loss investigated through the Franck-Hertz Experiment, sections 3–4](https://physlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Franck_exp_v12015.pdf).

The curve shows repeated current minima, typically about $4.9\,\mathrm V$ apart. In the collision model, an [Electron](electron.md) gains approximately $eV$ of [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md), with $e$ the positive [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md). An [inelastic collision](inelastic-collision.md) transfers about $4.9\,\mathrm{eV}$ to internal excitation. Near the grid, this can prevent the [Electron](electron.md) crossing the retarding region, lowering collected [electric current](electric-current.md). Further acceleration restores collection; repeated excitation losses produce further minima. These represent multiple collisions, rather than equally spaced atomic [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue.md). Contact potentials shift the first minimum, so compare several successive spacings instead of identifying the first dip's voltage with the excitation cost. [Shah, Alam and Anwar, sections 3.1–4 and Figure 3](https://physlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Franck_exp_v12015.pdf).

The optical comparison supplies a second observable: mercury radiation at a [wavelength](wavelength.md) near $253.6\,\mathrm{nm}$ was detected through a window in the tube. Its inferred [photon](photon.md) [energy](energy.md) $hc/\lambda\approx4.89\,\mathrm{eV}$ agrees with the characteristic collision loss, with $c$ the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). This agreement supports the interpretation as a transition between internal atomic energies. It does not make the collector trace a complete spectrum. In particular, the lowest excited mercury level, labeled $6\,{}^3P_0$, is metastable because an [electric-dipole selection rule](electric-dipole-selection-rule.md) forbids its direct transition to the [ground state](ground-state.md); the nearby $6\,{}^3P_1$ level emits the quoted [ultraviolet](ultraviolet.md) line. Collision excitation and optical detection therefore have different sensitivities to the available [excited states](excited-state.md). [MIT Junior Lab, The Franck-Hertz Experiment, section VI and the discussion following Figure 6](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-13-14-experimental-physics-i-ii-junior-lab-fall-2016-spring-2017/afdfff9f8bbe067239af19c8b178a764_MIT8_13-14F16-S17exp7.pdf).

Initial [Electron](electron.md) [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) spread and varying collision positions broaden real minima. Repeat traces at different retarding settings and oven temperatures to distinguish excitation from collection effects. This probes a discrete energy-transfer channel, rather than every [energy eigenspace](energy-eigenspace.md), its [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md), or an absolute [ground-state energy](ground-state-energy.md). [MIT Junior Lab, sections IV and VI](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-13-14-experimental-physics-i-ii-junior-lab-fall-2016-spring-2017/afdfff9f8bbe067239af19c8b178a764_MIT8_13-14F16-S17exp7.pdf).
