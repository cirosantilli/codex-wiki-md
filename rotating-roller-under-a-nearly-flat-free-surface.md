# Rotating roller under a nearly flat free surface

↑ **Parent:** [Lubrication theory](lubrication-theory.md)

A rotating roller with top speed $U$ and gap $d(x)=b+x^2/(2a)$ drives a shear-free [lubrication approximation](lubrication-theory.md) flow. No slip below and zero shear above give $u=U+p_x(y^2-d^2)/(2\mu)$ and $Q=Ud-d^3p_x/(3\mu)$. The [pressure recovery condition in lubrication flow](pressure-recovery-condition-in-lubrication-flow.md) then gives $Q=U\int d^{-2}dx/\int d^{-3}dx=4Ub/3$. With $s=x/\sqrt{2ab}$, the excess pressure is $-\mu U\sqrt{2a}\,b^{-3/2}s/(1+s^2)^2$. Gravity supports it through the small surface displacement $h=(p-p_0)/(\rho g)$; the flat leading geometry is consistent when $\mu|U|\sqrt a/(\rho g b^{5/2})\ll1$.
