**[GDP](../../../../../guanosine-diphosphate.md)–[GTP](../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md) exchange resets translation factors after [GTP](../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md) hydrolysis; it does not phosphorylate bound [GDP](../../../../../guanosine-diphosphate.md) back into [GTP](../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md).** A [guanine nucleotide exchange factor](../../../../../guanine-nucleotide-exchange-factor.md) lowers nucleotide affinity, allowing bound [GDP](../../../../../guanosine-diphosphate.md) to leave. The cytosolic nucleotide pool then supplies [GTP](../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md). The GTP-bound and GDP-bound conformations bind different partners, while the ribosome and associated factors control when hydrolysis occurs. This couples repeated factor use to the ordered steps of [biological translation](../../../../../translation-biology.md).

During bacterial elongation, [EF-Tu](../../../../../ef-tu.md) binds [GTP](../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md) and an [aminoacyl-tRNA](../../../../../aminoacyl-trna.md), delivering it to the ribosomal A site. Correct [codon](../../../../../codon.md)–[anticodon](../../../../../anticodon.md) recognition promotes rearrangements and [GTPase](../../../../../gtpase.md) activation. Hydrolysis and phosphate release permit EF-Tu departure and tRNA accommodation; rejection before and after hydrolysis contributes to [kinetic proofreading](../../../../../kinetic-proofreading.md). The GDP-bound factor is poorly suited to another delivery cycle until [EF-Ts](../../../../../ef-ts.md) accelerates nucleotide exchange. Structural analysis of their complex showed disruption of the magnesium-binding environment, explaining reduced nucleotide affinity rather than a transfer of phosphate onto bound [GDP](../../../../../guanosine-diphosphate.md).[Kawashima and colleagues, 1996, abstract and nucleotide-binding-site structural analysis](../../../../../the-structure-of-the-escherichia-coli-ef-tu-ef-ts-complex-at-2-5-a-resolution-by-t-kawashima-and-others.md) The analogous eukaryotic delivery factor [eEF1A](../../../../../eef1a.md) is recycled by the [eEF1B](../../../../../eef1b.md) exchange machinery.

After peptide-bond formation, [EF-G](../../../../../ef-g.md) uses [GTP](../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md) in promoting bacterial [ribosomal translocation](../../../../../ribosomal-translocation.md); [eEF2](../../../../../eef2.md) performs the corresponding eukaryotic role. [GDP](../../../../../guanosine-diphosphate.md) release and [GTP](../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md) binding again regenerate active factor, but EF-G does not depend on EF-Ts for recycling in the way EF-Tu does: its nucleotide exchange is sufficiently rapid under physiological conditions. The contrast matters because “every translational GTPase requires its own exchange factor” would be incorrect. Different factors employ a common nucleotide switch with different exchange kinetics and regulatory partners.

Exchange also regulates initiation. [eIF2](../../../../../eif2.md)–[GTP](../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md) carries initiating [methionyl-tRNA](../../../../../methionyl-trna.md) in a ternary complex. Start-site recognition is coupled to its GTPase cycle and release in the [GDP](../../../../../guanosine-diphosphate.md) form; [eIF2B](../../../../../eif2b.md) supplies exchange for another round. Stress-induced [phosphorylation](../../../../../phosphorylation.md) of eIF2's alpha subunit inhibits productive exchange by eIF2B and lowers ternary-complex availability. Overall initiation falls, while particular messages with regulatory [upstream open reading frames](../../../../../upstream-open-reading-frame.md), including ATF4, can be translated more efficiently. Experiments manipulating those upstream reading frames establish how changing initiation-factor availability changes the choice of initiation site.[Vattem and Wek, 2004, results on upstream reading frames and ATF4 reinitiation](../../../../../reinitiation-involving-upstream-orfs-regulates-atf4-mrna-translation-in-mammalian-cells-by-krishna-m-vattem-and-ronald-c-wek.md) This is regulation of the rate of regeneration, not an alteration of the genetic code.

In bacterial termination, the [RF3](../../../../../rf3.md) GTPase assists recycling of class-I release factors; hydrolysis of peptidyl-tRNA itself is catalysed by the release factor, not directly by [GTP](../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md) cleavage. Likewise, peptide-bond formation draws on the activated ester linkage of charged tRNA, whose preparation consumed [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md). Approximately two elongation [GTP](../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md) molecules are used per incorporated residue, one for delivery and one for translocation, apart from initiation, termination and exceptional events. Nonhydrolysable [GTP](../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md) analogues, exchange-factor depletion and nucleotide-release measurements distinguish binding, hydrolysis and exchange. **[GDP](../../../../../guanosine-diphosphate.md)–[GTP](../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md) exchange supplies reusable, regulatable molecular switches throughout translation.**
