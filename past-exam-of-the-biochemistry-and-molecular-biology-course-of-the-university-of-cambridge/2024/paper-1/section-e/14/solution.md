<h1 id="section-e/14/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [14](../14.md)

**The proton current supplies energy, while a conformational switch changes how that current is coupled to rotor motion.** Reversing torque therefore does not require reversing the electrochemical gradient. In the proton-driven [bacterial flagellar motor](../../../../../bacterial-flagellar-motor.md) of [Escherichia coli](../../../../../escherichia-coli.md), the cell maintains a [proton-motive force](../../../../../proton-motive-force.md) that favours proton entry. [MotA](../../../../../mota.md) and [MotB](../../../../../motb.md) form stator units anchored to the cell envelope. Proton-dependent conformational cycles of those units exert force on the rotor, including interactions with [FliG](../../../../../flig.md).

The rotor's switch complex includes [FliG](../../../../../flig.md), [FliM](../../../../../flim.md) and [FliN](../../../../../flin.md). Cooperative changes in its organization alter the relative geometry of rotor and stator contacts and hence the direction in which the energy-driven cycle produces angular displacement. Structural work on the torque ring supported a mechanism in which reorientation of interacting regions changes the sign of torque.[Lee and colleagues, 2010, abstract and structural model of rotational switching](../../../../../structure-of-the-torque-ring-of-the-flagellar-motor-and-the-molecular-basis-for-rotational-switching-by-lawrence-k-lee-and-others.md) Such structural models constrain the explanation, but should not be treated as a complete direct movie of every proton-transfer and mechanical step. The central distinction is robust: **the sign of mechanical rotation changes through the motor's coupling state, while the thermodynamically favourable direction of proton flow remains inward**.

This is consistent with energy conservation. With a fixed electrochemical difference, inward proton movement has a negative free-energy change. In either rotational state, the magnitude of that input can supply positive work against a resisting load. Both torque and angular displacement reverse when the motor reverses, leaving their product, the work delivered during motion against that load, positive. No principle requires a given sign of proton flow to correspond to one immutable sign of angular displacement when the [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) coupling apparatus can change conformation.

Chemotaxis controls the switch bias. Receptor complexes regulate [CheA](../../../../../chea.md), which transfers phosphoryl groups to [CheY](../../../../../chey.md). Phosphorylated [CheY](../../../../../chey.md) binds the switch, particularly FliM-associated sites, increasing clockwise bias in the familiar E. coli swimming system. Counterclockwise rotation promotes a coherent flagellar bundle and a run; clockwise episodes disrupt the bundle and permit reorientation. An attractant response commonly reduces [CheA](../../../../../chea.md) activity and [CheY](../../../../../chey.md) [phosphorylation](../../../../../phosphorylation.md), extending runs, while adaptation restores sensitivity. [CheY](../../../../../chey.md) [phosphorylation](../../../../../phosphorylation.md) is a regulatory signal; it does not provide the motor's main mechanical energy or reverse the proton gradient. Other bacteria can use different motor arrangements or sodium-driven stators, so this account should not be universalized to all species.

Experimentally, a tethered-cell or attached-bead assay resolves individual motor direction changes while external pH and membrane potential are controlled. Reversal without a measured reversal of the gradient supports switching of coupling. A protonophore should suppress energy supply and rotation, rather than reliably command the opposite direction. [CheY](../../../../../chey.md) perturbations and switch mutations can alter directional bias while leaving ion-driven torque generation intact. Simultaneous gradient measurements, motor speed and direction distinguish an energy deficit from a switch defect; a population swimming assay alone cannot do so. **Directional control and energetic drive are separable functions of the same molecular machine.**
