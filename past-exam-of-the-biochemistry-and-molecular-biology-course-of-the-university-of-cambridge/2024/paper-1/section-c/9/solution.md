<h1 id="section-c/9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9](../9.md)

**Antennae collect photons; reaction centres convert excitation into charge separation; electron flow then generates reducing power and a proton gradient for carbon assimilation.** [Chlorophyll](../../../../../chlorophyll.md) and accessory pigments in [light-harvesting complexes](../../../../../light-harvesting-complex.md) broaden light absorption and transfer excitation towards a [photosynthetic reaction centre](../../../../../photosynthetic-reaction-centre.md). Excitation-energy transfer between pigments is distinct from transferring an electron along the electron-transport chain. The reaction centre couples excitation to separation of an electron from its donor, creating an oxidizing partner and a reduced acceptor before the excitation is lost as heat or fluorescence.

In the [thylakoid membrane](../../../../../thylakoid-membrane.md), [photosystem II](../../../../../photosystem-ii.md) uses light excitation of its reaction centre to drive electron transfer to acceptors. Its oxidized donor is regenerated using electrons extracted from water by the [oxygen-evolving complex](../../../../../oxygen-evolving-complex.md), which contains a manganese–calcium cluster. Four oxidizing equivalents permit the net reaction

$$
2\mathrm H_2\mathrm O\longrightarrow\mathrm O_2+4\mathrm H^++4e^-.
$$

The protons are released on the lumenal side. Electrons pass through [plastoquinone](../../../../../plastoquinone.md), the [cytochrome b6f complex](../../../../../cytochrome-b6f-complex.md) and [plastocyanin](../../../../../plastocyanin.md) to [photosystem I](../../../../../photosystem-i.md). Plastoquinone takes up protons on reduction and contributes to their net delivery into the lumen through the cytochrome-b6f pathway. The two photosystems operate in series because excitation at one centre alone does not bridge the full energetic span from water oxidation to efficient [nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate](../../../../../nicotinamide-adenine-dinucleotide-phosphate.md) reduction.

Excitation in photosystem I enables electron transfer to [ferredoxin](../../../../../ferredoxin.md); [ferredoxin-NADP reductase](../../../../../ferredoxin-nadp-reductase.md) generates [NADPH](../../../../../nadph.md) on the stromal side. For the four electrons corresponding to one [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) molecule, the ideal minimum is eight photochemical excitations, four at each photosystem, yielding two [NADPH](../../../../../nadph.md). Real light requirements are higher because not every absorbed photon produces productive chemistry. The [proton-motive force](../../../../../proton-motive-force.md) drives [ATP synthase](../../../../../atp-synthase.md) as protons return from the lumen to the [chloroplast stroma](../../../../../chloroplast-stroma.md). Counterion movements often allow a large pH component to accumulate without an equally large membrane voltage. In a classical test, an imposed acid-to-base transition in isolated [chloroplast](../../../../../chloroplast.md) material supported [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) formation in darkness, separating the gradient's role from the immediate presence of light.[Jagendorf and Uribe, 1966, pp. 170–177, acid–base transition experiment](../../../../../atp-formation-caused-by-acid-base-transition-of-spinach-chloroplasts-by-a-t-jagendorf-and-others.md) Appropriate controls compare transitions that produce a transmembrane gradient with treatments that dissipate it.

In the stroma, the [Calvin cycle](../../../../../calvin-cycle.md) uses [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) and [NADPH](../../../../../nadph.md) to reduce fixed carbon and regenerate the carbon acceptor. [RuBisCO](../../../../../rubisco.md) adds [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) to ribulose bisphosphate; subsequent [phosphorylation](../../../../../phosphorylation.md) and reduction produce triose phosphate. Fixation of three [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) molecules to one net triose phosphate requires nine [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) and six [NADPH](../../../../../nadph.md) in the standard accounting, with most intermediate carbon recycled to regenerate the acceptor. “Light-independent” chemistry is not physiologically independent of light: energy supply, stromal conditions and enzyme regulation coordinate it with photochemistry.

Linear electron flow need not exactly match the [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md)/[NADPH](../../../../../nadph.md) demand. [Cyclic electron flow around photosystem I](../../../../../cyclic-electron-flow-around-photosystem-i.md) increases proton-gradient-driven [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) production without net [NADPH](../../../../../nadph.md) generation or water oxidation. Excess excitation is dangerous; [non-photochemical quenching](../../../../../non-photochemical-quenching.md), changes in antenna allocation and photosystem repair limit damage. **The [chloroplast](../../../../../chloroplast.md) combines efficient collection with regulated conversion and protection, rather than maximizing photon capture regardless of metabolic demand.**
