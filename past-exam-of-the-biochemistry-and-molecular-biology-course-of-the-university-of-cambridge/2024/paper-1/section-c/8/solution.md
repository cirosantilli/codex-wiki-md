<h1 id="section-c/8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

**Animal cells conserve part of the free energy from nutrient oxidation in [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) and electrochemical gradients, then couple their consumption to synthesis, transport and movement.** The relevant quantity is the actual [Gibbs free energy](../../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md) change,

$$
\Delta G=\Delta G^{\circ\prime}+RT\ln Q,
$$

where $Q$ is the appropriate [reaction quotient](../../../../../reaction-quotient.md), $R$ the [molar gas constant](../../../../../molar-gas-constant.md) and $T$ the absolute [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). A reaction with a positive free-energy change can proceed when physically coupled to one whose negative change makes the combined process favourable. Merely placing an exergonic reaction nearby does not accomplish coupling: an enzyme, shared intermediate or molecular machine must link the processes.

Oxidation of carbohydrates and fats supplies [NADH](../../../../../nadh.md) and other reducing equivalents. In the mitochondrial [electron transport chain](../../../../../electron-transport-chain.md), transfer towards [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) releases free energy. Complexes I, III and IV couple electron transfer to proton translocation from the matrix to the intermembrane space; complex II transfers electrons without directly pumping protons. The inner mitochondrial membrane retains the resulting [proton-motive force](../../../../../proton-motive-force.md). For movement of one mole of protons from outside to inside, define

$$
\Delta G_{\mathrm H^+}=F(\psi_{\mathrm{in}}-\psi_{\mathrm{out}})+RT\ln\frac{[\mathrm H^+]_{\mathrm{in}}}{[\mathrm H^+]_{\mathrm{out}}}.
$$

Here $F$ is the [Faraday constant](../../../../../faraday-constant.md) and $\psi$ is the electrical potential. Both the relatively negative matrix potential and its relatively alkaline pH favour inward movement. An inward driving voltage of magnitude $0.18\,\mathrm V$ corresponds to approximately $17.4\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ per mole of protons. This is an illustrative calculation, not a universal mitochondrial measurement.

Proton movement through [ATP synthase](../../../../../atp-synthase.md) drives rotation and conformational changes that couple [ADP](../../../../../adenosine-diphosphate.md) and phosphate to [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) formation. Several protons are required per [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md), and transport of phosphate and adenine nucleotides adds energetic costs; there is no universal whole-number [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) yield for every animal cell. Reconstitution of a light-driven proton pump with ATP-synthesizing machinery in lipid vesicles showed that an independently generated gradient can support [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) formation. This experiment used an artificial mixture of components, providing a mechanistic test rather than a model of animal-cell photosynthesis.[Racker and Stoeckenius, 1974, pp. 662–663, reconstituted light-driven proton uptake and ATP formation](../../../../../reconstitution-of-purple-membrane-vesicles-catalyzing-light-driven-proton-uptake-and-adenosine-triphosphate-formation-by-efraim-racker-and-others.md) In mitochondria, respiratory inhibitors, an ATP-synthase inhibitor and a [protonophore](../../../../../protonophore.md) give distinct predictions for [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) consumption, [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) production and the gradient, helping distinguish electron transfer from coupling.

[ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) then supplies a mobile chemical coupling intermediate. Transfer of a phosphate group can make an otherwise unfavourable biosynthetic step favourable through a common intermediate. [sodium-potassium ATPase](../../../../../sodium-potassium-atpase.md) couples [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) hydrolysis to ion pumping; sodium-dependent cotransport uses the stored gradient for [secondary active transport](../../../../../secondary-active-transport.md). [Myosin](../../../../../myosin.md) couples its ATPase cycle to force and displacement on [actin](../../../../../actin.md). [Substrate-level phosphorylation](../../../../../substrate-level-phosphorylation.md) also generates [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) directly during [glycolysis](../../../../../glycolysis.md) and other reactions without an obligatory respiratory gradient. [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) hydrolysis is favourable because of the free-energy difference between the full reactant and product states under cellular conditions, not because breaking a bond alone releases energy.

Conversion is never perfectly efficient: heat is produced, proton leak can bypass [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) synthase, and controlled uncoupling can favour heat production. [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) concentration and the [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md)/[ADP](../../../../../adenosine-diphosphate.md)/phosphate ratio reflect a maintained nonequilibrium state. **Energy transduction is therefore a chain of coupled chemical, electrical, osmotic and mechanical changes, sustained by metabolism and constrained by thermodynamics.**
