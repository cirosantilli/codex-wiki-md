↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

One candidate inward-facing [PCR primer](../../../../../../pcr-primer.md) pair, written $5'$ to $3'$, is

$$
\begin{aligned}
F&:\quad\mathrm{TGCCCTCTATTCACAGATAAC},\\
R&:\quad\mathrm{AGCTTCCATCTGAAGAGGGT}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The forward [PCR primer](../../../../../../pcr-primer.md) binds the intronic flank immediately upstream of exon 5; the reverse [PCR primer](../../../../../../pcr-primer.md) is the [reverse complement](../../../../../../reverse-complement.md) of a downstream exon-5 segment. **Their [amplicon](../../../../../../amplicon.md) includes the repeat while both binding sites lie outside it.** Using the displayed reference sequence gives an approximately $122$-base-pair product; an [indel](../../../../../../indel.md) changes the intervening length and sequence. The primers have matched simple Wallace-rule estimates near $60\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. These are candidates from the supplied sequence: confirm genomic specificity, absence of variable primer-binding sites, secondary structures and actual reaction conditions before use. Sequence from a unique flank toward the repeat and verify a single specific product.
