<h1 id="section-b/12/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The reported coefficients add to

$$
0.29+0.04+0.33+0.17+0.03+0=0.86.
$$

For a complete set of independently scaled activities controlling a steady-state flux, the [flux summation theorem](../../../../../../flux-summation-theorem.md) gives $\sum_i C_i^J=1$, where $C_i^J=\partial\ln J/\partial\ln E_i$. One way to see this is to scale all reaction capacities by the same factor: steady-state concentrations can remain the same while all fluxes scale by that factor, so the summed logarithmic sensitivities equal one. **The listed processes therefore leave a net coefficient of about $0.14$ unaccounted for under the model assumptions.** Measurement uncertainty, imperfect inhibitor specificity or incompatible measurements could also contribute to the discrepancy. Control is distributed; neither the sum nor the largest individual coefficient identifies a single universal rate-limiting step.
