<h1 id="section-b/12/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At low [ATP](../../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) demand, the [proton-motive force](../../../../../../proton-motive-force.md) is high and much of the remaining oxygen consumption supports [proton leak](../../../../../../proton-leak.md). Increasing leak conductance then creates a substantial extra demand for proton pumping and strongly changes respiratory flux. At high phosphorylation demand, proton return through [ATP synthase](../../../../../../atp-synthase.md) is large, the [proton-motive force](../../../../../../proton-motive-force.md) is lower, and leak contributes a much smaller part of total proton return. **Control of oxygen consumption shifts toward substrate supply and the respiratory machinery, so the leak [flux control coefficient](../../../../../../flux-control-coefficient.md) becomes small.** The plot's value near zero does not mean that the physical leak current vanishes; the separate maximal-rate table likewise gives a small nonzero coefficient, $0.04$.
