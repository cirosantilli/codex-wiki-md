<h1 id="section-b/13/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

**An activating [Ras](../../../../../../ras.md) lesion is a parsimonious candidate**, because [Ras](../../../../../../ras.md) lies upstream of the branch to [Raf](../../../../../../raf.md) and the branch to [phosphoinositide 3-kinase](../../../../../../phosphoinositide-3-kinase.md). Persistent [GTP](../../../../../../guanosine-triphosphate.md)-bound [Ras](../../../../../../ras.md) can therefore produce both basal patterns through a single change. A lesion restricted to [MEK](../../../../../../mek.md) would not directly explain the simultaneous [Akt](../../../../../../protein-kinase-b.md) signal. The blot does not uniquely locate the mutation: a constitutively active upstream [receptor tyrosine kinase](../../../../../../receptor-tyrosine-kinase.md) or altered regulation of [Ras](../../../../../../ras.md) could also activate both branches. Test the [Ras](../../../../../../ras.md) nucleotide state, sequence relevant [genes](../../../../../../gene.md), and determine which targeted perturbation suppresses both outputs.
