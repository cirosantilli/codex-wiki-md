<h1 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A detectable [burst kinetics](../../../../../burst-kinetics.md) phase requires rapid formation and detection of an initial [reaction product](../../../../../product-chemistry.md), followed by a slower step that regenerates competent [enzyme](../../../../../enzyme-split.md). A common [hydrolysis](../../../../../hydrolysis.md) mechanism is

$$
E+S\rightleftharpoons ES\xrightarrow{k_2}E^*+P_1\xrightarrow{k_3}E+P_2,
$$

where $E^*$ can be a covalent [acyl-enzyme intermediate](../../../../../acyl-enzyme-intermediate.md). With excess [substrate](../../../../../substrate-biochemistry.md), rapid binding and $k_2\gg k_3$, the first turnover rapidly releases $P_1$ and traps much of the [enzyme](../../../../../enzyme-split.md) as $E^*$. Subsequent turnovers wait for the slow regeneration step. **The measured product must report the fast step:** monitoring only $P_2$ need not reveal a burst. Fast mixing and sufficient time resolution are needed, while spontaneous [substrate](../../../../../substrate-biochemistry.md) [hydrolysis](../../../../../hydrolysis.md), depletion and [enzyme](../../../../../enzyme-split.md) inactivation must be excluded.

For an illustrative saturated two-step cycle, initially unmodified [enzyme](../../../../../enzyme-split.md) concentration $E_T$ gives

$$
[P_1](t)=\frac{E_Tk_2k_3}{k_2+k_3}t+E_T\left(\frac{k_2}{k_2+k_3}\right)^2\left(1-e^{-(k_2+k_3)t}\right).
$$

The second term approaches a burst amplitude near $E_T$ when $k_2\gg k_3$, followed by the slower steady-state slope. A covalent intermediate is a useful example, not a logical requirement for every possible burst mechanism.
