For a primary [sigma factor](../../molecular-genetics.md#sigma-factor) of the $\sigma^{70}$ family, distinguish the following regions. Region 1.1 restrains inappropriate [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) binding by free [sigma factor](../../molecular-genetics.md#sigma-factor) and must move out of the downstream-[DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) channel during formation of the open [RNA polymerase](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-polymerase) complex; region 1.2 contributes to recognition around the discriminator downstream of the $-10$ element. Domain 2 binds the [RNA polymerase](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-polymerase) core, recognizes the $-10$ [gene promoter](../../molecular-genetics.md#promoter-genetics) element and helps melt the duplex and stabilize the exposed non-template strand. Domain 3 recognizes an extended $-10$ element; its region 3.2 projects toward the active-centre channel, helps position template and initiating substrates, and must be displaced as the nascent [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) grows. Domain 4 recognizes the $-35$ element through a [helix-turn-helix](../../protein.md#helix-turn-helix) motif and contacts the [RNA polymerase](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-polymerase) beta flap and some [transcription activators](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-activator). **Domains 2 and 4 supply the principal $-10$ and $-35$ sequence recognition, with the other regions controlling opening and productive initiation.** This domain map concerns the $\sigma^{70}$ family, rather than every structurally distinct bacterial [sigma factor](../../molecular-genetics.md#sigma-factor).[Abstract and structural analysis of sigma domains 2, 3 and 4](../../biology-bibliography.md#structure-of-the-bacterial-rna-polymerase-promoter-specificity-sigma-subunit-by-elizabeth-a-campbell-and-others)

Incubate a labelled precursor [messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#messenger-rna) containing an authentic $3'$-processing signal with a [HeLa](../../biological-cell.md#hela) nuclear extract, then resolve the products by [denaturing polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis](../../biology.md#denaturing-polyacrylamide-gel-electrophoresis). With [ATP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate), the upstream cleavage product acquires a heterogeneous [poly(A) tail](../../polymer.md#poly-a-tail). Replace [ATP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate) with an appropriate non-incorporable analogue, such as alpha,beta-methylene-ATP: a discrete product ending at the normal cleavage site still appears, but the extended tail does not. Thus **cleavage can occur when [polyadenylation](../../molecular-genetics.md#polyadenylation) is blocked**; it is not cleavage brought about by synthesis of the tail.[Abstract: cleavage without polyadenylation in the presence of alpha,beta-methylene-ATP](../../biology-bibliography.md#accurate-cleavage-and-polyadenylation-of-exogenous-rna-substrate-by-claire-l-moore-and-phillip-a-sharp)

Conversely, supply an [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) substrate already ending at the normal cleavage site: addition of [ATP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate) and the appropriate [poly(A) polymerase](../../enzyme.md#poly-a-polymerase) machinery can extend its free $3'$ hydroxyl without any new endonucleolytic cut.[Abstract: efficient polyadenylation of pre-cleaved RNA substrates in nuclear extract](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-aauaaa-sequence-is-required-both-for-cleavage-and-for-polyadenylation-of-simian-virus-40-pre-mrna-in-vitro-by-david-zarkower-and-others) Compare precursor, pre-cleaved substrate, no-extract and no-[ATP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate) controls, and map the product ends to distinguish correct processing from non-specific degradation. **These are separable reactions, normally coordinated in one processing complex.** Their separability does not imply that they use completely independent recognition factors.

<h3 id="section-a/3">3</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section A](#section-a)

<h4 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#section-a/3)

A detectable [burst kinetics](../../mathematical-biology.md#burst-kinetics) phase requires rapid formation and detection of an initial [reaction product](../../chemical-reaction.md#product-chemistry), followed by a slower step that regenerates competent [enzyme](../../enzyme.md). A common [hydrolysis](../../chemical-reaction.md#hydrolysis) mechanism is

$$
E+S\rightleftharpoons ES\xrightarrow{k_2}E^*+P_1\xrightarrow{k_3}E+P_2,
$$

where $E^*$ can be a covalent [acyl-enzyme intermediate](../../enzyme.md#acyl-enzyme-intermediate). With excess [substrate](../../enzyme.md#substrate-biochemistry), rapid binding and $k_2\gg k_3$, the first turnover rapidly releases $P_1$ and traps much of the [enzyme](../../enzyme.md) as $E^*$. Subsequent turnovers wait for the slow regeneration step. **The measured product must report the fast step:** monitoring only $P_2$ need not reveal a burst. Fast mixing and sufficient time resolution are needed, while spontaneous [substrate](../../enzyme.md#substrate-biochemistry) [hydrolysis](../../chemical-reaction.md#hydrolysis), depletion and [enzyme](../../enzyme.md) inactivation must be excluded.

For an illustrative saturated two-step cycle, initially unmodified [enzyme](../../enzyme.md) concentration $E_T$ gives

$$
[P_1](t)=\frac{E_Tk_2k_3}{k_2+k_3}t+E_T\left(\frac{k_2}{k_2+k_3}\right)^2\left(1-e^{-(k_2+k_3)t}\right).
$$

The second term approaches a burst amplitude near $E_T$ when $k_2\gg k_3$, followed by the slower steady-state slope. A covalent intermediate is a useful example, not a logical requirement for every possible burst mechanism.

<h3 id="section-a/4">4</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section A](#section-a)

<h4 id="section-a/4/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#section-a/4)

The enzyme [adenylate kinase](../../enzyme.md#adenylate-kinase) rapidly interconverts the adenine [nucleotides](../../molecule.md#nucleotide):

$$
2\mathrm{ADP}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{ATP}+\mathrm{AMP},\qquad K=\frac{[\mathrm{ATP}][\mathrm{AMP}]}{[\mathrm{ADP}]^2}.
$$

For approximately fixed conditions and near-equilibrium activity ratios,

$$
\frac{[\mathrm{AMP}]}{[\mathrm{ATP}]}=K\left(\frac{[\mathrm{ADP}]}{[\mathrm{ATP}]}\right)^2.
$$

**The AMP:ATP ratio responds quadratically to ADP:ATP.** Doubling ADP:ATP therefore quadruples AMP:ATP. In working [skeletal muscle](../../physiology.md#skeletal-muscle), [ATP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate) is also buffered by [creatine kinase](../../enzyme.md#creatine-kinase) and continuing [ATP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate) production, so its fractional change is relatively small. The initially smaller [AMP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-monophosphate) pool can undergo a much larger fractional increase, providing a sensitive signal of energetic stress to regulators such as [AMP-activated protein kinase](../../enzyme.md#amp-activated-protein-kinase). This concerns sensitivity of relative changes; it does not mean that the absolute [AMP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-monophosphate) concentration exceeds [ATP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate).

The [mitochondrial fission-fusion cycle](../../biological-cell.md#mitochondrial-fission-fusion-cycle) is continuous remodeling of a mitochondrial population by division and merger. In mammalian [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), [mitofusins](../../protein.md#mitofusin) promote fusion of outer mitochondrial membranes, while [OPA1](../../protein.md#opa1) supports fusion of inner mitochondrial membranes. Fusion mixes matrix contents and permits functional complementation between [mitochondria](../../biological-cell.md#mitochondria). Genetic disruption and rescue of [mitofusins](../../protein.md#mitofusin) demonstrated their requirement for normal mitochondrial fusion.[Abstract and Results: mitofusin loss, fragmented mitochondria and rescue](../../biology-bibliography.md#mitofusins-mfn1-and-mfn2-coordinately-regulate-mitochondrial-fusion-and-are-essential-for-embryonic-development-by-hsiuchen-chen-and-others)

For fission, recruited [Drp1](../../protein.md#drp1) assembles at constrictions and uses [GTP](../../molecule.md#guanosine-triphosphate)-dependent remodeling to help divide the [organelle](../../biological-cell.md#organelle).[Abstract and Results: Drp1 recruitment and division defects](../../biology-bibliography.md#dynamin-related-protein-drp1-is-required-for-mitochondrial-division-in-mammalian-cells-by-elena-smirnova-and-others) **The balance controls network shape, distribution and quality control.** Division can help distribute [mitochondria](../../biological-cell.md#mitochondria) to daughter [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) and segregate damaged portions for [mitophagy](../../biological-cell.md#mitophagy); fusion can restore mixed contents. These are regulated competing events in a network, not an obligatory synchronized sequence that every [mitochondrion](../../biological-cell.md#mitochondria) completes on a fixed timetable.

A [steroid hormone](../../signal-transduction.md#steroid-hormone) crosses the [lipid bilayer](../../mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) and binds an intracellular [nuclear receptor](../../protein.md#nuclear-receptor). For a cytoplasmic [glucocorticoid receptor](../../protein.md#glucocorticoid-receptor), ligand binding alters association with [molecular chaperones](../../protein.md#chaperone-protein), exposes nuclear-targeting functions and promotes accumulation in the [cell nucleus](../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus). Other [steroid hormone](../../signal-transduction.md#steroid-hormone) receptors already have substantial nuclear localization. The activated [receptor](../../protein.md#receptor-biochemistry) binds specific [hormone response elements](../../molecular-genetics.md#hormone-response-element) in [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid), often as a dimer, and changes recruitment of [transcription coactivators](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-coactivator) or [transcription corepressors](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-corepressor). These factors alter local [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin) and recruitment or activity of the [RNA polymerase II](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-polymerase-ii) machinery. **The result is selective activation or repression of target [genes](../../genetics.md#gene)**, followed by altered [messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#messenger-rna) and [protein](../../protein.md) abundance; the [hormone](../../signal-transduction.md#hormone) itself does not act as an [RNA polymerase](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-polymerase) or directly rewrite the [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) sequence.

Two distinct possibilities are **a second [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) affecting drug sensitivity of the targeted [receptor tyrosine kinase](../../protein.md#receptor-tyrosine-kinase)**, so that it remains active at the achieved inhibitor concentration, and **activation of a bypass signaling route**, such as another [receptor tyrosine kinase](../../protein.md#receptor-tyrosine-kinase) or a downstream [Ras](../../protein.md#ras) or [protein kinase](../../enzyme.md#protein-kinase) component, restoring proliferation despite inhibition of the original target. The resistant population may arise by selection of rare pre-existing [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) or by later acquisition of a change; recurrence alone cannot distinguish those histories. Compare target engagement, target [phosphorylation](../../chemical-reaction.md#phosphorylation) and downstream signaling in the original and recurrent samples, with [DNA sequencing](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-sequencing) and functional rescue or perturbation of the candidate resistance mechanism.

An [antibody-drug conjugate](../../protein.md#antibody-drug-conjugate) couples an [antibody](../../protein.md#antibody) against a tumor-associated surface [antigen](../../protein.md#antigen) to a potent cytotoxic payload through a suitable chemical linker. Binding enriches delivery to [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) displaying the target. Many designs undergo receptor-mediated [endocytosis](../../cell-membrane.md#endocytosis), trafficking through [endosomes](../../biological-cell.md#endosome) to a [lysosome](../../biological-cell.md#lysosome), and release of an active drug through linker cleavage or degradation of the [antibody](../../protein.md#antibody). The released payload can disrupt [microtubules](../../mathematical-biology.md#microtubule) or damage [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid), killing the target [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md). **Specificity comes from targeted delivery combined with controlled payload release.** It is relative rather than absolute: normal [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) may share the [antigen](../../protein.md#antigen), and a membrane-permeable released payload can also affect neighboring [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md). Successful action therefore depends on antigen abundance, accessibility, internalization, linker stability and payload sensitivity.

The [hydrogenosomes](../../biological-cell.md#hydrogenosome) and [mitosomes](../../biological-cell.md#mitosome) are specialized mitochondrion-related [organelles](../../biological-cell.md#organelle) found in various anaerobic or oxygen-limited [eukaryotes](../../organism.md#eukaryote). **Typical [hydrogenosomes](../../biological-cell.md#hydrogenosome) support anaerobic [ATP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate) production**, converting substrates such as [pyruvate](../../chemical-substance.md#pyruvate) into products including [acetate](../../chemical-substance.md#acetate), [carbon dioxide](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) and molecular [hydrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen). [Substrate-level phosphorylation](../../biochemistry.md#substrate-level-phosphorylation) supplies [ATP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate), while [hydrogenase](../../enzyme.md#hydrogenase) disposes of reducing equivalents as [hydrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen); oxygen is not the terminal acceptor of a conventional respiratory chain in this pathway.[Abstract and biochemical characterization of hydrogenosomal pyruvate metabolism](../../biology-bibliography.md#hydrogenosome-a-cytoplasmic-organelle-of-the-anaerobic-flagellate-tritrichomonas-foetus-and-its-role-in-pyruvate-metabolism-by-donald-g-lindmark-and-miklos-muller)

**The [mitosomes](../../biological-cell.md#mitosome) retain selected essential metabolic functions but do not generate [ATP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate) by [oxidative phosphorylation](../../biochemistry.md#oxidative-phosphorylation).** In [Giardia](../../organism.md#giardia), their role includes [iron-sulfur cluster](../../molecule.md#iron-sulfur-cluster) assembly and [protein](../../protein.md) maturation, demonstrated using organelle markers and biochemical reconstitution.[Abstract: localization and iron-sulfur protein maturation](../../biology-bibliography.md#mitochondrial-remnant-organelles-of-giardia-function-in-iron-sulphur-protein-maturation-by-jorge-tovar-and-others) Their functions are lineage-dependent: [Entamoeba histolytica](../../organism.md#entamoeba-histolytica) [mitosomes](../../biological-cell.md#mitosome) compartmentalize a [sulfate activation](../../biology.md#sulfate-activation) pathway.[Abstract and Results: mitosome fractionation, protein localization and activated sulfate products](../../biology-bibliography.md#mitosomes-in-entamoeba-histolytica-contain-a-sulfate-activation-pathway-by-fumika-mi-ichi-and-others) Thus neither organelle class should be described simply as a nonfunctional [mitochondrion](../../biological-cell.md#mitochondria).

The catalytic [serine residue](../../protein.md#serine-residue) supplies a [nucleophile](../../chemical-reaction.md#nucleophile): its side-chain oxygen attacks the carbonyl carbon of the [beta-lactam](../../chemical-substance.md#beta-lactam) ring. This generates a tetrahedral intermediate, followed by opening of the ring and formation of a covalent [acyl-enzyme intermediate](../../enzyme.md#acyl-enzyme-intermediate). Subsequent attack by activated [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) hydrolyzes that intermediate and regenerates the [enzyme](../../enzyme.md). **The [serine residue](../../protein.md#serine-residue) performs covalent catalysis and is regenerated, rather than being consumed as a reagent.**

<h4 id="section-b/11/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [11](#section-b/11)

<h5 id="section-b/11/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-b/11/b)

An appropriately positioned [general base](../../chemical-kinetics.md#general-base) helps remove the [proton](../../physics.md#proton) from the [serine residue](../../protein.md#serine-residue) hydroxyl, increasing its nucleophilicity. A [lysine residue](../../protein.md#lysine-residue), such as Lys73 in the class A [beta-lactamase](../../enzyme.md#beta-lactamase) catalytic network, is a plausible participant; its protonation state depends on its active-site environment and interactions with other residues. A [glutamate residue](../../protein.md#glutamate-residue), notably Glu166, also participates in proton transfer and activation of the [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) needed for deacylation. **Efficient catalysis requires proton-transfer chemistry as well as positioning the attacking oxygen.** The precise division of roles during acylation depends on the [enzyme](../../enzyme.md) state, so the drawing alone cannot assign a complete mechanism to one residue.[Abstract and active-site protonation analysis](../../biology-bibliography.md#probing-the-role-of-the-conserved-residue-glu166-in-a-class-a-beta-lactamase-using-neutron-and-x-ray-protein-crystallography-by-patricia-s-langan-and-others)

**Bond c**, the carbonyl-carbon–nitrogen bond within the four-membered [beta-lactam](../../chemical-substance.md#beta-lactam) ring, is cleaved. [Hydrolysis](../../chemical-reaction.md#hydrolysis) opens the cyclic [amide](../../chemical-substance.md#amide); it does not cut the side-chain amide or a carbon–carbon bond. The resulting open-ring product no longer has the intact [beta-lactam](../../chemical-substance.md#beta-lactam) pharmacophore required for its usual antibacterial action.

The compound [imidazole](../../chemical-substance.md#imidazole) resembles the metal-coordinating part of the [histidine](../../chemical-substance.md#histidine) side chain and competes for binding to the [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel) sites in [immobilized metal affinity chromatography](../../chromatography.md#immobilized-metal-affinity-chromatography). At the lower concentration in the wash, it helps displace weakly or non-specifically bound [proteins](../../protein.md) while the multiple contacts of the [histidine tag](../../protein.md#histidine-tag) retain the desired [enzyme](../../enzyme.md). At the higher elution concentration, competition is strong enough to displace the tagged [enzyme](../../enzyme.md). **Low [imidazole](../../chemical-substance.md#imidazole) improves washing; high [imidazole](../../chemical-substance.md#imidazole) elutes the target.** Remove or match the elution-buffer components before interpreting downstream assays, and verify purity independently of total [protein](../../protein.md) concentration.

For the [Hanes-Woolf plot](../../mathematical-biology.md#hanes-woolf-plot), the slope is $1/V_{\max}$, the vertical intercept is $K_m/V_{\max}$, and the horizontal intercept is $-K_m$. $$

The negative-axis intercepts independently give approximately $-26$ and $-5\,\mathrm{\mu M}$. **[Succinate](../../chemical-substance.md#succinate) is the most plausible substrate.** Its oxidation by [succinate dehydrogenase](../../biochemistry.md#succinate-dehydrogenase), or [respiratory complex II](../../biochemistry.md#succinate-dehydrogenase), supplies electrons to the [ubiquinone](../../chemical-substance.md#ubiquinone) pool, bypassing [respiratory complex I](../../biochemistry.md#respiratory-complex-i). <h4 id="section-b/12/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [12](#section-b/12)

<h5 id="section-b/12/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#section-b/12/c)

[Hexokinase](../../enzyme.md#hexokinase) consumes exported [ATP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate) in the reaction

$$
\mathrm{glucose+ATP\longrightarrow glucose\ 6\!\! -\!phosphate+ADP}.
$$

The [adenine nucleotide translocator](../../biological-cell.md#adenine-nucleotide-translocator) imports regenerated [ADP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-diphosphate), permitting [ATP synthase](../../enzyme.md#atp-synthase) to use the [proton-motive force](../../chemical-potential.md#proton-motive-force). Increased proton return reduces the opposing gradient and allows faster activity of the [electron transport chain](../../biochemistry.md#electron-transport-chain) and oxygen consumption: this is [respiratory control](../../biochemistry.md#respiratory-control). The first [hexokinase](../../enzyme.md#hexokinase) additions therefore increase demand and respiration. **By about the fourth addition, demand no longer limits the measured flux**: the respiratory and transport system has reached its available capacity, so more [hexokinase](../../enzyme.md#hexokinase) cannot increase it. This does not imply that [hexokinase](../../enzyme.md#hexokinase) ceases functioning, and its zero incremental control at maximal demand does not make it dispensable for maintaining that demand.

<h4 id="section-b/12/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [12](#section-b/12)

<h5 id="section-b/12/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#section-b/12/d)

At low [ATP](../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate) demand, the [proton-motive force](../../chemical-potential.md#proton-motive-force) is high and much of the remaining oxygen consumption supports [proton leak](../../biochemistry.md#proton-leak). Increasing leak conductance then creates a substantial extra demand for proton pumping and strongly changes respiratory flux. At high phosphorylation demand, proton return through [ATP synthase](../../enzyme.md#atp-synthase) is large, the [proton-motive force](../../chemical-potential.md#proton-motive-force) is lower, and leak contributes a much smaller part of total proton return. **Control of oxygen consumption shifts toward substrate supply and the respiratory machinery, so the leak [flux control coefficient](../../biology.md#flux-control-coefficient) becomes small.** The plot's value near zero does not mean that the physical leak current vanishes; the separate maximal-rate table likewise gives a small nonzero coefficient, $0.04$.

<h4 id="section-b/12/e">e</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [12](#section-b/12)

<h5 id="section-b/12/e/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#section-b/12/e)

The [mitochondrial dicarboxylate carrier](../../biological-cell.md#mitochondrial-dicarboxylate-carrier) is an inner-membrane transport [protein](../../protein.md) that exchanges suitable dicarboxylates, including [succinate](../../chemical-substance.md#succinate) and [malate](../../chemical-substance.md#malate), with counter-substrates such as [phosphate](../../chemical-substance.md#phosphate). In this experiment, it supplies external [succinate](../../chemical-substance.md#succinate) to the matrix side of [respiratory complex II](../../biochemistry.md#succinate-dehydrogenase); the [inner mitochondrial membrane](../../biological-cell.md#inner-mitochondrial-membrane) does not simply allow unrestricted diffusion of the charged [substrate](../../enzyme.md#substrate-biochemistry). **Without adequate uptake, substrate delivery would restrict respiration even if the respiratory enzymes remained intact.** Its essentiality is substrate- and preparation-dependent: it is not the obligatory entry route for every possible respiratory fuel.

<h4 id="section-b/12/f">f</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [12](#section-b/12)

<h5 id="section-b/12/f/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#section-b/12/f)

The reported coefficients add to

$$
0.29+0.04+0.33+0.17+0.03+0=0.86.
$$

For a complete set of independently scaled activities controlling a steady-state flux, the [flux summation theorem](../../biology.md#flux-summation-theorem) gives $\sum_i C_i^J=1$, where $C_i^J=\partial\ln J/\partial\ln E_i$. One way to see this is to scale all reaction capacities by the same factor: steady-state concentrations can remain the same while all fluxes scale by that factor, so the summed logarithmic sensitivities equal one. **The listed processes therefore leave a net coefficient of about $0.14$ unaccounted for under the model assumptions.** Measurement uncertainty, imperfect inhibitor specificity or incompatible measurements could also contribute to the discrepancy. Control is distributed; neither the sum nor the largest individual coefficient identifies a single universal rate-limiting step.

Use a phosphotyrosine or validated phospho-[PDGFR](../../protein.md#pdgfr) [antibody](../../protein.md#antibody), ideally alongside total [PDGFR](../../protein.md#pdgfr). The larger [receptor](../../protein.md#receptor-biochemistry) may also require a different gel and transfer range, but changing the size range alone would not correct the epitope mismatch.

The forward [PCR primer](../../molecular-genetics.md#pcr-primer) binds the intronic flank immediately upstream of exon 5; the reverse [PCR primer](../../molecular-genetics.md#pcr-primer) is the [reverse complement](../../polymer.md#reverse-complement) of a downstream exon-5 segment. **Their [amplicon](../../molecular-genetics.md#amplicon) includes the repeat while both binding sites lie outside it.** Using the displayed reference sequence gives an approximately $122$-base-pair product; an [indel](../../genetics.md#indel) changes the intervening length and sequence. <h4 id="section-c/14/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [14](#section-c/14)

<h5 id="section-c/14/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-c/14/b)

Two desirable properties are **similar [primer melting temperatures](../../molecular-genetics.md#primer-melting-temperature)**, allowing one annealing condition to support both primers efficiently, and **specific binding with little self-complementarity or mutual complementarity**, especially at the $3'$ ends, to reduce off-target products and [primer dimers](../../molecular-genetics.md#primer-dimer). Suitable length and a balanced [GC content](../../polymer.md#gc-content) help achieve these goals. A long homopolymer, strong hairpin or repeated genomic binding site would compromise a candidate even if its calculated melting temperature looked acceptable.

<h4 id="section-c/14/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [14](#section-c/14)

<h5 id="section-c/14/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#section-c/14/c)

The repetitive tract can cause [DNA polymerase slippage](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-polymerase-slippage) during [PCR amplification](../../molecular-genetics.md#polymerase-chain-reaction) or the sequencing extension itself, generating stutter products. A heterozygous length variant creates overlapping [Sanger sequencing](../../molecular-genetics.md#sanger-sequencing) traces downstream of the [indel](../../genetics.md#indel) because the two templates lose register; this does not by itself indicate random sequencing failure. Tumor heterogeneity or contamination by normal tissue can add further mixed templates. **Avoid creating or obscuring the mutation during amplification and avoid interpreting a mixed trace as one clean allele.** Use a suitable high-fidelity [DNA polymerase](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-polymerase), independent amplifications and bidirectional reads, and resolve individual alleles by molecular cloning or an appropriate single-molecule/amplicon sequencing method if necessary. Check for non-specific products and allelic dropout caused by variation in a primer-binding site.

A practical design uses one common exon-4 forward [PCR primer](../../molecular-genetics.md#pcr-primer) with an exon-specific reverse [PCR primer](../../molecular-genetics.md#pcr-primer) for each [transcript isoform](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcript-isoform). Candidate sequences, all written $5'$ to $3'$, are

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_4&:\quad\mathrm{GCGCTTTGGCCTTGATCAAC},\\
R_5&:\quad\mathrm{AGCTTCCATCTGAAGAGGGT},\\
R_6&:\quad\mathrm{CTTGTATGTAGCGAACTCCG}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Use **$F_4/R_5$ for TCF-long and $F_4/R_6$ for TCF-short**: $R_5$ recognizes exon 5, while $R_6$ recognizes exon 6. The displayed mature reference sequences predict approximately $130$- and $67$-base-pair products, respectively. Check actual splice products because a transcript with unexpected exon combinations could defeat the assumed specificity. The simple melting-temperature estimates are approximately $62$, $60$ and $60\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$.

Prepare matched [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid), remove contaminating [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid), carry out [reverse transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#reverse-transcription) and validate each [RT-qPCR](../../molecular-genetics.md#rt-qpcr) assay with known isoform standards, a single-product check, a no-template control and a no-reverse-transcriptase control. The primers span an [intron](../../biology.md#intron) in the genomic arrangement but do not themselves cross a [splice junction](../../biology.md#splice-junction), so the DNA control remains important. **Calibrate amplification efficiency and fluorescence response before comparing molecule numbers between the two assays.** A redesigned junction-specific assay is another option once additional suitable flanking sequence is available.

<h4 id="section-c/14/s">s</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [14](#section-c/14)

<h5 id="section-c/14/s/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [S](#section-c/14/s)

Replacing the acceptor's A with C changes the canonical $3'$ [splice site](../../biology.md#splice-site) from AG to CG. **The predicted primary effect is impaired recognition of the exon-5 acceptor**, reducing normal exon-5 inclusion. <h5 id="section-c/14/t/solution">Solution</h5>

**An increased colorectal-cancer risk is mechanistically plausible in the supplied model, but not demonstrated by these data.** Reduced normal TCF-long production and a shift toward TCF-short could weaken a differentiated-cell expression program and favor proliferation.
