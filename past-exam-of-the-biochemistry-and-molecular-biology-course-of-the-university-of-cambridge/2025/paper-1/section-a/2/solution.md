↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

**The same base sequence can present different molecular surfaces in different helical conformations.** [A-DNA](../../../../../a-dna.md), [B-DNA](../../../../../b-dna.md) and [Z-DNA](../../../../../z-dna.md) all retain paired antiparallel strands, but differ in handedness, backbone geometry, [sugar puckering](../../../../../sugar-puckering.md) and groove accessibility. The familiar dimensions are approximate averages: sequence, hydration and bound molecules change the details, so they are structural signatures rather than rigid constants.

[B-DNA](../../../../../b-dna.md) is a right-handed [DNA double helix](../../../../../dna-double-helix.md) with about $10.5$ [base pairs](../../../../../base-pair.md) per turn, a rise near $0.34\ \mathrm{nm}$ per pair and a diameter near $2\ \mathrm{nm}$. Its [base pairs](../../../../../base-pair.md) lie approximately perpendicular to the helical axis; the sugars are predominantly C2′-endo and the [glycosidic bonds](../../../../../glycosidic-bond.md) are anti. A broad accessible [major groove](../../../../../major-groove.md) and a narrower [minor groove](../../../../../minor-groove.md) expose different edges of the paired [nucleobases](../../../../../nucleotide-base.md). Hydrated duplex [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) commonly adopts this family of conformations, although local bends and groove widths depend on sequence.

[A-DNA](../../../../../a-dna.md) is also right-handed, but is shorter and wider: roughly $11$ [base pairs](../../../../../base-pair.md) per turn, a rise around $0.26\ \mathrm{nm}$ per pair, and a diameter near $2.3\ \mathrm{nm}$. C3′-endo [sugar puckering](../../../../../sugar-puckering.md) brings successive phosphates closer along each strand. The [base pairs](../../../../../base-pair.md) are inclined and displaced from the axis, producing a deep narrow [major groove](../../../../../major-groove.md) and a broad shallow [minor groove](../../../../../minor-groove.md). Reduced hydration favours [A-DNA](../../../../../a-dna.md). Double-stranded [RNA](../../../../../ribonucleic-acid.md) and many [DNA-RNA hybrids](../../../../../dna-rna-hybrid.md) adopt A-like geometry because the ribose 2′-hydroxyl constrains the backbone; this is important when extending a DNA-only comparison to cellular [nucleic acids](../../../../../nucleic-acid.md).

[Z-DNA](../../../../../z-dna.md) is a left-handed duplex with a zigzag backbone and a two-base-pair conformational repeat. It has approximately $12$ [base pairs](../../../../../base-pair.md) per turn, a rise near $0.37\ \mathrm{nm}$ per pair and a diameter around $1.8\ \mathrm{nm}$. Alternating purine–pyrimidine sequences, especially alternating G and C, favour it under suitable conditions. Purines commonly adopt syn [glycosidic bonds](../../../../../glycosidic-bond.md) and C3′-endo sugars, whereas pyrimidines remain anti with C2′-endo sugars. Its [minor groove](../../../../../minor-groove.md) is narrow and deep, while the conventional [major groove](../../../../../major-groove.md) is flattened and poorly defined. Atomic-resolution analysis of an alternating CG oligomer established that a duplex can adopt this left-handed fold.[pp. 680–686, alternating CG duplex and left-handed structure](../../../../../molecular-structure-of-a-left-handed-double-helical-dna-fragment-at-atomic-resolution-by-a-h-j-wang-and-others.md) High salt in a crystal experiment is evidence of an accessible conformation, not proof that most cellular [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) is in that state; [negative supercoiling](../../../../../negative-supercoiling.md) and protein binding can instead favour local transitions.

These geometries determine how [proteins](../../../../../protein-split.md) read sequence. In [B-DNA](../../../../../b-dna.md), the pattern of [hydrogen bond](../../../../../hydrogen-bond.md) donors, acceptors and hydrophobic groups in the [major groove](../../../../../major-groove.md) distinguishes the four oriented [base pairs](../../../../../base-pair.md) without strand separation. The [minor groove](../../../../../minor-groove.md) carries less direct chemical information: A–T and T–A present similar patterns there, as do G–C and C–G. Specificity can nevertheless arise from [DNA shape readout](../../../../../dna-shape-readout.md), including groove width, electrostatic potential, deformability and hydration, combined with contacts to the phosphate backbone. The chemical accessibility of base edges underlies the classic analysis of sequence-specific recognition of duplex [nucleic acids](../../../../../nucleic-acid.md).[pp. 804–808, accessibility of base-edge recognition patterns](../../../../../sequence-specific-recognition-of-double-helical-nucleic-acids-by-proteins-by-nadrian-c-seeman-and-others.md)

An [alpha helix](../../../../../alpha-helix.md) from a [transcription factor](../../../../../transcription-factor.md) can fit a [B-DNA](../../../../../b-dna.md) [major groove](../../../../../major-groove.md), but the same docking arrangement need not fit the deep narrow groove of [A-DNA](../../../../../a-dna.md). Many double-stranded [RNA](../../../../../ribonucleic-acid.md)-binding interactions therefore emphasize backbone or shape, although A-form geometry does not make sequence-specific RNA recognition impossible. A [Z-DNA](../../../../../z-dna.md)-binding domain can recognize the characteristic left-handed backbone, making recognition partly conformation-specific rather than simply recognition of an exposed base code. In cells, [nucleosomes](../../../../../nucleosome.md), competing [proteins](../../../../../protein-split.md), torsional stress and local hydration further determine access. Binding assays should compare sequences and conformations independently, using structural or spectroscopic controls: a sequence mutation can change both the chemical recognition pattern and the probability of adopting the recognized structure.
