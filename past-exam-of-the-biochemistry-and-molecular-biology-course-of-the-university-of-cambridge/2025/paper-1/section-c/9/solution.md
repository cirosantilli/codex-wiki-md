↑ **Parent:** [9](../9.md)

**Photoprotection must regulate both the total excitation arriving at reaction centres and its distribution between the two photosystems.** When light absorption exceeds the capacity for productive electron transfer and carbon metabolism, excited chlorophyll and reduced electron carriers persist. Triplet chlorophyll can generate [singlet oxygen](../../../../../singlet-oxygen.md), while inappropriate electron transfer to oxygen can generate other [reactive oxygen species](../../../../../reactive-oxygen-species.md). These can damage pigments, lipids and proteins, including photosystem reaction centres. Excess light and unbalanced excitation are related but distinct problems.

The fastest major response to excess light in many plants is energy-dependent [non-photochemical quenching](../../../../../non-photochemical-quenching.md). Electron transport builds a proton gradient across the [thylakoid membrane](../../../../../thylakoid-membrane.md), acidifying the lumen. Protonation-dependent changes involving [PsbS](../../../../../psbs.md) and antenna proteins favour harmless dissipation of excitation as heat. The [xanthophyll cycle](../../../../../xanthophyll-cycle.md) contributes: under lumen acidification, [violaxanthin de-epoxidase](../../../../../violaxanthin-de-epoxidase.md) converts violaxanthin through antheraxanthin towards zeaxanthin, altering antenna quenching properties. In dimmer conditions these changes can reverse. Quenching therefore reduces the excitation reaching photosystem II before every captured photon becomes a potentially damaging charge-separation event.

An Arabidopsis mutant lacking normal PsbS-dependent behaviour showed a major loss of rapidly inducible quenching despite retaining light-harvesting capability; this separates a protective antenna-control function from the ability to absorb light.[Abstract and pp. 391–395, PsbS mutant and non-photochemical quenching](../../../../../a-pigment-binding-protein-essential-for-regulation-of-photosynthetic-light-harvesting-by-xiao-ping-li-and-others.md) A convincing interpretation compares absorbed light, pigment content, baseline photosynthetic competence and restoration by complementation. Reduced chlorophyll fluorescence alone is not sufficient evidence of protective heat dissipation: photochemical use of excitation and irreversible damage can also change fluorescence. Saturating-pulse measurements help separate photochemical and non-photochemical components.

[photosynthetic control](../../../../../photosynthetic-control.md) at the [cytochrome b6f complex](../../../../../cytochrome-b6f-complex.md) provides another response to lumen acidification, slowing electron delivery towards [photosystem I](../../../../../photosystem-i.md) and limiting excessive reduction of its acceptor side. [cyclic electron flow around photosystem I](../../../../../cyclic-electron-flow-around-photosystem-i.md) returns electrons to the intersystem chain, supporting proton-gradient formation and ATP production without net NADPH production. This can adjust the ATP-to-reductant supply and reinforce quenching; it is not simply a way of making more NADPH. Mutant analysis linking PGR5-dependent electron-flow regulation to photoprotection supports the connection between electron allocation and protection.[Abstract and pp. 361–371, mutant analysis of electron-flow control and photoprotection](../../../../../pgr5-is-involved-in-cyclic-electron-flow-around-photosystem-i-and-is-essential-for-photoprotection-in-arabidopsis-by-yuri-munekage-and-others.md) Alternative electron sinks, including oxygen-reducing pathways, can relieve pressure under some conditions, but require antioxidant systems because oxygen chemistry can itself be damaging. [photorespiration](../../../../../photorespiration.md) also consumes energy and reducing capacity in appropriate conditions. These routes differ across photosynthetic lineages and are not interchangeable universal safety valves.

Unequal excitation requires redistribution. Preferential excitation of [photosystem II](../../../../../photosystem-ii.md) reduces the [plastoquinone](../../../../../plastoquinone.md) pool, signalling an imbalance in electron supply to [photosystem I](../../../../../photosystem-i.md). In plant [photosynthetic state transitions](../../../../../photosynthetic-state-transition.md), activation of the [STN7 kinase](../../../../../stn7-kinase.md) promotes phosphorylation of a mobile fraction of [LHCII](../../../../../lhcii.md), decreasing excitation delivery to photosystem II and increasing its functional association with photosystem I. When the plastoquinone pool becomes more oxidized, dephosphorylation favours the reverse allocation. This balances excitation rather than dissipating all of it. A kinase-deficient Arabidopsis mutant defective in state transitions demonstrates the requirement for STN7 in this response.[Figure 1 and pp. 892–895, STN7 requirement for state transitions](../../../../../state-transitions-and-light-adaptation-require-chloroplast-thylakoid-protein-kinase-stn7-by-stephane-bellafiore-and-others.md)

State transitions can be tested by switching light preferentially exciting the two photosystems, measuring reversible fluorescence changes and antenna phosphorylation, and comparing wild type, mutant and complemented material. The same experiment should assess electron-transfer capacity so that loss of a state-transition signal is not confused with nonspecific photosynthetic failure. Different algae can use different antenna organizations, and cyanobacterial redistribution need not follow the plant LHCII mechanism. If photosystem I is preferentially excited, the reverse redox signal favours a greater share of antenna excitation reaching photosystem II.

Other short-term protections include carotenoid quenching of triplet chlorophyll or singlet oxygen, antioxidant removal of reactive products, and chloroplast avoidance movements that reduce absorbed light. Replacement of damaged [D1 protein](../../../../../d1-protein.md) repairs photosystem II on a slower short-term timescale; it compensates for damage rather than preventing the original photochemical injury. Rapid quenching, reversible antenna redistribution, electron-flow control and repair therefore address different failure points. **A protected organism keeps excitation, electron flow and metabolic demand sufficiently coordinated to avoid prolonged damaging states**, rather than merely maximizing light capture.
