<h1 id="section-b/5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

**A purified sample should be characterized along independent axes: identity, amount, chemical integrity, solution assembly, conformation and function.** A high-resolution structural experiment cannot compensate for analysing the wrong protein or a mixture of molecular states. Conversely, useful biochemical information does not require an atomic structure.

First, verify identity and integrity. [SDS-PAGE](../../../../../sds-page.md) under reducing and nonreducing conditions estimates apparent subunit size and reveals many contaminants or disulfide-linked species. A single band cannot exclude a same-sized contaminant, a small chemical modification or several co-migrating oligomers. An [immunoblot](../../../../../western-blot.md) adds epitope recognition, while peptide [mass spectrometry](../../../../../mass-spectrometry.md) checks sequence-derived peptides and intact-mass analysis tests the overall molecular mass. Comparing measured and predicted masses can reveal cleavage, retained tags or modifications, but incomplete peptide coverage and heterogeneity constrain the inference. A tag-specific signal establishes the tag's presence rather than the identity of every residue.

Measure [protein concentration](../../../../../protein-concentration.md) using absorbance and an appropriate extinction coefficient, or a calibrated colorimetric assay with compatible standards. The [Beer-Lambert law](../../../../../beer-lambert-law.md), $A=\varepsilon lc$, converts absorbance to concentration only when the assumed molecular species and optical conditions apply. [tryptophan residues](../../../../../tryptophan-residue.md), [tyrosine residues](../../../../../tyrosine-residue.md) and disulfides contribute to ultraviolet absorption; light scattering and nucleic-acid contamination can inflate readings. Buffer blanks, dilution linearity and a second quantification method help identify such problems. A concentration stated as monomer equivalents must not be confused with the concentration of oligomeric particles.

Next, determine whether the material is monodisperse and how it assembles in solution. [Size-exclusion chromatography](../../../../../size-exclusion-chromatography.md) reports hydrodynamic size and can separate aggregates, but elution calibrated with globular standards gives an apparent size, not an absolute molecular mass for an elongated protein. [SEC-MALS](../../../../../size-exclusion-chromatography-with-multi-angle-light-scattering.md) combines separation with [multi-angle light scattering](../../../../../multi-angle-light-scattering.md) and a concentration detector to estimate the molar mass across the eluting peak; it can distinguish a large monomer from a compact oligomer when the detector models and sample composition are suitable.[Abstract and protocol, hydrodynamic SEC calibration versus concentration-coupled light scattering](../../../../../characterization-of-proteins-by-size-exclusion-chromatography-coupled-to-multi-angle-light-scattering-sec-mals-by-daniel-some-and-others.md) [Analytical ultracentrifugation](../../../../../analytical-ultracentrifugation.md) supplies a complementary sedimentation-based approach, and concentration series can reveal reversible association. [dynamic light scattering](../../../../../dynamic-light-scattering.md) is sensitive to aggregates but is strongly influenced by a small number of large particles; a visually narrow intensity distribution is not a precise count of all molecular species.

Conformation and stability can then be assessed without determining an atomic model. Far-ultraviolet [circular dichroism](../../../../../circular-dichroism.md) reports ensemble secondary-structure features, while near-ultraviolet [circular dichroism](../../../../../circular-dichroism.md) and intrinsic aromatic [fluorescence](../../../../../fluorescence-split.md) probe environments associated with tertiary packing. These signatures support folding and conformational changes but do not uniquely specify a three-dimensional fold. Thermal or chemical unfolding followed spectroscopically estimates stability only within an appropriate model: an irreversible aggregation-driven transition does not give an equilibrium folding free energy. [Differential scanning calorimetry](../../../../../differential-scanning-calorimetry.md) measures heat-capacity changes during a transition, and [limited proteolysis](../../../../../limited-proteolysis.md) can reveal exposed regions or stable domains. Buffer-only controls, matched concentrations and repeated heating–cooling cycles help separate reversible conformational change from precipitation.

Finally, test biological competence. For an [enzyme](../../../../../enzyme-split.md), initial-rate measurements over substrate and enzyme concentration ranges establish specific activity and, where justified, [Michaelis-Menten kinetics](../../../../../michaelis-menten-kinetics.md). For a binding protein, [isothermal titration calorimetry](../../../../../isothermal-titration-calorimetry.md) can estimate binding stoichiometry, affinity and enthalpy, while a kinetic method such as [surface plasmon resonance](../../../../../surface-plasmon-resonance.md) can resolve association and dissociation. Controls must include heat of dilution or nonspecific surface binding, ligand quality and the possibility of multivalent [avidity](../../../../../avidity.md). A measured dissociation constant does not by itself establish physiological function.

The methods should be chosen to answer a defined question and cross-check one another. For example, a clean gel, the expected intact mass, a single SEC-MALS species and concentration-independent native activity jointly support a useful preparation; none establishes complete homogeneity alone. Comparing fresh, stored and freeze–thawed samples identifies conditions suitable for later structural work. The resulting dossier should state what species is present under the actual experimental conditions and which features remain unresolved.
