<h1 id="fejer-riesz-theorem">Fejér–Riesz theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Trigonometric polynomial](trigonometric-polynomial.md)

A [trigonometric polynomial](trigonometric-polynomial.md) nonnegative on the [unit circle](complex-unit-circle.md) has a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) modulus-square factor of [polynomial degree](degree-of-a-polynomial.md) at most its trigonometric order. For a nonzero [trigonometric polynomial](trigonometric-polynomial.md) of actual order $d$, [conjugate symmetry of trigonometric polynomial coefficients](conjugate-symmetry-of-trigonometric-polynomial-coefficients.md) yields [reciprocal-conjugate root pairing](reciprocal-conjugate-root-pairing.md) for $P=z^dp$. [Even multiplicity of unit-circle roots of a nonnegative trigonometric polynomial](even-multiplicity-of-unit-circle-roots-of-a-nonnegative-trigonometric-polynomial.md) permits pairing all $2d$ [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md). Select representatives $\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_d$ and use

$$
z-\frac1{\overline{\zeta_i}}
=-\frac z{\overline{\zeta_i}}(\overline z-\overline{\zeta_i})
\quad(|z|=1).
$$

It follows that $p(z)=c\prod_i|z-\zeta_i|^2$ with $c=(-1)^dp_d/\prod_i\overline{\zeta_i}$. Evaluating away from the [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) gives $c>0$. Thus $q(z)=\sqrt c\prod_i(z-\zeta_i)$ works. Constant and zero [trigonometric polynomials](trigonometric-polynomial.md) have constant or zero factors. Different selections of [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) and constant phases can give different factors.
