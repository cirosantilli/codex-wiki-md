# Dirichlet test for improper integrals

↑ **Parent:** [Improper integral](improper-integral.md)

If $F(t)=\int_a^t f(x)\,dx$ is bounded and $g$ is a nonnegative differentiable decreasing [function](function-split.md) tending to zero, then $\int_a^\infty f(x)g(x)\,dx$ converges as an [improper integral](improper-integral.md). [Integration by parts](integration-by-parts.md) bounds the tail $\int_A^Bfg$ by a constant multiple of $g(A)$, because $F$ is bounded and $\int_A^B|g'|=g(A)-g(B)$. In particular $\int_1^\infty x^{-\alpha}\sin x\,dx$ converges for every $\alpha>0$, even though [absolute integrability](integrability.md) of that tail requires $\alpha>1$.
