<h1 id="desargues-s-theorem">Desargues's theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Projective plane](projective-plane.md)

For two [projective triangles](projective-triangle.md) in a [projective plane](projective-plane.md) over a [field](field.md), [perspectivity from a point](perspectivity-from-a-point.md) implies [perspectivity from a line](perspectivity-from-a-line.md). The usual nondegenerate form assumes that the corresponding-side intersections are well defined and distinct. Its dual is the converse, by [point-line duality](point-line-duality.md). The result holds in the [real projective plane](real-projective-plane.md); it is not an axiom valid in every abstract incidence plane.

One [linear algebra](linear-algebra-split.md) proof explains the collinearity. Choose a representative $o$ of the center. Rescale the paired representatives to $p'=p+\alpha o$, $q'=q+\beta o$, and $r'=r+\gamma o$, with nonzero coefficients in a nondegenerate configuration whose vertices differ from the center. The first corresponding-side intersection is represented by $x=\beta p-\alpha q=\beta p'-\alpha q'$. Similarly choose $y=\gamma q-\beta r$ and $z=\alpha r-\gamma p$. Then $\gamma x+\alpha y+\beta z=0$, so the three representatives are [linearly dependent](linear-dependence.md). Their [projective points](projective-point.md) are consequently [collinear](collinearity.md).
