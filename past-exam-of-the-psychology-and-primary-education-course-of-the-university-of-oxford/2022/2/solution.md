<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

**Psychological research can improve mathematical intervention by identifying a child's component difficulties, translating a proposed mechanism into teachable activities and testing whether gains transfer to meaningful mathematics. Low attainment is an outcome to explain, not proof of one defective “[number sense](../../../number-sense.md)” system.** A useful intervention must also specify its comparison condition, dose, intended learners and success criteria. The same program need not suit children whose errors have different origins.

Assessment can separate [counting](../../../counting.md), [number](../../../number.md)-word and numeral knowledge, [symbolic magnitude comparison](../../../symbolic-magnitude-comparison.md), [number-line estimation](../../../number-line-estimation.md), fact retrieval, calculation procedures and understanding of word problems. Language and [working memory](../../../working-memory.md) can affect these tasks, as can prior instruction and opportunity to practice. A broad low score may therefore conceal a relatively specific bottleneck. [Developmental dyscalculia](../../../developmental-dyscalculia.md) describes persistent mathematical [learning](../../../learning.md) difficulty in an appropriate assessment context; it should not be inferred from one poor session or used to assume that every affected child has the same cognitive profile.

[Ann Dowker](../../../ann-dowker.md)'s [Numeracy](../../../numeracy.md) Recovery work illustrates a component-oriented approach: examine the child's performance in different arithmetical activities and provide targeted support rather than prescribing identical exercises from an overall score. Its early pilot status is important. Improvement after a program is compatible with benefit, but without a strong counterfactual it can also reflect ordinary teaching, repeated testing or [regression toward the mean](../../../regression-toward-the-mean.md). The psychological formulation supplies a rationale that subsequently requires controlled evaluation; it is not itself an effect estimate.[2001; component assessment and targeted intervention in the pilot scheme](../../../numeracy-recovery-a-pilot-scheme-for-early-intervention-with-young-children-with-numeracy-difficulties-by-ann-dowker.md)

For a child who can recite [numbers](../../../number.md) but cannot connect them to quantities, an intervention can make the relationship between [number](../../../number.md) words, numerals and ordered quantities explicit. [Geetha B. Ramani](../../../geetha-b-ramani.md) and colleagues tested a linear [number](../../../number.md) board game against an otherwise similar color board game with low-income preschoolers. Roughly an hour of play improved several numerical outcomes, including magnitude comparison, [counting](../../../counting.md), numeral identification and [number-line estimation](../../../number-line-estimation.md); gains were retained at a nine-week follow-up. The matched game comparison helps separate numerical content from merely receiving [attention](../../../attention.md) or playing with an adult.[2008; numerical versus color board, four outcomes and retention](../../../promoting-broad-and-stable-improvements-in-low-income-children-s-numerical-knowledge-through-playing-number-board-games-by-geetha-b-ramani-and-others.md)

The result supports a mechanism-informed activity, but its scope needs care. Low-income preschoolers are not equivalent to children diagnosed with [developmental dyscalculia](../../../developmental-dyscalculia.md). A [number](../../../number.md)-board game combines several cues—[counting](../../../counting.md) words, movement, spatial order and numerals—so improvement does not uniquely identify which cue was necessary. Transfer to the assessed numerical tasks is informative without proving a lasting effect on every school [arithmetic](../../../arithmetic-split.md) outcome. An extension should test classroom implementation and broader [learning](../../../learning.md) rather than equating a promising short intervention with complete remediation.

Practice can then be selected according to a learner's developing strategies. [Lynn S. Fuchs](../../../lynn-s-fuchs.md) and colleagues randomized at-risk first-graders to tutoring with speeded practice, tutoring with nonspeeded practice or no tutoring. Both tutoring conditions shared a substantial [number](../../../number.md)-knowledge component and differed in the final five minutes of each session. Sixteen weeks of tutoring improved mathematical outcomes, while the practice contrast provided additional evidence about efficient [counting](../../../counting.md) and [arithmetic](../../../arithmetic-split.md) [learning](../../../learning.md). Because most instruction was shared, the comparison does not test timed practice as a stand-alone replacement for teaching concepts.[2013; shared number-knowledge tutoring and randomized practice contrast](../../../effects-of-first-grade-number-knowledge-tutoring-with-contrasting-forms-of-practice-by-lynn-s-fuchs-and-others.md)

This design is useful because it distinguishes package benefit from a narrower component question. A teacher observing that children improve after frequent [arithmetic](../../../arithmetic-split.md) practice cannot tell whether time, feedback, explanation or practice format produced the gain. Randomizing a well-defined component within otherwise similar sessions provides a more interpretable answer. Yet a component can have different effects at different levels of prior knowledge. Testing those [interactions](../../../interaction-statistics.md) requires prespecified measures and sufficient [statistical power](../../../statistical-power.md), not selecting a subgroup only after the average result is disappointing.

Computer delivery can adapt task difficulty and record detailed responses, but an adaptive interface is not evidence of efficacy. [Pekka Räsänen](../../../pekka-rasanen.md) and colleagues randomized children with low [numeracy](../../../numeracy.md) between two computer games emphasizing different numerical representations. The study found improvements in [number](../../../number.md) comparison, with more limited [generalization](../../../generalization-in-statistical-learning.md) to other skills. It therefore supports asking which representation is trained and which outcome changes, rather than treating all improvement as a rise in general mathematical ability. The modest study and comparison structure also limit a universal ranking of software.[2009; randomized game comparison and outcome-specific transfer](../../../computer-assisted-intervention-for-children-with-low-numeracy-skills-by-pekka-rasanen-and-others.md)

A common alternative is to train a domain-general capacity thought to constrain mathematics. The crucial distinction is between association and intervention: [working memory](../../../working-memory.md) can predict performance without working-memory practice becoming an effective [arithmetic](../../../arithmetic-split.md) treatment. [Darren L. Dunning](../../../darren-l-dunning.md) and colleagues randomized children with low [working memory](../../../working-memory.md) to adaptive training, low-level practice or a comparison condition. Improvements in related memory performance did not establish a corresponding generalized academic benefit. The study illustrates why [near transfer](../../../near-transfer.md) should not be presented as demonstrated [far transfer](../../../far-transfer.md) to mathematics.[2013; randomized training comparison and limits of academic generalization](../../../does-working-memory-training-lead-to-generalized-improvements-in-children-with-low-working-memory-by-darren-l-dunning-and-others.md)

Intervention evaluation should include untaught examples, delayed tests and classroom-relevant performance. A program that only improves practiced items may still have value, but its claim is narrower than improving flexible calculation or word-problem understanding. Blinded assessors, comparable adult contact, monitoring of attendance and suitable analysis of class or school clustering strengthen inference. A no-treatment comparison estimates the package effect relative to ordinary opportunities; an active comparison is needed for stronger claims about distinctive instructional ingredients.

Researchers should also ask why an intervention fails. A child may not understand the [vocabulary](../../../vocabulary.md), may be overloaded by instructions or may have had too little opportunity to participate. Mathematical [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md) can affect engagement, but it should not become an unfalsifiable explanation for every weak response. Fidelity and acceptability matter alongside cognitive mechanism: an effective activity in a closely supported laboratory may be difficult to deliver in an ordinary classroom. Those constraints are part of the educational intervention, not distractions from its psychological theory.

The practical model is a cycle of assessment, targeted teaching and reassessment. Performance can determine the next task, while repeated review prevents a provisional description from becoming a permanent ceiling. A strong design measures proposed processes before outcomes, then tests whether the intervention changed the intended process and whether that change predicts relevant [learning](../../../learning.md). [Statistical mediation](../../../statistical-mediation.md) alone remains weaker than a selective manipulation of the mechanism, especially when motivation and extra teaching change together.

**The contribution of psychology is not one universal cure but a disciplined way to match an instructional problem to a testable account of [learning](../../../learning.md).** Targeted [number](../../../number.md) teaching has encouraging experimental support; the evidence also warns against mistaking commercial sophistication, trained-task improvement or a correlational cognitive predictor for demonstrated mathematical benefit. Evaluation should remain tied to the particular child population, content and transferable outcome claimed.
