# Supersolvable Lie algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Solvable Lie algebra](solvable-lie-algebra.md)

A finite-dimensional [Lie algebra](lie-algebra-split.md) is supersolvable if it has a full flag of [ideals of a Lie algebra](ideal-of-a-lie-algebra.md), increasing by one dimension at each step. It is solvable over every field: induct on dimension using the first one-dimensional ideal, which is abelian, and the quotient flag. Conversely over an [algebraically closed field](algebraically-closed-field.md) of characteristic zero, [Lie's theorem](lie-s-theorem.md) applied to the adjoint representation supplies a one-dimensional ideal in every nonzero [solvable Lie algebra](solvable-lie-algebra.md). Induct on the quotient and lift its flag. A full ideal flag in $L/I$ lifts across a one-dimensional ideal $I$ simply by taking inverse images and prepending zero. Thus solvability and supersolvability agree over the complex numbers, although they need not over an arbitrary field.
