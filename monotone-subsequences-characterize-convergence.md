# Monotone subsequences characterize convergence

↑ **Parent:** [Monotone subsequence theorem](monotone-subsequence-theorem.md)

For a [sequence](sequence.md) of [real numbers](real-number.md), **a common finite [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) for all [monotone subsequences](monotone-subsequence.md) forces the whole [sequence](sequence.md) to have that [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md).** There is no need to assume a [bounded sequence](bounded-sequence.md) if the hypothesis already includes convergence of every [monotone subsequence](monotone-subsequence.md).

If $a_n$ fails to tend to $L$, select a [subsequence](subsequence.md) with $|a_{n_j}-L|\geq\varepsilon$ for some fixed $\varepsilon>0$. The [monotone subsequence theorem](monotone-subsequence-theorem.md) gives a [monotone subsequence](monotone-subsequence.md) of this [subsequence](subsequence.md), which contradicts the common [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) $L$. Conversely, every [subsequence](subsequence.md) of a [convergent sequence](convergent-sequence.md) has the original [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md).

For a [bounded sequence](bounded-sequence.md), the [bounded monotone sequence theorem](bounded-monotone-sequence-theorem.md) already ensures that all [monotone subsequences](monotone-subsequence.md) converge. Only agreement of their [limits of sequences](limit-of-a-sequence.md) remains to be checked.
