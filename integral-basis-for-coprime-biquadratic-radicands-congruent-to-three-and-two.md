# Integral basis for coprime biquadratic radicands congruent to three and two

↑ **Parent:** [Integral basis](integral-basis.md)

Let $m,n$ be coprime [square-free integers](square-free-integer.md), $m\equiv3\pmod4$ and $n\equiv2\pmod4$. For the [biquadratic extension](biquadratic-extension.md) $L=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\beta)$, with $\alpha^2=m$ and $\beta^2=n$, an [integral basis](integral-basis.md) is $1,\alpha,\beta,(\beta+\alpha\beta)/2$. To prove spanning, write an [algebraic integer](algebraic-integer.md) as $(a+b\alpha+c\beta+d\alpha\beta)/2$. The [field traces](field-trace.md) to the three quadratic subfields and the [ring of integers of a quadratic field](ring-of-integers-of-a-quadratic-field.md) formula force $a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Z$. The [field norm](field-norm.md) to $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$ has coefficients $(a^2+mb^2-nc^2-mnd^2)/4$ and $(ab-ncd)/2$. Their integrality gives $ab\equiv0\pmod2$ and $a^2-b^2-2(c^2+d^2)\equiv0\pmod4$. Thus $a,b$ are even and $c\equiv d\pmod2$, exactly the asserted integer span. Conversely, the half-sum $w$ has $w^2=n(1+m)/4+(n/2)\alpha\in\mathbb Z[\alpha]$, so [integral dependence is transitive](integral-dependence-is-transitive.md) proves it is an [algebraic integer](algebraic-integer.md). Rational [linear independence](linear-independence.md) is immediate from its nonzero $\alpha\beta$ coefficient.
