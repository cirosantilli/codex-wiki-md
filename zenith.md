# Zenith

↑ **Parent:** [Horizontal coordinate system](horizontal-coordinate-system.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith)

The [zenith](zenith.md) is the point on the [celestial sphere](celestial-sphere.md) in the direction of the observer's local upward vertical. In the [horizontal coordinate system](horizontal-coordinate-system.md), its [unit vector](unit-vector.md) is $\mathbf e_U$ and its [altitude](altitude.md) is $90^\circ$. The [zenith](zenith.md) belongs to the observer's site; it is not generally the north [celestial pole](celestial-pole.md), whose [altitude](altitude.md) depends on [geographical latitude](geographical-latitude.md). A direction's [zenith distance](zenith-distance.md) is its nonnegative [angle](angle.md) from the [zenith](zenith.md), so $z=90^\circ-\gamma$. A direction of [altitude](altitude.md) $30^\circ$ therefore has [zenith distance](zenith-distance.md) $60^\circ$. [Jeremy Tatum: Celestial mechanics, section 6.2, zenith and complementary zenith distance](https://www.astro.uvic.ca/~tatum/celmechs/celm6.pdf).

At the [zenith](zenith.md), every value of [azimuth](azimuth.md) produces $(E,N,U)=(0,0,1)$. There is no horizontal component to identify a bearing: $\operatorname{atan2}(0,0)$ cannot provide a geometrically meaningful [azimuth](azimuth.md), even if software returns a number. This [coordinate singularity](coordinate-singularity.md) is a defect of the angular description; the viewing direction itself remains regular. The same ambiguity occurs at the opposite downward vertical. Near the [zenith](zenith.md), two directions at fixed [altitude](altitude.md) separated by a small [azimuth](azimuth.md) increment $d\eta$ have separation $\cos\gamma\,|d\eta|$ to first order, with [angles](angle.md) in [radians](radian.md). At [altitude](altitude.md) $89^\circ$, a $1^\circ$ [azimuth](azimuth.md) increment spans only about $0.01745^\circ$ on that small arc. An [optical telescope](optical-telescope.md) can consequently require a large bearing change for a small change of pointing near the [zenith](zenith.md); working with [unit vectors](unit-vector.md) avoids inventing a preferred bearing at the singular point.
