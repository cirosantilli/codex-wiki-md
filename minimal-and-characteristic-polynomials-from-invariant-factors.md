# Minimal and characteristic polynomials from invariant factors

↑ **Parent:** [Invariant factors of a linear operator](invariant-factors-of-a-linear-operator.md)

Suppose the [rational canonical form](rational-canonical-form.md) of $T$ is $C(f_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus C(f_r)$, with monic nonconstant [invariant factors of a linear operator](invariant-factors-of-a-linear-operator.md) satisfying $f_1\mid\cdots\mid f_r$. Each block has [minimal polynomial](minimal-polynomial.md) and [characteristic polynomial](characteristic-polynomial.md) $f_j$, because a [cyclic operator has equal minimal and characteristic polynomials](cyclic-operator-has-equal-minimal-and-characteristic-polynomials.md). The [determinant of a block diagonal matrix](determinant-of-a-block-diagonal-matrix.md) is the product of block determinants, giving $c_T=\prod_jf_j$. A [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) kills the direct sum exactly when it kills each block, equivalently when every $f_j$ divides it. Thus $m_T$ is their [least common multiple](least-common-multiple.md), which the divisibility chain reduces to $f_r$. On the zero vector space both polynomials are conventionally one.
