# Strictly distance-decreasing map

↑ **Parent:** [Nonexpansive mapping](nonexpansive-mapping.md)

A [strictly distance-decreasing map](strictly-distance-decreasing-map.md) decreases the distance between every pair of distinct points. Unlike a [contraction mapping](contraction-mapping.md), it need not have a single [Lipschitz constant](lipschitz-constant.md) below one. Two distinct [fixed points](fixed-point.md) would contradict strict decrease, so there is at most one. Existence can fail even on a [complete metric space](complete-metric-space.md): on $[0,\infty)$, $T(x)=x+1/(1+x)$ has no [fixed point](fixed-point.md), while the [mean value theorem](mean-value-theorem.md) and $0\le T'(x)<1$ show strict distance decrease.
