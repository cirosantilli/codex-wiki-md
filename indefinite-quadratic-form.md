# Indefinite quadratic form

↑ **Parent:** [Quadratic form](quadratic-form-split.md)

A real [quadratic form](quadratic-form-split.md) is indefinite when it takes both positive and negative values. For a real [symmetric matrix](symmetric-matrix.md) representing it, this means that its [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) include both signs. For example, $q(x,y)=x^2-y^2$ has $q(1,0)=1$ and $q(0,1)=-1$; a zero value at the nonzero [vector](vector.md) $(1,1)$ does not imply [positive semidefiniteness](positive-semidefinite-matrix.md).[Definition 6.21 and footnote 6, p. 40: definiteness and a nondegenerate indefinite example](bilinear-forms-by-keith-conrad.md)
