# Lax-Milgram theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Functional analysis](functional-analysis-split.md)

The theorem supplies solvability for a coercive [weak formulation](weak-formulation.md).

A [bounded bilinear form](bounded-bilinear-form.md) $a$ on a real [Hilbert space](hilbert-space-split.md) $H$ that is a [coercive bilinear form](coercive-bilinear-form.md) represents every bounded [linear functional](linear-functional.md) uniquely: for each $\ell\in H'$, there is a unique $u\in H$ with $a(u,v)=\ell(v)$ for every $v\in H$.

For a proof, the [Riesz representation theorem](riesz-representation-theorem.md) gives a [bounded linear operator](continuous-linear-operator.md) $T$ and a [vector](vector.md) $g$ with $a(u,v)=\langle Tu,v\rangle$ and $\ell(v)=\langle g,v\rangle$. Coercivity and the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) give $\|Tu\|\geq\alpha\|u\|$, so $T$ is injective and its image is closed: convergence of $Tu_n$ makes $u_n$ a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md), whose limit supplies the image limit. If $v$ belongs to the [orthogonal complement](orthogonal-complement.md) of the image, then $a(u,v)=0$ for every $u$; setting $u=v$ and using coercivity forces $v=0$. Thus the image is also dense and equals $H$. There is consequently a unique $u$ with $Tu=g$, and $\|u\|\leq\|\ell\|/\alpha$. The complex version uses a bounded sesquilinear form with $\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)\geq\alpha\|u\|^2$.
