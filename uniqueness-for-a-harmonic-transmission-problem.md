# Uniqueness for a harmonic transmission problem

↑ **Parent:** [Dirichlet uniqueness for the Poisson equation](dirichlet-uniqueness-for-the-poisson-equation.md)

Let a bounded connected body consist of two regions with positive constant [thermal conductivities](thermal-conductivity.md) $k_1,k_2$. A temperature is [harmonic](harmonic-function.md) in each region, continuous across their interface, has continuous normal heat flux there, and has prescribed outer [Dirichlet boundary conditions](dirichlet-boundary-condition.md). There is at most one sufficiently regular solution.

For the difference $w$ of two solutions, apply [Green's first identity](green-s-first-identity.md) in each region and multiply by its [thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md). The interface outward normals are opposite, so continuity of $w$ and $k\partial_n w$ cancels their boundary terms. The outer term vanishes because $w=0$. Hence the displayed sum of nonnegative [Dirichlet energies](dirichlet-energy.md) is zero. The [gradient](gradient.md) vanishes in both regions; continuity and the outer data then force $w=0$. The proof also applies to finitely many connected subregions, provided the union is connected and the same transmission conditions hold.
