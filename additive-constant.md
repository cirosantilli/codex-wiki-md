# Additive constant

↑ **Parent:** [Antiderivative](antiderivative.md)

A constant added to a [function](function-split.md) changes its reference level without changing its [derivative](derivative.md):

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\bigl(F(x)+C\bigr)=F'(x).
$$

Geometrically, it is a vertical [translation](translation-geometry.md) of the graph. For $F(x)=x^3/3$, changing $C$ moves every plotted value by the same amount while preserving every tangent slope. This is the geometric reason that [antiderivatives](antiderivative.md) form a family.

When $F$ represents a [physical quantity](physical-quantity.md), the [physical dimension](physical-dimension.md) of $C$ must match that of $F$. When [position](position.md) is obtained from [velocity](velocity.md), $C$ is a position offset. An unknown starting position remains unknown even when the rate of change is known exactly.

When describing the family of [antiderivatives](antiderivative.md) on an [interval](interval-mathematics.md), the arbitrary additive constant is the [constant of integration](constant-of-integration.md). On a disconnected open domain, the corresponding freedom is a [locally constant function](locally-constant-function.md), as in [antiderivatives on disconnected domains](antiderivatives-on-disconnected-domains.md), rather than necessarily one constant on the whole domain.
