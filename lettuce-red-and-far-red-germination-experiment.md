# Lettuce red and far-red germination experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Germination](germination.md)

The 1952 experiment used Grand Rapids [lettuce](lettuce.md) [seeds](seed.md) on wetted blotters in covered dishes. After 16 hours in darkness, seven lots of 200 received successive alternating exposures: one minute of red [light](light.md) at $580$–$680\,\mathrm{nm}$, then four minutes above $700\,\mathrm{nm}$, with one lot removed after each exposure. Treated lots and a dark [scientific control](scientific-control.md) remained at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$ for two days; germinated [seeds](seed.md) were counted. Separate wavelength tests used a two-glass-prism [spectrograph](spectrograph.md).[A Reversible Photoreaction Controlling Seed Germination, pp. 662–664: preparation, spectrograph and alternating-exposure protocol](a-reversible-photoreaction-controlling-seed-germination-by-harry-alfred-borthwick-and-others.md)

Table 1 shows alternating promotion and suppression according to the final exposure. Red [light](light.md) could restore promotion after far-red [light](light.md); suppression was incomplete. This reversibility supports a reversible sensing state rather than simple cumulative illumination. The paper did not isolate or identify the pigment; the response concerns this [lettuce](lettuce.md) preparation and timing. Its readout was a two-day [germination](germination.md) count, not subsequent [seedling](seedling.md) performance.[A Reversible Photoreaction Controlling Seed Germination, p. 663 pigment-identification limitation and p. 665 Table 1](a-reversible-photoreaction-controlling-seed-germination-by-harry-alfred-borthwick-and-others.md)

The modern [phytochrome](phytochrome.md) description distinguishes forms $P_r$ and $P_{fr}$ that interconvert under red and far-red [light](light.md). This provides a concrete example of [signal transduction](signal-transduction-split.md): [light](light.md) changes a sensing state that affects development. The response should be distinguished from [photosynthesis](photosynthesis.md), which supplies chemical resources.[Biology 2e, §30.6 The Phytochrome System and the Red/Far-Red Response, Figure 30.38: interconversion and cellular response](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others.md)
