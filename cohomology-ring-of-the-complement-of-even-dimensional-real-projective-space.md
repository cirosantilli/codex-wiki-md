# Cohomology ring of the complement of even-dimensional real projective space

↑ **Parent:** [Mod-two restriction from complex to real projective space](mod-two-restriction-from-complex-to-real-projective-space.md)

For $k\geq1$, put $U=\mathbb{CP}^{2k}\setminus\mathbb{RP}^{2k}$. Its [cohomology ring](cohomology-ring.md) is

$$
H^*(U;\mathbb F_2)\cong\mathbb F_2[x,y]/(x^k,y^2),\qquad |x|=2,\quad |y|=2k.
$$

Here $x$ is the restricted hyperplane class. The [cohomological Gysin map of an embedding](cohomological-gysin-map-of-an-embedding.md) sends $a^{2j}$ to $h^{k+j}$ and every odd-degree class to zero, by [Poincare duality](poincare-duality.md) and [mod-two restriction from complex to real projective space](mod-two-restriction-from-complex-to-real-projective-space.md). Its exact sequence gives one-dimensional groups in even degrees $0,2,\ldots,4k-2$, and zero otherwise. Choose $y$ with $\delta y=a$. The connecting-map module identity gives $\delta(x^j y)=a^{2j+1}$ for $0\leq j<k$, so these are the upper-half generators. The lower-half generators are $x^j$. The relations $x^k=0$ and $y^2=0$ follow respectively from $i_!1=h^k$ and $H^{4k}(U;\mathbb F_2)=0$. The result is a ring isomorphism with [Complex projective space](complex-projective-space.md) $\mathbb{CP}^{k-1}$ times $S^{2k}$; it does not by itself assert a [homotopy equivalence](homotopy-equivalence.md) of the spaces.
