# Integer congruence

↑ **Parent:** [Modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic.md)

For a positive modulus $m$, integers $a,b$ are congruent when their difference is divisible by $m$. This is an [equivalence relation](equivalence-relation.md): zero is divisible by $m$, changing sign preserves [divisibility](divisibility.md), and adding two divisible differences proves transitivity. Addition and multiplication respect it, because $(a+c)-(b+d)=(a-b)+(c-d)$ and $ac-bd=c(a-b)+b(c-d)$. Its [equivalence classes](equivalence-class.md) are the residue classes used in [modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic.md).
