# Strict decrease of best polynomial approximation under a derivative sign

↑ **Parent:** [Equioscillation theorem](equioscillation-theorem.md)

For real $f\in C^{n+1}[0,1]$ with positive $(n+1)$st [derivative](derivative.md), equality of consecutive [best uniform approximation](best-uniform-approximation.md) errors would make a best degree-at-most-$n$ [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) also best at degree $n+1$. The [Chebyshev alternation theorem](equioscillation-theorem.md) then supplies $n+3$ alternating extrema of its nonzero error, hence $n+2$ distinct zeros. Repeated [Rolle's theorem](rolle-theorem.md) forces a zero of the $(n+1)$st derivative of that error, contradicting the assumed sign. The same conclusion holds for a strictly negative derivative by replacing $f$ with $-f$.
