# Atomic nucleus

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear physics](nuclear-physics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus)

The central system of [protons](proton.md) and [neutrons](neutron-split.md) in an atom. Its mass number $A$ is its [baryon number](baryon-number.md) in ordinary nuclear states; $Z$ is the proton number and $N$ the neutron number.

The [nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md) contains almost all of an atom's [mass](mass.md) and carries positive [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $Ze$, where $e$ is the [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md). The [atomic number](atomic-number.md) $Z$ identifies the [chemical element](chemical-element.md); changing $N$ at fixed $Z$ gives another [isotope](isotope.md). The [mass number](mass-number.md) $A=Z+N$ counts [nucleons](nucleon.md), rather than specifying the exact nuclear [mass](mass.md): [nuclear binding energy](nuclear-binding-energy.md) lowers the [mass](mass.md) of a bound system relative to its separated constituents. The simplest [nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md) is a single [proton](proton.md); other [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md) can contain many [protons](proton.md) and [neutrons](neutron-split.md) bound by the [nuclear force](nuclear-force-split.md).

A nuclear [quantum state](quantum-state.md) has properties such as [spin](spin.md) and a spatial [nuclear charge distribution](nuclear-charge-distribution.md). These are different questions from merely counting [nucleons](nucleon.md). A small-angle [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md) measurement can reveal a [nuclear charge radius](nuclear-charge-radius.md), while a wider range of [momentum transfer](momentum-transfer.md) tests the distribution's shape. The [Hofstadter nuclear electron scattering experiment](hofstadter-nuclear-electron-scattering-experiment.md) made this connection between scattering patterns and nuclear structure experimentally concrete.
