# Triangle-removal proof of Roth theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Roth theorem on three-term arithmetic progressions](roth-theorem-on-three-term-arithmetic-progressions.md)

Embed $A\subseteq[N]$ in the odd [cyclic group](cyclic-group.md) $G=\mathbb Z/(2N+1)\mathbb Z$, so additive three-term relations in $A$ cannot wrap around. Form three copies of $G$, putting [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) $x\!\sim\!y$ when $y-x\in A$, $y\!\sim\!z$ when $z-y\in A$, and $x\!\sim\!z$ when $(z-x)/2\in A$. A [triangle in a graph](triangle-in-a-graph.md) encodes $a+b=2c$ in $A$. If $A$ has no nonconstant [arithmetic progression](arithmetic-progression.md), every [triangle in a graph](triangle-in-a-graph.md) has $a=b=c$; there are exactly $|G||A|$ [triangles in a graph](triangle-in-a-graph.md), and they are edge-disjoint. Thus at least $|G||A|$ [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) must be removed to eliminate them. For fixed positive [subset density](density-of-a-finite-subset.md) of $A$, this is a positive fraction of the squared [vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) count, whereas the [triangle in a graph](triangle-in-a-graph.md) count is only quadratic. The [triangle removal lemma](triangle-removal-lemma.md) permits deletion of an arbitrarily small quadratic fraction when the cubic [triangle in a graph](triangle-in-a-graph.md) [subset density](density-of-a-finite-subset.md) is sufficiently small. This contradiction establishes [Roth theorem on three-term arithmetic progressions](roth-theorem-on-three-term-arithmetic-progressions.md).
