# Energy eigenstate

↑ **Parent:** [Energy eigenvalue](energy-eigenvalue.md)

An [energy eigenstate](energy-eigenstate.md) is a [quantum state](quantum-state.md) represented by an [eigenvector](eigenvector.md) of the [Quantum Hamiltonian](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md). For a time-independent [Quantum Hamiltonian](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md), a normalized [eigenfunction](eigenfunction.md) $\phi$ with [energy eigenvalue](energy-eigenvalue.md) $E$ evolves according to the [Schrödinger equation](schrodinger-equation.md) as

$$
\psi(x,t)=\phi(x)e^{-iEt/\hbar},\qquad |\psi(x,t)|^2=|\phi(x)|^2.
$$

Here $\hbar=h/(2\pi)$ is the reduced [Planck constant](planck-constant.md). The changing factor is a [global phase](global-phase.md), so this is a [stationary state](stationary-state.md): its position [probability density](probability-density.md) is constant. For example, the second [infinite square well](infinite-square-well.md) mode has two density lobes separated by a [node of a standing wave](node-physics.md) at $x=L/2$; both lobes stay fixed while its [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md) rotates. An ideal [energy measurement](energy-measurement.md) returns $E$ with probability one, but a [position measurement](position-measurement.md) still has a distribution. Stationary density does not describe a particle sitting at the peak of a lobe. Distinct [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue.md) accumulate different [quantum phases](quantum-phase.md), making their [quantum superposition](quantum-superposition.md) behave differently.
