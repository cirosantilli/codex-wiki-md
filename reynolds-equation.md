# Reynolds equation

↑ **Parent:** [Moving-boundary lubrication flux](moving-boundary-lubrication-flux.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reynolds_equation)

Incompressible mass conservation in a moving gap gives

$$
\partial_t h+\partial_xQ=0.
$$

Combined with the local Couette-Poiseuille flux, this is the Reynolds lubrication equation.

For a thin gap $h$ with tangential wall velocities $\mathbf v_0,\mathbf v_h$, [lubrication theory](lubrication-theory.md) and the [no-slip boundary condition](no-slip-boundary-condition.md) give the integrated [volume flux](volumetric-flow-rate.md)

$$
\mathbf q=-\frac{h^3}{12\mu}\nabla_\parallel p+\frac h2(\mathbf v_0+\mathbf v_h).
$$

[Conservation of mass](mass-conservation.md) gives $h_t+\nabla_\parallel\cdot\mathbf q=0$. This is the Reynolds equation; it determines the [lubrication pressure](lubrication-pressure.md) from wall motion and gap geometry.
