# Step-index optical fiber

↑ **Parent:** [Optical fiber](optical-fiber.md)

A [step-index optical fiber](step-index-optical-fiber.md) has a nearly uniform [refractive index](refractive-index.md) $n_1$ throughout its [optical fiber core](optical-fiber-core.md), with an abrupt decrease to $n_2<n_1$ in its [optical fiber cladding](optical-fiber-cladding.md). In a straight, ideal guide, [meridional rays](meridional-ray.md) stay in a plane containing the [optical axis](optical-axis.md) and trace a zigzag through repeated [total internal reflection](total-internal-reflection.md). This is a [geometrical optics](geometrical-optics-split.md) approximation; it is particularly useful for a [multimode optical fiber](multimode-optical-fiber.md) whose core diameter is large compared with the [wavelength](wavelength.md).

**[Figure 11](#image-launch-angle-to-the-axis-and-incidence-angle-to-the-core-cladding-normal). Launch angle to the axis and incidence angle to the core–cladding normal**.

The upper panel shows an accepted [meridional ray](meridional-ray.md); the lower panel enlarges the limiting geometry. The [angle of incidence](angle-of-incidence.md) $i$ at the side wall is measured from its [normal vector](normal-vector.md), whereas the internal ray [angle](angle.md) $\phi$ and external launch [angle](angle.md) $\theta$ are measured from the [optical axis](optical-axis.md). Thus $i+\phi=90^\circ$. The limiting ray is a boundary of the ideal model, rather than a complete [normal mode](normal-mode.md) solution.
