# Multiplicative group of a field

↑ **Parent:** [Field](field.md)

For a [field](field.md) $K$, the nonzero elements form an [abelian group](abelian-group.md) under multiplication, called its [multiplicative group](multiplicative-group-of-a-field.md). Products of nonzero elements are nonzero because a [field](field.md) has no nonzero [zero divisors](zero-divisor.md); multiplication is associative and commutative, the identity is $1$, and every nonzero element has an inverse. Thus every finite [subgroup](subgroup.md) of $K^\times$ is a [finite abelian group](finite-abelian-group.md). The [finite subgroup of a field multiplicative group is cyclic](finite-subgroup-of-a-field-multiplicative-group-is-cyclic.md) theorem uses the [root bound for a polynomial](lagrange-root-bound-over-a-field.md) to prove a stronger restriction on such subgroups.
