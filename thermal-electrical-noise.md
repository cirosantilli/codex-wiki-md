# Thermal electrical noise

↑ **Parent:** [Noise (signal processing)](noise-signal-processing.md)

[Thermal electrical noise](thermal-electrical-noise.md) is the fluctuating [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) or [electric current](electric-current.md) produced by [thermal fluctuations](thermal-fluctuation.md) in a dissipative [electrical circuit](electrical-circuit-split.md), even without an applied voltage. A [resistor](resistor.md) at [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md) is both a source of [noise](noise-signal-processing.md) and a sink for electrical [energy](energy-split.md). Its [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) describes the dissipation, and its absolute [temperature](temperature-split.md) sets the scale of the fluctuations. This connects a practical limit on [signal-to-noise ratio](signal-to-noise-ratio.md) to [statistical mechanics](statistical-mechanics.md): a warm, unbiased [resistor](resistor.md) is not electrically silent.

[John Bertrand Johnson](john-bertrand-johnson.md) measured the effect using a vacuum-tube [amplifier](amplifier.md) and thermocouple; his 1928 paper reported agreement with the [Boltzmann constant](boltzmann-constant.md). [Harry Nyquist](harry-nyquist.md)'s companion paper calculated the effect from [thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md) and [statistical mechanics](statistical-mechanics.md).[Johnson, Thermal Agitation of Electricity in Conductors, Physical Review 32, 97–109 (1928), abstract: amplifier and thermocouple measurement](thermal-agitation-of-electricity-in-conductors-by-john-bertrand-johnson.md)[Nyquist, Thermal Agitation of Electric Charge in Conductors, Physical Review 32, 110–113 (1928), abstract: thermodynamic and statistical calculation](thermal-agitation-of-electric-charge-in-conductors-by-harry-nyquist.md)
