# Two-region domination for differentiation of the gamma integral

↑ **Parent:** [Derivative of the gamma function](derivative-of-the-gamma-function.md)

For $t$ in a compact positive interval $[a,b]$, split the integration region at one. Near zero the smallest exponent gives the displayed bound, whose integral is $1/a^2$. Above one the largest exponent gives the bound, integrable by [exponential decay](exponential-decay.md). A single [integrable function](lebesgue-integrable-function.md) therefore dominates all parameter derivatives locally. [Dominated differentiation under the integral sign](dominated-differentiation-under-the-integral-sign.md) gives $\Gamma'(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-x}x^{t-1}\log x\,dx$. The same argument with higher powers of $|\log x|$ gives all higher derivatives.
