# Convex combination

↑ **Parent:** [Convex set](convex-set-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_combination)

A convex combination is a finite weighted average of points in a real [vector space](vector-space-split.md). For $n\geq1$, it has the form

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i x_i,
\qquad \lambda_i\geq0,
\qquad \sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i=1.
$$

The nonnegative [coefficients](coefficient.md) forbid subtracting a component, and their unit sum keeps the result on the same affine scale as the inputs. With two points it parametrizes their [line segment](line-segment.md); equal [coefficients](coefficient.md) give their [centroid](centroid.md). A [convex set](convex-set-split.md) contains every finite convex combination of its points. Conversely, the two-point case already gives the defining property of a [convex set](convex-set-split.md).[§2.1.4, p. 24: finite combinations, weighted-average interpretation and convex hull](convex-optimization-by-stephen-boyd-and-lieven-vandenberghe.md)

The distinction from a [linear combination](linear-combination.md) is concrete. Combining $(0,0)$ and $(2,0)$ with [coefficients](coefficient.md) $-1$ and $2$ gives $(4,0)$, outside their [line segment](line-segment.md), despite the unit coefficient sum. Using two [coefficients](coefficient.md) both equal to one gives $(2,0)$ here, but their sum is two, so it is not a convex combination with those [coefficients](coefficient.md). A numerical output may happen to coincide with a point admitting a different convex representation; the definition applies to the specified [coefficients](coefficient.md).
