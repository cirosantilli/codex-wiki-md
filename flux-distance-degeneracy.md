# Flux-distance degeneracy

↑ **Parent:** [Observed radiative flux](observed-radiative-flux.md)

One [observed radiative flux](observed-radiative-flux.md) cannot determine both [luminosity](luminosity.md) and [Euclidean distance](euclidean-distance.md): for every $k>0$, replacing $(L,d)$ by $(k^2L,kd)$ leaves $F$ unchanged. In particular, $4\times10^{26}\,\mathrm W$ at $40$ [parsecs](parsec.md) produces the same [observed radiative flux](observed-radiative-flux.md) as $10^{26}\,\mathrm W$ at $20$ [parsecs](parsec.md). This is a concrete failure of [identifiability](identifiability.md) in an [inverse problem](inverse-problem-split.md).

An independent [stellar parallax](stellar-parallax.md) fixes the distance and breaks this degeneracy; alternatively, a calibrated [standard candle](standard-candle.md) supplies the [luminosity](luminosity.md). [Interstellar extinction](interstellar-extinction.md) can further reduce the [observed radiative flux](observed-radiative-flux.md), and a [photometric passband](photometric-passband.md) measures only a selected spectral range. At cosmological distances, use [luminosity distance](luminosity-distance.md) in the bolometric relation. Each inference must specify which [radiative flux](radiative-flux.md) was measured and what propagation assumptions connect it to [luminosity](luminosity.md).
