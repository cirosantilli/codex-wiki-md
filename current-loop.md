# Current loop

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md)

A [current loop](current-loop.md) is a closed path carrying [electric current](electric-current.md). For a thin wire following a closed [curve](curve.md) $C$, the [volume integral](volume-integral.md) defining its [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) becomes a [line integral](line-integral.md):

$$
\mathbf m=\frac I2\oint_C\mathbf r\times d\mathbf r=I\mathbf A,
\qquad
\mathbf A=\frac12\oint_C\mathbf r\times d\mathbf r.
$$

The [vector area](vector-area.md) $\mathbf A$ is $A\widehat{\mathbf n}$ for a planar [current loop](current-loop.md). For a nonplanar [current loop](current-loop.md), use the [vector area](vector-area.md), rather than the scalar [area](surface-area.md) of an arbitrarily chosen spanning surface. Reversing the [electric current](electric-current.md) reverses $\mathbf m$ without changing the geometry. [Localized Current Distribution](classical-electromagnetism-by-richard-fitzpatrick/localized-current-distribution.md).

![](current-loop-moment-right-hand-rule.png)

**[Figure 14](#image-current-direction-and-the-right-hand-rule-for-a-planar-magnetic-dipole-moment). Current direction and the right-hand rule for a planar magnetic dipole moment**. Viewed face-on, counterclockwise [conventional current](conventional-current.md) gives a [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) out of the page, shown by a dot; reversing the [electric current](electric-current.md) gives a [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) into the page, shown by a cross. The curled fingers follow the [electric current](electric-current.md) and the thumb follows the unit [normal vector](normal-vector.md).

For $N$ closely spaced identical turns carrying the same [electric current](electric-current.md), the [magnetic dipole moments](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) add: $\mathbf m=NIA\widehat{\mathbf n}$. Each turn contributes its own [vector area](vector-area.md); unequal areas or differently oriented turns require a [vector](vector.md) sum. As a calculated example, twelve circular turns of [radius](radius.md) $R=3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carrying $I=0.40\,\mathrm A$ give $m=NI\pi R^2=1.36\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{A\,m^2}$. This is an idealized example, not an experimental measurement.
