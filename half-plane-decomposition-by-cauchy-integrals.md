# Half-plane decomposition by Cauchy integrals

↑ **Parent:** [Cauchy integral formula](cauchy-integral-formula.md)

Let $g$ be a [meromorphic function](meromorphic-function.md) with simple poles $\alpha_+$ above $c$ and $\alpha_-$ below $-c$, and $zg(z)\to0$ uniformly at infinity in $\operatorname{Im}z\ge-c$. The left-to-right line integrals $H(\lambda)=(2\pi i)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R-ic}g(z)/(z-\lambda)\,dz$ and $J(\lambda)=-(2\pi i)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R+ic}g(z)/(z-\lambda)\,dz$ satisfy $H=g-r_+/(\lambda-\alpha_+)$ above $-c$ and $J=r_+/(\lambda-\alpha_+)$ below $c$, where $r_+$ is the upper [residue](residue.md). Upper semicircle closure proves both formulas by the [residue theorem](residue-theorem.md); subtracting the principal part makes $H$ [holomorphic](complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) at $\alpha_+$ too. Consequently $H+J=g$ on the intervening strip. No lower-half-plane decay is needed.
