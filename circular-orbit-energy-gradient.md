# Circular-orbit energy gradient

↑ **Parent:** [Circular orbit](circular-orbit.md)

In a spherically symmetric [gravitational potential](newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md) $\Phi(r)$, a [circular orbit](circular-orbit.md) has $r\Omega^2=\Phi'(r)$, [specific orbital energy](specific-orbital-energy.md) $\varepsilon=\Phi+r^2\Omega^2/2$ and [specific angular momentum](specific-angular-momentum.md) $\mathbf h=r^2\Omega\mathbf n$, with $|\mathbf n|=1$. Differentiating gives $\varepsilon'=2r\Omega^2+r^2\Omega\Omega'=\Omega(r^2\Omega)'$. Changes in orbital orientation have $\mathbf n\cdot d\mathbf n=0$, so $\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot d\mathbf h=\Omega\,d(r^2\Omega)=d\varepsilon$. For a particle of variable [mass](mass.md) $M$ and total [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) $\mathbf H=M\mathbf h$, this implies $d(M\varepsilon)=(\varepsilon-\Omega h)dM+\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot d\mathbf H$. Moreover $d(\varepsilon-\Omega h)/dr=-h\Omega'$, which is positive when the orbital [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) decreases outward.
