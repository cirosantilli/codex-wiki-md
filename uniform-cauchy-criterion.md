# Uniform Cauchy criterion

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform convergence](uniform-convergence.md)

A [sequence of functions](sequence-of-functions.md) $f_n:E\to\mathbb R$ has a [uniform limit](uniform-limit.md) if and only if, for every $\varepsilon>0$, there is $N$ such that $|f_n(x)-f_m(x)|<\varepsilon$ for every $m,n\geq N$ and every $x\in E$. Necessity follows from the [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md). For sufficiency, [completeness of the real numbers](completeness-of-the-real-numbers.md) first gives a [pointwise limit](pointwise-limit.md) $f$. Letting $m\to\infty$ in the bound with tolerance $\varepsilon/2$ gives $|f_n(x)-f(x)|\leq\varepsilon/2$ uniformly in $x$. The same proof works for a [complete metric space](complete-metric-space.md) as codomain; completeness is essential.
