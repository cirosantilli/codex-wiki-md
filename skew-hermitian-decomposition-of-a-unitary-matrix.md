# Skew-Hermitian decomposition of a unitary matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Unitary matrix](unitary-matrix.md)

The displayed parts of a [unitary matrix](unitary-matrix.md) are [skew-Hermitian matrices](skew-hermitian-matrix.md). Expanding $UU^\dagger=U^\dagger U=I$ gives $AB=BA$ and $A^2+B^2=-I$. They admit a common [orthonormal eigenbasis](orthonormal-eigenbasis.md). For a unitary [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) $u$, the corresponding part [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) are $i\operatorname{Im}u$ and $-i\operatorname{Re}u$. If $U^T=-U$, then $U^\dagger=-\overline U$, so these are exactly its real and imaginary parts and both are real [skew-symmetric matrices](skew-symmetric-matrix.md). Such an invertible antisymmetric matrix has even dimension, by its [determinant](determinant.md). If one part has distinct [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md), commutation alone shows its one-dimensional [eigenspaces](eigenspace.md) are preserved by the other part.
