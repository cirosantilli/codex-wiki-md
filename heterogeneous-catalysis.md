# Heterogeneous catalysis

↑ **Parent:** [Catalysis](catalysis.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterogeneous_catalysis)

In [heterogeneous catalysis](heterogeneous-catalysis.md), the [catalyst](catalyst.md) and [reactants](reactant.md) occupy different [thermodynamic phases](thermodynamic-phase.md); a solid surface can furnish the catalytic route.

For example, specify dissolved [hydrogen peroxide](hydrogen-peroxide.md) as the [reactant](reactant.md), [water](water.md) as the [solvent](solvent.md), and [solid](solid.md) [manganese dioxide](manganese-dioxide.md) as the [catalyst](catalyst.md). The [solid](solid.md) and liquid [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) are separate [thermodynamic phases](thermodynamic-phase.md). A dispersion that looks thoroughly mixed still contains those separate phases; mixing distributes contact, rather than making the [solid](solid.md) molecularly dissolved. The [manganese dioxide peroxide contact demonstration](manganese-dioxide-peroxide-contact-demonstration.md) makes the arrival of the liquid at the [solid](solid.md) tangible.

A useful surface-route picture is approach from the liquid, [adsorption](adsorption.md) at a [catalytic surface site](catalytic-surface-site.md), conversion through surface-bound [reaction intermediates](reaction-intermediate.md), and release of [reaction products](product-chemistry.md). [Chemistry 2e by Paul Flowers and others; §12.7, Heterogeneous Catalysts, steps 1–4: adsorption, activation, reaction and product desorption](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/12-7-catalysis.md) This sequence organizes possible roles; it is not a verified sequence of [elementary reactions](elementary-reaction.md) for every [catalyst](catalyst.md), or for the particular [manganese dioxide](manganese-dioxide.md) demonstration. The net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) supplies neither the binding geometry nor the identity of the working [catalytic surface site](catalytic-surface-site.md).

This contact requirement makes [surface area](surface-area.md) and [diffusion](diffusion.md) practically important. More exposed [solid](solid.md) can provide more [catalytic surface sites](catalytic-surface-site.md), whereas [diffusion](diffusion.md) through the surrounding liquid can limit their supply of [reactants](reactant.md). Changing stirring or particle size can therefore change the observed [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) without identifying the microscopic [rate constant](rate-constant.md). A recoverable [solid](solid.md) is attractive for reuse, but separation alone does not establish retained activity: compare with [catalyst deactivation](catalyst-deactivation.md).
