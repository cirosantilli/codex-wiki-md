# Oscillator energy dissipation

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous damping](viscous-damping.md)

For the [damped harmonic oscillator](damped-harmonic-oscillator.md), the [mechanical energy](mechanical-energy.md) is the sum of [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and [spring](spring.md) [potential energy](potential-energy.md):

$$
E=\frac12m\dot x^2+\frac12kx^2,\qquad
\frac{dE}{dt}=\dot x(m\ddot x+kx)=-b\dot x^2\leq0.
$$

Thus $b\dot x^2$ is the [power](power.md) transferred out of the oscillator's [mechanical energy](mechanical-energy.md). For $b>0$, the rate vanishes instantaneously at a turning point, despite nonzero stored [potential energy](potential-energy.md); it is not a constant loss per unit [time](time-in-physics.md). In [eddy-current damping](eddy-current-damping.md) the loss becomes [Joule heating](joule-heating.md). [Damped Harmonic Oscillation](oscillations-and-waves-by-richard-fitzpatrick/damped-harmonic-oscillation.md)

For an [underdamped](underdamping.md) response, advancing one [oscillation period](period-of-an-oscillation.md) multiplies both [displacement](displacement.md) and [velocity](velocity.md) by $e^{-\zeta\omega_0T_{\mathrm d}}$, so $E(t+T_{\mathrm d})=e^{-2\zeta\omega_0T_{\mathrm d}}E(t)$. This exact cycle relation does not imply that instantaneous [energy](energy.md) is a pure [exponential function](exponential-function.md) throughout each cycle.
