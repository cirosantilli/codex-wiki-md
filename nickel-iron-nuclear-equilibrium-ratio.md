<h1 id="nickel-iron-nuclear-equilibrium-ratio">Nickel–iron nuclear equilibrium ratio</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear statistical equilibrium](nuclear-statistical-equilibrium.md)

Nickel-56 and iron-54 have the same [neutron](neutron.md) number and differ by two [protons](proton.md). Dividing their [nuclear statistical equilibrium](nuclear-statistical-equilibrium.md) abundance formulas cancels the [neutron](neutron.md) density and leaves the displayed ratio, with $\Delta B=B_{56}-B_{54}$ and $K$ containing temperature-independent statistical and [mass](mass.md) factors in the simplified model. For material with equal total [proton](proton.md) and [neutron](neutron.md) numbers and only these nuclei plus free [protons](proton.md), charge balance gives $n_p=2n_{54}$. Thus $n_{56}/n_{54}^3\propto T^{-3}e^{\Delta B/(kT)}$. This has a finite [temperature](temperature.md) maximum only if the signed energy difference is negative; conventional positive total nuclear binding energies for these two actual isotopes instead give a positive difference.
