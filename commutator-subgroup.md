# Commutator subgroup

↑ **Parent:** [Group theory](group-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutator_subgroup)

The commutator subgroup of a [group](group-split.md) $G$ is the [subgroup](subgroup.md) generated by all its [group commutators](group-commutator.md):

$$
G'=[G,G]=\langle x^{-1}y^{-1}xy:x,y\in G\rangle.
$$

Here and throughout, $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$. A [group commutator](group-commutator.md) is the [identity element](identity-element.md) precisely when $x$ and $y$ commute. The generated [subgroup](subgroup.md) includes finite products of [group commutators](group-commutator.md) and their [inverse elements](inverse-element.md); an element of $G'$ need not itself be a single [group commutator](group-commutator.md). Thus $G'=\{e\}$ precisely when $G$ is an [abelian group](abelian-group.md).

The useful idea is to record exactly what must disappear to make multiplication commutative. The identity $xy=yx[x,y]$ shows how a [group commutator](group-commutator.md) corrects a change in multiplication order. Setting every such correction equal to $e$ produces the [abelianization](abelianization.md) $G/G'$, while preserving every piece of information that a [group homomorphism](group-homomorphism.md) to an [abelian group](abelian-group.md) can detect. This connects a local comparison of two operations to a canonical [quotient group](quotient-group.md) of the whole [group](group-split.md).

The commutator subgroup is a [characteristic subgroup](characteristic-subgroup.md), hence a [normal subgroup](normal-subgroup.md): a [group automorphism](group-automorphism.md) $\alpha$ sends $[x,y]$ to $[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)]$. For any [normal subgroup](normal-subgroup.md) $N$,

$$
G/N\text{ is abelian}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G'\subseteq N.
$$

Indeed, the [group commutator](group-commutator.md) of $xN$ and $yN$ is $[x,y]N$. This makes $G'$ the smallest [normal subgroup](normal-subgroup.md) whose [quotient group](quotient-group.md) is an [abelian group](abelian-group.md). See [Conrad, Subgroup series I, section 4: commutators and abelian quotients](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/grouptheory/subgpseries1.pdf).
