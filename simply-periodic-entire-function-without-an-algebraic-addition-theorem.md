# Simply periodic entire function without an algebraic addition theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Algebraic addition theorem](algebraic-addition-theorem.md)

The displayed [entire function](entire-function.md) has exactly the periods $2\pi i\mathbb Z$: a period $c$ would make $e^z(e^c-1)$ a constant integer multiple of $2\pi i$, forcing $e^c=1$. If a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) addition relation existed, specialize its middle argument at a point with $e^c=\alpha>0$ irrational, avoiding the finitely many specializations making the entire [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) vanish. With $t=e^z$, the relation becomes a nonzero finite sum of exponentials $e^{(m+\alpha n)t}$. These real exponents are distinct, so the largest one dominates as $t\to+\infty$, a contradiction. This demonstrates that an [algebraic addition theorem](algebraic-addition-theorem.md) is stronger than simple periodicity.
