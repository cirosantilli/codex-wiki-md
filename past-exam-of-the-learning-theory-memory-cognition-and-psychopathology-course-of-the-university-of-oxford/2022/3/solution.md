**Disrupted control of [fear](../../../fear.md) and biased [perception](../../../perception.md) of threat can both contribute to [psychopathology](../../../psychopathology.md), but feeling afraid, identifying another person's [fear](../../../fear.md) and judging whether an action provides safety are different processes.** An experiment should separate them rather than use one response as a general [fear](../../../fear.md) measure.

[Avoidance learning](../../../avoidance-learning.md) provides one control mechanism. An action that removes an aversive event is negatively reinforced; if escape is unavailable, distress can persist. Yet protective control and avoidance that maintains [fear](../../../fear.md) are not contradictory. An effective escape action during a genuinely dangerous event can be beneficial, while repeated [safety behavior](../../../safety-behavior.md) in a safe situation can prevent correction of a false threat [belief](../../../belief.md). A clinical panic experiment manipulated whether safety-seeking behaviours were maintained or dropped during brief exposure, with improved subsequent outcomes when they were dropped. The account predicts that what a person attributes safety to matters, beyond simple within-session [fear](../../../fear.md) reduction.[Behaviour Research and Therapy 37, 559–574; maintained versus dropped safety-seeking behaviours in brief exposure](../../../an-experimental-investigation-of-the-role-of-safety-seeking-behaviours-in-the-maintenance-of-panic-disorder-with-agoraphobia-by-paul-salkovskis-and-others.md)

Circuit interventions offer a different level of evidence. In [Jose Amat](../../../jose-amat.md)'s yoked rat experiments, control over otherwise matched stress exposure recruited medial prefrontal regulation of dorsal raphe responses; inactivating that prefrontal contribution eliminated the protective effect. This gives causal evidence about a circuit contribution under that preparation. It does not establish that every clinical [fear](../../../fear.md) problem results from deficient prefrontal inhibition, or that apparent lack of effort means the individual has [learned helplessness](../../../learned-helplessness.md).[Nature Neuroscience 8, 365–371; yoked escapable/inescapable stress and prefrontal inactivation](../../../medial-prefrontal-cortex-determines-how-stressor-controllability-affects-behavior-and-dorsal-raphe-nucleus-by-jose-amat-and-others.md)

[Perception](../../../perception.md) provides another route. In [Ralph Adolphs](../../../ralph-adolphs.md)'s study, patient SM's impaired recognition of fearful faces followed unusually little spontaneous sampling of the eyes; explicit instructions to look at the eyes normalised recognition. The finding separates access to diagnostic information from an irreversible inability to represent [fear](../../../fear.md). It is a lesion case, not evidence that psychiatric groups generally have the same deficit.[Nature 433, 68–72; patient SM, eye-region information and explicit fixation instructions](../../../a-mechanism-for-impaired-fear-recognition-after-amygdala-damage-by-ralph-adolphs-and-others.md)

Further, [Justin S. Feinstein](../../../justin-s-feinstein.md) and colleagues observed panic after carbon-dioxide inhalation in three people with bilateral [amygdala](../../../amygdala.md) damage, despite impaired anticipatory responses. External threat processing and internally induced [fear](../../../fear.md) can therefore dissociate. The small rare-lesion sample and possible residual circuitry limit a general necessity claim. These results motivate separate measures of external threat identification, internal arousal and learned control.[Nature Neuroscience 16, 270–272; Figure 1 and anticipatory versus induced responses](../../../fear-and-panic-in-humans-with-bilateral-amygdala-damage-by-justin-s-feinstein-and-others.md)

A proposed study would recruit adults with a diagnosed [anxiety disorder](../../../anxiety-disorder.md) and a matched comparison group, measuring medication, symptom severity and relevant comorbidity. Diagnosis is observed, not randomised. Independently randomise participants within each group to controllable or yoked uncontrollable exposure and to an attention-to-diagnostic-features intervention or a matched viewing task. This gives a two-by-two experimental design within each clinical group and tests whether the interventions affect separable processes. [Sample size](../../../sample-size.md) would follow simulation-based [statistical power](../../../statistical-power.md) analysis for the prespecified group-by-intervention interactions; there is no empirical [variance](../../../variance-split.md) estimate in the paper from which to invent a defensible exact number.

For [controllability](../../../controllability.md), a button can terminate a mildly unpleasant, individually calibrated sound; a yoked participant receives the same sequence of sound onsets and terminations, but their own button has no effect. Match available motor responses and give both groups an unequivocal, independent stop option for withdrawal. Record action-outcome [beliefs](../../../belief.md) and timing as [manipulation checks](../../../manipulation-check.md), since matching sound exposure does not automatically match predictability or experienced agency. Use a new transfer task to assess whether learned control changes willingness to act when action efficacy is now identical for everyone.

For [perception](../../../perception.md), use graded fearful-versus-neutral facial displays, with the [attention](../../../attention.md) intervention directing viewing towards diagnostic information. [Eye tracking](../../../eye-tracking.md) tests whether sampling changed. Estimate [perceptual sensitivity](../../../perceptual-sensitivity.md) and [decision criterion](../../../decision-criterion.md) separately: more [fear](../../../fear.md) responses can reflect a lower reporting threshold without better discrimination. Include other [emotions](../../../emotion.md) to test specificity and a delayed generalisation set to distinguish [learning](../../../learning.md) from memorising the training photographs.

Outcomes would include voluntary approach and avoidance, threat expectancy, subjective [fear](../../../fear.md), skin conductance and [heart rate](../../../heart-rate.md). None alone measures the whole construct: autonomic arousal is not fear-specific, and [self-report](../../../self-report.md) can change with demand. Assessors should be masked where possible; hypotheses, primary outcomes, exclusions and the repeated-measure [mixed model](../../../mixed-model.md) should be preregistered. Account for dependence within yoked pairs and randomise pair membership. Retain allocated groups in an [intention-to-treat analysis](../../../intention-to-treat-analysis.md) and report uncertainty and adverse experiences.

If [controllability](../../../controllability.md) selectively improves transfer behaviour despite matched exposure and unchanged facial discrimination, that supports a control pathway. If altered visual sampling improves [fear recognition](../../../fear-recognition.md) and independently changes social-threat judgments, that supports a perceptual contribution. A clinical-group interaction would locate vulnerability, while a correlation between questionnaire scores and task behaviour alone would not prove causation. A combined effect could indicate interacting pathways. Improvement in a laboratory outcome would still require a later [treatment](../../../treatment.md) trial with functioning and sustained symptoms as endpoints.

Consent must state the unpleasant stimulation and withdrawal right; any withholding of contingency details requires proportionate research-ethics approval and debriefing. **The decisive test manipulates control and information sampling independently, checks that each manipulation worked, and measures functional behaviour alongside [fear](../../../fear.md) [perception](../../../perception.md) and [physiology](../../../physiology-split.md).**
