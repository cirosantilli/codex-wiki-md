# Laser cooling

↑ **Parent:** [Atomic measurement methods](atomic-measurement-methods.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_cooling)

Laser cooling uses [laser](laser-split.md) [light](light.md) to reduce the random [velocities](velocity.md) of [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), and therefore their translational [temperature](temperature-split.md). The striking ingredient is selective [photon absorption](photon-absorption.md): an atom moving towards a beam sees a [Doppler shift](doppler-effect.md) that can make it absorb more strongly. Each absorbed [photon](photon.md) transfers [momentum](momentum-split.md) along the beam; repeated absorption and [spontaneous emission](spontaneous-emission.md) can then remove [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md). [Doppler cooling](doppler-cooling.md) makes this selection with the atom's motion, while [sub-Doppler cooling](sub-doppler-cooling.md) also exploits its internal [quantum states](quantum-state.md).

Cooling makes [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) move slowly enough for longer interrogation in [atomic clocks](atomic-clock.md) and [atom interferometry](atom-interferometry.md), and easier loading into an [optical trap](optical-tweezers.md). The same [radiation pressure](radiation-pressure.md) that pushes an atom can cool it: with suitably tuned opposing beams, whichever direction the atom moves, it receives the stronger push against that motion.
