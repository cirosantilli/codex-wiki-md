# Thermal buoyancy support of topography

↑ **Parent:** [Isostasy](isostasy.md)

Suppose a compensated vertical column contains thickness $L$ of material warmed by $\Delta T$. For a small anomaly, the volumetric [thermal expansion coefficient](coefficient-of-thermal-expansion.md) gives $\delta\rho\simeq-\rho_s\alpha\Delta T$. Its column mass change is $\delta M\simeq-\rho_s\alpha\Delta T L$. Define $\rho_{\rm ref}\Delta H$ to be the additional column mass associated with the permitted uplift and interface motion in the chosen [isostasy](isostasy.md) model. The mass balance $\rho_{\rm ref}\Delta H+\delta M=0$ proves the displayed scaling.

The model must specify $\rho_{\rm ref}$: translating an upper column of fixed internal thickness above mantle compensation gives a mantle-density scale, while fixing an internal interface and changing only surface-layer thickness gives that surface layer's density scale. If their ratio is near one, $L=7$ km, $\Delta T=350$ K and $\alpha=10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$ imply only about 25 m of relief. Extending this to a whole [lithosphere](lithosphere.md) requires its depth-dependent temperature anomaly, not only the mean temperature anomaly of the [crust](planetary-crust.md). [Dynamic topography](dynamic-topography.md) and flexural stresses lie outside a simple local-column calculation.
