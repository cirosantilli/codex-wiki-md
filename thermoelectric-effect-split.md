# Thermoelectric effect

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoelectric_effect)

A [thermoelectric effect](thermoelectric-effect-split.md) couples [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) and the transport of [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md): a [temperature gradient](temperature-gradient.md) can generate an [electromotive force](electromotive-force.md), while an [electric current](electric-current.md) can carry [heat](heat-split.md) and cause heating or cooling at a junction of different materials. This coupling makes electrical measurement of [temperature](temperature-split.md) possible through a [thermocouple](thermocouple.md), and permits conversion of a sustained flow of [heat](heat-split.md) into electrical [work](work.md). A finite open-circuit voltage does not itself deliver [power](power.md): electrical [power](power.md) requires an [electric current](electric-current.md) through a load. Thermoelectric conversion therefore involves both [electromagnetism](electromagnetism-split.md) and [thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md). [Northwestern thermoelectrics research group: thermoelectric effects and applications](https://thermoelectrics.matsci.northwestern.edu/thermoelectrics/history.html).
