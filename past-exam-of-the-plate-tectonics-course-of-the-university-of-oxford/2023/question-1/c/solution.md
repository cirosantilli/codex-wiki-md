<h1 id="question-1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**Repeated assembly and dispersal are supported by mutually consistent [paleogeography](../../../../paleogeography.md), [paleomagnetism](../../../../paleomagnetism.md), dated collision belts and subsequent rifted margins.** A useful reconstruction must explain both former connections and later separation, at the same geological ages.

For the youngest well-constrained example, [Pangaea](../../../../pangaea.md), matching continental outlines become more compelling when the fit uses the continental edges rather than mobile modern shorelines. More discriminating evidence includes the continuation of [mountain belts](../../../../mountain-belt.md), [sedimentary successions](../../../../sedimentary-succession.md) and distinctive basement rocks across now-separated continents. Correlated terrestrial [fossil assemblages](../../../../fossil-assemblage.md) and the distribution of [glacial deposits](../../../../glacial-deposit.md) become geographically coherent when the continents are restored. Each observation alone can have alternatives, but a single reconstruction that satisfies several independent tests is much harder to explain by coincidence.

For older examples, the most useful surviving record lies within the [cratons](../../../../craton.md) and their margins. Dated [suture zones](../../../../suture-zone.md), [ophiolites](../../../../ophiolite.md), [metamorphic belts](../../../../metamorphic-belt.md) and [pressure-temperature-time paths](../../../../pressure-temperature-time-path.md) document former ocean closure and [continental collision](../../../../continental-collision.md). Restoring now-separated belts to continuity tests an assembly. Subsequent [continental rifting](../../../../continental-rifting.md), matching [dike swarms](../../../../dike-swarm.md), [large igneous provinces](../../../../large-igneous-province.md) and the transition to [passive margins](../../../../passive-margin.md) test its breakup. [Uranium-lead dating](../../../../uranium-lead-dating.md) of [zircon](../../../../zircon.md), including [detrital zircon](../../../../detrital-zircon.md) populations and [sedimentary provenance](../../../../sedimentary-provenance.md), helps establish whether the proposed connections existed at the same time.

[Paleomagnetism](../../../../paleomagnetism.md) supplies an independent quantitative test. A primary [remanent magnetization](../../../../remanent-magnetization.md) constrains ancient latitude and orientation. After a proposed relative rotation brings two blocks together, compatible [apparent polar wander paths](../../../../apparent-polar-wander-path.md) over an interval support their travelling together; divergence of those paths can document separation. [Radiometric dating](../../../../radiometric-dating.md), a [paleomagnetic fold test](../../../../paleomagnetic-fold-test.md) and a [paleomagnetic reversal test](../../../../paleomagnetic-reversal-test.md) help identify primary records and distinguish them from [remagnetization](../../../../remagnetization.md). There remain important ambiguities: conventional [paleomagnetism](../../../../paleomagnetism.md) does not fix absolute longitude, and sparse ages, later deformation and [true polar wander](../../../../true-polar-wander.md) complicate a reconstruction.

The broad succession is [Columbia](../../../../columbia-supercontinent.md) or [Nuna](../../../../columbia-supercontinent.md), assembled mainly around 1.9–1.6 billion years ago; [Rodinia](../../../../rodinia.md), assembled mainly around 1.1–0.9 billion years ago and dispersed in stages during the later [Proterozoic](../../../../proterozoic.md); and [Pangaea](../../../../pangaea.md), assembled by roughly 300 million years ago and breaking apart from roughly 200 million years ago. These are broad intervals rather than synchronous global events. A quantitative [Nuna](../../../../columbia-supercontinent.md) reconstruction combines [paleomagnetism](../../../../paleomagnetism.md) with the histories of continental margins rather than relying only on matching rock ages.[pp. 443–446, reconstruction of the Nuna core and tests using paleomagnetic and tectonostratigraphic records.](../../../../assembly-and-breakup-of-the-core-of-paleoproterozoic-mesoproterozoic-supercontinent-nuna-by-david-a-d-evans-and-ross-n-mitchell.md)

Repeated global peaks in dated [magmatism](../../../../magmatism.md) and [metamorphism](../../../../metamorphism.md) offer supporting evidence, but cannot prove a [supercontinent cycle](../../../../supercontinent-cycle.md) by themselves: [erosion](../../../../erosion.md), burial and preferential preservation also change the observed age distribution. The persuasive case is the recurrence of dated assembly–breakup sequences in different [continental configurations](../../../../continental-configuration.md), with stronger geometric constraints for [Pangaea](../../../../pangaea.md) than for the older [supercontinents](../../../../supercontinent.md). A [Wilson cycle](../../../../wilson-cycle.md) describes an individual ocean's opening and closure; a [supercontinent cycle](../../../../supercontinent-cycle.md) concerns the much larger-scale continental assembly and dispersal.
