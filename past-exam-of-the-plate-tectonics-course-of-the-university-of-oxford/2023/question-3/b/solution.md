<h1 id="question-3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**The volume alone does not determine a unique change of elevation.** A [local isostatic response to an added surface layer](../../../../local-isostatic-response-to-an-added-surface-layer.md) depends on its thickness and [density](../../../../density.md), so the [Deccan Traps](../../../../deccan-traps.md)' footprint must be specified or retained as a parameter.

Let the deposited [basalt](../../../../basalt.md) have uniform thickness $H=V/A$ and [density](../../../../density.md) $\rho_b$, where $V=10^{15}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and $A$ is the loaded area. Let $w$ be downward displacement of the original land surface, and let $\rho_m$ be the [density](../../../../density.md) of displaced [mantle](../../../../planetary-mantle.md). Assume a subaerial, laterally extensive deposit, negligible air [density](../../../../density.md), unchanged thickness and [density](../../../../density.md) of the pre-existing [crust](../../../../planetary-crust.md), and complete local [isostatic adjustment](../../../../isostatic-adjustment.md). Take the [magma](../../../../magma.md) supply to be external to the local compensation column, and neglect cooling, [erosion](../../../../erosion.md), [magmatic underplating](../../../../magmatic-underplating.md) and any deep thermal or source-depletion anomaly.

Choose a [compensation depth](../../../../compensation-depth.md) beneath all the displaced interfaces. The added column [mass](../../../../mass.md) per unit area is $\rho_b H$. Downward translation of the pre-existing [crust](../../../../planetary-crust.md) replaces a thickness $w$ of [mantle](../../../../planetary-mantle.md) within the column, reducing its [mass](../../../../mass.md) by $\rho_m w$. Equality of [hydrostatic pressure](../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) at the [compensation depth](../../../../compensation-depth.md) therefore gives

$$
\rho_b gH-\rho_m gw=0,
\qquad
\boxed{w=\frac{\rho_b}{\rho_m}\frac{V}{A}}.
$$

The newly formed top of the [basalt](../../../../basalt.md) stands $H$ above the depressed original surface. Consequently the change of the region's _top-surface_ elevation is

$$
\boxed{\Delta z=H-w=\left(1-\frac{\rho_b}{\rho_m}\right)\frac{10^{15}}{A}\ \mathrm{m},\quad A\text{ in }\mathrm{m^2}}.
$$

Thus the original substrate **subsides**, but the top of the deposit **rises** if $\rho_b<\rho_m$. Distinguishing these two surfaces prevents reporting the large downward displacement as the change in plateau elevation.

For an explicitly illustrative choice $A=10^{12}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, $\rho_b=2900\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and $\rho_m=3300\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, the deposit is 1000 m thick, the substrate subsides about 879 m, and **the final surface rises about 121 m**. This is a conditional estimate, not a number fixed by the paper. Half that footprint would double all three heights under the same assumptions. For a nonuniform deposit, local [isostasy](../../../../isostasy.md) gives the same relation pointwise with $H(x,y)$; the total volume still cannot specify a unique local elevation.
