<h1 id="question-3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [asthenosphere](../../../../asthenosphere.md) exists because the [mantle](../../../../planetary-mantle.md)'s ability to deform is strongly dependent on [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md), composition, [water](../../../../water.md) and deformation mechanism. Cooling from the surface creates a strong [lithosphere](../../../../lithosphere.md). Beneath it, hotter [mantle](../../../../planetary-mantle.md) deforms by [material creep](../../../../creep-deformation.md) at much lower [stress](../../../../stress.md) on geological timescales. **The [asthenosphere](../../../../asthenosphere.md) is predominantly solid weak [rock](../../../../rock-geology.md), not a global ocean of [magma](../../../../magma.md).** On the much shorter timescale of a [seismic wave](../../../../seismic-wave.md), it still supports [S waves](../../../../s-wave.md).

The hot shallow [mantle](../../../../planetary-mantle.md) can be close to its [solidus](../../../../solidus.md). [Water weakening of rocks](../../../../water-weakening-of-rocks.md) and, in some settings, a small connected [melt fraction](../../../../melt-fraction.md) can further lower effective [viscosity](../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md). At greater depths, changes in [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md), [homologous temperature](../../../../homologous-temperature.md), mineral structure and [water](../../../../water.md) storage modify the deformation laws; increasing depth does not imply indefinitely decreasing [viscosity](../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md). Explaining a weak upper-mantle interval does not require every location to contain the same amount of [melt](../../../../geological-melt.md).

There are several physically distinct boundaries to test. The thermal [lithosphere-asthenosphere boundary](../../../../lithosphere-asthenosphere-boundary.md) describes a thermal transition, the mechanical boundary a contrast in long-term strength or [viscosity](../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md), and the [seismic lithosphere-asthenosphere boundary](../../../../seismic-lithosphere-asthenosphere-boundary.md) a change in [seismic velocity](../../../../seismic-velocity.md) or other wave properties. They need not have identical depths. A smooth [geothermal gradient](../../../../geothermal-gradient.md) can explain broad thermal weakening, but a very sharp decrease of [seismic velocity](../../../../seismic-velocity.md) may require changes in [melt fraction](../../../../melt-fraction.md), hydration, composition or [seismic anisotropy](../../../../seismic-anisotropy.md). A sharp shallow reflector within a [craton](../../../../craton.md) might instead be an internal lithospheric boundary.[Abstract and pp. 495–498: converted-wave sharpness and the possibility that the imaged cratonic discontinuity lies within thicker lithosphere.](../../../../a-global-view-of-the-lithosphere-asthenosphere-boundary-by-catherine-a-rychert-and-peter-m-shearer.md)

Begin with a thermal null model. A [half-space cooling model](../../../../half-space-cooling-model.md) gives

$$
T(z,t)=T_s+(T_m-T_s)\operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{z}{2\sqrt{\kappa t}}\right).
$$

If a thermal boundary corresponds to a fixed [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) $T_*$, its depth is

$$
z_*(t)=2\sqrt{\kappa t}\,\operatorname{erf}^{-1}\!\left(\frac{T_*-T_s}{T_m-T_s}\right),
$$

so it deepens as the square root of plate age. Test that prediction against independently determined seafloor ages, [bathymetry](../../../../bathymetry.md) and [heat flux](../../../../heat-flux-density.md). An [oceanic plate cooling model](../../../../oceanic-plate-cooling-model.md) adds finite thickness and basal heat supply, allowing the old-age depth to approach a finite value. For continents, include [radiogenic heating](../../../../radiogenic-heating.md), crustal thickness, the history of [continental rifting](../../../../continental-rifting.md) or [tectonic thickening](../../../../tectonic-thickening.md), and advective heating by [magma](../../../../magma.md) or [mantle flow](../../../../mantle-flow.md). A cold, dry [craton](../../../../craton.md) and a recently extended volcanic region should not share the same thermal profile.

Then calculate the observable consequences of competing mechanisms rather than merely assigning a boundary to a map:

- Use [receiver functions](../../../../receiver-function.md) from both [P-to-S conversion](../../../../p-to-s-conversion.md) and [S-to-P conversion](../../../../s-to-p-conversion.md) to constrain the depth and sharpness of a negative [seismic velocity](../../../../seismic-velocity.md) contrast. Check for crustal reverberations and whether the result changes with incident direction or frequency. Combine these with [Rayleigh waves](../../../../rayleigh-wave.md), [Love waves](../../../../love-wave.md) and [seismic tomography](../../../../seismic-tomography.md) to constrain the broader [shear-wave velocity](../../../../shear-wave-velocity.md) profile.
- Measure [seismic attenuation](../../../../seismic-attenuation.md) and [seismic anisotropy](../../../../seismic-anisotropy.md). Elevated [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), small amounts of [melt](../../../../geological-melt.md) and aligned [minerals](../../../../mineral.md) can all affect apparent [seismic velocity](../../../../seismic-velocity.md), but predict different attenuation, polarization and directional patterns. Convert laboratory elastic and anelastic measurements into synthetic seismic observations at the relevant frequencies.
- Obtain [magnetotelluric sounding](../../../../magnetotelluric-sounding.md) constraints on [electrical conductivity](../../../../electrical-conductivity.md), together with surface [heat flux](../../../../heat-flux-density.md). Connected [melt](../../../../geological-melt.md) or hydrated material can enhance [electrical conductivity](../../../../electrical-conductivity.md), but the interpretation depends on composition and connectivity. Joint thermal, seismic and electrical modelling is more discriminating than any single proxy.
- Use [mantle xenoliths](../../../../mantle-xenolith.md) and erupted [magmas](../../../../magma.md) to constrain [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md), [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), [water](../../../../water.md) content, [melt depletion](../../../../melt-depletion.md) and composition, recognizing that transport can alter a sample. Relate these observations to [geodynamic modelling](../../../../geodynamic-modelling.md) of [mantle convection](../../../../mantle-convection.md), [slab](../../../../slab.md) flow, [mantle plumes](../../../../mantle-plume.md) and [lithospheric rejuvenation](../../../../lithospheric-rejuvenation.md).

Compare models against the observed boundary depth _and its sharpness_, including uncertainty and resolution. An age–depth trend with matching [heat flux](../../../../heat-flux-density.md) favours thermal control; a sharp discontinuity that fails the thermal prediction but coincides with an electrical anomaly can support a melt- or hydration-related transition. Neither observation alone proves a unique cause. The spatially varying boundary is best understood as the result of thermal evolution interacting with composition, fluids and deformation history.
