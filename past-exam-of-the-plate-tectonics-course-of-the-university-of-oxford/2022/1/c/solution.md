<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The geological record is a collection of thermometers with different sampling biases, not a direct time series of the whole mantle.

**Primary melt compositions constrain melting temperatures.** Reconstruct [primary magma](../../../../primary-magma.md) compositions from dated, suitably unaltered [basalts](../../../../basalt.md) by correcting for [fractional crystallization](../../../../fractional-crystallization.md) and checking equilibrium with [olivine](../../../../olivine.md). Experimental [phase equilibrium](../../../../phase-equilibrium.md) constraints and mineral–melt thermometers then constrain the pressure and temperature of melt generation. In laboratory calibrations, a candidate melt and mineral assemblage are equilibrated at controlled pressure and temperature, quenched, and their crystals and glass analyzed. Repetition across adjacent conditions brackets the relevant [phase equilibrium](../../../../phase-equilibrium.md) boundaries. Correcting for decompression and [latent heat](../../../../latent-heat.md) then allows comparison as [mantle potential temperature](../../../../mantle-potential-temperature.md). Age-controlled, non-arc basalt suites are especially valuable for separating changes in ambient mantle from exceptional [mantle plumes](../../../../mantle-plume.md). The 2010 reconstruction by [Claude Herzberg](../../../../claude-herzberg.md) and colleagues combines this approach with the temporal distribution of high-Mg parental melts.[Sections on primary magma reconstruction and comparison of non-arc basalts with komatiites](../../../../thermal-history-of-the-earth-and-its-petrological-expression-by-claude-herzberg-and-others.md)

**[Komatiites](../../../../komatiite.md) provide evidence for unusually hot melting, with qualifications.** Many ancient high-Mg lavas, their [olivine](../../../../olivine.md) compositions and [spinifex textures](../../../../spinifex-texture.md) indicate large degrees of melting and high parental temperatures. Their abundance in Archean terrains and rarity in the recent record are consistent with a hotter past. However, a hot [mantle plume](../../../../mantle-plume.md), dissolved [water](../../../../water.md), unusual source composition or crystal accumulation can change the interpretation. A komatiite temperature is not automatically the ambient mantle temperature, and [spinifex texture](../../../../spinifex-texture.md) alone is not a calibrated thermometer.

**Melting residues and crustal products provide independent checks.** Depleted [peridotite](../../../../peridotite.md) [mantle xenoliths](../../../../mantle-xenolith.md), preserved mineral chemistry and compatible-element depletion constrain former melt fractions. More extensive melting can produce thicker mafic [crust](../../../../planetary-crust.md) and more refractory residual [mantle](../../../../planetary-mantle.md); these are predictions to compare with preserved rock packages and seismic or petrological constraints on ancient [lithosphere](../../../../lithosphere.md). [Metamorphic facies](../../../../metamorphic-facies.md) and pressure–temperature paths provide contextual evidence about geothermal regimes, but tectonic burial, decompression and preservation can mimic temporal changes. They do not directly measure $T_p$.

The most persuasive inference is therefore **agreement between dated melt thermometry, melting residues and their predicted crustal consequences**, while separating source fertility, volatile content, [mantle plume](../../../../mantle-plume.md) anomalies, alteration and selective preservation. Those controls explain why hotter ancient melting is better established than one exact global cooling curve.
