<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Subduction zone segmentation](../../../../subduction-zone-segmentation.md) is the observation that [slab](../../../../slab.md) structure, fault slip and upper-plate behavior change along a margin; their boundaries need not coincide exactly. In [Cascadia](../../../../cascadia-subduction-zone.md), it is documented by several independent measurements.

Onshore [GNSS](../../../../gnss.md) velocities, coastal leveling and tide gauges constrain along-strike changes in [megathrust locking](../../../../megathrust-locking.md) and interseismic uplift. [Episodic tremor and slip](../../../../episodic-tremor-and-slip.md) has distinct recurrence and migration patterns, and its occurrence is lower in parts of central Oregon than farther north and south. Coastal geological records constrain differences in coseismic subsidence and the extents of past ruptures. Those observations sample different depths and time windows, so a present-day locking boundary should not automatically be declared a permanent earthquake rupture barrier. A 2014 analysis found persistent central-margin partial creep compatible with reduced uplift, reduced paleoseismic subsidence and lower cumulative slow slip.[Conclusions, central Cascadia approximately 43–46°N and comparison of GPS, uplift and paleoseismic observations](../../../../central-cascadia-subduction-zone-creep-by-gina-m-schmalzle-and-others.md)

A physically specific hypothesis links these variations to [subslab mantle buoyancy](../../../../subslab-mantle-buoyancy.md). An onshore–offshore [seismic tomography](../../../../seismic-tomography.md) study imaged low-velocity asthenosphere beneath northern and southern [Cascadia](../../../../cascadia-subduction-zone.md), spatially associated with stronger plate locking and more tremor, with different central-margin structure. Warmer or partly molten [asthenosphere](../../../../asthenosphere.md) can be less dense. Its buoyant traction can push upward against the slab and change contact stresses on the [megathrust](../../../../megathrust.md). Hotspot-influenced material is one proposed northern source; three-dimensional deformation near the Gorda region offers a southern upwelling mechanism.[Abstract; sections 3 and 4, onshore–offshore tomography and proposed northern/southern buoyancy sources](../../../../buoyant-asthenosphere-beneath-cascadia-influences-megathrust-segmentation-by-miles-bodmer-and-others.md)

There are additional controls. The [Siletzia](../../../../siletzia.md) basaltic basement beneath the [overriding plate](../../../../overriding-plate.md) varies in extent and thickness. Its permeability and stiffness can affect fluid escape and fault stresses: poorly drained material can preserve high [pore pressure](../../../../pore-pressure.md), lower effective normal stress and promote creep. Along-strike changes in incoming plate age, [slab dip](../../../../slab-dip.md), fracture-zone hydration, sediment supply and plate-edge stresses can also modify the thermal and frictional environment. Central-margin creep has specifically been associated with the distribution of Siletzia and inferred fluid trapping.[Discussion and conclusions, Siletzia gravity signature and the permeability/fluid-pressure interpretation](../../../../central-cascadia-subduction-zone-creep-by-gina-m-schmalzle-and-others.md)

How do we distinguish these causes? Offshore and onshore seismic arrays test the geometry of the anomalies; resolution and synthetic-recovery tests determine whether apparent segmentation could result from uneven station coverage. Seismic attenuation, anisotropy, [magnetotelluric sounding](../../../../magnetotelluric-sounding.md), [rock physics](../../../../rock-physics.md) and heat-flow data help separate [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), melt, water and composition. [Gravity anomalies](../../../../gravity-anomaly.md) and seismic interfaces constrain basement and slab geometry; geodetic inversions test whether the inferred locking pattern persists across plausible fault geometries. Forward models then ask whether the measured density or pore-pressure contrasts produce stresses and slip patterns of the required size and sign.

**Segmentation is observed robustly; a unique cause is not established by spatial correlation alone.** Seismic velocity is not a direct density measurement, central creep and deep tremor are not the same phenomenon, and multiple coupled structures can reinforce one another. The buoyant-mantle and upper-plate fluid hypotheses are testable explanations rather than mutually exclusive certainties.
