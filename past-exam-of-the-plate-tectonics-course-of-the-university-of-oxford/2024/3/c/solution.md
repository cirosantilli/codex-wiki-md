<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**Cooling makes the [oceanic lithosphere](../../../../oceanic-lithosphere.md) denser and contracts it; [isostatic adjustment](../../../../isostatic-adjustment.md) therefore lowers the seafloor.** The cooling layer includes substantial [mantle](../../../../planetary-mantle.md), not just the approximately fixed-thickness igneous [oceanic crust](../../../../oceanic-crust.md).

New plate near a [spreading ridge](../../../../spreading-ridge.md) is hot and relatively buoyant. As it moves away, conductive cooling penetrates more deeply, and material formerly in the hot [asthenosphere](../../../../asthenosphere.md) becomes part of the colder [thermal boundary layer](../../../../thermal-boundary-layer.md). To first order,

$$
\delta\rho(z,t)=\rho_m\alpha\,[T_m-T(z,t)],
$$

where $\alpha$ is the volumetric [thermal expansion coefficient](../../../../coefficient-of-thermal-expansion.md), $\rho_m$ a reference mantle [density](../../../../density.md), $T_m$ the hot reference [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), and $t$ plate age. A positive depth-integrated [density](../../../../density.md) anomaly makes the column negatively buoyant relative to the ridge column. [Isostasy](../../../../isostasy.md) is restored by downward displacement and mantle redistribution; cold [rock](../../../../rock-geology.md) also occupies less [volume](../../../../volume.md).

At a fixed sufficiently deep datum, deepening the seafloor by $\Delta d$ removes an equivalent thickness of [rock](../../../../rock-geology.md) from the column and replaces it with less dense [seawater](../../../../seawater.md). In the usual constant-property approximation this gives

$$
(\rho_m-\rho_w)\Delta d\simeq
\int_0^{z_c}\delta\rho(z,t)\,dz,
$$

where $\rho_w$ is [seawater](../../../../seawater.md) [density](../../../../density.md) and $z_c$ lies below the relevant thermal anomaly. This explains why contraction alone and water-loaded seafloor subsidence are not identical numerical quantities.

For a [half-space cooling model](../../../../half-space-cooling-model.md),

$$
T(z,t)=T_s+(T_m-T_s)\operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{z}{2\sqrt{\kappa t}}\right),
$$

so [integration](../../../../integral-split.md) yields

$$
\boxed{\Delta d(t)=\frac{2\rho_m\alpha(T_m-T_s)}{\rho_m-\rho_w}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa t}{\pi}}.}
$$

Here $\kappa$ is [thermal diffusivity](../../../../thermal-diffusivity.md) and $z$ is measured downward from the surface of the thermal model. The leading [oceanic thermal subsidence](../../../../oceanic-thermal-subsidence.md) is proportional to $\sqrt t$. A finite [oceanic plate cooling model](../../../../oceanic-plate-cooling-model.md) limits this growth at old ages. [Sediment loading](../../../../sediment-loading.md), [dynamic topography](../../../../dynamic-topography.md), anomalous [crust](../../../../planetary-crust.md) thickness and hydrothermal heat transport can produce departures from the simple relation.[An analysis of the variation of ocean floor bathymetry and heat flow with age by Barry Parsons and John G. Sclater](../../../../an-analysis-of-the-variation-of-ocean-floor-bathymetry-and-heat-flow-with-age-by-barry-parsons-and-john-g-sclater.md)
