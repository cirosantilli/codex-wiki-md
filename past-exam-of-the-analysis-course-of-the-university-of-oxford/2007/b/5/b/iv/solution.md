<h1 id="b/5/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For a fixed second argument the [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) formula makes $P$ continuous in its first argument. Rational homogeneity therefore extends to real homogeneity by rational approximation; additivity and $P(iu,v)=iP(u,v)$ then give full complex linearity in that argument.

The real pairing $B$ is symmetric, and $B(v,iu)=B(iu,v)=-B(u,iv)$. Hence $P(v,u)=\overline{P(u,v)}$, giving conjugate linearity in the second argument. Finally $B(u,u)=Q(u)$ and $B(u,iu)=0$, because $\|(1+i)u\|=\|(1-i)u\|$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P(u,u)=\|u\|^2,}
$$

which is positive for $u\ne0$. This proves **$P$ is a complex [inner product](../../../../../../inner-product-split.md) inducing exactly the original [norm](../../../../../../norm.md)**. The assumed Banach [completeness](../../../../../../completeness.md) makes the resulting [inner product](../../../../../../inner-product-split.md) space Hilbert. This is the [Jordan-von Neumann theorem](../../../../../../jordan-von-neumann-theorem.md).
