<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The graph $G(T)=\{(x,Tx):x\in X\}$ is closed if it is a closed subset of $X\times Y$. Equivalently, $x_n\to x$ and $Tx_n\to y$ imply $Tx=y$.

The [closed graph theorem](../../../../closed-graph-theorem.md) states that **an everywhere-defined [linear map](../../../../linear-map.md) between [Banach spaces](../../../../banach-space-split.md) is bounded if and only if its graph is closed**. Completeness of both spaces is part of the theorem. A bounded map has a closed graph by continuity.

Conversely, if the graph is closed, it is a [Banach space](../../../../banach-space-split.md) in the product [norm](../../../../norm.md) $\|(x,y)\|=\|x\|+\|y\|$. The coordinate projection $P:G(T)\to X$, $P(x,Tx)=x$, is a bounded linear bijection. The [open mapping theorem](../../../../open-mapping-theorem-functional-analysis.md) makes $P^{-1}$ bounded. The other coordinate projection $Q:G(T)\to Y$ is bounded, and $T=QP^{-1}$ is therefore bounded. This proves the theorem from the permitted result.
