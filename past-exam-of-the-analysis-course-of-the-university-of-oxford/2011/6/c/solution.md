<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

If the real part vanishes identically, the [norm](../../../../norm.md)-difference identity becomes equality, so

$$
\|(I-T)x\|=\|(I+T)x\|\quad\text{for all }x.
$$

Thus the same substitution as above gives $\|Sz\|=\|z\|$ for every $z$. To prove surjectivity as well, observe that both $T$ and $-T$ satisfy the nonnegative-real-part hypothesis. The preceding part makes both $I+T$ and $I-T$ invertible. Their product $S=(I-T)(I+T)^{-1}$ is therefore invertible and onto. It is a surjective [norm](../../../../norm.md)-preserving [linear operator](../../../../linear-operator.md), so the equivalences in (a) prove

$$
\boxed{S\text{ is unitary}.}
$$
