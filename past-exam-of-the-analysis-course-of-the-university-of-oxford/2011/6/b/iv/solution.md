<h1 id="6/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The lower bound in (ii) makes the [image](../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) of $I+T$ closed: if $(I+T)x_n$ converges, the bound makes $(x_n)$ Cauchy; completeness gives its limit and hence a [image](../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) preimage. The same bound applies to $I+T^*$ by (iii), so its [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) is zero. The [orthogonal complement](../../../../../orthogonal-complement.md) identity

$$
\operatorname{ran}(I+T)^\perp=\ker(I+T^*)=\{0\}
$$

shows that the [image](../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) is dense, using the [orthogonal decomposition by a closed subspace](../../../../../orthogonal-decomposition-by-a-closed-subspace.md) for its [closure](../../../../../closure-topology.md). Being both closed and dense, it equals $X$. Thus $I+T$ is onto and bounded below, and by part 3(a)(iii) it has a bounded inverse. Moreover

$$
\boxed{\|(I+T)^{-1}\|\leq1.}
$$
