<h1 id="b/5/vii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vii](../vii.md)

Commutativity and equality of squares give

$$
(S-T)(S+T)=S^2-T^2=0.
$$

Thus $S-T$ vanishes on $\operatorname{Ran}(S+T)$, and by [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) on its closure. The preceding [kernel of a sum of positive operators](../../../../../kernel-of-a-sum-of-positive-operators.md) identity shows that it also vanishes on $\ker(S+T)$. Since $S+T$ is [self-adjoint](../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md), part (i) and the [Hilbert projection theorem](../../../../../hilbert-projection-theorem.md) give the [orthogonal direct sum](../../../../../orthogonal-direct-sum.md)

$$
X=\overline{\operatorname{Ran}(S+T)}\oplus\ker(S+T).
$$

Therefore $S-T$ vanishes on all of $X$, proving

$$
\boxed{S=T.}
$$

This proves [commuting positive operators with equal squares are equal](../../../../../commuting-positive-operators-with-equal-squares-are-equal.md) without assuming a square-root uniqueness theorem.
