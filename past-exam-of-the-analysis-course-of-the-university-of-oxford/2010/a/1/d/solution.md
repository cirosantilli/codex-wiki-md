↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the first [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md), take $(\alpha,\beta)=(0,e_n)$. Its input [norm](../../../../../norm.md) is one, while $T_1(0,e_n)=(e_n,0)$ has [norm](../../../../../norm.md) $n^2$. Thus **$T_1$ is unbounded**.

For the second map, since $j^2\geq1$,

$$
\|T_2(\alpha,\beta)\|_X=\|\alpha+\beta\|_1\leq\|\alpha\|_1+\|\beta\|_1\leq\|\alpha\|_{1,2}+\|\beta\|_1=\|(\alpha,\beta)\|_X.
$$

Equality holds at $(0,e_1)$, so

$$
\boxed{T_2\text{ is bounded and }\|T_2\|=1.}
$$

Both formulas map $c_{00}\times c_{00}$ into itself because adding two [finitely supported sequences](../../../../../finitely-supported-sequence.md) preserves finite support.
