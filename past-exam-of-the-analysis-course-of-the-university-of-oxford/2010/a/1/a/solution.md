<h1 id="a/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For $\alpha,\beta\in\ell_{1,2}$ and real scalars $c,d$, the scalar [triangle inequality](../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) gives

$$
\sum_{j\geq1}j^2|c\alpha_j+d\beta_j|\leq|c|\sum_jj^2|\alpha_j|+|d|\sum_jj^2|\beta_j|<\infty.
$$

The zero sequence belongs to the space, so it is a real [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md). The weighted sum is nonnegative and homogeneous, and the same inequality with $c=d=1$ proves its [triangle inequality](../../../../../triangle-inequality.md). Since every weight $j^2$ is strictly positive, zero weighted sum forces every coordinate to vanish. Therefore **$\|\cdot\|_{1,2}$ is a [norm](../../../../../norm.md)**.
