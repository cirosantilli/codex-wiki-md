↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Define $U:\ell_{1,2}\to\ell_1$ by $(U\alpha)_j=j^2\alpha_j$. This is a linear bijection, with inverse $(U^{-1}\gamma)_j=j^{-2}\gamma_j$, and

$$
\boxed{\|U\alpha\|_1=\|\alpha\|_{1,2}.}
$$

Thus $U$ is an [isometric isomorphism of normed spaces](../../../../../isometric-isomorphism-of-normed-spaces.md) onto the [absolutely summable sequence space](../../../../../absolutely-summable-sequence-space.md), which is a [Banach space](../../../../../banach-space-split.md). Explicitly, a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) $\alpha^{(n)}$ maps to a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) $U\alpha^{(n)}\to\gamma\in\ell_1$; setting $\alpha=U^{-1}\gamma$ gives [convergence](../../../../../convergence.md) in the weighted [norm](../../../../../norm.md). Hence **$\ell_{1,2}$ is a [Banach space](../../../../../banach-space-split.md)**, an instance of [weighted summable sequence space isometry](../../../../../weighted-summable-sequence-space-isometry.md).
