<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The generator $\eta=f\,dh$ is a nonzero [holomorphic differential form](../../../../holomorphic-differential-form.md). Its [canonical divisor](../../../../canonical-divisor.md) $(\eta)$ is an [effective divisor](../../../../effective-cartier-divisor.md), since there are no [poles](../../../../pole.md). In [genus](../../../../genus-of-a-surface.md) one, the [degree of a divisor](../../../../degree-of-a-divisor.md) formula in part (a) gives

$$
\deg(\eta)=2g-2=0.
$$

An [effective divisor](../../../../effective-cartier-divisor.md) of degree zero has every coefficient zero. Thus

$$
\boxed{(\eta)=0,\qquad f\,dh\text{ has no zeros anywhere on }C.}
$$

This proves [nonvanishing of holomorphic differentials in genus one](../../../../nonvanishing-of-holomorphic-differentials-in-genus-one.md). It concerns the differential itself: a zero or [pole](../../../../pole.md) of the particular coefficient $f$ may be cancelled by the local behavior of $dh$.
