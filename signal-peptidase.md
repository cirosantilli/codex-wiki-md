# Signal peptidase

↑ **Parent:** [Signal peptide](signal-peptide.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_peptidase)

A [signal peptidase](signal-peptidase.md) removes a cleavable [signal peptide](signal-peptide.md); a suitable cleavage site and access are required.[Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition by Bruce Alberts and others; “The ER Signal Sequence Is Removed from Most Soluble Proteins After Translocation”: adjacent cleavage-site requirement](molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others/the-endoplasmic-reticulum.md)

For the stipulated $180$-[amino acid residue](amino-acid-residue.md) precursor under [ER signal sequence](er-signal-sequence.md), removal of the first $20$ [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md) leaves $160$. Now stipulate that positions $101$–$120$ of that precursor are instead retained as a [stop-transfer sequence](stop-transfer-sequence.md). Their positions in the mature chain become $81$–$100$, because the numbering origin changes when the first $20$ positions are removed. The mature chain still has $160$ [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md), but its membrane-spanning segment is retained. A second hypothetical chain with an uncleaved internal [signal anchor](signal-anchor.md) loses no positions at all. These are conditional bookkeeping examples, not predictions from invented biological sequences.
