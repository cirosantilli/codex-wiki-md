# Carrying capacity

↑ **Parent:** [Population dynamics](population-dynamics.md)

A [carrying capacity](carrying-capacity.md) is an abundance scale at which a specified environment's net population growth vanishes. In the [logistic growth equation](logistic-growth-equation.md), positive unharvested populations approach $K$. It depends on the species, resources and environment rather than being an immutable universal population [limit](limit-of-a-function.md).

The useful distinction is between a growth rate and a sustainable abundance. In a [logistic differential equation](logistic-differential-equation.md), $r$ controls how quickly a population responds, while the [carrying capacity](carrying-capacity.md) $K$ sets the positive [equilibrium point](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md). Increasing $r$ with $K$ fixed accelerates approach without raising that [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md). Increasing $K$ changes the destination. The [logistic differential equation](logistic-differential-equation.md) diagrams show populations approaching the same [carrying capacity](carrying-capacity.md) from below and above.

In ecological use, the [carrying capacity](carrying-capacity.md) describes what a particular environment can support under specified conditions. Nutrient supply, available space, and accumulated waste can change those conditions; it is not a permanent property of a [species](species.md) alone. A population temporarily above a [carrying capacity](carrying-capacity.md) is possible. In the simplest constant-$K$ [logistic growth equation](logistic-growth-equation.md), its net growth becomes negative; real [population dynamics](population-dynamics.md) can include delays, seasonal variation, and changing resources that the basic [mathematical model](mathematical-model.md) omits. [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, §45.3: carrying capacity and environmental limitations](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/45-3-environmental-limits-to-population-growth).
