# Latent heat from a vapor-pressure slope

↑ **Parent:** [Integrated Clausius-Clapeyron relation](integrated-clausius-clapeyron-relation.md)

Within the [Ideal-vapor Clausius-Clapeyron approximation](ideal-vapor-clausius-clapeyron-approximation.md), a local slope of the [natural logarithm](natural-logarithm.md) of [saturation pressure](vapor-pressure.md) against [inverse temperature](inverse-temperature.md) estimates the molar [latent heat](latent-heat.md):

$$
L_m(T)\simeq-R\frac{d\ln(p/p_*)}{d(1/T)}.
$$

In the [stipulated vapor-pressure calculation](stipulated-vapor-pressure-calculation.md), the slope is $-4811.16\,\mathrm K$, which recovers $40.0\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. For variable $L_m(T)$, a two-point estimate gives an average weighted by $T^{-2}$, rather than the [latent heat](latent-heat.md) at an arbitrary midpoint. Differentiating noisy [pressure](pressure.md) measurements amplifies their errors. Outside the ideal-vapor regime, use independently determined [molar volumes](molar-volume.md) in $L_m=T\Delta v_m\,dp/dT$; a straight-looking graph alone cannot establish that the [gas](gas.md) is ideal.
