<h1 id="section-b/b1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For [elastic scattering](../../../../../elastic-scattering.md), $|\mathbf k_{\rm in}|=|\mathbf k_{\rm out}|=2\pi/\lambda$. Contributions from sites differing by any [Bravais lattice](../../../../../bravais-lattice.md) translation $\mathbf R$ add in phase exactly when

$$
e^{i(\mathbf k_{\rm out}-\mathbf k_{\rm in})\cdot\mathbf R}=1
$$

for every $\mathbf R$, namely when the momentum-transfer wavevector is a [reciprocal lattice](../../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md) vector $\mathbf G$. For a family of planes of spacing $d$, the $n$th reciprocal vector normal to those planes has magnitude $2\pi n/d$. The scattering geometry gives $|\mathbf G|=2k\sin\theta$. Combining the two expressions proves

$$
\boxed{2d\sin\theta=n\lambda.}
$$

Conversely, reflection from that plane family satisfying [Bragg's law](../../../../../bragg-s-law.md) supplies the corresponding normal reciprocal vector. The scalar angle condition alone needs this plane-normal geometry; it does not specify an arbitrary outgoing direction. A nontrivial atomic basis can extinguish an otherwise allowed reciprocal-lattice reflection through its [structure factor](../../../../../structure-factor.md).
