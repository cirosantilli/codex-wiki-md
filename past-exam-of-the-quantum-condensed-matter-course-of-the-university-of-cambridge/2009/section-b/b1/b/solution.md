<h1 id="section-b/b1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A wave packet in an [energy band](../../../../../energy-band.md) has [group velocity](../../../../../group-velocity.md) $v=\hbar^{-1}dE/dk$. The semiclassical force law $\hbar\dot k=F$ then gives $\dot v=\hbar^{-2}(d^2E/dk^2)F$. The curvature therefore defines the one-dimensional [effective mass](../../../../../band-effective-mass.md) by $1/m^*=E''/\hbar^2$. Here $E''(k)=Aa^2\cos(ka)$, so at the band minimum $k=0$,

$$
\boxed{m^*=\frac{\hbar^2}{Aa^2}>0.}
$$

It measures acceleration within the band, rather than a change in the electron's rest mass. At a band maximum the electron curvature mass can be negative; a missing electron is then described by a positively charged [hole](../../../../../electron-hole.md) with a positive curvature mass.
