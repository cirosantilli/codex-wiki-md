↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [charge density wave](../../../../../charge-density-wave.md) is a spatial modulation of the electron [charge density](../../../../../charge-density.md), often accompanied by a periodic lattice distortion. Write a weak modulation as $\rho(x)=\rho_0+\rho_1\cos(Qx+\varphi)$. In a one-dimensional metal, the [Peierls distortion](../../../../../peierls-distortion.md) couples states near opposite [Fermi points](../../../../../fermi-point.md) most strongly for $Q=2k_F$. Opening a gap near the [Fermi energy](../../../../../fermi-energy.md) lowers occupied electronic energies, while the lattice deformation has a positive elastic energy cost. Their competition can make a periodic state favorable below a transition temperature.

The modulation can be commensurate or incommensurate with the underlying [Bravais lattice](../../../../../bravais-lattice.md). Diffraction detects additional satellite peaks, while transport can show reduced low-energy carrier density. Impurities can pin its phase; above a depinning field a collectively sliding modulation can contribute nonlinear current. These effects distinguish collective density modulation from simply assigning independent static charges to isolated lattice sites.
