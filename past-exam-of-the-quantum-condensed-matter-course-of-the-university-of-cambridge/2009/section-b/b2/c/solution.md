<h1 id="section-b/b2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In a [Hall effect](../../../../../hall-effect.md) measurement, a current flows along $x$ and a [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) is applied along $z$. The transverse [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md) initially deflects the mobile charges, building a transverse [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) until the net transverse current vanishes. Defining $R_H=E_y/(j_xB_z)$, the one-carrier [Drude model](../../../../../drude-model.md) gives $R_H=1/(nq)$. Thus electron-like carriers give $R_H=-1/(ne)$ and hole-like carriers give $R_H=+1/(pe)$.

A nearly filled band can conduct through [holes](../../../../../electron-hole.md): its missing electrons behave as positive mobile charges. Metals can have both electron-like and hole-like [Fermi surface](../../../../../fermi-surface.md) sheets. In the weak-field two-carrier approximation the [Hall coefficient](../../../../../hall-coefficient.md) is

$$
\boxed{R_H=\frac{p\mu_h^2-n\mu_e^2}{e(p\mu_h+n\mu_e)^2}.}
$$

The sign depends on the carrier densities and squared mobilities; it need not identify the most numerous carrier. Experimentally the [Hall voltage](../../../../../hall-voltage.md) changes sign when the field or current reverses, allowing subtraction of unwanted longitudinal-voltage pickup. Anisotropic bands and different scattering rates complicate a literal conversion of $R_H$ into a carrier count.
