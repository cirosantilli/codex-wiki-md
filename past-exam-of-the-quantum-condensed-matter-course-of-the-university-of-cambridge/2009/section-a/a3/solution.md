<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

In a [nematic liquid crystal](../../../../nematic-liquid-crystal.md), elongated molecules have orientational order around a [nematic director](../../../../nematic-director.md) $\mathbf n$, but lack the long-range positional order of a [crystal](../../../../crystal.md). Head-tail symmetry identifies $\mathbf n$ and $-\mathbf n$. Molecular anisotropy and sufficient density favor collective alignment: for long hard rods this can increase translational [entropy](../../../../entropy-split.md) by reducing excluded-volume constraints, while attractive interactions can also favor alignment. Thermal orientational disorder competes with these effects.

The scalar [nematic order parameter](../../../../nematic-order-parameter.md) convention here is

$$
S=\left\langle\frac{3\cos^2\theta-1}{2}\right\rangle.
$$

Thus $S=0.44$ means $\langle\cos^2\theta\rangle=0.6267$. The [effective orientation angle from nematic order](../../../../effective-orientation-angle-from-nematic-order.md) is

$$
\boxed{\theta_{\rm eff}=\arccos\sqrt{\frac{2S+1}{3}}=37.7^\circ.}
$$

This is not enough information to determine the literal mean angle $\langle\theta\rangle$: different orientation distributions can have the same second moment. Isotropic orientations have $S=0$, and perfect axial alignment has $S=1$.

In the supplied threshold expression, $d$ is the cell thickness; $K_1,K_2,K_3$ are the splay, twist and bend elastic constants of the [Frank elastic energy](../../../../distortion-free-energy-density.md). The dimensionless relative [dielectric anisotropy of a nematic](../../../../dielectric-anisotropy-of-a-nematic.md) is $\Delta\epsilon=\epsilon_\parallel-\epsilon_\perp$, and $\epsilon_0$ is the [vacuum permittivity](../../../../vacuum-permittivity.md). Positive anisotropy favors a director along the applied [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md). Strong surface anchoring fixes orthogonal in-plane directions on the two plates. The low-field director therefore twists by a quarter-turn through the cell. Between crossed [polarizers](../../../../polarizer.md), with the entrance polarization matched to the first surface director, [adiabatic optical following in a twisted nematic](../../../../adiabatic-optical-following-in-a-twisted-nematic.md) rotates the polarization and allows transmission. A sufficiently large normal field tilts the interior director towards the field, suppressing that rotation and making the crossed-polarizer cell dark; the anchored boundary layers remain distorted. Reversing the polarizer arrangement can reverse the optical contrast.

The threshold is a [Fréedericksz transition](../../../../freedericksz-transition.md): the electric energy gain overcomes the restoring elastic energy of an allowed distortion. Using the expression explicitly supplied by the paper gives

$$
\boxed{V_c=dE_c=\pi\sqrt{\frac{1.1\times10^{-11}+(1.5\times10^{-11}-6\times10^{-12})/4}{0.5\epsilon_0}}=5.43\ \mathrm V.}
$$

The thickness cancels for this strong-anchoring model. This is the onset of director reorientation, not an assertion that every molecule instantaneously becomes perfectly aligned. The quoted elastic combination is taken as given for this numerical task; a threshold derived from a specified full director-energy model must use that model's own elastic combination consistently. The companion illustration in A2(b) shows the plate anchoring, polarizers and bulk field-aligned state.
