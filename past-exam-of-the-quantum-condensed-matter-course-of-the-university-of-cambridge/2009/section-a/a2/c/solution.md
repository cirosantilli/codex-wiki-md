<h1 id="section-a/a2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [polymer](../../../../../polymer-split.md) melt has an observation-time-dependent response because chains need time to rearrange. Short compared with their terminal relaxation time, entangled chains constrain one another and can store elastic [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md). Long compared with that time, chains escape their original constraints by [reptation](../../../../../reptation.md) and the uncrosslinked melt flows. A permanently crosslinked [elastomer](../../../../../elastomer.md) instead retains a long-time elastic modulus.

A single-time approximation is the [Maxwell fluid](../../../../../linear-maxwell-fluid.md): a spring of shear modulus $G$ and a viscous dashpot of [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) $\eta$ in series. Their common [stress](../../../../../stress.md) and additive shear strains give $\dot\gamma=\dot\sigma/G+\sigma/\eta$, with relaxation time $\tau=\eta/G$. For sinusoidal deformation proportional to $e^{i\omega t}$, the [storage modulus](../../../../../storage-modulus.md) and [loss modulus](../../../../../loss-modulus.md) are

$$
G'=G\frac{(\omega\tau)^2}{1+(\omega\tau)^2},\qquad G''=G\frac{\omega\tau}{1+(\omega\tau)^2}.
$$

At low frequency $G''\simeq\eta\omega$ dominates; at high frequency within this coarse-grained model $G'\simeq G$ dominates. Real melts have a spectrum of relaxation times and additional segmental or glassy regimes, so the single-time model does not describe every frequency. A step-strain experiment measures the decay of [stress](../../../../../stress.md), whereas oscillatory rheometry separates stored energy from dissipated energy through the phase of the response.
