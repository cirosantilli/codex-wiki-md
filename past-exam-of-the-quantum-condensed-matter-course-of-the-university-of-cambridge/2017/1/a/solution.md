<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose the $e^{-i\omega t}$ convention. The steady displacement in the [Lorentz oscillator model](../../../../lorentz-oscillator-model.md) has [complex amplitude](../../../../complex-amplitude.md)

$$
u_\omega=\frac{qE_\omega/m}{\omega_T^2-\omega^2-i\gamma\omega}.
$$

The induced [electric dipole moment](../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) is $p_\omega=qu_\omega$, so the microscopic [electric polarizability](../../../../electric-polarizability.md) is $\alpha(\omega)=q^2/[m(\omega_T^2-\omega^2-i\gamma\omega)]$. For $N$ identical oscillators per unit volume, $P_\omega=Np_\omega=\epsilon_0\chi_\omega E_\omega$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\chi_\omega=\frac{\omega_p^2}{\omega_T^2-\omega^2-i\gamma\omega},\qquad \epsilon_r(\omega)=1+\chi_\omega,\qquad \omega_p^2=\frac{Nq^2}{m\epsilon_0}.}
$$

Here the dimensionless quantity called polarizability in the paper is the [electric susceptibility](../../../../electric-susceptibility.md); the single-electron polarizability $\alpha$ has different units. The sign of the [electron](../../../../electron.md) charge disappears because both the driving force and dipole moment contain $q$.

Multiplying by the [complex conjugate](../../../../complex-conjugate.md) of the denominator gives

$$
\epsilon'_r=1+\frac{\omega_p^2(\omega_T^2-\omega^2)}{(\omega_T^2-\omega^2)^2+\gamma^2\omega^2},\qquad \epsilon''_r=\frac{\omega_p^2\gamma\omega}{(\omega_T^2-\omega^2)^2+\gamma^2\omega^2}.
$$

For positive [damping](../../../../damping.md) and positive [frequency](../../../../frequency.md), $\epsilon''_r\geq0$, as required for passive [wave absorption](../../../../wave-absorption.md) in this Fourier convention. At zero [frequency](../../../../frequency.md), $\epsilon'_r=1+\omega_p^2/\omega_T^2$ and $\epsilon''_r=0$. Near a weakly damped [resonance](../../../../resonance.md) the imaginary part has a narrow positive peak, while the real part rises on the low-frequency side and then falls steeply through $\epsilon'_r=1$ at $\omega_T$. It can become negative above [resonance](../../../../resonance.md) for sufficiently strong oscillator strength and weak [damping](../../../../damping.md). At large [frequency](../../../../frequency.md), $\epsilon'_r=1-\omega_p^2/\omega^2+O(\omega^{-4})$ and $\epsilon''_r\sim\omega_p^2\gamma/\omega^3$: both approach the vacuum values. The absorption maximum is close to, but need not be exactly at, $\omega_T$ for finite [damping](../../../../damping.md).
