<h1 id="section-a/a1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [lipid bilayer](../../../../../lipid-bilayer.md) self-assembles from [amphiphiles](../../../../../amphiphile.md): hydrophilic head groups face the surrounding [water](../../../../../water.md), while hydrocarbon tails form a hydrophobic interior. In a fluid membrane, molecules diffuse laterally within each [membrane leaflet](../../../../../membrane-leaflet.md). Closing the bilayer into a vesicle avoids an exposed edge. The interior is a substantial barrier to isolated ions and many polar solutes; membrane proteins provide selective transport pathways in a [cell membrane](../../../../../cell-membrane-split.md).

The [Helfrich energy](../../../../../helfrich-energy.md) describes long-wavelength bending:

$$
F_b=\int\left[\frac{\kappa_b}{2}(2H-C_0)^2+\bar\kappa K\right]dA+\sigma A.
$$

Here $H=(c_1+c_2)/2$ and $K=c_1c_2$ are [mean curvature](../../../../../mean-curvature.md) and [Gaussian curvature](../../../../../gaussian-curvature.md), $C_0$ is spontaneous curvature, $\kappa_b$ the bending modulus, $\bar\kappa$ the Gaussian-curvature coefficient and $\sigma$ the membrane tension. Bending is comparatively inexpensive, whereas stretching the molecular area strongly is costly. Leaflet asymmetry can favor a nonzero spontaneous curvature.

In [micropipette aspiration](../../../../../micropipette-aspiration.md), controlled suction draws part of a large vesicle into a narrow pipette. Pressure and geometry determine membrane tension, and the aspirated length measures apparent area change. At low tension, smoothing thermal undulations supplies much of that area; stronger tension probes actual stretching. Tracking vesicle shape fluctuations provides a complementary estimate of the bending modulus.
