<h1 id="section-b/b4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [Peierls transition](../../../../../peierls-transition.md) is a lattice distortion coupled to a gap in an initially partly filled quasi-one-dimensional electronic band. A modulation at $2k_F$ couples the opposite Fermi points. At half filling with one orbital per atom, it doubles the cell and alternates strong and weak bonds; the [dimerized tight-binding chain](../../../../../dimerized-tight-binding-chain.md) shows explicitly how the gap opens at the Fermi energy. In the ideal weak-coupling zero-temperature model, the [Peierls energy gain](../../../../../logarithmic-energy-gain-from-a-peierls-distortion.md) has a negative term proportional to $-\delta^2\ln(t/|\delta|)/t$, which can outweigh a positive quadratic elastic cost.

Observation should connect electronic and structural evidence: cooling through the transition can reduce conductivity as the gap develops, while diffraction satellites identify the ordering wavevector. A softened phonon near $2k_F$ tests the precursor electron-lattice coupling. In [inelastic neutron scattering of TTF-TCNQ](../../../../../inelastic-neutron-scattering-of-ttf-tcnq.md), measurements from $60$ to $300\,\mathrm K$ found an increasing [Kohn anomaly](../../../../../kohn-anomaly.md) near $2k_F$ as the $54\,\mathrm K$ transition was approached. Those particular measurements were above the transition, so they probe the precursor dynamics rather than a static low-temperature diffraction pattern.[Abstract: temperature interval and wavevector-dependent anomaly](../../../../../phonon-dispersion-and-kohn-anomaly-in-tetrathiafulvalene-tetracyanoquinodimethane-ttf-tcnq-by-gen-shirane-and-others.md)

A real finite-temperature transition also depends on interchain coupling and fluctuations. The ideal one-dimensional energy argument by itself does not prove finite-temperature long-range order.
