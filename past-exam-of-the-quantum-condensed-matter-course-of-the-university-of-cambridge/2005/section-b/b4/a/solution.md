<h1 id="section-b/b4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Exchange interaction](../../../../../exchange-interaction.md) arises from Coulomb interaction together with the antisymmetry required for identical electrons. Different spin states require different spatial symmetry and therefore different pair correlations and Coulomb energy; exchange is not the classical magnetic dipole force between spins. For electrons in distinct atomic orbitals, positive direct exchange favors parallel spins, underlying [Hund's rules](../../../../../hund-s-rules.md) and the formation of local moments.

In a solid with localized moments, a common convention is $H=-J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j$. Then $J>0$ favors [ferromagnetism](../../../../../ferromagnetism.md) and $J<0$ favors [antiferromagnetism](../../../../../antiferromagnetism.md). [Superexchange](../../../../../superexchange.md) through a ligand and other virtual hopping processes can produce either effective sign depending on orbital occupancy and geometry; half-filled orbitals often give antiferromagnetic coupling. For itinerant electrons, the [Stoner criterion](../../../../../stoner-criterion.md) describes a competition between exchange energy gain and the kinetic cost of splitting the spin populations.

Experiments distinguish these descriptions through both static and dynamic response. [Magnetic neutron diffraction](../../../../../magnetic-neutron-diffraction.md) locates magnetic Bragg peaks and therefore the ordering wavevector; an antiferromagnet can have such peaks despite zero bulk moment. [Inelastic neutron scattering](../../../../../inelastic-neutron-scattering.md) measures spin-wave dispersion, from which an exchange scale can be fitted. A Curie-Weiss susceptibility intercept gives complementary information about dominant coupling, but its sign alone does not establish the actual ordered structure.
