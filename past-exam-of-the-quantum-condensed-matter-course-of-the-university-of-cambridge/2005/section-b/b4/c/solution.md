<h1 id="section-b/b4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [p-n junction](../../../../../p-n-junction.md) joins p-type and n-type semiconductors. Initial carrier diffusion exposes ionized acceptors and donors, creating a [depletion region](../../../../../depletion-region.md) and an internal electric field. At equilibrium the [Fermi level](../../../../../fermi-level.md) is uniform and drift balances diffusion, so the built-in potential produces no net terminal current. For nondegenerate abrupt doping, its magnitude is $V_{\rm bi}=(k_BT/e)\ln(N_AN_D/n_i^2)$.

Forward bias lowers the barrier and injects minority carriers across the junction. In the ideal low-injection diffusion model, the [Shockley diode equation](../../../../../shockley-diode-equation.md) is $I=I_s[e^{eV/(k_BT)}-1]$. Reverse bias broadens the depletion region and leaves a small leakage current until breakdown mechanisms become important.

The asymmetric current-voltage curve enables rectification. Illumination produces electron-hole pairs that the junction field can separate, giving a [photodiode](../../../../../photodiode.md) or [solar cell](../../../../../solar-cell.md). Forward injection and radiative recombination in a suitable direct-gap material give a [light-emitting diode](../../../../../light-emitting-diode.md). Measuring dark and illuminated current-voltage curves distinguishes electrical injection from photogenerated current; an ideal junction need not have zero reverse leakage or identical behavior in every material.
