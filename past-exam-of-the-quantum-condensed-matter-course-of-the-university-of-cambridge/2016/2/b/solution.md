↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Direct exchange](../../../../direct-exchange.md) is a quantum-mechanical spin-dependent [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) arising from the [Coulomb interaction](../../../../coulomb-interaction.md) and the required antisymmetry of a two-electron state. It requires spatial overlap of the relevant orbitals. It is not the classical magnetic dipole [force](../../../../force-split.md) between two small magnets, and it can be much stronger than that [force](../../../../force-split.md).

For an explicit fixed-orbital illustration, take orthonormal spatial orbitals $a,b$. [Orthonormality](../../../../orthonormal-set.md) means $\langle a|b\rangle=0$; it does not require their spatial densities to occupy disjoint regions. The normalized spatial states are

$$
\Psi_\pm(1,2)=\frac{a(1)b(2)\pm b(1)a(2)}{\sqrt2}.
$$

The symmetric spatial state combines with the antisymmetric [spin-one-half singlet state](../../../../spin-one-half-singlet-state.md), and the antisymmetric spatial state combines with a symmetric [spin-one-half triplet state](../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md), so that the complete fermionic state is antisymmetric.

For [Coulomb potential](../../../../coulomb-potential-energy.md) $V_{12}=e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon|\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2|)$, distinguish the direct electrostatic integral and exchange integral:

$$
D=\int d^3r_1d^3r_2\,|a(1)|^2V_{12}|b(2)|^2,
$$



$$
K=\int d^3r_1d^3r_2\,a^*(1)b(1)V_{12}b^*(2)a(2).
$$

The [Coulomb exchange splitting of two orbitals](../../../../coulomb-exchange-splitting-of-two-orbitals.md) gives

$$
E_S=\epsilon_a+\epsilon_b+D+K,\qquad E_T=\epsilon_a+\epsilon_b+D-K,\qquad \boxed{E_S-E_T=2K.}
$$

The repulsive [Coulomb potential](../../../../coulomb-potential-energy.md) makes $K\geq0$ in this model. Thus the triplet is favored when the orbitals have nonzero exchange overlap. Its antisymmetric spatial [wavefunction](../../../../wave-function-split.md) suppresses close [electron](../../../../electron.md) encounters and reduces their repulsion. Since the [Coulomb interaction](../../../../coulomb-interaction.md) contains no explicit [spin](../../../../spin.md) operator, the [spin](../../../../spin.md) dependence enters through the allowed spatial [symmetry](../../../../symmetry-physics.md).

For dimensionless [spin](../../../../spin.md) operators $\mathbf s_i=\boldsymbol\sigma_i/2$, whose pair [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md) are $1/4$ in the triplet and $-3/4$ in the singlet, the same splitting is represented by

$$
\boxed{H_{\rm ex}=C-2K\,\mathbf s_1\cdot\mathbf s_2.}
$$

This explicitly states the sign convention: positive $K$ here favors parallel spins. A convention $H=J\mathbf s_1\cdot\mathbf s_2$ would instead have $J=-2K$.

In a [crystal](../../../../crystal.md), [direct exchange](../../../../direct-exchange.md) therefore depends sensitively on orbital extent, overlap and interatomic spacing. It often decreases rapidly as localized orbitals separate. The fixed-orbital example does not prove that every material must be ferromagnetic: orbital rearrangement and competing kinetic or virtual-hopping processes alter the singlet-triplet balance. In particular, [antiferromagnetic](../../../../antiferromagnetism.md) [superexchange](../../../../superexchange.md) through an intermediate ligand is a different mechanism and should not be confused with direct overlap exchange.
