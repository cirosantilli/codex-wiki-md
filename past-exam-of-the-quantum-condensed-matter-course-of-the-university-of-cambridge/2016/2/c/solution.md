<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) quantizes a charged carrier's cyclotron motion into [Landau levels](../../../../landau-level.md). For an isotropic parabolic band, neglecting [spin](../../../../spin.md) splitting,

$$
\varepsilon_{\ell,k_z}=\hbar\omega_c\left(\ell+\frac12\right)+\frac{\hbar^2k_z^2}{2m^*},\qquad \omega_c=\frac{eB}{m^*},\qquad \ell=0,1,2,\ldots.
$$

The degeneracy of each orbital level per unit perpendicular area is $eB/h$ per [spin](../../../../spin.md). Increasing the field changes both the level spacing and the number of states in each level. Successive levels cross the [Fermi energy](../../../../fermi-energy.md), producing an oscillatory [density of states](../../../../density-of-states.md) and hence [quantum oscillations](../../../../quantum-oscillation.md) in thermodynamic and transport quantities.

For a general closed semiclassical orbit, [Onsager quantization](../../../../onsager-quantization.md) relates its wavevector-space area to field:

$$
A_k(\varepsilon_\ell)=\frac{2\pi eB}{\hbar}(\ell+\gamma).
$$

Setting this area to an extremal Fermi-surface cross-section gives successive inverse-field crossings separated by

$$
\boxed{\Delta\left(\frac1B\right)=\frac{2\pi e}{\hbar A_{\rm ext}},\qquad F=\frac{\hbar A_{\rm ext}}{2\pi e}.}
$$

A typical oscillatory contribution is proportional to $\cos(2\pi F/B+\varphi)$. The oscillations are periodic in $1/B$, not in $B$. In three dimensions, extremal cross-sections dominate because contributions from other longitudinal momenta cancel by phase averaging. Rotating the field therefore maps the shape of the [Fermi surface](../../../../fermi-surface.md).

Oscillations in [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) are the [de Haas-van Alphen effect](../../../../de-haas-van-alphen-effect.md); oscillations in longitudinal [electrical resistivity](../../../../electrical-resistivity.md) are the [Shubnikov-de Haas effect](../../../../shubnikov-de-haas-effect.md). They are most readily observed in clean samples at low [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md). The thermal [damping](../../../../damping.md) factor is

$$
R_T=\frac{X}{\sinh X},\qquad X=\frac{2\pi^2k_BT}{\hbar\omega_c},
$$

and a common first-harmonic disorder factor is $R_D=\exp[-\pi/(\omega_c\tau_q)]$. Thus the Landau spacing must be sufficiently large compared with thermal broadening and disorder broadening, approximately $k_BT\lesssim\hbar\omega_c$ and $\omega_c\tau_q\gtrsim1$, with the displayed factors giving the quantitative suppression.

The [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) dependence measures the [cyclotron effective mass](../../../../cyclotron-effective-mass.md), and the field-dependent [damping](../../../../damping.md) probes the [quantum lifetime](../../../../quantum-lifetime.md) $\tau_q$. That lifetime need not equal the [momentum relaxation time](../../../../momentum-relaxation-time.md) controlling [electrical conductivity](../../../../electrical-conductivity.md): small-angle scattering can broaden [Landau levels](../../../../landau-level.md) while relaxing [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) inefficiently. [Spin](../../../../spin.md) splitting can generate further modulation or resolved [spin](../../../../spin.md) structure. Oscillations are not the same as the ordinary smooth [magnetoresistance](../../../../magnetoresistance.md) or [cyclotron resonance](../../../../cyclotron-resonance.md); they probe quantized orbital state counting near the [Fermi energy](../../../../fermi-energy.md).
