<h1 id="section-a/a4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Creating a liquid surface changes the molecular environment and costs interfacial [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md). In the dispersion-only cleavage model, the attractive [Van der Waals force](../../../../../van-der-waals-force.md) per area has magnitude $A/(6\pi h^3)$. Separating two contacting halves from a molecular cutoff $h_0$ to infinity takes work per area

$$
\frac WA=\int_{h_0}^{\infty}\frac{A_H}{6\pi h^3}dh=\frac{A_H}{12\pi h_0^2}.
$$

Here $A_H$ denotes the [Hamaker constant](../../../../../hamaker-constant.md), to distinguish it from an area. Cleavage creates two free surfaces, giving the [Hamaker estimate of liquid surface tension](../../../../../hamaker-estimate-of-liquid-surface-tension.md)

$$
\boxed{\gamma\simeq\frac{A_H}{24\pi h_0^2}.}
$$

The continuum attraction diverges at zero separation and neglects molecular impenetrability and short-range repulsion. The cutoff is therefore an effective contact separation of molecular size, commonly of order $0.1$–$0.3\,\mathrm{nm}$, not a limit that can be taken to zero.

An experimental limitation is the strong temperature dependence of [surface tension](../../../../../surface-tension-split.md). For water in equilibrium with its vapour, the recommended experimental values decrease from $71.98\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}$ at $25\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ to $58.92\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}$ at $100\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, and the fitted tension vanishes at the critical point. A fixed pairwise cleavage energy misses interfacial entropy, changing density and many-body correlations.[2014 release, printed pp. 3–4: temperature-dependent equation and Table 1](../../../../../revised-release-on-surface-tension-of-ordinary-water-substance-by-international-association-for-the-properties-of-water-and-steam.md) The dispersion-only model is unsuitable when cohesion is dominated by [hydrogen bonds](../../../../../hydrogen-bond.md), ionic interactions or [metallic bonding](../../../../../metallic-bonding.md); liquid metals are a clear example of the latter failure.
