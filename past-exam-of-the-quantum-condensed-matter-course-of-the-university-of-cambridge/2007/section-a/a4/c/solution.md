<h1 id="section-a/a4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The intended chemically matched surface approximation is $\gamma_{SL}\simeq0$. It gives $S\simeq23-21.3=1.7\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}>0$, so increasing the wetted area lowers [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md). The initially steep drop spreads and flattens toward [complete wetting](../../../../../complete-wetting.md):

$$
\boxed{\theta_{\rm eq}=0\quad\text{in the negligible-}\gamma_{SL}\text{ approximation}.}
$$

The initial $90^\circ$ angle describes a nonequilibrium shape and must not be inserted into equilibrium [Young's equation](../../../../../young-s-equation.md) to fix $\gamma_{SL}$. More precisely, complete wetting requires $\gamma_{SL}\leq1.7\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}$ in this surface-energy model; chemical similarity alone is not a measurement of that interfacial energy.

Water on such a low-energy, oil-like surface is expected to bead into a comparatively rounded, high-angle drop because the substrate does not compensate its cohesive interactions. Its numerical [contact angle](../../../../../contact-angle.md) needs the water-solid interfacial energy, which is not supplied.
