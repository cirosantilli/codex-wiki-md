<h1 id="section-a/a2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Long anisometric molecules cannot pack independently. In the [Onsager excluded-volume mechanism](../../../../../onsager-excluded-volume-mechanism.md), two slender rods of length $L$ and diameter $D$ exclude a relative-position volume approximately $2L^2D|\sin\gamma|$, where $\gamma$ is their mutual angle. Aligning rods reduces this [excluded volume](../../../../../excluded-volume.md), increasing the translational entropy available at a fixed [number density](../../../../../number-density.md). It simultaneously reduces orientational [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md). At sufficiently high concentration the positional gain wins, producing a [nematic liquid crystal](../../../../../nematic-liquid-crystal.md) even for purely repulsive rods.

The [nematic director](../../../../../nematic-director.md) is apolar: $\mathbf n$ and $-\mathbf n$ describe the same orientation. The molecules retain liquid-like positional disorder, in contrast to a [smectic phase](../../../../../smectic-phase.md) with layered order. A typical dimensionless concentration controlling the slender-rod competition is $cL^2D$, although finite aspect ratio, flexibility and attractions change the transition. The mechanism illustrates that ordering can increase total [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md): the lost orientational freedom is compensated by less mutual obstruction.
