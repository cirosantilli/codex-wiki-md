<h1 id="section-a/a2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At a micrometre scale, [viscous forces](../../../../../viscous-force.md) normally dominate inertia. As an illustrative Newtonian estimate, $L=1\,\mathrm{\mu m}$, $U=10\,\mathrm{\mu m\,s^{-1}}$, $\rho=10^3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and $\eta=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$ give a [Reynolds number](../../../../../reynolds-number.md) $\rho UL/\eta=10^{-5}$. The surrounding flow is approximately a [Stokes flow](../../../../../stokes-flow-split.md), satisfying $-\nabla p+\eta\nabla^2\mathbf u=0$ and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=0$.

A body of radius $0.5\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ translated at that speed in an unbounded Newtonian liquid would need [Stokes drag](../../../../../stokes-s-law.md) $6\pi\eta aU\simeq9.4\times10^{-14}\,\mathrm N$. It stops almost immediately when propulsion stops. The [scallop theorem](../../../../../scallop-theorem.md) rules out propulsion by a single reciprocal stroke in this ideal regime; a rotating helix or a nonreciprocal cycle of shapes can propel it. [Brownian motion](../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md) also randomizes position and orientation: [Stokes–Einstein relation](../../../../../stokes-einstein-relation.md) scales as $a^{-1}$ and [rotational diffusion](../../../../../rotational-diffusion.md) as $a^{-3}$, so retaining heading becomes harder as size decreases.

The Newtonian calculation is a scale estimate, not a complete model of blood. Vessel walls alter drag, the imposed flow advects the body, and nearby [red blood cells](../../../../../red-blood-cell.md) make the medium heterogeneous at this scale. [Non-Newtonian fluid](../../../../../non-newtonian-fluid.md) effects and deformable obstacles can matter; steering must be assessed against those effects rather than inertial coasting.
