<h1 id="section-a/a1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the [polybutadiene](../../../../../polybutadiene.md) chain in [1,4-dioxane](../../../../../1-4-dioxane.md), define $R_x$ from the negative end charge toward the positive one. The [electric dipole](../../../../../electric-dipole.md) energy is $-eER_x$, so the force conjugate to end separation is $eE$, even though the two endpoint [forces](../../../../../force-split.md) are opposite. Balancing it against the [entropic spring](../../../../../entropic-spring.md) force gives

$$
\langle R_x\rangle=\frac{Na^2eE}{3k_BT},\qquad T=299.65\,\mathrm K.
$$

Numerically,

$$
\boxed{\langle R_x\rangle=46.5\,\mathrm{nm},\qquad R_{\rm rms,0}=a\sqrt N=60.0\,\mathrm{nm},\qquad\frac{\langle R_x\rangle}{R_{\rm rms,0}}=0.775.}
$$

The zero-field rms of just the $x$ component is $60/\sqrt3=34.6\,\mathrm{nm}$, a different comparison. The mean extension is still much smaller than the contour length $Na=6\,\mathrm{\mu m}$, consistent with the Gaussian [ideal chain](../../../../../ideal-chain.md) force law. This is an [end-charged ideal polymer in an electric field](../../../../../end-charged-ideal-polymer-in-an-electric-field.md); the direct endpoint [Coulomb interaction](../../../../../coulomb-interaction.md) is omitted as stipulated.
