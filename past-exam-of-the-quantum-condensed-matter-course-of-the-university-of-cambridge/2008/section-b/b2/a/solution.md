<h1 id="section-b/b2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [tight-binding approximation](../../../../../tight-binding.md) starts with localized atomic orbitals. Their overlap allows hopping, broadening each atomic [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) into a band. For example, a single orbital on a simple cubic lattice with nearest-neighbor hopping gives $E(\boldsymbol k)=E_0-2t[\cos(k_xa)+\cos(k_ya)+\cos(k_za)]$. Small overlap means a narrow band; orbital symmetry and hopping signs determine its extrema and dispersion.

The [nearly-free electron model](../../../../../nearly-free-electron-model.md) starts instead with free-electron parabolas, folded into the [Brillouin zone](../../../../../brillouin-zone.md), and treats the periodic crystal potential as a perturbation. Near a Bragg plane, states differing by a reciprocal vector become degenerate and mix, opening a gap approximately $2|V_{\boldsymbol G}|$. Away from such degeneracies, the [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) remains close to the free-electron dispersion. These are complementary limits of the same Bloch-band framework, not different counts of electronic states. Both can give metals or insulators depending on filling and band overlap, but their natural basis functions and useful expansion parameters differ.
