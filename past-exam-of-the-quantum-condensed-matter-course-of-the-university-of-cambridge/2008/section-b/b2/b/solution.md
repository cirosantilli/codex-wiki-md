<h1 id="section-b/b2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Thomas-Fermi screening](../../../../../thomas-fermi-screening.md) treats the carrier density locally as that of a uniform [electron](../../../../../electron.md) gas whose chemical potential is shifted by the slowly varying [electrostatic potential](../../../../../electric-potential.md). Linearizing gives $\delta n=(\partial n/\partial\mu)e\varphi$ for electrons. The resulting screened Poisson equation has inverse screening length

$$
k_s^2=\frac{e^2}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial n}{\partial\mu}.
$$

For a degenerate gas, $\partial n/\partial\mu=g_{\rm vol}(E_F)$ including both spins, giving [Thomas-Fermi screening length](../../../../../thomas-fermi-screening-length.md) $k_s^{-1}$. A nondegenerate [semiconductor](../../../../../semiconductor-split.md) instead has $\partial n/\partial\mu=n/(k_BT)$, giving the [Debye length](../../../../../debye-length.md) $\sqrt{\varepsilon k_BT/(ne^2)}$. Electrons and holes contribute additively to the charge susceptibility when both are present; the lattice permittivity replaces the vacuum value.

The approximation requires a potential varying slowly compared with microscopic electronic length scales and a small perturbation for the linearized result. It captures smooth electrostatic screening, but not the full nonlocal response or [Friedel oscillations](../../../../../friedel-oscillations.md). Low carrier density or carrier freeze-out can make a [semiconductor](../../../../../semiconductor-split.md)'s screening much weaker than a metal's.
