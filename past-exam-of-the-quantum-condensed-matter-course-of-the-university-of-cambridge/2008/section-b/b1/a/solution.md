<h1 id="section-b/b1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a single carrier type with unit Hall factor, the magnitude of the [Hall voltage](../../../../../hall-voltage.md) is $V_H=IB/(net)$, where $t$ is the slab thickness parallel to the [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{n=\frac{IB}{etV_H}=1.25\times10^{24}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}.}
$$

The longitudinal [electrical resistivity](../../../../../electrical-resistivity.md) is $\rho=Rwt/L=1000(2\times10^{-3})(0.5\times10^{-3})/(5\times10^{-3})=0.20\ \Omega\mathrm m$. Using $1/\rho=ne\mu$, the [electron mobility](../../../../../electron-mobility.md) magnitude is

$$
\boxed{\mu=\frac1{\rho ne}=2.5\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m^2\,V^{-1}s^{-1}}=0.25\ \mathrm{cm^2\,V^{-1}s^{-1}}.}
$$

Without the Hall polarity these data do not identify the carrier charge sign. The inference assumes one dominant carrier population; electron-hole compensation would require a multicarrier analysis.
