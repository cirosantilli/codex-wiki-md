↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For [photon absorption](../../../../../photon-absorption.md) or emission, [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) changes by $\pm\hbar\omega$ and crystal [wavevector](../../../../../wavevector.md) by $\pm\boldsymbol q$ modulo a reciprocal lattice vector. Optical [photon](../../../../../photon.md) momentum is normally tiny on the [Brillouin zone](../../../../../brillouin-zone.md) scale, so interband optical transitions are approximately vertical on an energy-wavevector diagram. A direct-gap [semiconductor](../../../../../semiconductor-split.md) can therefore absorb a [photon](../../../../../photon.md) between valence and conduction states near the same [wavevector](../../../../../wavevector.md); an indirect transition usually needs an additional [phonon](../../../../../phonon.md) or another source of momentum. Occupation and optical matrix elements impose further restrictions.

Within one ideal parabolic band of a clean metal, such a small [photon](../../../../../photon.md) momentum cannot normally supply the required finite [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) difference. Intraband optical absorption then relies on momentum relaxation by impurities, phonons or boundaries, as in the finite-scattering-time Drude response. Interband transitions can still occur. The first panel shows the near-vertical direct transition.
