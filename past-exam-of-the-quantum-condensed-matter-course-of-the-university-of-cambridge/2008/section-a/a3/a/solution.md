<h1 id="section-a/a3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [linear viscoelastic fluid](../../../../../linear-viscoelastic-fluid.md) responds linearly to small imposed [strain](../../../../../strain.md) histories but retains memory of earlier deformation. For shear [strain](../../../../../strain.md) $\gamma(t)$ and a relaxed initial state, [Boltzmann superposition](../../../../../boltzmann-superposition.md) gives

$$
\sigma(t)=\int_{-\infty}^tG(t-s)\dot\gamma(s)\,ds.
$$

Here $G(t)$ is the causal [relaxation modulus](../../../../../relaxation-modulus.md), the [stress](../../../../../stress.md) following a unit step [strain](../../../../../strain.md). Doubling a sufficiently small [strain](../../../../../strain.md) history doubles the [stress](../../../../../stress.md), and responses to added histories add. Elasticity stores deformation [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md); [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) dissipates it. Linearity is an amplitude approximation, not an assertion that the material has no relaxation times.
