<h1 id="section-a/a2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The restoring force in ideal [rubber elasticity](../../../../../rubber-elasticity.md) is mainly entropic: stretching restricts the conformations of network strands. A [Gaussian chain](../../../../../gaussian-chain.md) of $N_s$ statistical segments of length $b$ has extension [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) $3k_BTR^2/(2N_sb^2)$, so its small-extension spring constant is proportional to $T$. For an affine network with active-strand [number density](../../../../../number-density.md) $\nu_s$, the shear modulus is approximately $G=\nu_sk_BT$. Increasing the [cross-link](../../../../../cross-link.md) density normally raises $G$ and shortens the strands; [polymer entanglement](../../../../../polymer-entanglement.md) can add effective constraints. Dangling ends and loops do not contribute like elastically active bridges, while junction fluctuations reduce the affine-network estimate.

For incompressible uniaxial stretching by ratio $\lambda$, the nominal tensile [stress](../../../../../stress.md) is $G(\lambda-\lambda^{-2})$ in the ideal Gaussian-network model. Finite contour length causes stronger stiffening as strands approach full extension; swelling dilutes the active strands and changes their reference conformations. A direct demonstration is to hang a load from a stretched rubber strip and warm it gently: an entropy-dominated network tends to contract under that fixed load. This [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) dependence contrasts with a model based solely on stretching bond energies. The simple formula assumes equilibrium, adequate cross-linking and temperatures away from glass transitions or degradation.
