↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [liquid crystal](../../../../../liquid-crystal.md) flows like a liquid while retaining some molecular order. In a [nematic liquid crystal](../../../../../nematic-liquid-crystal.md), elongated molecules have long-range orientational order but no ordinary positional lattice. The [nematic director](../../../../../nematic-director.md) $\boldsymbol n$ specifies the preferred axis, with $\boldsymbol n$ and $-\boldsymbol n$ equivalent for head-tail-symmetric molecules. The [nematic order parameter](../../../../../nematic-order-parameter.md) $S=\langle(3\cos^2\theta-1)/2\rangle$ distinguishes an isotropic distribution, $S=0$, from perfect parallel alignment, $S=1$. A [smectic phase](../../../../../smectic-phase.md) additionally has a periodic layered density modulation, while molecules remain mobile within layers.

The broken rotational symmetry allows [birefringence](../../../../../birefringence.md) and distinct elastic costs for [nematic splay](../../../../../nematic-splay.md), [nematic twist](../../../../../nematic-twist.md) and [nematic bend](../../../../../nematic-bend.md). Under crossed polarizers, rotating a thin aligned sample changes the transmitted intensity because its two optical polarization components acquire different phases. Heating through a nematic-to-isotropic transition removes that orientational birefringence. An [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) can rotate the director if the [dielectric anisotropy of a nematic](../../../../../dielectric-anisotropy-of-a-nematic.md) favors alignment, explaining the optical switching principle of a liquid-crystal cell. Molecular shape, [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), concentration and surface anchoring determine which ordered phase appears.
