<h1 id="section-a/a1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The mean interface height fluctuation is zero; its [root mean square](../../../../../root-mean-square.md) roughness is obtained by summing the mode variances. Replacing the two-dimensional [wavevector](../../../../../wavevector.md) sum by its continuum density gives the [thermal roughness of a tension-dominated interface](../../../../../thermal-roughness-of-a-tension-dominated-interface.md):

$$
\langle h^2\rangle\simeq\frac{A}{(2\pi)^2}\int_{q_{\min}}^{q_{\max}}2\pi q\,dq\,\frac{k_BT}{A\gamma q^2}
=\frac{k_BT}{2\pi\gamma}\log\frac{q_{\max}}{q_{\min}}.
$$

Take $q_{\min}=2\pi/L$ with $L=\sqrt A=10^{-2}\ \mathrm m$, and $q_{\max}=2\pi/d$ with $d=2\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm m$. At $T=300\ \mathrm K$,

$$
\boxed{\langle h^2\rangle\simeq3.08\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{m^2},\qquad\sqrt{\langle h^2\rangle}\simeq0.55\ \mathrm{nm}.}
$$

The logarithm makes the answer only weakly sensitive to order-one choices of cutoff. Gravity has been omitted as stipulated; the fixed mean height removes the zero [wavevector](../../../../../wavevector.md) mode.
