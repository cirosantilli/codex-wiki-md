<h1 id="section-a/a1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For complete wetting, the meniscus has curvature radius equal to the capillary radius $r=d/2$. The [Young–Laplace equation](../../../../../young-laplace-equation.md) gives the supporting [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) difference $\Delta p=2\gamma/r=4\gamma/d$. Balance it against the [hydrostatic pressure](../../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) $\rho gh$. Taking the sap's [mass density](../../../../../density.md) to be water-like, $\rho\simeq10^3\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, gives

$$
\boxed{h_{\max}=\frac{4\gamma}{\rho gd}=\frac{4(0.072)}{(10^3)(9.81)(10^{-6})}\simeq29\ \mathrm m.}
$$

This is the height supported by the stipulated capillary-pressure model. The density assumption is needed because the [surface tension](../../../../../surface-tension-split.md) and capillary diameter alone do not determine a height.
