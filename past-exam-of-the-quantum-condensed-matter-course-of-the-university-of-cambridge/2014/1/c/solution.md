<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) favoring [spin polarization](../../../../spin-polarization.md) must compete with the increase in Fermi [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md). Assume the usual three-dimensional zero-temperature parabolic [conduction band](../../../../conduction-band.md) at fixed $N$ and $V$. Write the [spin polarization](../../../../spin-polarization.md) as $\zeta=(N_\uparrow-N_\downarrow)/N$, so $N_{\uparrow,\downarrow}=N(1\pm\zeta)/2$. The unpolarized [Fermi energy](../../../../fermi-energy.md) is

$$
E_F=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left(\frac{3\pi^2N}{V}\right)^{2/3}.
$$

The two [spin](../../../../spin.md) populations have [Fermi energies](../../../../fermi-energy.md) $E_F(1\pm\zeta)^{2/3}$. Summing their individual $3E_{F,\sigma}/5$ mean kinetic energies gives

$$
\frac{E_{\rm kin}}N=\frac{3E_F}{10}\left[(1+\zeta)^{5/3}+(1-\zeta)^{5/3}\right].
$$

The specified interaction is an [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) **per [electron](../../../../electron.md)**, $U(1-\zeta^2)/4$. Thus the [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) change per [electron](../../../../electron.md) near the unpolarized state is

$$
\Delta f=\left(\frac{E_F}{3}-\frac U4\right)\zeta^2+\frac{E_F}{81}\zeta^4+O(\zeta^6).
$$

The quadratic coefficient changes sign at the [Stoner threshold for an interaction normalized per electron](../../../../stoner-threshold-for-an-interaction-normalized-per-electron.md),

$$
\boxed{U_c=\frac43E_F=\frac{2\hbar^2}{3m}(3\pi^2)^{2/3}\frac{N^{2/3}}{V^{2/3}},\qquad U_c\propto N^{2/3}V^{-2/3}.}
$$

The positive quartic term gives a continuous onset in this model. More globally, $f''(\zeta)=(E_F/3)[(1+\zeta)^{-1/3}+(1-\zeta)^{-1/3}]-U/2$. [Convexity](../../../../convex-function.md) of $x^{-1/3}$ makes this nonnegative for $U\leq4E_F/3$, so no lower polarized minimum appears before that threshold.

At fixed [number density](../../../../number-density.md) $N/V$, the critical $U$ is independent of the total sample size. A comparison restricted to a fully polarized state gives a different numerical coefficient, $U>\frac{12}{5}E_F(2^{2/3}-1)$, but the same requested density scaling; it misses the partially polarized onset. One must also distinguish this $U$, with units of [energy](../../../../energy-split.md), from a contact coupling multiplying an interaction-energy density $g n_\uparrow n_\downarrow$: here $g=U/(N/V)$. Importing a differently normalized [Stoner criterion](../../../../stoner-criterion.md) would introduce an erroneous extra density factor.
