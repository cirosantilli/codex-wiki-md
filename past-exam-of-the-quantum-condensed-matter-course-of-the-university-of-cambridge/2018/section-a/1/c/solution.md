<h1 id="section-a/1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a single carrier species with positive [charge carrier mobility](../../../../../charge-carrier-mobility.md) $\mu$, the [Hall coefficient](../../../../../hall-coefficient.md) and longitudinal [electrical conductivity](../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md) are

$$
R_H=\frac1{nq},\qquad \sigma=n|q|\mu.
$$

Take the [electric current density](../../../../../current-density.md) along $x$, magnetic field along $z$, and no transverse current. Then $E_y=R_Hj_xB$ and $j_x=\sigma E_x$, giving the [Hall angle](../../../../../hall-angle.md) relation

$$
\boxed{\frac{E_y}{E_x}=R_H\sigma B=\operatorname{sgn}(q)\mu B=-\mu B\quad(q=-e).}
$$

The sign specifies the electron Hall polarity; the ratio of field magnitudes is $|E_H|/|E|=\mu|B|$. Converting the stated [electron mobility](../../../../../electron-mobility.md) to [SI](../../../../../international-system-of-units.md) gives $\mu=900\times10^{-4}=0.0900\,\mathrm{m^2\,V^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{|B|=\frac{0.015}{0.0900}=0.167\,\mathrm T.}
$$

This uses the homogeneous single-carrier [Drude model](../../../../../drude-model.md); the carrier density cancels.
