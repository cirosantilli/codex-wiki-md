<h1 id="section-b/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a degenerate [Fermi gas](../../../../../fermi-gas.md), the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) restricts thermally changed occupations to an energy shell of width $k_BT$ around the [Fermi energy](../../../../../fermi-energy.md). The number of active [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) is of order $g(E_F)k_BT\sim Nk_BT/E_F$; each carries an excitation energy of order $k_BT$. Thus the thermal energy grows as $N(k_BT)^2/E_F$ and the [electronic heat capacity](../../../../../electronic-heat-capacity.md) is proportional to $T$.

More precisely, the fixed-particle-number [Sommerfeld expansion](../../../../../sommerfeld-expansion.md) gives

$$
U(T)-U(0)=\frac{\pi^2}{6}g(E_F)(k_BT)^2+O(T^4),\qquad
C_e=\frac{\pi^2}{3}g(E_F)k_B^2T+O(T^3).
$$

Here $g$ is the total [spin-summed density of states](../../../../../spin-summed-density-of-states.md) for the sample. For a three-dimensional parabolic band, $g(E_F)=3N/(2E_F)$, yielding

$$
\boxed{C_e=A\frac{Nk_B^2T}{E_F},\qquad A=\frac{\pi^2}{2},\qquad k_BT\ll E_F.}
$$

The linear law concerns the electronic contribution; the low-temperature lattice [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md) usually adds a $T^3$ term.
