<h1 id="section-b/5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $n=N/\mathcal V$ and $c_e=C_e/\mathcal V$ be the [carrier density](../../../../../carrier-density.md) and electronic [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md) per unit volume. In isotropic [kinetic theory](../../../../../kinetic-theory-split.md), the [angular average](../../../../../angular-average.md) $\langle v_x^2\rangle=v_F^2/3$ gives the electronic [thermal conductivity](../../../../../thermal-conductivity.md)

$$
K_e=\frac13c_ev_F\ell_Q=\frac13c_ev_F^2\tau_Q,
$$

where $\ell_Q=v_F\tau_Q$ is the [mean free path](../../../../../mean-free-path.md) associated with the [heat-current relaxation time](../../../../../heat-current-relaxation-time.md). With $v_F^2=2E_F/m^*$,

$$
K_e=\frac{2A}{3}\frac{nk_B^2T\tau_Q}{m^*}.
$$

The [Drude model](../../../../../drude-model.md) gives the [electrical conductivity](../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md) $\sigma=ne^2\tau_\sigma/m^*$, where $\tau_\sigma$ is the [momentum relaxation time](../../../../../momentum-relaxation-time.md). Therefore

$$
\frac{K_e}{\sigma T}=\frac{2A}{3}\left(\frac{k_B}{e}\right)^2\frac{\tau_Q}{\tau_\sigma}.
$$

When the same approximately elastic collisions relax heat and charge currents, $\tau_Q=\tau_\sigma$. Using $A=\pi^2/2$ gives the [Wiedemann-Franz law](../../../../../wiedemann-franz-law.md)

$$
\boxed{K_e=L_0\sigma T,\qquad L_0=\frac{\pi^2}{3}\left(\frac{k_B}{e}\right)^2
\simeq2.44\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{W\,\Omega\,K^{-2}}.}
$$

$L_0$ is the [Lorenz number](../../../../../lorenz-number.md). Degenerate electrons move at the [Fermi velocity](../../../../../fermi-velocity.md), not the classical thermal velocity; using classical equipartition would give the wrong coefficient.
