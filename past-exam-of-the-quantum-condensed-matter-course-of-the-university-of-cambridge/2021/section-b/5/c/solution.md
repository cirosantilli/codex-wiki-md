<h1 id="section-b/5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [Wiedemann-Franz law](../../../../../wiedemann-franz-law.md) applies to a normal degenerate [metal](../../../../../metal.md) when the same itinerant [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) carry heat and charge, scattering is approximately elastic over the thermal shell, and the transport probability varies slowly across the energy interval $k_BT$ around the [Fermi level](../../../../../fermi-level.md). The comparison uses the electronic [thermal conductivity](../../../../../thermal-conductivity.md) $K_e$, with any lattice contribution removed.

It works particularly well as $T\to0$ when elastic impurity or defect scattering dominates. It also often holds approximately for $\Theta_D\ll T\ll T_F$, where thermally populated [phonons](../../../../../phonon.md) act as a nearly elastic classical scattering field. Room temperature need not lie above the [Debye temperature](../../../../../debye-temperature.md) for every metal.

At intermediate temperatures, inelastic small-angle [electron-phonon scattering](../../../../../electron-phonon-scattering.md) can relax heat much more efficiently than charge. Then $\tau_Q\ne\tau_\sigma$ and $K_e/(\sigma T)$ can be smaller than $L_0$. Strong energy dependence of scattering, additional neutral heat carriers, or a transition out of the normal metallic state also invalidates the simple derivation. An observed deviation alone does not identify which assumption failed.
