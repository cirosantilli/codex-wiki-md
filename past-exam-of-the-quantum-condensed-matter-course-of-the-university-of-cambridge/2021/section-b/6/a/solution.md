<h1 id="section-b/6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write $\varepsilon_s=\epsilon_0\epsilon$ and take $e>0$, $\alpha>0$, so the p side is $x<0$. In the [depletion approximation](../../../../../depletion-approximation.md), mobile [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) and [electron holes](../../../../../electron-hole.md) are negligible compared with the fixed ionized dopants. Their [charge density](../../../../../charge-density.md) is

$$
\rho(x)=e[n_D^+(x)-n_A^-(x)]\simeq e\alpha x.
$$

Applying [Poisson equation](../../../../../poisson-s-equation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2V}{dx^2}=-\frac{\rho(x)}{\varepsilon_s}=-\frac{e\alpha x}{\epsilon_0\epsilon}.}
$$

Outside the [depletion region](../../../../../depletion-region.md), mobile carriers approximately neutralize the dopants, so $\rho\simeq0$ and $V''\simeq0$. Full dopant ionization alone does not remove the mobile-carrier charge: the result also requires the [depletion approximation](../../../../../depletion-approximation.md).
