<h1 id="section-a/a1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A [liquid crystal](../../../../../liquid-crystal.md) has some molecular order while retaining liquid-like motion. The distinction between orientational and positional order separates its main phases.

A [nematic liquid crystal](../../../../../nematic-liquid-crystal.md) has long-range orientational order about a [nematic director](../../../../../nematic-director.md) $\mathbf n$, with $\mathbf n$ and $-\mathbf n$ equivalent for apolar molecules, but no periodic density lattice. Its [nematic order parameter](../../../../../nematic-order-parameter.md) is $S=\langle(3\cos^2\theta-1)/2\rangle$, where $\theta$ measures molecular alignment with the director. A [cholesteric liquid crystal](../../../../../cholesteric-liquid-crystal.md) is a chiral nematic whose director rotates in space, producing a helical pitch while remaining locally nematic.

A [smectic phase](../../../../../smectic-phase.md) adds a periodic density modulation in one direction, forming layers with liquid-like motion within each layer. In a [smectic A phase](../../../../../smectic-a-phase.md), the director is perpendicular to the layers; in a [smectic C phase](../../../../../smectic-c-phase.md), it is tilted relative to the layer normal. A [columnar liquid crystal](../../../../../columnar-liquid-crystal.md) instead has columns with a two-dimensional positional lattice and comparatively fluid motion along them.

These orders have different experimental signatures. [Birefringence](../../../../../birefringence.md) under crossed [polarizers](../../../../../polarizer.md) reveals orientational anisotropy, while [X-ray diffraction](../../../../../x-ray-diffraction.md) can detect a smectic layer spacing $d$ through a peak near $q=2\pi/d$. Optical anisotropy alone does not establish a layer lattice.
