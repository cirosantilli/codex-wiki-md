<h1 id="section-a/a1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

An [amphiphile](../../../../../amphiphile.md) contains a solvent-compatible head and a solvent-avoiding tail. In [water](../../../../../water.md), aggregation can lower the [Gibbs free energy](../../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md) by reducing exposed hydrocarbon area through the [hydrophobic effect](../../../../../hydrophobic-effect.md). This competes with loss of translational [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md), head-group repulsion and the cost of deforming the tails. Above the [critical micelle concentration](../../../../../critical-micelle-concentration.md), added amphiphiles mainly enter aggregates rather than continuing to increase the isolated-molecule concentration proportionally.

The [amphiphile packing parameter](../../../../../amphiphile-packing-parameter.md) $P=v/(a_0\ell_c)$ compares tail volume $v$, preferred head area $a_0$ and tail length $\ell_c$. Rough geometric ranges favor spherical [micelles](../../../../../micelle.md) for $P<1/3$, cylindrical aggregates for $1/3<P<1/2$, and [lipid bilayers](../../../../../lipid-bilayer.md) for $1/2<P<1$. These are organizing estimates rather than universal sharp phase boundaries. A bilayer can close into a vesicle to eliminate exposed edges; concentration, salt and temperature can change the preferred aggregate.

A simple experimental test varies concentration and measures [surface tension](../../../../../surface-tension-split.md): its slope against log concentration changes near the [critical micelle concentration](../../../../../critical-micelle-concentration.md), while [light scattering](../../../../../scattering-of-light.md) detects the appearance of larger aggregates. These observations distinguish the concentration threshold from an assumption that every molecule forms an aggregate at arbitrarily low concentration.
