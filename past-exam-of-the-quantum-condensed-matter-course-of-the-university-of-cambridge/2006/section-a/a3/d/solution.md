<h1 id="section-a/a3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [Debye–Hückel approximation](../../../../../debye-huckel-approximation.md) linearizes the hyperbolic sine, giving $(\nabla^2-\kappa^2)\psi=0$ with

$$
\boxed{\kappa^2=\frac{2n_0q^2}{\varepsilon k_BT},\qquad \lambda_D=\kappa^{-1}.}
$$

The [planar Debye-Hückel double layer](../../../../../planar-debye-huckel-double-layer.md) has $\psi(s)=\psi_0e^{-\kappa s}$. For an isolated sphere of radius $a$, the [Debye-Hückel potential of a charged sphere](../../../../../debye-huckel-potential-of-a-charged-sphere.md) is $\psi(r)=\psi_0(a/r)e^{-\kappa(r-a)}$ outside the sphere; the factor $a/r$ accounts for spherical spreading as well as screening.

The [Debye–Hückel screening length](../../../../../debye-huckel-screening-length.md) is the characteristic distance over which the ion cloud suppresses a weak surface potential. Adding salt increases [ionic strength](../../../../../ionic-strength.md) and therefore increases $\kappa$, shortening the interaction range. Linearization requires **$|q\psi|\ll k_BT$**, particularly near the surface where the potential is largest. It means the ionic electrostatic energy is small compared with thermal energy; for monovalent ions near room temperature, $k_BT/e\simeq26\,\mathrm{mV}$. This condition alone does not remove other limitations of point-ion mean-field theory at high concentration or strong correlations.
