<h1 id="section-a/a3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For smooth uncharged spheres, the basic attraction is the [Van der Waals force](../../../../../van-der-waals-force.md), arising from correlated electric fluctuations. The [Hamaker constant](../../../../../hamaker-constant.md) $A_H$ incorporates the particles and intervening medium. For equal spheres of radius $a$ separated by a surface gap $h\ll a$, the [near-contact van der Waals attraction of spheres](../../../../../near-contact-van-der-waals-attraction-of-spheres.md) is

$$
\boxed{U_{\rm vdW}(h)\simeq-\frac{A_Ha}{12h},\qquad F_{\rm vdW}(h)=-\frac{A_Ha}{12h^2}.}
$$

Here positive force means repulsion, and the attractive case has $A_H>0$. At molecular separation, strong short-range repulsion prevents overlap and replaces the continuum divergence with a deep primary minimum. The energy approaches zero from below at large separation; an uncoated, uncharged attractive pair has no electrostatic barrier.

For illustration, $A_H=10^{-20}\,\mathrm J$, $a=100\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $h=1\,\mathrm{nm}$ give $|U|\simeq8.3\times10^{-20}\,\mathrm J\simeq20k_BT$ at $300\,\mathrm K$. These are chosen scales, not universal material constants. Such a well can trap particles after a thermal encounter and cause [particle aggregation](../../../../../particle-aggregation.md), unless another mechanism such as [steric stabilization](../../../../../steric-stabilization.md) intervenes.
