<h1 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The [drift velocity](../../../../drift-velocity.md) is the small ensemble-average velocity acquired by [charge carriers](../../../../charge-carrier.md) in an applied [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md). It is distinct from their much larger random thermal or [Fermi velocity](../../../../fermi-velocity.md). For conduction [electrons](../../../../electron.md) of charge $-e$, the isotropic [Drude model](../../../../drude-model.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf v_d=-\frac{e\tau}{m^*}\mathbf E=-\mu_e\mathbf E,}
$$

where $\tau$ is the [momentum relaxation time](../../../../momentum-relaxation-time.md).

The positive [electron mobility](../../../../electron-mobility.md) $\mu_e=|\mathbf v_d|/|\mathbf E|$ measures the ease of this drift in [linear response](../../../../linear-response-theory.md). In the same approximation,

$$
\boxed{\mu_e=\frac{e\tau}{m^*},\qquad\sigma=ne\mu_e.}
$$

Its units are $\mathrm{m^2\,V^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$; in an anisotropic crystal the mobility is a [tensor](../../../../tensor.md).

The [band effective mass](../../../../band-effective-mass.md) describes the acceleration implied by the curvature of the [dispersion relation](../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md). The [group velocity](../../../../group-velocity.md) is $\mathbf v=\hbar^{-1}\nabla_{\mathbf k}E$, while $\hbar\dot{\mathbf k}=-e\mathbf E$. Thus

$$
\boxed{(m^{*-1})_{ij}=\frac1{\hbar^2}\frac{\partial^2E}{\partial k_i\partial k_j}.}
$$

For an isotropic parabolic minimum this reduces to $E=E_c+\hbar^2k^2/(2m^*)$ and $m^*\dot{\mathbf v}=-e\mathbf E$. It incorporates the periodic crystal potential and need not equal the bare [electron mass](../../../../electron-mass.md). Negative electronic curvature near a band maximum is conveniently described by positive-mass [electron holes](../../../../electron-hole.md).
