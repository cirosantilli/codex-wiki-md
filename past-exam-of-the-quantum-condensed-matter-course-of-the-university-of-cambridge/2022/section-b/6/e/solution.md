<h1 id="section-b/6/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

A current-carrying sample in a transverse [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) develops a transverse electric field because the [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md) deflects its [charge carriers](../../../../../charge-carrier.md). In steady state this [Hall voltage](../../../../../hall-voltage.md) balances the transverse deflection. With [Hall coefficient](../../../../../hall-coefficient.md) $R_H=E_y/(j_xB_z)$, one isotropic electron band gives $R_H=-1/(ne)$ and one hole band gives $R_H=+1/(pe)$.

When [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) and [electron holes](../../../../../electron-hole.md) both conduct, the weak-field two-carrier result is

$$
\boxed{R_H=\frac{p\mu_h^2-n\mu_e^2}{e(p\mu_h+n\mu_e)^2},\qquad
\sigma=e(n\mu_e+p\mu_h).}
$$

The [two-carrier Hall coefficient](../../../../../two-carrier-hall-coefficient.md) weights each species by mobility squared. Its sign changes when

$$
\boxed{p\mu_h^2=n\mu_e^2.}
$$

[Temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) changes dopant ionization, intrinsic electron-hole generation, and the two [charge carrier mobilities](../../../../../charge-carrier-mobility.md), so it can move the system across this condition. For example, a [p-type semiconductor](../../../../../p-type-semiconductor.md) can be hole-dominated in its extrinsic regime but become negative in its intrinsic regime if $n\simeq p$ and $\mu_e>\mu_h$. A sign change is possible, not mandatory: in many [n-type semiconductors](../../../../../n-type-semiconductor.md) [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) dominate at both temperatures. The density majority alone does not determine the Hall sign in a two-carrier material.
