<h1 id="section-b/4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

An unbound conduction [electron](../../../../../electron.md) has no local restoring force, so set $\omega_0=0$ while retaining inertia and [momentum relaxation time](../../../../../momentum-relaxation-time.md) $\tau=1/\gamma$. This is the [Drude model](../../../../../drude-model.md):

$$
\boxed{\epsilon_r(\omega)=1-\frac{\omega_p^2}{\omega(\omega+i\gamma)}.}
$$

Separating the response gives

$$
\boxed{\epsilon_r'=1-\frac{\omega_p^2}{\omega^2+\gamma^2},\qquad
\epsilon_r''=\frac{\omega_p^2\gamma}{\omega(\omega^2+\gamma^2)}.}
$$

For a good [metal](../../../../../metal.md), $\gamma\ll\omega_p$. The real part is strongly negative at low frequency, crosses zero at $\sqrt{\omega_p^2-\gamma^2}\simeq\omega_p$, and approaches one from below. The imaginary part diverges as $\omega_p^2/(\gamma\omega)$ at low frequency, reflecting the finite dc [electrical conductivity](../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md) $\sigma_0=\epsilon_0\omega_p^2/\gamma$, then decays as $\omega^{-3}$. The low-frequency imaginary response describes conduction loss, not a finite-frequency bound-electron absorption line. Core-electron and interband responses can add a background in a real metal.
