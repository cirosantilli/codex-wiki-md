<h1 id="section-b/4/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

At normal incidence from vacuum, the [Fresnel equations](../../../../../fresnel-equations.md) give the [reflectance](../../../../../reflectance.md)

$$
\boxed{R(\omega)=\left|\frac{N(\omega)-1}{N(\omega)+1}\right|^2,\qquad N^2=\epsilon_r.}
$$

A good metal has $R$ close to one below its [plasma frequency](../../../../../plasma-frequency.md). At very low frequency the electromagnetic field penetrates only a [skin depth](../../../../../skin-depth.md); the local Drude limit gives $1-R\simeq\sqrt{8\epsilon_0\omega/\sigma_0}$. In the collisionless negative-permittivity range the transmitted field is evanescent and reflection remains strong. Near the plasma edge the reflectance drops sharply; above it $\epsilon_r'>0$ and propagating waves become possible. In the ideal free-electron high-frequency limit,

$$
\boxed{R\sim\frac{\omega_p^4}{16\omega^4}\longrightarrow0.}
$$

Finite damping rounds the plasma edge, and interband absorption changes the detailed response of actual metals.
