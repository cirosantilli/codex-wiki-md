↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For independent [electronic transitions](../../../../../electronic-transition.md), add the [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) responses:

$$
\boxed{\epsilon_r(\omega)=1+\sum_j\frac{S_j}{\omega_j^2-\omega^2-i\gamma_j\omega},\qquad S_j>0.}
$$

The positive constants $S_j$ include electron density and transition [atomic oscillator strength](../../../../../atomic-oscillator-strength.md). Each term adds an absorption peak to $\epsilon_r''$ and a dispersive positive-to-negative contribution to $\epsilon_r'-1$, as in the middle row of the figure.

Between well-separated absorption lines, where the medium is transparent, the [refractive index](../../../../../refractive-index.md) generally increases with frequency: $dn_r/d\omega>0$, or $dn_r/d\lambda<0$. This is [normal dispersion](../../../../../normal-dispersion.md). Near a resonance the real response turns down with increasing frequency, producing an interval of [anomalous dispersion](../../../../../anomalous-dispersion.md), $dn_r/d\omega<0$, accompanied by appreciable absorption. The absorptive and dispersive parts are linked by the [Kramers-Kronig relations](../../../../../kramers-kronig-relations.md); the steep variation is not independent of the loss spectrum.
