<h1 id="section-b/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose one [atomic orbital](../../../../../atomic-orbital.md) $\phi_A$ and $\phi_B$ on the two atoms and approximate a [molecular orbital](../../../../../molecular-orbital.md) by the [linear combination of atomic orbitals](../../../../../linear-combination-of-atomic-orbitals.md)

$$
\psi=c_A\phi_A+c_B\phi_B.
$$

Projecting the [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) onto the two orbitals gives a [generalized eigenvalue problem](../../../../../generalized-eigenvalue-problem.md) $H\mathbf c=ES\mathbf c$, where $H_{ij}=\langle\phi_i|\hat H|\phi_j\rangle$ and $S_{ij}=\langle\phi_i|\phi_j\rangle$. In an orthonormal basis the matrix is

$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_A&t\\t^*&\epsilon_B\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
E_\pm=\frac{\epsilon_A+\epsilon_B}{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{(\epsilon_A-\epsilon_B)^2}{4}+|t|^2}.
$$

The coupling mixes the atomic states and produces a lower [bonding molecular orbital](../../../../../bonding-orbital.md) and upper [antibonding molecular orbital](../../../../../antibonding-orbital.md). For equivalent real orbitals with overlap $s$ the normalized combinations are $(\phi_A\pm\phi_B)/\sqrt{2(1\pm s)}$; the precise energy ordering follows from the Hamiltonian matrix elements. This restricted orbital basis is an approximation to the molecule's wavefunction, rather than a claim that exact isolated-atom orbitals remain unchanged in the molecule.
