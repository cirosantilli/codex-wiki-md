<h1 id="section-a/5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

For a low [circular orbit](../../../../circular-orbit.md), [Newton's law of universal gravitation](../../../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation-split.md) gives $P=2\pi\sqrt{R^3/(GM)}$. Using $M=4\pi\bar\rho R^3/3$ yields

$$
\boxed{P^2=\frac{3\pi}{G\bar\rho}.}
$$

Thus **the Moon and Mars have roughly comparable [mean densities](../../../../mean-density.md)**: [low circular-orbit period measures mean density](../../../../low-circular-orbit-period-measures-mean-density.md). Equal periods would mean equal [mean densities](../../../../mean-density.md) in this approximation. This does not require their masses or [radii](../../../../radius.md) to be similar. The orbit altitudes must be small compared with the body [radii](../../../../radius.md), and the bodies are being treated as approximately spherical.
