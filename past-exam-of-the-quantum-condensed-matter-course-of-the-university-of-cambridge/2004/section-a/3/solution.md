<h1 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

In a [p-n junction](../../../../p-n-junction.md), [diffusion](../../../../diffusion.md) and [electron-hole recombination](../../../../electron-hole-recombination.md) near the contact leave a [depletion region](../../../../depletion-region.md) of ionized dopants. Its built-in [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) opposes further majority-carrier [diffusion](../../../../diffusion.md). At [thermodynamic equilibrium](../../../../thermodynamic-equilibrium.md) drift and [diffusion](../../../../diffusion.md) currents cancel. Forward bias lowers the electrostatic barrier and injects [electrons](../../../../electron.md) and [electron holes](../../../../electron-hole.md) across the junction; reverse bias raises the barrier and suppresses that majority-carrier injection. For an ideal diode below breakdown,

$$
\boxed{I=I_s\left(e^{eV/(k_BT)}-1\right).}
$$

Thus a modest forward voltage gives a rapidly increasing current, whereas reverse current remains approximately $-I_s$. This asymmetric current-voltage relation makes the junction a [rectifier](../../../../rectifier.md). Real leakage, [electron-hole recombination](../../../../electron-hole-recombination.md) and breakdown limit the ideal expression.
