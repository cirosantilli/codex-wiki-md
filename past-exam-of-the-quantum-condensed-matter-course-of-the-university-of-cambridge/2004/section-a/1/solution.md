↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The [radial distribution function](../../../../radial-distribution-function.md) is the conditional [number density](../../../../number-density.md) of neighbors at separation $r$, divided by the bulk [number density](../../../../number-density.md) $n$. In an isotropic three-dimensional liquid, the mean number in a thin spherical shell is $dN=4\pi nr^2g(r)dr$. Strong short-range repulsion makes $g(r)$ very small inside the effective particle core. A first maximum marks the nearest-neighbor coordination shell. More distant shells give weaker oscillations, which decay toward $g(r)=1$ as long-range positional correlations disappear. A typical simple liquid has no indefinitely repeated sharp lattice peaks.

The horizontal limit is one; the neighbor count additionally contains the spherical-shell factor $4\pi nr^2$.

The [static structure factor](../../../../static-structure-factor.md) measured by diffraction is related to the Fourier transform of $g(r)-1$. Thus the broad diffraction maxima of a liquid and the damped real-space coordination shells describe the same short-range organization.
