<h1 id="section-a/6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

The [gain bandwidth](../../../../gain-bandwidth.md) must be expressed as a [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) range:

$$
\Delta\nu=c\left(\frac1{600\,\mathrm{nm}}-\frac1{700\,\mathrm{nm}}\right)\simeq7.14\times10^{13}\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

The [gain bandwidth limit on a laser pulse](../../../../gain-bandwidth-limit-on-a-laser-pulse.md) then gives

$$
\boxed{\Delta t\sim\frac1{\Delta\nu}\simeq1.4\times10^{-14}\,\mathrm s,\quad\text{of order }10\,\mathrm{fs}.}
$$

The numerical coefficient depends on the pulse shape and width definition. For example a [Gaussian pulse](../../../../gaussian-pulse.md) whose intensity spectral FWHM actually filled this width would have a transform-limited intensity duration about $6.2\,\mathrm{fs}$. A hard gain interval does not by itself specify such a FWHM. Reaching the estimated scale requires coherent [mode locking](../../../../mode-locking.md) and control of [group velocity dispersion](../../../../group-velocity-dispersion.md); the cavity round-trip time determines the pulse separation. An [intensity autocorrelation](../../../../intensity-autocorrelation.md) measurement can estimate the pulse duration once its shape is specified.
