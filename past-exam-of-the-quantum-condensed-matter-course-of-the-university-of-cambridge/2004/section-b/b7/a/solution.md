<h1 id="section-b/b7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

This is the [Joule-Thomson coefficient](../../../../../joule-thomson-coefficient.md), $\mu_{JT}=(\partial T/\partial P)_H$, describing a [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) change during an isenthalpic [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) change. Here $H$ is [enthalpy](../../../../../enthalpy-split.md), $C_P=(\partial H/\partial T)_P$ is the [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md) at constant [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md), and $V$ is [volume](../../../../../volume.md), all for the same chosen amount of substance. Starting with $dH=T\,dS+V\,dP$ and using the [Gibbs free-energy Maxwell relation](../../../../../gibbs-free-energy-maxwell-relation.md) $(\partial S/\partial P)_T=-(\partial V/\partial T)_P$ gives

$$
dH=C_PdT+\left[V-T\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial T}\right)_P\right]dP.
$$

Setting $dH=0$ proves

$$
\boxed{\mu_{JT}=\frac1{C_P}\left[T\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial T}\right)_P-V\right].}
$$

In a steady insulated throttle or porous plug, negligible kinetic- and potential-energy changes make the upstream and downstream enthalpies equal. A positive coefficient gives cooling on a [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) decrease; a negative one gives warming. For an [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md), $V\propto T$ at fixed $P$, so the coefficient is zero. Real-gas interactions permit cooling and an inversion curve, making the effect useful in gas refrigeration and liquefaction.

The [measurement of the Joule-Thomson effect with a porous plug](../../../../../measuring-the-joule-thomson-effect-with-a-porous-plug.md) compares pressures and thermometers on the two sides of an insulated flow restriction. Suppressing external [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) exchange and separating the readings from high-speed jets are part of making the [enthalpy](../../../../../enthalpy-split.md) argument applicable; an arbitrary adiabatic expansion need not be isenthalpic.
