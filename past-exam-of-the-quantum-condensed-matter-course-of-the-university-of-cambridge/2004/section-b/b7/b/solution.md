<h1 id="section-b/b7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Clapeyron equation](../../../../../clausius-clapeyron-relation.md) gives the slope of a two-phase [phase coexistence curve](../../../../../phase-coexistence-curve.md). Let $s_l,s_v$ and $v_l,v_v$ be [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) and [volume](../../../../../volume.md) per molecule, or consistently per mole. Equality of the two [chemical potentials](../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) and $d\mu=-s\,dT+v\,dP$ imply

$$
0=d(\mu_v-\mu_l)=-(s_v-s_l)dT+(v_v-v_l)dP.
$$

At reversible coexistence the latent [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) in the same amount convention is $\mathcal L=T(s_v-s_l)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{dP}{dT}=\frac{\mathcal L}{T\Delta v},\qquad\Delta v=v_v-v_l.}
$$

The relation assumes [thermodynamic equilibrium](../../../../../thermodynamic-equilibrium.md) between two bulk phases of a pure substance, neglecting curvature and other work terms. For vaporization both latent [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) and [volume](../../../../../volume.md) change are positive, giving a positive vapor-pressure slope. If the vapor is approximately ideal and its [volume](../../../../../volume.md) dominates, the [ideal-vapor Clausius-Clapeyron approximation](../../../../../ideal-vapor-clausius-clapeyron-approximation.md) gives $d\ln P/dT\simeq\mathcal L/(k_BT^2)$ with latent [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) per molecule.

Experimentally, a sealed two-phase sample in a temperature-controlled bath supplies the coexistence [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) as [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) changes. Independent calorimetry and phase-density measurements supply $\mathcal L$ and $\Delta v$, allowing the [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) slope to test the equation. The formula connects those distinct measurements and is a practical route to latent heats.
