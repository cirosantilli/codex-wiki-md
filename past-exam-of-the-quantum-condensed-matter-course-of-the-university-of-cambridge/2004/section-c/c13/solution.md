<h1 id="section-c/c13/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C13](../c13.md)

For an isotropic free-electron gas at zero [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), the [Fermi wavevector](../../../../fermi-wavevector.md) is the [radius](../../../../radius.md) of the occupied region in [wavevector](../../../../wavevector.md) space. Its [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is $E_F=\hbar^2k_F^2/(2m)$. The first [Brillouin zone](../../../../brillouin-zone.md) is the Wigner-Seitz cell of the [reciprocal lattice](../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md); it contains one distinct crystal momentum per primitive cell and per band, with [spin](../../../../spin.md) counted separately.

For a two-dimensional sample of area $A$, allowed wavevectors have density $A/(2\pi)^2$. Including two [spin](../../../../spin.md) states, filling a circle gives $N=2[A/(2\pi)^2]\pi k_F^2$. Dividing by area therefore yields

$$
\boxed{k_F=\sqrt{2\pi n}.}
$$

The square lattice has one atom per area $a^2$, so for valence $z$ the free-electron density is $z/a^2$. Its [Brillouin zone](../../../../brillouin-zone.md) has $|k_x|,|k_y|\leq\pi/a$. Thus

$$
\boxed{k_F^{(1)}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}a\simeq8.36\times10^9\,\mathrm{m^{-1}},\qquad k_F^{(2)}=\frac{\sqrt{4\pi}}a\simeq1.18\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}.}
$$

The monovalent Fermi circle lies inside the square. The divalent circle crosses the faces but misses the corners; its area equals the zone's area. These are the free-electron contours before distortion by the periodic potential.

Original reciprocal-space diagrams in units of $\pi/a$. The divalent circle has the same area as the square zone but a different shape, extending beyond the faces while leaving corner regions outside the circle.

At a Bragg plane, the periodic potential mixes degenerate plane waves differing by a [reciprocal lattice vector](../../../../reciprocal-lattice-vector.md). The two standing-wave combinations have different charge densities relative to the ions and split in [energy](../../../../energy-split.md), opening the gap. In a two-wave approximation, the boundary energies are $E_B\pm E_g/2$, with $E_g=2|V_G|$.

The boundary [wavevector](../../../../wavevector.md) magnitude is $\pi/a$ along $[10]$ and $\sqrt2\pi/a$ along $[11]$. The free-electron boundary energies are therefore

$$
E_X=\frac{\hbar^2\pi^2}{2ma^2}\simeq4.18\,\mathrm{eV},\qquad E_M=2E_X\simeq8.36\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

Within the stated direction-independent gap picture, the upper band's lowest face-edge [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is approximately $E_X+2\,\mathrm{eV}=6.18\,\mathrm{eV}$, while the lower band's corner-edge [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is approximately $E_M-2\,\mathrm{eV}=6.36\,\mathrm{eV}$. There is consequently a small overlap, about $0.18\,\mathrm{eV}$, rather than a global insulating gap. The divalent Fermi level lies in that overlap, producing occupied upper-band states and unfilled lower-band states.

An equal local splitting can leave overlap between different zone-edge directions**. Original schematic two-wave dispersions near the $[10]$ and $[11]$ boundaries. The shaded Fermi-level range marks the overlap implied by the question's direction-independent gap approximation; no precise Fermi energy is inferred. These separate directional sketches do not specify a complete two-dimensional periodic potential.

Thus **the intended isotropic-gap approximation predicts a metal**, with small [electron](../../../../electron.md) and hole pockets. [Positive direct gaps can coexist with band overlap](../../../../positive-direct-gaps-can-coexist-with-band-overlap.md). A fully specified square-lattice potential would require simultaneous treatment of the corner degeneracies; a numerical boundary splitting alone does not uniquely determine an arbitrary two-dimensional band structure. The conclusion here uses the given simplified gap picture, rather than [electron](../../../../electron.md) count alone.
