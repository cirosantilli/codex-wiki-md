<h1 id="section-b/4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For a spherical [Fermi surface](../../../../../fermi-surface.md) inside a [primitive cubic lattice](../../../../../primitive-cubic-lattice.md) zone, the shortest reciprocal vector has magnitude $G=2\pi/a$. The nearest translated Fermi sphere is separated by the [electron-phonon Umklapp threshold](../../../../../electron-phonon-umklapp-threshold.md)

$$
q_U=G-2k_F=\frac{0.6\pi}{a}
\simeq3.77\times10^9\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}.
$$

A phonon of this wavevector can connect nearly opposite-velocity Fermi states using $\mathbf k+\mathbf q=\mathbf k'+\mathbf G$, transferring reciprocal-lattice momentum to the crystal and efficiently relaxing electrical current. With the supplied sound speed, its thermal scale is

$$
\boxed{T_U\sim\frac{\hbar v_s q_U}{k_B}
\simeq86\,\mathrm K,\quad\text{of order }10^2\,\mathrm K.}
$$

For $T\ll T_U$ such [phonons](../../../../../phonon.md) have exponentially small occupation. As they become populated, [Umklapp scattering](../../../../../umklapp-scattering.md) can make the [electrical conductivity](../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md) fall rapidly. Lower-energy normal scattering and residual disorder still give resistance below this scale; this is a crossover estimate, not a sharp transition. The numerical estimate extrapolates the linear acoustic dispersion to $q_U$ and assumes the spherical [Fermi surface](../../../../../fermi-surface.md) implicit in giving one $k_F$.

Using $2k_F$ alone as the phonon wavevector misses this mechanism: direct backscattering would need $1.4\pi/a$, beyond the first-zone edge along this direction, while the reciprocal-lattice transfer permits it with $0.6\pi/a$.
