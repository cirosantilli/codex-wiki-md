<h1 id="section-b/4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $\epsilon_B=\hbar^2g^2/(8m)$ and take $V_0>0$ as the coupling magnitude. The dispersion becomes

$$
E_\pm(q)=\epsilon_B+\frac{\hbar^2q^2}{2m}
\pm\sqrt{V_0^2+\frac{\hbar^4g^2q^2}{4m^2}}.
$$

Expanding the square root at the boundary gives

$$
E_\pm(q)=\epsilon_B\pm V_0
+\frac{\hbar^2q^2}{2m}\left(1\pm\frac{2\epsilon_B}{V_0}\right)+O(q^4).
$$

Using $1/m^*=\hbar^{-2}d^2E/dq^2$, the [band-edge effective mass in a weak periodic potential](../../../../../band-edge-effective-mass-in-a-weak-periodic-potential.md) is

$$
\boxed{\frac{m_\pm^*}{m}=\frac1{1\pm2\epsilon_B/V_0}
\simeq\pm\frac{V_0}{2\epsilon_B}\qquad(V_0\ll\epsilon_B).}
$$

The requested expression is the leading weak-potential limit. The upper band has positive [band curvature](../../../../../band-curvature.md) and small positive [effective mass](../../../../../band-effective-mass.md); the lower band has negative curvature at its maximum and can instead be described by positive-mass [electron holes](../../../../../electron-hole.md). The small-$q$ expansion additionally requires $\hbar^2g|q|/(2m)\ll V_0$; its validity interval shrinks with the gap.
