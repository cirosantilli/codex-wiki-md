<h1 id="section-b/4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the [nearly-free electron model](../../../../../nearly-free-electron-model.md), a weak [periodic potential](../../../../../periodic-potential.md) has Fourier components $V_{\mathbf G}$ that couple [plane waves](../../../../../plane-wave.md) at $\mathbf k$ and $\mathbf k-\mathbf G$. Far from a degeneracy their admixture gives small perturbative energy shifts. At a Bragg plane, however,

$$
\epsilon_{\mathbf k}=\epsilon_{\mathbf k-\mathbf G},
$$

so [degenerate perturbation theory](../../../../../degenerate-perturbation-theory.md) is required. In one dimension the first such crossing is at $k=\pm G/2$, the boundary of the first [Brillouin zone](../../../../../brillouin-zone.md).

Within the degenerate pair the eigenvalues are $\epsilon_{G/2}\pm|V_G|$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{energy gap at the Bragg crossing}=2|V_G|.}
$$

The eigenstates are standing-wave combinations of counterpropagating waves. Their charge densities sit differently relative to the ionic potential, giving different potential energies and removing the free-electron degeneracy. The resulting [avoided crossing](../../../../../avoided-crossing.md) produces continuous allowed [energy bands](../../../../../energy-band.md) separated by a forbidden interval. A Fourier component or symmetry-allowed coupling is needed: geometry alone does not require every possible gap to be nonzero.
