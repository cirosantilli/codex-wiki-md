The large effective field primarily represents [exchange interactions](../../../../../exchange-interaction.md), which arise from Coulomb interactions, electronic hopping and the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md). It is not usually the much smaller classical magnetic dipole field of neighbouring ions. [Direct exchange](../../../../../direct-exchange.md) involves overlapping magnetic-ion orbitals. [Superexchange](../../../../../superexchange.md) instead uses virtual charge transfer through a ligand while real charge transport remains gapped, allowing substantial magnetic order in an [electrical insulator](../../../../../electrical-insulator.md).

Use dimensionless spin operators $\mathbf S_i$ with eigenvalue $S=1/2$, and count each bond once. One consistent [Heisenberg model](../../../../../heisenberg-model.md) convention is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal H=\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}J_{ij}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j
-g_J\mu_B\sum_i\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf S_i.}
$$

Then $J_{ij}<0$ favours a [Heisenberg ferromagnet](../../../../../heisenberg-ferromagnet.md), while $J_{ij}>0$ favours a [Heisenberg antiferromagnet](../../../../../heisenberg-antiferromagnet.md). Replacing neighbouring spins by their mean values gives the effective field

$$
\mathbf B_{E,i}=-\frac1{g_J\mu_B}\sum_jJ_{ij}\langle\mathbf S_j\rangle.
$$

For $z$ equivalent ferromagnetic neighbours with $J_{ij}=-J_F<0$ and uniform order, $M=ng_J\mu_B\langle S_z\rangle$, so

$$
\boxed{\mu_0\lambda=\frac{zJ_F}{n(g_J\mu_B)^2},\qquad
k_BT_C=\frac{zJ_F}{4}\quad(g_J=2,\ S=1/2).}
$$

The last equality is the same molecular-field result derived above, not an exact transition temperature for every lattice.

Microscopically, a half-filled orbital pair with virtual hopping $t_{ij}$ and a large on-site repulsion $U$ permits an energy lowering of the singlet but not the parallel-spin triplet, yielding antiferromagnetic $J\simeq4|t_{ij}|^2/U$ in this convention. Ligand-mediated hopping is the analogous mechanism in many ionic insulators. Different orbital occupancies and nearly orthogonal ligand paths can suppress this antiferromagnetic term and permit ferromagnetic exchange to dominate. Thus the sign depends on electronic structure and bonding geometry, not simply whether the sample conducts.

For antiferromagnetic coupling on a bipartite lattice, the field on each sublattice is proportional to the opposite sublattice [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md), with opposite alignment preferred. Two coupled self-consistency equations then permit $M_A=-M_B\ne0$ and vanishing total [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md): the order parameter is staggered. The single positive-$\lambda$ uniform equation describes ferromagnetism and must be extended to these sublattices to describe an [antiferromagnet](../../../../../antiferromagnetism.md).
