<h1 id="section-b/5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For interface area $\mathcal A$, each resolved spin branch has one state per flux increment $h/e$, so its [degeneracy of a Landau level](../../../../../degeneracy-of-a-landau-level.md) is

$$
N_\phi=\frac{B\mathcal A}{h/e},\qquad \frac{N_\phi}{\mathcal A}=\frac{eB}{h}.
$$

If $\nu$ such branches are occupied, including fractional occupation of the highest, the [filling factor](../../../../../filling-factor-quantum-hall-effect.md) gives

$$
\boxed{n_s=\frac{N}{\mathcal A}=\nu\frac{eB}{h}.}
$$

The filling counts spin and any other internal branches separately; it is an integer when a whole number of resolved levels are full.

From the visible PDF axes, approximately ten unsplit periods lie between $1/B=1.0$ and $2.5\,\mathrm{T^{-1}}$, giving $\Delta(1/B)\simeq0.15\,\mathrm{T^{-1}}$. Consecutive unsplit orbital peaks differ by two occupied spin branches, so [carrier density from spin-degenerate quantum oscillations](../../../../../carrier-density-from-spin-degenerate-quantum-oscillations.md) gives

$$
\boxed{n_s=\frac{2e}{h\Delta(1/B)}
\simeq3.2\times10^{15}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}
=3.2\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}.}
$$

The precision is limited by reading the plotted trace, roughly a few percent. The factor two assumes only spin remains unresolved in that part of the graph; counting individual spin-split peaks would instead use a one-branch filling increment.
