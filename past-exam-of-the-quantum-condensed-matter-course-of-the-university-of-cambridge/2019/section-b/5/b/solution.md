<h1 id="section-b/5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

As $B$ changes, each quantized orbit area grows linearly with field while the [Fermi surface](../../../../../fermi-surface.md) cross-section stays approximately fixed. Landau energies repeatedly cross the [Fermi energy](../../../../../fermi-energy.md), making the [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md) there oscillate. If the relevant extremal orbit has area $S_F$, its crossings satisfy

$$
\frac1{B_n}=\frac{2\pi e}{\hbar S_F}\left(n+\frac12\right),\qquad
\boxed{\Delta(1/B)=\frac{2\pi e}{\hbar S_F}.}
$$

Thus the oscillations are periodic in inverse field rather than field. In three dimensions integration over longitudinal momentum makes extremal cross-sections dominate.

One observable is the [de Haas-van Alphen effect](../../../../../de-haas-van-alphen-effect.md): the field-dependent occupation changes the electronic free energy, so its field derivative, the [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md), oscillates. The corresponding oscillations of longitudinal [electrical resistance](../../../../../electrical-resistance.md) are the [Shubnikov-de Haas effect](../../../../../shubnikov-de-haas-effect.md). Both probe Fermi-surface geometry.
