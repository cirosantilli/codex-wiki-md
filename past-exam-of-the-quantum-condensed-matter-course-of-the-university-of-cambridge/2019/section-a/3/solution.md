<h1 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The [Moiré pattern](../../../../moire-pattern.md) sets a [superlattice](../../../../superlattice.md) period

$$
L=50a=12.5\,\mathrm{nm},\qquad G=\frac{2\pi}{L}.
$$

For the stipulated scalar one-dimensional model, right- and left-moving states have $E(k)=\hbar v|k|$. The $n$th Bragg crossing satisfies $E(k)=E(k-nG)$ and hence $k=nG/2$. The [Bragg gap ladder for a linear dispersion](../../../../bragg-gap-ladder-for-a-linear-dispersion.md) therefore has centres

$$
E_n\simeq\frac{n\pi\hbar v}{L},\qquad
\boxed{\Delta E\simeq\frac{\pi\hbar v}{L}=\frac{hv}{2L}
\simeq0.165\,\mathrm{eV}\approx0.17\,\mathrm{eV}.}
$$

This is the spacing of successive possible Bragg gaps. The corresponding atomic-lattice scale is $hv/(2a)\simeq8.27\,\mathrm{eV}$, fifty times larger.

Shifting a linear branch by one full reciprocal period $G$ instead gives $\hbar vG\simeq0.331\,\mathrm{eV}$, twice the consecutive Bragg-threshold spacing; the first zone boundary is at $G/2$. These scales should not be confused with the gap width, which depends on the weak potential's [Fourier coefficients](../../../../fourier-coefficient.md). The geometry alone does not guarantee equally wide or even observable gaps at every threshold, nor an exactly periodic full spectrum. The estimate neglects graphene's additional pseudospin selection rules and interlayer correlations, as required by the scalar-chain approximation.
