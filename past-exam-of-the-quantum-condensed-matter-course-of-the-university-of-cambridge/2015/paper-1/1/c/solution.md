<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A hydrogenic donor has the [effective Bohr radius of a hydrogenic donor](../../../../../effective-bohr-radius-of-a-hydrogenic-donor.md)

$$
\boxed{a_B^*=\epsilon_r\frac{m_e}{m^*}a_0=80a_0=4.23\,\mathrm{nm},}
$$

using the external $a_0=0.52917721067\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm m$.[Bohr radius](../../../../../codata-recommended-values-of-the-fundamental-physical-constants-2014-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology.md) Increasing donor density causes donor states to overlap and screens their binding, allowing a metallic impurity system rather than isolated frozen-out donors.

Using the standard approximate [Mott criterion](../../../../../mott-criterion.md), $n_D^{1/3}a_B^*\simeq0.25$, gives

$$
\boxed{n_{D,c}\sim\left(\frac{0.25}{a_B^*}\right)^3\simeq2.1\times10^{23}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}=2.1\times10^{17}\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}}.}
$$

The factor 0.25 is an externally documented empirical criterion, not supplied exam data or an exact universal threshold.[Abstract and discussion of the effective-Bohr-radius criterion](../../../../../the-mott-criterion-so-simple-and-yet-so-complex-by-alexander-pergament-and-others.md) An order-of-magnitude overlap argument without that prefactor would give $(a_B^*)^{-3}\simeq1.3\times10^{25}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$. Compensation, disorder and multivalley anisotropy affect a real material's transition; this estimate uses the scalar mass and permittivity actually supplied, without substituting other germanium parameters.
