↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the isotropic parabolic-band approximation implied by the single supplied curvature, the [hole mass from a valence-band maximum](../../../../../hole-mass-from-a-valence-band-maximum.md) is

$$
m_h^*=\frac{\hbar^2}{|E''(0)|}=4.56\times10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}\simeq0.500\,m_e.
$$

The [hole](../../../../../electron-hole.md) has charge $+e$, so its cyclotron angular frequency is

$$
\boxed{\omega_c=\frac{eB}{m_h^*}=3.52\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

If “frequency” means cycles per second, **$f_c=\omega_c/(2\pi)=5.59\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{Hz}$**. The curvature unit is $\mathrm{J\,m^2}$, so $\hbar^2/E''$ has mass units. The external constants used are $\hbar=1.054571800\times10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}$, $e=1.6021766208\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ and $m_e=9.10938356\times10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$.[Reduced Planck constant, elementary charge and electron mass](../../../../../codata-recommended-values-of-the-fundamental-physical-constants-2014-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology.md) A genuinely anisotropic band would require the cyclotron mass for the orbit plane, not a single arbitrary directional curvature.
