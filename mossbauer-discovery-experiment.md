<h1 id="mossbauer-discovery-experiment">Mössbauer discovery experiment</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Mössbauer effect](mossbauer-effect-split.md)

[Rudolf Ludwig Mössbauer](rudolf-ludwig-mossbauer.md) found the effect during his Heidelberg work in 1957.[CV, opening paragraphs: Heidelberg discovery in 1957](rudolf-ludwig-mossbauer-cv-by-lindau-nobel-laureate-meetings.md) His 1961 lecture _Recoilless nuclear resonance absorption of gamma radiation_ describes crystalline preparations using the $129\,\mathrm{keV}$ transition of the [iridium](iridium.md) [isotope](isotope.md) $^{191}\mathrm{Ir}$. Separate cryostats, lead collimation and a [particle scintillation](scintillation-physics.md) detector measured transmission. Liquid-air cooling of both preparations enhanced absorption; cooling only the source against a room-temperature absorber slightly reduced it. A rotating source cryostat supplied nearly constant projected velocities over selected arcs. Centimetres per second suppressed absorption relative to zero [velocity](velocity.md); a nonresonant absorber supplied the comparison. This tested a narrow common emission/absorption [energy](energy-split.md) beyond the detector's resolution. Temperature data alone had not established its width. The lecture cites the initial and direct-verification publications from 1958.[Authorized 2012 reprint, pp. 6–8, 13–15, 17; Figs. 1.3–1.4, 1.7–1.8](recoilless-nuclear-resonance-absorption-of-gamma-radiation-by-rudolf-ludwig-mossbauer.md)
