# Spectroscopic wavenumber

↑ **Parent:** [Wavenumber](wavenumber.md)

A [spectroscopic wavenumber](spectroscopic-wavenumber.md) commonly means the reciprocal of the vacuum [wavelength](wavelength.md) $\lambda_0$ of [electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation.md), often in $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$. It is an ordinary [spatial frequency](spatial-frequency.md), so the vacuum angular [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) is $K_0=2\pi\tilde\nu$. Specify vacuum or in-medium [wavelength](wavelength.md): the same [frequency](frequency.md) can have different [wavelengths](wavelength.md) in different media. IUPAC's “wavenumber in medium” entry defines the in-medium reciprocal $1/\lambda$ and notes that unqualified spectroscopic usage often means the vacuum reciprocal.[wavenumber in medium](wavenumber-in-medium.md)

For example, a vacuum [wavelength](wavelength.md) of $500\,\mathrm{nm}=5\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{cm}$ corresponds to $\tilde\nu=20000\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}=2\times10^6\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. Multiplication by $2\pi$ gives $K_0=4\pi\times10^6\,\mathrm{rad\,m^{-1}}$. Converting centimetres to metres and converting cycles to [radians](radian.md) are separate operations.
