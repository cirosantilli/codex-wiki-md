# Symmetric part of an orthogonal matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Symmetric part of a matrix](symmetric-part-of-a-matrix.md)

For a real [orthogonal matrix](orthogonal-matrix.md) $A$, $A^{\mathsf T}=A^{-1}$. If $Av=\lambda v$ for a nonzero complex [eigenvector](eigenvector.md), then $A^{\mathsf T}v=\lambda^{-1}v$. Therefore the same [eigenvector](eigenvector.md) has [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) $(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})/2$ for the [symmetric part of a matrix](symmetric-part-of-a-matrix.md) $(A+A^{\mathsf T})/2$. Orthogonality implies $|\lambda|=1$, so writing $\lambda=e^{i\theta}$ gives $\cos\theta$. The complete complex [orthonormal eigenbasis](orthonormal-eigenbasis.md) of an [orthogonal matrix](orthogonal-matrix.md) shows that all the [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) of its symmetric part lie in $[-1,1]$.
