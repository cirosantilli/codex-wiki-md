# Weak-field Zeeman splitting

↑ **Parent:** [Zeeman splitting](zeeman-splitting.md)

Use the leading nonrelativistic magnetic coupling for an electronic level with total [angular momentum operator](angular-momentum-operator-split.md) $\mathbf J$, and choose the $z$ axis along a uniform external [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) $\mathbf B=B\hat{\mathbf z}$, $B\ge0$. Write its [magnetic quantum number](magnetic-quantum-number.md) as $m_J$, so $J_z|J,m_J\rangle=\hbar m_J|J,m_J\rangle$. There are $2J+1$ possibilities from $-J$ to $J$. With [Landé g-factor](lande-g-factor.md) $g_J$, the first-order shift is

$$
\Delta E=\mu_Bg_Jm_JB,\qquad
\mu_B=\frac{e\hbar}{2m_e},\qquad
\Delta\nu_{ul}=\frac{\mu_BB}{h}(g_um_u-g_lm_l).
$$

Here $e>0$ is the magnitude of the [electron](electron.md) [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md), $m_e$ its [mass](mass.md), $h$ the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md), and $\mu_B$ the [Bohr magneton](bohr-magneton.md). Both $g_J$ and $m_J$ are dimensionless; with $B$ measured in [tesla](tesla-unit.md) and $\mu_B$ in [joules](joule.md) per [tesla](tesla-unit.md), $\Delta E$ is in [joules](joule.md). The [photon](photon.md) [frequency](frequency.md) shift $\Delta\nu_{ul}$ is in [hertz](hertz.md), not [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md); indices $u,l$ denote upper and lower levels. NIST states this weak-field convention and its orbital/spin assumptions explicitly.[Atomic Spectroscopy - A Compendium of Basic Ideas, Notation, Data, and Formulas by William C. Martin and Wolfgang Wiese; Atomic Spectroscopy, §12, Eqs. (6)–(8): magnetic-sublevel shift, tesla convention and magnetic splitting factor](atomic-spectroscopy-a-compendium-of-basic-ideas-notation-data-and-formulas-by-william-c-martin-and-wolfgang-wiese/12-zeeman-effect.md)

The sign follows directly from [magnetic dipole potential energy](magnetic-dipole-potential-energy.md). Within this electronic $J$ multiplet, the projected [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) is $\boldsymbol\mu=-g_J\mu_B\mathbf J/\hbar$. Therefore $H_Z=-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B$ has the displayed positive coefficient. Subtracting the shifts of the two levels and using [photon](photon.md) [energy](energy-split.md) $h\nu_{ul}=E_u-E_l$ gives the transition formula. For the orbital case, $\mu_B/h=e/(4\pi m_e)$ gives the same leading [frequency](frequency.md) spacing as the classical charged oscillator. This explains the historical sensitivity to charge divided by [mass](mass.md); the [quantum state](quantum-state.md) derivation here is modern.

“Weak” compares interaction [energies](energy-split.md): mixing with neighbouring [fine structure](fine-structure.md) levels must be small, so $J$ remains a useful [quantum number](quantum-number.md). The formula keeps the term linear in $B$ and neglects quadratic shifts. It also omits [hyperfine structure](hyperfine-structure.md); if that is resolved and controls the coupling, the appropriate total electronic-plus-nuclear [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) and splitting factor must replace $J,g_J$. Weak compared with [fine structure](fine-structure.md) does not automatically mean weak compared with [hyperfine structure](hyperfine-structure.md).
