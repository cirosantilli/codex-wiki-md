# Measure space

↑ **Parent:** [Measure](measure.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_space)

A measure space is a [set](set-split.md) $X$, a [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md) $\mathcal M$ of its [subsets](subset.md), and a [measure](measure.md) $\mu$ defined on $\mathcal M$. Specifying only $X$ leaves both the measurable questions and their weights undetermined.

For a concrete [probability space](probability-space.md), take $X=\{a,b,c\}$, $\mathcal M$ its [power set](power-set.md), and weights $1/2,1/3,1/6$. Define $\mu(A)$ by adding the weights of the elements in $A$. Then

$$
\mu(X)=\frac12+\frac13+\frac16=1,\qquad
\mu(\{b,c\})=\frac12.
$$

A [probability measure](probability-measure.md) is exactly a [measure](measure.md) normalized by $\mu(X)=1$, usually written $\mathbb P(\Omega)=1$. An [indicator function](indicator-function.md) has [expected value](expected-value.md) equal to its [event](event.md)'s [probability](probability.md). For a different continuous example, $[0,1]$ with the restricted [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) is a [probability space](probability-space.md); the [event](event.md) $[0,1/4]$ has [probability](probability.md) $1/4$.
