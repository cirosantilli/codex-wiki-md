<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The first-order excitation amplitude is the [Fourier transform](../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) of the temporal pulse envelope evaluated at the [Rabi detuning](../../../../rabi-detuning.md). Two rectangular time windows therefore give exactly the mathematical structure of [double-slit interference](../../../../double-slit-interference.md): each window gives a [sinc function](../../../../sinc-function.md) amplitude, and their separation gives a relative [quantum phase](../../../../quantum-phase.md) $\delta T$. The [transition probability](../../../../transition-probability.md) is a [sinc function](../../../../sinc-function.md) squared envelope multiplied by $\cos^2(\delta T/2)$.

Pulse duration corresponds to slit width, pulse-start separation to slit-center separation, and [Rabi detuning](../../../../rabi-detuning.md) to the transverse [wavevector](../../../../wavevector.md) sampled on the screen. The narrow fringes measure the long separation, while the broad envelope measures the short interaction duration. As in spatial [interference](../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md), adding [probabilities](../../../../probability.md) instead of coherent [probability amplitudes](../../../../probability-amplitude.md) would lose the fringes. Randomizing the relative pulse phase averages the cosine squared to $1/2$.
