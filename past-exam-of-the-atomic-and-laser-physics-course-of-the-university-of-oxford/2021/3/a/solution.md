<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Specify the radiation normalization first. Let $u_\nu$ be [radiation energy density](../../../../radiation-energy-density.md) per ordinary [frequency](../../../../frequency.md), with SI units $\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}\,Hz^{-1}}$. Then $A_{21}$ is the [spontaneous emission](../../../../spontaneous-emission.md) rate per upper-state [chemical atom](../../../../atom-physics.md), while $B_{12}u_\nu$ and $B_{21}u_\nu$ are the induced [absorption](../../../../absorption-electromagnetic-radiation.md) and [stimulated emission](../../../../stimulated-emission.md) rates, evaluated at a sharp transition [frequency](../../../../frequency.md). For a finite [spectral line profile](../../../../spectral-line-profile.md), replace $u_\nu$ by its profile-weighted average.

The [Einstein coefficients](../../../../einstein-coefficients.md) obey

$$
\boxed{g_1B_{12}=g_2B_{21},\qquad
\frac{A_{21}}{B_{21}}=\frac{8\pi h\nu_0^3}{c^3}.}
$$

Here $g_i$ are the [statistical weights](../../../../statistical-weight.md) of the two [energy levels](../../../../energy-level.md), and the field is unpolarized and isotropic. The units and [physical dimensions](../../../../physical-dimension.md) are

$$
[A]=\mathrm{s^{-1}},\quad [A]=\mathsf T^{-1};\qquad
[B]=\mathrm{m^3\,J^{-1}\,s^{-2}},\quad [B]=\mathsf L\mathsf M^{-1}.
$$

The apparently time-independent dimension of $B$ follows because a per-[hertz](../../../../hertz.md) density includes an extra unit of time. If instead $B$ multiplies the [mean intensity](../../../../mean-intensity.md) $J_\nu$, use $B^{(J)}=(4\pi/c)B$ and $A/B^{(J)}=2h\nu_0^3/c^2$. These distinct normalizations must not be mixed in the [radiative transfer equation](../../../../radiative-transfer-equation.md).
