<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In the idealized isolated-[carbon](../../../../carbon.md) [chemical atom](../../../../atom-physics.md) interpretation, both beams carry approximately $2.68\,\mathrm{eV}$ per incident particle, close to the ${}^1S_0$ threshold. However **equal incident [energies](../../../../energy-split.md) do not imply equal excitation channels**.

A single optical [photon](../../../../photon.md) cannot strongly excite any of the listed even-[parity](../../../../parity.md) $2p^2$ [energy levels](../../../../energy-level.md) from the even-[parity](../../../../parity.md) ${}^3P_0$ ground state through an [electric dipole transition](../../../../electric-dipole-transition.md). In particular ${}^3P_0\to{}^1S_0$ also violates $\Delta S=0$ and the $J=0\leftrightarrow0$ restriction. The lowest strong opposite-[parity](../../../../parity.md) excitations lie well above $2.68\,\mathrm{eV}$. Thus one expects no strong resonant [absorption](../../../../absorption-electromagnetic-radiation.md) or corresponding strong atomic fluorescence from these ground-state [chemical atoms](../../../../atom-physics.md) at this optical [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md); weak higher-order processes and scattering are separate effects.

Incident [electrons](../../../../electron.md) can undergo [inelastic scattering](../../../../inelastic-scattering.md) and [collisional excitation](../../../../collisional-excitation.md). Their [orbital angular momentum](../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) and exchange with bound [electrons](../../../../electron.md) allow transfers excluded for a single [electric dipole](../../../../electric-dipole.md) [photon](../../../../photon.md). The ${}^1D_2$ [energy level](../../../../energy-level.md) at $1.264\,\mathrm{eV}$ and the low [fine structure](../../../../fine-structure.md) excitations are energetically accessible. One therefore expects scattered [electrons](../../../../electron.md) with discrete [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) losses, possible weak forbidden [spectral lines](../../../../spectral-line.md), and collisional or surface relaxation. For example ${}^1D_2\to{}^3P_2$ has [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) approximately $10^7/(10192-43.4)=985\,\mathrm{nm}$; spin mixing supplies the weak spin-forbidden radiative channel.

There is a numerical threshold caveat. Literally,

$$
E({}^1S_0)=21648hc\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}\simeq2.68401\,\mathrm{eV},\quad
E_\gamma(462.6\,\mathrm{nm})\simeq2.68016\,\mathrm{eV},\quad E_e=2.68000\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

Neither quoted beam reaches ${}^1S_0$ from the exact ground state if these are taken as exact monoenergetic values. The [electron](../../../../electron.md) value rounded to two decimal places could represent an actual [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) above threshold; if so, ${}^1S_0$ is collisionally accessible and its decay to ${}^1D_2$ produces the weak [electric quadrupole transition](../../../../electric-quadrupole-transition.md) at $10^7/11456\simeq873\,\mathrm{nm}$, followed by lower-level relaxation. These emissions require populations and sufficiently low [collisional quenching of a metastable line](../../../../collisional-quenching-of-a-metastable-line.md).

An actual solid [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) foil has bands, collective excitations and surface losses, so it need not reproduce the isolated-[chemical atom](../../../../atom-physics.md) spectrum or selection rules. Its optical and electron-induced emission cannot be predicted quantitatively from the five atomic [energy levels](../../../../energy-level.md) alone. This is the limitation of the foil wording in the source.
