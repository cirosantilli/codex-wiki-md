<h1 id="4/e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [finite nuclear size correction](../../../../../finite-nuclear-size-correction.md) perturbation is unchanged for the same [lead](../../../../../lead.md) [atomic nucleus](../../../../../atomic-nucleus-split.md). Its leading effect on one occupied $1s$ [orbital](../../../../../single-particle-orbital.md) is still proportional to that orbital's contact density,

$$
\boxed{\Delta E_{1s}\simeq\frac{2\pi}{5}k_ZR^2|\psi_{1s}(0)|^2.}
$$

Other [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) screen the nuclear attraction and alter the self-consistent $1s$ [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md), usually reducing its contact density and hence its positive shift relative to the bare [hydrogen-like ion](../../../../../hydrogen-like-ion.md). The reduction is modest for a deep $1s$ [orbital](../../../../../single-particle-orbital.md): it penetrates well inside most other shells and still samples nearly the full $Z=82$ attraction. Replacing $Z$ by the neutral atom's asymptotic charge one would be inappropriate for this core [orbital](../../../../../single-particle-orbital.md).

A neutral [lead](../../../../../lead.md) atom has two $1s$ [electrons](../../../../../electron.md). Their contributions add in a frozen-orbital estimate of total atomic [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), roughly twice the per-[electron](../../../../../electron.md) shift, with relaxation and correlation corrections. An experimentally observed transition [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) again depends on the difference between the finite-size shifts of both participating many-[electron](../../../../../electron.md) [quantum states](../../../../../quantum-state.md). A quantitative neutral-[lead](../../../../../lead.md) result requires a screened relativistic many-[electron](../../../../../electron.md) calculation, not the one-[electron](../../../../../electron.md) formula with a single universally valid effective charge.
