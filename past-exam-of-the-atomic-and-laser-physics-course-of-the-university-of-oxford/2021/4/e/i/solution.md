<h1 id="4/e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A nonrelativistic $2p$ [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) vanishes linearly at the origin. Its probability density is therefore proportional to $r^2$, so its first-order [finite nuclear size correction](../../../../../finite-nuclear-size-correction.md) shift scales as $R^4$, rather than the $R^2$ result for $1s$. It is positive but much smaller.

More explicitly, the normalized [hydrogen-like ion](../../../../../hydrogen-like-ion.md) radial function obeys $R_{21}^2(r)\simeq Z^5r^2/(24a_0^5)$ near the origin. Since the [spherical harmonic](../../../../../spherical-harmonic.md) has unit angular norm,

$$
\Delta E_{2p}\simeq\frac{k_ZZ^5}{24a_0^5}\int_0^R\left(r^3-\frac{3r^4}{2R}+\frac{r^6}{2R^3}\right)dr
=\frac{k_ZZ^5R^4}{1120a_0^5}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\frac{\Delta E_{2p}}{\Delta E_{1s}}\simeq\frac1{448}\left(\frac{ZR}{a_0}\right)^2\simeq2.71\times10^{-7}.}
$$

This is about $2.4\,\mu\mathrm{eV}$ in the nonrelativistic model. At large $Z$, the relativistic $2p_{1/2}$ [Dirac spinor](../../../../../dirac-spinor.md) has an $s$-type small component and a much stronger nuclear sensitivity than this nonrelativistic argument predicts; $2p_{3/2}$ remains much more suppressed. Neglecting [spin-orbit interaction](../../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md) corrections would miss that distinction.
