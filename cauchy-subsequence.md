# Cauchy subsequence

↑ **Parent:** [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md)

A Cauchy subsequence is a [subsequence](subsequence.md) that is a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md). Every [subsequence](subsequence.md) of a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md) is a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md), because its increasing indices eventually lie beyond any index required by the original pairwise bound. Every [convergent subsequence](convergent-subsequence.md) is also a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md).

The converse about the original [sequence](sequence.md) fails: $a_n=(-1)^n$ has a constant [subsequence](subsequence.md) at its [even integers](even-number.md), hence a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md), while $a_n$ itself is not a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md). If the original [sequence](sequence.md) is already a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md) and one [subsequence](subsequence.md) converges to $L$, however, the whole [sequence](sequence.md) converges to $L$. Choose a late subsequence term close to $L$ and use the [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md) to compare it with every sufficiently late original term. [MIT 18.100A, Lecture 10, Theorem 6: a convergent subsequence of a Cauchy sequence](https://www.ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-100a-real-analysis-fall-2020/mit18_100af20_lec102.pdf).
