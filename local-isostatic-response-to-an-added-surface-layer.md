# Local isostatic response to an added surface layer

↑ **Parent:** [Isostasy](isostasy.md)

Let a deposited layer have thickness $H$ and [density](density.md) $\rho_b$, replacing a fluid of [density](density.md) $\rho_f$, while the original [crust](planetary-crust.md) translates downward by $w$ above [mantle](planetary-mantle.md) of [density](density.md) $\rho_m$. Assume all deposited material stays in the same fluid environment, unchanged pre-existing layer thicknesses, an external material source, and complete local [isostatic adjustment](isostatic-adjustment.md) without bending resistance.

At a fixed [compensation depth](compensation-depth.md), added column [mass](mass.md) is $(\rho_b-\rho_f)H$ per unit area, whereas depression replaces a column contrast $(\rho_m-\rho_f)w$. Equal [hydrostatic pressure](hydrostatic-pressure.md) therefore requires

$$
(\rho_b-\rho_f)gH=(\rho_m-\rho_f)gw.
$$

It follows that

$$
w=\frac{\rho_b-\rho_f}{\rho_m-\rho_f}H,\qquad
\Delta z=H-w=\frac{\rho_m-\rho_b}{\rho_m-\rho_f}H.
$$

The pre-existing ground subsides by $w$, while the top of the new layer rises by $\Delta z$ if $\rho_b<\rho_m$. For a subaerial deposit, air [density](density.md) is negligible. For a fully submerged deposit, use [seawater](seawater.md) [density](density.md). A deposit that crosses the fluid surface requires separate treatment of its submerged and emerged portions. Specifying only volume $V$ leaves the answer dependent on loaded area through $H=V/A$.
