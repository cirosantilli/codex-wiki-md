# Western blot transfer

↑ **Parent:** [Western blot](western-blot.md)

[Western blot transfer](western-blot-transfer.md) moves separated [proteins](protein.md) out of the gel onto a [Western blot membrane](western-blot-membrane.md), using an [electric field](electric-field.md) across the gel thickness. Maintaining close contact preserves the lane pattern. Contact gaps, incomplete removal from the gel and passage through the receiving sheet can change how much [protein](protein.md) is retained.

The original 1979 experiment pressed a wetted, $0.45\,\mu\mathrm m$-pore [nitrocellulose Western blot membrane](nitrocellulose-western-blot-membrane.md) against the gel between scouring pads and plastic pipette-tray grids, held by elastic bands. The [urea](urea.md)-gel assembly faced the [cathode](cathode.md) in acidic solution, with $6\,\mathrm{V/cm}$ applied for one hour; the [SDS-PAGE](sds-page.md) variant reversed polarity and used a [glycine](glycine.md)/[methanol](methanol.md) [buffer solution](buffer-solution.md). Stained gel/blot pairs checked pattern preservation. Corresponding labelled [ribosome](ribosome.md) [protein](protein.md) spots from two gels were compared through [radioactive decay](radioactive-decay.md) readouts after combustion. The stained gel remaining after transfer supplied a removal check; a second receiving sheet revealed breakthrough on overloading. These tests supported recovery for the examined [urea](urea.md)-gel [proteins](protein.md), within experimental variation, while the [SDS-PAGE](sds-page.md) results showed unequal staining recovery. Preserved band positions therefore did not establish universal quantitative transfer.
