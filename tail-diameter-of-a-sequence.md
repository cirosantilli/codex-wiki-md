# Tail diameter of a sequence

↑ **Parent:** [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md)

The tail diameter of a [sequence](sequence.md) in a [metric space](metric-space.md) is $D_N=\sup\{d(x_m,x_n):m,n\geq N\}$, allowing $+\infty$. The bounds are nonincreasing as $N$ grows because fewer pairs remain. A [sequence](sequence.md) is a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md) exactly when $D_N\to0$. The [supremum](supremum.md) need not be attained: for $q_n=1-2^{-n}$, $D_N=2^{-N}$ even though no two finite terms achieve that difference. For $(-1)^n$, $D_N=2$ for every $N$. For the [harmonic numbers](harmonic-number.md), $D_N=+\infty$ for every $N$, because each remaining tail is unbounded.
