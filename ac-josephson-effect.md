# AC Josephson effect

↑ **Parent:** [Josephson effect](josephson-effect.md)

For constant nonzero voltage $V$, direct integration gives

$$
\phi(t)=\phi_0+\frac{2eV}{\hbar}t,\qquad
I_s(t)=I_c\sin\!\left(\phi_0+\frac{2eV}{\hbar}t\right),\qquad
f_J=\frac{2e|V|}{h}.
$$

A constant [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) therefore produces an oscillating pair [electric current](electric-current.md). The average of this ideal sinusoid over a complete cycle is zero; a measured nonzero steady [electric current](electric-current.md) can include [single-electron superconducting tunneling](single-electron-superconducting-tunneling.md). Josephson's lecture explicitly separates the ordinary quasiparticle term from the phase-dependent term. [Josephson, Nobel lecture, discussion of I₀(V), the phase-dependent term and Fig. 2](the-discovery-of-tunnelling-supercurrents-by-brian-d-josephson.md)

For an original numerical example, use the exact SI values $e=1.602176634\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ and $h=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}$. Then

$$
\frac{2e}{h}=483.5978484\ldots\,\mathrm{MHz/\mu V},\qquad
V=10\,\mathrm{\mu V}\ \Longrightarrow\ f_J=4.835978484\ldots\,\mathrm{GHz}.
$$

The corresponding period is about $0.2067834\,\mathrm{ns}$. These values describe an assumed exact applied voltage, not a historical measurement. The factor $2e$ identifies coherent pair transfer; replacing it by $e$ would halve the predicted [frequency](frequency.md).
