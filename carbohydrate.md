# Carbohydrate

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical substance](chemical-substance-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate)

[Carbohydrates](carbohydrate.md) are a family of [chemical substances](chemical-substance-split.md) encompassing [monosaccharides](monosaccharide.md), combinations of [monosaccharides](monosaccharide.md), and their derivatives. [Glucose](glucose.md) is a small [carbohydrate](carbohydrate.md); [cellulose](cellulose.md) is a long-chain [polysaccharide](polysaccharide.md). [Deoxyribose](deoxyribose.md) illustrates why composition alone does not define the family: replacing a [hydroxy group](hydroxy-group.md) in [ribose](ribose.md) by [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) still leaves a [carbohydrate](carbohydrate.md). See [IUPAC Gold Book: carbohydrates](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/C00820).

Many familiar [monosaccharides](monosaccharide.md) have a [molecular formula](molecular-formula.md) of the form $\mathrm C_n\mathrm H_{2n}\mathrm O_n$, equivalently $\mathrm C_n(\mathrm H_2\mathrm O)_n$. This explains the historical name, “hydrate of [carbon](carbon.md)”, but does not mean the [molecule](molecule-split.md) consists of [carbon](carbon.md) mixed with intact [water](water.md) [molecules](molecule-split.md). Nor is it a universal formula: [deoxyribose](deoxyribose.md) has $\mathrm C_5\mathrm H_{10}\mathrm O_4$. The family is defined through chemical structure and relationships, not that ratio. See [IUPAC's definition and origin of the name carbohydrate](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/C00820).

The useful surprise is the variety of roles obtainable from related building blocks. [Glucose](glucose.md) participates in [metabolism](metabolism.md), [ribose](ribose.md) forms part of [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md), and linked [glucose](glucose.md) units make structural [cellulose](cellulose.md) in a plant [cell wall](cell-wall.md). Understanding [carbohydrates](carbohydrate.md) therefore connects cellular [energy](energy-split.md), molecular information, and material structure. See [Alberts et al., Molecular Biology of the Cell: the chemical components of a cell](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26883/).
