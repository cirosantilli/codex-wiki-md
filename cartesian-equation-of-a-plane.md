# Cartesian equation of a plane

↑ **Parent:** [Plane](plane.md)

In three-dimensional [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md), a nonzero [normal vector](normal-vector.md) $\mathbf n=(a,b,c)$ determines the [plane equation](cartesian-equation-of-a-plane.md)

$$
ax+by+cz=d.
$$

For a point $\mathbf a_0$ on the [plane](plane.md), $d=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a_0$, so the equivalent point-normal form is $\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf a_0)=0$. The difference of any two points on the [plane](plane.md) is [orthogonal](orthogonal-vectors.md) to $\mathbf n$, directly explaining its role as a [normal vector](normal-vector.md).

Multiplying $a,b,c,d$ by the same nonzero [scalar](scalar.md) preserves the [set](set-split.md) of solutions. Thus $2x-y+2z=3$ and $-6x+3y-6z=-9$ describe the same [plane](plane.md). Their [normal vectors](normal-vector.md) point in opposite directions, so a [signed distance from a point to a plane](signed-distance-from-a-point-to-a-plane.md) changes sign, while the [distance from a point to a plane](distance-from-a-point-to-a-plane.md) is unchanged. If $a=b=c=0$, the [linear equation](linear-equation.md) describes either all of space when $d=0$, or the [empty set](empty-set.md) when $d\ne0$; it does not select a [plane](plane.md).
