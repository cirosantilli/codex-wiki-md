# Leading Taylor term

↑ **Parent:** [Power series](power-series.md)

Suppose an [analytic function](holomorphic-function.md) near $c$ is not identically zero there. Its [Taylor series](taylor-series.md) has a first nonzero [coefficient](coefficient.md) $a_m$, and

$$
f(z)=a_m(z-c)^m+O((z-c)^{m+1}).
$$

The term $a_m(z-c)^m$ is its [leading Taylor term](leading-taylor-term.md). Dividing by $(z-c)^m$ gives a [limit](limit-of-a-function.md) of $a_m\ne0$ as $z\to c$, so this term determines the leading local magnitude and angular behaviour. The remainder uses [big O notation](big-o-notation.md); it is smaller by one power near the centre.

For a real-valued [analytic function](holomorphic-function.md) of a real variable, an [even integer](even-number.md) $m$ gives the same nearby sign on both sides of $c$, while an [odd integer](odd-integer.md) $m$ gives opposite signs. For example, $f(x)=x^2(1+x)$ has [leading Taylor term](leading-taylor-term.md) $x^2$ and is positive on both sides of zero sufficiently nearby; $g(x)=x^3(1+x)$ has [leading Taylor term](leading-taylor-term.md) $x^3$ and changes sign there. The finite factor $1+x$ stays positive for $|x|<1$. The ordinary constant term is the leading one when $f(c)\ne0$.
