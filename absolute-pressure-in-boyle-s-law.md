<h1 id="absolute-pressure-in-boyle-s-law">Absolute pressure in Boyle's law</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Boyle's law](boyle-s-law.md)

With [gauge pressure](gauge-pressure.md) $p_g$ referenced to [atmospheric pressure](atmospheric-pressure.md) $p_{\rm atm}$, [absolute pressure](absolute-pressure.md) is $p=p_g+p_{\rm atm}$. Thus [Boyle's law](boyle-s-law.md) becomes

$$
(p_{g,1}+p_{{\rm atm},1})V_1
=(p_{g,2}+p_{{\rm atm},2})V_2.
$$

Even a constant [atmospheric pressure](atmospheric-pressure.md) cannot be dropped from these sums. For the calculated $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$ to $200\,\mathrm{kPa}$ example, take $p_{\rm atm}=100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The initial and final [gauge pressures](gauge-pressure.md) are $0$ and $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$; their products with the respective [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) are $0$ and $5000\,\mathrm{kPa\,cm^3}$, which are unequal. Zero [gauge pressure](gauge-pressure.md) means balance with the surroundings, not absence of [gas](gas.md) or absence of wall [momentum](momentum-split.md) transfer.

For a frictionless piston of [surface area](surface-area.md) $A$ with its outer face exposed to [atmospheric pressure](atmospheric-pressure.md), the additional inward [force](force-split.md) required for [mechanical equilibrium](mechanical-equilibrium.md) is $F=(p-p_{\rm atm})A=p_gA$. This is why a mechanical load can reveal a [pressure](pressure-split.md) difference even though the [equation of state](equation-of-state.md) requires [absolute pressure](absolute-pressure.md). With piston [friction](friction.md), the load alone no longer determines $p_gA$; a direct [pressure sensor](pressure-sensor.md) avoids that particular inference.
