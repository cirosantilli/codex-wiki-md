<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [entropy form of dry potential temperature](../../../../../entropy-form-of-dry-potential-temperature.md) follows from its parcel definition. The [potential temperature](../../../../../potential-temperature.md) is the temperature a parcel would reach if brought reversibly and adiabatically to a reference pressure $p_0$. For dry air of fixed composition, treated as an [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) with constant [specific heat capacity](../../../../../specific-heat-capacity.md),

$$
\boxed{\theta=T\left(\frac{p_0}{p}\right)^\kappa,\qquad \kappa=R_d/c_p.}
$$

Per unit mass, the [first law of thermodynamics](../../../../../first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md), together with $\alpha=R_dT/p$ for [specific volume](../../../../../specific-volume.md), gives $\delta q=c_p\,dT-\alpha\,dp$. The reversible form of the [Second law of thermodynamics](../../../../../second-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) then gives

$$
 ds=\frac{\delta q_{\rm rev}}T=c_p\,d\ln T-R_d\,d\ln p
 =c_p\,d\ln\theta.
$$

Hence **dry reversible adiabatic motion has $D\theta/Dt=0$**. Pressure changes cause expansion cooling or compression warming, while the pressure factor in theta compensates exactly. Adiabaticity alone is insufficient if irreversible mixing or viscous dissipation produces [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md). Condensation or freezing supplies latent heating, so ordinary dry potential temperature need not be conserved in a moist adiabatically moving parcel.
