<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Treat the quoted solar spectrum as irradiance normal to the beam, approximately constant over the narrow band. The incident band irradiance is $F_\perp=19\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ and the horizontal value is $F_0=F_\perp\sin30^\circ=9.5\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. The cloud's vertical [optical depth](../../../../../optical-depth.md) is $25(0.4)=10$; including the clear atmosphere gives $\tau_v=10.07$. The slant optical depth is $\tau_s=\tau_v/\sin30^\circ=20.14$. Thus the [Beer-Lambert law](../../../../../beer-lambert-law.md) gives

$$
\boxed{t_{\rm dir}=e^{-20.14}=1.792\times10^{-9},\qquad F_{\rm surface,dir}=1.702\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}.}
$$

The removed fraction is $1-t_{\rm dir}=0.9999999982$, almost the full incident horizontal $9.5\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. Beam-normal surface irradiance is twice the horizontal value. These are **direct-beam** quantities, not the total diffuse-plus-direct surface flux. If the supplied irradiance were already horizontal, the projection factor would instead be omitted.
