↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Beer-Lambert law](../../../../../beer-lambert-law.md) solves the source-free attenuation term $dI/ds=-\beta_{\rm ext}I$ of the [radiative transfer equation](../../../../../radiative-transfer-equation.md). Extinction includes absorption and scattering **out of that ray**. Missing terms are thermal emission and scattering into the ray from other directions, including repeated scattering and surface reflection.

A cloud can remove nearly all direct sunlight while still supplying substantial diffuse downward radiation. Consequently the total surface solar flux can greatly exceed the direct-beam answer, although reflection sends some energy back to space and absorption can heat matter. At $550\,\mathrm{nm}$ ordinary atmospheric thermal emission is negligible. No unique total flux follows from extinction alone; the single-scattering albedo, angular redistribution and boundary conditions are needed.[Absorption, scattering and thermal-emission processes](../../../../../radiative-transfer-in-the-atmosphere-by-met-office.md)
