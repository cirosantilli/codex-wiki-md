<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A convex water surface has higher saturation vapour pressure than a flat one. Equality of chemical potentials with the Laplace pressure $2\sigma/r$ gives the [Kelvin equation](../../../../../kelvin-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{e_s(r)=e_s(\infty)\exp\!\left(\frac{2\sigma M_w}{\rho_wRT r}\right).}
$$

Small pure droplets therefore need large supersaturation; many subcritical embryos evaporate, making homogeneous droplet formation inefficient under ordinary cloud conditions. [Cloud condensation nuclei](../../../../../cloud-condensation-nucleus.md) provide a surface and, when soluble, lower water activity. [Köhler theory](../../../../../kohler-theory.md) combines the curvature increase with this solute decrease, giving a critical supersaturation and size for activation.
