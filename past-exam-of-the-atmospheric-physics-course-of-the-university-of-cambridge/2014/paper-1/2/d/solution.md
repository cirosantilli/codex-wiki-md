**Direct effect:** nonabsorbing aerosol scatters sunlight, usually increasing reflected shortwave flux over dark surfaces. Absorbing aerosol also heats its atmospheric layer while reducing surface sunlight; the top-of-atmosphere sign depends on its altitude, optical properties and underlying albedo.

**Cloud albedo effect:** soluble, hygroscopic aerosol readily takes up water and can activate as [cloud condensation nuclei](../../../../../cloud-condensation-nucleus.md). At fixed liquid-water path, more smaller droplets increase optical depth and cloud reflectivity: the [Twomey effect](../../../../../twomey-effect.md). Suppressed collision-coalescence can delay rain and change cloud amount or lifetime, though dynamical adjustments prevent a universal sign.

**Semi-direct effect:** absorption can alter stability and evaporate clouds, or change entrainment and cloud cover. Insoluble dust may be a poor soluble-droplet nucleus but still scatters/absorbs radiation or nucleates ice; coatings can alter its activation. Solubility, absorptivity and ice-nucleating ability are distinct properties, not mutually equivalent categories.[Fixed-water cloud albedo response](../../../../../the-influence-of-pollution-on-the-shortwave-albedo-of-clouds-by-s-twomey.md)
