# Mechanical mobility

↑ **Parent:** [Linear drag](linear-drag.md)

Mechanical mobility relates a steady drift [velocity](velocity.md) to a weak applied mechanical [force](force-split.md). For isotropic [linear drag](linear-drag.md) $-\zeta\mathbf v$, terminal force balance gives $\mathbf v_{\mathrm{drift}}=\mu_{\mathrm{mech}}\mathbf F$ with $\mu_{\mathrm{mech}}=1/\zeta$, in units $\mathrm{s\,kg^{-1}}$. For a sphere obeying [Stokes's law](stokes-s-law.md), it is $1/(6\pi\eta R)$. The corresponding translational [hydrodynamic mobility matrix](hydrodynamic-mobility-matrix.md) is $\mu_{\mathrm{mech}}I$. The [Stokes–Einstein relation](stokes-einstein-relation.md) reads $D=k_BT\mu_{\mathrm{mech}}$, and the [velocity relaxation time](velocity-relaxation-time.md) is $m\mu_{\mathrm{mech}}$. In the bare-force spherical model, a particle with [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $q$ has [electrophoretic mobility](electrophoretic-mobility.md) $\mu_{\mathrm{ep}}=q\mu_{\mathrm{mech}}$; ionic-atmosphere effects can modify that simple model.
