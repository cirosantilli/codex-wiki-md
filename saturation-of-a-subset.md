# Saturation of a subset

↑ **Parent:** [Quotient topology](quotient-topology.md)

For the [canonical projection of a quotient set](canonical-projection-of-a-quotient-set.md) $p:X\to X/{\sim}$, the saturation of $A\subseteq X$ is the union of all [equivalence classes](equivalence-class.md) meeting $A$, namely $p^{-1}(p(A))$. A subset is called saturated when it equals its saturation. The [quotient topology](quotient-topology.md) says that $p(A)$ is [closed](closed-set.md) exactly when the saturation of $A$ is [closed](closed-set.md), and similarly for openness. A [closed set](closed-set.md) need not have [closed](closed-set.md) saturation: collapsing $(0,1)\subset\mathbb R$ to one point makes the saturation of $\{1/2\}$ equal to $(0,1)$. Hence a [quotient map](quotient-map.md) need not be a [closed map](closed-map.md).
