# Independent and identically distributed random variables

↑ **Parent:** [Independent random variables](independent-random-variables.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_and_identically_distributed_random_variables)

An [independent and identically distributed sequence](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) combines two conditions: its members are [independent random variables](independent-random-variables.md), and every member has the same [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md). For a finite [sequence](sequence.md) $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ with common law $\nu$, this means that for all [measurable sets](measurable-set.md) $B_1,\ldots,B_n$,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(X_1\in B_1,\ldots,X_n\in B_n)=\prod_{i=1}^n\nu(B_i).}
$$

For an infinite [sequence](sequence.md), require this condition for every finite collection of distinct indices. Equal [marginal distributions](marginal-distribution.md) supply the common law; [independence](independent-random-variables.md) supplies the [product](product-of-numbers.md). Neither condition supplies the other. Grinstead and Snell call the combination an independent trials process.[§4.1, Definitions 4.4–4.5, printed pp. 143–144: mutual independence and a common distribution](introduction-to-probability-by-charles-m-grinstead-and-j-laurie-snell.md)

Choose a [Bernoulli distribution](bernoulli-distribution.md) with success [probability](probability.md) $1/2$. Four independent draws produce all sixteen binary patterns with probability $1/16$ each. If instead $X_1=X_2=X_3=X_4=Z$ for one fair [Bernoulli random variable](bernoulli-distribution.md) $Z$, every member still has that same [Bernoulli distribution](bernoulli-distribution.md), but only patterns `0000` and `1111` occur. Conversely, independently drawn [Bernoulli random variables](bernoulli-distribution.md) with success probabilities $1/4,3/4,1/4,3/4$ have a product [joint probability distribution](joint-probability-distribution.md) but unequal [marginal distributions](marginal-distribution.md).

![](iid-sample-assumption-models.png)

**[Figure 6](#image-iid-sampling-combines-a-common-distribution-with-separate-independent-draws). IID sampling combines a common distribution with separate independent draws**. Original constructions of four [Bernoulli random variables](bernoulli-distribution.md). Separate arrows in the first and third panels represent explicitly independent source draws; the middle panel copies one shared draw. Dependence and marginal laws are specified by the chosen models.

The combination is useful because one [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) describes each observation, while a [product measure](product-measure.md) describes the full [IID sample](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md). In particular, an [IID sample](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) of finite-[variance](variance-split.md), real-valued observations has a predictable [sample mean](sample-mean.md): its [expected value](expected-value-split.md) stays at the population [expected value](expected-value-split.md), while its [variance of a sample mean](variance-of-a-sample-mean.md) decreases as $1/n$. This is a mechanism behind [Monte Carlo integration](monte-carlo-integration.md) and the finite-variance [weak law of large numbers](weak-law-of-large-numbers.md). Whether real observations satisfy the [independence](independent-random-variables.md) and common-law assumptions is a separate modelling question.
