# Abelian ideals lie in the radical of the Killing form

↑ **Parent:** [Killing form](killing-form.md)

If $\mathfrak a$ is an abelian [ideal of a Lie algebra](ideal-of-a-lie-algebra.md), then $\operatorname{ad}_x\operatorname{ad}_y$ maps $\mathfrak g$ into $\mathfrak a$ and vanishes on $\mathfrak a$ whenever $x\in\mathfrak a$. Its trace is zero, so $\kappa(\mathfrak a,\mathfrak g)=0$. Every nonzero solvable ideal has a last nonzero term in its [derived series of a Lie algebra](derived-series-of-a-lie-algebra.md), which is such an abelian ideal. Consequently a nondegenerate [Killing form](killing-form.md) forces the [solvable radical](radical-of-a-lie-algebra.md) to vanish.
