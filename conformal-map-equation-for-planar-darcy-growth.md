# Conformal map equation for planar Darcy growth

↑ **Parent:** [Darcy flow](darcy-flow.md)

Let $F(\zeta,t)$ map the [unit disk](unit-disk.md) conformally to a planar [Darcy flow](darcy-flow.md) region, with $F(0,t)=0$ at a point source of [flux](flux.md) $2\pi Q$. With zero [velocity potential](velocity-potential.md) on the [boundary](boundary-of-a-set.md), the logarithmic source potential becomes $\phi=Q\log|\zeta|$. Differentiating the inverse map at fixed physical position gives $\phi_t=-Q\operatorname{Re}(F_t/(\zeta F_\zeta))$, while $|\nabla\phi|^2=Q^2/|\zeta F_\zeta|^2$. The [boundary](boundary-of-a-set.md) law $\phi_t+|\nabla\phi|^2=0$ therefore gives the displayed equation, for nonzero $Q$.

The vector $\zeta F_\zeta\,d\theta$ is outward normal times arclength on $\zeta=e^{i\theta}$. Consequently the equation gives the [area](surface-area.md) growth law $d|D|/dt=2\pi Q$. For a circular map $F=R(t)\zeta$, it reduces to $R\dot R=Q$, and hence $R(t)^2=R(0)^2+2Qt$.
