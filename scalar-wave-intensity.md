# Scalar wave intensity

↑ **Parent:** [Wave amplitude](wave-amplitude.md)

For a scalar complex [wave amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) $E$, the squared-amplitude [scalar wave intensity](scalar-wave-intensity.md) convention is $I=EE^*$. It is nonnegative and unchanged by multiplying $E$ by a unit-modulus [wave phase](phase-waves.md) factor. This convention is useful in [paraxial propagation](paraxial-approximation.md) and [phase screen](phase-screen.md) models. Conversion to physical [energy flux](energy-flux.md) also depends on the field variable and medium; an arbitrary superposition need not have its full flux determined by this scalar alone. For a random field, $\langle I\rangle$ is the [mean wave intensity](ensemble-averaged-wave-intensity.md), whereas $|\langle E\rangle|^2$ is the squared [coherent field](coherent-wave-field.md); these generally differ.
