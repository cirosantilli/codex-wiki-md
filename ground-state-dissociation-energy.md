# Ground-state dissociation energy

↑ **Parent:** [Bond-dissociation energy](bond-dissociation-energy.md)

The [ground-state dissociation energy](ground-state-dissociation-energy.md) starts at the lowest vibrational [quantum state](quantum-state.md), with the lowest allowed rotational [quantum state](quantum-state.md) and the fragment states explicitly specified. For a [diatomic molecule](diatomic-molecule.md) whose fragments are ground-state [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), omitting any additional rotational correction,

$$
D_0=D_e-E_{\rm ZP},
$$

where $E_{\rm ZP}$ is the vibrational [zero-point energy](zero-point-energy.md) measured upward from the well minimum. The initial [molecule](molecule-split.md) already has this [energy](energy-split.md), so less additional [energy](energy-split.md) is needed to reach the same final limit.[definition of bond-dissociation energy in theoretical chemistry: D0 = De minus zero-point vibrational energy](iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/bond-dissociation-energy-in-theoretical-chemistry.md)

For fragments that are themselves [molecules](molecule-split.md), include their [zero-point energies](zero-point-energy.md) too. Define $D_e$ as the electronic [energy](energy-split.md) difference between separately relaxed products and reactant; then, with consistent rotational conventions,

$$
D_0=D_e+\sum_{\rm fragments}E_{\rm ZP}-E_{\rm ZP,parent}.
$$

Thus “subtract the parent's [zero-point energy](zero-point-energy.md)” is sufficient for atomic products but generally incomplete for molecular products. An excited final [quantum state](quantum-state.md) raises its channel threshold by its excitation [energy](energy-split.md); that threshold does not become the ground-product $D_0$ just because a detector selects it.
