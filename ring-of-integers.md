# Ring of integers

↑ **Parent:** [Algebraic integer](algebraic-integer.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_integers)

The ring of integers of a [number field](number-field.md) $K$ is

$$
\mathcal O_K=\{\alpha\in K:\alpha\text{ is an algebraic integer}\}.
$$

It is the [integral closure](integral-closure.md) of $\mathbb Z$ in $K$. Its [additive group](additive-group.md) is a [free abelian group](free-abelian-group.md) of rank $[K:\mathbb Q]$, so an [integral basis](integral-basis.md) $\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_n$ writes every element uniquely as $\sum_j a_j\omega_j$ with $a_j\in\mathbb Z$. Its [field of fractions](field-of-fractions.md) is $K$.[Milne, Algebraic Number Theory, Chapter 2, rings of integers and integral bases](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf)

For $K=\mathbb Q$, this gives $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z$; for $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, it gives $\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]$. The [Gaussian integers](gaussian-integer.md) give the corresponding example inside $\mathbb Q(i)$. The [ring of integers](ring-of-integers.md) is determined by the [field](field.md), and need not equal $\mathbb Z[\theta]$ for a chosen [algebraic integer](algebraic-integer.md) generator: in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, the larger [ring](ring.md) $\mathbb Z[(1+\sqrt5)/2]$ contains $\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]$ with index two.

This is the setting for [divisibility](divisibility.md), [global units](global-unit-of-a-number-field.md), and [prime ideal factorization](prime-ideal-factorization.md). A [ring of integers](ring-of-integers.md) is a [Dedekind domain](dedekind-domain.md), so every nonzero proper [ideal](ideal.md) factors uniquely into nonzero [prime ideals](prime-ideal.md), even when [unique factorization](unique-factorization-in-an-integral-domain.md) of elements fails.[Dedekind, Sur la théorie des nombres entiers algébriques, 1877, §25](https://www.numdam.org/item/BSMA_1877_2_1_1_207_1.pdf)
