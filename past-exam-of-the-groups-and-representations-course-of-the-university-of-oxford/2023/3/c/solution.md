<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The identity belongs to the [centralizer](../../../../centralizer.md) $C_G(H)$. If $x,y\in C_G(H)$, then for every $h\in H$,

$$
(xy)h=x(yh)=x(hy)=(xh)y=h(xy).
$$

Also $xh=hx$ implies $h x^{-1}=x^{-1}h$ by multiplying on both sides by $x^{-1}$. Hence $xy$ and $x^{-1}$ belong to $C_G(H)$, proving by the [subgroup test](../../../../subgroup-test.md) that $\boxed{C_G(H)\le G}$. This argument works even when $H$ is any subset, giving the [centralizer of a subset](../../../../centralizer-of-a-subset.md).
