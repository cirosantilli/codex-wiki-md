<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Use the realization $R_3=W\subset\mathbb C^4$ from [solution](../c/solution.md). Put $x=(\phi_1,\phi_2,\phi_3,-\phi_1-\phi_2-\phi_3)$, which identifies the three-dimensional [scalar field](../../../../scalar-field.md) with $W$. The [polynomial invariants of the standard S4 representation](../../../../polynomial-invariants-of-the-standard-s4-representation.md) include the symmetric power sums

$$
I_2=\sum_{i=1}^4x_i^2,
\qquad I_3=\sum_{i=1}^4x_i^3,
$$

because every [permutation](../../../../permutation.md) merely rearranges their summands. In these coordinates,

$$
\boxed{I_2=\phi_1^2+\phi_2^2+\phi_3^2+(\phi_1+\phi_2+\phi_3)^2,\qquad
I_3=-3(\phi_1+\phi_2)(\phi_2+\phi_3)(\phi_3+\phi_1).}
$$

Neither [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) vanishes identically: at $(\phi_1,\phi_2,\phi_3)=(1,1,1)$ they equal $12$ and $-24$. Hence coefficients multiplying $I_2$ and $I_3$ provide nonzero invariant quadratic and cubic terms. A change of [basis](../../../../basis.md) transports these [polynomial invariants](../../../../polynomial-invariant.md) to any equivalent realization of $R_3$. For a real [scalar field](../../../../scalar-field.md), both are real. For a [complex scalar field](../../../../complex-scalar-field.md), a real [scalar potential](../../../../scalar-potential.md) may contain $\lambda_k I_k+\lambda_k^*I_k^*$; the quadratic [Hermitian form](../../../../hermitian-form.md) $\sum_i|x_i|^2$ is another invariant.

As an independent [character](../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) check, the [symmetric square of a group representation](../../../../symmetric-square-of-a-group-representation.md) has [character](../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) $[\chi(g)^2+\chi(g^2)]/2$, and the [symmetric cube of a group representation](../../../../symmetric-cube-of-a-group-representation.md) has [character](../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) $[\chi(g)^3+3\chi(g)\chi(g^2)+2\chi(g^3)]/6$. With $\chi=\chi_3$ these are $(6,2,2,0,0)$ and $(10,2,-2,1,0)$. Their [character inner products](../../../../character-inner-product.md) with $\chi_0$ are both one, so each degree has exactly one independent [polynomial invariant](../../../../polynomial-invariant.md).
