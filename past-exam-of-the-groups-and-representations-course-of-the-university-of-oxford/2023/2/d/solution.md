<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Only the [character](../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) $\chi_2$ of the two-dimensional [irreducible representation](../../../../irreducible-representation.md) remains unknown. Write it as $(2,a,b,c,d)$. The [regular representation](../../../../regular-representation.md) has [character](../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) zero off the identity and decomposes with multiplicities equal to the [irreducible representation](../../../../irreducible-representation.md) dimensions. Thus, for a nonidentity element $g$,

$$
\chi_0(g)+\chi_1(g)+2\chi_2(g)+3\chi_3(g)+3\chi_4(g)=0.
$$

For the four nonidentity [conjugacy classes](../../../../conjugacy-class.md) this gives $a=0$, $b=2$, $c=-1$, $d=0$. The complete [character table](../../../../character-table.md) is consequently

$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
&e&(12)&(12)(34)&(123)&(1234)\\\hline
\text{class size}&1&6&3&8&6\\\hline
\chi_0&1&1&1&1&1\\
\chi_1&1&-1&1&1&-1\\
\chi_2&2&0&2&-1&0\\
\chi_3&3&1&-1&0&-1\\
\chi_4&3&-1&-1&0&1
\end{array}}
$$

For example, $\langle\chi_2,\chi_2\rangle=(4+12+8)/24=1$, agreeing with [character orthogonality](../../../../character-orthogonality.md).
