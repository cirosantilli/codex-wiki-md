<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Write a homogeneous [quadratic polynomial](../../../../quadratic-polynomial.md) as $p(x,y)=ax^2+bxy+cy^2$. Invariance under $B:(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)$ forces $b=0$. Invariance under $A:(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)$ then forces $a=c$. Since $A,B$ generate the [group](../../../../group-split.md), this proves that all homogeneous quadratic [polynomial invariants](../../../../polynomial-invariant.md) are

$$
\boxed{p(x,y)=a(x^2+y^2),\qquad a\in\mathbb C.}
$$

This agrees with the single [trivial representation](../../../../trivial-representation.md) in the [symmetric square](../../../../symmetric-square.md) in [solution](../d/solution.md); more precisely, coordinate [polynomials](../../../../polynomial-split.md) lie in the [symmetric square](../../../../symmetric-square.md) of the [dual representation](../../../../dual-representation.md), which here is equivalent to $\rho$. If “quadratic” permits terms of smaller degree, arbitrary constants can be added. There are no invariant linear terms because $-I\in G$ negates every linear [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md).
