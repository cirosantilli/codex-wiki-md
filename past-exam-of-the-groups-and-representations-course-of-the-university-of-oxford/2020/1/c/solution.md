<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Now $m_1=m_2=n$ and the first two [matrix representations](../../../../matrix-representation.md) agree in the chosen [bases](../../../../basis.md). Each of $P_{11},P_{12},P_{21},P_{22}$ is therefore a commuting [endomorphism](../../../../endomorphism.md) of that common [irreducible representation](../../../../irreducible-representation.md), so each is scalar by the [Schur lemma](../../../../schur-s-lemma.md). All blocks connecting either of these copies with the third vanish, since the third [irreducible representation](../../../../irreducible-representation.md) is inequivalent. Hence

$$
\boxed{P=\begin{pmatrix}
aI_n&bI_n&0\\
cI_n&dI_n&0\\
0&0&eI_{m_3}
\end{pmatrix},\qquad a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb C.}
$$

Every such [matrix](../../../../matrix.md) commutes with $R(g)$. In particular the off-diagonal scalars $b,c$ can mix the two identical copies. The [commutant of a semisimple representation](../../../../commutant-of-a-semisimple-representation.md) is $M_2(\mathbb C)\oplus\mathbb C$, of complex [dimension](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) five. If the first two [representations](../../../../group-representation.md) were merely equivalent, rather than literally equal, the mixed blocks would instead be scalar multiples of a fixed invertible [intertwiner](../../../../intertwiner.md) and its inverse.
