<h1 id="1/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the basis of [solution](../../b/solution.md) for the two doublets, treating the barred left-handed fields as the components to which the printed [representation](../../../../../group-representation.md) assignment applies. Write $x=(\bar\psi_L^1,\bar\psi_L^2)^T$ and $y=(\psi_R^1,\psi_R^2)^T$. The doublet contribution to the [Yukawa interaction](../../../../../yukawa-interaction.md) is $H x^T L y$, so [invariant tensors](../../../../../invariant-tensor.md) obey

$$
D_1(Q)^TLD_1(Q)=L,\qquad P^TLP=L.
$$

The first equation forces $L_{11}=L_{22}=0$, since their factors are $\alpha^{\pm2}\ne1$. The second sets $L_{12}=L_{21}$. Thus the doublet [invariant tensor](../../../../../invariant-tensor.md) is $x_1y_2+x_2y_1$. The product of either doublet with $R_1$ has no [trivial representation](../../../../../trivial-representation.md), whereas $R_1\otimes R_1=R_0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\lambda^{\mathrm{(i)}}=
\begin{pmatrix}0&y&0\\y&0&0\\0&0&y_3\end{pmatrix},\qquad y,y_3\in\mathbb C.}
$$

If instead the unbarred left-handed doublet is chosen to transform by $D_1$, its barred components transform by $D_1^*$; in that equivalent basis the upper block is $yI_2$. Exchanging the two left components relates these conventions. The [singular value decomposition](../../../../../singular-value-decomposition.md) gives the same physical masses in either basis.
