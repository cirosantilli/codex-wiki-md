<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $a=a_1$, $b=a_2$ with $a,b\geq0$, and let $\lambda=a\omega_1+b\omega_2$ be the [highest weight](../../../../highest-weight-of-a-representation.md). The [positive roots](../../../../positive-root.md) are $\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$ and $\alpha_1+\alpha_2$, and the [Weyl vector](../../../../half-sum-of-positive-roots.md) is $\rho=\omega_1+\omega_2$. The [Weyl dimension formula](../../../../weyl-dimension-formula.md) gives

$$
d(a,b)=\prod_{\alpha>0}\frac{(\lambda+\rho,\alpha)}{(\rho,\alpha)}
=\frac{a+1}{1}\frac{b+1}{1}\frac{a+b+2}{2},\qquad
\boxed{d(a,b)=\tfrac12(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2).}
$$

For the [quadratic Casimir operator](../../../../quadratic-casimir-operator.md), use the root-length normalization $C(\lambda)=(\lambda,\lambda+2\rho)$. Substituting the [Gram matrix](../../../../gram-matrix.md) in [solution](../a/solution.md) yields

$$
\boxed{C(a,b)=\frac23\bigl(a^2+ab+b^2+3a+3b\bigr).}
$$

Taking the [trace](../../../../matrix-trace.md) of the defining [quadratic Casimir operator](../../../../quadratic-casimir-operator.md) identity gives $d(a,b)C(a,b)=\dim(\mathfrak{su}(3))c(a,b)=8c(a,b)$, where $c$ is the [Dynkin index](../../../../dynkin-index.md) in the matching normalization. Therefore

$$
\boxed{c(a,b)=\frac{(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)}{24}
\bigl(a^2+ab+b^2+3a+3b\bigr).}
$$

In particular $c(\mathbf3)=1$ and $c(\mathbf8)=6$. The common particle-physics convention uses $C_2=C/2$ and $T=c/2$, giving $C_2(\mathbf3)=4/3$ and $T(\mathbf3)=1/2$. The subsequent discrete [gauge anomaly](../../../../gauge-anomaly.md) congruences use the integer [Dynkin index](../../../../dynkin-index.md) $c$, while the sign test for the supplied [beta function](../../../../beta-function-physics.md) is independent of a common rescaling of all indices.
