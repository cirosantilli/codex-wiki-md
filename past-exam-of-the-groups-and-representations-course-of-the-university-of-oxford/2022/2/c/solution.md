<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [Weyl dimension formula](../../../../weyl-dimension-formula.md) is increasing in each nonnegative [Dynkin label](../../../../dynkin-label.md). If $a\geq3$ or $b\geq3$, the dimension is at least $d(3,0)=10$. Among $0\leq a,b\leq2$, the cases with both labels nonzero, other than $(1,1)$, have dimension at least $d(2,1)=15$. Thus the following is the complete list of [irreducible representations](../../../../irreducible-representation.md) of dimension strictly less than $10$:

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c|r|r|r}
(a,b)&R&d&C&c\\\hline
(0,0)&\mathbf1&1&0&0\\
(1,0)&\mathbf3&3&8/3&1\\
(0,1)&\overline{\mathbf3}&3&8/3&1\\
(2,0)&\mathbf6&6&20/3&5\\
(0,2)&\overline{\mathbf6}&6&20/3&5\\
(1,1)&\mathbf8&8&6&6
\end{array}}
$$

Here $\mathbf6=\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathbf3$ is the [two-index symmetric representation](../../../../two-index-symmetric-representation.md) and $\mathbf8$ is the [Adjoint representation](../../../../adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra.md). Interchanging $a$ and $b$ gives the [conjugate representation](../../../../conjugate-representation.md), explaining equal dimensions, [Casimir eigenvalues](../../../../casimir-eigenvalue.md) and [Dynkin indices](../../../../dynkin-index.md) in each conjugate pair. In the alternative convention of [solution](../b/solution.md), divide the $C$ and $c$ columns by two.
