<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [Clifford algebra](../../../../clifford-algebra.md) condition for signature $(-,+,+)$ is

$$
\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}=2\eta_{\mu\nu}I_2.
$$

Each [Pauli matrix](../../../../pauli-matrices.md) squares to $I_2$, and distinct [Pauli matrices](../../../../pauli-matrices.md) anticommute. Therefore the given choice obeys

$$
\gamma_0^2=(-i\sigma_1)^2=-I_2,\qquad
\gamma_1^2=\gamma_2^2=I_2,\qquad
\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu+\gamma_\nu\gamma_\mu=0\quad(\mu\ne\nu).
$$

These check all diagonal and off-diagonal [anticommutators](../../../../anticommutator.md), proving

$$
\boxed{\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}=2\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1)_{\mu\nu}I_2.}
$$

Thus they are viable three-dimensional [gamma matrices](../../../../gamma-matrices.md). Their [Hermitian adjoints](../../../../adjoint-operator.md) satisfy $\gamma_0^\dagger=-\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_a^\dagger=\gamma_a$ for $a=1,2$. Two components are minimal: one-dimensional nonzero scalar [matrices](../../../../matrix.md) cannot anticommute while all having nonzero squares.
