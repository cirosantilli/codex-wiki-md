<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the usual colour [SU(3)](../../../../su-3-group.md) fixing the fourth coordinate of the [SU(4)](../../../../su-4-group.md) defining [representation](../../../../group-representation.md),

$$
\mathbf4\downarrow_{SU(3)}=\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf1,\qquad
\overline{\mathbf4}\downarrow_{SU(3)}=\overline{\mathbf3}\oplus\mathbf1.
$$

Taking the [exterior square](../../../../exterior-square.md) gives

$$
\Lambda^2(\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf1)=\Lambda^2\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf3
=\overline{\mathbf3}\oplus\mathbf3.
$$

The identification $\Lambda^2\mathbf3\cong\overline{\mathbf3}$ follows by contracting an [antisymmetric tensor](../../../../antisymmetric-tensor.md) with the invariant three-index [Levi-Civita symbol](../../../../levi-civita-symbol.md). Combining this with [c](../c.md) gives all colour [multiplets](../../../../multiplet.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathbf7\downarrow_{SU(3)}=\mathbf3\oplus\overline{\mathbf3}\oplus\mathbf1,\qquad
\mathbf8\downarrow_{SU(3)}=\mathbf3\oplus\overline{\mathbf3}\oplus2\mathbf1.}
$$

A colour triplet has the [color charge](../../../../color-charge.md) transformation law of a [quark](../../../../quark.md), an antitriplet that of an [antiquark](../../../../antiquark.md), and a [colour singlet](../../../../colour-singlet.md) is colour-neutral, as a [lepton](../../../../lepton.md) is. Thus both parent [representations](../../../../group-representation.md) permit quark-like and antiquark-like colour sectors; the vector has one neutral sector and the spinor two. These are colour assignments only. They do not determine particle [spin](../../../../spin.md), statistics, [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md), weak quantum numbers or a full generation of the [Standard Model](../../../../standard-model-split.md). In a real parent field the conjugate colour sectors may describe one complex colour field and its conjugate, rather than independent particle species.

**Global group qualification.** The printed chain is correct at the [Lie algebra](../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) level, $\mathfrak{su}(3)\subset\mathfrak{su}(4)\cong\mathfrak{so}(6)\subset\mathfrak{so}(7)$. Globally its simply connected form is $SU(3)\subset SU(4)\cong\operatorname{Spin}(6)\subset\operatorname{Spin}(7)$. The standard map of [SU(4)](../../../../su-4-group.md) into $SO(7)$ through $\mathbf6\oplus\mathbf1$ has a nontrivial [kernel](../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md): the concrete central element $-I_4$ acts as $+I_6$ on $\Lambda^2\mathbf4$ and trivially on the [singlet representation](../../../../singlet-representation.md). Its image is $SO(6)\cong SU(4)/\{\pm I_4\}$, rather than an injective copy of [SU(4)](../../../../su-4-group.md) under this map.

The [global form obstruction for the Spin7 spinor](../../../../global-form-obstruction-for-the-spin7-spinor.md) is explicit: the central element $-1$ of the [Spin group](../../../../spin-group.md) $\operatorname{Spin}(7)$ maps to the identity in $SO(7)$ but acts as $-I_8$ on the eight-dimensional [spin representation](../../../../spin-representation.md). Equivalently $-I_4$ acts as $-I$ on both $\mathbf4$ and $\overline{\mathbf4}$ in its restriction. Therefore the spinor does not descend to a genuine [SO(7)](../../../../so-7-group.md) [group representation](../../../../group-representation.md). A theory containing that spinor needs the [Spin group](../../../../spin-group.md) global form, or must understand the source's notation as specifying only its [Lie algebra](../../../../lie-algebra-split.md). The displayed colour [branchings](../../../../branching-rule.md) remain valid with this correction.
