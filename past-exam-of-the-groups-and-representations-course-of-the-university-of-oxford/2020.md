<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

A [group representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) on a [vector space](../vector-space.md) $V$ over a [field](../algebra.md#field) $K$ is a [group homomorphism](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $R:G\to\operatorname{GL}(V)$. In particular, $R(gh)=R(g)R(h)$, $R(e)=I$ and $R(g^{-1})=R(g)^{-1}$. Choosing a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) identifies it with a [matrix representation](../representation-theory.md#matrix-representation).

Two [group representations](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) $R$ on $V$ and $S$ on $W$ are [equivalent representations](../representation-theory.md#equivalence-of-group-representations) when an invertible [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map) $T:V\to W$ satisfies $TR(g)=S(g)T$ for every $g$. Thus $S(g)=TR(g)T^{-1}$; they differ only by a choice of [basis](../vector-space.md#basis).

A nonzero [group representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) is a [reducible representation](../representation-theory.md#reducible-representation) if there is a [vector subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) $U$ with $0\ne U\ne V$ and $R(g)U\subseteq U$ for every $g$. It is an [irreducible representation](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) if it has no such [invariant subspace](../representation-theory.md#invariant-subspace). Reducibility alone does not assert the existence of an invariant complementary [vector subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace); that stronger conclusion says that the [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) is a [semisimple representation](../representation-theory.md#semisimple-representation).

The [Schur lemma](../representation-theory.md#schur-s-lemma) states that an [intertwiner](../representation-theory.md#intertwiner) $T$ between [irreducible representations](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) is either zero or an [isomorphism](../algebra.md#isomorphism). Consequently an [intertwiner](../representation-theory.md#intertwiner) between inequivalent [irreducible representations](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) is zero. For a finite-dimensional [irreducible representation](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) over the [complex numbers](../complex-analysis.md#complex-number), every commuting [endomorphism](../algebra.md#endomorphism) is scalar:

$$
\boxed{TR(g)=R(g)T\quad(\forall g)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T=\lambda I.}
$$

For the first assertion, the [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) and [image](../set-theory.md#image-of-a-function) of $T$ are [invariant subspaces](../representation-theory.md#invariant-subspace), so a nonzero $T$ is injective and surjective. For the scalar conclusion, choose an [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $\lambda$ of $T$. The [intertwiner](../representation-theory.md#intertwiner) $T-\lambda I$ has a nonzero [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md), so it must vanish. The finite-dimensional complex hypothesis matters: the scalar conclusion need not hold over the [real numbers](../arithmetic.md#real-number), as a real planar rotation through $\pi/2$ is an [irreducible representation](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) of the cyclic [group](../group.md) of order four, while the rotation itself is a commuting nonscalar [endomorphism](../algebra.md#endomorphism).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Write $P=(P_{ij})_{i,j=1}^3$, where $P_{ij}:\mathbb C^{m_j}\to\mathbb C^{m_i}$. Block multiplication makes the commuting condition equivalent to

$$
P_{ij}R_j(g)=R_i(g)P_{ij}\qquad(\forall g\in G).
$$

Thus every block is an [intertwiner](../representation-theory.md#intertwiner). For $i\ne j$, the [irreducible representations](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) are inequivalent, so the [Schur lemma](../representation-theory.md#schur-s-lemma) gives $P_{ij}=0$. On each diagonal block, the same [Schur lemma](../representation-theory.md#schur-s-lemma) gives $P_{ii}=\lambda_iI_{m_i}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P=\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda_1I_{m_1}&0&0\\
0&\lambda_2I_{m_2}&0\\
0&0&\lambda_3I_{m_3}
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\in\mathbb C.}
$$

Conversely, every displayed [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) commutes with $R(g)$, proving both necessity and sufficiency. This [commutant of a semisimple representation](../representation-theory.md#commutant-of-a-semisimple-representation) has complex [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) three. The parameters may vanish: $P$ was not required to be invertible.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Now $m_1=m_2=n$ and the first two [matrix representations](../representation-theory.md#matrix-representation) agree in the chosen [bases](../vector-space.md#basis). Each of $P_{11},P_{12},P_{21},P_{22}$ is therefore a commuting [endomorphism](../algebra.md#endomorphism) of that common [irreducible representation](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation), so each is scalar by the [Schur lemma](../representation-theory.md#schur-s-lemma). All blocks connecting either of these copies with the third vanish, since the third [irreducible representation](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) is inequivalent. Hence

$$
\boxed{P=\begin{pmatrix}
aI_n&bI_n&0\\
cI_n&dI_n&0\\
0&0&eI_{m_3}
\end{pmatrix},\qquad a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb C.}
$$

Every such [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) commutes with $R(g)$. In particular the off-diagonal scalars $b,c$ can mix the two identical copies. The [commutant of a semisimple representation](../representation-theory.md#commutant-of-a-semisimple-representation) is $M_2(\mathbb C)\oplus\mathbb C$, of complex [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) five. If the first two [representations](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) were merely equivalent, rather than literally equal, the mixed blocks would instead be scalar multiples of a fixed invertible [intertwiner](../representation-theory.md#intertwiner) and its inverse.

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

All three [vector spaces](../vector-space.md) now have the same [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) $n$. Each of the nine blocks of $P$ commutes with the common [irreducible representation](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation), so the [Schur lemma](../representation-theory.md#schur-s-lemma) gives

$$
\boxed{P=\begin{pmatrix}
a_{11}I_n&a_{12}I_n&a_{13}I_n\\
a_{21}I_n&a_{22}I_n&a_{23}I_n\\
a_{31}I_n&a_{32}I_n&a_{33}I_n
\end{pmatrix}=A\otimes I_n,\qquad A\in M_3(\mathbb C).}
$$

Conversely $R(g)=I_3\otimes R_1(g)$, so the [Kronecker product](../vector-space.md#kronecker-product) identity immediately verifies commutation for every $A$. The [commutant of a semisimple representation](../representation-theory.md#commutant-of-a-semisimple-representation) acts arbitrarily on the three-dimensional multiplicity [vector space](../vector-space.md) while acting as a scalar on each irreducible factor. Its complex [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) is nine.

Here the star means entrywise [complex conjugation](../complex-analysis.md#complex-conjugation), not [Hermitian conjugation](../hilbert-space.md#hermitian-conjugation). Put $s=g_3$ and $r=g_1g_3$. Since $\alpha^3=1$ and $1+\alpha+\alpha^2=0$,

$$
r=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^2&0\\0&\alpha\end{pmatrix},\qquad
s=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
r^3=s^2=I,\qquad srs=r^{-1}.
$$

The generators are $g_1=rs$, $g_2=r^2s$ and $g_3=s$. Moving every occurrence of $s$ to the right using $sr=r^{-1}s$ reduces every word to $r^k$ or $r^ks$ with $k=0,1,2$. The three diagonal [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) are distinct, as are the three off-diagonal [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix), so

$$
\boxed{G=\{I,r,r^2,s,rs,r^2s\},\qquad |G|=6.}
$$

For an explicit [group isomorphism](../algebra.md#group-isomorphism), send $r$ to $(123)$ and $s$ to $(12)$ in the [symmetric group](../finite-group-theory.md#symmetric-group) $S_3$, composing [permutations](../combinatorics.md#permutation) from right to left. These [permutations](../combinatorics.md#permutation) satisfy the same relations and generate $S_3$. The normal forms above then give an isomorphism; in particular $g_1\mapsto(13)$, $g_2\mapsto(23)$ and $g_3\mapsto(12)$.

Conjugation by $s$ exchanges $r$ with $r^2$, and conjugation by $r$ cycles the three reflections. The [conjugacy classes](../group-action.md#conjugacy-class) are therefore

$$
\boxed{\{I\},\qquad \{r,r^2\},\qquad \{s,rs,r^2s\}=\{g_1,g_2,g_3\}.}
$$

Their sizes are $1,2,3$, respectively. They correspond to the identity, three-cycles and [transpositions](../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation) in $S_3$.

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

There are three complex [irreducible representations](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation), because there are three [conjugacy classes](../group-action.md#conjugacy-class). Two are the [trivial representation](../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation) $\mathbf1$ and the [sign representation](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#sign-representation) $\varepsilon$: both send $r$ to $1$, while they send $s$ to $1$ and $-1$, respectively. These exhaust the [one-dimensional representations](../representation-theory.md#one-dimensional-representation), since their scalar images must satisfy $r=r^{-1}$ and $r^3=1$, forcing $r=1$, and then $s^2=1$.

The defining two-dimensional [matrix representation](../representation-theory.md#matrix-representation) $D$ has [character](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation)

$$
\chi_D(I)=2,\qquad \chi_D(r)=\chi_D(r^2)=\alpha+\alpha^2=-1,\qquad \chi_D(r^ks)=0.
$$

Its [character inner product](../representation-theory.md#character-inner-product) with itself is

$$
\langle\chi_D,\chi_D\rangle=\frac{1}{6}(1\cdot4+2\cdot1+3\cdot0)=1,
$$

so it is an [irreducible representation](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation). Directly, the two coordinate lines are the distinct [eigenspaces](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) of $r$, and $s$ exchanges them, leaving no common invariant line. The [sum of squares of irreducible representation dimensions](../representation-theory.md#sum-of-squares-of-irreducible-representation-dimensions) is $1^2+1^2+2^2=6$, proving completeness. In the [conjugacy class](../group-action.md#conjugacy-class) order of [solution](#2/a/solution), the [character table](../representation-theory.md#character-table) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|rrr}
&I&r&s\\\hline
\text{class size}&1&2&3\\\hline
\mathbf1&1&1&1\\
\varepsilon&1&1&-1\\
D&2&-1&0
\end{array}}
$$

Representatives $r,s$ in this table stand for their full [conjugacy classes](../group-action.md#conjugacy-class). This lists all [irreducible representations](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) up to [equivalence of group representations](../representation-theory.md#equivalence-of-group-representations).

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

The specified [linear maps](../vector-space.md#linear-map) form a [permutation representation](../representation-theory.md#permutation-representation), since

$$
R(\sigma)R(\tau)e_i=e_{\sigma(\tau(i))}=R(\sigma\tau)e_i.
$$

The [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) of a [permutation matrix](../vector-space.md#permutation-matrix) counts fixed coordinate [basis vectors](../vector-space.md#basis-vector). On the identity, three-cycles and [transpositions](../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation), respectively, this gives $\chi_R=(3,0,1)$. Comparing with [solution](#2/b/solution),

$$
\boxed{\chi_R=\chi_{\mathbf1}+\chi_D,\qquad R\cong\mathbf1\oplus D.}
$$

For an explicit [direct sum](../vector-space.md#direct-sum),

$$
\mathbb C^3=\mathbb C(e_1+e_2+e_3)\oplus
\{z_1e_1+z_2e_2+z_3e_3:z_1+z_2+z_3=0\}.
$$

Both summands are [invariant subspaces](../representation-theory.md#invariant-subspace) under coordinate [permutations](../combinatorics.md#permutation). The first is fixed pointwise and hence gives the [trivial representation](../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation); the second has [character](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) $(2,-1,0)$ and hence gives $D$. The [sign representation](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#sign-representation) has multiplicity zero.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

The variables commute, so $x_ix_j=x_jx_i$. Thus $V$ has [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) six, with [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) $x_1^2,x_2^2,x_3^2,x_1x_2,x_1x_3,x_2x_3$, even though nine coefficients occur in the redundant expression in the source. Its [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) is the [symmetric square](../multilinear-algebra.md#symmetric-square) of the three-dimensional [permutation representation](../representation-theory.md#permutation-representation).

The identity fixes all six [monomials](../polynomial.md#monomial). A three-cycle fixes none. For a [transposition](../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation), say $(12)$, exactly $x_3^2$ and $x_1x_2$ are fixed; the remaining [monomials](../polynomial.md#monomial) occur in exchanged pairs. Therefore $\chi_{\widetilde R}=(6,0,2)$ in the [conjugacy class](../group-action.md#conjugacy-class) order of [solution](#2/b/solution). The [character inner products](../representation-theory.md#character-inner-product) give multiplicities

$$
n_{\mathbf1}=\frac{6+3\cdot2}{6}=2,\qquad
n_\varepsilon=\frac{6-3\cdot2}{6}=0,\qquad
n_D=\frac{6\cdot2}{6}=2.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\widetilde R\cong2\mathbf1\oplus2D.}
$$

There is also a concrete decomposition: the span of the three squares is one copy of the [permutation representation](../representation-theory.md#permutation-representation). The span of the three mixed [monomials](../polynomial.md#monomial) is another, since $x_2x_3,x_1x_3,x_1x_2$ are permuted exactly like their omitted indices $1,2,3$. Within each, the coefficient-sum-zero [vector subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) gives a copy of $D$, and the sum of its three [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) [monomials](../polynomial.md#monomial) gives a [trivial representation](../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation).

A fixed [quadratic polynomial](../polynomial.md#quadratic-polynomial) must have equal coefficients on each of the two [orbits](../dynamical-systems.md#orbit-dynamical-system) of [monomials](../polynomial.md#monomial). Hence its fixed [vector subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) is

$$
\boxed{V^{S_3}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2,\quad x_1x_2+x_1x_3+x_2x_3\}.}
$$

The two displayed [polynomial invariants](../representation-theory.md#polynomial-invariant) are linearly independent and account for both singlets. Equivalently, one may use $x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2$ and $(x_1+x_2+x_3)^2$ as a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis). No constant term belongs to this homogeneous degree-two [vector space](../vector-space.md).

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

The componentwise product is closed in $G_1\times G_2$. Associativity follows componentwise from associativity in each [group](../group.md):

$$
((g_1,g_2)(h_1,h_2))(k_1,k_2)
=((g_1h_1)k_1,(g_2h_2)k_2)
=(g_1(h_1k_1),g_2(h_2k_2)).
$$

The identity is $(e_1,e_2)$, and $(g_1,g_2)^{-1}=(g_1^{-1},g_2^{-1})$. These verify all the [group axioms](../group.md#group-axioms), so this is the [direct product of groups](../group-theory.md#direct-product-of-groups).

Write the paper's [Kronecker product](../vector-space.md#kronecker-product) $\times$ as $\otimes$. Its mixed-product identity and the [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) laws of $R_1,R_2$ give

$$
\begin{aligned}
R(g_1,g_2)R(h_1,h_2)
&=(R_1(g_1)\otimes R_2(g_2))(R_1(h_1)\otimes R_2(h_2))\\
&=R_1(g_1)R_1(h_1)\otimes R_2(g_2)R_2(h_2)\\
&=R_1(g_1h_1)\otimes R_2(g_2h_2)\\
&=R((g_1,g_2)(h_1,h_2)).
\end{aligned}
$$

Also $R(e_1,e_2)=I_{n_1}\otimes I_{n_2}=I_{n_1n_2}$, and

$$
R(g_1,g_2)^{-1}=R_1(g_1)^{-1}\otimes R_2(g_2)^{-1}.
$$

Thus the images are invertible and this is a [group representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) of [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) $n_1n_2$, called the [external tensor product of group representations](../representation-theory.md#external-tensor-product-of-group-representations). No irreducibility assumption is needed for this construction.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Use upper indices for the defining [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) of [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) and lower indices for its [dual representation](../representation-theory.md#dual-representation). A [Young diagram](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-diagram) with row lengths $(\ell_1,\ell_2)$ has [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) [Dynkin labels](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-label) $(p,q)=(\ell_1-\ell_2,\ell_2)$; a full column of height three is a [determinant representation](../representation-theory.md#determinant-representation) and can be removed for [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group). The [Weyl dimension formula](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weyl-dimension-formula) reduces to

$$
\dim(p,q)=\frac{(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)}{2}.
$$

The six [irreducible representations](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) of smallest [dimensions](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) are $\mathbf1,\mathbf3,\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf6,\overline{\mathbf6},\mathbf8$. The following box arrays draw their [Young diagrams](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-diagram); filling boxes by tensor-slot labels gives representative [Young tableaux](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-tableau):

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\text{representation}&\text{diagram}&(\ell_1,\ell_2)&(p,q)&\dim\\\hline
\mathbf1&\varnothing&(0,0)&(0,0)&1\\[2pt]
\mathbf3&\boxed{\phantom{i}}&(1,0)&(1,0)&3\\[2pt]
\overline{\mathbf3}&\begin{array}{|c|}\hline\phantom{i}\\\hline\phantom{i}\\\hline\end{array}&(1,1)&(0,1)&3\\[4pt]
\mathbf6&\begin{array}{|c|c|}\hline\phantom{i}&\phantom{i}\\\hline\end{array}&(2,0)&(2,0)&6\\[4pt]
\overline{\mathbf6}&\begin{array}{|c|c|}\hline\phantom{i}&\phantom{i}\\\hline\phantom{i}&\phantom{i}\\\hline\end{array}&(2,2)&(0,2)&6\\[4pt]
\mathbf8&\begin{array}{l}\boxed{\phantom{i}}\boxed{\phantom{i}}\\[-3pt]\boxed{\phantom{i}}\end{array}&(2,1)&(1,1)&8
\end{array}
$$

Their associated [tensors](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor), in the same order, can be taken as follows:

- $\mathbf1$: a scalar $s$, unchanged by [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group).
- $\mathbf3$: a [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) $v^i$, with $v^i\mapsto U^i{}_jv^j$.
- $\overline{\mathbf3}$: an [antisymmetric tensor](../multilinear-algebra.md#antisymmetric-tensor) $A^{[ij]}$, equivalently a [covector](../dual-space.md#covector) $w_k=\tfrac12\epsilon_{kij}A^{ij}$, with $w_i\mapsto w_j(U^{-1})^j{}_i$.
- $\mathbf6$: a [symmetric tensor](../multilinear-algebra.md#symmetric-tensor) $S^{(ij)}$, with $S^{ij}\mapsto U^i{}_kU^j{}_\ell S^{k\ell}$.
- $\overline{\mathbf6}$: a symmetric [covariant tensor](../multilinear-algebra.md#covariant-tensor) $S_{(ij)}$, with $S_{ij}\mapsto S_{k\ell}(U^{-1})^k{}_i(U^{-1})^\ell{}_j$. In the four-upper-index [Young tableau](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-tableau) description, use two antisymmetric index pairs with symmetry under exchange of pairs, and convert each pair to a lower index with the invariant $\epsilon$ [tensor](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor).
- $\mathbf8$: a mixed [tensor](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor) $T^i{}_j$ with $T^i{}_i=0$, transforming as $T\mapsto UTU^{-1}$. This is the [Adjoint representation](../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra). Its nine components obey one independent [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) constraint.

These [low-dimensional SU3 tensor representations](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#low-dimensional-su3-tensor-representations) have [dimensions](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) $1,3,3,6,6,8$ by direct component counting. To check that none were missed, if $p+q\geq3$ then $(p+1)(q+1)\geq p+q+1$, so the displayed [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) is at least $(p+q+1)(p+q+2)/2\geq10$. The pairs with $p+q\leq2$ are precisely the six listed pairs.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Under the stated [SU(2)](../topological-group.md#su-2-group) embedding, the first two components of the defining [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) form a doublet, while the third is fixed. Hence $\mathbf3\downarrow_{SU(2)}=\mathbf2\oplus\mathbf1$. Its [dual representation](../representation-theory.md#dual-representation) restricts in the same way because the [SU(2)](../topological-group.md#su-2-group) doublet is self-dual.

For the [Adjoint representation](../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra), restrict $\mathbf8=\mathbf3\otimes\overline{\mathbf3}-\mathbf1$. The [SU(2)](../topological-group.md#su-2-group) [tensor product](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor-product) $\mathbf2\otimes\mathbf2=\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf1$ gives

$$
\mathbf8\downarrow_{SU(2)}
=(\mathbf2\oplus\mathbf1)\otimes(\mathbf2\oplus\mathbf1)-\mathbf1
=\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf2\oplus\mathbf2\oplus\mathbf1.
$$

Here subtraction denotes removal of the [trivial representation](../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation) generated by the identity [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix); it is not a negative-dimensional summand. The first [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) factor is unchanged, so the requested [branching of a representation](../representation-theory.md#branching-of-a-representation) rules are

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
(\mathbf3,\mathbf3)&\downarrow_H=(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)\oplus(\mathbf3,\mathbf1),\\
(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)&\downarrow_H=(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1),\\
(\mathbf1,\mathbf3)&\downarrow_H=(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1),\\
(\mathbf1,\mathbf8)&\downarrow_H=(\mathbf1,\mathbf3)\oplus2(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1).
\end{aligned}}
$$

The [dimensions](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) on the two sides are respectively $9=6+3$, $3=3$, $3=2+1$ and $8=3+2+2+1$.

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

An element commuting with the full first [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) factor has its first component in that factor's [center of a group](../group-theory.md#center-of-a-group), which is discrete. In the second factor, commutation with every $\operatorname{diag}(U,1)$ forces the doublet block to be scalar and the mixed blocks to vanish, by the [Schur lemma](../representation-theory.md#schur-s-lemma). Unitarity and unit [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) then give the connected commuting [subgroup](../group.md#subgroup)

$$
\boxed{U(1)_X=\{(I_3,\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta},e^{i\theta},e^{-2i\theta})):\theta\in\mathbb R\},\qquad X=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2).}
$$

This is the [centralizer of the standard SU2 subgroup in SU3](../group-action.md#centralizer-of-the-standard-su2-subgroup-in-su3) in the second factor; the full [centralizer](../group-action.md#centralizer) in $G$ also includes the first factor's discrete [center of a group](../group-theory.md#center-of-a-group) $\mathbb Z_3$. The [Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md) generator is $iX$ in the [anti-Hermitian](../linear-operator-theory.md#skew-hermitian-matrix) convention. The opposite sign or a common nonzero rescaling changes the charge convention, not the commuting direction.

In the defining [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation), the [U(1) charges](../representation-theory.md#u-1-charge) are $1,1,-2$. In the [dual representation](../representation-theory.md#dual-representation) they have the opposite signs. In the [Adjoint representation](../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra) a component $T^i{}_j$ has [U(1) charge](../representation-theory.md#u-1-charge) $X_i-X_j$. Thus its upper-right doublet has charge $3$, its lower-left doublet has charge $-3$, and its diagonal blocks have charge zero. Adding the charges to [solution](#3/c/solution) gives

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
(\mathbf3,\mathbf3)&\downarrow=(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)_{1}\oplus(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_{-2},\\
(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)&\downarrow=(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{0},\\
(\mathbf1,\mathbf3)&\downarrow=(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{1}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{-2},\\
(\mathbf1,\mathbf8)&\downarrow=(\mathbf1,\mathbf3)_{0}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{3}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-3}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{0}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

Each subscript denotes the [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) of $X$, so a state of charge $q$ acquires the phase $e^{iq\theta}$. The connected [subgroup](../group.md#subgroup) generated by this circle and the second [SU(2)](../topological-group.md#su-2-group) is $(SU(2)\times U(1)_X)/\mathbb Z_2$: $\theta=\pi$ gives $\operatorname{diag}(-I_2,1)$, also the central element of the embedded [SU(2)](../topological-group.md#su-2-group). This finite intersection does not change the displayed [Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md) [branching of a representation](../representation-theory.md#branching-of-a-representation) rules.

<h3 id="3/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#3/e)

The commuting direction in [solution](#3/d/solution) provides the correct candidate [Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md) for a [gauge group](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-group), $\mathfrak{su}(3)_c\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_L\oplus\mathfrak u(1)_Y$. This is a necessary structural condition for the [Standard Model](../standard-model.md), but it does not by itself establish a viable [grand unified theory](../classical-field-theory.md#grand-unified-theory).

The [representations](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) in [solution](#3/c/solution) do not give a [Standard Model fermion](../standard-model.md#standard-model-fermion) generation with the correct [weak hypercharges](../standard-model.md#hypercharge). Use $Q_{\mathrm{electric}}=T_3+Y$. Identifying $(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)_1$ with the left-handed [quark doublet](../standard-model.md#quark-doublet), whose [weak hypercharge](../standard-model.md#hypercharge) is $1/6$, fixes $Y=X/6$. But then the doublet in $(\mathbf1,\mathbf3)$ has $Y=1/6$, whereas the left-handed [lepton](../standard-model.md#lepton) doublet requires $Y=-1/2$. Its singlet has $Y=-1/3$, whereas a right-handed [electron](../physics.md#electron) requires $Y=-1$. The $(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_0$ is a further concrete mismatch: right-handed [quarks](../standard-model.md#quark) written as left-handed [antiquarks](../standard-model.md#antiquark) require $Y=-2/3$ or $1/3$, and a rescaling cannot turn zero into either value. These comparisons use the [electroweak representation and hypercharge table](../standard-model.md#electroweak-representation-and-hypercharge-table).

The charge-$-3$ doublet in $(\mathbf1,\mathbf8)$ does have the required lepton [weak hypercharge](../standard-model.md#hypercharge) under this normalization, but the same [Adjoint representation](../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra) also brings a doublet of the opposite charge and an extra triplet. Therefore merely adding that [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) does not supply the missing chiral generation without additional states and a mechanism for their [masses](../classical-mechanics.md#mass).

Furthermore $SU(3)\times SU(3)$ is a product of two [Simple Lie groups](../lie-theory.md#simple-lie-group), permitting two independent [gauge couplings](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-coupling); it does not enforce a common strong and electroweak [gauge coupling](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-coupling). Thus **the embedding exists, but the displayed matter content does not furnish a viable minimal unification of the Standard Model**. A more elaborate model would require additional [representations](../representation-theory.md#group-representation), consistent chiral matter, [anomaly cancellation](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#anomaly-cancellation), and a specified [spontaneous symmetry breaking](../quantum-field-theory.md#spontaneous-symmetry-breaking) mechanism. The calculations here do not prove that every possible extension using this product [group](../group.md) is impossible.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [2020](2020.md)

<h3 id="4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/a)

Differentiate a curve $U(t)\in SU(4)$ through $U(0)=I$, writing $X=U'(0)$. Differentiating $U(t)^\dagger U(t)=I$ gives $X^\dagger+X=0$; differentiating $\det U(t)=1$ gives $\operatorname{tr}X=0$. Conversely, for every [anti-Hermitian matrix](../linear-operator-theory.md#skew-hermitian-matrix) $X$ with zero [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace), the [matrix exponential](../linear-operator-theory.md#matrix-exponential) $e^{tX}$ is unitary and has [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) $e^{t\operatorname{tr}X}=1$. Thus the real [Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md) is

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak{su}(4)=\{X\in M_4(\mathbb C):X^\dagger=-X,\ \operatorname{tr}X=0\},\qquad [X,Y]=XY-YX.}
$$

The [commutator](../lie-algebra.md#commutator) again has zero [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) and is [anti-Hermitian](../linear-operator-theory.md#skew-hermitian-matrix), so this set is closed under the [Lie bracket](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket). Let $E_{jk}$ be the [matrix units](../vector-space.md#matrix-unit). A convenient real [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) is

$$
\boxed{A_{jk}=E_{jk}-E_{kj},\quad B_{jk}=i(E_{jk}+E_{kj})\quad(1\leq j<k\leq4),\qquad H_a=i(E_{aa}-E_{a+1,a+1})\quad(a=1,2,3).}
$$

There are six independent $A_{jk}$, six independent $B_{jk}$, and three independent $H_a$. Every permitted off-diagonal complex entry is supplied by one real combination of $A_{jk},B_{jk}$; the imaginary diagonal entries have one [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) constraint and are spanned by the $H_a$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak{su}(4)=15,\qquad\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{su}(4)=3.}
$$

For the [rank of a semisimple Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#rank-of-a-semisimple-lie-algebra), the diagonal [Cartan subalgebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#cartan-subalgebra) consists of $i\operatorname{diag}(t_1,t_2,t_3,t_4)$ with real $t_i$ and $\sum_i t_i=0$, of [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) three. Commuting [anti-Hermitian matrices](../linear-operator-theory.md#skew-hermitian-matrix) are simultaneously unitarily diagonalizable, so no larger commuting toral [Lie subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#lie-subalgebra) exists. In the common physics convention one uses the [Hermitian](../hilbert-space.md#hermitian-operator) generators $-iA_{jk},-iB_{jk},-iH_a$ and writes [group](../group.md) elements as $e^{itT}$; these are the same generators with the factor of $i$ moved into the exponential.

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

The [complexification of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#complexification-of-a-lie-algebra) gives $\mathfrak{su}(4)_{\mathbb C}=\mathfrak{sl}_4(\mathbb C)$, of type [A3 root system](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#a3-root-system). Choose [simple roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root) $\alpha_1=e_1-e_2$, $\alpha_2=e_2-e_3$, $\alpha_3=e_3-e_4$, with squared lengths two. The [Cartan matrix](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#cartan-matrix) and [Dynkin diagram](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-diagram) are

$$
\boxed{A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\underset{1}{\circ}\! -\! \underset{2}{\circ}\! -\! \underset{3}{\circ}.}
$$

Each line represents a single bond. In the [Dynkin label](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-label) convention $w_a=\langle w,\alpha_a^\vee\rangle$, the [simple roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root) have coordinates equal to the rows of $A$.

In the defining [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation), apply the [simple coroots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-coroot) $E_{aa}-E_{a+1,a+1}$ to the four coordinate [basis vectors](../vector-space.md#basis-vector). Their [weights](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-representation-theory) are

$$
\boxed{\mathbf4=(1,0,0):\quad
v_1=(1,0,0),\quad v_2=(-1,1,0),\quad v_3=(0,-1,1),\quad v_4=(0,0,-1).}
$$

Equivalently these are obtained by subtracting $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$ successively from the [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation). The [dual representation](../representation-theory.md#dual-representation) has the negative [weights](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-representation-theory), hence

$$
\boxed{\overline{\mathbf4}=(0,0,1):\quad
(-1,0,0),\quad(1,-1,0),\quad(0,1,-1),\quad(0,0,1).}
$$

All listed [weight multiplicities](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-multiplicity) are one, so both [dimensions](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) are four.

The [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) $(1,0,1)$ is $\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3$, the [highest root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-root). Its [irreducible representation](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) is therefore the [Adjoint representation](../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra). Under the action on [traceless matrices](../linear-algebra.md#traceless-matrix) by conjugation, the off-diagonal [matrix unit](../vector-space.md#matrix-unit) $E_{ij}$ has [weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-representation-theory) $v_i-v_j$, while the diagonal [Cartan subalgebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#cartan-subalgebra) has zero [weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-representation-theory). Thus

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{15}=(1,0,1):\quad
&\pm(2,-1,0),\quad\pm(-1,2,-1),\quad\pm(0,-1,2),\\
&\pm(1,1,-1),\quad\pm(-1,1,1),\quad\pm(1,0,1),\\
&(0,0,0)^{[3]}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The superscript $[3]$ indicates [weight multiplicity](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-multiplicity) three. The twelve nonzero [weights](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-representation-theory) each have [weight multiplicity](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-multiplicity) one, giving $12+3=15$ in total. This also follows from the [tensor product representation](../representation-theory.md#tensor-product-of-group-representations) $\mathbf4\otimes\overline{\mathbf4}=\mathbf{15}\oplus\mathbf1$: the sixteen differences $v_i-v_j$ contain four zero [weights](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-representation-theory), and removing the identity [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) removes one of them.

<h3 id="4/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#4/c)

The projection deletes the third [Dynkin label](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-label), so the four [weights](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-representation-theory) $v_i$ of [solution](#4/b/solution) restrict to $(1,0),(-1,1),(0,-1),(0,0)$. The first three are the defining [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) [weight system](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-system), and the fourth is a singlet. Negating them gives the [dual representation](../representation-theory.md#dual-representation). Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathbf4\downarrow_{SU(3)}=\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf1,\qquad
\overline{\mathbf4}\downarrow_{SU(3)}=\overline{\mathbf3}\oplus\mathbf1.}
$$

For the [Adjoint representation](../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra), the projected nonzero [weights](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-representation-theory) consist of the six [A2 root system](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#a2-root-system) [roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system), the three defining [weights](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-representation-theory) and their negatives. There are also three zero [weights](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-representation-theory). Two zero [weights](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-representation-theory) belong to the [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) [Adjoint representation](../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra), leaving one singlet. Therefore the [SU4 adjoint branching to SU3](../representation-theory.md#su4-adjoint-branching-to-su3) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{15}\downarrow_{SU(3)}=\mathbf8\oplus\mathbf3\oplus\overline{\mathbf3}\oplus\mathbf1.}
$$

One can see all four [invariant subspaces](../representation-theory.md#invariant-subspace) directly by writing a [traceless matrix](../linear-algebra.md#traceless-matrix) as

$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}A&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad \operatorname{tr}A+d=0.
$$

The traceless part of $A$ is $\mathbf8$, the column $b$ is $\mathbf3$, the row $c$ is $\overline{\mathbf3}$, and the remaining diagonal [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) is proportional to $\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-3)$ and is fixed by [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group). These have [dimensions](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) $8+3+3+1=15$. Equivalently restrict $\mathbf4\otimes\overline{\mathbf4}-\mathbf1$ and use $\mathbf3\otimes\overline{\mathbf3}=\mathbf8\oplus\mathbf1$.

<h3 id="4/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#4/d)

Take the [quarks](../standard-model.md#quark) $q=(u,d,s,c)^T$ in the defining $\mathbf4$ of flavour [SU(4)](../topological-group.md#su-4-group) and the [antiquarks](../standard-model.md#antiquark) in $\overline{\mathbf4}$. The flavour part of a [meson](../physics.md#meson) is a [quark](../standard-model.md#quark)-[antiquark](../standard-model.md#antiquark) pair $M^i{}_j=q^i\bar q_j$. It transforms as $M\mapsto UMU^{-1}$, so the complete [four-flavour meson multiplet](../physics.md#four-flavour-meson-multiplet) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf4\otimes\overline{\mathbf4}=\mathbf{15}\oplus\mathbf1.}
$$

Its traceless part $M-\tfrac14(\operatorname{tr}M)I_4$ is the [Adjoint representation](../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra) $\mathbf{15}$, the direct four-flavour analogue of the three-flavour [meson octet](../standard-model.md#meson-octet). The [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) supplies an additional [trivial representation](../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation). Thus “the mesons” can refer to the traceless multiplet or to the complete [quark model](../physics.md#quark-model) [tensor product](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor-product); keeping both explicit avoids dropping a state.

Using [solution](#4/c/solution), the [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) contents are

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{15}\downarrow_{SU(3)}=\mathbf8\oplus\mathbf3\oplus\overline{\mathbf3}\oplus\mathbf1,\qquad
(\mathbf{15}\oplus\mathbf1)\downarrow_{SU(3)}=\mathbf8\oplus\mathbf3\oplus\overline{\mathbf3}\oplus2\mathbf1.}
$$

Directly, the upper-left block $q^i\bar q_j$ with $i,j\leq3$ is the old $\mathbf3\otimes\overline{\mathbf3}=\mathbf8\oplus\mathbf1$; the three entries $q^i\bar c$ form $\mathbf3$, the three entries $c\bar q_j$ form $\overline{\mathbf3}$, and $c\bar c$ is another singlet. The [dimensions](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) are $9+3+3+1=16$. This is a classification by [flavour symmetry](../standard-model.md#flavor-symmetry), rather than colour or spin. The much larger [charm quark](../standard-model.md#charm-quark) [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) breaks the flavour [SU(4)](../topological-group.md#su-4-group) approximation strongly; it does not change this [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) counting.

<h3 id="4/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#4/e)

Compared with the complete three-flavour [meson](../physics.md#meson) [tensor product](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor-product), the additional flavour states are exactly

$$
\boxed{\underbrace{(u\bar c,\ d\bar c,\ s\bar c)}_{\mathbf3},\qquad
\underbrace{(c\bar u,\ c\bar d,\ c\bar s)}_{\overline{\mathbf3}},\qquad
\underbrace{c\bar c}_{\mathbf1}.}
$$

Thus there are seven new flavour states. For the [pseudoscalar mesons](../physics.md#pseudoscalar-meson), the six open-charm states are $\bar D^0,D^-,D_s^-$ and $D^0,D^+,D_s^+$, respectively, using the [D meson](../physics.md#d-meson) and [Ds meson](../physics.md#ds-meson) conventions. A pure $c\bar c$ [pseudoscalar meson](../physics.md#pseudoscalar-meson) is the idealized flavour state associated with the [eta c meson](../physics.md#eta-c-meson). The [quark model](../physics.md#quark-model) flavour calculation alone does not specify spin or spatial excitations, so the same flavour labels also organize other [meson](../physics.md#meson) species.

If comparing the traceless [SU(4)](../topological-group.md#su-4-group) $\mathbf{15}$ with the traceless [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) [meson octet](../standard-model.md#meson-octet), the extra seven states are instead the same two triplets and the normalized [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) singlet

$$
\boxed{\eta_{15}=\frac{u\bar u+d\bar d+s\bar s-3c\bar c}{\sqrt{12}}.}
$$

Its coefficient [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) is traceless and has squared norm one. In particular, $c\bar c$ by itself is not a vector in the traceless $\mathbf{15}$; its coefficient [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) has [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) one.

To relate these two descriptions, let $S_4=(u\bar u+d\bar d+s\bar s+c\bar c)/2$ be the normalized [SU(4)](../topological-group.md#su-4-group) singlet and $S_3=(u\bar u+d\bar d+s\bar s)/\sqrt3$ the old [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) singlet. Then

$$
\boxed{S_3=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}S_4+\frac12\eta_{15},\qquad
c\bar c=\frac12S_4-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\eta_{15}.}
$$

This orthonormal change of [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) shows explicitly why the complete [tensor product](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor-product) contains two light-flavour singlets but adds only one state to the old light-flavour singlet sector.
