<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [matrix](../../../../matrix.md) $r$ has distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\alpha,\alpha^2$. Any one-dimensional [invariant subspace](../../../../invariant-subspace.md) for the defining [representation](../../../../group-representation.md) would therefore have to be one of its two coordinate [eigenspaces](../../../../eigenspace.md). But $s$ exchanges those lines, so neither is invariant under the whole [group](../../../../group-split.md). Hence $R_2$ is an [irreducible representation](../../../../irreducible-representation.md) over the [complex numbers](../../../../complex-number.md).

There are three complex [irreducible representations](../../../../irreducible-representation.md) up to [equivalence of group representations](../../../../equivalence-of-group-representations.md), because there are three [conjugacy classes](../../../../conjugacy-class.md). The [trivial representation](../../../../trivial-representation.md) and the [sign representation](../../../../sign-representation.md) already give two inequivalent [one-dimensional representations](../../../../one-dimensional-representation.md). Together with $R_2$, their [dimensions](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) satisfy the [sum of squares of irreducible representation dimensions](../../../../sum-of-squares-of-irreducible-representation-dimensions.md):

$$
\boxed{1^2+1^2+2^2=6=|G|,\qquad\text{dimensions }1,1,2.}
$$

Thus there are no further [irreducible representations](../../../../irreducible-representation.md). Alternatively the [character](../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) of $R_2$ is $(2,-1,0)$, and its [character inner product](../../../../character-inner-product.md) with itself is $(4+2)/6=1$, independently confirming irreducibility.
