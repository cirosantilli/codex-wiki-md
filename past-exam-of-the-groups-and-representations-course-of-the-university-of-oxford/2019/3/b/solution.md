<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

First the [group homomorphism](../../../../group-homomorphism.md) is surjective. For any $W\in U(n)$, choose $z\in U(1)$ with $z^n=\det W$ and set $U=z^{-1}W$. Then $U$ is unitary and $\det U=z^{-n}\det W=1$, so $W=f(z,U)$.

Next $f(z,U)=I_n$ is equivalent to $U=z^{-1}I_n$, and the [special unitary group](../../../../special-unitary-group.md) condition becomes $z^n=1$. With $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/n}$, the [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{K=\{(\zeta^k,\zeta^{-k}I_n):k=0,1,\ldots,n-1\}\cong\mathbb Z_n.}
$$

It is the cyclic central [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) generated by $(\zeta,\zeta^{-1}I_n)$; the scalar and [special unitary group](../../../../special-unitary-group.md) factors are paired with inverse phases. The [first isomorphism theorem](../../../../first-isomorphism-theorem.md) now gives

$$
\boxed{U(n)\cong\big(U(1)\times SU(n)\big)/K.}
$$

Explicitly the isomorphism sends the [coset](../../../../coset.md) $[(z,U)]$ to $zU$. The $n$ possible choices of the root $z$ differ precisely by multiplication by elements of $K$, which explains why the product before quotienting is not a unique factorization.
