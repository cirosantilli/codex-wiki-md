<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Nonzero scalar [matrices](../../../../matrix.md) form a [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) $N$ of the [general linear group](../../../../general-linear-group.md), since products and inverses remain nonzero scalar [matrices](../../../../matrix.md). Moreover, for every invertible $g$,

$$
g(\lambda I_n)g^{-1}=\lambda I_n.
$$

Thus $N$ lies in the [center of a group](../../../../center-of-a-group.md) and is a [normal subgroup](../../../../normal-subgroup.md). The [quotient group](../../../../quotient-group.md) is the complex [projective linear group](../../../../projective-linear-group.md) $\operatorname{PGL}_n(\mathbb C)$, whose elements identify invertible [matrices](../../../../matrix.md) differing by a nonzero scalar.

The canonical [group homomorphism](../../../../group-homomorphism.md) $\pi:g\mapsto[g]$ obeys $[g][h]=[gh]$. Composing it with the given [group representation](../../../../group-representation.md) gives

$$
\widetilde R(gh)=[R(gh)]=[R(g)R(h)]=[R(g)][R(h)]=\widetilde R(g)\widetilde R(h).
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\widetilde R=\pi\circ R:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{PGL}_n(\mathbb C)\text{ is a group homomorphism}.}
$$

This is the [projective representation](../../../../projective-representation.md) associated with $R$: a nonzero scalar acts trivially on each line in the [projective space](../../../../projective-space-split.md) of $V$. Its [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) consists of those $g$ for which $R(g)$ is scalar, which can be larger than the [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) of $R$ itself.
