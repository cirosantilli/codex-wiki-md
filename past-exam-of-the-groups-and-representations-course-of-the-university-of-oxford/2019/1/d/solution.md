<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use the [matrices](../../../../matrix.md) of [solution](../c/solution.md). Their projective classes satisfy $[-M]=[M]$, since $-I_2\in N$. The [group homomorphism](../../../../group-homomorphism.md) is therefore explicitly

$$
\boxed{\widetilde R(\pm I_2)=[I_2],\quad
\widetilde R(\pm A)=[A],\quad
\widetilde R(\pm B)=[B],\quad
\widetilde R(\pm C)=[C].}
$$

These four classes are distinct: the two diagonal representatives are not scalar multiples of one another, the two off-diagonal representatives are not scalar multiples of one another, and a diagonal representative cannot be a nonzero scalar multiple of an off-diagonal one.

Although $A$ and $B$ anticommute as [matrices](../../../../matrix.md), their projective classes commute:

$$
[A][B]=[AB]=[-BA]=[BA]=[B][A].
$$

Furthermore $[A]^2=[B]^2=[I_2]$ and $[C]^2=[-I_2]=[I_2]$. Thus every nonidentity element of the image has order two. An explicit [group isomorphism](../../../../group-isomorphism.md) to the [Klein four-group](../../../../klein-four-group.md) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Im}\widetilde R\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2,
\qquad [A^aB^b]\longmapsto(a,b),\qquad a,b\in\{0,1\}.}
$$

Indeed reordering products introduces only a sign, which disappears in the [projective linear group](../../../../projective-linear-group.md), so this map turns multiplication into addition modulo two in each coordinate. Equivalently, $\ker\widetilde R=G\cap N=\{\pm I_2\}$ and the [first isomorphism theorem](../../../../first-isomorphism-theorem.md) gives

$$
G/\{\pm I_2\}\cong\operatorname{Im}\widetilde R\cong\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2.
$$

This [projective image of the square symmetry group](../../../../projective-image-of-the-square-symmetry-group.md) illustrates how a noncommutative [group](../../../../group-split.md) can have an [Abelian](../../../../abelian-group.md) projective image: its scalar commutator becomes the identity class.
