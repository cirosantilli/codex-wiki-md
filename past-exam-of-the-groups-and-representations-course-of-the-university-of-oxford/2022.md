<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

The [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) obey $Q^5=P^2=I$ and $PQP=Q^{-1}$. Moving every $P$ to the right therefore reduces every product to $Q^k$ or $Q^kP$, with $0\leq k<5$. The five diagonal [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) $Q^k$ are distinct because $\alpha$ is a primitive fifth [root of unity](../algebra.md#root-of-unity); the five $Q^kP$ are distinct and off-diagonal. Thus

$$
\boxed{G=\{Q^k,Q^kP:0\leq k<5\},\qquad |G|=10.}
$$

This is the [dihedral group](../finite-group-theory.md#dihedral-group) of the regular pentagon, with $Q$ a rotation and $P$ a reflection.

Conjugation by $P$ interchanges $Q^k$ and $Q^{-k}$, while conjugation by a power of $Q$ fixes rotations. Also

$$
Q^j(Q^kP)Q^{-j}=Q^{k+2j}P.
$$

Since multiplication by $2$ permutes the residues modulo $5$, all five reflections form one [conjugacy class](../group-action.md#conjugacy-class). The full list of [conjugacy classes](../group-action.md#conjugacy-class) is

$$
\boxed{\{I\},\quad\{Q,Q^4\},\quad\{Q^2,Q^3\},\quad\{P,QP,Q^2P,Q^3P,Q^4P\}.}
$$

To prove the [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) is [irreducible](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) over $\mathbb C$, any proper nonzero [invariant subspace](../representation-theory.md#invariant-subspace) would be a line invariant under $Q$. Its two distinct [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) force such a line to be one of its coordinate [eigenspaces](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace). But $P$ exchanges those two lines, so neither is $G$-invariant. Hence the given [complex representation](../representation-theory.md#complex-representation) is [irreducible](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

In a [one-dimensional representation](../representation-theory.md#one-dimensional-representation), the scalar images of $Q$ and $P$ commute. The relation $PQP=Q^{-1}$ gives $R(Q)^2=1$, while $R(Q)^5=1$, so $R(Q)=1$ and $R(P)=\pm1$. These give exactly $R_0$ and $R_1$.

For $k=1,2$, define the two-dimensional [representations](../representation-theory.md#group-representation)

$$
D_k(Q)=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha^k,\alpha^{-k}),\qquad
D_k(P)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The same [invariant subspace](../representation-theory.md#invariant-subspace) argument as in [solution](#1/a/solution) proves that each is [irreducible](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation). Set $R_2=D_1$ and $R_3=D_2$. Their [characters](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) differ at $Q$, so they are inequivalent. The [sum of squares of irreducible representation dimensions](../representation-theory.md#sum-of-squares-of-irreducible-representation-dimensions) is already $1^2+1^2+2^2+2^2=10$, proving completeness.

Put $s=2\cos(2\pi/5)=(\sqrt5-1)/2$ and $t=2\cos(4\pi/5)=-(\sqrt5+1)/2$. Taking [traces](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) yields the [character table](../representation-theory.md#character-table)

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 & I & Q,Q^4 & Q^2,Q^3 & Q^kP\\\hline
\text{class size}&1&2&2&5\\\hline
R_0&1&1&1&1\\
R_1&1&1&1&-1\\
R_2&2&s&t&0\\
R_3&2&t&s&0
\end{array}}
$$

The identities $s+t=-1$, $st=-1$, and $s^2+t^2=3$ also verify [character orthogonality](../representation-theory.md#character-orthogonality) directly.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

A [tensor product of group representations](../representation-theory.md#tensor-product-of-group-representations) has [character](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) equal to the product of its factor [characters](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation). Multiplying the rows of the [character table](../representation-theory.md#character-table) in [solution](#1/b/solution), using $s^2=2+t$, $t^2=2+s$ and $st=-1$, gives all the [Clebsch-Gordan decomposition of group representations](../representation-theory.md#clebsch-gordan-decomposition-of-group-representations):

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|cccc}
\otimes&R_0&R_1&R_2&R_3\\\hline
R_0&R_0&R_1&R_2&R_3\\
R_1&R_1&R_0&R_2&R_3\\
R_2&R_2&R_2&R_0\oplus R_1\oplus R_3&R_2\oplus R_3\\
R_3&R_3&R_3&R_2\oplus R_3&R_0\oplus R_1\oplus R_2
\end{array}}
$$

For example, the [character](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) of $R_2\otimes R_2$ is $(4,s^2,t^2,0)=(4,2+t,2+s,0)$, the sum of the [characters](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) of $R_0$, $R_1$ and $R_3$. Likewise $R_2\otimes R_3$ has [character](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) $(4,-1,-1,0)=\chi_2+\chi_3$. The [trivial representation](../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation) and $R_1\otimes R_1=R_0$ account for the remaining entries.

<h4 id="1/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/d/i)

Use the basis of [solution](#1/b/solution) for the two doublets, treating the barred left-handed fields as the components to which the printed [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) assignment applies. Write $x=(\bar\psi_L^1,\bar\psi_L^2)^T$ and $y=(\psi_R^1,\psi_R^2)^T$. The doublet contribution to the [Yukawa interaction](../standard-model.md#yukawa-interaction) is $H x^T L y$, so [invariant tensors](../representation-theory.md#invariant-tensor) obey

$$
D_1(Q)^TLD_1(Q)=L,\qquad P^TLP=L.
$$

The first equation forces $L_{11}=L_{22}=0$, since their factors are $\alpha^{\pm2}\ne1$. The second sets $L_{12}=L_{21}$. Thus the doublet [invariant tensor](../representation-theory.md#invariant-tensor) is $x_1y_2+x_2y_1$. The product of either doublet with $R_1$ has no [trivial representation](../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation), whereas $R_1\otimes R_1=R_0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\lambda^{\mathrm{(i)}}=
\begin{pmatrix}0&y&0\\y&0&0\\0&0&y_3\end{pmatrix},\qquad y,y_3\in\mathbb C.}
$$

If instead the unbarred left-handed doublet is chosen to transform by $D_1$, its barred components transform by $D_1^*$; in that equivalent basis the upper block is $yI_2$. Exchanging the two left components relates these conventions. The [singular value decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-decomposition) gives the same physical masses in either basis.

<h4 id="1/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/d/ii)

Now the doublet [invariant tensor](../representation-theory.md#invariant-tensor) would satisfy $D_1(Q)^T L D_2(Q)=L$. Its four entries acquire factors $\alpha^3$, $\alpha^{-1}$, $\alpha$, and $\alpha^{-3}$, respectively. None equals $1$, so $L=0$. Equivalently, $R_2\otimes R_3=R_2\oplus R_3$ has no [trivial representation](../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation). The mixed doublet and third-family terms are again forbidden because their [tensor product of group representations](../representation-theory.md#tensor-product-of-group-representations) contain no [trivial representation](../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation). Only the product of the two $R_1$ fields survives:

$$
\boxed{\lambda^{\mathrm{(ii)}}=
\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&0\\0&0&y_3\end{pmatrix},\qquad y_3\in\mathbb C.}
$$

<h3 id="1/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#1/e)

Let $v_H=\langle H\rangle$ be the [vacuum expectation value](../quantum-field-theory.md#vacuum-expectation-value) in the convention $M=v_H\lambda$. For canonically normalized [fermion](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#fermion) kinetic terms, physical [charged lepton](../standard-model.md#charged-lepton) masses are the nonnegative values in the [singular value decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-decomposition) of $M$, equivalently the square roots of the [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) of $M^\dagger M$.

In case (i),

$$
M^\dagger M=|v_H|^2\operatorname{diag}(|y|^2,|y|^2,|y_3|^2),\qquad
\boxed{\{m_1,m_2,m_3\}=|v_H|\{|y|,|y|,|y_3|\}.}
$$

The opposite algebraic [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $y$ and $-y$ of the doublet block do not represent different physical masses. The unavoidable twofold degeneracy conflicts with the three distinct nonzero measured [charged lepton](../standard-model.md#charged-lepton) masses, for any assignment of the three families.

In case (ii),

$$
\boxed{\{m_1,m_2,m_3\}=\{0,0,|v_Hy_3|\}.}
$$

One can fit the [tau lepton](../standard-model.md#tau-particle) mass by choosing $|v_Hy_3|$, but the [electron](../physics.md#electron) and [muon](../standard-model.md#muon) remain massless. Thus neither exact [dihedral group](../finite-group-theory.md#dihedral-group) assignment with this single singlet [complex scalar field](../scalar-field-theory.md#complex-scalar-field) reproduces the observed spectrum. Additional symmetry-breaking [Yukawa interactions](../standard-model.md#yukawa-interaction) or other sources of mass are required; a basis change cannot remove the degeneracy or change the [matrix rank](../vector-space.md#matrix-rank).

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Normalize the [simple roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root) to $(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)=2$. The [A2 root system](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#a2-root-system) has $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)=-1$, so its [Dynkin diagram](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-diagram) is two nodes joined by one bond:

$$
\underset{\alpha_1}{\circ}\! -\!\underset{\alpha_2}{\circ},\qquad
\boxed{A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

The [fundamental weights](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#fundamental-weight) satisfy $(\omega_i,\alpha_j)=\delta_{ij}$. Hence the [Gram matrix](../inner-product.md#gram-matrix) in the [fundamental weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#fundamental-weight) basis, the metric for [Dynkin label](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-label) coordinates, is

$$
\boxed{G_{ij}=(\omega_i,\omega_j)=(A^{-1})_{ij}
=\frac13\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

For [weights of a representation](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) with [Dynkin labels](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-label) $a$ and $b$, their [inner product](../inner-product.md) is $a^TGb$. In the [simple root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root) basis the metric is instead $A$ itself; stating the basis removes this apparent convention ambiguity.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Write $a=a_1$, $b=a_2$ with $a,b\geq0$, and let $\lambda=a\omega_1+b\omega_2$ be the [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation). The [positive roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#positive-root) are $\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$ and $\alpha_1+\alpha_2$, and the [Weyl vector](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#half-sum-of-positive-roots) is $\rho=\omega_1+\omega_2$. The [Weyl dimension formula](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weyl-dimension-formula) gives

$$
d(a,b)=\prod_{\alpha>0}\frac{(\lambda+\rho,\alpha)}{(\rho,\alpha)}
=\frac{a+1}{1}\frac{b+1}{1}\frac{a+b+2}{2},\qquad
\boxed{d(a,b)=\tfrac12(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2).}
$$

For the [quadratic Casimir operator](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#quadratic-casimir-operator), use the root-length normalization $C(\lambda)=(\lambda,\lambda+2\rho)$. Substituting the [Gram matrix](../inner-product.md#gram-matrix) in [solution](#2/a/solution) yields

$$
\boxed{C(a,b)=\frac23\bigl(a^2+ab+b^2+3a+3b\bigr).}
$$

Taking the [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) of the defining [quadratic Casimir operator](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#quadratic-casimir-operator) identity gives $d(a,b)C(a,b)=\dim(\mathfrak{su}(3))c(a,b)=8c(a,b)$, where $c$ is the [Dynkin index](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-index) in the matching normalization. Therefore

$$
\boxed{c(a,b)=\frac{(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)}{24}
\bigl(a^2+ab+b^2+3a+3b\bigr).}
$$

In particular $c(\mathbf3)=1$ and $c(\mathbf8)=6$. The common particle-physics convention uses $C_2=C/2$ and $T=c/2$, giving $C_2(\mathbf3)=4/3$ and $T(\mathbf3)=1/2$. The subsequent discrete [gauge anomaly](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-anomaly) congruences use the integer [Dynkin index](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-index) $c$, while the sign test for the supplied [beta function](../perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#beta-function-physics) is independent of a common rescaling of all indices.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

The [Weyl dimension formula](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weyl-dimension-formula) is increasing in each nonnegative [Dynkin label](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-label). If $a\geq3$ or $b\geq3$, the dimension is at least $d(3,0)=10$. Among $0\leq a,b\leq2$, the cases with both labels nonzero, other than $(1,1)$, have dimension at least $d(2,1)=15$. Thus the following is the complete list of [irreducible representations](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) of dimension strictly less than $10$:

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c|r|r|r}
(a,b)&R&d&C&c\\\hline
(0,0)&\mathbf1&1&0&0\\
(1,0)&\mathbf3&3&8/3&1\\
(0,1)&\overline{\mathbf3}&3&8/3&1\\
(2,0)&\mathbf6&6&20/3&5\\
(0,2)&\overline{\mathbf6}&6&20/3&5\\
(1,1)&\mathbf8&8&6&6
\end{array}}
$$

Here $\mathbf6=\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathbf3$ is the [two-index symmetric representation](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#two-index-symmetric-representation) and $\mathbf8$ is the [Adjoint representation](../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra). Interchanging $a$ and $b$ gives the [conjugate representation](../representation-theory.md#conjugate-representation), explaining equal dimensions, [Casimir eigenvalues](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#casimir-eigenvalue) and [Dynkin indices](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-index) in each conjugate pair. In the alternative convention of [solution](#2/b/solution), divide the $C$ and $c$ columns by two.

<h4 id="2/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

<h5 id="2/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/d/i)

The [Dynkin index](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-index) is additive over a [direct sum of group representations](../representation-theory.md#direct-sum-of-group-representations), so $n$ copies of the [fundamental representation](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#fundamental-representation) have $c(r_S)=n$. With $c(\mathrm{ad})=6$, the supplied [beta function](../perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#beta-function-physics) becomes

$$
\beta(g)=-\frac{g^3}{192\pi^2}(132-n).
$$

For positive [gauge coupling](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-coupling) $g$, [asymptotic freedom](../perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#asymptotic-freedom) at this order requires $\beta(g)<0$, hence $n<132$. For nonnegative integer $n$,

$$
\boxed{n_{\max}=131.}
$$

At $n=132$ the one-loop coefficient vanishes, so this formula alone does not establish [asymptotic freedom](../perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#asymptotic-freedom). This count follows the scalar multiplicity convention of the printed [beta function](../perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#beta-function-physics); changing how a real or complex [scalar field](../classical-field-theory.md#scalar-field) is counted requires changing its coefficient too.

<h4 id="2/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

<h5 id="2/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/d/ii)

The [two-index symmetric representation](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#two-index-symmetric-representation) of [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) is $\mathbf6$, with [Dynkin index](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-index) $5$. Thus $n$ copies have $c(r_S)=5n$, and the supplied [beta function](../perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#beta-function-physics) is

$$
\beta(g)=-\frac{g^3}{192\pi^2}(132-5n).
$$

The strict sign condition for [asymptotic freedom](../perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#asymptotic-freedom) is $n<132/5=26.4$, giving

$$
\boxed{n_{\max}=26.}
$$

Indeed $132-5(26)=2>0$, while $132-5(27)=-3<0$.

<h3 id="2/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/e/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#2/e)

<h5 id="2/e/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/e/i)

Use the integer normalization $c(\mathbf3)=1$ from [solution](#2/b/solution). For two [fundamental representations](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#fundamental-representation), the specified discrete [gauge anomaly](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-anomaly) coefficient is $\mathcal A=q_1+q_2$. Consequently all solutions in the [finite cyclic group](../group.md#finite-cyclic-group) of charges are

$$
\boxed{(q_1,q_2)=(q,-q)\pmod n,\qquad q\in\mathbb Z_n.}
$$

There are $n$ ordered charge pairs. The trivial pair is included: the problem does not require a faithful action. If a faithful action on these [Weyl fermions](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#weyl-spinor) were additionally required, one would retain precisely the $q$ coprime to $n$.

This cancels the discrete [gauge anomaly](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-anomaly) defined in the paper. Two left-handed [fundamental representations](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#fundamental-representation) by themselves have a nonzero pure cubic [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) [gauge anomaly](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-anomaly); a complete [gauge theory](../classical-field-theory.md#gauge-theory) would need additional [fermions](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#fermion) to cancel it. That separate condition does not alter the requested discrete congruence.

<h4 id="2/e/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#2/e)

<h5 id="2/e/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/e/ii)

For the [fundamental representation](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#fundamental-representation) and the [two-index symmetric representation](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#two-index-symmetric-representation), the [Dynkin indices](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-index) are $1$ and $5$. Therefore the specified discrete [gauge anomaly](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-anomaly) condition for $\mathbb Z_4$ is

$$
q_1+5q_2\equiv q_1+q_2\equiv0\pmod4.
$$

All ordered solutions are

$$
\boxed{(q_1,q_2)\in\{(0,0),(1,3),(2,2),(3,1)\}\pmod4.}
$$

Only $(1,3)$ and $(3,1)$ give a faithful $\mathbb Z_4$ action on this pair of [Weyl fermions](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#weyl-spinor); faithfulness was not imposed. As in [solution](#2/e/i/solution), cancellation here concerns the stipulated discrete [gauge anomaly](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-anomaly), not cancellation of the pure cubic [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) [gauge anomaly](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-anomaly). The integer normalization matters: one must not replace $c$ by a half-integer trace index inside an unchanged modular congruence.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2022](2022.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

The identity commutes with every $h\in H$, so the [centralizer](../group-action.md#centralizer) $C_G(H)$ is nonempty. If $x,y\in C_G(H)$, then for every $h\in H$,

$$
(xy)h=x(yh)=x(hy)=(xh)y=(hx)y=h(xy).
$$

Thus it is closed under multiplication. From $xh=hx$, multiplication by $x^{-1}$ on both sides gives $hx^{-1}=x^{-1}h$, so $x^{-1}\in C_G(H)$. These are exactly the [subgroup](../group.md#subgroup) conditions; hence

$$
\boxed{C_G(H)\leq G.}
$$

The proof works for the [centralizer of a subset](../group-action.md#centralizer-of-a-subset) as well; it does not require that $H$ itself be a [subgroup](../group.md#subgroup).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Write $X=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}$. Commuting with all $\operatorname{diag}(U_1,U_2)$ requires

$$
AU_1=U_1A,\quad DU_2=U_2D,\quad BU_2=U_1B,\quad CU_1=U_2C.
$$

For $n_1,n_2\geq2$, the defining [complex representations](../representation-theory.md#complex-representation) of each factor are [irreducible](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation). [Schur lemma](../representation-theory.md#schur-s-lemma) gives $A=zI_{n_1}$ and $D=wI_{n_2}$. The two block [vector spaces](../vector-space.md) are inequivalent [representations](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) of the product [group](../group.md), even when $n_1=n_2$: the first factor acts nontrivially on just the first block. Thus their off-diagonal [intertwiners](../representation-theory.md#intertwiner) vanish, $B=C=0$. [Unitarity](../vector-space.md#unitary-operator) and the unit [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) give

$$
\boxed{C_G(H)=\{\operatorname{diag}(zI_{n_1},wI_{n_2}):|z|=|w|=1,\ z^{n_1}w^{n_2}=1\}.}
$$

**The assertion that this full group is always a circle needs correction.** Put $d=\gcd(n_1,n_2)$, $a=n_1/d$, $b=n_2/d$. The continuous [group homomorphism](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism)

$$
(z,w)\longmapsto z^aw^b
$$

maps this [centralizer](../group-action.md#centralizer) onto the $d$th [roots of unity](../algebra.md#root-of-unity). Its [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is the [identity component of a Lie group](../lie-theory.md#identity-component-of-a-lie-group), parametrized injectively by $e^{i\theta}\mapsto(e^{ib\theta},e^{-ia\theta})$. Coprimality of $a,b$ proves injectivity; a [Bezout identity](../algebra.md#bezout-s-identity) proves that this parametrization exhausts the [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md). Extending the primitive integer row $(a,b)$ to a determinant-one integer matrix gives coordinates in which the constraint is simply $t^d=1$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{C_G(H)\cong U(1)\times\mathbb Z_d,\qquad C_G(H)^0\cong U(1).}
$$

For a concrete counterexample, take $n_1=n_2=2$. The constraint is $(zw)^2=1$, giving two separate circles $zw=1$ and $zw=-1$. The element $\operatorname{diag}(I_2,-I_2)$ lies in the second component, so this [centralizer](../group-action.md#centralizer) cannot be isomorphic as a [Lie group](../lie-theory.md#lie-group) to the connected [circle group](../lie-theory.md#circle-group).

For the intended specialization of this [centralizer of two special unitary blocks](../group-action.md#centralizer-of-two-special-unitary-blocks), $d=\gcd(2,3)=1$ and therefore the full [centralizer](../group-action.md#centralizer) really is a [circle group](../lie-theory.md#circle-group):

$$
\boxed{U_c(1)=\{e^{i\theta X_c}:\theta\in\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z\},\qquad
X_c=\operatorname{diag}(3,3,-2,-2,-2).}
$$

We use this primitive integer [U(1) charge](../representation-theory.md#u-1-charge) normalization below. If exactly one $n_i$ equals $1$ and the other is at least $2$, the same block-scalar formula still holds. If $n_1=n_2=1$, both factors are trivial and the [centralizer](../group-action.md#centralizer) is all of [SU(2)](../topological-group.md#su-2-group), another exception to the printed claim.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Write the defining space as $V=A\oplus B$, where $A\cong\mathbb C^2$ and $B\cong\mathbb C^3$. Use ordered pairs for the [SU(2)](../topological-group.md#su-2-group) and [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) factors, in that order. The generator $X_c$ in [solution](#3/b/solution) gives [U(1) charges](../representation-theory.md#u-1-charge) $3$ on $A$ and $-2$ on $B$, so

$$
\boxed{\mathbf5\downarrow=(\mathbf2,\mathbf1)_3\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf3)_{-2}.}
$$

Taking the [conjugate representation](../representation-theory.md#conjugate-representation) reverses the [U(1) charges](../representation-theory.md#u-1-charge). The [SU(2)](../topological-group.md#su-2-group) doublet is equivalent to its [dual representation](../representation-theory.md#dual-representation), while the [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) triplet becomes the antitriplet:

$$
\boxed{\overline{\mathbf5}\downarrow=(\mathbf2,\mathbf1)_{-3}\oplus(\mathbf1,\overline{\mathbf3})_2.}
$$

For the [two-index antisymmetric representation](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#two-index-antisymmetric-representation), split the [antisymmetric tensor](../multilinear-algebra.md#antisymmetric-tensor) $T^{IJ}=-T^{JI}$ into $T^{ab}$, $T^{a\mu}$ and $T^{\mu\nu}$, with $a,b=1,2$ and $\mu,\nu=3,4,5$. The [exterior square of a direct sum](../multilinear-algebra.md#exterior-square-of-a-direct-sum) gives

$$
\Lambda^2(A\oplus B)=\Lambda^2A\oplus(A\otimes B)\oplus\Lambda^2B.
$$

The first term is an [SU(2)](../topological-group.md#su-2-group) singlet, the middle is $(\mathbf2,\mathbf3)$, and $\Lambda^2\mathbf3\cong\overline{\mathbf3}$ by contraction with the invariant alternating [tensor](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor). Adding the [U(1) charges](../representation-theory.md#u-1-charge) of the two indices gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{10}\downarrow=(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_6\oplus(\mathbf2,\mathbf3)_1\oplus(\mathbf1,\overline{\mathbf3})_{-4}.}
$$

The dimensions $1+6+3=10$ check the [branching of a representation](../representation-theory.md#branching-of-a-representation). In the convention $Q_{\rm em}=T_3+Y$, [weak hypercharge](../standard-model.md#hypercharge) is $Y=X_c/6$.

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

The [complexification of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#complexification-of-a-lie-algebra) in the [Adjoint representation](../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra) of [SU(5)](../topological-group.md#su-5) consists of traceless endomorphisms of $V=A\oplus B$. Splitting an endomorphism into its four blocks gives traceless endomorphisms of $A$ and $B$, one relative block-scalar direction, and the two off-diagonal spaces $\operatorname{Hom}(B,A)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)$.

The first three pieces transform as $(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_0$, $(\mathbf1,\mathbf8)_0$ and $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_0$. The off-diagonal pieces have [U(1) charges](../representation-theory.md#u-1-charge) equal to output minus input charge, namely $3-(-2)=5$ and $-2-3=-5$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{24}\downarrow=(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_0\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf8)_0\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_0
\oplus(\mathbf2,\overline{\mathbf3})_5\oplus(\mathbf2,\mathbf3)_{-5}.}
$$

The dimensions are $3+8+1+6+6=24$. The first three summands are precisely the [gauge fields](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-field) of the local [gauge group](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-group) $H\times U_c(1)$. The extra [gauge fields](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-field) of the [SU(5)](../topological-group.md#su-5) [gauge theory](../classical-field-theory.md#gauge-theory) transform as

$$
\boxed{(\mathbf2,\overline{\mathbf3})_5\oplus(\mathbf2,\mathbf3)_{-5}.}
$$

Hermiticity relates the two off-diagonal blocks, so these describe twelve real [gauge fields](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-field), or six complex fields and their conjugates. Globally, the subgroup generated by $H$ and $U_c(1)$ inside [SU(5)](../topological-group.md#su-5) is $(SU(2)\times SU(3)\times U(1))/\mathbb Z_6$, since the parametrization $(U_2,U_3,e^{i\theta})\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(e^{3i\theta}U_2,e^{-2i\theta}U_3)$ has a six-element [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md). This finite quotient does not alter the stated [branching of a representation](../representation-theory.md#branching-of-a-representation).

The [symmetric square of a direct sum](../multilinear-algebra.md#symmetric-square-of-a-direct-sum) gives

$$
\operatorname{Sym}^2(A\oplus B)=\operatorname{Sym}^2A\oplus(A\otimes B)\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2B.
$$

Now $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathbf2=\mathbf3$ and $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathbf3=\mathbf6$, rather than the singlet and antitriplet arising from [exterior squares](../multilinear-algebra.md#exterior-square). Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{15}\downarrow=(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_6\oplus(\mathbf2,\mathbf3)_1\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf6)_{-4}.}
$$

The dimensions are $3+6+6=15$.

By contrast, [solution](#3/c/solution) gives the following left-handed [Weyl fermion](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#weyl-spinor) content of $\overline{\mathbf5}\oplus\mathbf{10}$:

$$
L:(\mathbf2,\mathbf1)_{-3},\quad d^c:(\mathbf1,\overline{\mathbf3})_2,\quad
Q:(\mathbf2,\mathbf3)_1,\quad u^c:(\mathbf1,\overline{\mathbf3})_{-4},\quad
e^c:(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_6.
$$

These are one [Standard Model](../standard-model.md) family, with [weak hypercharge](../standard-model.md#hypercharge) $Y=q_c/6$. The $\mathbf{15}$ contains $Q$ but lacks $L$, $d^c$, $u^c$ and $e^c$; instead it contains an [SU(2)](../topological-group.md#su-2-group) triplet and an [SU(3)](../topological-group.md#su-3-group) sextet. Equality of total dimensions therefore does not give the same [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) content, and $\mathbf{15}$ cannot supply the family.

For the [special unitary group](../topological-group.md#special-unitary-group) $SU(N)$ define $\varepsilon_i=e_i-\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^Ne_j$. <h3 id="4/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#4/e)

One left-handed [Standard Model](../standard-model.md) family requires the [SU(5)](../topological-group.md#su-5) [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) content $\mathbf{10}\oplus\overline{\mathbf5}$. No single [irreducible representation](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) in [solution](#4/b/solution) contains both. At the level of containing the required fields, the smallest choice is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{15}\oplus\overline{\mathbf6}\downarrow
=(\mathbf{10}\oplus\overline{\mathbf5})\oplus\mathbf5\oplus\mathbf1.}
$$

The additional [SU(5)](../topological-group.md#su-5) multiplets are a $\mathbf5$ and a [trivial representation](../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation). Another minimal choice using only two of the listed [irreducible representations](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) is

$$
\mathbf{15}\oplus\overline{\mathbf{15}}\downarrow
=(\mathbf{10}\oplus\overline{\mathbf5})\oplus(\overline{\mathbf{10}}\oplus\mathbf5).
$$

Its additional multiplets form the [conjugate representation](../representation-theory.md#conjugate-representation) of a family. More generally, for multiplicities $(m_6,m_{\bar6},m_{15},m_{\overline{15}})$, [branching of a representation](../representation-theory.md#branching-of-a-representation) gives

$$
(m_6+m_{15})\mathbf5\oplus(m_{\bar6}+m_{\overline{15}})\overline{\mathbf5}
\oplus m_{15}\mathbf{10}\oplus m_{\overline{15}}\overline{\mathbf{10}}
\oplus(m_6+m_{\bar6})\mathbf1.
$$

Thus containing a family requires $m_{15}\geq1$ and $m_{\bar6}+m_{\overline{15}}\geq1$.

For a consistent chiral [grand unified theory](../classical-field-theory.md#grand-unified-theory), containment alone is insufficient. Normalize the cubic [gauge anomaly](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-anomaly) coefficient by $A(\mathbf6)=1$. For the [special unitary group](../topological-group.md#special-unitary-group) $SU(N)$, $A(\Lambda^2\mathbf N)=N-4$, so for [SU(6)](../topological-group.md#su-6) it is $2$. To verify this directly, take a traceless diagonal generator with entries $x_i$: its [exterior square](../multilinear-algebra.md#exterior-square) has entries $x_i+x_j$, and

$$
\sum_{i<j}(x_i+x_j)^3=(N-1)\sum_i x_i^3+3\sum_{i\ne j}x_i^2x_j
=(N-4)\sum_i x_i^3.
$$

The [conjugate representation](../representation-theory.md#conjugate-representation) reverses this cubic [gauge anomaly](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-anomaly). The combination $\mathbf{15}\oplus\overline{\mathbf6}$ has coefficient $2-1=1$, so cannot alone be a quantum-consistent chiral [SU(6)](../topological-group.md#su-6) [gauge theory](../classical-field-theory.md#gauge-theory). The minimal anomaly-free choice with one net [chiral gauge spectrum](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#chiral-gauge-spectrum) of a family is instead

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{15}\oplus2\overline{\mathbf6}\downarrow
=(\mathbf{10}\oplus\overline{\mathbf5})\oplus(\mathbf5\oplus\overline{\mathbf5})\oplus2\mathbf1.}
$$

The additional nonsinglet fields form a [vectorlike gauge spectrum](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#vectorlike-gauge-spectrum) that can acquire an [SU(5)](../topological-group.md#su-5)-invariant mass, and the two [gauge singlets](../representation-theory.md#gauge-singlet) may serve as sterile [neutrinos](../standard-model.md#neutrino). More generally, precisely one net [chiral gauge spectrum](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#chiral-gauge-spectrum) of a family with vectorlike extra nonsinglets requires $m_{15}-m_{\overline{15}}=1$ and $m_{\bar6}-m_6=2$; these conditions also cancel the cubic [SU(6)](../topological-group.md#su-6) [gauge anomaly](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-anomaly). The $\mathbf{15}\oplus\overline{\mathbf{15}}$ choice above is anomaly-free but has zero net [chirality](../relativistic-quantum-field.md#chirality-physics).
