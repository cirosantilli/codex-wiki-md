<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

One generation of left-handed [Standard Model fermions](../../../../standard-model-fermion.md) fits into $\overline{\mathbf5}\oplus\mathbf{10}$ of an [SU(5) grand unified theory](../../../../su-5-grand-unified-theory.md). With [hypercharge](../../../../hypercharge.md) defined by $Q=T_3+Y$, their [restriction of a representation](../../../../restriction-of-a-representation.md) to $SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{\mathbf5}&\longrightarrow(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{1/3}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-1/2}
&&\text{carrying }d^c,L,\\
\mathbf{10}&\longrightarrow(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)_{1/6}\oplus(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{-2/3}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_1
&&\text{carrying }Q,u^c,e^c.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $Q$ in the multiplet list denotes the left-handed [quark](../../../../quark.md) doublet, and the superscript $c$ labels left-handed [charge-conjugate fields](../../../../charge-conjugate-field.md) of the corresponding right-handed [fermions](../../../../fermion.md).

The results of [c](../c.md) therefore give

$$
\boxed{\overline{\mathbf6}\oplus\mathbf{15}\longrightarrow
\underbrace{\overline{\mathbf5}\oplus\mathbf{10}}_{\text{one ordinary generation}}
\oplus\underbrace{\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf5}_{\text{additional multiplets}}.}
$$

Thus the required matter [representations](../../../../group-representation.md) are present. The extra singlet has [Standard Model representation](../../../../standard-model-representation.md) $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_0$ and can describe the left-handed [charge-conjugate field](../../../../charge-conjugate-field.md) of a [right-handed neutrino](../../../../right-handed-neutrino.md). Its [gauge singlet](../../../../gauge-singlet.md) character permits a [Majorana mass term](../../../../majorana-mass-term.md); a [Yukawa interaction](../../../../yukawa-interaction.md) with a lepton doublet and a suitable [Higgs field](../../../../higgs-field.md) can then supply neutrino masses. The [branching](../../../../branching-rule.md) alone does not fix those interactions or their scales.

The extra $\mathbf5$ has

$$
\mathbf5\longrightarrow(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_{-1/3}\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{1/2}.
$$

If it describes [Weyl fermions](../../../../weyl-spinor.md), these are additional coloured and electroweak [fermions](../../../../fermion.md) in the conjugate [Standard Model representations](../../../../standard-model-representation.md) to the ordinary $\overline{\mathbf5}$. With an additional $\overline{\mathbf5}$, they can form a [vectorlike gauge spectrum](../../../../vectorlike-gauge-spectrum.md) and acquire invariant masses. Pairing them with the only ordinary $\overline{\mathbf5}$ would also remove that generation's $d^c,L$ fields, so a realistic heavy pairing needs extra matter. If the $\mathbf5$ instead describes a [scalar field](../../../../scalar-field.md), it has the quantum numbers of the usual $SU(5)$ [Higgs field](../../../../higgs-field.md): a weak doublet and a coloured triplet. A single fermionic $\mathbf{15}$ does not turn its extra fermions into a scalar [Higgs field](../../../../higgs-field.md) merely by [branching](../../../../branching-rule.md).

There is a further consistency condition if these are the entire chiral matter content of an $SU(6)$ [gauge theory](../../../../gauge-theory.md). Normalize the [cubic anomaly coefficient](../../../../cubic-anomaly-coefficient.md) by $A(\mathbf6)=1$. The [dual representation](../../../../dual-representation.md) has $A(\overline{\mathbf6})=-1$, and the [two-index antisymmetric representation](../../../../two-index-antisymmetric-representation.md) has $A(\mathbf{15})=6-4=2$. Thus one copy of each has nonzero [gauge anomaly](../../../../gauge-anomaly.md), $A_{\mathrm{total}}=1$. Their inclusion of the desired $SU(5)$ multiplets is therefore not, by itself, a complete consistent chiral unified theory.

For example, [anomaly cancellation](../../../../anomaly-cancellation.md) holds for $\mathbf{15}\oplus2\overline{\mathbf6}$, whose [restriction of a representation](../../../../restriction-of-a-representation.md) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{15}\oplus2\overline{\mathbf6}\longrightarrow
(\mathbf{10}\oplus\overline{\mathbf5})\oplus(\mathbf5\oplus\overline{\mathbf5})\oplus2\mathbf1.}
$$

This leaves one ordinary generation, a potentially massive [vectorlike gauge spectrum](../../../../vectorlike-gauge-spectrum.md), and two possible singlet neutrinos. This anomaly-free matter choice is also used in explicit $SU(6)$ model constructions.[Section 3, equations (14)–(16) and following discussion: the fifteen-dimensional antisymmetric representation has anomaly coefficient 2, and the conjugate fundamental has coefficient −1](../../../../number-of-fermion-generations-from-a-novel-grand-unified-model-by-pritibhajan-byakti-and-others.md) These interpretations require additional choices of matter statistics, [spontaneous symmetry breaking](../../../../spontaneous-symmetry-breaking.md), and interactions; the [weight systems](../../../../weight-system.md) determine the multiplet content.
