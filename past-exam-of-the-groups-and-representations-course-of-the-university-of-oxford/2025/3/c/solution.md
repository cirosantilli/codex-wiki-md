<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

All [gamma matrices](../../../../gamma-matrices.md) in this [basis](../../../../basis.md) are real, and the [Lorentz transformation](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) $S(\theta)$ is real. Therefore [complex conjugation](../../../../complex-conjugation.md) commutes with the connected [Spinor representation of the Lorentz group](../../../../spinor-representation-of-the-lorentz-group.md). With a suitable phase convention, [charge conjugation](../../../../charge-conjugation.md) is simply $\psi^c=\psi^*$. The [Majorana spinor](../../../../majorana-spinor.md) condition $\psi^c=\psi$ becomes

$$
\boxed{\psi_M=\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix},\qquad a^*=a,\quad b^*=b.}
$$

The reality constraint is preserved by [Lorentz transformations](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) because $(S\psi_M)^*=S\psi_M$. For fermion fields, real components mean self-conjugate [Grassmann variables](../../../../grassmann-variable.md) or self-adjoint field components, rather than ordinary commuting real numbers.

Moreover, $\Gamma=\sigma_3$ is real, so [charge conjugation](../../../../charge-conjugation.md) commutes with the [chiral projectors](../../../../chiral-projector.md). The [Majorana spinor](../../../../majorana-spinor.md) and [Weyl spinor](../../../../weyl-spinor.md) conditions can therefore be imposed simultaneously. The [Majorana-Weyl spinors](../../../../majorana-weyl-spinor.md) are

$$
\boxed{\psi_{MR}=\begin{pmatrix}a\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\psi_{ML}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\b\end{pmatrix},\qquad a^*=a,\ b^*=b.}
$$

The independent real component counts are

$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{spinor type}&\text{real components}\\\hline
\text{Dirac}&4\\
\text{Weyl, either chirality}&2\\
\text{Majorana}&2\\
\text{Majorana-Weyl, either chirality}&1
\end{array}.}
$$

These are component counts before imposing an equation of motion. In particular the simultaneous reality and [chirality](../../../../chirality-physics.md) constraints are compatible in this spacetime dimension, unlike the familiar four-dimensional case.
