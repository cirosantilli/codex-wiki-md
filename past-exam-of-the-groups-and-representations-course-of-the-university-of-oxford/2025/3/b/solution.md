<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Choose the [chirality](../../../../chirality-physics.md) operator

$$
\Gamma=\gamma^0\gamma^1=\sigma_3,\qquad \Gamma^2=I,\qquad \{\Gamma,\gamma^\mu\}=0.
$$

A [Weyl spinor](../../../../weyl-spinor.md) is a [Dirac spinor](../../../../dirac-spinor.md) restricted to one [eigenspace](../../../../eigenspace.md) of $\Gamma$. The [chiral projectors](../../../../chiral-projector.md) are $P_R=(I+\Gamma)/2$ and $P_L=(I-\Gamma)/2$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\psi_R=P_R\psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_+\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\psi_L=P_L\psi=\begin{pmatrix}0\\\psi_-\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

The two independent [Weyl spinors](../../../../weyl-spinor.md) each contain one complex component. Since $[S(\theta),\Gamma]=0$, each [chirality](../../../../chirality-physics.md) is preserved by every connected [Lorentz transformation](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md), with $\psi_R\mapsto e^{\theta/2}\psi_R$ and $\psi_L\mapsto e^{-\theta/2}\psi_L$. A spatial reflection exchanges the two [chiralities](../../../../chirality-physics.md).

The left/right names depend on the sign convention for $\Gamma$. Choosing $\gamma_0\gamma_1=-\sigma_3$ as the [chirality](../../../../chirality-physics.md) operator exchanges the labels $L$ and $R$; the two invariant one-component [representations](../../../../group-representation.md) are the same.
