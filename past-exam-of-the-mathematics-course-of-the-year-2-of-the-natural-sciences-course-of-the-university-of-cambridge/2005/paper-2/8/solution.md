<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

A left [coset](../../../../coset.md) is $gH=\{gh:h\in H\}$. The cosets partition a finite group into equally sized sets of $|H|$ elements, so [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) gives $|G|=[G:H]|H|$; in particular subgroup order divides group order.

Here $A=-I$, $C=-B$ and $B^2=I$. The [Cayley table](../../../../cayley-table.md), with row element multiplied on the left of the column element, is

| $\cdot$ | $I$ | $A$ | $B$ | $C$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $I$ | $I$ | $A$ | $B$ | $C$ |
| $A$ | $A$ | $I$ | $C$ | $B$ |
| $B$ | $B$ | $C$ | $I$ | $A$ |
| $C$ | $C$ | $B$ | $A$ | $I$ |

The table proves closure, $I$ is the identity and every element is its own inverse. Associativity is inherited from matrix multiplication, so this is the [Klein four-group](../../../../klein-four-group.md).

Finally, if every nonidentity element has order two, every element satisfies $h^{-1}=h$, including the identity. Then $ab=(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}=ba$. Thus a [group of exponent two is abelian](../../../../group-of-exponent-two-is-abelian.md).
