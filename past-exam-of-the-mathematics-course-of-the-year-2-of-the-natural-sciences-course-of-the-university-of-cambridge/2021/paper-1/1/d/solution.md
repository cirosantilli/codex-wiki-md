<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Put $s^2=x^2+y^2$. For $\rho>0$, elimination of $u$ gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{s^2}{\cosh^2\rho}+\frac{z^2}{\sinh^2\rho}=1.}
$$

These are [oblate spheroids](../../../../../oblate-spheroid.md) with equatorial radius $\cosh\rho$ and polar radius $\sinh\rho$, flattened along the $z$ axis. At $\rho=0$ the limiting surface is the disk $z=0$, $s\leq1$.

For $0<|u|<\pi/2$, elimination of $\rho$ instead gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{s^2}{\cos^2u}-\frac{z^2}{\sin^2u}=1.}
$$

These are [one-sheet hyperboloids](../../../../../one-sheet-hyperboloid.md) of revolution, restricted to their upper half for $u>0$ and lower half for $u<0$. The restriction matters because $\rho\geq0$. At $u=0$, the surface degenerates to the exterior equatorial plane $z=0$, $s\geq1$; at $u=\pm\pi/2$ it degenerates to the corresponding half of the $z$ axis.

Finally, fixed $v$ specifies a vertical half-plane through the $z$ axis, with horizontal direction $(\cos v,\sin v)$. The two values $v$ and $v+\pi$ together give a full plane. These surfaces form the mutually orthogonal families of the [hyperbolic parametrization of oblate spheroidal coordinates](../../../../../hyperbolic-parametrization-of-oblate-spheroidal-coordinates.md).
