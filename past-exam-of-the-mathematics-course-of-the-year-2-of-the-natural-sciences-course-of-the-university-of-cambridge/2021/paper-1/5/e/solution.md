<h1 id="5/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The [Hermitian conjugate](../../../../../hermitian-conjugation.md) of a real matrix is its [transpose](../../../../../transpose.md), so

$$
M^\dagger=(R_1-R_1^T)^T=R_1^T-R_1=-M.
$$

Therefore $\boxed{M\text{ is anti-Hermitian for all }\theta,\phi.}$ It is also a real [skew-symmetric matrix](../../../../../skew-symmetric-matrix.md). It is Hermitian as well only in the exceptional case $M=0$, precisely when $R_1$ is symmetric. This includes the identity and half-turn cases from part (d), so the zero-matrix overlap should not be excluded.
