<h1 id="5/d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A real [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) is a [Hermitian matrix](../../../../../../hermitian-operator.md) exactly when it is a [symmetric matrix](../../../../../../symmetric-matrix.md). Since $R_1$ is orthogonal, this is equivalent to $R_1=R_1^{-1}$, or $R_1^2=I$. A generic rotation does not satisfy this; for example $\theta=0$, $\phi=\pi/2$ gives a nonsymmetric quarter-turn. Thus $\boxed{R_1\text{ is not Hermitian in general.}}$

The exceptions are the identity and half-turns, as characterized by [real rotation matrices that are Hermitian](../../../../../../real-rotation-matrices-that-are-hermitian.md). For this product, a half-turn occurs when either $\theta$ or $\phi$ is an odd multiple of $\pi$; the identity occurs when both are even multiples. For example, the half-turn condition follows from $\operatorname{tr}R_1=(1+\cos\theta)(1+\cos\phi)-1=-1$.
