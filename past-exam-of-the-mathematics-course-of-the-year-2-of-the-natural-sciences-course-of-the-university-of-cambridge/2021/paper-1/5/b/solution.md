<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The $z$ direction is fixed, giving the [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) one. The planar block has [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md)

$$
(\cos\phi-\lambda)^2+\sin^2\phi=\lambda^2-2\lambda\cos\phi+1.
$$

Thus the full list of [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is

$$
\boxed{1,\quad e^{i\phi},\quad e^{-i\phi}.}
$$

The last two are real precisely when $\sin\phi=0$, so all three are real exactly for $\boxed{\phi\in\pi\mathbb Z}$. At even multiples of $\pi$ the matrix is the identity and every vector is fixed. At odd multiples it is a half-turn: the axial [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) remains $+1$, while the whole perpendicular plane has [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $-1$.

For other angles, the only real [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) direction is the rotation axis; the perpendicular plane is rotated rather than preserving an individual real direction. Its complex [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) can be taken as $(1,-i,0)^T$ and $(1,i,0)^T$ for $e^{i\phi}$ and $e^{-i\phi}$, respectively. These represent the two [complex conjugate](../../../../../complex-conjugate.md) planar eigenvalues.
