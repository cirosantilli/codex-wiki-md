<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $A=A^\dagger$ be a [Hermitian matrix](../../../../../hermitian-operator.md) and $Av=\lambda v$ with $v\ne0$. The scalar $v^\dagger Av$ equals its own [complex conjugate](../../../../../complex-conjugate.md), because

$$
(v^\dagger Av)^*=v^\dagger A^\dagger v=v^\dagger Av.
$$

Since $v^\dagger Av=\lambda v^\dagger v$ and the squared [norm](../../../../../norm.md) $v^\dagger v$ is positive, $\boxed{\lambda\in\mathbb R}$.

Now take $Au=\lambda u$ and $Av=\mu v$. The [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) satisfies

$$
u^\dagger Av=\mu u^\dagger v,\qquad u^\dagger Av=(Au)^\dagger v=\overline\lambda\,u^\dagger v=\lambda u^\dagger v.
$$

Subtracting gives $(\mu-\lambda)u^\dagger v=0$. Thus distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) imply $\boxed{u^\dagger v=0}$, proving [orthogonality](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) of the corresponding [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md). Within a degenerate [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md), an orthonormal basis may be chosen, but arbitrary distinct vectors in that same [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) need not be orthogonal.
