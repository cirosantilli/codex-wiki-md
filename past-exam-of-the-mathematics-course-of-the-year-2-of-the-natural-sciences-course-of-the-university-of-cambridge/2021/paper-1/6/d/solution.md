<h1 id="6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use $U^\dagger=U^{-1}$ and take the [Hermitian conjugate](../../../../../hermitian-conjugation.md) in the reverse product order:

$$
B^\dagger=-i(I-U^{-1})^{-1}(I+U^{-1}).
$$

The factorization $I-U^{-1}=-U^{-1}(I-U)$ gives $(I-U^{-1})^{-1}=-(I-U)^{-1}U$, while $I+U^{-1}=U^{-1}(I+U)$. Consequently

$$
B^\dagger=i(I-U)^{-1}(I+U)=i(I+U)(I-U)^{-1}=\boxed{B}.
$$

The last equality uses the fact that $I+U$ commutes with $I-U$ and its inverse. Thus $B$ is a [Hermitian matrix](../../../../../hermitian-operator.md). The exclusion of the [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) one is essential to define the inverse. Spectrally, an [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $e^{i\vartheta}\ne1$ is sent to $-\cot(\vartheta/2)$, a real number. This is the [inverse Cayley transform of a unitary matrix without eigenvalue one](../../../../../inverse-cayley-transform-of-a-unitary-matrix-without-eigenvalue-one.md).
