<h1 id="7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Using the exponential form of the complex cosine, $\cos z=0$ is equivalent to $e^{2iz}=-1$. Thus the [zeros](../../../../../zero-of-a-function.md) are exactly

$$
\boxed{z_n=\frac\pi2+n\pi,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

At each point $\cos'(z_n)=-\sin z_n\ne0$, so the cosine has a simple zero. Cubing it gives

$$
\cos^3z=[-\sin z_n]^3(z-z_n)^3+O((z-z_n)^4).
$$

The [order of a zero of a holomorphic function](../../../../../order-of-a-zero-of-a-holomorphic-function.md) is therefore three at every zero. There are no finite singularities because this is an [entire function](../../../../../entire-function.md).

To classify infinity, examine $\cos^3(1/w)$ at $w=0$. The identity $\cos^3z=(3\cos z+\cos3z)/4$ shows that its [Laurent series](../../../../../laurent-series.md) contains the nonzero negative-power coefficients

$$
\frac{(-1)^m(3+3^{2m})}{4(2m)!}\,w^{-2m},\qquad m\geq1.
$$

There are infinitely many terms in the [principal part](../../../../../principal-part-of-a-meromorphic-function.md), the criterion for an [essential singularity](../../../../../essential-singularity.md). Therefore $\boxed{\infty\text{ is an essential singularity, not a pole.}}$ This is also the general characterization of a nonpolynomial [entire function](../../../../../entire-function.md) at infinity.
