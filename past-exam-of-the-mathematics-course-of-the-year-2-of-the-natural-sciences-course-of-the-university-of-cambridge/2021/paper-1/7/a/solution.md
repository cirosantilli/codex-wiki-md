<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a [holomorphic function](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) with $z=re^{i\theta}$, the [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives

$$
f_r=e^{i\theta}f'(z),\qquad f_\theta=ire^{i\theta}f'(z)=irf_r.
$$

Writing $f=u+iv$ and equating [real parts](../../../../../real-part.md) and [imaginary parts](../../../../../imaginary-part.md),

$$
u_\theta=-rv_r,\qquad v_\theta=ru_r.
$$

For $r>0$, division by $r$ gives the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) in [polar coordinates](../../../../../polar-coordinates.md):

$$
\boxed{\frac1r u_\theta=-v_r,\qquad u_r=\frac1r v_\theta.}
$$

The restriction $r>0$ reflects the coordinate singularity at the origin; the Cartesian equations have no such restriction.
