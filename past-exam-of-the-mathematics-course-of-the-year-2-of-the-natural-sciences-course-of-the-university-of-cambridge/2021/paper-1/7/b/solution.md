<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Insert the specified real part into the polar [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md). The first gives $v_r=nr^{n-1}\sin(n\theta)$, and integration with respect to $r$ gives

$$
v(r,\theta)=r^n\sin(n\theta)+C(\theta).
$$

The second equation then requires $v_\theta=nr^n\cos(n\theta)$, so $C'(\theta)=0$. The remaining imaginary additive constant is fixed by $f(0)=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{v(r,\theta)=r^n\sin(n\theta),\qquad f(z)=r^ne^{in\theta}=z^n.}
$$

The resulting [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is holomorphic at the origin too, consistently extending the calculation made in polar coordinates with $r>0$.
