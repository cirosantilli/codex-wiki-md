<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

After division by the leading coefficient, write $y''+P(x)y'+Q(x)y=0$, where

$$
P(x)=-\frac1{x(1+x)},\qquad Q(x)=\frac{x+3}{4x^2(1+x)}.
$$

The only finite [singular points of a differential equation](../../../../../singular-point-of-a-differential-equation.md) are $\boxed{x=0\text{ and }x=-1}$. At zero, $xP=-1/(1+x)$ and $x^2Q=(x+3)/[4(1+x)]$ are analytic. At $-1$, $(x+1)P=-1/x$ and $(x+1)^2Q=(x+1)(x+3)/(4x^2)$ are analytic. The [regular singular point criterion for a second-order equation](../../../../../regular-singular-point-criterion-for-a-second-order-equation.md) therefore makes both points regular.

If infinity is included, it is a [regular singular point at infinity](../../../../../regular-singular-point-at-infinity.md) as well: $P(x)=O(x^{-2})$ and $Q(x)=1/(4x^2)+O(x^{-3})$. Equivalently, with $t=1/x$ the transformed coefficients are $\widetilde P=2/t+1/(1+t)$ and $\widetilde Q=(1+3t)/[4t^2(1+t)]$, which satisfy the same regular-singular criterion at $t=0$.
