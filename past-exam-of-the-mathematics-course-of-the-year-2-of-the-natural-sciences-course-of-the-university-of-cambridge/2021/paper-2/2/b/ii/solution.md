<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Choose the separated cosine solutions instead:

$$
\boxed{\Phi(x,y)=\cos(mx)\cos(py).}
$$

Its derivatives are $\Phi_x=-m\sin(mx)\cos(py)$ and $\Phi_y=-p\cos(mx)\sin(py)$. They vanish on the appropriate opposite sides of the square for integer $m,p$, so the outward normal derivative is zero everywhere on the boundary. Also $\Delta\Phi=-(m^2+p^2)\Phi$.

This is nonzero for every pair of integer indices, including a zero index. For $m=p=0$ it is the constant solution, illustrating the zero mode allowed by [Neumann boundary conditions](../../../../../../neumann-boundary-condition.md). Negative indices give the same cosine modes. The construction is the Neumann part of the [Dirichlet and Neumann spectra of a square](../../../../../../dirichlet-and-neumann-spectra-of-a-square.md).
