<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Direct multiplication gives $T^2=P^2=I$, $TP=PT=-I$, and

$$
TB(x)T=PB(x)P=B(-x).
$$

Thus conjugation by each generator of $G'$ preserves $G$; so does conjugation by every product of the generators. Therefore $\boxed{G\trianglelefteq G'}$.

Using $TB(x)=B(-x)T$ and its analogue for $P$, every group word can be reduced to $B(x)S$ with $S\in\{I,T,P,TP\}$. Consequently the quotient has at most four [cosets](../../../../../coset.md). They are distinct: a nonidentity diagonal matrix among $T,P,-I$ cannot equal $B(x)$, since a diagonal $B(x)$ requires $\sinh x=0$ and hence $B(x)=I$. Therefore

$$
G'/G=\{G,TG,PG,TPG\}.
$$

The three nonidentity [cosets](../../../../../coset.md) all square to $G$, and $TG$ and $PG$ commute because $T,P$ commute. Hence

$$
\boxed{G'/G\cong C_2\times C_2,\quad\text{the Klein four-group}.}
$$

Equivalently, $G'$ is the full real $O(1,1)$ group and $G$ its [identity component](../../../../../identity-component.md). This is the [component quotient of the real Lorentz group in two dimensions](../../../../../component-quotient-of-the-real-lorentz-group-in-two-dimensions.md); the quotient records the four components, not the continuous boost parameter.
