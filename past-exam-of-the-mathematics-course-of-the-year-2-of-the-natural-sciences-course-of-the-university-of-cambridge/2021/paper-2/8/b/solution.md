<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $N\leq H$ and $h\in H$, a left [coset](../../../../../coset.md) is $hN=\{hn:n\in N\}$ and a right [coset](../../../../../coset.md) is $Nh=\{nh:n\in N\}$. A [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) satisfies $hNh^{-1}=N$ for every $h\in H$, equivalently $hN=Nh$.

If $N$ is normal, the [quotient group](../../../../../quotient-group.md) is the set $H/N$ of [cosets](../../../../../coset.md), with multiplication

$$
\boxed{(hN)(kN)=(hk)N.}
$$

To check independence of representatives, replace $h,k$ by $hn_1,kn_2$. Then

$$
(hn_1)(kn_2)=hk(k^{-1}n_1k)n_2\in hkN,
$$

because normality puts $k^{-1}n_1k$ in $N$. Thus the operation is well defined. Associativity follows from that in $H$, the identity is $N$, and the inverse of $hN$ is $h^{-1}N$. This proves the group axioms for $H/N$ and explains the role of [coset multiplication is well-defined exactly for normal subgroups](../../../../../coset-multiplication-is-well-defined-exactly-for-normal-subgroups.md).
