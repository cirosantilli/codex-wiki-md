<h1 id="9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Rotations commute with one another, while reflection conjugation sends $R^j$ to $R^{-j}$. Thus the rotational [conjugacy classes](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) are pairs $\{R^j,R^{-j}\}$, except when the two entries coincide. For reflections, direct use of the relations gives

$$
R^s(R^jm)R^{-s}=R^{j+2s}m,\qquad m(R^jm)m^{-1}=R^{-j}m.
$$

If $n$ is odd, multiplication by two permutes [residues](../../../../../residue.md) modulo $n$, so all reflections are conjugate. The complete class list is

$$
\boxed{\{I\};\quad\{R^j,R^{-j}\}\ (1\leq j\leq(n-1)/2);\quad\{R^jm:0\leq j<n\}.}
$$

Their sizes are $1$, then $(n-1)/2$ classes of size two, then one class of size $n$, giving $(n+3)/2$ classes and $2n$ elements in total.

If $n$ is even, the half-turn $R^{n/2}$ is central and forms its own singleton. Conjugation changes a reflection exponent by an even amount or negates it, so it preserves parity and reaches every exponent of that parity. The list is

$$
\boxed{\begin{gathered}\{I\},\quad\{R^{n/2}\};\qquad\{R^j,R^{-j}\}\ (1\leq j<n/2);\\
\{R^{2j}m:0\leq j<n/2\},\qquad\{R^{2j+1}m:0\leq j<n/2\}.
\end{gathered}}
$$

There are $n/2+3$ classes; their sizes are $1,1$, then $n/2-1$ pairs, then two classes of size $n/2$. Geometrically the two reflection classes correspond to vertex-axis and edge-axis reflections, in either order depending on the choice of $m$. These are the [conjugacy classes of a finite dihedral group](../../../../../conjugacy-classes-of-a-finite-dihedral-group.md).
