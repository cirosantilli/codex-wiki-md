<h1 id="6/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [pseudotensor](../../../../../../pseudotensor.md) has an additional [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) factor under an orthogonal transformation. Thus its rank-three transformation law is

$$
\epsilon'_{ijk}=(\det L)L_{ia}L_{jb}L_{kc}\epsilon_{abc}.
$$

The contracted expression $L_{ia}L_{jb}L_{kc}\epsilon_{abc}$ is fully antisymmetric in $i,j,k$, so it equals a scalar multiple of $\epsilon_{ijk}$. For $(i,j,k)=(1,2,3)$ it is precisely the [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) expansion of $L$, fixing that scalar to $\det L$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\epsilon'_{ijk}=(\det L)^2\epsilon_{ijk}=\epsilon_{ijk}.}
$$

The [Levi-Civita symbol](../../../../../../levi-civita-symbol.md) consequently has the same numerical entries in every orthonormal frame when interpreted as a pseudotensor. With an ordinary rank-three [tensor](../../../../../../tensor.md) transformation law alone it would instead acquire a minus sign under an orientation-reversing transformation.
