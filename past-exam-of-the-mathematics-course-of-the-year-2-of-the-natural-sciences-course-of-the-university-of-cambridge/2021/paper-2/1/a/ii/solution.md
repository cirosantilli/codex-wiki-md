<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $L\phi=\lambda\phi$ with $\phi\ne0$ in the [self-adjoint](../../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md) domain. By the boundary identity from part (i),

$$
\lambda\langle\phi,\phi\rangle=\langle\phi,L\phi\rangle=\langle L\phi,\phi\rangle=\lambda^*\langle\phi,\phi\rangle.
$$

Since its squared weighted [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) is positive, $\boxed{\lambda\in\mathbb R}$.

For $L\phi_n=\lambda_n\phi_n$ and $L\phi_m=\lambda_m\phi_m$, the same identity gives

$$
\lambda_n\langle\phi_m,\phi_n\rangle=\lambda_m\langle\phi_m,\phi_n\rangle.
$$

Thus $\lambda_n\ne\lambda_m$ implies

$$
\boxed{\int_a^b\phi_m^*(x)\phi_n(x)w(x)\,dx=0.}
$$

This is [orthogonality of Sturm-Liouville eigenfunctions](../../../../../../orthogonality-of-sturm-liouville-eigenfunctions.md). Degenerate [eigenspaces](../../../../../../eigenspace.md) can be given an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md), although vectors in a degenerate [eigenspace](../../../../../../eigenspace.md) are not automatically orthogonal.
