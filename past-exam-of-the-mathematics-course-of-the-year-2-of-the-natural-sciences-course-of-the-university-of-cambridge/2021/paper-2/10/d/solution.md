<h1 id="10/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For a one-dimensional [character](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md), write $\chi(i)=\epsilon$ and $\chi(j)=\eta$. Since $\chi(-1)=1$, the relations $i^2=j^2=-1$ require $\epsilon,\eta\in\{\pm1\}$. Also $ij=k$ gives $\chi(k)=\epsilon\eta$. The four sign choices therefore supply all four linear characters.

For the remaining two-dimensional [character](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) $\chi_2$, its value at the identity is two. Column [orthogonality](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) between the identity and $-1$ gives $4+2\chi_2(-1)=0$, so $\chi_2(-1)=-2$. Between the identity and each of the other classes, the four linear-character values sum to zero, forcing the remaining [character](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) values to vanish. Thus the [character table](../../../../../character-table.md) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
&\{1\}&\{-1\}&\{\pm i\}&\{\pm j\}&\{\pm k\}\\\hline
\text{class size}&1&1&2&2&2\\\hline
\chi_{++}&1&1&1&1&1\\
\chi_{+-}&1&1&1&-1&-1\\
\chi_{-+}&1&1&-1&1&-1\\
\chi_{--}&1&1&-1&-1&1\\
\chi_2&2&-2&0&0&0
\end{array}.}
$$

Here column [orthogonality](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) is the standard finite-group [character](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) theorem $\sum_\rho\chi_\rho(e)^*\chi_\rho(g)=0$ for $g\ne e$. The rows also have unit [norm](../../../../../norm.md) and are mutually orthogonal in the [character inner product](../../../../../character-inner-product.md) with the displayed class-size weights. This completes the [character table of the quaternion group](../../../../../character-table-of-the-quaternion-group.md).
