<h1 id="10/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Taking traces of the given matrices gives the [character](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) on the five classes,

$$
\chi_D=(4,-4,0,0,0).
$$

For a complex finite-group representation, the multiplicity of an irrep $\rho$ is its [character inner product](../../../../../character-inner-product.md) with $\chi_D$:

$$
n_\rho=\frac1{|H|}\sum_{C}|C|\,\chi_\rho(C)^*\chi_D(C).
$$

Every linear [character](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) has value one at both $1$ and $-1$, so its multiplicity is $(4-4)/8=0$. For the two-dimensional [character](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md),

$$
n_2=\frac18[2\cdot4+(-2)(-4)]=2.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{D\cong\rho_2\oplus\rho_2,\qquad(n_{++},n_{+-},n_{-+},n_{--},n_2)=(0,0,0,0,2).}
$$

The dimensions check: $2\times2=4$.

The choice of scalar field matters here, even though the displayed matrices are real. Over $\mathbb R$, this particular four-dimensional representation is irreducible: for any nonzero real vector $v$, the vectors $v,D(i)v,D(j)v,D(k)v$ are mutually orthogonal with the same nonzero [norm](../../../../../norm.md). This follows from the skew-symmetry and quaternion multiplication identities of the three matrices. A nonzero invariant real subspace contains all four vectors and is therefore the whole space. Its complexification is the two-copy decomposition above.
