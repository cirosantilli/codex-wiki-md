<h1 id="10/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The elements $1,-1$ commute with the whole [quaternion group](../../../../../quaternion-group.md), so they form singleton classes. Each of the three remaining pairs is a [conjugacy class](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md): for example conjugation by $j$ sends $i$ to $-i$, and likewise cyclically. No such pair can mix with a different pair, because the quotient by $\{\pm1\}$ is abelian and its [cosets](../../../../../coset.md) $\{\pm i\}$, $\{\pm j\}$, $\{\pm k\}$ are preserved by conjugation. Hence

$$
\boxed{\{1\},\quad\{-1\},\quad\{i,-i\},\quad\{j,-j\},\quad\{k,-k\}.}
$$

For a finite group over $\mathbb C$, the number of inequivalent [irreducible representations](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) equals the number of [conjugacy classes](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md), so there are five. The [sum of squares of irreducible representation dimensions](../../../../../sum-of-squares-of-irreducible-representation-dimensions.md) is the [group order](../../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md), eight. With five positive integer dimensions, the only possibility is

$$
\boxed{1,1,1,1,2.}
$$

Indeed five one-dimensional contributions give five, and replacing one by a two-dimensional contribution adds exactly three; any further enlargement would exceed eight. These are the dimensions used in the [character table of the quaternion group](../../../../../character-table-of-the-quaternion-group.md).
