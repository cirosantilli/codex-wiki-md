<h1 id="9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $H$ be a proper [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md). By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), $|H|$ divides $21=3\cdot7$. Since $H\ne G$, its possibilities are

$$
|H|\in\{1,3,7\}.
$$

The subgroup of order one is generated by the identity and is cyclic. For either prime order $p=3$ or $7$, choose $h\in H$ other than the identity. Its [order of a group element](../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) divides $p$ and is not one, so it is $p$. Thus its powers exhaust $H$, proving $H=\langle h\rangle$.

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{Every proper subgroup of }G\text{ is cyclic, of order }1,3\text{ or }7.}
$$

This uses the fact that [a group of prime order is cyclic](../../../../../a-group-of-prime-order-is-cyclic.md). It does not require the order-21 group itself to be Abelian or cyclic.
