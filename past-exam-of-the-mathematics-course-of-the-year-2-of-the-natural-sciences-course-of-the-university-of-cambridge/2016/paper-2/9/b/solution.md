A [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) $\phi:H\to G$ is a map preserving multiplication:

$$
\boxed{\phi(h_1h_2)=\phi(h_1)\phi(h_2)\quad\text{for all }h_1,h_2\in H.}
$$

It necessarily sends $e_H$ to $e_G$ and sends inverses to inverses, by applying this relation to $e_H^2$ and $hh^{-1}$. Its [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) is

$$
\boxed{\ker\phi=\{h\in H:\phi(h)=e_G\}.}
$$

The kernel is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of the domain: if $k\in\ker\phi$ and $h\in H$, then $\phi(hkh^{-1})=\phi(h)e_G\phi(h)^{-1}=e_G$. The kernel’s identity condition refers to the identity in the target group.
