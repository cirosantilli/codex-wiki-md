<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write $H=\{e,h\}$ with $h\ne e$. For any $g\in G$, the [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) property implies $ghg^{-1}\in H$. This conjugate cannot be $e$, since multiplication by $g^{-1}$ and $g$ would then give $h=e$. There is only one remaining choice, so

$$
ghg^{-1}=h,\qquad gh=hg.
$$

Thus $h$ commutes with every element of $G$; the identity does too. Hence

$$
\boxed{H\subseteq Z(G).}
$$

This proves that a [normal subgroup of order two is central](../../../../../normal-subgroup-of-order-two-is-central.md). The proof does not require $G$ to be finite: conjugation permutes $H$ and fixes its identity, so it must fix its only other element.
