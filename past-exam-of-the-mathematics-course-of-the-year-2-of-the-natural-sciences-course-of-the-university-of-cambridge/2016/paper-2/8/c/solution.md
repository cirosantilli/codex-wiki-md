<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For every positive integer $m$, the group laws give $(g^{-1})^m=(g^m)^{-1}$. Consequently

$$
g^m=e\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(g^{-1})^m=e.
$$

The sets of positive exponents returning the two elements to the [identity element](../../../../../identity-element.md) are identical. If this set is nonempty, its least member is the [order of a group element](../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) in both cases. If it is empty, neither element has finite order. Therefore, including the infinite-order case,

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}(g^{-1})=\operatorname{ord}(g).}
$$

For a direct divisibility argument in the finite case, if $g$ has order $n$, then $(g^{-1})^n=e$, so its inverse’s order divides $n$. Reversing the roles shows $n$ divides that order, forcing equality. This is the general fact that [an element and its inverse have the same order](../../../../../an-element-and-its-inverse-have-the-same-order.md); it also reflects $\langle g\rangle=\langle g^{-1}\rangle$ as [cyclic subgroups](../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md).
