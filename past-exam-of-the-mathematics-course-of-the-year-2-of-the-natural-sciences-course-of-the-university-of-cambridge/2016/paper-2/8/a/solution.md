<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [quotient group](../../../../../quotient-group.md) $G/N$ consists of the [cosets](../../../../../coset.md) $gN$, with multiplication

$$
\boxed{(gN)(hN)=(gh)N.}
$$

For well-definedness, replacing representatives by $gn_1$ and $hn_2$ changes their product to $gh(h^{-1}n_1h)n_2$. Since $N$ is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), the final two factors lie in $N$, so the product coset does not change.

[Associativity](../../../../../associative-property.md) follows from that in $G$; the [identity element](../../../../../identity-element.md) is the coset $N$, and $(gN)^{-1}=g^{-1}N$. Hence these cosets form a [group](../../../../../group-split.md). By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), every subgroup $H\leq G$ has order dividing $|G|$, and its [subgroup index](../../../../../index-of-a-subgroup.md) is $[G:H]=|G|/|H|$. In particular

$$
\boxed{|G/N|=\frac{|G|}{|N|}.}
$$

Normality is needed for the quotient multiplication, whereas Lagrange’s counting formula holds for every subgroup.
