<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Apply the [product rule](../../../../../product-rule.md) to the [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md) $F=\phi\nabla\psi-\psi\nabla\phi$:

$$
\nabla\cdot F=\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi+\phi\nabla^2\psi-\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\phi-\psi\nabla^2\phi=\phi\nabla^2\psi-\psi\nabla^2\phi.
$$

The two mixed [dot products](../../../../../dot-product.md) cancel. The [divergence theorem](../../../../../divergence-theorem.md), using the outward [unit normal](../../../../../unit-normal.md), then gives [Green's second identity](../../../../../green-second-identity.md):

$$
\boxed{\int_V(\phi\nabla^2\psi-\psi\nabla^2\phi)\,dV=\int_S(\phi\,n\cdot\nabla\psi-\psi\,n\cdot\nabla\phi)\,dS.}
$$

The usual assumptions are a sufficiently regular boundary and fields with the derivatives and integrability needed for the theorem; singular sources can instead be treated by excising small balls or using [distributions](../../../../../distribution-mathematical-analysis.md).
