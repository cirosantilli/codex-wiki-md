<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $R$ be an orthogonal change of axes. Since $A$ is a second-order [tensor](../../../../../tensor.md), $A'_{ij}=R_{ik}R_{j\ell}A_{k\ell}$. The scalar-contraction assumption gives

$$
C'_{ij}R_{ik}R_{j\ell}A_{k\ell}=C_{k\ell}A_{k\ell}
$$

for every tensor $A$. In one fixed frame its components can be any array: in particular, choose one component at a time to be one, all others zero. Equality of the coefficients then forces

$$
C'_{ij}R_{ik}R_{j\ell}=C_{k\ell},\qquad R^TC'R=C.
$$

Multiplying by $R$ and $R^T$ yields

$$
\boxed{C'=RCR^T,\qquad C'_{ij}=R_{ik}R_{j\ell}C_{k\ell}.}
$$

This is exactly the [tensor component transformation law](../../../../../tensor-component-transformation-law.md). It is the full-contraction version of the [quotient theorem for Cartesian tensors](../../../../../quotient-theorem-for-cartesian-tensors.md). The words “for every” are essential: testing a single $A$, such as the zero tensor or the identity, would not determine all components.
