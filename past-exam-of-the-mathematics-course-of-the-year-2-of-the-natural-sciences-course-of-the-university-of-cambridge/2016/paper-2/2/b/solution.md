<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The only nonzero angular [Fourier mode](../../../../../fourier-mode.md) is $\cos(2\sigma)$. Its radial equation is $T''-4T=0$. A solution vanishing at $\tau=a$ is proportional to $\sinh[2(\tau-a)]$; making its value one at $b$ fixes the constant. The [Dirichlet problem between bipolar coordinate circles](../../../../../dirichlet-problem-between-bipolar-coordinate-circles.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\Phi(\sigma,\tau)=\frac{\sinh[2(\tau-a)]}{\sinh[2(b-a)]}\cos(2\sigma).}
$$

Its second derivatives in $\sigma$ and $\tau$ cancel, and the endpoint values are respectively zero and $\cos(2\sigma)$. The difference of any two solutions would be a [harmonic function](../../../../../harmonic-function.md) with zero [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../../../../../dirichlet-boundary-condition.md), so the [maximum principle](../../../../../maximum-principle.md) proves [uniqueness](../../../../../uniqueness.md).

**Geometric wording.** In the printed [bipolar coordinates](../../../../../bipolar-coordinates.md), the circle radius $1/\sinh\tau$ decreases with $\tau$. For $0<a<b$, the $b$ circle is contained inside the $a$ circle. Thus the source’s “inner” and “outer” labels are interchanged geometrically. The solution above follows the explicit coordinate boundary values, which unambiguously specify the problem.
