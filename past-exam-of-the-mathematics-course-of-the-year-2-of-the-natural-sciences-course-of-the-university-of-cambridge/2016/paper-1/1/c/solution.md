<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md) is smooth throughout the solid, so the [divergence theorem](../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) applies. Differentiation gives

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=(2y+6x^5)+(-2y+4y^3)+1=6x^5+4y^3+1.
$$

The volume is invariant under both $x\mapsto-x$ and $y\mapsto-y$. Hence the [integrals](../../../../../integral-split.md) of the two odd terms vanish, and the [flux](../../../../../flux.md) is simply the volume. Using the circular cross-sections,

$$
\iint_S\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf S=\pi\int_0^1 dz+\pi\int_1^2(2-z)\,dz=\boxed{\frac{3\pi}{2}}.
$$

The seams between the surface pieces have zero surface measure and contribute no extra term.
