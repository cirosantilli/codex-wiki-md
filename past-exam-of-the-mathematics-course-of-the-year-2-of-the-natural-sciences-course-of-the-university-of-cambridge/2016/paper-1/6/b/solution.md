<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Taking [determinants](../../../../../determinant.md) of $A^p=0$ gives $(\det A)^p=0$, so $A$ is singular and has the [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) zero. More strongly, if $Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq0$, then $0=A^pv=\lambda^pv$, so **every [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is zero**.

If the [nilpotent matrix](../../../../../nilpotent-matrix.md) were [diagonalizable](../../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md), write $A=PDP^{-1}$. Then $0=A^p=PD^pP^{-1}$, hence each diagonal entry of $D$ has zero $p$th power and is zero. Thus $D=0$ and $A=0$. Conversely the zero [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) is diagonal. Therefore

$$
\boxed{A^p=0:\quad A\text{ diagonalizable}\iff A=0.}
$$

The conclusion is valid over $\mathbb C$ and also over $\mathbb R$.
