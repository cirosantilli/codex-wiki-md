<h1 id="5/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a possibly complex [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) $v\neq0$ with $Av=\lambda v$, invertibility of $I-A$ gives $(I-A)^{-1}v=(1-\lambda)^{-1}v$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{Nv=\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}v,\qquad\nu=\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}.}
$$

Thus every [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $A$ remains an [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $N$. For a real [skew-symmetric matrix](../../../../../../skew-symmetric-matrix.md), its [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) have the form $\lambda=it$ with $t$ real, so $\nu=e^{2i\arctan t}$ lies on the [unit circle](../../../../../../complex-unit-circle.md), consistently with [orthogonality](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md).
