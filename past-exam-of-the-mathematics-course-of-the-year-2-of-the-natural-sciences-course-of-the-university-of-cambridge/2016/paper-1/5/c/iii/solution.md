<h1 id="5/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Taking [determinants](../../../../../../determinant.md) and using invariance under [transpose](../../../../../../transpose.md),

$$
\det(I+A)=\det((I+A)^T)=\det(I-A),\qquad\boxed{\det N=1.}
$$

This [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) identity actually holds in every dimension. In dimension three, skew symmetry additionally gives

$$
\det A=\det A^T=\det(-A)=(-1)^3\det A=-\det A,
$$

so $\det A=0$. Hence there is a real nonzero [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) $x$ in the [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md):

$$
\boxed{Ax=0,\qquad x\neq0.}
$$

The [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) relation then gives $Nx=x$. Thus the [Cayley transform](../../../../../../cayley-transform-lie-theory.md) fixes an axis and is a proper three-dimensional rotation, including the identity as a degenerate case.
