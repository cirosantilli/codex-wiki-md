<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $Q$ be the real [orthogonal matrix](../../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md). If $Qv=\lambda v$ for a nonzero complex [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md), then preservation of the complex Euclidean [norm](../../../../../norm.md) gives

$$
\|v\|^2=\|Qv\|^2=|\lambda|^2\|v\|^2,
$$

so $|\lambda|=1$. Its [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md) has real coefficients, hence nonreal [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) occur in conjugate pairs. Such a pair is $e^{i\alpha},e^{-i\alpha}$, and the remaining real [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is $+1$ or $-1$.

If all three [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are real, each is $\pm1$; two have the same sign and can be paired as $e^{\pm i\alpha}$ with $\alpha=0$ or $\pi$. Thus in every case the spectrum has the form

$$
\boxed{\{e^{i\alpha},e^{-i\alpha},\varepsilon\},\quad\alpha\in\mathbb R,\quad\varepsilon=\pm1.}
$$

Their product shows that $\varepsilon=\det Q$.
