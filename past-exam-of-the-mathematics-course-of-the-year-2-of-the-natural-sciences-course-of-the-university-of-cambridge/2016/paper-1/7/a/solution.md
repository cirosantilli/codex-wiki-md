<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A complex function is [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) at a point if it is complex differentiable throughout some open neighbourhood of that point. Writing $\zeta=\xi+i\eta$ and $\psi=P+iQ$, a convenient sufficient and necessary criterion under continuously differentiable real components is the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md)

$$
\boxed{P_\xi=Q_\eta,\qquad P_\eta=-Q_\xi.}
$$

They must hold throughout an open neighbourhood, rather than at an isolated point. Merely having partial [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) that satisfy these equalities at one point is not enough without an appropriate differentiability hypothesis. For the composition $\psi=G\circ f$, [holomorphy](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) of $f$ and $G$ on the relevant open sets suffices; the additional condition $f'\neq0$ is needed when using a local inverse [conformal map](../../../../../conformal-map.md), not for [holomorphy](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) of the composition itself.
