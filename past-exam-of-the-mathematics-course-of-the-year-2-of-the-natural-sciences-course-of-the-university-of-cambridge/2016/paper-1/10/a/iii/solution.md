<h1 id="10/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Assume a complete [orthonormal eigenbasis](../../../../../../orthonormal-eigenbasis.md) and a [self-adjoint operator](../../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md) bounded below, with the trial state in its [operator domain](../../../../../../operator-domain.md) or associated energy-form domain. The expansion and [orthogonality](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) give

$$
\langle\phi,\phi\rangle=\sum_n|a_n|^2,\qquad\langle\phi,H\phi\rangle=\sum_nE_n|a_n|^2.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{F[\phi]=\frac{\sum_nE_n|a_n|^2}{\sum_n|a_n|^2}\geq E_0.}
$$

It is a weighted average of [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md), all at least $E_0$. This is the [Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle](../../../../../../rayleigh-ritz-variational-principle.md); equality holds precisely when the trial state lies in the [ground-state subspace](../../../../../../ground-state-subspace.md). A continuous part of the spectrum would require the corresponding spectral [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md) in place of part of the sum.
