<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume $c(y)>0$ is differentiable along the ray, with sufficient regularity for the following variation, and fix the endpoints. [Fermat principle](../../../../../fermat-principle.md) makes the travel time stationary:

$$
T[y]=\int\frac{\sqrt{1+y'^2}}{c(y)}\,dx.
$$

For the variational [integrand](../../../../../integrand.md) $f=\sqrt{1+y'^2}/c$, the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) is

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{y'}{c\sqrt{1+y'^2}}\right]+\frac{c'}{c^2}\sqrt{1+y'^2}=0.
$$

Expanding the [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) and multiplying by $c^2(1+y'^2)^{3/2}$ gives

$$
c y''-c'y'^2(1+y'^2)+c'(1+y'^2)^2=0,
$$

and therefore

$$
\boxed{c(y)y''+c'(y)(1+y'^2)=0.}
$$

Equivalently, the [Beltrami identity](../../../../../beltrami-identity.md) gives $1/[c(y)\sqrt{1+y'^2}]=\text{constant}$. Mere continuity of $c$, as stated in the wording, does not by itself justify the appearance of $c'$; the displayed classical ray equation requires this additional differentiability.
