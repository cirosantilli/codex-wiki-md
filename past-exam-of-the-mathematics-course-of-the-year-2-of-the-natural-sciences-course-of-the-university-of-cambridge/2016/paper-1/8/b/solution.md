<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Wronskian](../../../../../wronskian.md) is

$$
\boxed{W(f,g)(x)=\begin{vmatrix}f(x)&g(x)\\f'(x)&g'(x)\end{vmatrix}=f(x)g'(x)-f'(x)g(x).}
$$

Suppose constants $A,B$ satisfy $Af+Bg=0$ throughout the interval. Differentiating gives $Af'+Bg'=0$. At $x_0$, these form the homogeneous linear system

$$
\begin{pmatrix}f(x_0)&g(x_0)\\f'(x_0)&g'(x_0)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A\\B\end{pmatrix}=0.
$$

Its [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) is nonzero by hypothesis, so $A=B=0$. This is exactly [linear independence](../../../../../linear-independence.md) of the two functions on the interval. No differential equation is needed for this implication; the converse for arbitrary differentiable functions would require additional hypotheses.
