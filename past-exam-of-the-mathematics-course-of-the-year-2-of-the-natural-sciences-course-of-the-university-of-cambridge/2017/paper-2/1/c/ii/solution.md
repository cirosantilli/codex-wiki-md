<h1 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Only two coefficients in the [Sturm-Liouville eigenfunction expansion](../../../../../../sturm-liouville-eigenfunction-expansion.md) are nonzero: $a_0=2$ and $a_1=2-\alpha$. Since $\alpha\ne2$, the result of part (i) simplifies to

$$
Y=\frac{2}{1-\alpha}y_0+\frac{2-\alpha}{2-\alpha}y_1=\frac{2}{1-\alpha}y_0+y_1.
$$

Using [Parseval's identity](../../../../../../parseval-identity.md), the prescribed [weighted norm](../../../../../../weighted-norm.md) becomes

$$
\frac{4}{(1-\alpha)^2}+1=2.
$$

Thus $1-\alpha=\pm2$, so $\alpha=-1$ or $3$. Positivity selects

$$
\boxed{\alpha=3,\qquad Y=-y_0+y_1.}
$$

The standing nonresonance assumption still applies: this answer requires that $3$ is not one of the unspecified higher [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md). Their actual values are not supplied. If a higher [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) were $3$, the stated exclusion would fail and the data would not have an admissible $\alpha$ under that exclusion.
