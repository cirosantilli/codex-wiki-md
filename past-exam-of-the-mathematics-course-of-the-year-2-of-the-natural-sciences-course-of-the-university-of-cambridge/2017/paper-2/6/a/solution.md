<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In orthonormal [Cartesian coordinates](../../../../../cartesian-coordinate-system.md), a rank-$n$ [tensor](../../../../../tensor.md) has components transforming under a change of axes $R$ as

$$
\boxed{T'_{i_1\cdots i_n}=R_{i_1j_1}\cdots R_{i_nj_n}T_{j_1\cdots j_n},\qquad R^TR=I.}
$$

Repeated indices are summed. This is the [tensor component transformation law](../../../../../tensor-component-transformation-law.md); for rank one it is precisely the transformation law of a [vector](../../../../../vector.md).

Use the [vector triple product](../../../../../vector-triple-product.md) to write the double [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) as

$$
W=u\times(\Omega\times u)=(u\cdot u)\Omega-(u\cdot\Omega)u.
$$

Both [dot products](../../../../../dot-product.md) are invariant under an orthogonal change of axes, while $u'=Ru$ and $\Omega'=R\Omega$. Therefore

$$
W'=(u\cdot u)R\Omega-(u\cdot\Omega)Ru=RW.
$$

Hence **$W$ is a vector**. This argument also avoids the sign issue for an individual [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) under an orientation-reversing orthogonal transformation: the double product has two such factors, whose signs cancel.
