<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Direct [matrix multiplication](../../../../../matrix-multiplication.md) gives $P^2=P-I$ and hence $P^3=-I$. It follows that

$$
\begin{aligned}
P^0&=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},&P&=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},&P^2&=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix},\\
P^3&=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},&P^4&=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},&P^5&=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\end{aligned}
$$

and $P^6=I$. These six matrices are distinct; consequently no smaller positive power is the [identity element](../../../../../identity-element.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\langle P\rangle=\{I,P,P^2,P^3,P^4,P^5\}\cong C_6,\qquad\operatorname{ord}(P)=6.}
$$

Products add exponents modulo six and $P^{-1}=P^5$. This verifies the [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) structure explicitly.
