<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $T$ be the set of [torsion elements](../../../../../torsion-element.md) of the given [Abelian group](../../../../../abelian-group.md). It contains the [identity element](../../../../../identity-element.md), which has order one. If $g,h\in T$ have orders $m,n$, set $\ell=\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)$. Commutativity permits powers of the product to split:

$$
(gh^{-1})^\ell=g^\ell h^{-\ell}=e.
$$

Hence $gh^{-1}$ has finite order, dividing $\ell$, and lies in $T$. The nonempty set $T$ therefore satisfies the [subgroup test](../../../../../subgroup-test.md) and is a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md):

$$
\boxed{T=\{g\in G:\operatorname{ord}(g)<\infty\}\leq G.}
$$

This is the [torsion subgroup](../../../../../torsion-subgroup.md). The proof uses commutativity exactly when rearranging the factors in $(gh^{-1})^\ell$; one cannot use that step in an arbitrary noncommutative [group](../../../../../group-split.md). The subgroup need not itself be a finite set.
