<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Observe that $A=-I$, $B^2=I$ and $C=-B$. Thus $A^2=B^2=C^2=I$, while $AB=BA=C$, $AC=CA=B$ and $BC=CB=A$. The [multiplication table](../../../../../multiplication-table.md), with the left factor labeling the row, is

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|cccc}
&I&A&B&C\\\hline
I&I&A&B&C\\
A&A&I&C&B\\
B&B&C&I&A\\
C&C&B&A&I
\end{array}.}
$$

It proves [closure](../../../../../closure-topology.md) of the set. [Associativity](../../../../../associative-property.md) is inherited from [matrix multiplication](../../../../../matrix-multiplication.md), $I$ is the [identity element](../../../../../identity-element.md), and every element is its own [inverse element](../../../../../inverse-element.md). Therefore this set is a [group](../../../../../group-split.md). The table is symmetric, so it is an [Abelian group](../../../../../abelian-group.md); its three nonidentity elements have order two, identifying it as the [Klein four-group](../../../../../klein-four-group.md) $C_2\times C_2$.
