<h1 id="10/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the identity relation as printed in the PDF, $XYZ=I$. Since $Z^2=I$, multiplying on the right by $Z$ gives $XY=Z$. Also

$$
YX=(XY)^{-1}=Z^{-1}=Z=XY,
$$

because $X,Y,Z$ are involutions. Thus $X$ and $Y$ commute, and so does their product $Z$ with either of them. Therefore **the group is Abelian**, and is the [Klein four-group](../../../../../klein-four-group.md).

Each [conjugacy class](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) is a singleton. Over the complex numbers every [irreducible representation of a finite abelian group](../../../../../irreducible-representation-of-a-finite-abelian-group.md) is one-dimensional. Choose independent signs $\varepsilon=\chi(X)$ and $\eta=\chi(Y)$; the relations force $\varepsilon^2=\eta^2=1$ and $\chi(Z)=\varepsilon\eta$. Hence the complete [character table](../../../../../character-table.md) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
&I&X&Y&Z\\\hline
\chi_{++}&1&1&1&1\\
\chi_{+-}&1&1&-1&-1\\
\chi_{-+}&1&-1&1&-1\\
\chi_{--}&1&-1&-1&1
\end{array}.}
$$

These four distinct [one-dimensional representations](../../../../../one-dimensional-representation.md) exhaust the irreducible possibilities: there are four [conjugacy classes](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md), and their squared dimensions already sum to $|G|=4$. The rows also satisfy [character orthogonality](../../../../../character-orthogonality.md). The local transcription damages the final symbol in the defining relation; the PDF makes the identity $I$ unambiguous.
