<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [simple zero](../../../../../simple-zero.md) of an [analytic function](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) has a local factorization $f(z)=(z-z_0)u(z)$, where $u$ is an [analytic function](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) and $u(z_0)=f'(z_0)\ne0$. Since $1/u$ is also an [analytic function](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) near $z_0$, its [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) gives

$$
\frac1{f(z)}=\frac1{z-z_0}\left[\frac1{f'(z_0)}+O(z-z_0)\right].
$$

The nonzero coefficient of $(z-z_0)^{-1}$ proves that this is a [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md), with

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}\frac1{f(z)}=\frac1{f'(z_0)}.}
$$

Multiplying by the [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) $g(z)=g(z_0)+O(z-z_0)$ similarly gives

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}\frac{g(z)}{f(z)}=\frac{g(z_0)}{f'(z_0)}.}
$$

The assumption $g(z_0)\ne0$ ensures that the [pole](../../../../../pole.md) is not canceled. In either formula the [residue](../../../../../residue.md) is precisely the coefficient of $(z-z_0)^{-1}$ in the [Laurent series](../../../../../laurent-series.md).
