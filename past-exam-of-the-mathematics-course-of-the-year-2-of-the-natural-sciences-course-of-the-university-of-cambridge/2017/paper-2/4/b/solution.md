<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Clear the apparent negative power of $z$ to obtain the [rational function](../../../../../rational-function.md)

$$
h(z)=\frac{-2iz}{z^2-2iaz-1}.
$$

Let $\Delta=\sqrt{a^2-1}>0$. Its denominator factors as $(z-z_-)(z-z_+)$, with

$$
\boxed{z_-=i(a-\Delta),\qquad z_+=i(a+\Delta).}
$$

These are distinct [simple poles](../../../../../simple-pole.md). Since $(a-\Delta)(a+\Delta)=1$ and $a+\Delta>1$, we have $0<a-\Delta<1$: only $z_-$ lies inside the [unit circle](../../../../../complex-unit-circle.md). The apparent singularity at $z=0$ in the original expression is a [removable singularity](../../../../../removable-singularity.md), with the continued value $h(0)=0$; it is not another pole.

Using the [residue](../../../../../residue.md) formula from part (a), with denominator derivative $2z-2ia$, gives

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_-}h=\frac{-2iz_-}{2z_--2ia}=\frac{i(a-\Delta)}{\Delta}.}
$$

There are no other finite singularities of the continued [rational function](../../../../../rational-function.md).
