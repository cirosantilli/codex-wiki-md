<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the [Gaussian function](../../../../../gaussian-function.md), direct differentiation gives $f'=-tf$. Applying the [Fourier transform](../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) identities from part (a),

$$
i\omega F=-iF',\qquad F'=-\omega F.
$$

Solving this first-order [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{h(\omega)=-\omega,\qquad F(\omega)=C e^{-\omega^2/2}.}
$$

In fact, the normalization can also be fixed: the [Gaussian integral](../../../../../gaussian-integral.md) gives $F(0)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-t^2/2}\,dt=\sqrt{2\pi}$, so $C=\sqrt{2\pi}$ with the convention used here. The transform has the same Gaussian shape.
