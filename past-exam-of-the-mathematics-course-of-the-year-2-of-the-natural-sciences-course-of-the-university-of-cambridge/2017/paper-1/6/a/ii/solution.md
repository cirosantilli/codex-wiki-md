<h1 id="6/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose first that $AB=BA$, and take an [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) $v_j$ of $A$, with $Av_j=a_jv_j$. Commutation gives

$$
A(Bv_j)=BAv_j=a_jBv_j.
$$

Since the [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $a_j$ is simple, its [eigenspace](../../../../../../eigenspace.md) is one-dimensional, so $Bv_j=b_jv_j$ for some scalar $b_j$. Thus every vector in an [eigenbasis](../../../../../../eigenbasis.md) of $A$ is also an [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $B$. The simple spectrum of $B$ ensures these account for its [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) directions as well. [Eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) are understood up to nonzero scaling and permutation, rather than as literally identical numerical vectors.

Conversely, if $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ are a common [eigenbasis](../../../../../../eigenbasis.md) with $Av_j=a_jv_j$ and $Bv_j=b_jv_j$, then

$$
(AB-BA)v_j=(a_jb_j-b_ja_j)v_j=0
$$

for every basis vector. Hence $AB-BA=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{common eigenvector directions}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad AB=BA.}
$$

This is [simple spectrum gives simultaneous diagonalization](../../../../../../simple-spectrum-gives-simultaneous-diagonalization.md). Without simple spectra, commuting [Hermitian matrices](../../../../../../hermitian-operator.md) still possess a common [orthonormal eigenbasis](../../../../../../orthonormal-eigenbasis.md), but not every arbitrary [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of one [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) must be an [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of the other.
