<h1 id="6/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Choose the common [orthonormal eigenbasis](../../../../../../orthonormal-eigenbasis.md) established in part (ii), and let $U$ be the [unitary matrix](../../../../../../unitary-matrix.md) of its columns. Then

$$
U^\dagger AU=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n),\qquad
U^\dagger BU=\operatorname{diag}(b_1,\ldots,b_n).
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{U^\dagger(A+iB)U=\operatorname{diag}(a_1+ib_1,\ldots,a_n+ib_n).}
$$

Thus $N$ is unitarily diagonalizable, even though it need not be Hermitian. As a consistency check, commutation also gives $NN^\dagger=N^\dagger N=A^2+B^2$, so $N$ is a [normal matrix](../../../../../../normal-matrix.md), as also follows from [unitary diagonalization of a normal matrix](../../../../../../unitary-diagonalization-of-a-normal-matrix.md).
