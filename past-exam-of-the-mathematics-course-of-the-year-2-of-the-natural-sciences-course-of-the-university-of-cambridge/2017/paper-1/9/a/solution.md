<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Define $h=f-y'f_{y'}$. For a density without explicit $x$ dependence, the chain rule gives

$$
\frac{dh}{dx}=f_yy'+f_{y'}y''-y''f_{y'}-y'\frac d{dx}f_{y'}
=y'\left(f_y-\frac d{dx}f_{y'}\right).
$$

Thus the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) implies the [Beltrami identity](../../../../../beltrami-identity.md)

$$
\boxed{h=f-y'\frac{\partial f}{\partial y'}=\text{constant},\qquad\frac{dh}{dx}=0.}
$$

Where $y'\ne0$ this first integral is equivalent to the original equation. At a turning point it extends by continuity. On an entire constant segment, however, its derivative alone can vanish without imposing the full [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md), so such candidate paths must still be checked in the original equation.
