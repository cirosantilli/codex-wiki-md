<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

If $Hv=\lambda v$ for a [Hermitian matrix](../../../../hermitian-operator.md) and a nonzero [eigenvector](../../../../eigenvector.md), then $v^\dagger Hv=\lambda v^\dagger v$. The [scalar](../../../../scalar.md) on the left equals its complex conjugate because $H^\dagger=H$, while $v^\dagger v>0$. Thus $\lambda$ is real. If also $Hw=\mu w$, then

$$
\mu v^\dagger w=v^\dagger Hw=(Hv)^\dagger w=\lambda v^\dagger w.
$$

Consequently distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md) force $v^\dagger w=0$, proving [orthogonality](../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) of their [eigenvectors](../../../../eigenvector.md).

For a [unitary matrix](../../../../unitary-matrix.md), $U^\dagger U=I$ preserves the [norm](../../../../norm.md) induced by the standard [Hermitian inner product](../../../../hermitian-inner-product.md). If $Uv=\zeta v$ with $v\ne0$, then $\|v\|^2=\|Uv\|^2=|\zeta|^2\|v\|^2$, so $\boxed{|\zeta|=1}$.
