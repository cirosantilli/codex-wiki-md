<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $Av=\alpha v$. The commutation relation gives $A(Bv)=B(Av)=\alpha Bv$, so $B$ preserves the [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) of $A$ with [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\alpha$. Because it is a [nondegenerate eigenvalue](../../../../../simple-eigenvalue.md), the [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) is one-dimensional, and $Bv=\beta v$ for some real $\beta$. This includes $Bv=0$. Thus the [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $A$ form a common [eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenbasis.md), and

$$
Uv=(\alpha+i\beta)v.
$$

Writing the corresponding unit-modulus [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $U$ as $\zeta=e^{i\theta}$ gives

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\operatorname{Re}\zeta=\cos\theta,\qquad\beta=\operatorname{Im}\zeta=\sin\theta.}
$$

The pairing refers to the same common [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md); independent reorderings of the two real spectra would lose this information. The [simultaneous diagonalization](../../../../../simultaneous-diagonalization.md) can also be constructed without requiring [simple eigenvalues](../../../../../simple-eigenvalue.md) by diagonalizing $B$ within each [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) of $A$.
