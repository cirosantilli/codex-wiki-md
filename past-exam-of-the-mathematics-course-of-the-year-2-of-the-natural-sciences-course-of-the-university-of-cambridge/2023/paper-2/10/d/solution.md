<h1 id="10/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The complete [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) list is

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{l|c|c}
\text{subgroup}&\text{order}&\text{isomorphism type}\\\hline
\{I\}&1&C_1\\
\{I,U^2\}&2&C_2\\
\{I,U^jV\},\quad j=0,1,2,3&2&C_2\\
\{I,U,U^2,U^3\}&4&C_4\\
\{I,U^2,V,U^2V\}&4&C_2\times C_2\\
\{I,U^2,UV,U^3V\}&4&C_2\times C_2\\
G&8&D_8
\end{array}}
$$

Thus there are ten [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md): one trivial, five of order two, three of order four, and the whole group. The five order-two subgroups are mutually isomorphic. The two noncyclic order-four subgroups are mutually isomorphic [Klein four-groups](../../../../../klein-four-group.md); neither is isomorphic to the [cyclic group of order four](../../../../../cyclic-group-of-order-four.md).

For completeness, [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) permits only orders $1,2,4,8$. An order-four subgroup containing $U$ or $U^3$ must be $\langle U\rangle$. Otherwise it can contain only $I,U^2$ and elements $U^jV$. The product of two distinct such elements is $U^{j-k}$. To avoid generating $U$ or $U^3$, their exponents must differ by two, giving exactly the two listed [Klein four-groups](../../../../../klein-four-group.md). The order-two subgroups come from the five [involutions](../../../../../involution.md) $U^2$ and $U^jV$. This proves completeness.
