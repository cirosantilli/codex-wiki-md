<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A single-valued potential must be $2\pi$-periodic in $\theta$. For a separated solution $\Phi=F(r)H(\theta)$, the [Laplace equation in polar coordinates](../../../../../laplace-equation-in-polar-coordinates.md) becomes

$$
\frac{r(rF')'}F=-\frac{H''}H=\mu.
$$

The periodic angular [eigenvalue problem](../../../../../eigenvalue-problem.md) has $\mu=n^2$, $n\geq0$: its nonconstant [eigenfunctions](../../../../../eigenfunction.md) are $\cos n\theta,\sin n\theta$. Negative $\mu$ gives exponentials that cannot be periodic, and $\mu=0$ gives only a constant angular factor. For $n\geq1$, the radial [Cauchy-Euler equation](../../../../../cauchy-euler-equation.md) $r^2F''+rF'-n^2F=0$ gives $F=Ar^n+Br^{-n}$. At $n=0$ the two radial solutions are $1$ and $\log r$. [Superposition](../../../../../superposition-principle.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\Phi=A_0+B_0\log r+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[(A_nr^n+B_nr^{-n})\cos n\theta+(C_nr^n+D_nr^{-n})\sin n\theta\right].}
$$

The expansion is interpreted on an annulus where the [harmonic function](../../../../../harmonic-function.md) is defined; particular [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md) determine its constants and convergence region. Single-valuedness is essential: allowing a cut in the angular domain would also permit nonperiodic angular solutions.
