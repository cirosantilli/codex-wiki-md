<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Using the complex cosine identity,

$$
\cos(x+iy)=\cos x\cosh y-i\sin x\sinh y,
$$

we find

$$
(x+iy)\cos(x+iy)=x\cos x\cosh y+y\sin x\sinh y+i[y\cos x\cosh y-x\sin x\sinh y].
$$

Its [real part](../../../../../real-part.md) is exactly the prescribed function. Thus a [harmonic conjugate](../../../../../harmonic-conjugate.md) is $v=y\cos x\cosh y-x\sin x\sinh y$, and the general answer is

$$
\boxed{f(z)=z\cos z+iC,\qquad C\in\mathbb R.}
$$

To check the only freedom, two [analytic functions](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) with this [real part](../../../../../real-part.md) differ by a function with zero real part. The [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) then force both partial [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) of its imaginary part to vanish, so it is constant on the connected domain. No normalization was supplied to fix $C$.
