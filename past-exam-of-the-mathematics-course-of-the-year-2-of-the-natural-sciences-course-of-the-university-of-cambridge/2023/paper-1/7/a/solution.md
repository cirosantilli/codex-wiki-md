<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For an [analytic function](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md), the [complex differentiability](../../../../../complex-differentiability.md) limit is independent of the direction of approach. A real increment gives $f'(z)=u_x+iv_x$, whereas an imaginary increment gives

$$
f'(z)=\frac{u_y+iv_y}{i}=v_y-iu_y.
$$

Equality of real and imaginary parts proves the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md):

$$
\boxed{u_x=v_y,\qquad u_y=-v_x.}
$$

Conversely, if $u,v$ have continuous first partial [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) near a point and obey these equations, their real differentiability gives, for a complex increment $h$,

$$
f(z+h)-f(z)=(u_x+iv_x)\operatorname{Re}h+(u_y+iv_y)\operatorname{Im}h+o(|h|)=(u_x+iv_x)h+o(|h|).
$$

Dividing by $h$ proves the [complex differentiability](../../../../../complex-differentiability.md) there. Continuous first partial [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) are a sufficient regularity hypothesis for this converse; agreement along the two coordinate directions alone is only a necessary condition.
