<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [orthogonal change of coordinates](../../../../../orthogonal-change-of-coordinates.md) preserves the sphere equation, giving $u^2+v^2+w^2=4$. Subtract it from the spheroid equation in part (b):

$$
8(v^2+w^2)=5,\qquad v^2+w^2=\frac58,\qquad u^2=4-\frac58=\frac{27}{8}.
$$

Thus the intersection consists of the two [circles](../../../../../circle.md)

$$
\boxed{u=\pm\frac{3\sqrt6}{4},\qquad v^2+w^2=\frac58.}
$$

Their common radius is $\boxed{\sqrt{5/8}=\sqrt{10}/4}$. Transforming their centers back to the original coordinates gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf c_\pm=\pm\left(\frac{3\sqrt3}{4},\frac{3\sqrt3}{4},0\right).}
$$

Their planes have equations $x_1+x_2=\pm3\sqrt3/2$, each with [normal vector](../../../../../normal-vector.md) $(1,1,0)$. The difference $\mathbf c_+-\mathbf c_-$ is parallel to this normal, proving that the line joining the centers is perpendicular to both planes. This is the [intersection of a prolate spheroid and a concentric sphere](../../../../../intersection-of-a-prolate-spheroid-and-a-concentric-sphere.md) in the regime between the short and long semiaxes.
