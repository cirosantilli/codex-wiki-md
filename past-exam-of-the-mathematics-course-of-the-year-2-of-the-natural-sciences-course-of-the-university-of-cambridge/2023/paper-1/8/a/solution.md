<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Away from zeros of the leading coefficient, the normal-form [differential equation](../../../../../differential-equation-split.md) is

$$
y''+P(z)y'+Q(z)y=0,\qquad P(z)=\frac3{z(1+z^2)^2},\qquad Q(z)=\frac1{z^2(1+z^2)^2}.
$$

The only finite singular points are $z=0,i,-i$. At zero, $zP(z)=3/(1+z^2)^2$ and $z^2Q(z)=1/(1+z^2)^2$ are analytic, so zero is a [regular singular point](../../../../../regular-singular-point.md). At $z=\pm i$, the coefficient $P$ has a genuine double [pole](../../../../../pole.md), so $(z\mp i)P$ is not analytic; these are [irregular singular points](../../../../../irregular-singular-point.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{z=0\text{ regular singular};\qquad z=\pm i\text{ irregular singular}.}
$$

If infinity is included, set $t=1/z$ and $Y(t)=y(1/t)$. The transformed coefficients are $\widetilde P=2/t-3t^3/(1+t^2)^2$ and $\widetilde Q=t^2/(1+t^2)^2$. They satisfy the [regular singular point at infinity](../../../../../regular-singular-point-at-infinity.md) criterion, so $\boxed{\infty\text{ is regular singular}}$ as well.
