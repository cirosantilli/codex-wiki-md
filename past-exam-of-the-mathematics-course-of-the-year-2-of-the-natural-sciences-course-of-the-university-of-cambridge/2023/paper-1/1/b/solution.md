<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The azimuthal vector $\mathbf h_\phi$ is automatically orthogonal to the other two. Their remaining [dot product](../../../../../dot-product.md) is

$$
\mathbf h_\xi\cdot\mathbf h_\theta=[\sinh\xi\cosh\xi-g(\xi)g'(\xi)]\sin\theta\cos\theta.
$$

Thus [orthogonal coordinates](../../../../../orthogonal-coordinates.md) require $gg'=\sinh\xi\cosh\xi$, which integrates to $g^2=\sinh^2\xi+C$. The initial value gives $C=1$, and the smooth branch with $g(0)=1$ is

$$
\boxed{g(\xi)=\cosh\xi.}
$$

The corresponding coordinate-vector [norms](../../../../../norm.md) are $|\mathbf h_\xi|=|\mathbf h_\theta|=\sqrt{\sinh^2\xi+\sin^2\theta}$ and $|\mathbf h_\phi|=\sinh\xi\sin\theta$, as in the [hyperbolic parametrization of prolate spheroidal coordinates](../../../../../hyperbolic-parametrization-of-prolate-spheroidal-coordinates.md). Coordinate degeneracies on the axis and focal segment are understood.

Eliminating the angular coordinates on the requested surface gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{x^2+y^2}{\sinh^21}+\frac{z^2}{\cosh^21}=1.}
$$

It is a [prolate spheroid](../../../../../prolate-spheroid.md) with long axis $z$, equatorial radius $\sinh1$, polar semiaxis $\cosh1$, and foci $(0,0,\pm1)$.
