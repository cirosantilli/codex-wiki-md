<h1 id="5/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose $AB=BA$ and $Av_j=a_jv_j$. Then

$$
A(Bv_j)=B(Av_j)=a_jBv_j.
$$

Because the [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $A$ are distinct, the [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) for $a_j$ is one-dimensional. It follows that $Bv_j=b_jv_j$, including the possibility $b_j=0$. Thus an orthonormal [eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenbasis.md) of $A$ is also an [eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenbasis.md) of $B$. The simplicity of both spectra means their [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) coincide as directions, up to ordering, phases and nonzero scalar multiples.

Conversely, if they share a complete [eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenbasis.md), choose the [unitary matrix](../../../../../unitary-matrix.md) $U$ whose columns are these common normalized [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md). Then $U^\dagger AU=D_A$ and $U^\dagger BU=D_B$ are diagonal, so $D_AD_B=D_BD_A$ and $AB=BA$. Hence

$$
\boxed{AB=BA\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A,B\text{ have a common orthonormal eigenbasis}.}
$$

This is [simultaneous diagonalization](../../../../../simultaneous-diagonalization.md) of the two [Hermitian matrices](../../../../../hermitian-operator.md).
