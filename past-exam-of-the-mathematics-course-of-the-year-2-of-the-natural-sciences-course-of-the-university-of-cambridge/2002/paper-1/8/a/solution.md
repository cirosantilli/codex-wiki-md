<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use [reduction of order](../../../../../reduction-of-order.md) by setting $y=xv$. Then $y'=v+xv'$ and $y''=2v'+xv''$, so the differential expression simplifies to

$$
y''-\frac{y'}x+\frac y{x^2}=xv''+v'=(xv')'.
$$

In the homogeneous case $xv'$ is constant. Hence $v=c_1+c_2\ln x$, and a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) of the solution space is

$$
\boxed{y_1=x,\qquad y_2=x\ln x.}
$$

Their [Wronskian](../../../../../wronskian.md) is $x\ne0$ on $[1,e]$, proving [linear independence](../../../../../linear-independence.md). No nonzero homogeneous solution satisfies both endpoint conditions: $y(1)=0$ sets $c_1=0$, and $y(e)=0$ then sets $c_2=0$.
