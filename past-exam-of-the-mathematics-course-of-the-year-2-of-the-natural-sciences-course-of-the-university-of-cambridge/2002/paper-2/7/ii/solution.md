<h1 id="7/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Order coordinates as $q=(x_1,x_2,x_3;y_1,y_2,y_3)$. For either uniform displacement, $t_x=(1,1,1;0,0,0)$ or $t_y=(0,0,0;1,1,1)$, every relative displacement and every extension vanishes. These [normal modes](../../../../../normal-mode.md) have zero [normal frequency](../../../../../eigenfrequency.md) and represent two independent planar [translations](../../../../../translation-geometry.md).

For $r=(-1,-1,2;\sqrt3,-\sqrt3,0)$, substitution gives

$$
e_{12}=0,\qquad e_{13}=\tfrac32-\tfrac32=0,\qquad e_{23}=-\tfrac32+\tfrac32=0.
$$

Thus $Kr=0$ too. A small rigid [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) through angle $\alpha$ moves equilibrium position $(X_i,Y_i)$ by $\alpha(-Y_i,X_i)$. With the specified positions this displacement is $-\alpha r/2$, identifying the third vector as infinitesimal rigid [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{\omega^2(t_x)=\omega^2(t_y)=\omega^2(r)=0.}
$$

A zero-frequency [normal coordinate](../../../../../normal-coordinate.md) obeys $\ddot Q=0$, so $Q=A+Bt$. This permits a constant displacement or a uniform velocity at the level of the linearized equations. In particular, the rotational pattern preserves lengths to first order; finite-angle rotation follows a curved path outside a single linear displacement pattern.
