<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose $\gamma>1/2$. The inverse integrand is $\lambda^{-s}/[2(s-1/2)]$. Because $-\log\lambda>0$, the exponential factor decays towards the left. Closing the [Bromwich contour](../../../../../bromwich-contour.md) to the left and using the [residue theorem](../../../../../residue-theorem.md) picks up just the [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md) at $s=1/2$, with [residue](../../../../../residue.md) $\lambda^{-1/2}/2$. Hence

$$
\boxed{h(\lambda)=\frac1{2\sqrt\lambda},\qquad0<\lambda<1.}
$$

Its defining [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) checks both the answer and its convergence condition:

$$
\int_0^1\lambda^{s-1}\frac1{2\sqrt\lambda}\,d\lambda=\frac1{2(s-1/2)}=\frac1{2s-1},\qquad\operatorname{Re}s>\tfrac12.
$$
