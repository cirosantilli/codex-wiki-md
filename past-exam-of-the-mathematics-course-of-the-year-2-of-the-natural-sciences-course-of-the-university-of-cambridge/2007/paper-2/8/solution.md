<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

[Lagrange's theorem](../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) states that the [group order](../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) of a finite [group](../../../../group-split.md) divides the [group order](../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md); more precisely $|G|=[G:H]|H|$.

If every element squares to the [identity element](../../../../identity-element.md), every element is its own [inverse element](../../../../inverse-element.md), and $ab=(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}=ba$. Thus a [group of exponent two is abelian](../../../../group-of-exponent-two-is-abelian.md). For distinct nonidentity $a,b$, the four elements $1,a,b,ab$ are distinct: for example $ab=1$ would imply $a=b$, and $ab=a$ would imply $b=1$. Products remain in this set because $a,b$ commute and square to one. Thus they form a [Klein four-group](../../../../klein-four-group.md) [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) of order four.

Now let $|G|=2p$ with odd [prime number](../../../../prime-number-split.md) $p$. By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), every [element order](../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) is one of $1,2,p,2p$. If an element has order $2p$, its square has order $p$. If no element had order $p$, all nonidentity elements would therefore have order two. There are at least two distinct such elements, producing a subgroup of order four. This contradicts $4\nmid2p$. Hence **there is an element of order $p$**.
