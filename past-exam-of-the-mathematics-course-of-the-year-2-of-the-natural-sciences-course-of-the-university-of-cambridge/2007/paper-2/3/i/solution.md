<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The square-root factor gives an algebraic [branch point](../../../../../branch-point.md) at $z=0$. The logarithm is locally analytic there but has value an odd multiple of $i\pi$, which is nonzero, so it cannot cancel that branching. The zero and pole of $(z-1)/(z+1)$ give logarithmic [branch points](../../../../../branch-point.md) at $z=1$ and $z=-1$, respectively. Thus the finite singularities are

$$
\boxed{z=0,\quad1,\quad-1\quad\text{(all branch points; no finite poles).}}
$$

Infinity is also a [branch point](../../../../../branch-point.md). On the logarithm branch tending to zero there, $\log[(z-1)/(z+1)]=-2/z+O(z^{-3})$, so the product behaves as $-2z^{-1/2}$. Other logarithm branches add $2\pi ik\sqrt z$ and retain branching. A chosen [branch cut](../../../../../branch-cut.md) joins or separates these branch points; its ordinary interior points are not additional branch points of the underlying multivalued [function](../../../../../function-split.md).
