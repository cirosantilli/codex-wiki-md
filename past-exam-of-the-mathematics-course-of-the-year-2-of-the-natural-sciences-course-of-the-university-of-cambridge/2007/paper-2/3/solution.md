<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

At a point of complex [differentiability](../../../../differentiability.md), the [complex derivative](../../../../complex-derivative.md) is independent of the direction of approach. Taking increments along the real axis gives $f'=u_x+iv_x$; taking imaginary increments gives $f'=v_y-iu_y$. Equality of their [real parts](../../../../real-part.md) and [imaginary parts](../../../../imaginary-part.md) proves the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) $u_x=v_y$ and $u_y=-v_x$.

For the specified [real part](../../../../real-part.md), observe that $z^2e^z$ has exactly that real part. Equivalently, integrating $v_y=u_x$ and checking $v_x=-u_y$ gives

$$
v=e^x[(x^2-y^2)\sin y+2xy\cos y]+C.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{f(z)=z^2e^z+iC,\qquad C\in\mathbb R.}
$$

The freedom is only a constant imaginary part: the difference of two such [holomorphic functions](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) has zero real part, so its [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) make it constant on each connected domain.
