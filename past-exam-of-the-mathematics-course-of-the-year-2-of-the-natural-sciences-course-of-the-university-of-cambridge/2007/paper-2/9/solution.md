<h1 id="9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9](../9.md)

A [group homomorphism](../../../../group-homomorphism.md) satisfies $\varphi(g_1g_2)=\varphi(g_1)\varphi(g_2)$; a bijective one is a [group isomorphism](../../../../group-isomorphism.md). Its [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) is $K=\{g:\varphi(g)=1_H\}$. It is a [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md), and for $k\in K$, $g\in G$,

$$
\varphi(gkg^{-1})=\varphi(g)\varphi(k)\varphi(g)^{-1}=1_H.
$$

Thus $gKg^{-1}\subseteq K$, and applying the same argument to $g^{-1}$ gives equality. Therefore $K$ is a [normal subgroup](../../../../normal-subgroup.md).

Write $B_n=\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. Direct [matrix multiplication](../../../../matrix-multiplication.md) gives $B_nB_m=B_{n+m}$, $B_0=I$ and $B_n^{-1}=B_{-n}$. Consequently these matrices form the [integer shear group](../../../../integer-shear-group.md), a [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) of $GL(2,\mathbb R)$. It is an [infinite cyclic group](../../../../infinite-cyclic-group.md), generated by $B_1$, since $B_1^n=B_n$ and no nonzero integer power is the identity.

The map $n\mapsto B_n$ is a bijective [group homomorphism](../../../../group-homomorphism.md) from the additive [group of integers](../../../../additive-group-of-integers.md), proving the required [group isomorphism](../../../../group-isomorphism.md). **There are proper subgroups**: for example $\{B_{2n}:n\in\mathbb Z\}$ omits $B_1$. In fact every subgroup is $\{B_{mn}:n\in\mathbb Z\}$ for an integer $m\geq0$, by the classification of [subgroups of an infinite cyclic group](../../../../subgroup-of-an-infinite-cyclic-group.md); $m=0$ gives the trivial subgroup and $m=1$ the whole group.
