<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Away from the origin, $\hat{\mathbf r}=\nabla r$. Hence the [curl of a gradient](../../../../../curl-of-a-gradient.md) gives $\nabla\times\hat{\mathbf r}=\mathbf0$. Its [divergence](../../../../../divergence.md) is the [Laplacian](../../../../../laplacian.md) of the radial coordinate, which in [spherical coordinates](../../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) is

$$
\boxed{\nabla\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}=\nabla^2r=\frac1{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}(r^2)=\frac2r,\qquad r>0.}
$$

Equivalently, differentiating $x_i/r$ in [Cartesian coordinates](../../../../../cartesian-coordinate-system.md) gives $3/r-r^2/r^3=2/r$.
