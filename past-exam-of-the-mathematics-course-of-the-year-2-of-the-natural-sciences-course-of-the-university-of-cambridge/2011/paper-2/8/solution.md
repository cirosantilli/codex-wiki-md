<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

The [group order](../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) of a finite [group](../../../../group-split.md) is its number of elements, $|G|$. The [order of a group element](../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $g$ is the least positive [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) $r$ with $g^r=e$. Such an $r$ exists in a finite group because two powers must coincide, and cancellation then gives a positive power equal to the [identity element](../../../../identity-element.md).

For the [direct product of groups](../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md), the componentwise operation is closed because each factor is closed. [Associativity](../../../../associative-property.md) holds in each coordinate:

$$
[(g,h)(g',h')](g'',h'')=(gg'g'',hh'h'')=(g,h)[(g',h')(g'',h'')].
$$

The [identity element](../../../../identity-element.md) is $(e_1,e_2)$, and the [inverse element](../../../../inverse-element.md) of $(g,h)$ is $(g^{-1},h^{-1})$. Thus all [group axioms](../../../../group-axioms.md) hold. For finite factors, the [multiplication principle](../../../../rule-of-product.md) gives

$$
\boxed{|G_1\times G_2|=|G_1||G_2|.}
$$

For $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$, write $E=(e,e)$, $u=(w,e)$, $v=(e,w)$, $z=(w,w)$, with $w^2=e$. Its [Cayley table](../../../../cayley-table.md), with the row element multiplying the column element, is

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &E&u&v&z\\\hline
E&E&u&v&z\\
u&u&E&z&v\\
v&v&z&E&u\\
z&z&v&u&E
\end{array}.
$$

This is the [Klein four-group](../../../../klein-four-group.md): every nonidentity element has order two.

In the [cyclic group](../../../../cyclic-group.md) $\mathbb Z_4=\langle a\rangle$, the set $\{e,a^2\}$ is closed because $(a^2)^2=e$, and both elements have inverses in the set. It is therefore a proper [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) of order two. By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), a proper subgroup has order one or two. An order-two subgroup must be generated by an element of order two. Neither $a$ nor $a^3$ has order two, whereas $a^2$ does. Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{the proper subgroups of }\mathbb Z_4\text{ are }\{e\}\text{ and }\{e,a^2\}.}
$$

**The PDF's claim needs “nontrivial”:** $\{e,a^2\}$ is the only nontrivial proper subgroup, but $\{e\}$ is also a proper subgroup under the usual definition.

A [group isomorphism](../../../../group-isomorphism.md) preserves element orders: $\phi(g)^r=e$ if and only if $g^r=e$, using the homomorphism property and injectivity. Since $\mathbb Z_4$ has elements of order four and the [Klein four-group](../../../../klein-four-group.md) does not,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2\not\cong\mathbb Z_4.}
$$
