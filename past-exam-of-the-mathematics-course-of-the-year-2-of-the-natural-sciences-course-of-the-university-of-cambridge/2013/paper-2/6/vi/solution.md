<h1 id="6/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

Start from the three-index transformation law and contract the first two Cartesian indices:

$$
\begin{aligned}
C'_k=\sum_iS'_{iik}&=\sum_{i,a,b,c}Q_{ia}Q_{ib}Q_{kc}S_{abc}\\
&=\sum_{a,b,c}\delta_{ab}Q_{kc}S_{abc}=\sum_cQ_{kc}\sum_aS_{aac}=Q_{kc}C_c.
\end{aligned}
$$

Orthogonality supplies $\sum_iQ_{ia}Q_{ib}=\delta_{ab}$, where $\delta$ is the [Kronecker delta](../../../../../kronecker-delta.md). The remaining factor is exactly the [vector](../../../../../vector.md) transformation rule. Therefore **$C_k$ is a vector**.

This [tensor contraction](../../../../../tensor-contraction.md) removes two indices by pairing them through the Euclidean metric. In general nonorthonormal coordinates, contraction must use the appropriate metric or pair a covariant and a contravariant index; the bare repeated-lower-index sum relies on Cartesian orthonormal coordinates.
