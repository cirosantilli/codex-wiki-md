<h1 id="6/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Adding and subtracting the transpose uniquely separates a [Cartesian second-rank tensor](../../../../../cartesian-second-rank-tensor.md) into its [symmetric tensor](../../../../../symmetric-tensor.md) and [antisymmetric tensor](../../../../../antisymmetric-tensor.md) parts:

$$
\boxed{S_{ij}=\frac12(B_{ij}+B_{ji}),\qquad A_{ij}=\frac12(B_{ij}-B_{ji}).}
$$

Then $S_{ji}=S_{ij}$, $A_{ji}=-A_{ij}$ and $S_{ij}+A_{ij}=B_{ij}$. Both parts transform as tensors because transposition commutes with the orthogonal similarity $B'=QBQ^T$. Uniqueness follows by taking the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of any such decomposition.
