<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The only possible singularity of $f(z)/(z-z_0)$ inside the contour is at $z_0$, with [residue](../../../../../residue.md) $f(z_0)$. It is at most a simple pole; when $f(z_0)=0$, it is removable. Thus the [residue theorem](../../../../../residue-theorem.md) gives the [Cauchy integral formula](../../../../../cauchy-integral-formula.md),

$$
\boxed{\oint_C\frac{f(z)}{z-z_0}dz=2\pi i\,f(z_0),}
$$

for a counterclockwise simple contour containing $z_0$ once, with $f$ analytic on a neighbourhood of the contour and its interior. More generally the answer is $2\pi i\,\operatorname{Ind}(C,z_0)f(z_0)$, where the [winding number](../../../../../winding-number.md) accounts for multiple turns or reversed orientation. A clockwise simple contour has the opposite sign.
