<h1 id="8/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a subgroup $H$ of a finite group $G$, [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) states

$$
\boxed{|G|=[G:H]|H|.}
$$

The left [cosets](../../../../../coset.md) partition the group and each has $|H|$ members, proving the formula. If $|G|=p$ is prime, the only divisors are one and $p$, so $H=\{e\}$ or $H=G$. Hence **a group of prime order has no nontrivial proper subgroups**.

The word “nontrivial” is necessary: $\{e\}$ is a proper subgroup of every prime-order group. Thus the source's shorter wording cannot literally exclude all proper subgroups.
