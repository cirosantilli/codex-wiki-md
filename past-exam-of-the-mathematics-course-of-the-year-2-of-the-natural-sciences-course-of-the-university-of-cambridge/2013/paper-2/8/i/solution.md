<h1 id="8/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Interpret the functions as maps on $\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$, so every reciprocal is defined. Let $e(x)=x$, $a(x)=-x$, $b(x)=1/x$ and $c(x)=-1/x$. With the left factor applied last, direct [function composition](../../../../../function-composition.md) gives the [Cayley table](../../../../../cayley-table.md)

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|cccc}
\circ&e&a&b&c\\\hline
e&e&a&b&c\\
a&a&e&c&b\\
b&b&c&e&a\\
c&c&b&a&e
\end{array}.}
$$

The table proves closure and identifies $e$ as the identity. Every element is its own inverse. Associativity is inherited from composition of maps, so the set is a [group](../../../../../group-split.md). The three nonidentity elements are commuting [involutions](../../../../../involution.md).

Its subgroups are

$$
\boxed{\{e\},\quad\{e,a\},\quad\{e,b\},\quad\{e,c\},\quad\{e,a,b,c\}.}
$$

There are no others: a subgroup containing two distinct nonidentity elements also contains their product, the third, and hence is the whole group; a subgroup containing just one has order two.

The abstract [dihedral group](../../../../../dihedral-group.md) $D_2$ has commuting generators $R,m$ with $R^2=m^2=I$, for example a rectangle's half-turn and reflection in one axis. The assignment $I\mapsto e$, $R\mapsto a$, $m\mapsto b$, $Rm\mapsto c$ respects products and is bijective. Thus

$$
\boxed{F\cong D_2\cong C_2\times C_2.}
$$

This is the [group generated by sign reversal and reciprocation](../../../../../group-generated-by-sign-reversal-and-reciprocation.md), a realization of the [Klein four-group](../../../../../klein-four-group.md). The nonzero domain is essential; treating these as functions defined at zero would not give the stated group.
