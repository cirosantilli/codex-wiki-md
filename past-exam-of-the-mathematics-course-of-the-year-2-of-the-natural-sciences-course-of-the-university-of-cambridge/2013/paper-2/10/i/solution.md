<h1 id="10/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a [group representation](../../../../../group-representation.md) $D:G\to\operatorname{GL}(V)$, a [faithful representation](../../../../../faithful-representation.md) is injective, equivalently $D(g)=I$ only for $g=e$. Thus no distinct group elements are represented by the same linear map.

Two [equivalent representations](../../../../../equivalence-of-group-representations.md) are related by one fixed invertible change of basis: $E(g)=S^{-1}D(g)S$ for every $g$. The same $S$ must work simultaneously for all group elements.

An [irreducible representation](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) acts on a nonzero vector space and has no invariant subspace other than $\{0\}$ and the whole space. An [invariant subspace](../../../../../invariant-subspace.md) is one carried into itself by every representing matrix.

The [character of a representation](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) is

$$
\boxed{\chi_D(g)=\operatorname{tr}D(g).}
$$

The [cyclic property of the trace](../../../../../cyclic-property-of-the-trace.md) makes it invariant under equivalence and constant on conjugacy classes. Its value at the identity is the dimension of the representation.
