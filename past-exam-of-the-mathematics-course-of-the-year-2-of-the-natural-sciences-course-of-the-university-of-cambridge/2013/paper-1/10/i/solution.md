<h1 id="10/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The time dependence gives $u_{tt}=-\omega^2u$, and the angular derivative vanishes for these radial [normal modes](../../../../../normal-mode.md). The [Laplacian](../../../../../laplacian.md) in [polar coordinates](../../../../../polar-coordinates.md) therefore yields

$$
R''+\frac1rR'+\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}R=0.
$$

Multiplication by $r$ puts the [Sturm-Liouville problem](../../../../../sturm-liouville-problem.md) in the form

$$
\boxed{-(rR')'=\lambda rR,\qquad p(r)=r,\quad q(r)=0,\quad w(r)=r,\quad\lambda=\omega^2/c^2.}
$$

The relevant [self-adjoint operator](../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md) is $\mathcal A R=-r^{-1}(rR')'$ in the [weighted inner product](../../../../../weighted-inner-product.md) with weight $r$. A physical displacement is regular at the origin, excluding the logarithmic singular solution. In particular $R$ is bounded and $rR'\to0$ there; regular eigenfunctions also have $R'(0)=0$. This origin condition is needed in addition to the fixed outer boundary.
