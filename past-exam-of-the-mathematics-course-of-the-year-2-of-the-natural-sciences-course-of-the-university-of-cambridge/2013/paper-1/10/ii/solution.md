<h1 id="10/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The conditions of regularity at $r=0$ and $R(a)=0$ select the [radial vibrations of a circular membrane](../../../../../radial-vibrations-of-a-circular-membrane.md) as an [eigenvalue problem](../../../../../eigenvalue-problem.md). Multiply $-(rR')'=\lambda rR$ by $\overline R$ and integrate. [Integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) gives

$$
\lambda\int_0^ar|R|^2dr=\int_0^ar|R'|^2dr-[r\overline RR']_0^a=\int_0^ar|R'|^2dr.
$$

The boundary term vanishes by regularity and $R(a)=0$. For a nonzero eigenfunction the denominator is positive; the numerator could vanish only for a constant function, and the outer condition would then make that function zero. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{\int_0^ar|R'|^2dr}{\int_0^ar|R|^2dr}>0,\qquad \nu=\frac{c\sqrt\lambda}{2\pi}\in\mathbb R.}
$$

This also proves reality without presuming real-valued eigenfunctions. Explicitly, regular solutions are [Bessel functions of the first kind](../../../../../bessel-function-of-the-first-kind.md) $R=J_0(\sqrt\lambda r)$, and the positive zeros of $J_0(\sqrt\lambda a)$ give the discrete spectrum.
