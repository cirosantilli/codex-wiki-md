<h1 id="9/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a variation $y+\varepsilon\eta$ with $\eta(a)=\eta(b)=0$, the first variation of the [functional](../../../../../functional.md) is

$$
\delta I=\int_a^b(F_y\eta+F_{y'}\eta')dx=\int_a^b\left(F_y-\frac{d}{dx}F_{y'}\right)\eta\,dx.
$$

The boundary term vanishes by the fixed endpoints. The [fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations](../../../../../fundamental-lemma-of-the-calculus-of-variations.md) therefore gives the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}F_{y'}-F_y=0.}
$$
