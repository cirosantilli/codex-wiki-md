<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Set $g=f$. The [cross-correlation](../../../../../cross-correlation.md) at zero is $h(0)=\int_{\mathbb R}|f(x)|^2dx$, while part (i) gives $\widehat h=|\widehat f|^2$. Evaluating the [inverse Fourier transform](../../../../../inverse-fourier-transform.md) at zero yields the [Parseval identity](../../../../../parseval-identity.md)

$$
\boxed{\int_{\mathbb R}|f(x)|^2dx=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat f(k)|^2dk.}
$$

This derivation is immediate for [Schwartz functions](../../../../../schwartz-function.md), for which all integrations and [Fourier inversion](../../../../../fourier-inversion-theorem.md) are justified. Approximation by such functions and completeness then extend the identity to [L2 space](../../../../../l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space.md), defining its [Fourier transform](../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) by the corresponding norm limit. This is the [Plancherel theorem](../../../../../plancherel-theorem.md) extension; no absolute integrability of an arbitrary square-integrable function is presumed.
