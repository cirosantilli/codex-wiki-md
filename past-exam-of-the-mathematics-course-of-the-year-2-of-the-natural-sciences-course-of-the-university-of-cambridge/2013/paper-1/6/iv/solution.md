<h1 id="6/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Normalize $e_1$ and put $f_1=Me_1/\mu_1$. The two vectors are an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of a plane $E$ invariant under $M$, since $Me_1=\mu_1f_1$ and $Mf_1=-\mu_1e_1$. Its [orthogonal complement](../../../../../orthogonal-complement.md) is also invariant: for $w\perp E$ and $z\in E$,

$$
\langle Mw,z\rangle=-\langle w,Mz\rangle=0.
$$

Apply the [spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices](../../../../../spectral-theorem-for-real-symmetric-matrices.md) to the restriction of $M^2$ to $E^\perp$. Any remaining negative [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) supplies another orthonormal pair by the same construction. Induction leaves only the [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md). Thus **every nonzero eigenvalue of $M^2$ has even multiplicity**. The pairs can share an eigenvalue, so this does not assert distinctness of the different $-\mu_j^2$.
