<h1 id="6/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Since $M$ is real, $(M^2)^T=(M^T)^2=(-M)^2=M^2$. For every real [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $x$,

$$
\boxed{x^TM^2x=-(Mx)^T(Mx)=-\|Mx\|^2\leq0.}
$$

Thus $M^2$ is a real [symmetric matrix](../../../../../symmetric-matrix.md) and is [negative semidefinite](../../../../../negative-semidefinite-matrix.md). When $n$ is odd, part (i) supplies a nonzero $x$ in the [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) of $M$, and then $M^2x=0$. Hence zero is an [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $M^2$. In fact $\ker M^2=\ker M$, as the displayed norm identity shows.
