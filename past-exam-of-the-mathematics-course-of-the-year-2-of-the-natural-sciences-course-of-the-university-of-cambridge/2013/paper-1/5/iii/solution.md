<h1 id="5/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Using part (i) and $A^\dagger=-A$ in the [Cayley transform](../../../../../cayley-transform-lie-theory.md) with argument $-A$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
U^\dagger&=[(I+A)^{-1}]^\dagger(I-A)^\dagger\\
&=(I-A)^{-1}(I+A)\\
&=(I+A)(I-A)^{-1}=U^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The factors commute because $I-A$ and $I+A$ are polynomials in the same [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md), and an invertible commuting matrix also commutes with the other factor's [matrix inverse](../../../../../matrix-inverse-split.md). Both inverses exist by part (ii). Thus **$U$ is unitary**.
