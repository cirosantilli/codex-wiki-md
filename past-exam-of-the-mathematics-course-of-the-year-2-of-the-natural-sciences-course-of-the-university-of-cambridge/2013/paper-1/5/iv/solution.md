<h1 id="5/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Multiplying the defining equation on the right by $I+A$ gives $(I+U)A=I-U$. Consequently the only possible answer is

$$
\boxed{A=(I+U)^{-1}(I-U)=(I-U)(I+U)^{-1}.}
$$

The factors commute because they are rational functions of $U$. Its [conjugate transpose](../../../../../conjugate-transpose.md), using the [unitarity](../../../../../unitary-operator.md) $U^\dagger=U^{-1}$, is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^\dagger&=(I+U^{-1})^{-1}(I-U^{-1})\\
&=(I+U)^{-1}U\bigl[-U^{-1}(I-U)\bigr]=-A.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $A$ is [Skew-Hermitian](../../../../../skew-hermitian-matrix.md). Moreover $I+A=2(I+U)^{-1}$ and $I-A=2U(I+U)^{-1}$, so substitution recovers $U$ and establishes existence as well as uniqueness. The [unitary matrix](../../../../../unitary-matrix.md) $U=-I$ is a counterexample to invertibility of $I+U$; more generally the obstruction is an [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $-1$.
