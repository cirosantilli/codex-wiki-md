<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The substitution $y=e^xg$ reduces the [homogeneous linear differential equation](../../../../../homogeneous-linear-differential-equation.md) to

$$
g''+\left(1-\frac1x\right)g'=0.
$$

A first [integration](../../../../../integral-split.md) gives $g'=Cxe^{-x}$, and a second gives $g=D-C(x+1)e^{-x}$. Hence

$$
\boxed{y=Ae^x+B(1+x).}
$$

The [Wronskian](../../../../../wronskian.md) of $1+x,e^x$ is $xe^x$, so they are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md) on either side of zero. At zero the differential equation has a [regular singular point](../../../../../regular-singular-point.md). The displayed solutions extend analytically through it; their linear combinations describe the global solutions when two continuous derivatives are required there. If no matching condition is imposed, the two sides have independent constants.
