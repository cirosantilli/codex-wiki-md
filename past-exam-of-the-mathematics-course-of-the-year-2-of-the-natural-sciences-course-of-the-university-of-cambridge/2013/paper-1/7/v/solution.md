<h1 id="7/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Use the [Wirtinger derivatives](../../../../../wirtinger-derivatives.md) $\partial_z=(\partial_x-i\partial_y)/2$ and $\partial_{\bar z}=(\partial_x+i\partial_y)/2$, for which $\Delta=4\partial_z\partial_{\bar z}$. Since $H$ and $G$ are [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md),

$$
\partial_{\bar z}F=H(z),\qquad \Delta F=4H'(z),\qquad \Delta^2F=16\partial_z\partial_{\bar z}H'(z)=0.
$$

The [Laplacian](../../../../../laplacian.md) has real coefficients, so taking real and imaginary parts proves

$$
\boxed{\Delta^2U=0,\qquad\Delta^2V=0.}
$$

This is the [complex analytic representation of biharmonic functions](../../../../../complex-analytic-representation-of-biharmonic-functions.md); $F$ itself is generally not holomorphic because of its $\bar z$ factor.
