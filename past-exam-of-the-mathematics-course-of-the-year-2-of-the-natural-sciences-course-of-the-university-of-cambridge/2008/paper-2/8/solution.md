<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

A [normal subgroup](../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $H$ satisfies $gHg^{-1}=H$ for every $g\in G$, equivalently $gH=Hg$. A left [coset](../../../../coset.md) is $gH=\{gh:h\in H\}$ and a right coset is $Hg=\{hg:h\in H\}$.

If $|G|=2|H|$, then for $g\in H$ both [cosets](../../../../coset.md) equal $H$. For $g\notin H$, neither coset intersects $H$: an intersection would imply $g\in H$. Both have $|H|$ elements, because multiplication by $g$ is a [bijection](../../../../bijection.md). They therefore both equal $G\setminus H$, proving $gH=Hg$ in this case too. This establishes that an [index-two subgroup is normal](../../../../index-two-subgroup-is-normal.md).

For [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), left [cosets](../../../../coset.md) partition $G$. Indeed if $g_1h_1=g_2h_2$, then $g_1H=g_2H$ by multiplying by elements of $H$; otherwise the [cosets](../../../../coset.md) are disjoint. Every coset has $|H|$ elements, so if there are $[G:H]$ [cosets](../../../../coset.md),

$$
\boxed{|G|=[G:H]|H|.}
$$

The [group order](../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) of $H$ thus divides the [group order](../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) of $G$. Normality is not needed for this divisibility statement.
