<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Multiply by the $(m,n)$ sine product and integrate over the square. [Fourier orthogonality](../../../../../fourier-orthogonality.md) gives squared norm $\ell^2/4$, and thus

$$
c_{mn}=\frac4{\ell^2}\left(\int_0^\ell\sin\frac{m\pi x}{\ell}dx\right)\left(\int_0^\ell\sin\frac{n\pi y}{\ell}dy\right)
=\frac{4[1-(-1)^m][1-(-1)^n]}{\pi^2mn}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{c_{mn}=\begin{cases}16/(\pi^2mn),&m,n\text{ both odd},\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}}
$$

This [double Fourier sine series](../../../../../double-fourier-sine-series.md) equals one at interior points. At boundary points every summand vanishes, consistent with the endpoint value of the sine extension.
