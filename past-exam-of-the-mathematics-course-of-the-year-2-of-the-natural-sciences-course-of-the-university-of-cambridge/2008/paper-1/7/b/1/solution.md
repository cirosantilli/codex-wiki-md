<h1 id="7/b/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

For an isolated [pole](../../../../../../pole.md) of order $N\geq1$, the [Laurent coefficients](../../../../../../laurent-coefficient.md) must satisfy

$$
\boxed{a_{-N}\ne0,\qquad a_n=0\quad(n<-N).}
$$

Then the [principal part of a Laurent series](../../../../../../principal-part-of-a-laurent-series.md) is a finite sum with lowest power $-N$. The classification at the center assumes analyticity on a punctured neighborhood $0<|z-z_0|<b$, or an analytic continuation there. A Laurent expansion on an annulus with positive inner radius alone does not imply that the center is an isolated singularity.
