<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For $f=u+iv$ holomorphic in $z=x+iy$, the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) are $u_x=v_y$ and $u_y=-v_x$. Therefore the normal [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) to the two level curves obey

$$
\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=u_xv_x+u_yv_y=u_x(-u_y)+u_yu_x=0.
$$

At a point where $f'\ne0$, both [gradients](../../../../../gradient.md) are nonzero, so their perpendicular tangent lines are also perpendicular. At critical points this argument does not assert that regular level curves exist.

Take

$$
\boxed{f(z)=z^2.}
$$

Its [real part](../../../../../real-part.md) is $u=x^2-y^2$, so the specified first family is $u=-\alpha$. Its [imaginary part](../../../../../imaginary-part.md) is $v=2xy$, so the second family is $v=2\beta$. Since $\beta\ne0$, their intersections exclude the origin, where $f'(z)=2z$ would vanish. Thus these [hyperbolas](../../../../../hyperbola.md) meet orthogonally as required.
