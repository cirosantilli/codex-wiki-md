<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

On the [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md), constant $\theta$ gives a latitude [circle](../../../../../circle.md) of radius $a\sin\theta$ and height $a\cos\theta$. Constant $\phi$ gives a meridian semicircle in a vertical plane through the $z$ axis. The [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of [spherical coordinates](../../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) is

$$
\boldsymbol e_r=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta),\quad
\boldsymbol e_\theta=(\cos\theta\cos\phi,\cos\theta\sin\phi,-\sin\theta),\quad
\boldsymbol e_\phi=(-\sin\phi,\cos\phi,0).
$$

Here $\boldsymbol e_r$ points outward, $\boldsymbol e_\theta$ along increasing [polar angle](../../../../../polar-angle.md), and $\boldsymbol e_\phi$ along increasing [azimuth](../../../../../azimuth.md). At either pole the radial [unit vector](../../../../../unit-vector.md) is unambiguous, but the two tangential coordinate vectors depend on the undefined longitude. The figure shows their directions away from the poles.

<a id="1/a/image-latitude-and-meridian-curves-with-the-three-spherical-coordinate-unit-vectors-at-a-regular-point"></a>
![](../../../../../past-exam-of-the-mathematics-course-of-the-year-2-of-the-natural-sciences-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2008/paper-1-spherical-coordinate-basis.png)

**[Figure 1](#1/a/image-latitude-and-meridian-curves-with-the-three-spherical-coordinate-unit-vectors-at-a-regular-point). Latitude and meridian curves with the three spherical coordinate unit vectors at a regular point**.

This coordinate ambiguity does not make a vector field singular if its tangential coefficient vanishes suitably. In [Cartesian coordinates](../../../../../cartesian-coordinate-system.md),

$$
\boldsymbol A^{(+)}=\frac{(-y,x,0)}{r(r+z)},\qquad
\boldsymbol A^{(-)}=\frac{(y,-x,0)}{r(r-z)}.
$$

The first is smooth at the north pole and zero there; the second is smooth and zero at the south pole. Their actual singular axes are the opposite ones. These are the regular patches of a [two-patch vector potential for a radial monopole](../../../../../two-patch-vector-potential-for-a-radial-monopole.md).
