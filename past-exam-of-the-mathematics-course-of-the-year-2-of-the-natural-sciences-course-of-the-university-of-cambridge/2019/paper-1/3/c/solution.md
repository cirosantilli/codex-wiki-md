<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the [Euler-Cauchy equation](../../../../../euler-cauchy-equation.md), substitute $y=x^n$ on an interval not containing zero. The coefficient of $x^{n-2}$ is

$$
n(n-1)-3n+3=n^2-4n+3=(n-1)(n-3).
$$

The distinct roots give

$$
\boxed{y(x)=C_1x+C_2x^3.}
$$

These solutions are linearly independent since their [Wronskian](../../../../../wronskian.md) is $2x^3\ne0$ away from zero. Both nevertheless approach zero as $x\to0$, which will make the endpoint conditions in the next part insufficient for uniqueness.
