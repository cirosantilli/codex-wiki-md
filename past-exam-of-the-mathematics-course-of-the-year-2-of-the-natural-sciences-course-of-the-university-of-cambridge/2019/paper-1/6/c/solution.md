<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The second coordinate direction is already an [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) with [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) three. The first and third coordinates form a symmetric block, whose sum and difference directions give

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{eigenvalue}&\text{unit eigenvector}\\\hline
6&(1,0,1)^T/\sqrt2\\
3&(0,1,0)^T\\
2c-4&(1,0,-1)^T/\sqrt2
\end{array}.}
$$

For example, the sum of the block's diagonal and off-diagonal entries is $(1+c)+(5-c)=6$, while their difference is $(1+c)-(5-c)=2c-4$. Equivalently the characteristic polynomial is $(\lambda-6)(\lambda-3)(\lambda-2c+4)$.

The displayed basis works for every real $c$. At $c=7/2$, the [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) three has [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) spanned by the second and third listed vectors; at $c=5$, the [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) six has [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) spanned by the first and third. Every nonzero vector in a repeated [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) is an [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md), and any [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of it may replace the displayed choices.
