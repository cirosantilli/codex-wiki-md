<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A real [symmetric matrix](../../../../../symmetric-matrix.md) has a real orthonormal eigenbasis. Put those [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) into an [orthogonal matrix](../../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) $Q$, so $Q^TAQ=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$. With the change of coordinates $x=Qx'$, or $x'=Q^Tx$,

$$
\boxed{\sum_{ij}A_{ij}x_ix_j=x^TAx=x'^T(Q^TAQ)x'=\sum_i a_i(x_i')^2.}
$$

Thus the coefficients are the [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) and the new coordinate directions are the [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md). This [orthogonal diagonalization](../../../../../orthogonal-diagonalization-of-a-real-symmetric-matrix.md) preserves Euclidean lengths and angles while removing cross terms from the [quadratic form](../../../../../quadratic-form-split.md).
