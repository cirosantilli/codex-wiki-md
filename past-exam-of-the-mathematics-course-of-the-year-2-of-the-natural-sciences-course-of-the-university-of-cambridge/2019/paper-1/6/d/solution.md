<h1 id="6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use the principal coordinates

$$
p=\frac{x_1+x_3}{\sqrt2},\qquad q=x_2,\qquad r=\frac{x_1-x_3}{\sqrt2}.
$$

Part (c) puts the equation in the canonical form

$$
\boxed{6p^2+3q^2+(2c-4)r^2=1.}
$$

If $c>2$, all three coefficients are positive: the surface is an [ellipsoid](../../../../../ellipsoid.md) with semiaxes $1/\sqrt6$, $1/\sqrt3$ and $1/\sqrt{2c-4}$ along the three [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) listed in part (c).

If $c=2$, the equation is independent of $r$: it is an [elliptic cylinder](../../../../../elliptic-cylinder.md) parallel to $(1,0,-1)$, with cross-sectional semiaxes $1/\sqrt6$ and $1/\sqrt3$.

If $c<2$, the form has two positive coefficients and one negative coefficient: it is a [one-sheet hyperboloid](../../../../../one-sheet-hyperboloid.md), with axis $(1,0,-1)$ and waist ellipse $6p^2+3q^2=1$ at $r=0$. Its axial scale is $1/\sqrt{4-2c}$.

These are the three cases of the [signature transition from an ellipsoid to an elliptic cylinder and a one-sheet hyperboloid](../../../../../signature-transition-from-an-ellipsoid-to-an-elliptic-cylinder-and-a-one-sheet-hyperboloid.md). At the repeated [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $c=7/2$ and $c=5$, [principal axes](../../../../../principal-axis.md) in the corresponding two-dimensional [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) are not unique: the [ellipsoid](../../../../../ellipsoid.md) is respectively an [oblate spheroid](../../../../../oblate-spheroid.md) about the $p$ axis or a [prolate spheroid](../../../../../prolate-spheroid.md) about the $q$ axis.
