<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [adjoint operator](../../../../../adjoint-operator.md) $A^\dagger$, when it exists, is defined by

$$
\boxed{\langle Ax,y\rangle=\langle x,A^\dagger y\rangle}
$$

for all vectors in the relevant domains. This convention takes the [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) to be conjugate-linear in its first argument. Nondegeneracy makes the adjoint unique.

For the standard complex [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) $\langle x,y\rangle=\overline{x}^{\,T}y$, the equality becomes $\overline{x}^{\,T}\overline{A}^{\,T}y=\overline{x}^{\,T}A^\dagger y$ for every $x,y$, so

$$
\boxed{A^\dagger=\overline A^{\,T}.}
$$

Thus the matrix adjoint is the [Hermitian conjugate](../../../../../hermitian-conjugation.md), not merely the [transpose](../../../../../transpose.md) unless the entries are real. In a basis with positive Hermitian [Gram matrix](../../../../../gram-matrix.md) $G$, the same definition instead gives $A^\dagger_G=G^{-1}\overline A^{\,T}G$, making the dependence on the chosen [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) explicit.
