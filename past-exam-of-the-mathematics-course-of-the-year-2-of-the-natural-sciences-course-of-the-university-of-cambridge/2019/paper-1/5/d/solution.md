<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the positive integer $k$ in the nilpotence condition, diagonalize $B=P\Lambda P^{-1}$. Then

$$
0=B^k=P\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1^k,\ldots,\lambda_n^k)P^{-1}.
$$

Every diagonal entry must vanish, so each $\lambda_j=0$. Consequently $\Lambda=0$ and $\boxed{B=0}$. This proves that [a diagonalizable nilpotent matrix is zero](../../../../../a-diagonalizable-nilpotent-matrix-is-zero.md).

Without diagonalizability a counterexample is

$$
\boxed{C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\ne0,\qquad C^2=0.}
$$

Its only [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is zero but its kernel has dimension one, so it cannot have a basis of [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md). It is the simplest nontrivial nilpotent [Jordan block](../../../../../jordan-block.md).
