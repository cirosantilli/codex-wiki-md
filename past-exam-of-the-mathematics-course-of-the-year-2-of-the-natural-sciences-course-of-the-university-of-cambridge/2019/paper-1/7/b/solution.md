<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) give

$$
\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=u_xv_x+u_yv_y=u_x(-u_y)+u_yu_x=0.
$$

At a regular point, each level curve has tangent perpendicular to its nonzero [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md). The two [gradients](../../../../../gradient.md), and hence the two tangents, are orthogonal. Therefore **regular level curves meet at right angles**.

The qualification is necessary: $|\nabla u|=|\nabla v|=|f'(z)|$, so at a critical point both [gradients](../../../../../gradient.md) vanish and this argument gives no tangent direction. For example, for $f(z)=z^2$, the zero level curves of the real and imaginary parts are diagonals and coordinate axes; they meet at $45^\circ$ at zero. This is the regular-point statement of [orthogonal level curves of harmonic conjugates](../../../../../orthogonal-level-curves-of-harmonic-conjugates.md).
