<h1 id="7/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [complex exponential](../../../../../../complex-exponential-function.md) never vanishes. The zeros of $\sinh z$ occur at $z=n\pi i$, $n\in\mathbb Z$, and they are simple because $\cosh(n\pi i)=(-1)^n\ne0$. The numerator is $e^{2n\pi i}=1$ there, so no cancellation occurs. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{simple poles at }z=n\pi i\ (n\in\mathbb Z);\qquad\text{no finite zeros}.}
$$

For example their residues are $1/\cosh(n\pi i)=(-1)^n$.

Infinity requires care: the [poles](../../../../../../pole.md) themselves tend to infinity. Equivalently $f(1/w)$ has [poles](../../../../../../pole.md) at $w=1/(n\pi i)$ accumulating at zero. Thus $\boxed{\infty\text{ is a non-isolated singularity}}$, and it is not a zero. In the usual classification of isolated singularities, it cannot be called a [removable singularity](../../../../../../removable-singularity.md), [pole](../../../../../../pole.md) or isolated essential singularity. The [non-isolated singularity](../../../../../../non-isolated-singularity.md) distinction is necessary even though the function is meromorphic at every finite point.
