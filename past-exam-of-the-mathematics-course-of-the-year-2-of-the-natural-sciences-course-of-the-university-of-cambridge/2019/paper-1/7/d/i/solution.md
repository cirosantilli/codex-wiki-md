<h1 id="7/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The denominator factors as $z^2+iz+6=(z-2i)(z+3i)$, with no cancellation against the numerator. Thus there are

$$
\boxed{\text{simple poles at }z=2i,-3i;\qquad\text{a simple zero at }z=4.}
$$

To inspect infinity, put $w=1/z$:

$$
f(1/w)=\frac{w-4w^2}{1+iw+6w^2}=w+O(w^2).
$$

Hence infinity is a [removable singularity at infinity](../../../../../../removable-singularity-at-infinity.md), with limiting value zero. After this natural extension to the [Riemann sphere](../../../../../../riemann-sphere.md) it is also a **simple zero**. There are no other zeros or singularities.
