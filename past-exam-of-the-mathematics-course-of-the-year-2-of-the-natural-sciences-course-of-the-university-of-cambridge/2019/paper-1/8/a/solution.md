<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the normalized equation, $x_0$ is an [ordinary point](../../../../../ordinary-point-criterion-for-a-second-order-equation.md) when both $p$ and $q$ are analytic near $x_0$. A singular point is a [regular singular point](../../../../../regular-singular-point.md) when $(x-x_0)p(x)$ and $(x-x_0)^2q(x)$ extend analytically to $x_0$; equivalently $p$ has at most a [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md) and $q$ at most a double [pole](../../../../../pole.md) there.

Thus ordinary points admit ordinary power-series solutions, while the [Frobenius method](../../../../../frobenius-method.md) applies at a regular singular point. The definitions refer to the coefficients after dividing by the leading coefficient of $y''$.
