<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Evenness means the negative half of the function is determined as well: this is the [triangular function](../../../../../triangular-function.md) $q(x)=1-|x|$ on $|x|\leq1$, zero outside. Its transform is real and even:

$$
\widetilde q(k)=2\int_0^1(1-x)\cos(kx)\,dx.
$$

[Integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md), or integrating the two terms separately, gives

$$
\boxed{\widetilde q(k)=\frac{2(1-\cos k)}{k^2}=\left(\frac{\sin(k/2)}{k/2}\right)^2,\qquad\widetilde q(0)=1.}
$$

The apparent singularity at zero is removable, and its limiting value is the area under $q$. This is the [Fourier transform of a triangular function](../../../../../fourier-transform-of-a-triangular-function.md).
