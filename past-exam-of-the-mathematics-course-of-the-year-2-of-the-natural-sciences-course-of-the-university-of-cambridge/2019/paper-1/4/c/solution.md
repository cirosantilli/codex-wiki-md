<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Differentiate the defining Fourier integral $n$ times with respect to $k$:

$$
\frac{d^n\widetilde f}{dk^n}=\int_{\mathbb R}(-ix)^nf(x)e^{-ikx}\,dx.
$$

For a nonnegative integer $n$, this gives

$$
\boxed{\mathcal F[x^nf(x)](k)=i^n\frac{d^n\widetilde f(k)}{dk^n}.}
$$

Integrability of $x^nf$ together with suitable lower moments justifies differentiation under the integral. The same multiplication-derivative identity holds more generally for [tempered distributions](../../../../../tempered-distribution.md).
