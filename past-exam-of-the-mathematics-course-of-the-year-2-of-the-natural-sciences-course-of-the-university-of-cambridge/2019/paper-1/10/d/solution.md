<h1 id="10/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For any real coupling, the same normalized Gaussian family has [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md)

$$
E_\epsilon(\alpha)=\frac\alpha2+\frac1{2\alpha}+\frac{3\epsilon}{4\alpha^2}.
$$

If $\epsilon<0$, then as $\alpha\downarrow0$ the negative $\alpha^{-2}$ term dominates the positive $\alpha^{-1}$ and $\alpha$ terms. Each trial still has finite [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), is normalized and decays at infinity, but its [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) tends to $-\infty$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\inf_{\|\psi\|_2=1}E[\psi]=-\infty;\qquad\text{there is no minimum energy.}}
$$

This variational sequence makes the instability of the negative quartic potential explicit, without mistaking any local well near the origin for a global ground-state bound.
