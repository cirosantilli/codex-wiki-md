A [group](../../../../../group-split.md) is a set $G$ equipped with a binary operation satisfying the [group axioms](../../../../../group-axioms.md): closure; [associativity](../../../../../associative-property.md), $(gh)k=g(hk)$; an [identity element](../../../../../identity-element.md) $e$ with $eg=ge=g$; and an [inverse element](../../../../../inverse-element.md) $g^{-1}$ for every $g$, with $gg^{-1}=g^{-1}g=e$.

A [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $H$ is a subset that is a [group](../../../../../group-split.md) under the restricted operation. Equivalently, it is nonempty and contains $xy^{-1}$ whenever $x,y\in H$. A [generating set of a group](../../../../../generating-set-of-a-group.md) is a set whose elements and inverses can be multiplied in finite words to produce every group element.

The [group order](../../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) is its number of elements, $|G|$. The [order of a group element](../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $g$ is the least positive integer $r$ with $g^r=e$, or infinity if there is none. [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) states that for a finite [group](../../../../../group-split.md) and [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $H$, $|H|$ divides $|G|$ and $|G|=[G:H]|H|$. In particular, every finite element order divides the [group order](../../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md).

A [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) is generated by one element, so every element is a power of that generator. A [group isomorphism](../../../../../group-isomorphism.md) is a bijective [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md): a bijection $\phi:G\to G'$ satisfying $\phi(gh)=\phi(g)\phi(h)$ for all $g,h$.
