The [symmetric group](../../../../../symmetric-group.md) $S_n$ consists of all bijections of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, with composition as its operation; its [group order](../../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) is $n!$.

An $m$-dimensional complex [group representation](../../../../../group-representation.md) of $G$ is a [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) $\rho:G\to GL(V)$, where $V$ is an $m$-dimensional complex [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md). After a choice of [basis](../../../../../basis.md), each $\rho(g)$ is an invertible $m\times m$ complex [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md), with $\rho(gh)=\rho(g)\rho(h)$.

An [irreducible representation](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) has $V\ne0$ and no invariant [vector subspace](../../../../../vector-subspace.md) other than $\{0\}$ and $V$. Two [group representations](../../../../../group-representation.md) $\rho$ on $V$ and $\sigma$ on $W$ are [isomorphic representations](../../../../../isomorphic-representations.md) if there exists an invertible [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md) $S:V\to W$ such that $S\rho(g)=\sigma(g)S$ for every $g$. They are inequivalent if no such map exists; in equal-dimensional [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) form this says no fixed change of [basis](../../../../../basis.md) conjugates every $\rho(g)$ to $\sigma(g)$.
