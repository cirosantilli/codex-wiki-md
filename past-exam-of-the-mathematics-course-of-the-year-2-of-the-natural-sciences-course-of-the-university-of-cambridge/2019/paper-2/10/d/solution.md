<h1 id="10/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For a finite [group](../../../../../group-split.md) over $\mathbb C$, the number of inequivalent [irreducible representations](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) is its number of [conjugacy classes](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md), and the [sum of squares of irreducible representation dimensions](../../../../../sum-of-squares-of-irreducible-representation-dimensions.md) equals the [group order](../../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md). There are therefore five irreducibles for $S_4$, with squared dimensions summing to $24$.

First establish that part (c) found every [one-dimensional representation](../../../../../one-dimensional-representation.md). In such a representation, conjugate elements have the same scalar image. All [transpositions](../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) are conjugate and square to the identity, so they all have one common image $s$ with $s^2=1$. [Transpositions](../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) generate $S_4$, hence $s=1$ gives exactly the [trivial representation](../../../../../trivial-representation.md), while $s=-1$ gives exactly the [sign representation](../../../../../sign-representation.md). There are precisely two degree-one irreducibles.

The other three dimensions $d_1,d_2,d_3$ are integers at least two and satisfy

$$
d_1^2+d_2^2+d_3^2=24-1-1=22.
$$

No one of them can be at least four, since $4^2+2^2+2^2=24>22$. They are therefore all two or three. Starting from three twos gives sum of squares $12$; each replacement of a two by a three adds $5$. To reach $22$ requires exactly two replacements. Thus the complete list is

$$
\boxed{1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 3}.
$$

The count of [conjugacy classes](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) guarantees that these are all the inequivalent complex [irreducible representations](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md), not just dimensions of a proposed incomplete list.
