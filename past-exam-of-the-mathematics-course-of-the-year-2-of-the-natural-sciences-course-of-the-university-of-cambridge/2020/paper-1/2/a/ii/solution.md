<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For an [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) $n\geq0$, choose the [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) whose [integer parity](../../../../../../parity-mathematics.md) agrees with $n$ and set the other initial coefficient to zero. At $m=n$, the numerator in the recurrence vanishes, so $a_{n+2}=0$ and every later coefficient in that [integer parity](../../../../../../parity-mathematics.md) vanishes. With a nonzero coefficient of $x^n$, this gives a [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) of degree exactly $n$. Normalize it by $P_n(1)=1$.

For $n=0$, the even branch is constant; for $n=1$, the odd branch is proportional to $x$. For $n=2$, the recurrence gives $a_2=-3a_0$, so normalization fixes $a_0=-1/2$. Hence

$$
\boxed{P_0(x)=1,\qquad P_1(x)=x,\qquad P_2(x)=\frac12(3x^2-1).}
$$

The opposite-parity branch generally does not terminate; [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) termination does not mean that both independent solutions become [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md).
