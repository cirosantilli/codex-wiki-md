<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Factor the denominator as $(z-z_0)(z+z_0)$. Since $z_0\ne0$, its zeros are distinct, and the potential simple [poles](../../../../../pole.md) and their [residues](../../../../../residue.md) are

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_0}f=\frac{g(z_0)}{2z_0},\qquad
\operatorname{Res}_{z=-z_0}f=-\frac{g(-z_0)}{2z_0}.}
$$

For example, the first follows by taking $\lim_{z\to z_0}(z-z_0)f(z)$. If the numerator vanishes at either denominator zero, the singularity there is removable and the corresponding [residue](../../../../../residue.md) is zero. Thus the data identify the possible [poles](../../../../../pole.md), not an unconditional [pole](../../../../../pole.md) at a point canceled by $g$.
