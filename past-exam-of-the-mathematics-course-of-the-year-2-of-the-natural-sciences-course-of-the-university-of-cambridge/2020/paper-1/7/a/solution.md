<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Cauchy integral formula](../../../../../cauchy-integral-formula.md) for [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md), with $z_0=0$, gives

$$
\oint_C\frac{f(z)}{z^{n+1}}\,dz=\frac{2\pi i}{n!}f^{(n)}(0).
$$

The [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) cycles of [sine](../../../../../sine.md) and [cosine](../../../../../cosine.md) give $\sin^{(n)}(0)=\sin(n\pi/2)$ and $\cos^{(n)}(0)=\cos(n\pi/2)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\oint_C\frac{\sin z}{z^{n+1}}\,dz=\frac{2\pi i}{n!}\sin\frac{n\pi}{2},\qquad
\oint_C\frac{\cos z}{z^{n+1}}\,dz=\frac{2\pi i}{n!}\cos\frac{n\pi}{2}.}
$$

In particular the [sine](../../../../../sine.md) [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) vanishes for even $n$, and the [cosine](../../../../../cosine.md) [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) vanishes for odd $n$. For the remaining cases their signs alternate. The radius is irrelevant provided $R>0$, since the numerator is entire and the origin is enclosed once counterclockwise.
