<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a Frobenius exponent $r$, put $p_0=\lim_{x\to0}xP(x)=1$ and $q_0=\lim_{x\to0}x^2Q(x)=0$. The [indicial equation](../../../../../indicial-equation.md) is

$$
r(r-1)+p_0r+q_0=r^2=0,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{r_1=r_2=0.}
$$

A fundamental pair has the repeated-root Frobenius form

$$
y_1=\sum_{n=0}^\infty u_nx^n,\qquad
y_2=y_1\ln x+\sum_{n=0}^\infty v_nx^n,
\qquad y=C_1y_1+C_2y_2
$$

on a positive-$x$ interval. In this case a [real analytic](../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) solution is particularly simple: $y_1=1-x$ satisfies the equation exactly. A convenient logarithmic companion, obtained below, is $y_2=x+(1-x)\ln[x/(1-x)]$ for $0<x<1$. It is independent of $y_1$ because of its logarithmic divergence at zero.
