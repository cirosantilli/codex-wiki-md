<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $A(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_nx^n$ denote the analytic part of the prescribed ansatz. Its logarithmic part has [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md)

$$
\frac d{dx}[(b+cx)\ln x]=c\ln x+\frac bx+c,\qquad
\frac{d^2}{dx^2}[(b+cx)\ln x]=\frac cx-\frac b{x^2}.
$$

After substitution, the full [differential equation](../../../../../differential-equation-split.md) reduces to

$$
x(1-x)A''+(1-x)A'+A+2c(1-x)+(b+c)\ln x=0.
$$

The first terms are [real analytic](../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) at zero and cannot cancel a nonzero [logarithm](../../../../../logarithm.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{c=-b.}
$$

The cancellation of the apparent $1/x$ terms is already built into this calculation.
