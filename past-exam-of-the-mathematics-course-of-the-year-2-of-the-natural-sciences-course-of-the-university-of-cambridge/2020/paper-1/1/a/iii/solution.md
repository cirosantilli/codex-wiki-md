<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Differentiate the position [vector](../../../../../../vector.md), using the angular factor from the [scale factors of orthogonal coordinates](../../../../../../scale-factors-of-orthogonal-coordinates.md):

$$
\boxed{d\mathbf r=\mathbf e_\rho\,d\rho+\rho\mathbf e_\phi\,d\phi+\mathbf e_z\,dz.}
$$

The [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) is an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md), so all cross terms in its squared length vanish. The Euclidean [line element](../../../../../../line-element.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{|d\mathbf r|^2=d\rho^2+\rho^2d\phi^2+dz^2.}
$$

In particular, an angular change $d\phi$ at fixed radius traverses a physical distance $\rho\,|d\phi|$.
