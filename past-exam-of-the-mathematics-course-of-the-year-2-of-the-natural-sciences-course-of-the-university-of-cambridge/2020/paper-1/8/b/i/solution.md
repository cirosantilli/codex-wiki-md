<h1 id="8/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Transform in time, leaving $r$ as a parameter. Applying the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) identity twice gives $\widetilde{\partial_t^2\sigma}=-\omega^2\widetilde\sigma$. Therefore

$$
-\omega^2\widetilde\sigma-\partial_r^2\widetilde\sigma+\mu^2\widetilde\sigma=0,
\qquad
\boxed{\partial_r^2\widetilde\sigma=(\mu^2-\omega^2)\widetilde\sigma.}
$$

These manipulations can be justified for sufficiently decaying smooth fields, or with distributional [Fourier transforms](../../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) when ordinary integrability is unavailable.
