<h1 id="8/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Reality requires $\widetilde\sigma(\omega,r)=\widetilde\sigma(-\omega,r)^*$. The way it constrains the amplitudes depends on whether the spatial [wave number](../../../../../../wavenumber.md) is real or imaginary.

For $|\omega|>\mu$, $k$ is real and even. [Complex conjugation](../../../../../../complex-conjugation.md) interchanges the two exponentials, so comparing independent spatial factors gives

$$
\boxed{A(\omega)=B(-\omega)^*\qquad(|\omega|>\mu).}
$$

The companion equation $B(\omega)=A(-\omega)^*$ is the same constraint with [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) reversed and conjugated. Neither oscillatory spatial branch grows as $r\to\infty$.

For $|\omega|<\mu$, put $k=i\kappa$ with $\kappa>0$. Then

$$
\widetilde\sigma=A(\omega)e^{-\kappa r}+B(\omega)e^{\kappa r}.
$$

Boundedness excludes the growing exponential, and reality then pairs the remaining amplitudes:

$$
\boxed{B(\omega)=0,\qquad A(\omega)=A(-\omega)^*\qquad(|\omega|<\mu).}
$$

These are the two independent low-frequency requirements. At threshold boundedness eliminates the separate linear-in-$r$ solution discussed in (ii). The constraints are understood for a [Fourier transform](../../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) representation in which spatial mode amplitudes are well defined; sufficient amplitude integrability also ensures the spectral [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md) itself is bounded.
