<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [skew-Hermitian matrix](../../../../../skew-hermitian-matrix.md) satisfies $A^\dagger=-A$, where $\dagger$ denotes the [conjugate transpose](../../../../../conjugate-transpose.md). If $Av=\lambda v$ with $v\ne0$, then

$$
\overline\lambda\,v^\dagger v=v^\dagger A^\dagger v=-v^\dagger Av=-\lambda\,v^\dagger v.
$$

Since $v^\dagger v>0$, this gives $\boxed{\overline\lambda=-\lambda,\text{ so }\lambda\in i\mathbb R}$.

A [unitary matrix](../../../../../unitary-matrix.md) satisfies $U^\dagger U=UU^\dagger=I$. It preserves the [Hermitian inner product](../../../../../hermitian-inner-product.md) and [norm](../../../../../norm.md). Thus if $Uv=\mu v$,

$$
\|v\|^2=\|Uv\|^2=|\mu|^2\|v\|^2,
$$

and therefore $\boxed{|\mu|=1}$. Zero is allowed among the purely imaginary [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) in the first statement, but not among the unitary [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md).
