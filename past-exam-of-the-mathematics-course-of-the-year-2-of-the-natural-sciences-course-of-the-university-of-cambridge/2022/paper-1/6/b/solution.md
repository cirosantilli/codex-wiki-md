<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $Av_i=\lambda_i v_i$ and $Av_j=\lambda_jv_j$. The [skew-Hermitian matrix](../../../../../skew-hermitian-matrix.md) property gives

$$
\lambda_jv_i^\dagger v_j=v_i^\dagger Av_j=-(Av_i)^\dagger v_j=-\overline\lambda_i\,v_i^\dagger v_j=\lambda_i v_i^\dagger v_j,
$$

where the last equality uses part (a). Distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) therefore imply $\boxed{v_i^\dagger v_j=0\text{ for }i\ne j}$. Normalize each [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) to length one. Since there are $n$ distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md), these vectors form an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md).

Put them in the columns of $U$. Then $U$ is a [unitary matrix](../../../../../unitary-matrix.md) and $AU=UD$, so

$$
\boxed{A=UDU^\dagger,\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n).}
$$
