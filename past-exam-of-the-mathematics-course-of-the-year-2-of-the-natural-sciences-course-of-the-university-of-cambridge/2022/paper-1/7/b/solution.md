<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $g=u+iv$ and let its constant [modulus](../../../../../modulus.md) be $C$. If $C=0$, then $g=0$. For $C>0$, differentiating $u^2+v^2=C^2$ with respect to $x$ and $y$, then applying the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md), gives

$$
u u_x+v v_x=0,\qquad u u_y+v v_y=-u v_x+v u_x=0.
$$

Thus

$$
\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\v&-u\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u_x\\v_x\end{pmatrix}=0.
$$

The coefficient [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) has [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) $-(u^2+v^2)=-C^2\ne0$, forcing $u_x=v_x=0$. The [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) then also give $u_y=v_y=0$. Therefore $u,v$ are constant on a connected domain and

$$
\boxed{g\text{ is constant}.}
$$

This proves the [constant-modulus holomorphic function](../../../../../constant-modulus-holomorphic-function.md) result. If a domain is disconnected, the conclusion applies separately to each connected component.
