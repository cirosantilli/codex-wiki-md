<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) are $\boxed{u_x=v_y,\quad u_y=-v_x}$. They give the [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) identity

$$
\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=u_xv_x+u_yv_y=v_yv_x-v_xv_y=0.
$$

At a regular intersection, where $f'(z)\ne0$, the two nonzero [gradients](../../../../../gradient.md) are [normal vectors](../../../../../normal-vector.md) to the two [level sets](../../../../../level-set.md), so their tangent directions are perpendicular. This is the [orthogonality of regular real and imaginary level curves](../../../../../orthogonality-of-regular-real-and-imaginary-level-curves.md).

The regularity qualification is necessary: $|\nabla u|=|\nabla v|=|f'|$, so at a [critical point](../../../../../critical-point.md) both normals vanish and the curves need not have unique tangents. For example $f=z^2$ gives diagonal lines for $u=0$ and coordinate axes for $v=0$, all meeting at the origin. Thus the conclusion is $\boxed{\text{right-angle intersection wherever }f'(z)\ne0}$, rather than an unrestricted claim at critical points.
