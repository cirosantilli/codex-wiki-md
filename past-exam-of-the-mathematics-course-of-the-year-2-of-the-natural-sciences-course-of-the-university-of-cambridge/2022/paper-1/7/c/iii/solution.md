<h1 id="7/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At every $z=n\pi$, the inner function has a [simple pole](../../../../../../simple-pole.md), with $\cot z=1/(z-n\pi)+h(z)$ for analytic $h$. Thus

$$
e^{\cot z}=e^{h(z)}\exp\left(\frac1{z-n\pi}\right).
$$

The first factor is analytic and nonzero, while the second has infinitely many negative powers in its [Laurent series](../../../../../../laurent-series.md). Therefore, by the [exponential of a pole is an essential singularity](../../../../../../exponential-of-a-pole-is-an-essential-singularity.md) result,

$$
\boxed{z=n\pi,\quad n\in\mathbb Z,\quad\text{all essential singularities}.}
$$

At all other finite points the composition is analytic, so there are no additional finite singularities.
