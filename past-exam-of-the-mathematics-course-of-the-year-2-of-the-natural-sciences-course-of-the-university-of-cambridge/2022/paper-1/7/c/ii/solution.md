<h1 id="7/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $z\ne0$, singularities occur when $1/z=n\pi$, with $n\ne0$. Each is a [simple pole](../../../../../../simple-pole.md), since the substitution has nonzero [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) there:

$$
\boxed{z_n=\frac1{n\pi},\qquad n\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}.}
$$

More explicitly, $1/z-n\pi\sim-(z-z_n)/z_n^2$, so $\cot(1/z)\sim-z_n^2/(z-z_n)$ and its [residue](../../../../../../residue.md) is $-1/(n^2\pi^2)$.

These [poles](../../../../../../pole.md) accumulate at zero. Consequently $\boxed{z=0\text{ is a non-isolated singularity}}$. The removable/pole/essential classification for [isolated singularities](../../../../../../isolated-singularity-split.md) cannot be applied at that accumulation point; it is not an isolated [essential singularity](../../../../../../essential-singularity.md).
