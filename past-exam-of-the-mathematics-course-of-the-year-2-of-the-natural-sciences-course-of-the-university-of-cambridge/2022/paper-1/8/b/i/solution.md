<h1 id="8/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Expanding the derivative and dividing by $1-x$ gives

$$
y''-\frac1{1-x}y'+\left(c^2+\frac\lambda{1-x}\right)y=0.
$$

The only finite singular point is $\boxed{x=1}$. There $(x-1)p(x)=1$ and $(x-1)^2q(x)=c^2(x-1)^2-\lambda(x-1)$ are analytic, so it is a [regular singular point](../../../../../../regular-singular-point.md). At infinity the coefficient of $y$ tends to $c^2\ne0$, giving an [irregular singular point](../../../../../../irregular-singular-point.md) after reciprocal substitution; the finite classification is independent of $\lambda$.
