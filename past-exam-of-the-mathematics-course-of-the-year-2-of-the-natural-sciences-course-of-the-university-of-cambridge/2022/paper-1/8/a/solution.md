<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A finite point $x_0$ is an [ordinary point](../../../../../ordinary-point-criterion-for-a-second-order-equation.md) when the normal-form coefficients $p,q$ are analytic at $x_0$. Then arbitrary values $y(x_0),y'(x_0)$ determine a convergent [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md), and two independent choices give two [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md) local analytic solutions.

A singular point is a [regular singular point](../../../../../regular-singular-point.md) when $(x-x_0)p(x)$ and $(x-x_0)^2q(x)$ extend analytically to $x_0$. The [Frobenius method](../../../../../frobenius-method.md) then provides at least one solution

$$
\boxed{y=(x-x_0)^\sigma\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n(x-x_0)^n,\qquad a_0\ne0,}
$$

with a locally convergent analytic factor and an exponent fixed by the [indicial equation](../../../../../indicial-equation.md). If its two [indicial roots](../../../../../indicial-root.md) differ by a noninteger, there are two independent [Frobenius solutions](../../../../../frobenius-solution.md). For integer-separated roots a second solution may include a logarithm; a repeated root gives a [Logarithmic Frobenius solution](../../../../../logarithmic-solution-from-a-repeated-frobenius-exponent.md) accompanying the single pure series. Such solutions need not be finite or ordinary [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) at the singular point.
