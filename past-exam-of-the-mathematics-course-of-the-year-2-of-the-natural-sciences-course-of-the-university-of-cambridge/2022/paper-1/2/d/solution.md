<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For [separation of variables](../../../../../separation-of-variables.md) matching the boundary [Fourier mode](../../../../../fourier-mode.md), take $\psi_0=X(x)e^{iky}$ with $X(0)=1$. Because $\partial_y^3e^{iky}=-ik^3e^{iky}$, the reduced [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) is

$$
2X'+k^3X=0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\psi_0(x,y)=e^{-k^3x/2}e^{iky}.}
$$

The [decay of a Fourier mode in a third-order spatial evolution equation](../../../../../decay-of-a-fourier-mode-in-a-third-order-spatial-evolution-equation.md) requires a sign check: this satisfies the requested limit at positive infinity precisely when $\boxed{k>0}$. For $k=0$ it remains identically one, while for $k<0$ its amplitude grows exponentially. Hence for those nonpositive values there is no separated solution meeting both prescribed [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md); the printed assumption that $k$ is merely real needs this additional restriction for decay.
