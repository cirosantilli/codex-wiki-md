<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A square [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) is a [diagonalizable matrix](../../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md) over the chosen field when there are an [invertible matrix](../../../../../invertible-matrix.md) $P$ and a [diagonal matrix](../../../../../diagonal-matrix.md) $D$ such that $A=PDP^{-1}$. If $v_j$ is column $j$ of $P$ and $D_{jj}=\lambda_j$, the identity $AP=PD$ gives $Av_j=\lambda_jv_j$. Invertibility makes these $n$ columns [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md) [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md).

Conversely, put $n$ [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md) [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) in the columns of $P$ and their [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) in $D$. Then $P$ is invertible and $AP=PD$, giving

$$
\boxed{A\text{ is diagonalizable}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ A\text{ has an eigenbasis of }n\text{ vectors}.}
$$

Repeated [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are allowed; the requirement is a complete [eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenbasis.md), not distinctness of the eigenvalues.
