<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Expand the [quadratic form](../../../../../quadratic-form-split.md) of this [Gram matrix](../../../../../gram-matrix.md):

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(\mathbf c)&=\sum_{i,j}c_ic_j\mathbf x_i^T\mathbf x_j\\
&=\left(\sum_i c_i\mathbf x_i\right)^T\left(\sum_jc_j\mathbf x_j\right)
=\left\|\sum_i c_i\mathbf x_i\right\|^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

For $\mathbf c\ne0$, [linear independence](../../../../../linear-independence.md) of the real vectors implies $\sum_i c_i\mathbf x_i\ne0$, so its squared [Euclidean norm](../../../../../euclidean-norm.md) is strictly positive. Thus

$$
\boxed{Q(\mathbf c)>0\text{ for every }\mathbf c\ne0,}
$$

proving the [positive definiteness of a Gram matrix](../../../../../positive-definiteness-of-a-gram-matrix.md) in the independent-vector case.
