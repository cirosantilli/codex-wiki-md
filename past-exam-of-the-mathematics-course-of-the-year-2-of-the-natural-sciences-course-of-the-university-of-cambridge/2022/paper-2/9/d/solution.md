<h1 id="9/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) is generated by one element: $G=\langle g\rangle=\{g^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}$. The hypothesis needs the qualification **no proper nontrivial [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md)**: every nontrivial [group](../../../../../group-split.md) has the proper [trivial subgroup](../../../../../trivial-subgroup.md) $\{e\}$, so the literal wording admits no examples.

Under that intended hypothesis, choose $g\ne e$. The [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $\langle g\rangle$ is nontrivial and must therefore be all of $G$, proving cyclicity. If $g$ had infinite order, then $\langle g^2\rangle$ would be nontrivial and proper, since $g$ is not an even power of an infinite-order generator. Thus $g$ has finite order $N>1$ and $|G|=N$.

If $N$ were composite, choose a divisor $d$ with $1<d<N$. Then $\langle g^d\rangle$ would have order $N/d$, strictly between one and $N$, again contradicting the hypothesis. Consequently

$$
\boxed{G\cong C_p\text{ for a prime }p.}
$$

Conversely, [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) shows that a [group](../../../../../group-split.md) of prime order has only itself and its trivial [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md). This proves the characterization of [groups with no proper nontrivial subgroups](../../../../../groups-with-no-proper-nontrivial-subgroups.md), even without assuming finiteness at the outset.
