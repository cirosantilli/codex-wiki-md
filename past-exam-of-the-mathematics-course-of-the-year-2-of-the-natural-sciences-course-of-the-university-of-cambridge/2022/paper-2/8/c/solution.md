<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Each reflection has order two, giving exactly the three order-two [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md)

$$
\boxed{\langle s\rangle=\{e,s\},\quad\langle rs\rangle=\{e,rs\},\quad\langle r^2s\rangle=\{e,r^2s\}.}
$$

[Conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md) by $r$ sends $r^js$ to $r^{j+2}s$, cycling the three reflections. None of these [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) is therefore normal.

The two nonidentity rotations generate the same order-three [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md):

$$
\boxed{R=\langle r\rangle=\{e,r,r^2\}\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}D_3.}
$$

It is invariant under [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md) by $r$, and $srs=r^{-1}$ shows invariance under $s$ as well; since $r,s$ generate the [group](../../../../../group-split.md), this proves normality. The complete partition into [conjugacy classes](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) is

$$
\boxed{\{e\},\qquad\{r,r^2\},\qquad\{s,rs,r^2s\}.}
$$

The rotations are mutually conjugate by $s$, and the reflections by $r$; no rotation is conjugate to a reflection because [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md) preserves the [order of a group element](../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md).
