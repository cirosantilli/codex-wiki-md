<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $y\ne0$ be an [eigenfunction](../../../../../eigenfunction.md), allowing complex values for the reality argument. The [self-adjoint differential operator](../../../../../self-adjoint-differential-operator.md) identity and [integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) give

$$
\lambda\int_1^R x^2|y|^2dx=\int_1^R\left[x(x-1)|y'|^2+l(l+1)|y|^2\right]dx.
$$

The boundary term $[x(x-1)\overline y y']_1^R$ vanishes for the same reasons as in part ii. The denominator is real and positive; the numerator is real and strictly positive because $l\geq1$ and $y\ne0$. Hence the [Rayleigh quotient](../../../../../rayleigh-quotient.md) is

$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{\displaystyle\int_1^R[x(x-1)|y'|^2+l(l+1)|y|^2]dx}{\displaystyle\int_1^Rx^2|y|^2dx}\in\mathbb R,\qquad\lambda>0.}
$$

The positivity uses a nondegenerate interval $R>1$.
