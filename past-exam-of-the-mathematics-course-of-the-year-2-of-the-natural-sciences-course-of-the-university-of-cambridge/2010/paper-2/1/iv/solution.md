<h1 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For real [eigenfunctions](../../../../../eigenfunction.md) $y_i,y_j$, multiply their [Sturm-Liouville problems](../../../../../sturm-liouville-problem.md) crosswise and subtract. Integration gives

$$
(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)\int_1^R x^2y_iy_j\,dx
=\left[x(x-1)(y_iy_j'-y_i'y_j)\right]_1^R.
$$

At $R$, both [eigenfunctions](../../../../../eigenfunction.md) vanish, while at $1$ their bounded values and bounded first [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) multiply a factor tending to zero. The right side is zero. Distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) therefore give the [orthogonality of Sturm-Liouville eigenfunctions](../../../../../orthogonality-of-sturm-liouville-eigenfunctions.md)

$$
\boxed{\int_1^R x^2y_i(x)y_j(x)\,dx=0\qquad(\lambda_i\ne\lambda_j).}
$$

For complex [eigenfunctions](../../../../../eigenfunction.md), replace $y_i$ by $\overline{y_i}$, in accordance with the [weighted inner product](../../../../../weighted-inner-product.md).
