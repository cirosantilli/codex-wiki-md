<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) of a square [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) is $\operatorname{tr}A=\sum_iA_{ii}$. Expanding a product and interchanging its finite sums gives

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}B_{ji}=\sum_{j,i}B_{ji}A_{ij}=\operatorname{tr}(BA).}
$$

Hence every [commutator](../../../../../commutator.md) has trace zero. Taking the trace of $AB-BA=cI$ gives $0=cn$. Over the complex numbers, with $n\geq1$, this forces

$$
\boxed{c=0.}
$$

For that value, choose $A=B=0$, or any commuting pair. For every nonzero $c$ the trace contradiction proves nonexistence. The finite [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) size is essential to this trace argument.
