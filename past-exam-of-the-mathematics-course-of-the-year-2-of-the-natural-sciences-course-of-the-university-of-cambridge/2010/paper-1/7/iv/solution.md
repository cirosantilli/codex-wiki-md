<h1 id="7/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For a [harmonic function](../../../../../harmonic-function.md), put $a=u_{xx}$ and $b=u_{xy}=u_{yx}$. Since $u_{yy}=-u_{xx}$, its [Hessian matrix](../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) is

$$
D^2u=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&-a\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det D^2u=-a^2-b^2.
$$

A nonzero determinant is therefore negative, and the two real Hessian [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are $\pm\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$. At a [stationary point](../../../../../stationary-point.md) the quadratic term of the [Taylor expansion](../../../../../taylor-expansion.md) has both signs. Consequently **every such nondegenerate [stationary point](../../../../../stationary-point.md) is a [saddle point](../../../../../saddle-point.md)**. This is the [nondegenerate stationary points of a planar harmonic function](../../../../../nondegenerate-stationary-points-of-a-planar-harmonic-function.md) criterion. The same argument applies to $v$, whose [stationary points](../../../../../stationary-point.md) coincide with those of $u$. A zero Hessian determinant is outside this second-order classification.
