<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

On the boundary, integrate the expansion from part (a) over one angular period. Every nonconstant [sine](../../../../../sine.md) and [cosine](../../../../../cosine.md) has zero average, so

$$
\int_0^{2\pi}f(\phi)\,d\phi=2\pi A_0.
$$

All positive-power radial terms vanish at $r=0$, leaving $\Psi(0)=A_0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\Psi(0)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(\phi)\,d\phi
=\frac1{2\pi R}\int_{r=R}f\,ds}.
$$

This is the [mean value property for harmonic functions](../../../../../mean-value-property-for-harmonic-functions.md). It is an average over arclength as well as over angle, since $ds=R\,d\phi$. The usual continuity or integrable-boundary-data hypotheses justify taking this angular average.
