<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A sufficient assumption is that $f$ is absolutely continuous, $f,f'\in L^1(\mathbb R)$, and $f(t)\to0$ at both infinities. [Integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) in the [Fourier transform](../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) gives

$$
\widetilde{f'}(\omega)
=\left[f(t)e^{-i\omega t}\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty}
+i\omega\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-i\omega t}\,dt
=\boxed{i\omega\widetilde f(\omega)}.
$$

The decay assumption removes the boundary term, and integrability makes both transforms ordinary convergent integrals. The same [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) identity extends to [tempered distributions](../../../../../tempered-distribution.md).
