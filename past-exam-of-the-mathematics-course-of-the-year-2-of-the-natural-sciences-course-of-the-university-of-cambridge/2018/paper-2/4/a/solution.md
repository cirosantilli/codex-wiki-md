<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A sufficient [contour](../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) setting for both results is a positively oriented, simple, closed, piecewise smooth [contour](../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) $C$, with the function [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on an open neighbourhood of $C$ and its entire interior. The [Cauchy integral theorem](../../../../../cauchy-s-integral-theorem.md) then states

$$
\boxed{\oint_C f(z)\,dz=0}.
$$

If $z_0$ is strictly inside $C$, the [Cauchy integral formula](../../../../../cauchy-integral-formula.md) states

$$
\boxed{f(z_0)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{f(z)}{z-z_0}\,dz}.
$$

The [contour](../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) must not pass through $z_0$. Positive orientation means anticlockwise; reversing it reverses the sign of the [contour](../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) integral. The holomorphy assumption throughout the interior excludes hidden singularities there.
