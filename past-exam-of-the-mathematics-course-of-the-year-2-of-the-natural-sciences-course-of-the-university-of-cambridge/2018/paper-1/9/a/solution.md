<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For fixed endpoint values and a smooth [variation](../../../../../variation.md) vanishing at the endpoints, the first variation is

$$
\delta T=\int_a^b(f_y\delta y+f_{y'}\delta y')\,dx
=\int_a^b\left(f_y-\frac d{dx}f_{y'}\right)\delta y\,dx.
$$

The [fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations](../../../../../fundamental-lemma-of-the-calculus-of-variations.md) gives the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{\frac d{dx}\frac{\partial f}{\partial y'}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}=0}.
$$

If $f$ is independent of $y$, then $y$ is a [cyclic coordinate](../../../../../cyclic-coordinate.md), and integration gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{\partial f}{\partial y'}=C}.
$$

This is conservation of the corresponding [generalized momentum](../../../../../generalized-momentum.md).
