<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put $w=z-4i$. Factor out the nonzero constant in the denominator and use the [geometric series](../../../../../geometric-series.md):

$$
\frac1{3-z}=\frac1{3-4i-w}
=\frac1{3-4i}\frac1{1-w/(3-4i)}
=\boxed{\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(z-4i)^n}{(3-4i)^{n+1}}}.
$$

This [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) converges when $|z-4i|<|3-4i|$. The [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md) at $z=3$ is the only finite obstruction to analyticity, whose distance from the expansion centre is five. Hence the [radius of convergence](../../../../../radius-of-convergence.md) is

$$
\boxed{R=5}.
$$
