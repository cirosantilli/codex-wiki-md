A square [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) $A$ is a [diagonalizable matrix](../../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md) over a specified field if some invertible [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) $P$ makes $P^{-1}AP$ diagonal. Equivalently, the underlying [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) has a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) of [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $A$.

For example,

$$
\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}\ \text{is diagonalizable},\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\ \text{is not diagonalizable}}.
$$

The first is already diagonal. The second has only the [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) one, of algebraic multiplicity two, but its [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) consists of $(x,0)^T$ and has dimension one. It cannot supply a two-vector [eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenbasis.md).
