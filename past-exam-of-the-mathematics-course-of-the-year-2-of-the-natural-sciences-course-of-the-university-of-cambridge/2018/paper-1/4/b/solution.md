<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a real nonzero $b$, put $s=bt$ in the [Fourier transform](../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md). Reversing the integration limits when $b<0$ produces the absolute value:

$$
\boxed{\mathcal F[f(b\,\cdot)](\omega)=\frac1{|b|}\widetilde f\left(\frac\omega b\right)}.
$$

This is the [scaling property of the Fourier transform](../../../../../scaling-property-of-the-fourier-transform.md). If $b=0$ and $f(0)$ exists, the transformed function is constant in time; its transform is $2\pi f(0)\delta(\omega)$ in the sense of [tempered distributions](../../../../../tempered-distribution.md), rather than an ordinary convergent Fourier integral.
