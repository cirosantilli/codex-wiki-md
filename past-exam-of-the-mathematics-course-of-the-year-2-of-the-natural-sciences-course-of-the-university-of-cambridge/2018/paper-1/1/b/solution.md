<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take any constant [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $\mathbf a$. Its [curl](../../../../../curl.md) is zero, so part (a) and the [divergence theorem](../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) give

$$
\mathbf a\cdot\int_V\nabla\times\mathbf F\,dV
=-\int_V\nabla\cdot(\mathbf a\times\mathbf F)\,dV
=-\int_S(\mathbf a\times\mathbf F)\cdot\mathbf n\,dS,
$$

where $\mathbf n$ is the outward unit [normal vector](../../../../../normal-vector.md). The [scalar triple product](../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md) identity rewrites the last integrand as $\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf F\times\mathbf n)$. Since $\mathbf a$ is arbitrary, equality of these [scalar products](../../../../../dot-product.md) implies the [vector](../../../../../vector.md) identity

$$
\boxed{\int_V\nabla\times\mathbf F\,dV
=-\int_S\mathbf F\times\mathbf n\,dS
=\int_S\mathbf n\times\mathbf F\,dS}.
$$
