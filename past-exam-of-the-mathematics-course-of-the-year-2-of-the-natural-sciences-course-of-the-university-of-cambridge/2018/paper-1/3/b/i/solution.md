<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $x>0$, differentiation of $u=y/x$ gives

$$
u'=\frac{y'}x-\frac y{x^2},\qquad
u''=\frac{y''}x-\frac{2y'}{x^2}+\frac{2y}{x^3},\qquad
u''+\frac2xu'-u=\frac{y''-y}{x}.
$$

Thus the [radial Laplacian](../../../../../../radial-laplacian.md) equation reduces to

$$
\boxed{y''-y=xg(x),\qquad f(x)=xg(x)}.
$$

Boundedness of $u$ at zero implies $y(0)=0$. Conversely, a regular reduced solution with $y(0)=0$ gives finite $u(0)=y'(0)$.

There is an important qualification to the reduction in the printed question: bounded $u$ at infinity requires $y=O(x)$, not bounded $y$. The example in part (ii) actually has $y\sim-x$, so it cannot be the bounded solution described in part (a). The differential equation and origin condition reduce as asserted, and the same [Green's function](../../../../../../green-s-function-split.md) can still be used with the correct growth condition. This is the [radial reduction and growth at infinity](../../../../../../radial-reduction-and-growth-at-infinity.md) distinction.
