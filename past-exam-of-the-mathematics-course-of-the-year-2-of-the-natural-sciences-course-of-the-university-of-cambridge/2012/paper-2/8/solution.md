<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

The [group order](../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) $|G|$ is the number of elements of the finite group. A [normal subgroup](../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $H\leq G$ satisfies $gHg^{-1}=H$ for every $g\in G$, equivalently its left and right cosets agree.

For the [square](../../../../square.md), let $R$ be counterclockwise rotation by $\pi/2$, and $s$ reflection in the horizontal axis. The [dihedral group](../../../../dihedral-group.md) is

$$
D_4=\{I,R,R^2,R^3,s,Rs,R^2s,R^3s\},\qquad R^4=s^2=I,\quad sRs=R^{-1}.
$$

The first four elements rotate by $0,90,180,270$ degrees. The other four are [Euclidean reflections](../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) in the horizontal axis, the line $y=x$, the vertical axis and the line $y=-x$, respectively, for a square centred at the origin with sides parallel to the axes.

The eight **nontrivial proper subgroups** are

$$
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
\{I,R^2\},\qquad\{I,R^js\}\quad(j=0,1,2,3),\\
\langle R\rangle=\{I,R,R^2,R^3\},\\
\{I,R^2,s,R^2s\},\qquad\{I,R^2,Rs,R^3s\}.
\end{gathered}}
$$

To check completeness, [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) allows proper orders one, two and four. The order-two subgroups are generated by the five involutions displayed. An order-four subgroup containing $R$ or $R^3$ is the rotation subgroup. Otherwise it must contain reflections; their product is a rotation, which must be $R^2$, so its reflections differ by that half-turn, giving the two listed Klein subgroups. The trivial subgroup $\{I\}$ is an additional proper subgroup. Thus the PDF's count of eight uses “proper” to exclude the trivial subgroup; with the usual inclusive definition there are nine proper subgroups.

A [normal subgroup of order two is central](../../../../normal-subgroup-of-order-two-is-central.md): conjugation fixes its identity and must also fix its only other member. The half-turn $R^2$ commutes with every square symmetry, whereas the reflection subgroups are exchanged by conjugation. Hence the unique order-two normal subgroup is

$$
\boxed{\{I,R^2\}.}
$$

For the general polygon, write $D_n=\{R^j,R^js:0\leq j<n\}$ with $R^n=s^2=I$ and $sRs=R^{-1}$. A rotation $R^j$ commutes with $s$ exactly when $R^j=R^{-j}$, or $2j\equiv0\pmod n$. A reflection cannot commute with $R$ when $n\geq3$, because

$$
R(R^js)R^{-1}=R^{j+2}s\ne R^js.
$$

Therefore the [center of a group](../../../../center-of-a-group.md) here is $\{I\}$ for odd $n$, and $\{I,R^{n/2}\}$ for even $n$. The order-two normal subgroups correspond exactly to nonidentity central involutions, proving

$$
\boxed{\begin{cases}\text{no order-two normal subgroup},&n\text{ odd},\\\{I,R^{n/2}\}\text{ is the unique one},&n\text{ even}.
\end{cases}}
$$

This agrees with the [involutions in an even dihedral group](../../../../involutions-in-an-even-dihedral-group.md) and uses the convention $|D_n|=2n$ throughout.
