<h1 id="10/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [conjugacy class](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) of $g\in G$ is $\{hgh^{-1}:h\in G\}$. An [irreducible representation](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) acts on a nonzero vector space with no nonzero proper subspace invariant under every group element.

In the [quaternion group](../../../../../quaternion-group.md), $1$ and $-1$ commute with all elements, so each forms its own conjugacy class. The element $i$ commutes with $\pm1,\pm i$, while conjugation by $j$ or $k$ changes it to $-i$. Thus its class is $\{i,-i\}$. The same argument for $j,k$ gives all five classes:

$$
\boxed{\{1\},\qquad\{-1\},\qquad\{i,-i\},\qquad\{j,-j\},\qquad\{k,-k\}.}
$$

Their sizes sum to eight. Equivalently each noncentral element has a four-element centralizer and hence a two-element class.

Use the following finite-group representation theorems over $\mathbb C$: the number of inequivalent irreducible representations equals the number of conjugacy classes, and their dimensions obey the [sum of squares of irreducible representation dimensions](../../../../../sum-of-squares-of-irreducible-representation-dimensions.md), $\sum d_j^2=|G|$. Thus there are five irreducibles, with $\sum_{j=1}^5d_j^2=8$. Starting with five dimensions equal to one uses five of those eight units; increasing one dimension from one to two uses exactly the remaining three, while any larger increase would exceed eight. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{five complex irreducible representations, with dimensions }1,1,1,1,2.}
$$

These are the dimensions underlying the [character table of the quaternion group](../../../../../character-table-of-the-quaternion-group.md). The scalar field matters: the quaternionic irreducible becomes four-dimensional over the reals. The class-count and dimension-sum statements used here have their standard complex meaning.
