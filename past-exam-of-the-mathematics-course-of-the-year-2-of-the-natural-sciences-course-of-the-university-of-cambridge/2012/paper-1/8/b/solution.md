<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a nonnegative integer $\ell$, the chain of the same parity as $\ell$ reaches $n=\ell$, where the numerator of the [recurrence relation](../../../../../recurrence-relation.md) vanishes. The next coefficient and every later coefficient in that chain are zero, producing a polynomial of degree $\ell$. Normalizing it at $x=1$ gives the [Legendre polynomial](../../../../../legendre-polynomial.md) $P_\ell$.

The opposite-parity chain never reaches $n=\ell$. Its other possible zero, $n=-\ell-1$, is negative and cannot occur, so a nonzero initial coefficient produces a nonterminating [power series](../../../../../power-series.md). It is linearly independent of $P_\ell$ and can be scaled to the [Legendre function of the second kind](../../../../../legendre-function-of-the-second-kind.md) $Q_\ell$.

The degree statement requires **$\ell\geq0$**, as the examples in part (c) indicate. For a negative integer $\ell$, the same equation has the nonnegative degree $-\ell-1$, because $\ell(\ell+1)=(-\ell-1)(-\ell)$. A polynomial cannot literally have negative degree.
