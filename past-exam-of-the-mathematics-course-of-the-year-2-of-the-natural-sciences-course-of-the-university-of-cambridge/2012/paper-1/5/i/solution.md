<h1 id="5/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ be the [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md) [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md), and form $V=(v_1\ \cdots\ v_n)$. This [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) is invertible and $AV=VD$, where $D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)$. Hence

$$
\boxed{V^{-1}AV=D,}
$$

which proves [diagonalizability](../../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md).

If $B=P^{-1}AP$, then $P^{-1}v_j$ are linearly independent [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $B$ with the same [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md). Explicitly, with $W=P^{-1}V$,

$$
\boxed{W^{-1}BW=V^{-1}P(P^{-1}AP)P^{-1}V=D.}
$$

Thus [matrix similarity](../../../../../matrix-similarity.md) preserves diagonalizability.
