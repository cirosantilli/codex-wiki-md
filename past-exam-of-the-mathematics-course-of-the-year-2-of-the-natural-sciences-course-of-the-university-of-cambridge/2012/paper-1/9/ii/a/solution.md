<h1 id="9/ii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The integrand $f=n(x)\sqrt{1+(y')^2}$ has no explicit dependence on $y$, so part (i) gives

$$
\frac{n(x)y'}{\sqrt{1+(y')^2}}=c.
$$

For positive [refractive index](../../../../../../refractive-index.md) and a regular graph with $n(x)>|c|$, squaring and then restoring the sign of $y'$ yields

$$
\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{c}{\sqrt{n(x)^2-c^2}}.}
$$

It is the continuous-medium version of [Snell's law](../../../../../../snell-s-law.md), expressing conserved horizontal optical momentum. The [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) establishes stationarity; on a monotone-height segment, convexity of the integrand in $y'$ also gives a minimum among graphs with fixed endpoints.
