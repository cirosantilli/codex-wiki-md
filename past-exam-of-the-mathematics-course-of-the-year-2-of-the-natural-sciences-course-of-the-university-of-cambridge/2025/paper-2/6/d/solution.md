<h1 id="6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let $J_{ij}=\int_B x_ix_j\,d^3x$. Rotational symmetry makes $J$ an [isotropic second-rank tensor](../../../../../isotropic-second-rank-tensor.md), so $J_{ij}=C\delta_{ij}$. Contracting the indices and integrating in [spherical coordinates](../../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) gives

$$
3C=\int_Br^2\,d^3x=4\pi\int_0^1r^4dr=\frac{4\pi}5.
$$

The [isotropic second moment of a ball](../../../../../isotropic-second-moment-of-a-ball.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{I=M_{ij}J_{ij}=\frac{4\pi}{15}\operatorname{tr}M.}
$$

Only the scalar part of $M$ contributes; its [antisymmetric tensor](../../../../../antisymmetric-tensor.md) and symmetric-traceless parts give zero.
