<h1 id="9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Choose $g\ne e$. By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), its cyclic [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) has order dividing the prime 101. It is not of order one, so $\langle g\rangle=G$. Thus **the group is cyclic, and hence abelian**. In particular, every pair of elements is $g^i,g^j$, and $g^ig^j=g^{i+j}=g^{j+i}=g^jg^i$.
