<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For an [axisymmetric harmonic function](../../../../../axisymmetric-harmonic-function.md), put $\Psi=R(r)\Theta(\theta)$ in the [Laplacian in spherical coordinates](../../../../../laplacian-in-spherical-coordinates.md) and multiply by $r^2/(R\Theta)$. [Separation of variables](../../../../../separation-of-variables.md) gives

$$
\frac{(r^2R')'}R=-\frac{(\sin\theta\,\Theta')'}{\sin\theta\,\Theta}=\ell(\ell+1).
$$

With $u=\cos\theta$, the angular equation becomes the [Legendre differential equation](../../../../../legendre-differential-equation.md). Regularity at both poles selects the [Legendre polynomials](../../../../../legendre-polynomial.md) $P_\ell(u)$ with integer $\ell\ge0$; the singular second solutions are excluded. The radial [Euler-Cauchy equation](../../../../../euler-cauchy-equation.md) has solutions $r^\ell$ and $r^{-\ell-1}$. [Superposition](../../../../../superposition-principle.md) therefore gives

$$
\Psi=\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty(A_\ell r^\ell+B_\ell r^{-\ell-1})P_\ell(\cos\theta),
$$

with coefficients chosen to ensure convergence and the required boundary regularity. The [axisymmetric harmonic extension from a sphere](../../../../../axisymmetric-harmonic-extension-from-a-sphere.md) gives these coefficients directly from boundary data.
