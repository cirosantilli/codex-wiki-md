<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the first [Cayley table](../../../../../cayley-table.md), [associativity](../../../../../associative-property.md) fails: $(a\ast a)\ast b=1\ast b=b$, whereas $a\ast(a\ast b)=a\ast c=d$. It therefore **does not define a group**.

For the second table, assign the residues $0,1,2,3,4$ to $1,a,c,b,d$, respectively. Every table entry agrees with [addition modulo an integer](../../../../../addition-modulo-an-integer.md) modulo five. Thus this table **defines the cyclic group of order five**, with identity $1$ and inverses $a^{-1}=d$, $c^{-1}=b$. The identification with a known [group](../../../../../group-split.md) verifies [associativity](../../../../../associative-property.md) as well as closure and the inverse axioms; inspecting only the identity row and inverses would not suffice.
