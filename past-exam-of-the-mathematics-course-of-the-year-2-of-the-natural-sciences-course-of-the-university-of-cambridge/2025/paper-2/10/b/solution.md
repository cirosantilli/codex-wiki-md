<h1 id="10/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**The requested two-dimensional irreducible representation does not exist for the Vierergruppe.** The [Klein four-group](../../../../../klein-four-group.md) $V_4$ is an [abelian group](../../../../../abelian-group.md) in which every nonidentity element has order two. Its complex [irreducible representations](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) are all one-dimensional. The same holds over the real numbers here: the commuting representing involutions are simultaneously diagonalizable with real eigenvalues $\pm1$, so every two-dimensional representation has an invariant line. Each genuine irreducible character satisfies $\chi(g^2)=\chi(1)=1$, and hence has [Frobenius-Schur indicator](../../../../../frobenius-schur-indicator.md) $\nu=1$.

The printed notation $C_4$ suggests a different possible interpretation, which must not be conflated with the [Klein four-group](../../../../../klein-four-group.md). For the [cyclic group of order four](../../../../../cyclic-group-of-order-four.md), a real two-dimensional [irreducible representation](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) is generated by

$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its character on $1,J,-I,-J$ is $(2,0,-2,0)$, giving $\nu=(2-2+2-2)/4=0$. Over the complex numbers this representation splits into two conjugate one-dimensional characters, so it is not a two-dimensional complex irreducible representation. These alternatives explain what can be computed without silently changing the printed group or the ground field.
