<h1 id="10/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The printed wording calls $\Sigma_3$ cyclic, but its meaning in question 9 is the [symmetric group](../../../../../symmetric-group.md) $S_3$. Under that interpretation, take its planar [dihedral group](../../../../../dihedral-group.md) representation, with

$$
D(r)=\begin{pmatrix}-1/2&-\sqrt3/2\\\sqrt3/2&-1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D(s)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Here $r^3=s^2=1$ and $srs=r^{-1}$. This [representation](../../../../../group-representation.md) is [irreducible](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) over the complex numbers: the two distinct rotation [eigenspaces](../../../../../eigenspace.md) are exchanged by the reflection, so neither is invariant under the whole group. Its [character](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) is $2$ on the identity, $-1$ on the two 3-cycles and $0$ on the three transpositions. Squaring maps each transposition to the identity and permutes the 3-cycles. Its [Frobenius-Schur indicator](../../../../../frobenius-schur-indicator.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\nu=\frac{2+3(2)+2(-1)}6=1.}
$$

If “cyclic” were intended literally, no two-dimensional complex [irreducible representation](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) would exist, since an [irreducible representation of a finite abelian group](../../../../../irreducible-representation-of-a-finite-abelian-group.md) is one-dimensional.
