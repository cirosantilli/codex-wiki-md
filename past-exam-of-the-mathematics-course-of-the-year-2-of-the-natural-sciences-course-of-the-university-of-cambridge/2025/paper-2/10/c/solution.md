<h1 id="10/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the [symmetric group](../../../../../symmetric-group.md) representation in part (a), the identity and three transpositions contribute $4I$ to $A=\sum_gD(g^2)$. The two 3-cycles contribute $D(r)+D(r^2)=-I$. Thus

$$
\boxed{A=3I=\frac{6\cdot1}{2}I.}
$$

More generally, the [central average of representation matrices at squares](../../../../../central-average-of-representation-matrices-at-squares.md) commutes with the representation, because conjugation permutes the summands. For a complex [irreducible representation](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md), [Schur's lemma](../../../../../schur-s-lemma.md) therefore gives $A=cI$. Taking the [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) yields $c\dim D=|G|\nu$, proving the claimed formula under its correct hypotheses.

For the [Klein four-group](../../../../../klein-four-group.md) there is no two-dimensional irreducible representation, as established in part (b). Its genuine one-dimensional irreducibles have $A=4$ and $\nu=1$, so $A=(4\cdot1/1)I$.

If the intended alternative is the real quarter-turn representation of the [cyclic group of order four](../../../../../cyclic-group-of-order-four.md), its squared matrices sum to $I-I+I-I=0$. Thus $A=0=(4\cdot0/2)I$ in that example. This equality is a direct calculation; the complex version of [Schur's lemma](../../../../../schur-s-lemma.md) cannot be applied to its reducible complexification.
