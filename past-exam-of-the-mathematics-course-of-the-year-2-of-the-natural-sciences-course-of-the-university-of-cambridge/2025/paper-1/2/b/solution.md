<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The fastest temporal growth occurs for the lowest spatial [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md), with $p=q=r=1$. Its growth rate is

$$
s_{111}=\gamma-\frac{3\mu\pi^2}{L^2}.
$$

For a generic initial condition its coefficient is nonzero, so this term determines the long-time behavior. In fact, every nonzero, nonnegative initial density has $a_{111}>0$, since the product of the three first sine [eigenfunctions](../../../../../eigenfunction.md) is strictly positive in the interior. Consequently

$$
\boxed{L_{\mathrm{crit}}=\pi\sqrt{\frac{3\mu}{\gamma}},\qquad \text{exponential growth precisely when }L>L_{\mathrm{crit}}.}
$$

For $L<L_{\mathrm{crit}}$ every term decays. At equality the first term is stationary and all higher terms decay: equality does not produce exponential growth. This is a [reaction-diffusion equation](../../../../../reaction-diffusion-system.md) threshold arising from competition between production and loss through the [Dirichlet boundary condition](../../../../../dirichlet-boundary-condition.md).
