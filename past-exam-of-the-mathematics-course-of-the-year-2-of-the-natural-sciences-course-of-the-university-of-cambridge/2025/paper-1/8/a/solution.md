<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Away from $y=\pm1$, divide by the coefficient of $x''$ to obtain the normal form

$$
x''+P(y)x'+Q(y)x=0,\qquad P(y)=Q(y)=\frac1{y^2-1}.
$$

Thus $\boxed{y=1\text{ and }y=-1}$ are the only finite singular points. For a second-order [linear differential equation](../../../../../linear-differential-equation.md), $y_0$ is a [regular singular point](../../../../../regular-singular-point.md) if $(y-y_0)P(y)$ and $(y-y_0)^2Q(y)$ extend analytically to $y_0$. At $y_0=1$ these functions are

$$
\frac1{y+1},\qquad \frac{y-1}{y+1},
$$

and at $y_0=-1$ they are

$$
\frac1{y-1},\qquad \frac{y+1}{y-1}.
$$

Each pair is analytic at its respective point. Therefore $\boxed{\text{both finite singular points are regular}}$.
