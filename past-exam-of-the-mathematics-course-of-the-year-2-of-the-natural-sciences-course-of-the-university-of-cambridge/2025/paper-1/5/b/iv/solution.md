<h1 id="5/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Put $D=A-iB$, so $D^\dagger=A+iB$. Because the [Hermitian matrices](../../../../../../hermitian-operator.md) $A,B$ [commute](../../../../../../commuting-elements.md),

$$
D^\dagger D=DD^\dagger=A^2+B^2.
$$

For every nonzero [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) $v$,

$$
v^\dagger(A^2+B^2)v=\|Av\|^2+\|Bv\|^2>0,
$$

since $A$ is an [invertible matrix](../../../../../../invertible-matrix.md). Thus $D$ and $D^\dagger$ are [invertible matrices](../../../../../../invertible-matrix.md), justifying the inverse in the definition of $U$. The [conjugate transpose](../../../../../../conjugate-transpose.md) of $U=D^\dagger D^{-1}$ is $(D^\dagger)^{-1}D$. Since $D$ and $D^\dagger$ [commute](../../../../../../commuting-elements.md), their inverses do too, and

$$
\boxed{U^\dagger U=(D^\dagger)^{-1}DD^\dagger D^{-1}=I.}
$$

Hence $U$ is a [unitary matrix](../../../../../../unitary-matrix.md).
