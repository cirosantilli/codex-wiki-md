<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The left [coset](../../../../../coset.md) is

$$
\boxed{gH=\{gh:h\in H\}.}
$$

Every $g\in G$ belongs to $gH$, because $e\in H$, so the cosets cover $G$. If $gH$ and $kH$ intersect, choose $gh_1=kh_2$ in the intersection. Then $k^{-1}g=h_2h_1^{-1}\in H$, so $g=kh$ for some $h\in H$. Thus $gH=khH=kH$.

Hence two left cosets are either identical or disjoint, and the distinct left cosets form a [partition of a set](../../../../../partition-of-a-set.md) whose union is $G$. Finiteness is unnecessary for this partition argument. In the finite case each coset has $|H|$ members, since multiplication by $g$ is a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md), giving [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md).
