<h1 id="4/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Close the interval $[-R,R]$ with an upper [semicircle](../../../../../../semicircle.md), traversed counterclockwise. On its large arc,

$$
\left|\frac{z^2}{1+z^4}\right|\leq\frac{R^2}{R^4-1},
$$

so the [estimation lemma](../../../../../../estimation-lemma.md) bounds the arc integral by $\pi R^3/(R^4-1)\to0$. The enclosed [residues](../../../../../../residue.md) are those at $(1+i)/\sqrt2$ and $(-1+i)/\sqrt2$, whose sum is $-i/(2\sqrt2)$. The [residue theorem](../../../../../../residue-theorem.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x^2}{1+x^4}\,dx=2\pi i\left(-\frac{i}{2\sqrt2}\right)=\frac\pi{\sqrt2}.}
$$

The positive sign agrees with the nonnegative real integrand.
