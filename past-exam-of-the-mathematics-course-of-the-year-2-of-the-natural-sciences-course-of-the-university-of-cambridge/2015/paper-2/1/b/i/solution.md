<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

To obtain [Sturm-Liouville form](../../../../../../sturm-liouville-form.md), multiply the equation by $(1-x^2)^{-1/2}$. The derivative identity

$$
\frac d{dx}\big[\sqrt{1-x^2}\,y_n'\big]=\sqrt{1-x^2}\,y_n''-\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}y_n'
$$

gives

$$
\boxed{-\frac d{dx}\left[\sqrt{1-x^2}\,y_n'\right]=n^2\frac{y_n}{\sqrt{1-x^2}},\qquad p(x)=\sqrt{1-x^2},\quad q(x)=0,\quad w(x)=\frac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}}.}
$$

The weight determines the [weighted inner product](../../../../../../weighted-inner-product.md) on $(-1,1)$. It is integrable but singular at the endpoints, where $p$ also vanishes. Thus this is a singular-endpoint [Sturm-Liouville problem](../../../../../../sturm-liouville-problem.md), not a regular problem with strictly positive endpoint values of $p$. The transformation in part (ii) specifies its admissible Dirichlet realization; these are the [Dirichlet eigenfunctions of the Chebyshev differential operator](../../../../../../dirichlet-eigenfunctions-of-the-chebyshev-differential-operator.md).
