<h1 id="5/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The result of part (i) vanishes identically on the open interval $t<0$, so its first and second derivatives also vanish there. Substituting into the equation of motion immediately gives

$$
\boxed{f(t)=0\quad(t<0).}
$$

Thus the force has no negative-time prehistory, consistent with the [causal Green function of a damped oscillator](../../../../../../causal-green-function-of-a-damped-oscillator.md). In fact, direct inversion or the positive-time substitution above identifies $f(t)=-e^{-2t}H(t)$; the given numerator $+i$ corresponds to a negative exponential force with this Fourier convention. Here $H$ is the [Heaviside step function](../../../../../../heaviside-step-function.md); its value exactly at the jump does not affect the transform.
