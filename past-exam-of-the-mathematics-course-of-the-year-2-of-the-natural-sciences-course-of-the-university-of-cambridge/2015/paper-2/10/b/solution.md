<h1 id="10/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

All six reflections square to the identity, and the rotation $R^3$ is the half-turn. These exhaust the nonidentity involutions: $R^k$ has square one only when $2k\equiv0\pmod6$, giving $k=3$ apart from the identity. Thus the order-two [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) are

$$
\boxed{\{I,R^3\},\qquad\{I,m_j\}\quad(j=1,\ldots,6).}
$$

A reflection cannot have order three. The rotations of order three are $R^2$ and $R^4$, and they generate the same subgroup:

$$
\boxed{\{I,R^2,R^4\}=\langle R^2\rangle.}
$$

**All these subgroups are cyclic**, because a group of prime order is cyclic; the displayed nonidentity elements supply their generators. This gives seven subgroups of order two and one of order three, consistently with [involutions in an even dihedral group](../../../../../involutions-in-an-even-dihedral-group.md) and the [classification of subgroups of a dihedral group](../../../../../classification-of-subgroups-of-a-dihedral-group.md).
