<h1 id="7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Set $L_i=\widehat L_i+y_i$. The equilibrium equations cancel the constant gravitational and elastic terms, leaving the [small-oscillation mass and stiffness matrices](../../../../../small-oscillation-mass-and-stiffness-matrices.md)

$$
\boxed{M\ddot y+Ky=0,\qquad M=\begin{pmatrix}m_1+m_2+m_3&m_2&m_3\\m_2&m_2&0\\m_3&0&m_3\end{pmatrix},\qquad K=kI_3.}
$$

The off-diagonal mass terms arise because rope-length coordinates are not absolute climber displacements. For any nonzero velocity vector, $\dot y^TM\dot y=m_1\dot y_1^2+m_2(\dot y_1+\dot y_2)^2+m_3(\dot y_1+\dot y_3)^2>0$, so $M$ is a [positive-definite matrix](../../../../../positive-definite-matrix.md).

Within the vertical Hookean model, the displacement equations are exactly linear while all ropes stay taut. Small amplitudes ensure that none reaches the slack regime $L_i\leq L_0$.
