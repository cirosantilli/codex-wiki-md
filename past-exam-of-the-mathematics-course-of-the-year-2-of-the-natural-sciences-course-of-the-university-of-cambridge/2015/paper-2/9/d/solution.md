<h1 id="9/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The kernel is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), so it contains the identity class and is a union of the classes listed in part (c). Its candidate orders are therefore $1$, $1+3=4$, $1+2=3$ and $1+3+2=6$. [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) excludes four, since it does not divide six. The remaining possibilities are

$$
\boxed{K=\{e\},\qquad K=A_3=\{e,(123),(132)\},\qquad K=S_3.}
$$

All three are normal: the first and last trivially, while $A_3$ is the kernel of the map giving the [sign of a permutation](../../../../../sign-of-a-permutation.md), or the index-two subgroup generated by a 3-cycle.

Because $\Phi$ is onto, the [first isomorphism theorem](../../../../../first-isomorphism-theorem.md) gives $G'\cong S_3/K$. Explicitly, the map $gK\mapsto\Phi(g)$ is well defined, multiplicative and surjective; equality of its values means $h^{-1}g\in K$, so it is also injective. Thus

$$
\boxed{G'\cong S_3\quad\text{or}\quad C_2\quad\text{or}\quad\{e\}.}
$$

Each is realized by the identity map, the sign map and the trivial map, respectively. This completes the classification of [normal subgroups and quotients of the symmetric group on three letters](../../../../../normal-subgroups-and-quotients-of-the-symmetric-group-on-three-letters.md).
