<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Complex differentiability requires the same limit for increments along the real and imaginary directions. Real increments give $f'=u_x+iv_x$. Imaginary increments give

$$
f'=\frac{u_y+iv_y}{i}=v_y-iu_y.
$$

Equating real and imaginary parts yields the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md)

$$
\boxed{u_x=v_y,\qquad u_y=-v_x.}
$$

They hold throughout the open region where $f$ is [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md). Conversely, continuous first real [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) satisfying these equations on an open set ensure holomorphy there.
