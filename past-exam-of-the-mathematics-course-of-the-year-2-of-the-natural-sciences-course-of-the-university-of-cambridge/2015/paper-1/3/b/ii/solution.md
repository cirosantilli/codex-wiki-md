<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The PDF bounds the _absolute value_ of the forcing. Taking absolute values in the [Green function](../../../../../../green-s-function-split.md) [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md) and using $|\sin|\leq1$ yields

$$
|x(t)|\leq\frac C\mu\int_0^te^{-(t-s)}e^{-as}\,ds.
$$

For $a\neq1$, this gives

$$
\boxed{|x(t)|\leq\frac C\mu\frac{e^{-at}-e^{-t}}{1-a}\longrightarrow0.}
$$

The ratio is nonnegative for either $a<1$ or $a>1$. The missing special case is $a=1$, for which

$$
\boxed{|x(t)|\leq\frac C\mu te^{-t}\longrightarrow0.}
$$

This proves [decay of a damped oscillator under exponentially decaying forcing](../../../../../../decay-of-a-damped-oscillator-under-exponentially-decaying-forcing.md). A one-sided upper bound on $g$ without an absolute value, as in the transcription, would not justify the conclusion.
