<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a nonterminating parity series, consecutive nonzero terms satisfy

$$
\left|\frac{a_{j+2}x^{j+2}}{a_jx^j}\right|=\left|\frac{j^2-n^2}{(j+2)(j+1)}\right||x|^2\longrightarrow|x|^2.
$$

The [ratio test](../../../../../ratio-test.md) therefore gives $\boxed{R=1}$ for each nonterminating series. The nearest singular coefficient points are $x=\pm1$, both distance one from the expansion centre.

The polynomial exception is essential here. If $n$ is an integer, the series of the same parity as $|n|$ terminates and has $\boxed{R=\infty}$; the opposite-parity independent series remains nonterminating with radius one. For noninteger $n$, both have radius one. A [regular singular point](../../../../../regular-singular-point.md) can be removable for one particular solution, so its presence does not force every individual solution to have finite convergence radius.
