<h1 id="10/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Set $\psi(r)=u(r)/r$ for $r>0$. Direct differentiation gives $(r^2\psi')'=ru''$, so the radial equation reduces to

$$
u''+k^2u=0.
$$

Boundedness of $\psi$ requires $u(0)=0$, and the wall condition requires $u(a)=0$. The nontrivial solutions are consequently

$$
\boxed{k_j=\frac{j\pi}{a},\qquad\psi_j(r)=C_j\frac{\sin(j\pi r/a)}r,\qquad j=1,2,\ldots.}
$$

Their origin values are the removable limits $C_jj\pi/a$. At $k=0$, the only bounded radial solution is constant and the wall forces it to vanish, so there is no zero mode. The lowest [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is

$$
\boxed{k_0^2=\frac{\pi^2}{a^2}\simeq\frac{9.86960}{a^2}.}
$$

It is below $10.5/a^2$, as required by the [Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle](../../../../../../rayleigh-ritz-variational-principle.md). The trial value overestimates $k_0^2$ by approximately $\boxed{6.39\%}$.
