<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The quadratic-form [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) has [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md)

$$
e_+=\frac{(1,1,0)}{\sqrt2},\qquad e_-=\frac{(1,-1,0)}{\sqrt2},\qquad e_3=(0,0,1),
$$

with [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $1,9,9$. In the [principal axes](../../../../../principal-axis.md) coordinates $\xi=(x_1+x_2)/\sqrt2$, $\eta=(x_1-x_2)/\sqrt2$ and $\zeta=x_3$, the equation becomes

$$
\boxed{\frac{\xi^2}{9}+\eta^2+\zeta^2=1.}
$$

It is a [prolate spheroid](../../../../../prolate-spheroid.md), centred at the origin, with semiaxis **three along $e_+$ and one in every perpendicular direction**. The displayed $e_-,e_3$ are one choice of short [principal axes](../../../../../principal-axis.md).

The [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) has equation $\xi^2+\eta^2+\zeta^2=4$. Combining it with the ellipsoid equation gives

$$
\boxed{\xi^2=\frac{27}{8},\qquad\eta^2+\zeta^2=\frac58.}
$$

The [intersection of a prolate spheroid and a concentric sphere](../../../../../intersection-of-a-prolate-spheroid-and-a-concentric-sphere.md) therefore consists of two [circles](../../../../../circle.md) in planes perpendicular to $e_+$. Each has radius $\boxed{\sqrt{5/8}=\sqrt{10}/4}$, and their centres are

$$
\boxed{c_\pm=\pm\sqrt{\frac{27}{8}}\,e_+=\pm\left(\frac{3\sqrt3}{4},\frac{3\sqrt3}{4},0\right).}
$$

Their plane equations in the original coordinates are $x_1+x_2=\pm3\sqrt3/2$.
