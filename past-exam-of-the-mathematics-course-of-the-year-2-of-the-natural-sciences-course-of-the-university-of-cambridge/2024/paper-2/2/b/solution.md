<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Fourier orthogonality](../../../../../fourier-orthogonality.md) relation on this angular interval is

$$
\int_0^{2\pi\alpha}\sin\frac{n\theta}{2\alpha}\sin\frac{m\theta}{2\alpha}\,d\theta
=\pi\alpha\,\delta_{nm}.
$$

Projecting the outer [boundary condition](../../../../../boundary-condition.md) onto each angular [eigenfunction](../../../../../eigenfunction.md) gives

$$
\boxed{A_n=\frac{R^{-n/(2\alpha)}}{\pi\alpha}\int_0^{2\pi\alpha}\varphi(\theta)\sin\frac{n\theta}{2\alpha}\,d\theta.}
$$

The solution is **unique** in the class of bounded [harmonic functions](../../../../../harmonic-function.md) with the prescribed boundary trace. For continuous boundary data, the difference of two solutions is zero on the boundary and bounded at the vertex, so the [maximum principle for harmonic functions](../../../../../maximum-principle-for-harmonic-functions.md) makes it zero throughout the sector. Equivalently all its [Fourier sine series](../../../../../fourier-sine-series.md) coefficients vanish. The usual convergence interpretation applies if the data are only piecewise smooth.
