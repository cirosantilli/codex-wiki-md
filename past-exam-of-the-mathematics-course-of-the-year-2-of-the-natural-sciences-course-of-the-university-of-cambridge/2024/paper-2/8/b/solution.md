↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) states that for a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $H$ of a [finite group](../../../../../finite-group.md) $G$,

$$
\boxed{|G|=[G:H]\,|H|,\qquad |H|\mid |G|.}
$$

The left [cosets](../../../../../coset.md) of $H$ partition $G$, and multiplication by a coset representative is a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) from $H$ onto that coset, explaining the formula. If $|G|=p$ is [prime](../../../../../prime-number-split.md), then $|H|$ is either $1$ or $p$, so $H=\{e\}$ or $H=G$. Thus the correct conclusion is **no proper nontrivial subgroup**. The [trivial subgroup](../../../../../trivial-subgroup.md) $\{e\}$ is proper for every group of prime order, so the wording in the question needs this qualification.
