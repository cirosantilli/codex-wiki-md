<h1 id="10/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Distinguish the [quaternion group](../../../../../quaternion-group.md) generators $\mathbf i,\mathbf j,\mathbf k$ from the complex scalar $i$. Denote their proposed image matrices by

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C=\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\0&-i\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Direct multiplication gives

$$
A^2=B^2=C^2=-I_2,\qquad
AB=C,\quad BC=A,\quad CA=B,\qquad
BA=-C,\quad CB=-A,\quad AC=-B.
$$

For example $AB=\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)=C$ and $ABC=C^2=-I_2$. Sending $I$ to $I_2$ and each negative quaternion element to the negative of its positive image now reproduces every quaternion product. The matrices all have [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) one and are invertible, so this is a [group representation](../../../../../group-representation.md) in $GL(2,\mathbb C)$.

The eight matrices $\pm I_2,\pm A,\pm B,\pm C$ are distinct. Therefore the [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) is injective, and the representation is **faithful**; equivalently its [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) is $\{I\}$.

The [character of a representation](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) is the [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) of its representing matrix. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\chi(I)=2,\qquad\chi(-I)=-2,\qquad
\chi(\pm\mathbf i)=\chi(\pm\mathbf j)=\chi(\pm\mathbf k)=0.}
$$

These are constant on the five [conjugacy classes](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) $\{I\}$, $\{-I\}$, $\{\pm\mathbf i\}$, $\{\pm\mathbf j\}$, $\{\pm\mathbf k\}$.

The complete list of [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{subgroup}&\text{order}\\\hline
\{I\}&1\\
\{I,-I\}&2\\
\langle\mathbf i\rangle=\{I,-I,\mathbf i,-\mathbf i\}&4\\
\langle\mathbf j\rangle=\{I,-I,\mathbf j,-\mathbf j\}&4\\
\langle\mathbf k\rangle=\{I,-I,\mathbf k,-\mathbf k\}&4\\
Q&8
\end{array}}
$$

To see completeness, [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) allows only orders $1,2,4,8$. The only element of order two is $-I$, which fixes the order-two subgroup. Every noncentral element has order four and generates the corresponding listed [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md). Any order-four subgroup contains such an element, because there are only two central elements; it must equal the [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) generated by that element. This accounts for all possibilities.
