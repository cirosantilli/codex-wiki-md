<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For a source in the exterior, its inverse point $\mathbf p$ lies strictly inside the excluded disc. The [method of images](../../../../../method-of-images.md) construction of the [bounded exterior disk Green function](../../../../../bounded-exterior-disk-green-function.md) therefore allows an opposite logarithmic source there without adding a singularity to the domain. The candidate [Dirichlet Green function](../../../../../dirichlet-green-function.md) is

$$
\boxed{G(\mathbf r;\mathbf r')=\frac1{2\pi}\left(\log|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|-\log|\mathbf r-\mathbf p|-\log|\mathbf r'|\right).}
$$

Its [Laplacian](../../../../../laplacian.md) in the exterior is exactly $\delta(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')$, since the image singularity is outside that domain. The identity in (d) makes $G=0$ on the unit circle. Moreover

$$
\lim_{|\mathbf r|\to\infty}G(\mathbf r;\mathbf r')=-\frac1{2\pi}\log|\mathbf r'|,
$$

so it obeys the required boundedness condition. These checks establish the stated logarithm-of-a-ratio expression with $A=1/(2\pi)$. Bounded exterior [Dirichlet uniqueness for the Poisson equation](../../../../../dirichlet-uniqueness-for-the-poisson-equation.md) follows by [inversion in a circle](../../../../../inversion-in-a-circle.md) and removal of the bounded singularity at the origin.
