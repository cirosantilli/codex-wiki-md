<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let $s=|\mathbf r'|>0$ and $\mathbf p=\mathbf r'/s^2$. For $|\mathbf r|=1$, expanding both squared [Euclidean distances](../../../../../euclidean-distance.md) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
s^2|\mathbf r-\mathbf p|^2
&=s^2-2\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf r'+1\\
&=|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Taking nonnegative square roots proves

$$
\boxed{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|=|\mathbf r'|\,|\mathbf r-\mathbf p|.}
$$

This is the distance identity behind [inversion in a circle](../../../../../inversion-in-a-circle.md) and the [method of images](../../../../../method-of-images.md) for a circular boundary.
