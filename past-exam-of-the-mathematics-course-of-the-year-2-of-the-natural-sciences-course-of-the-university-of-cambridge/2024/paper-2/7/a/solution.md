<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the bead [angles](../../../../../angle.md) $\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3$ as [generalized coordinates](../../../../../generalized-coordinate.md). The straight-line spring length between two beads follows from the [law of cosines](../../../../../law-of-cosines.md):

$$
\ell_{ij}=\sqrt{2-2\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j)}.
$$

The hoop is horizontal, so the [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../gravitational-energy.md) is constant and can be omitted. The [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is $(m/2)\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2$, giving the [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md)

$$
\boxed{L=\frac m2\sum_{i=1}^3\dot\theta_i^2-\frac k2\sum_{i<j}\left[\sqrt{2-2\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j)}-\sqrt3\right]^2.}
$$

At $\theta_i^0=\Theta+2\pi(i-1)/3$, every spring has its natural length, so the [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) is zero and its first [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) vanish. This is an [equilibrium](../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) for every constant $\Theta$. It is stable against relative displacement because each spring energy is nonnegative; the overall rotation is a neutral direction.
