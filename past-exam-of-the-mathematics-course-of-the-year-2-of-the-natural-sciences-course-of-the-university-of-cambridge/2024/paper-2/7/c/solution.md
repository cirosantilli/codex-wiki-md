<h1 id="7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A common shift of all three [angles](../../../../../angle.md) leaves every spring length unchanged. This continuous rotational [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) gives the [zero mode](../../../../../zero-mode.md): there is no restoring force against rigid rotation.

The equilateral [equilibrium](../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) also has the [dihedral group](../../../../../dihedral-group.md) symmetry $D_3$. Its action on the three angular displacements preserves the uniform one-dimensional subspace and the two-dimensional sum-zero subspace. The latter is an [irreducible representation](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) over the [real numbers](../../../../../real-number.md); a reflection may reverse all angular signs as well as exchange bead labels, which does not change these invariant subspaces. The symmetric [stiffness matrix](../../../../../stiffness-matrix.md) must act as a scalar on the sum-zero subspace, explaining the equal vibrational [angular frequencies](../../../../../angular-frequency.md). Directly, each sum-zero vector satisfies $(3I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^T)\mathbf v=3\mathbf v$. This is an example of [degenerate normal modes from permutation symmetry](../../../../../degenerate-normal-modes-from-permutation-symmetry.md), and of [normal modes of three equal masses with a symmetric gap potential](../../../../../normal-modes-of-three-equal-masses-with-a-symmetric-gap-potential.md).
