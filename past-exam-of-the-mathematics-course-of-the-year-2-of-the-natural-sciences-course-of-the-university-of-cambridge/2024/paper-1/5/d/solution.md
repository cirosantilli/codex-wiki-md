<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

If $Av=\lambda v$, then $A^nv=\lambda^nv$, and the [matrix exponential](../../../../../matrix-exponential.md) series gives $Uv=e^\lambda v$. Also $(I+A)^{-1}v=(1+\lambda)^{-1}v$, since $1+\lambda\ne0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\lambda_U=e^\lambda,\qquad\lambda_V=\frac{1-\lambda}{1+\lambda}.}
$$

The assumed diagonalization ensures that these account for all [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md), including multiplicities. In particular, for $\lambda=i\theta$,

$$
\boxed{\lambda_U=e^{i\theta},\qquad\lambda_V=\frac{1-i\theta}{1+i\theta}=e^{-2i\arctan\theta},}
$$

both of [modulus](../../../../../modulus.md) one, in agreement with the [unitary matrix](../../../../../unitary-matrix.md) property.
