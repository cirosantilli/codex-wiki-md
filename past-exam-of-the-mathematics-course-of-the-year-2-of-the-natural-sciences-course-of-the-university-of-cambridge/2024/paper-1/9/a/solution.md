<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For fixed initial and final coordinates, the two [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) are

$$
\boxed{\frac d{dt}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\dot x}\right)-\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}=0,\qquad\frac d{dt}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\dot y}\right)-\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}=0.}
$$

Indeed the [first variation](../../../../../first-variation.md) of the [functional](../../../../../functional.md) is

$$
\delta F=\int_0^T(f_x\delta x+f_y\delta y+f_{\dot x}\delta\dot x+f_{\dot y}\delta\dot y)\,dt.
$$

[Integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) moves the time [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) from the [variations](../../../../../variation.md); their endpoint values are zero. The [fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations](../../../../../fundamental-lemma-of-the-calculus-of-variations.md) then gives the two equations above.
