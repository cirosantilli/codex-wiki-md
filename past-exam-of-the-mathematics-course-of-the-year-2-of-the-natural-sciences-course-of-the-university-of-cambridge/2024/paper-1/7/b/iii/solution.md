<h1 id="7/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Iterating the [Gamma function recurrence](../../../../../../gamma-function-recurrence.md) gives a [meromorphic continuation](../../../../../../meromorphic-continuation.md)

$$
\Gamma(z)=\frac{\Gamma(z+m+1)}{z(z+1)\cdots(z+m)},\qquad m=0,1,2,\ldots.
$$

Near $z=-m$, the numerator is analytic and tends to $\Gamma(1)=1$. Exactly one denominator factor, $z+m$, vanishes, while all other factors tend to the nonzero product $(-1)^m m!$. Therefore

$$
\Gamma(z)\sim\frac{(-1)^m}{m!}\frac1{z+m}.
$$

Thus $\boxed{z=0,-1,-2,\ldots\text{ are all simple poles}}$. More precisely, the [Gamma function residue at a nonpositive integer](../../../../../../gamma-function-residue-at-a-nonpositive-integer.md) is $\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{z=-m}\Gamma(z)=(-1)^m/m!}$. Taking $m$ large enough to put $z+m+1$ in the original right half-plane also shows that this continuation introduces no other finite [poles](../../../../../../pole.md).
