<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Since $\mathbf a$ is constant, the [product rule](../../../../../product-rule.md) gives $\nabla\cdot(\mathbf a\psi)=\mathbf a\cdot\nabla\psi$. Applying the [divergence theorem](../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) therefore gives

$$
\mathbf a\cdot\int_V\nabla\psi\,dV=\int_S\psi\,\mathbf a\cdot d\mathbf S=\mathbf a\cdot\int_S\psi\,d\mathbf S.
$$

This holds for every constant [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $\mathbf a$, so each component of the difference vanishes. Consequently the [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) has the boundary representation

$$
\boxed{\int_V\nabla\psi\,dV=\int_S\psi\,d\mathbf S.}
$$
