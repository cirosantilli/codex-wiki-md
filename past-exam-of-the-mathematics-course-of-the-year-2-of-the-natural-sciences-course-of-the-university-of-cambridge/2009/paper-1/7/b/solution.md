<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Factor $1-z^3=(1-z)(1+z+z^2)$. The [geometric series](../../../../../geometric-series.md) gives, for $|z|<1$,

$$
\frac1{1+z+z^2}=\frac{1-z}{1-z^3}=1-z+z^3-z^4+z^6-z^7+\cdots.
$$

Thus the first three nonzero terms are **$1-z+z^3$**. If “first three terms” means the coefficients through degree two, that Taylor polynomial is $1-z+0z^2$. The distinction matters because the quadratic coefficient vanishes. The nearest singularities are the two nontrivial cube [roots of unity](../../../../../root-of-unity.md), so the [radius of convergence](../../../../../radius-of-convergence.md) is exactly one.
