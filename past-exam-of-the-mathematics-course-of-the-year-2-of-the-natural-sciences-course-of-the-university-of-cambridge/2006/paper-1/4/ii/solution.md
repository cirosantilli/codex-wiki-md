<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Put $\beta=\|b\|>0$ and choose an oriented [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) with third vector $b/\beta$. The [Levi-Civita symbol](../../../../../levi-civita-symbol.md) term has action $\epsilon_{ijk}b_kx_j=x\times b$, the negative of $b\times x$. Thus this [axisymmetric linear map](../../../../../axisymmetric-linear-map.md) has block form

$$
B^\pm\sim\begin{pmatrix}1&\beta&0\\-\beta&1&0\\0&0&1\pm\beta^2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The transverse eigenvalues $1\pm i\beta$ are not real. The only real [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) and its [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) are therefore

$$
\boxed{\lambda(B^\pm)=1\pm\beta^2,\qquad v\in\operatorname{span}\{b\}\setminus\{0\}.}
$$

The determinants are $\det B^+=(1+\beta^2)^2>0$ and $\det B^-=(1-\beta^2)(1+\beta^2)$, proving that **$B^+$ is always invertible, and $B^-$ is invertible exactly when $\|b\|\ne1$**. The complex transverse pair is important for this determinant even though the question asks for real eigenvalues.
