<h1 id="8/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In a [group of order six](../../../../../group-of-order-six.md), element orders divide six. If there were no element of order three or six, every nonidentity element would have order two, making the group abelian by the preceding argument. Choose $a\ne e$ and $b\notin\{e,a\}$. Then $\{e,a,b,ab\}$ is a subgroup of order four: its elements are distinct, it is closed because $a,b$ commute and square to $e$, and each element is its own inverse. This contradicts [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), since four does not divide six. Hence **an element of order three or six exists**.
