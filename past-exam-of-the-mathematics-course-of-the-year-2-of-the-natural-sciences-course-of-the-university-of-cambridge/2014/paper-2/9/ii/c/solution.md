↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [conjugacy class](../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) of $g$ is $\{hgh^{-1}:h\in G\}$. Rotations commute with one another, while conjugation by a reflection changes $R^j$ to $R^{-j}$. Thus the rotation classes are $\{I\}$, $\{R,R^4\}$ and $\{R^2,R^3\}$.

For reflections,

$$
R^a(R^jm)R^{-a}=R^{j+2a}m.
$$

Because multiplication by two permutes the residues modulo five, these conjugates exhaust all five reflections. Consequently the complete class partition is

$$
\boxed{\{I\},\qquad\{R,R^4\},\qquad\{R^2,R^3\},\qquad\{m,Rm,R^2m,R^3m,R^4m\}.}
$$

Their sizes $1+2+2+5=10$ account for every element, as in the [conjugacy classes of a finite dihedral group](../../../../../../conjugacy-classes-of-a-finite-dihedral-group.md).

A [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) is a union of complete conjugacy classes. The trivial subgroup is normal, and the rotation subgroup is normal because conjugation preserves it; equivalently it has index two. None of the five order-two subgroups is normal, because it contains just one member of the five-element reflection class. Thus the proper normal subgroups are exactly

$$
\boxed{\{I\}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle R\rangle.}
$$
