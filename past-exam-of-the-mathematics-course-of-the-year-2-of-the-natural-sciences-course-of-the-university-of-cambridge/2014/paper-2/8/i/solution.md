<h1 id="8/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The identity commutes with every group element, so it lies in the [center of a group](../../../../../center-of-a-group.md). If $z,w$ are central and $g\in G$, then $gw=wg$ implies $gw^{-1}=w^{-1}g$. Consequently

$$
g(zw^{-1})=zg w^{-1}=zw^{-1}g.
$$

Thus $zw^{-1}$ is central. The [subgroup test](../../../../../subgroup-test.md) proves

$$
\boxed{Z(G)\leq G.}
$$

Equivalently, the centre is nonempty, closed under products, and closed under inverses. This proof does not require finiteness. In fact the centre is an [Abelian subgroup](../../../../../abelian-subgroup.md), and it is normal because $gzg^{-1}=z$ for each central element.
