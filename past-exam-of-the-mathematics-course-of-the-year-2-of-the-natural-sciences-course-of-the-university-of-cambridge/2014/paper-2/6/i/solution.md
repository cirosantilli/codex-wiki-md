For a constant orthogonal Cartesian change of coordinates $x'_i=Q_{ij}x_j$, the [tensor component transformation law](../../../../../tensor-component-transformation-law.md) for order $n$ is

$$
\boxed{T'_{i_1\cdots i_n}=Q_{i_1j_1}\cdots Q_{i_nj_n}T_{j_1\cdots j_n}.}
$$

Repeated indices are summed. In Cartesian orthonormal coordinates the metric identifies upper and lower indices, so the same orthogonal matrix acts on every index.

An [isotropic tensor](../../../../../isotropic-tensor.md) has the same numerical components after every proper orthogonal change of axes: $Q^{\otimes n}T=T$ for every $Q$ with $Q^TQ=I$ and $\det Q=1$. For example, $\delta_{ij}$ is isotropic because $Q_{ik}Q_{jk}=\delta_{ij}$. Some conventions require invariance under all orthogonal transformations, including reflections; this stronger convention must be distinguished for odd-order tensors, notably the [Levi-Civita symbol](../../../../../levi-civita-symbol.md), which is invariant under proper rotations but changes sign under its ordinary three-index transformation for a reflection.
