<h1 id="6/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Under the same Cartesian change, a [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md) has $u'_i(x')=Q_{ip}u_p(x)$ with $x=Q^Tx'$. The [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives $\partial/\partial x'_j=Q_{jq}\partial/\partial x_q$. Since $Q$ is constant,

$$
\boxed{\frac{\partial u'_i}{\partial x'_j}=Q_{ip}Q_{jq}\frac{\partial u_p}{\partial x_q}.}
$$

This is exactly the order-two [tensor component transformation law](../../../../../tensor-component-transformation-law.md). Thus the Cartesian vector gradient is a [Cartesian second-rank tensor](../../../../../cartesian-second-rank-tensor.md), as in the [velocity gradient tensor](../../../../../velocity-gradient-tensor.md) when $u$ is a velocity field.

The restriction to Cartesian transformations matters. In a general position-dependent basis, differentiating the basis contributes extra terms, so the tensorial derivative is the [covariant derivative](../../../../../covariant-derivative.md), not an arbitrary array of partial derivatives.
