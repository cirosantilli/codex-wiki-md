<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For the [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md) $F=\phi\nabla\psi-\psi\nabla\phi$, the [product rule](../../../../../product-rule.md) gives

$$
\nabla\cdot F=\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi+\phi\nabla^2\psi-\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\phi-\psi\nabla^2\phi=\phi\nabla^2\psi-\psi\nabla^2\phi.
$$

The mixed [dot products](../../../../../dot-product.md) cancel. The [divergence theorem](../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) therefore yields [Green's second identity](../../../../../green-second-identity.md),

$$
\boxed{\int_V(\phi\nabla^2\psi-\psi\nabla^2\phi)dV=\int_S(\phi\,\hat n\cdot\nabla\psi-\psi\,\hat n\cdot\nabla\phi)dS.}
$$

The fields and boundary are assumed sufficiently regular. A singular [Green function](../../../../../green-s-function-split.md) can be treated by excising a small ball around its source and taking the radius to zero, or using [distributions](../../../../../distribution-mathematical-analysis.md).
