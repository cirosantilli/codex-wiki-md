↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [group representation](../../../../../group-representation.md) assigns each $g\in G$ an invertible linear map $D(g)$ on a fixed vector space, preserving multiplication:

$$
D(gh)=D(g)D(h),\qquad D(e)=I.
$$

Choosing a basis turns those maps into matrices. A [faithful representation](../../../../../faithful-representation.md) is injective, equivalently its kernel is $\{e\}$, so distinct elements have distinct matrices.

Two [group representations](../../../../../group-representation.md) on spaces of the same dimension are called [equivalent representations](../../../../../equivalence-of-group-representations.md) if one fixed invertible change of basis conjugates every representing matrix: $E(g)=S^{-1}D(g)S$ for all $g$. Choosing a different $S$ separately for each element does not establish equivalence.

The [character of a representation](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) is

$$
\boxed{\chi_D(g)=\operatorname{tr}D(g).}
$$

The [cyclic property of the trace](../../../../../cyclic-property-of-the-trace.md) makes it invariant under a change of basis and constant on conjugacy classes. Thus different characters at even one group element prove inequivalence.
