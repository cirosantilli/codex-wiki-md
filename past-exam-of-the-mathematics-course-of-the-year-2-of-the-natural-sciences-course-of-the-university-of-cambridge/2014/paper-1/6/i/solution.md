<h1 id="6/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices](../../../../../spectral-theorem-for-real-symmetric-matrices.md) supplies real [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) and an [orthonormal eigenbasis](../../../../../orthonormal-eigenbasis.md). Compute [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md), normalize them, and within each repeated-eigenvalue [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) choose an orthonormal basis. Distinct [eigenspaces](../../../../../eigenspace.md) are already orthogonal because symmetry gives $(\lambda-\mu)v^Tw=0$ for [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) with [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda,\mu$.

Use the resulting basis [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) as columns of $O$. Then

$$
\boxed{O^TO=I,\qquad O^TMO=D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n).}
$$

The order and signs of the columns can be chosen freely; rotations within a repeated-eigenvalue [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) are also allowed.
