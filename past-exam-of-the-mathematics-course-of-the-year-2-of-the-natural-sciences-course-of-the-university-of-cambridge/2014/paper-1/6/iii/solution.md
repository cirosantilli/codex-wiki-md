<h1 id="6/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) is $M=2I-ee^T$, where $e=(1,1,1)^T$. Along $\hat e=e/\sqrt3$, its [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is $2-3=-1$. Every [vector](../../../../../vector.md) perpendicular to $e$ has [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) two. For example, use the orthonormal basis

$$
u_1=\frac{(1,-1,0)}{\sqrt2},\qquad u_2=\frac{(1,1,-2)}{\sqrt6},\qquad u_3=\frac{(1,1,1)}{\sqrt3}.
$$

The principal-coordinate equation is

$$
2\xi^2+2\eta^2-\zeta^2=3,\qquad\frac{\xi^2+\eta^2}{3/2}-\frac{\zeta^2}{3}=1.
$$

It depends only on distance from the $u_3$ axis and position along it, so every rotation about that axis preserves the surface. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{the rotational axis is the line }x_1=x_2=x_3,\text{ with direction }(1,1,1).}
$$

This is a [one-sheet hyperboloid](../../../../../one-sheet-hyperboloid.md) with waist radius $\sqrt{3/2}$. The repeated positive [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) makes the two transverse [principal axes](../../../../../principal-axis.md) nonunique.
