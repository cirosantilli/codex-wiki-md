<h1 id="9/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a variation $q\mapsto q+\epsilon\eta$ with $\eta(t_0)=\eta(t_1)=0$, the first variation is

$$
\delta S=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}(L_q\eta+L_{\dot q}\dot\eta)dt=[L_{\dot q}\eta]_{t_0}^{t_1}+\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\left(L_q-\frac d{dt}L_{\dot q}\right)\eta\,dt.
$$

The endpoint term vanishes. Since the interior variation is arbitrary, the [fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations](../../../../../fundamental-lemma-of-the-calculus-of-variations.md) gives the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{\frac d{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q}=0.}
$$

Along such a trajectory,

$$
\frac d{dt}\left(\dot qL_{\dot q}-L\right)=\dot q\left(\frac d{dt}L_{\dot q}-L_q\right)-L_t=-L_t.
$$

If $L$ has no explicit time dependence, the first [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\dot q\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}-L=\text{constant}.}
$$

Its negative is the equivalent Beltrami form. For the usual time-independent mechanical [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md) $L=T-V$ with quadratic velocity dependence, this conserved quantity is the total [mechanical energy](../../../../../mechanical-energy.md) $T+V$.
