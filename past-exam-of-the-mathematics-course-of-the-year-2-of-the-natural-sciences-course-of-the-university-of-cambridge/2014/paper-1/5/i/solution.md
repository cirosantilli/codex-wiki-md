<h1 id="5/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [Hermitian matrix](../../../../../hermitian-operator.md) satisfies $H^\dagger=H$, where $\dagger$ denotes the [conjugate transpose](../../../../../conjugate-transpose.md). For a nonzero [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) $v$ with $Hv=\lambda v$, the scalar $v^\dagger Hv$ equals its own [complex conjugate](../../../../../complex-conjugate.md), and

$$
\lambda=\frac{v^\dagger Hv}{v^\dagger v}\in\mathbb R.
$$

Thus **every Hermitian [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is real**.

A [unitary matrix](../../../../../unitary-matrix.md) satisfies $U^\dagger U=UU^\dagger=I$. If $Uv=\mu v$, preservation of the squared [norm](../../../../../norm.md) gives

$$
v^\dagger v=(Uv)^\dagger(Uv)=|\mu|^2v^\dagger v.
$$

Since $v\neq0$, $\boxed{|\mu|=1}$. These statements concern complex [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md), even when the [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) entries happen to be real.
