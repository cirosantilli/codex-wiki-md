# Negative-divergence obstruction to invariant tori

↑ **Parent:** [Divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md)

A smooth embedded invariant two-torus in three-dimensional Euclidean [phase space](phase-space.md) bounds a solid [torus](torus.md) of positive volume. The [vector field](vector-field.md) is tangent to its boundary, so its normal flux is zero. If the [divergence](divergence.md) is strictly negative throughout the enclosed volume, the [divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md) gives a strictly negative flux, a contradiction. Thus a globally negative-divergence three-dimensional [vector field](vector-field.md) has no such quasiperiodic [invariant torus](invariant-torus.md). This argument is stronger than merely noting contraction of transported phase volume; an [attractor](attractor.md)'s own zero volume is not a contradiction.
