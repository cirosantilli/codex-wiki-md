# Connectedness of an intersection from connected finite intersections

↑ **Parent:** [Connected space](connected-space.md)

Let $X$ be a [compact Hausdorff space](compact-hausdorff-space.md) and let $K_i$ be [compact subsets](compact-space.md) such that all finite [intersections](set-intersection.md) are nonempty and [connected](connected-space.md). Their total [intersection](set-intersection.md) $K$ is nonempty by the [finite intersection characterization of compactness](finite-intersection-characterization-of-compactness.md), and [compact](compact-space.md) because it is [closed](closed-set.md) in $X$.

If $K$ were not [connected](connected-space.md), its [separation of a topological space](separation-of-a-topological-space.md) would divide it into two nonempty [compact subsets](compact-space.md). [Separation of disjoint compact subsets](separation-of-disjoint-compact-subsets.md) gives [disjoint](disjoint-sets.md) ambient [open sets](open-set.md) $U,V$ containing the two pieces. The family consisting of $X\setminus(U\cup V)$ and all $K_i$ has empty total [intersection](set-intersection.md), so some finite subfamily has empty [intersection](set-intersection.md). Hence a finite [intersection](set-intersection.md) $K_J$ of the $K_i$ lies in $U\cup V$. Since $K\subseteq K_J$ meets both [open sets](open-set.md), they disconnect $K_J$, a contradiction. The hypothesis on all finite [intersections](set-intersection.md) is stronger than requiring each individual $K_i$ to be [connected](connected-space.md).
