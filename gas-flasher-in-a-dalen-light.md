<h1 id="gas-flasher-in-a-dalen-light">Gas flasher in a Dalén light</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Dalén light](dalen-light.md)

Dalén's 1910 patent describes gas entering a diaphragm chamber. When its [pressure](pressure-split.md) overcomes a spring and magnetic restraint, a valve opens and the pilot ignites the burner. Escape of gas lowers the [pressure](pressure-split.md), letting the valve close. A ratchet and cam alter an additional feed to create groups of flashes. This specification describes a pressure-driven flashing mechanism, rather than the [sun valve](sun-valve.md)'s daylight sensor.[Description, figures 1–4: diaphragm-pressure cycle, pilot flame and additional-feed grouping mechanism; published 15 March 1910](light-signal-apparatus-us952420a-by-gustaf-dalen.md)

The patent is evidence of the proposed mechanism, not an independent endurance or fuel-consumption test. For a simple original budget, suppose the pilot consumes gas at a steady rate $q_p$, the main burner consumes $q_m$ while lit, daylight shutoff permits operation for fraction $f$ of the day, and flashes occupy fraction $d$ of that permitted time. Then the daily mean consumption is

$$
\boxed{\overline q=q_p+fdq_m.}
$$

For stipulated $f=1/2$, $d=0.10$ and $q_p=0.02q_m$, this is $0.07q_m$, compared with $1.02q_m$ for a continuously lit main burner with the same pilot. The calculated saving is about $93.1\%$. The arithmetic explains the two independent opportunities for saving fuel; it does not establish those fractions or that performance for an actual lighthouse.
