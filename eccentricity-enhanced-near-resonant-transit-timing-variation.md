# Eccentricity-enhanced near-resonant transit-timing variation

↑ **Parent:** [Near-resonant transit-timing superperiod](near-resonant-transit-timing-superperiod.md)

A first-order [resonant orbital perturbation](resonant-orbital-perturbation.md) changes the [semi-major axis](semi-major-axis.md) and therefore the [mean motion](mean-motion.md). Integrating the sinusoidal mean-motion variation over the [near-resonant transit-timing superperiod](near-resonant-transit-timing-superperiod.md) gives a mean-longitude amplitude proportional to $\mu_2e/\Delta^2$. Converting phase to transit time gives the displayed amplitude ratio. It assumes small perturbations, circulating [resonant argument](resonant-argument.md), effectively fixed free [orbital eccentricity](orbital-eccentricity.md), and dominance of this enhanced term over direct phase and forced-eccentricity contributions. The formal divergence as $\Delta\to0$ signals failure of this expansion, not infinitely large transit times.
