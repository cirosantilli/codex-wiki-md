# Banach-Steinhaus convergence theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform boundedness principle](uniform-boundedness-principle.md)

Let $X$ be a [normed space](normed-vector-space-split.md), $Y$ a [Banach space](banach-space-split.md), and $A_n:X\to Y$ be [bounded linear operators](continuous-linear-operator.md) with $C=\sup_n\|A_n\|<\infty$. Convergence of $A_nd$ for every $d$ in a [dense subset](dense-set.md) $D$ implies convergence of $A_nx$ for every $x\in X$ to a [bounded linear operator](continuous-linear-operator.md) $A$, with $\|A\|\leq\liminf_n\|A_n\|$. Indeed, $\|A_nx-A_mx\|\leq2C\|x-d\|+\|A_nd-A_md\|$ makes $(A_nx)$ a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md); completeness gives its limit, and linearity and the bound pass to that limit. If $X$ is a [Banach space](banach-space-split.md), pointwise convergence conversely implies the uniform bound by the [Uniform boundedness principle](uniform-boundedness-principle.md). If the limit [bounded linear operator](continuous-linear-operator.md) is specified in advance, completeness of $Y$ is unnecessary.
