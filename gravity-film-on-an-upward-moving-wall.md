# Gravity film on an upward-moving wall

↑ **Parent:** [Lubrication theory](lubrication-theory.md)

Take the downward coordinate along a vertical wall that moves upward with speed $U$. For a stress-free thin Newtonian film with negligible inertia and surface-pressure gradients, the [lubrication approximation](lubrication-theory.md) gives $\mu u_{zz}=-\rho g$, $u(0)=-U$, and $u_z(h)=0$. Thus $u=-U+(\rho g/\mu)(hz-z^2/2)$ and the volume flux per unit width is $q=-Uh+\rho gh^3/(3\mu)$. Integrating [incompressibility](incompressible-flow.md) across the material film gives the displayed conservation law. Gravity drives fluid downward, while the moving wall entrains it upward; the local characteristic speed is $q'(h)=-U+\rho gh^2/\mu$.
