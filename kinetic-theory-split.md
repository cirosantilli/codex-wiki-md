# Kinetic theory

↑ **Parent:** [Statistical physics](statistical-physics-split.md)

Kinetic theory describes many-particle systems through a distribution on [phase space](phase-space.md), retaining both position and [velocity](velocity.md). In the number-density convention, $f(t,\mathbf x,\mathbf v)\,d^3x\,d^3v$ is the expected number of particles in a small position–[velocity](velocity.md) region. For identical particles of [mass](mass.md) $m$, its [integrals](integral-split.md) give the [number density](number-density.md) $n$ and bulk [velocity](velocity.md) $\mathbf u$:

$$
n(t,\mathbf x)=\int_{\mathbb R^3}f\,d^3v,\qquad n\mathbf u=\int_{\mathbb R^3}\mathbf v f\,d^3v.
$$

This $f$ is not normalized to one: its [integral](integral-split.md) over the whole [phase space](phase-space.md) gives the total expected particle number. Transport moves particles along trajectories, while [collision](collision.md) or [diffusion](diffusion.md) terms change the distribution. The [Boltzmann equation](boltzmann-equation-split.md), [Hamiltonian Liouville equation](hamiltonian-liouville-equation.md) and [Fokker-Planck equation](fokker-planck-equation.md) describe different physical assumptions, rather than interchangeable collision laws.

An [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) is a motivating example: the [pressure](pressure-split.md) on a wall comes from microscopic [momentum](momentum-split.md) transfers, even when the bulk [velocity](velocity.md) is zero. The [Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution](maxwell-boltzmann-velocity-distribution.md) separates this random molecular motion from bulk motion; see [ideal gas pressure from wall collisions](ideal-gas-pressure-from-wall-collisions.md). The attractive feature of [kinetic theory](kinetic-theory-split.md) is that a distribution can describe two opposing populations where one average [velocity](velocity.md) hides almost everything. It also tells us when averaging into a [continuum approximation](continuum-approximation.md) loses essential information: compare the intermolecular [mean free path](mean-free-path.md) with the apparatus scale using the [Knudsen number](knudsen-number.md). [Richard Fitzpatrick, Kinetic Theory: fundamental assumptions and wall pressure](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/355/Surveyhtml/node212.html)
