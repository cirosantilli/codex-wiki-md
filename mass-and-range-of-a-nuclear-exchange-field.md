# Mass and range of a nuclear exchange field

↑ **Parent:** [Yukawa meson theory of nuclear forces](yukawa-meson-theory-of-nuclear-forces.md)

A free scalar [Klein-Gordon equation](klein-gordon-equation.md) with [rest mass](invariant-mass.md) $m$ is

$$
\left(\frac1{c^2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}-\nabla^2+\frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\right)\phi=0.
$$

Here $c$ is the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) and $\hbar$ the reduced [Planck constant](planck-constant.md). In the static limit, comparison with the [static Yukawa Green function](static-yukawa-green-function.md) identifies

$$
\kappa=\frac{mc}{\hbar},\qquad \lambda=\kappa^{-1}=\frac{\hbar}{mc}=\frac{\hbar c}{mc^2}.
$$

This $\lambda$ is the reduced [Compton wavelength](compton-wavelength.md), rather than $h/(mc)$; the two differ by $2\pi$. It is an exponential decay length of this exchange field, not the geometric size of a [nucleon](nucleon.md) or an exact radius of a [bound state](bound-state.md). This article uses $\lambda$ for a length; the 1935 paper used that letter for its inverse.

For a deliberately rounded worked example, stipulate $\hbar c=197.3\,\mathrm{MeV\,fm}$ and $mc^2=140\,\mathrm{MeV}$. Then

$$
\lambda=\frac{197.3}{140}\,\mathrm{fm}=1.4092857\ldots\,\mathrm{fm}\approx1.41\,\mathrm{fm},\qquad \kappa\approx0.70958\,\mathrm{fm}^{-1}.
$$

A [femtometre](femtometre.md) is $10^{-15}\,\mathrm m$. The chosen [rest energy](rest-energy.md) is a convenient [pion](pion.md)-scale input; neither it nor the resulting [Yukawa potential](yukawa-potential.md) is asserted to be an exact measured nuclear interaction. Holding $\hbar c$ fixed, chosen [rest energies](rest-energy.md) $70$, $140$ and $280\,\mathrm{MeV}$ give lengths $2.819$, $1.409$ and $0.7046\,\mathrm{fm}$. Doubling the exchange [rest mass](invariant-mass.md) halves the decay length.

Range alone does not guarantee binding: the [potential energy](potential-energy.md) must overcome the [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) cost of localizing a [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md). The existing [exponential variational bound for the Yukawa potential](exponential-variational-bound-for-the-yukawa-potential.md) makes this precise. For the same toy $V$, with [reduced mass](reduced-mass.md) $M$, choosing a normalized exponential [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) proportional to $e^{-\alpha r}$ with $\alpha=\kappa/2$ yields

$$
\langle H\rangle=\frac{\hbar^2\kappa^2}{8M}-\frac{A\kappa}{8}.
$$

For additional stipulated inputs $Mc^2=470\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and $A=100\,\mathrm{MeV\,fm}$, this gives $\langle H\rangle\approx-3.66\,\mathrm{MeV}$. A negative value proves a [bound state](bound-state.md) in this scalar model by the [quantum variational principle](quantum-variational-principle.md); it is an upper bound on its [ground-state energy](ground-state-energy.md), not a prediction for the [deuteron](deuteron.md). A nonnegative trial value would not prove the absence of binding.
