<h1 id="a/4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Every open interval is Borel, since $(a,b)=\bigcup_{m\geq1}(a,b-1/m]$, omitting empty pieces. Open subsets of the line are countable unions of open intervals and hence Borel. Conversely, each half-open interval is obtained from open sets by sigma-algebra operations, so this is the usual Borel sigma-algebra.

A continuous function has open, and therefore Borel, inverse images of open sets. Since open sets generate the target Borel sigma-algebra, it is a [Borel measurable function](../../../../../../borel-measurable-function.md). A discontinuous example is **$1_{[0,\infty)}$**, whose only level sets are built from the Borel half-lines; it jumps at zero.
