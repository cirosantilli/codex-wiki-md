[Kolmogorov zero-one law](../../../../../../kolmogorov-s-zero-one-law.md) states that every event in the tail sigma-algebra of an independent sequence has probability zero or one. Removing any fixed number of summands changes $S_n/n$ by a finite random constant divided by $n$, so does not change its extended-real limsup. Therefore $\overline X$ is measurable with respect to the tail sigma-algebra. Each event $\{\overline X\leq q\}$ for rational $q$ has probability zero or one. Monotonicity of these events forces the distribution to be concentrated at one extended-real value, allowing either infinity.

The [strong law of large numbers](../../../../../../strong-law-of-large-numbers.md) says that for independent identically distributed variables with $\mathbb E|X_1|<\infty$,

$$
\boxed{S_n/n\longrightarrow\mathbb E X_1\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$
