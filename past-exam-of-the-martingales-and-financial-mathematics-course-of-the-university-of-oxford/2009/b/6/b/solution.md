<h1 id="b/6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For holdings $h_1,h_2$, a sure payment of 100 requires

$$
140h_1+120h_2=100,\qquad80h_1+90h_2=100.
$$

Subtracting gives $2h_1+h_2=0$, so $h_1=-1$ and $h_2=2$. Thus sell one share of stock 1 and buy two shares of stock 2. Its initial cost is $100(-1+2)=100$.

This [synthetic zero-coupon bond](../../../../../synthetic-zero-coupon-bond.md) pays exactly its initial cost after one period, and therefore

$$
\boxed{B(0)=100,\qquad r=\log(100/B(0))=0.}
$$

At later nodes the holdings can be rescaled to generate the same one-period risk-free return; a physical bond need not be listed separately.
