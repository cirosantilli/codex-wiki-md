<h1 id="b/6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

With the rate $r=0$, the [risk-neutral probability in a binomial market](../../../../../risk-neutral-probability-in-a-binomial-market.md) must satisfy

$$
q u_i+(1-q)d_i=1,\qquad i=1,2.
$$

The first stock gives $0.8+0.6q=1$, hence $q=1/3$. The second gives $0.9+0.3q=1$, confirming the same answer. Thus

$$
\boxed{(q,1-q)=(1/3,2/3).}
$$

Take the successive up/down choices independent under this measure, so both stock prices are [martingales](../../../../../martingale-split.md). The martingale transition condition applies from times zero to one and one to two; the displayed $t+1$ condition in the source should be read for these two transitions.
