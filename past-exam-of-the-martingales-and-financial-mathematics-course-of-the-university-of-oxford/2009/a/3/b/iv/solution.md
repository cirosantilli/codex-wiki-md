<h1 id="a/3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Intersecting the probability-one hitting events over all positive integers shows that the [simple symmetric random walk](../../../../../../simple-symmetric-random-walk.md) reaches arbitrarily large positive levels almost surely. The hitting times of levels tending to infinity also tend to infinity, since $|S_n|\leq n$. Hence $\mathbb P(B_+)=1$, and in particular $\mathbb P(B_-\cup B_+)=1$.

Alternatively, the union conclusion, the equal probabilities from symmetry, and the [Kolmogorov zero-one law](../../../../../../kolmogorov-s-zero-one-law.md) force both probabilities to equal one. Thus almost every path has arbitrarily large positive and negative excursions. A nearest-neighbor integer path must pass through zero between a positive and a negative excursion. Such crossings occur after every finite time, giving

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(B_-)=\mathbb P(B_+)=\mathbb P(A)=1.}
$$

This is recurrence of the [simple symmetric random walk](../../../../../../simple-symmetric-random-walk.md).
