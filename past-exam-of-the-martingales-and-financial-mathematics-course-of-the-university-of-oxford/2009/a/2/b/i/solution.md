<h1 id="a/2/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Conditional on $\mathcal F_n$, put $x=X_n$. The two possible values have conditional mean

$$
(1-x)\frac x2+x\frac{1+x}2=x.
$$

The process is adapted and bounded, hence integrable. Consequently it is a [martingale](../../../../../../martingale-split.md). Since $\sup_n\mathbb E|X_n|\leq1$, the [martingale convergence theorem](../../../../../../martingale-convergence-theorem.md) gives an almost surely finite limit $X_\infty$, necessarily in $[0,1]$.

Moreover $|X_n-X_\infty|^2\leq1$ and these squared differences tend to zero almost surely. The [dominated convergence theorem](../../../../../../dominated-convergence-theorem.md) gives $\mathbb E|X_n-X_\infty|^2\to0$. Thus the [Bernoulli polarization martingale](../../../../../../bernoulli-polarization-martingale.md) converges **almost surely and in $L^2$**.
