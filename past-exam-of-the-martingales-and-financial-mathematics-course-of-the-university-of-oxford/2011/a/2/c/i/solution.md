<h1 id="a/2/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and $n>\varepsilon$,

$$
\mathbb P(|X_n-1|>\varepsilon)=\frac1{n\log n}\to0,
$$

so there is [convergence in probability](../../../../../../convergence-in-probability.md) to one. The absolute difference is exactly $n$ on the exceptional event, hence

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E|X_n-1|^p=\frac{n^{p-1}}{\log n}.}
$$

For every $p>1$ this tends to infinity, so [convergence in Lp](../../../../../../convergence-in-lp.md) fails. In contrast its first moment is $1/\log n\to0$, so convergence actually holds in $L^1$.
