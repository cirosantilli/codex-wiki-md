<h1 id="b/4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write $R=e^r$, $u=1.2$ and $d=0.9$. In a one-step [binomial options pricing model](../../../../../binomial-options-pricing-model.md), absence of [arbitrage](../../../../../arbitrage.md) requires $d<R<u$. If $R\leq d$, borrowing to buy the stock gives a nonnegative gain in either state and a positive gain in at least one. If $R\geq u$, shorting the stock and investing the proceeds does the same. Conversely, strict inequalities give the [risk-neutral probability](../../../../../risk-neutral-probability.md) $q=(R-d)/(u-d)\in(0,1)$, under which the discounted stock is a martingale; this excludes arbitrage at each node. Hence

$$
\boxed{\log0.9<r<\log1.2.}
$$
