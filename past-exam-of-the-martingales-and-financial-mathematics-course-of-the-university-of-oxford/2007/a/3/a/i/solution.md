<h1 id="a/3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A version of $\mathbb E(X\mid\mathcal G)$ is an integrable $\mathcal G$-measurable [random variable](../../../../../../random-variable-split.md) $Y$ such that

$$
\boxed{\int_A Y\,d\mathbb P=\int_A X\,d\mathbb P
\quad\text{for every }A\in\mathcal G.}
$$

Let $D=Y_1-Y_2$ for two versions. It is integrable and $\mathcal G$-measurable, and its integral over every $\mathcal G$ event is zero. In particular $A=\{D>0\}$ is allowed, giving $\int D^+d\mathbb P=0$, so $D^+=0$ almost surely. Applying the same reasoning to $\{D<0\}$ gives $D^-=0$ almost surely. Therefore **$Y_1=Y_2$ almost surely**. Equality at every point is not required, since integrals cannot distinguish versions differing on a [null set](../../../../../../null-set.md).
