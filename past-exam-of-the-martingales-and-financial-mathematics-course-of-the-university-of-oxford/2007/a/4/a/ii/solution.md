<h1 id="a/4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [pi-systems](../../../../../../pi-system.md) criterion says that if $\mathcal I_1,\mathcal I_2$ are [pi-systems](../../../../../../pi-system.md) and $\mathbb P(A\cap B)=\mathbb P(A)\mathbb P(B)$ for all $A\in\mathcal I_1$, $B\in\mathcal I_2$, then their generated [sigma-algebras](../../../../../../sigma-algebra.md) are independent. Necessity is immediate. We may add $\Omega$ to either [pi-system](../../../../../../pi-system.md), since it does not alter the generated [sigma-algebra](../../../../../../sigma-algebra.md) or the product identities.

For sufficiency, fix $A\in\mathcal I_1$ and define

$$
\mathcal D_A=\{B\in\mathcal G_2:
\mathbb P(A\cap B)=\mathbb P(A)\mathbb P(B)\}.
$$

It contains $\Omega$, is closed under complements by subtraction from $\mathbb P(A)$, and is closed under countable disjoint unions by countable additivity. Thus it is a [lambda-system](../../../../../../dynkin-system.md). It contains $\mathcal I_2$, so the [pi-lambda theorem](../../../../../../pi-lambda-theorem.md) gives $\mathcal D_A=\mathcal G_2$.

Now fix any $B\in\mathcal G_2$ and apply the same [lambda-system](../../../../../../dynkin-system.md) argument to the sets $A\in\mathcal G_1$ satisfying the identity. The first step shows that this [lambda-system](../../../../../../dynkin-system.md) contains $\mathcal I_1$, so it contains all $\mathcal G_1$. Thus the product identity holds on both entire [sigma-algebras](../../../../../../sigma-algebra.md), proving their [independence](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md).
