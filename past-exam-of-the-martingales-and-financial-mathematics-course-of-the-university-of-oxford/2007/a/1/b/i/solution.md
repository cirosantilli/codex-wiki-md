<h1 id="a/1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

[measurability](../../../../../../measurability.md) with respect to $\sigma(f)$ gives a [Borel set](../../../../../../borel-set.md) $C_{n,k}$ with $g^{-1}(B_{n,k})=f^{-1}(C_{n,k})$. Restrict it to the actual image:

$$
\boxed{A_{n,k}=C_{n,k}\cap f(M).}
$$

This is Borel by the assumed Borel property of $f(M)$, and its [preimage](../../../../../../preimage.md) under $f$ is unchanged.

The intervals $B_{n,k}$ partition the real line, so their [preimages](../../../../../../preimage.md) under $g$ partition $M$. If two of the restricted sets overlapped, a point in their intersection would equal $f(x)$ for some $x$, making $g(x)$ lie in two disjoint intervals, a contradiction. Similarly any $t\in f(M)$ has a preimage $x$, whose real value $g(x)$ belongs to one of those intervals; that puts $t$ in the corresponding $A_{n,k}$. Thus the restricted sets are disjoint and their union is exactly $f(M)$. This restriction is what removes arbitrary overlaps of the initially chosen [Borel sets](../../../../../../borel-set.md) outside the image.
