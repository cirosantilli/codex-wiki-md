↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $t\notin f(M)$, all [indicator functions](../../../../../../indicator-function.md) vanish and $h_n(t)=0$. If $t\in f(M)$, the partition in part (i) puts it in exactly one $A_{n,k}$, so exactly one term can be nonzero and the sum is a finite real number at that point. There is no ambiguous infinite sum of positive and negative terms.

For [measurability](../../../../../../measurability.md), each symmetric partial sum

$$
h_{n,m}(t)=\sum_{k=-m}^m k2^{-n}\mathbf1_{A_{n,k}}(t)
$$

is [Borel measurable](../../../../../../borel-measurable-function.md) by closure under finite linear combinations. For each $t$, these partial sums eventually include its unique possible term and converge to $h_n(t)$. The measurable-limit result from part (a) therefore proves **$h_n$ is [Borel measurable](../../../../../../borel-measurable-function.md)**.
