# Twin paradox simultaneity change

↑ **Parent:** [Twin paradox](twin-paradox.md)

During either inertial leg, the traveller can correctly say that the moving home clock runs slowly. The missing step is deciding which distant home-clock reading is simultaneous with turnaround. By the [Lorentz transformation](lorentz-transformation.md), the outward [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md)'s constant-time line through $P$ obeys $t-vx/c^2=\text{constant}$. At $x=0$ it meets the home [world line](world-line.md) at

$$
t_H^-=\frac T2-\frac{v^2T}{2c^2}.
$$

For the return [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md), use $t+vx/c^2=\text{constant}$ through the same [spacetime event](event-relativity.md) $P$; its home-clock assignment is

$$
t_H^+=\frac T2+\frac{v^2T}{2c^2},\qquad
t_H^+-t_H^-=\frac{v^2T}{c^2}.
$$

The dashed lines in the [spacetime diagram](spacetime-diagram.md) illustrate this [relativity of simultaneity](relativity-of-simultaneity.md). For $v=0.6c$ and $T=10\,\mathrm{yr}$, they assign $3.2\,\mathrm{yr}$ and $6.8\,\mathrm{yr}$ to the home clock. The difference of $3.6\,\mathrm{yr}$ is a change in the traveller's distant-time assignment, not a sudden physical advance of the home clock.

On each leg, the traveller's $4\,\mathrm{yr}$ of [proper time](proper-time.md) corresponds to $4/\gamma=3.2\,\mathrm{yr}$ of home-clock advance according to that leg's [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md). Including the [twin paradox simultaneity change](twin-paradox-simultaneity-change.md) gives $3.2+3.6+3.2=10\,\mathrm{yr}$, exactly the reading at reunion. Actual received [light](light.md) signals remain continuous across a physical turnaround; their arrival times are distinct from the distant [spacetime events](event-relativity.md) assigned as simultaneous by [Einstein synchronisation](einstein-synchronisation.md).
