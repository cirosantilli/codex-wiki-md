# Minimum of a coercive convex functional on a Hilbert space

↑ **Parent:** [Weak lower semicontinuity](weak-lower-semicontinuity.md)

Let $K$ be a nonempty norm-closed [convex set](convex-set-split.md) in a real [Hilbert space](hilbert-space-split.md), and let $F:K\to\mathbb R$ be continuous and a [convex function](convex-function.md). If $K$ is bounded, or $F(x)\to\infty$ as $x\in K$ tends to infinity in [norm](norm.md), then $F$ attains its [infimum](infimum.md). Every [sublevel set](sublevel-set.md) is bounded, norm-closed, and convex, hence contains the weak limits of its sequences by [norm-closed convex set is weakly closed](norm-closed-convex-set-is-weakly-closed.md). A [minimizing sequence](minimizing-sequence.md) in one fixed [sublevel set](sublevel-set.md) has a [weakly convergent](weak-convergence.md) subsequence by [weak sequential compactness in a Hilbert space](weak-sequential-compactness-in-a-hilbert-space.md). Its limit lies in every [sublevel set](sublevel-set.md) above the [infimum](infimum.md). Since $F$ is real-valued, this also rules out [infimum](infimum.md) $-\infty$ and proves attainment. This is an instance of the [direct method in the calculus of variations](direct-method-in-the-calculus-of-variations.md).
