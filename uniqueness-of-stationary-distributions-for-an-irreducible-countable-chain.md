# Uniqueness of stationary distributions for an irreducible countable chain

↑ **Parent:** [Stationary distribution](stationary-distribution.md)

An [irreducible Markov chain](irreducible-markov-chain.md) on a finite or countably infinite [state space](state-space.md) has at most one [stationary distribution](stationary-distribution.md). On a countably infinite state space it exists exactly when the chain is [positive recurrent](positive-recurrent-markov-chain.md). In that case, for every state $i$,

$$
\pi_i=\frac1{\mathbb E_iT_i^+},
$$

where $T_i^+$ is the [first return time](first-return-time.md). The expression determines every component uniquely. The formula follows by dividing the one visit to $i$ in each return cycle by the [mean recurrence time](mean-recurrence-time.md); the [strong law of large numbers](strong-law-of-large-numbers.md) for successive cycles makes this occupation fraction precise.

Every irreducible [finite Markov chain](finite-markov-chain.md) is positive recurrent and has exactly one stationary distribution. On an infinite state space, an irreducible [null recurrent Markov chain](null-recurrent-markov-chain.md) or [transient Markov chain](transient-markov-chain.md) has none. For example, the [simple symmetric random walk](simple-symmetric-random-walk.md) on the integers is null recurrent. [Aperiodicity](aperiodic-markov-chain.md) affects convergence of the one-time laws, but is unnecessary for existence or uniqueness of a stationary distribution.
