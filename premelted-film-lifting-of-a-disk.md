# Premelted-film lifting of a disk

↑ **Parent:** [Frost heave](frost-heave.md)

In an equal-density, no-slip [lubrication approximation](lubrication-theory.md), repulsive [disjoining pressure](disjoining-pressure.md) $A/(6\pi d^3)$ sustains a [premelting](premelting.md) film of thickness $d$ beneath a rigid disk of radius $a$. With imposed radial undercooling $\Delta T(1-r^2/a^2)$, local [thermomolecular pressure](thermomolecular-pressure.md) is $P_T=\rho L\Delta T(1-r^2/a^2)/T_m$, and $d^3=A/(6\pi P_T)$. A constant vertical lifting rate $U$ requires inward volume flux $q_r=-Ur/2$. Integrating the resulting [lubrication pressure](lubrication-pressure.md) and disk [force balance](force-balance.md) gives the displayed rate, where $W$ is the apparent load, $\mu$ is liquid [dynamic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md), $L$ is specific [latent heat](latent-heat.md), and $T_m$ is the absolute bulk melting [temperature](temperature.md). Positive lifting requires $W<\pi a^2\rho L\Delta T/(2T_m)$. For fixed positive load, the rate rises from zero at that threshold towards $A/(6\pi\mu a^2)$ as undercooling increases. That plateau is an asymptotic limit within this constitutive model, not a claim that continuum-film physics persists at arbitrarily low [temperatures](temperature.md).
