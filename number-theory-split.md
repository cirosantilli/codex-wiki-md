# Number theory

↑ **Parent:** [Area of mathematics](area-of-mathematics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory)

Number theory studies [integers](integer-split.md), their [divisibility](divisibility-split.md), and the patterns created by [prime numbers](prime-number-split.md). Its questions often have short statements but rigid answers: which [integers](integer-split.md) divide a given one, when does an [equation](equation.md) have integer solutions, and when can arithmetic with [congruence classes](congruence-class.md) be reversed? The [Euclidean algorithm](euclidean-algorithm.md) and [modular multiplicative inverses](modular-multiplicative-inverse.md) connect these questions through explicit calculations.

For example, $252=2^2\cdot3^2\cdot7$ and $198=2\cdot3^2\cdot11$. Their shared [prime factors](prime-factor.md) give [greatest common divisor](greatest-common-divisor.md) $18$. The [Euclidean algorithm](euclidean-algorithm.md) reaches that answer without finding either complete [prime factorization](fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md). It also produces $18=4\cdot252-5\cdot198$, an instance of [Bézout's identity](bezout-s-identity.md). This relation certifies every common [divisor](divisor.md) at once and solves an associated [linear Diophantine equation](linear-diophantine-equation.md).

[Modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic-split.md) retains only remainders, but the lost information can be surprisingly controlled. Multiplication by $5$ modulo $17$ is a [permutation](permutation.md) of all residue classes, because $5$ has [modular inverse](modular-multiplicative-inverse.md) $7$. Multiplication by $5$ modulo $15$ collapses fifteen residue classes to three. Thus an apparently small change in the modulus changes whether a calculation can be reversed. The [Chinese remainder theorem](chinese-remainder-theorem.md) explains another kind of compression: remainders modulo [coprime integers](coprime-integers.md) can be combined into one remainder modulo their product.

Simple restrictions help reveal the structure without telling the whole story. Every [prime number](prime-number-split.md) greater than three is congruent to $1$ or $5$ modulo $6$: the other four residue classes force [divisibility](divisibility-split.md) by $2$ or $3$. The converse fails because $25$ and $35$ lie in those classes but are [composite numbers](composite-number.md). Necessary conditions are useful filters, not substitutes for [primality testing](primality-testing.md).

The subject's appeal lies partly in this tension: [prime factorization](fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md) gives a clean description of every positive [integer](integer-split.md), while identifying factors and predicting their distribution lead to substantial further questions. [Analytic number theory](analytic-number-theory-split.md) uses tools from [analysis](analysis-split.md) to study global patterns, and [algebraic number theory](algebraic-number-theory-split.md) extends arithmetic into [number fields](number-field.md). Applications include [cryptography](cryptography.md) and [error-correcting codes](error-correcting-code.md); MIT's discrete applied mathematics course develops modular structures for both purposes. [Ankur Moitra, MIT 18.200, Modular Arithmetic and Elementary Algebra, 2024, introduction](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-200-principles-of-discrete-applied-mathematics-spring-2024/mit18_200_s24_lec15.pdf)

Euclid's _Elements_ already gives a greatest-common-measure construction in Book VII, Proposition 2, and the argument that a finite list cannot exhaust the [prime numbers](prime-number-split.md) in Book IX, Proposition 20. These are documented ancient treatments, rather than evidence that all of number theory began with one author.
