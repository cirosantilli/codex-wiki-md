<h1 id="2/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

For real $k^2(x)$, the [wave equation](../../../../wave-equation-split.md) has the conserved [Wronskian flux](../../../../wronskian-flux.md)

$$
\mathcal J=\frac{1}{2i}(E^*E'-EE'^*)=\operatorname{Im}(E^*E').
$$

For this [ordinary plasma wave](../../../../ordinary-plasma-wave.md), the time-averaged [Poynting flux](../../../../poynting-flux.md) is $\langle S_x\rangle=c^2\mathcal J/(8\pi\omega)$. With no incident [wave](../../../../wave-split.md) from the right, evaluating the same [Wronskian flux](../../../../wronskian-flux.md) on both sides proves

$$
\boxed{R+T=1,\qquad R=|r|^2,\quad T=|t|^2.}
$$

The coefficients in [f](../f.md) also satisfy this identity algebraically, before the opaque-barrier expansion.

A [plasma wave resonance](../../../../plasma-wave-resonance.md) makes the ideal cold description singular and requires an appropriate causal regularization. A passive resonant layer can absorb [wave energy](../../../../wave-energy.md) into particle motion, or convert it into another branch. For the two measured outgoing channels one then has

$$
\boxed{R+T=1-A\le1,}
$$

with $A>0$ when resonant absorption or uncounted [mode conversion](../../../../mode-conversion.md) occurs. A numerical value of $A$ requires the layer physics; a resonance by itself does not specify a universal loss fraction. Counting all converted and absorbed [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) preserves [conservation of energy](../../../../conservation-of-energy.md).
