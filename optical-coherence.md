# Optical coherence

↑ **Parent:** [Physical optics](physical-optics-split.md)

[Optical coherence](optical-coherence.md) describes correlations between the [wave amplitudes](wave-amplitude.md) and [wave phases](phase-waves.md) of [light](light.md) sampled at different places or times. Its most direct classical test is [wave interference](interference-wave-propagation.md): combine two samples of a field and see how much the detected [light intensity](light-intensity.md) changes when their relative [wave phase](phase-waves.md) is scanned. [First-order optical coherence](first-order-optical-coherence.md) makes this statement quantitative; [temporal optical coherence](temporal-optical-coherence.md) compares delayed samples, while [spatial optical coherence](spatial-optical-coherence.md) compares different positions. These properties are relative to the samples and averaging procedure, rather than a single yes-or-no label for a source. [Rüdiger Paschotta, RP Photonics Encyclopedia, Coherence: field correlations and the two kinds of coherence](https://www.rp-photonics.com/coherence.html).

A useful surprise is that two copies of a fluctuating source can interfere strongly when they have matched delays: the copies share the fluctuations. Conversely, two individually steady-looking beams need not have correlated [wave phases](phase-waves.md). The ideal [diffraction grating](diffraction-grating.md) calculation requires [spatial optical coherence](spatial-optical-coherence.md) across its illuminated elements; unequal-arm measurements with a [Michelson interferometer](michelson-interferometer.md) test [temporal optical coherence](temporal-optical-coherence.md).
