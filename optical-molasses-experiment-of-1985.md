# Optical molasses experiment of 1985

↑ **Parent:** [Optical molasses](optical-molasses.md)

[Steven Chu](steven-chu.md)'s Bell Laboratories team prepared [sodium](sodium.md) atoms by pulsed-[laser](laser-split.md) evaporation of a pellet. A frequency-swept opposing beam slowed them before six crossed [laser](laser-split.md) beams supplied [optical molasses](optical-molasses.md). The [vacuum](vacuum.md) chamber used a shield coated with [graphite](graphite.md) and cooled with liquid [nitrogen](nitrogen.md) as a pump. [Fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) provided the readout; the initial beams were aligned as closely counterpropagating as possible. [Steven Chu, The manipulation of neutral particles, 1998 publication of his Nobel lecture, pp. 687–690, Figs. 3–4 and footnote 5: sodium source, precooling, vacuum apparatus, beam geometry and fluorescence detection](the-manipulation-of-neutral-particles-by-steven-chu.md).

After equilibration, the team switched off the cooling [light](light.md) for a variable interval, then restored it. Faster atoms escaped during the dark interval; slower atoms were recaptured. The recaptured fraction versus delay constrained the [velocity](velocity.md) distribution and hence [temperature](temperature-split.md). The inference depended on the initial cloud shape and recapture volume: [Steven Chu](steven-chu.md) later acknowledged adjusting a geometry correction towards the expected [Doppler cooling limit](doppler-cooling-limit.md). Delay was the scanned variable, and the cooling beams themselves supplied the release/recapture control; visible [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) alone could not establish [temperature](temperature-split.md). [Steven Chu, The manipulation of neutral particles, p. 688, release-and-recapture paragraph: measurement sequence and retrospective account of the geometry correction](the-manipulation-of-neutral-particles-by-steven-chu.md).
