# Wheatstone bridge

↑ **Parent:** [Resistance bridge](resistance-bridge.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheatstone_bridge)

The Wheatstone bridge has four [resistor](resistor.md) arms and two diagonals. Here the supply holds node $A$ at constant [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $V_s>0$ relative to node $D$, our [electrical reference potential](electrical-reference-potential.md). The left path is $A\to R_1\to B\to R_2\to D$; the right path is $A\to R_3\to C\to R_x\to D$. The unknown is $R_x$, and $R_3$ is adjustable. A [voltmeter](voltmeter.md) or [galvanometer](galvanometer.md) connects $B$ to $C$. We define its positive reading as $V_B-V_C$; exchanging its leads reverses the sign. All four [electrical resistances](electrical-resistance.md) are positive, the supply is ideal, and the calculation describes steady [electric currents](electric-current.md) at fixed [temperature](temperature.md).

[Source](wheatstone-bridge-circuit-and-thevenin-equivalent.py). The supply spans $A,D$; the detector spans $B,C$ and reads $V_B-V_C$. The right panel uses [Thévenin's theorem](thevenin-s-theorem.md) to replace the bridge by its open-circuit [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) and a series [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md).

[Charles Wheatstone](charles-wheatstone.md) described the instrument in his 1843 Bakerian lecture while explicitly crediting [Samuel Hunter Christie](samuel-hunter-christie.md). The account from King's College London's collection identifies that attribution; the familiar name should not be read as an assertion that Wheatstone alone originated the bridge.[Wheatstone Collection, section “Wheatstone's contributions to electricity”: 1843 lecture and credit to Samuel Hunter Christie](wheatstone-collection-by-king-s-college-london.md)
