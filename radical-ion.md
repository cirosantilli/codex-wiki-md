# Radical ion

↑ **Parent:** [Radical (chemistry)](radical-chemistry.md)

A [radical ion](radical-ion.md) is both a [radical](radical-chemistry.md) and an [ion](ion.md). Removing one [electron](electron.md) from neutral [benzene](benzene.md) can produce $\mathrm{C_6H_6}^{\bullet+}$, a [radical cation](radical-cation.md); adding one can produce $\mathrm{C_6H_6}^{\bullet-}$, a [radical anion](radical-anion.md). These formulae specify net [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) and an unpaired [electron](electron.md), without claiming that both reside on one [chemical atom](atom-physics.md).[Glossary of Terms Used in Physical Organic Chemistry, IUPAC Recommendations 1994: radical ion, benzene examples and dot/charge placement](glossary-of-terms-used-in-physical-organic-chemistry-iupac-recommendations-1994-r-entries-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry.md)
