↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

**The [equivalence principle](../../../../../equivalence-principle.md) connects the universality of freely falling motion to the local description of gravity.** In Newtonian language a test body's response to a given field is proportional to $m_g/m_i$. Equality of that ratio for different non-gravitating test bodies gives the [weak equivalence principle](../../../../../weak-equivalence-principle.md): identical initial conditions yield identical free-fall trajectories, independent of composition. Negligible nongravitational forces and negligible disturbance of the background are part of the test-body limit.

The [Einstein equivalence principle](../../../../../einstein-equivalence-principle.md) adds [local Lorentz invariance](../../../../../local-lorentz-invariance.md) and [local position invariance](../../../../../local-position-invariance.md) for nongravitational experiments. A sufficiently small freely falling laboratory obeys the local laws of [special relativity](../../../../../special-relativity-split.md) independently of its spacetime location and freely falling velocity. Uniform acceleration of a small laboratory can imitate the leading local effects attributed to a gravitational field. An observer held at rest in a gravitational field generally needs [proper acceleration](../../../../../proper-acceleration.md); a freely falling observer does not.

Mathematically, local inertial coordinates can set the metric to Minkowski form and the [Christoffel symbols](../../../../../christoffel-symbol.md) to zero at an event. The [geodesic equation](../../../../../geodesic-equation.md) there has no connection term at that point:

$$
\frac{\mathrm d^2x^\mu}{\mathrm d\tau^2}
+\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}
\frac{\mathrm dx^\alpha}{\mathrm d\tau}
\frac{\mathrm dx^\beta}{\mathrm d\tau}=0.
$$

This coordinate choice does not remove curvature. With a standard curvature convention, nearby free-fall trajectories have relative acceleration

$$
\frac{D^2\xi^\mu}{\mathrm d\tau^2}
=-R^\mu{}_{\nu\alpha\beta}u^\nu\xi^\alpha u^\beta.
$$

The tidal signal survives in an extended laboratory. Thus the principle is local and does not identify every gravitational field with acceleration globally. Finite-size spin and tidal couplings also qualify the ideal point-test-body statement.

The [strong equivalence principle](../../../../../strong-equivalence-principle.md) extends the universality to gravitational experiments and bodies whose gravitational self-energy is important. It is stronger than the Einstein principle and need not hold in every metric theory that satisfies local nongravitational equivalence. Comparing the [Earth](../../../../../earth.md) and [Moon](../../../../../moon.md) as they fall toward the [Sun](../../../../../sun.md) probes this distinction as well as composition effects. Lunar laser-ranging analyses test differential acceleration by fitting the lunar orbit and measured ranges; an interpretation in terms of self-energy requires separating ordinary composition-dependent violations.[2012 abstract, differential Earth-Moon free fall and dependence on composition and gravitational self-energy](../../../../../lunar-laser-ranging-tests-of-the-equivalence-principle-by-james-g-williams-and-others.md)

[Redshift](../../../../../redshift.md) and clock comparisons test [local position invariance](../../../../../local-position-invariance.md); laboratory Lorentz tests and universality-of-free-fall experiments test other components. These are complementary tests, not deductions from a single numerical equality of inertial and [gravitational mass](../../../../../gravitational-mass.md). The principle motivates geometric gravity and minimal coupling, but does not by itself derive the complete Einstein field equations or determine the value of the [gravitational constant](../../../../../gravitational-constant.md).
