**[Gravitational lensing](../../../../../gravitational-lensing-split.md) maps source directions into observed image directions because spacetime curvature deflects [null geodesics](../../../../../null-geodesic.md).** In the weak-field limit the deflection by a point mass at impact parameter $b$ is

$$
\widehat\alpha=\frac{4GM}{c^2b}.
$$

For a thin lens, [angular diameter distances](../../../../../angular-diameter-distance.md) $D_l,D_s,D_{ls}$ relate the angles through

$$
\boldsymbol\beta=\boldsymbol\theta-
\frac{D_{ls}}{D_s}\widehat{\boldsymbol\alpha}(D_l\boldsymbol\theta).
$$

A point lens has Einstein angle

$$
\boxed{\theta_E^2=\frac{4GM}{c^2}\frac{D_{ls}}{D_lD_s},}
$$

and in a one-dimensional source alignment its two images satisfy

$$
\theta_\pm=\frac12\left(\beta\pm\sqrt{\beta^2+4\theta_E^2}\right).
$$

Exact alignment gives an [Einstein ring](../../../../../einstein-ring.md) for an ideal circular lens; finite source size and departures from symmetry alter the ideal image.

The mapping's Jacobian determines area magnification and parity. Its determinant can vanish on critical curves, whose source-plane images are caustics; a source near a caustic can produce highly stretched arcs or changing image multiplicity. In [geometric optics](../../../../../geometrical-optics-split.md), gravity is achromatic and preserves [surface brightness](../../../../../surface-brightness.md) after the usual [redshift](../../../../../redshift.md) factors, while changing the apparent solid angle and total flux. Frequency-dependent source size, extinction and [plasma](../../../../../plasma-physics.md) propagation can modify an observed broadband image without changing this basic gravitational law.

Strong lensing produces multiple images, arcs and relative time delays. In angular variables the delay surface is proportional to

$$
\Delta t(\boldsymbol\theta)=
\frac{1+z_l}{c}\frac{D_lD_s}{D_{ls}}
\left[\frac12|\boldsymbol\theta-\boldsymbol\beta|^2-
\psi(\boldsymbol\theta)\right],
$$

up to an irrelevant additive constant. The geometrical path and [gravitational potential](../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md) delay both contribute. Monitoring a variable multiply imaged source can constrain the [time-delay distance](../../../../../time-delay-distance.md), provided the lens mass distribution and external convergence are modeled.

Weak lensing produces small coherent distortions in background [galaxy](../../../../../galaxy-split.md) shapes. Convergence describes isotropic focusing and shear describes anisotropic stretching. In a thin-lens convention,

$$
\kappa=\frac{\Sigma}{\Sigma_{\rm crit}},\qquad
\Sigma_{\rm crit}=\frac{c^2}{4\pi G}\frac{D_s}{D_lD_{ls}}.
$$

Statistical shear correlations probe projected matter structure and cosmological growth; intrinsic alignments, shape noise and selection require control. Compact masses can instead produce microlensing magnification changes when individual image separations are unresolved.

Lensing weighs both luminous and dark gravitating matter and can reveal mass concentrations without requiring hydrostatic equilibrium. It is nevertheless a projected and model-dependent inference: the [mass-sheet degeneracy](../../../../../mass-sheet-degeneracy.md), line-of-sight structure and uncertain source [redshifts](../../../../../redshift.md) can mimic changes in inferred mass or distance. Strong, weak and microlensing therefore constrain different scales and complement dynamical, photometric and cosmological measurements rather than automatically recovering a unique three-dimensional mass map.
