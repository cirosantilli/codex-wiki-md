<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[cosmic inflation](../../../../../cosmic-inflation-split.md) is an early interval of accelerated expansion, $\ddot R>0$. For expansion this means a shrinking [comoving Hubble radius](../../../../../comoving-hubble-radius.md), $d[(RH)^{-1}]/dt<0$. A nearly constant positive [scalar potential](../../../../../scalar-potential.md) can supply it: in $c=1$ units,

$$
\rho_\phi=\frac{\dot\phi^2}{2}+V(\phi),\qquad
P_\phi=\frac{\dot\phi^2}{2}-V(\phi).
$$

Potential domination gives $\rho_\phi+3P_\phi<0$ and approximately exponential expansion. Sustaining the interval requires sufficiently slow evolution, not just setting the initial field velocity to zero on a steep potential.

The [horizon problem](../../../../../horizon-problem.md) is the near equality of conditions in widely separated microwave-background regions that could not equilibrate in the standard decelerating hot [Big Bang](../../../../../big-bang.md) history. [cosmic inflation](../../../../../cosmic-inflation-split.md) permits the currently observed patch to descend from an earlier region small enough to have been causally connected, before that patch is stretched to enormous size. The claim concerns a causal history extended before the conventional hot phase; merely accelerating a collection of already disconnected regions would not explain their common [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md).

For the [flatness problem](../../../../../flatness-problem.md), the Friedmann constraint gives

$$
|\Omega_{\rm tot}-1|=\frac{|k|c^2}{R^2H^2}.
$$

A growing $RH$ drives this curvature fraction toward zero, whereas ordinary matter- or radiation-dominated deceleration makes it grow. [cosmic inflation](../../../../../cosmic-inflation-split.md) therefore suppresses observable curvature without requiring an equally extreme initial tuning within the later hot evolution.

Pre-existing monopoles and other unwanted relics are also diluted by the volume increase: a conserved particle number [density](../../../../../density.md) falls as $e^{-3N}$ over $N$ e-folds. The transition to the hot universe must reheat efficiently, and its [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) must not regenerate an excluded relic population. The required number of e-folds is model and [reheating](../../../../../reheating.md) dependent; “about sixty” is a useful scale for many scenarios rather than a universal exact requirement.

[cosmic inflation](../../../../../cosmic-inflation-split.md) also supplies a mechanism for perturbations. [quantum fluctuations](../../../../../quantum-fluctuation.md) are stretched beyond the Hubble [radius](../../../../../radius.md), and nearly scale-invariant [comoving curvature perturbations](../../../../../comoving-curvature-perturbation.md) can later seed cosmic structure. Simple single-field slow-roll models commonly predict approximately adiabatic, nearly Gaussian fluctuations, with a small spectral tilt; tensor perturbations would provide an additional test. Acoustic coherence and the observed scalar spectrum are compatible with this account, but they do not single out one [inflaton](../../../../../inflaton.md) potential. **[cosmic inflation](../../../../../cosmic-inflation-split.md) addresses causal homogeneity, curvature and relic dilution while motivating the initial perturbations; it does not automatically solve the [cosmological constant](../../../../../cosmological-constant.md) problem or determine [reheating](../../../../../reheating.md).**
