<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A positive [cosmological constant](../../../../../cosmological-constant.md) contributes a constant [vacuum energy](../../../../../vacuum-energy.md) density and negative pressure. In the time-unit convention of Question 2,

$$
\rho_\Lambda=\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G},\qquad p_\Lambda=-\rho_\Lambda c^2,\qquad
\frac{\ddot R}{R}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3}\left(\rho+\frac{3p}{c^2}\right)+\frac{\Lambda}{3}.
$$

It therefore causes [accelerating expansion](../../../../../accelerating-expansion-of-the-universe.md) once its negative-pressure effect outweighs the decelerating components. Its density stays constant while matter dilutes, so a small early contribution can dominate late. For an isolated positive-vacuum component the [scale factor](../../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md) grows exponentially. The [cosmological constant](../../../../../cosmological-constant.md) problem is the mismatch between the small inferred vacuum density and generic quantum-field vacuum-energy expectations, rather than a failure of the background solution itself.

The key direct expansion evidence came from [Type Ia supernova](../../../../../type-ia-supernova.md) distance-redshift measurements. After light-curve and colour standardization, distant events were fainter than expected in simple decelerating matter-only models, indicating larger [luminosity distances](../../../../../luminosity-distance.md). Riess and collaborators' 1998 analysis and Perlmutter and collaborators' independent 42-supernova study favoured an accelerating low-matter-density cosmology with a positive vacuum contribution. Dust, source evolution, selection effects, [photometric calibration](../../../../../photometric-calibration.md) and [peculiar velocities](../../../../../peculiar-velocity.md) must be controlled: faintness alone is not a model-independent measurement of $\Lambda$.[abstract; distance fitting and systematic-error discussion](../../../../../observational-evidence-from-supernovae-for-an-accelerating-universe-and-a-cosmological-constant-by-adam-riess-and-others.md)[abstract; cosmological likelihoods and systematic checks](../../../../../measurements-of-omega-and-lambda-from-42-high-redshift-supernovae-by-saul-perlmutter-and-others.md)

[cosmic microwave background](../../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-split.md) acoustic geometry and independent matter-density constraints help break the supernova parameter degeneracy: nearly flat geometry with substantially less than critical matter requires another component within the FLRW model. [large-scale structure of the universe](../../../../../large-scale-structure-of-the-universe-split.md) and [distance](../../../../../distance.md) measurements provide further consistency tests. **The combined evidence supports a dark-energy contribution compatible with a constant; [accelerating expansion](../../../../../accelerating-expansion-of-the-universe.md) by itself does not prove exactly constant [vacuum energy](../../../../../vacuum-energy.md) or rule out every alternative gravity model.**
