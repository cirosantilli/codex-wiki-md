<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The [age of the universe](../../../../../age-of-the-universe.md) is a model-dependent elapsed [cosmic time](../../../../../cosmic-time.md), not simply the inverse present [Hubble constant](../../../../../hubble-constant.md). For a model with a hot [Big Bang](../../../../../big-bang.md) origin,

$$
\boxed{t_0=\int_0^\infty\frac{dz}{(1+z)H(z)}}.
$$

An [Einstein-de Sitter universe](../../../../../einstein-de-sitter-universe.md) gives $t_0=2/(3H_0)$. The [age of a flat matter-Lambda universe](../../../../../age-of-a-flat-matter-lambda-universe.md), neglecting early [radiation](../../../../../radiation.md), is

$$
t_0=\frac{2}{3H_0\sqrt{\Omega_{\Lambda0}}}
\operatorname{arsinh}\sqrt{\frac{\Omega_{\Lambda0}}{\Omega_{m0}}},\qquad
\Omega_{m0}=1-\Omega_{\Lambda0}.
$$

This follows by integrating $dt=da/(aH)$ and substituting $x=\sqrt{\Omega_{\Lambda0}/\Omega_{m0}}\,(a/a_0)^{3/2}$. It tends to $2/(3H_0)$ as the vacuum fraction tends to zero. Pure [de Sitter spacetime](../../../../../de-sitter-spacetime.md) instead has constant $H$ and no finite dust [Big Bang](../../../../../big-bang.md) age obtained from this integral. [cosmic microwave background](../../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-split.md) ages therefore depend on the assumed expansion model and fitted parameters; the [WMAP](../../../../../wilkinson-microwave-anisotropy-probe.md) nine-year combined-data [Lambda-CDM model](../../../../../lambda-cdm-model.md) inference was approximately 13.8 billion years, not a direct clock reading.[Version 1 abstract, inferred cosmic age](../../../../../nine-year-wilkinson-microwave-anisotropy-probe-wmap-observations-final-maps-and-results-by-c-l-bennett-and-others.md)

Old [globular clusters](../../../../../globular-cluster.md) provide an independent lower bound. Their [main-sequence turnoff](../../../../../main-sequence-turnoff.md) identifies stars just exhausting core hydrogen; the nuclear fuel divided by [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) sets a mass-dependent lifetime, so a fitted [colour-magnitude diagram](../../../../../colour-magnitude-diagram.md) constrains the common [stellar age](../../../../../stellar-age.md). Distance, extinction, [metallicity](../../../../../metallicity.md), helium abundance, diffusion and stellar-model physics introduce uncertainties. The elapsed time before cluster formation must be added when comparing with the cosmic age.[Abstract and joint stellar-age/cosmological interpretation](../../../../../new-globular-cluster-age-estimates-and-constraints-on-the-cosmic-equation-of-state-and-the-matter-density-of-the-universe-by-lawrence-m-krauss-and-brian-chaboyer.md)

[white-dwarf cooling ages](../../../../../white-dwarf-cooling-age.md) offer another clock: faint remnants have lost more stored thermal [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), but progenitor lifetimes must be added and [crystallization](../../../../../crystallization.md), envelope properties and sample completeness affect the inference. [nucleocosmochronology](../../../../../nucleocosmochronology.md) uses long-lived radioactive abundance ratios, with $Q/Q_0=e^{-(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)t}$ for two radioactive species, or a stable reference with $\lambda_2=0$. Uncertain initial production ratios and subsequent enrichment limit precision. **Every reliably dated object must be younger than the universe by at least its formation delay**; consistency among distinct clocks tests both cosmology and their astrophysical calibrations.
