# Quasi-inverse of a quasi-isometry

↑ **Parent:** [Quasi-isometry](quasi-isometry.md)

For a [quasi-isometry](quasi-isometry.md) $f:X\to Y$ with multiplicative constant $\lambda$ and additive/coarse-surjectivity constant $\epsilon$, choose $f'(y)$ with $d_Y(f(f'(y)),y)\le\epsilon$. The triangle inequality and the two distortion inequalities give

$$
\lambda^{-1}d_Y(y,y')-3\epsilon/\lambda\le d_X(f'(y),f'(y'))\le\lambda d_Y(y,y')+3\lambda\epsilon.
$$

Also $d_X(f'(f(x)),x)\le2\lambda\epsilon$. Hence $f'$ is a [quasi-isometry](quasi-isometry.md) and both composites stay uniformly close to their respective identities. No continuity is required.
