# Bragg path difference

↑ **Parent:** [Bragg's law](bragg-s-law.md)

Compare two rays scattered into parallel outgoing directions from neighboring [lattice planes](lattice-plane.md). In the diagram, $A$ lies on the upper plane and $B$ on the lower plane, directly beneath $A$ with $AB=d$. Draw through $A$ the incoming and outgoing equal-[wave phase](phase-waves.md) fronts, perpendicular to their respective rays. They meet the lower ray at $C$ and $D$. Each right triangle gives $CB=BD=d\sin\theta$, so the lower ray travels the extra distance

$$
\Delta\ell=CB+BD=2d\sin\theta.
$$

The resulting [phase difference](phase-difference.md) is $\Delta\varphi=2\pi\Delta\ell/\lambda$. [Constructive interference](constructive-interference.md) requires $\Delta\varphi=2\pi n$, which proves [Bragg's law](bragg-s-law.md) in this ray construction. Comparing equal-[wave phase](phase-waves.md) fronts, rather than arbitrary ray endpoints, is what makes the distance comparison meaningful.

The orange segments give the extra path $CB+BD$; dashed segments compare equal-[wave phase](phase-waves.md) fronts. The separate angle sketch shows why the outgoing beam is deflected by $2\theta$ when the [Bragg angle](bragg-angle.md) is $\theta$. The illustrated angle is schematic.

The same geometry connects to the [reciprocal lattice](reciprocal-lattice.md). With [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) $k=2\pi/\lambda$, the [elastic Bragg scattering condition](elastic-bragg-scattering-condition.md) gives a transfer magnitude $|q|=2k\sin\theta$. A reciprocal vector normal to these [lattice planes](lattice-plane.md) has $|q|=2\pi n/d$. Equating them again yields [Bragg's law](bragg-s-law.md). The ray and [reciprocal lattice](reciprocal-lattice.md) descriptions therefore express the same [wave phase](phase-waves.md) matching.
