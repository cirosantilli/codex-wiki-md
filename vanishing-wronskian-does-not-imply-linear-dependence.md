# Vanishing Wronskian does not imply linear dependence

↑ **Parent:** [Wronskian](wronskian.md)

Two [differentiable functions](differentiable-function.md) can be [linearly independent](linear-independence.md) on a connected [interval](interval-mathematics.md) while their [Wronskian](wronskian.md) vanishes everywhere. For example, let $f(x)=(\max\{-x,0\})^2$ and $g(x)=(\max\{x,0\})^2$. Both have a continuous first [derivative](derivative.md), and their values and first derivatives vanish at zero. On either side of zero one of the functions is identically zero, so $fg'-f'g=0$ everywhere. Evaluating a constant [linear combination](linear-combination.md) $Af+Bg=0$ at $-1$ and $1$ forces $A=B=0$.

The obstruction is that the two halves supply independent information without simultaneous nonzero value-and-derivative data at any point. For solutions of a common regular [second-order linear differential equation](second-order-linear-differential-equation.md), the [uniqueness theorem for ordinary differential equations](uniqueness-theorem-for-ordinary-differential-equations.md) removes this possibility; [Abel's identity](abel-s-identity.md) gives the zero-or-nowhere-zero alternative for their [Wronskian](wronskian.md).
