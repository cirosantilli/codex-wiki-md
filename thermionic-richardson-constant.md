# Thermionic Richardson constant

↑ **Parent:** [Richardson-Dushman law](richardson-dushman-law.md)

The coefficient $A$ has dimensions of [electric current density](current-density.md) divided by squared [temperature](temperature-split.md). For ideal [nonrelativistic particles](nonrelativistic-particle.md) with the free [electron mass](electron-mass.md), two [spin](spin.md) states, a planar uniform surface, and unit escape transmission, the [thermionic tail-flux derivation](thermionic-tail-flux-derivation.md) gives

$$
A_0=\frac{4\pi m_e e k_B^2}{h^3}
\simeq1.202\times10^6\,\mathrm{A\,m^{-2}\,K^{-2}}
=120.2\,\mathrm{A\,cm^{-2}\,K^{-2}},
$$

where $e$ is the positive [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md) and $h$ is the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md). The two area units differ by a factor of $10^4$. A fitted $A$ need not equal $A_0$: reflection at the surface, the [electronic band structure](electronic-band-structure.md), and uncertain emitting area affect the inferred coefficient. A constant escape transmission $\tau$ would give $A=\tau A_0$ in this simple model; an [energy](energy-split.md)-dependent transmission generally requires an integral instead.

The name joins [Owen Willans Richardson](owen-willans-richardson.md) with [Saul Dushman](saul-dushman.md). Dushman's 1923 paper develops a thermodynamic treatment of [electron](electron.md) emission analogous to [evaporation](evaporation.md) and evaluates the prefactor using quantum ideas.[Abstract, general equation and theoretical evaluation of A](electron-emission-from-metals-as-a-function-of-temperature-by-saul-dushman.md)
