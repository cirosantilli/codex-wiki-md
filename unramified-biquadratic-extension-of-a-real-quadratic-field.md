# Unramified biquadratic extension of a real quadratic field

↑ **Parent:** [Biquadratic extension](biquadratic-extension.md)

For distinct primes $p,q\equiv1\pmod4$, put $E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt p,\sqrt q)$ and $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{pq})$. The [algebraic integers](algebraic-integer.md) $a=(1+\sqrt p)/2$ and $b=(1+\sqrt q)/2$ generate an order whose basis has [discriminant of elements of a number field](discriminant-of-elements-of-a-number-field.md) $p^2q^2$: the [trace pairing](trace-pairing.md) matrix is, up to basis order, the tensor product of the quadratic trace matrices of determinants $p$ and $q$. If its index in $\mathcal O_E$ is $j$, the [discriminant-index formula for an integral lattice](discriminant-index-formula-for-an-integral-lattice.md) and the [relative discriminant](relative-discriminant.md) tower formula give

$$
p^2q^2/j^2=d_E=(pq)^2N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak d_{E/K}).
$$

Both $j$ and this [ideal norm](ideal-norm.md) are positive integers, forcing $j=1$ and $\mathfrak d_{E/K}=\mathcal O_K$. Thus $E/K$ is [unramified](unramified-extension.md) at all finite primes. It is also totally real, so no real place ramifies. This quadratic abelian extension is contained in the ordinary [Hilbert class field](hilbert-class-field.md) of $K$, and [Artin reciprocity](artin-reciprocity-law.md) gives a quotient of its [ideal class group](ideal-class-group.md) of order two. The extensions $K(\sqrt p)$ and $K(\sqrt q)$ are equal to $E$, not two different extensions of $K$.
