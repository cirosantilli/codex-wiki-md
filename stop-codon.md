# Stop codon

↑ **Parent:** [Codon](codon.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_codon)

In the standard [genetic code](genetic-code.md), UAA, UAG and UGA are [stop codons](stop-codon.md): they signal termination of [biological translation](translation-biology.md) rather than an [amino acid](amino-acid.md) assignment. See [Clark, Douglas and Choi: stop codons](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/15-1-the-genetic-code).

A [stop codon](stop-codon.md) supplies no additional [amino acid residue](amino-acid-residue.md). In the constructed AUG GCU UUC UGA example, replacing UGA by UAA preserves termination after the same three [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md). Replacing UUC by UAA instead terminates after [methionine](methionine.md) and [alanine](alanine.md). These are consequences of the stated [genetic code](genetic-code.md), not measurements of expression efficiency.

At a standard [stop codon](stop-codon.md), a [release factor](release-factor.md) promotes release of the [polypeptide](polypeptide.md) from the [ribosome](ribosome.md). Ordinary delivery of an [amino acid](amino-acid.md) by [transfer RNA](transfer-rna.md) does not supply a “stop [amino acid](amino-acid.md)”.[§15.5, Translation, Elongation, and Termination, paragraph beginning “Termination of translation”: stop codons and release factors](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others.md)
