# Expression (mathematics)

↑ **Parent:** [Algebra](algebra-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expression_(mathematics))

A [mathematical expression](expression-mathematics.md) combines symbols according to formation rules, such as $2x+1$, $(x+1)^2$, or $\sqrt{x}$. With values for its [variables](variable-mathematics.md) and meanings for its [algebraic operations](algebraic-operation-split.md), it can be evaluated. An [equation](equation.md) relates two [mathematical expressions](expression-mathematics.md); an expression alone need not assert anything.

For example, $2x+3$ evaluates to seven at $x=2$ and nine at $x=3$. Neither value solves anything until a condition is imposed: the [equation](equation.md) $2x+3=9$ selects $x=3$ from the [real numbers](real-number.md). This distinction is useful when moving from a formula to a question about which inputs satisfy it. Evaluation by replacing the [variables](variable-mathematics.md) with assigned values is described in [College Algebra 2e, §1.1: Evaluating Algebraic Expressions](https://openstax.org/books/college-algebra-2e/pages/1-1-real-numbers-algebra-essentials).

Different written [mathematical expressions](expression-mathematics.md) can have the same value for every input in a common [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md). The [distributive property](distributive-property.md) expands $(x+1)^2$ to $x^2+2x+1$, while factorizing reverses that change of form. The [mathematical identity](identity-mathematics.md) between them lets one choose the form suited to the next task: the factored form exposes a [multiple root](multiple-root.md) at $x=-1$, and the expanded form exposes its [coefficients](coefficient.md). Simplifying should preserve values on the stated domain, rather than silently enlarging it.
