# Meso compound

↑ **Parent:** [Chirality (chemistry)](chirality-chemistry.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meso_compound)

A [meso compound](meso-compound.md) is an [achiral](achirality.md) [molecule](molecule-split.md) despite having [stereocenters](stereocenter.md). [Tartaric acid](tartaric-acid.md), $\mathrm{HOOC{-}CH(OH){-}CH(OH){-}COOH}$, has two tetrahedral [carbon](carbon.md) [stereocenters](stereocenter.md). Its $(2R,3R)$ and $(2S,3S)$ forms are [enantiomers](enantiomer.md), but $(2R,3S)$ and $(2S,3R)$ name the same meso form after equivalent ends are identified. A symmetric spatial arrangement has a mirror plane exchanging its two halves. Thus there are three [stereoisomers](stereoisomer.md), not four.[McMurry, Organic Chemistry, §5.7, Figure 5.12: meso-tartaric acid and its symmetry plane](organic-chemistry-by-john-mcmurry/5-7-meso-compounds.md)

Opposite [R and S configurations](r-and-s-configuration.md) alone do not guarantee a [meso compound](meso-compound.md): the rest of the [molecule](molecule-split.md) must permit mirror superposition. Likewise, a [meso compound](meso-compound.md) is one [achiral](achirality.md) [chemical compound](chemical-compound.md), whereas a [racemate](racemate.md) is a mixture of two [chiral molecule](chiral-molecule.md) populations. Both can give zero [optical rotation](optical-rotation.md) for different reasons.
