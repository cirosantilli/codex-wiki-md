# Two images of a point-mass lens

↑ **Parent:** [Point-mass gravitational lens](point-mass-gravitational-lens.md)

Choose a signed angular coordinate along the source-lens line. The [point-mass gravitational lens](point-mass-gravitational-lens.md) becomes

$$
\beta=\theta-\frac{\theta_E^2}{\theta},\qquad\theta\ne0.
$$

For $\beta\ne0$, multiplication by $\theta$ gives the [quadratic equation](quadratic-equation.md) $\theta^2-\beta\theta-\theta_E^2=0$. Its positive [discriminant](discriminant.md) yields the two displayed roots. Their product is $\theta_+\theta_-=-\theta_E^2$, so they lie on opposite sides of the lens. With $\beta>0$, $\theta_+>\theta_E$ and $-\theta_E<\theta_-<0$: the outer image lies on the source side. For $\beta<0$, the outer image is instead the minus root. These are image directions seen by the observer, not two emitting objects.[Section 2.2, printed pp. 15-16, equations (100)-(110): point-mass model, two roots, critical circle and magnification](gravitational-lensing-by-matthias-bartelmann.md)

For a dimensionless example, set $u=\beta/\theta_E=0.6$ and $x=\theta/\theta_E$. Solving $u=x-1/x$ gives $x_+\simeq1.344$ and $x_-\simeq-0.744$, with separation $\sqrt{u^2+4}\simeq2.088$. The [lensing magnification](lensing-magnification.md) follows from [image parity in gravitational lensing](image-parity-in-gravitational-lensing.md): $\mu_+\simeq1.442$, $\mu_-\simeq-0.442$, and total amplification $\simeq1.884$. Changing $M$ or the distances rescales the angles through the [Einstein angular radius](einstein-angular-radius.md), while these dimensionless values remain the same.

For $u\gg1$, the [quadratic equation](quadratic-equation.md) gives $x_+\simeq u+1/u$ and $x_-\simeq-1/u$. Using $\mu=1/(1-x^{-4})$, the outer image tends to unit [lensing magnification](lensing-magnification.md) and the inner image has $|\mu_-|\simeq u^{-4}$. Two mathematical images can therefore look like one at finite sensitivity or [angular resolution](angular-resolution.md). Furthermore, the inner [impact parameter](impact-parameter.md) eventually becomes too small for the stated [weak-field approximation](weak-field-approximation.md) or passes through the opaque lens: the ideal formula must then be replaced by an appropriate physical model.
