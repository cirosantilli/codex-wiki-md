# Nuclear elastic electron scattering kinematics

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear recoil](nuclear-recoil.md)

An [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md) event leaves the nuclear internal [quantum state](quantum-state.md) unchanged while transferring [energy](energy-split.md) to its motion. Let a target of [mass](mass.md) $M$ initially be at rest. Neglect the [electron](electron.md) rest [mass](mass.md), but retain relativistic target recoil. [Conservation of momentum](momentum-conservation.md) and [conservation of relativistic energy](conservation-of-relativistic-energy.md) give

$$
\hbar^2c^2q^2=E^2+E'^2-2EE'\cos\theta,
\qquad
(Mc^2+E-E')^2=M^2c^4+\hbar^2c^2q^2.
$$

Eliminating $q$ yields $Mc^2(E-E')=EE'(1-\cos\theta)$ and hence

$$
E'=\frac{E}{1+\dfrac{2E}{Mc^2}\sin^2(\theta/2)},\qquad
T_R=E-E'.
$$

The static relation $q=2E\sin(\theta/2)/(\hbar c)$ follows only when $E/(Mc^2)$ is negligible. For small recoil, $T_R\simeq\hbar^2q^2/(2M)$; this last approximation is nonrelativistic for the recoiling [nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md), not for the incident [electron](electron.md). The invariant spacelike transfer, expressed in energy-squared units, is

$$
Q^2=\hbar^2c^2q^2-(E-E')^2=4EE'\sin^2(\theta/2),
$$

so $Q/(\hbar c)$ and the spatial inverse length $q$ coincide only in the static limit. Recoil kinematics alone does not correct the static [differential scattering cross-section](differential-scattering-cross-section.md): a finite-mass target also changes its kinematic prefactor and the appropriate current matrix elements.
