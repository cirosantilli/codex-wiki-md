# Base pair

↑ **Parent:** [Nucleic acid](nucleic-acid.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_pair)

A [base pair](base-pair.md) is an association of two [nucleobases](nucleotide-base.md) through [hydrogen bonds](hydrogen-bond.md) in a compatible arrangement. It commonly joins opposite strands of a [nucleic acid double helix](nucleic-acid-double-helix.md), but can also join two regions of one folded [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) strand. Canonical Watson–Crick [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) [base pairs](base-pair.md) match [adenine](adenine.md) with [thymine](thymine.md) and [guanine](guanine.md) with [cytosine](cytosine.md); canonical [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) [base pairs](base-pair.md) use [adenine](adenine.md) with [uracil](uracil.md) instead. The [nucleobases](nucleotide-base.md) belong to complete [nucleotides](nucleotide.md), including their attached [sugars](sugar.md) and backbone [phosphate](phosphate.md). See [NHGRI: base pair](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Base-Pair).

The abbreviation bp also measures the length of a paired region. Our ACGTAC/TGCATG duplex has six [base pairs](base-pair.md), containing twelve [nucleotides](nucleotide.md) altogether. Its upper strand contains two [adenine](adenine.md), two [cytosine](cytosine.md), one [guanine](guanine.md), and one [thymine](thymine.md) [nucleobase](nucleotide-base.md); the lower contains one, one, two, and two respectively. Combined, the counts are three of each [nucleobase](nucleotide-base.md). Equality of total [adenine](adenine.md) and [thymine](thymine.md), or of total [guanine](guanine.md) and [cytosine](cytosine.md), need not hold within either individual strand.
