# Beryllium-ion quantum Zeno experiment of 1990

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum Zeno effect](quantum-zeno-effect.md)

In 1990, [Wayne M. Itano](wayne-m-itano.md), [Daniel J. Heinzen](daniel-j-heinzen.md), [J. J. Bollinger](john-j-bollinger.md) and [David J. Wineland](david-j-wineland.md) reported inhibited transitions in about 5,000 $^{9}\mathrm{Be}^{+}$ [ions](ion.md) held in a cylindrical [Penning trap](penning-trap.md). Trapped [magnesium](magnesium.md) [ions](ion.md), illuminated at 280 nm, kept the [beryllium](beryllium.md) [ions](ion.md) cold. A 320.7 MHz [radio frequency](radio-frequency.md) field coupled two ground-state [hyperfine structure](hyperfine-structure.md) sublevels during a 256 ms pulse adjusted for complete transfer. Up to 64 intervening 313 nm pulses addressed an optical transition from level 1: scattering distinguished it from level 2. Afterward, calibrated [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) counts over 100 ms measured the final [quantum state population](quantum-state-population.md). This ensemble readout did not select [ions](ion.md) that remained in the initial [quantum state](quantum-state.md) at every check; departure followed by return also contributes. The apparatus made inhibition by repeated [quantum measurements](quantum-measurement-split.md) experimentally accessible.[§III, p. 2297 and Fig. 2: apparatus, levels, pulses and fluorescence calibration; §V, p. 2299: ensemble measurement. Final-population versus full-record distinction follows from this readout protocol.](quantum-zeno-effect-by-wayne-m-itano-daniel-j-heinzen-john-j-bollinger-and-david-j-wineland.md)
