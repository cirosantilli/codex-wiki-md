# Positive-energy quasiparticle density from a folded spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Density of states](density-of-states.md)

A many-to-one change of energy variable requires summing every inverse branch. For $E(\xi)=\sqrt{\xi^2+\Delta^2}$ and constant normal [density of states](density-of-states.md) $g_F$ on both sides of zero, there are two roots for $E>\Delta$, giving $D_+(E)=2g_FE/\sqrt{E^2-\Delta^2}$. A single branch instead gives $g_FE/\sqrt{E^2-\Delta^2}$. The distinction persists when $\Delta\to0$: folding $\xi$ into $|\xi|$ doubles a two-sided constant density on the positive axis. Spin degeneracy is a separate normalization choice; it must be applied consistently to both normal and transformed densities. The [BCS electronic density of states](bcs-electronic-density-of-states.md) has the single-factor expression because it is spectrally weighted rather than an unweighted branch count.
