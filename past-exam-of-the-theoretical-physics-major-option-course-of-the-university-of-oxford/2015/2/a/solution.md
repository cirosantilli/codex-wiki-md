<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The negative sign of the exchange term and $J>0$ make this a [Heisenberg ferromagnet](../../../../heisenberg-ferromagnet.md). Each classical bond minimizes its energy when its two fixed-length spins are parallel. On the connected [cubic lattice](../../../../cubic-lattice.md) all ground states are therefore uniform:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf S_j=S\mathbf n,\qquad |\mathbf n|=1,}
$$

using length $S$ for the classical large-spin convention. Any two orientations are related by a common spatial rotation of every spin. The global spin-rotation symmetry is $SO(3)$, and a chosen direction retains its $SO(2)$ subgroup of rotations about that axis.

[Spontaneous symmetry breaking](../../../../spontaneous-symmetry-breaking.md) means selecting one member of this continuous set in the [thermodynamic limit](../../../../thermodynamic-limit.md), for example by taking volume to infinity before sending an aligning field to zero. The zero-field [Hamiltonian operator](../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) remains rotationally invariant even though the selected state is not. There are gapless transverse [spin waves](../../../../spin-wave.md). Although two rotation generators are broken, an isotropic ferromagnet has one quadratic [magnon](../../../../magnon.md) branch, whose two transverse spin components form a conjugate dynamical pair. It is not necessary to invent two independent linear-dispersion branches from the number of broken generators. The long-wavelength dispersion is derived in [d](../d.md).
