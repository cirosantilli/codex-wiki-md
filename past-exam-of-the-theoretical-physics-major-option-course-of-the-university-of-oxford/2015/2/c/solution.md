<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a local spin quantized along its positive $\tilde z$ axis, the exact [Holstein–Primakoff transformation](../../../../holstein-primakoff-transformation.md) is

$$
\boxed{\tilde S_j^z=S-n_j,\quad
\tilde S_j^+=\sqrt{2S-n_j}\,a_j,\quad
\tilde S_j^-=a_j^\dagger\sqrt{2S-n_j},\quad n_j=a_j^\dagger a_j.}
$$

The bosonic operators obey $[a_j,a_k^\dagger]=\delta_{jk}$. The physical local subspace is $0\leq n_j\leq2S$; the larger unconstrained boson [Fock space](../../../../fock-space.md) is not itself the finite spin Hilbert space. The ordering of the square roots matters, since $n_j$ does not commute with $a_j$.

This represents spin lowering using a bosonic [creation operator](../../../../creation-operator.md), making a fully polarized spin state the boson vacuum. On the physical subspace it reproduces the spin [commutators](../../../../commutator.md) and the Casimir $\tilde{\mathbf S}_j^2=S(S+1)$. Expanding the square roots for small occupation relative to $S$ organizes a controlled large-$S$ [linear spin-wave approximation](../../../../linear-spin-wave-approximation.md); it is not an assumption that a spin has an infinite number of physical levels.
