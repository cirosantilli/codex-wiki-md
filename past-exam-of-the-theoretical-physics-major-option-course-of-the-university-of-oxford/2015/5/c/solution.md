<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

At an arbitrary nonzero vacuum write $\boldsymbol\phi_*=|v|\mathbf n$, where $\mathbf n^T\mathbf n=1$. The [Hessian matrix](../../../../hessian-matrix.md) of the potential is

$$
\frac{\partial^2V}{\partial\phi_a\partial\phi_b}
=8\lambda\phi_a\phi_b+4\lambda(\boldsymbol\phi^2-v^2)\delta_{ab}.
$$

On the vacuum circle this reduces to

$$
\boxed{M^2=8\lambda v^2\mathbf n\mathbf n^T.}
$$

It has [eigenvalue](../../../../eigenvalue.md) $8\lambda v^2$ along $\mathbf n$ and [eigenvalue](../../../../eigenvalue.md) zero along the one perpendicular direction. Rotating the vacuum conjugates this matrix by an [orthogonal matrix](../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) and cannot change its [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md). Thus every point on the circle has exactly one tangential massless [Goldstone boson](../../../../goldstone-boson.md).

Geometrically, motion along the circle costs no potential energy whereas motion normal to it does. The circle's dimension, equivalently $\dim O(2)-\dim O(1)=1$, is independent of its point. Counting the number of real field components would instead give two and would incorrectly count the massive radial direction as a [Goldstone mode](../../../../goldstone-boson.md).
