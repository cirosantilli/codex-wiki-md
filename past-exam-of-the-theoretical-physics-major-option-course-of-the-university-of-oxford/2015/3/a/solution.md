<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For an analytic, local [Landau theory](../../../../landau-theory.md) with independent global rotations of the [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) vector, a uniform scalar potential can depend only on $\rho=\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf m$. Keeping terms through fourth order in the field, dropping an additive constant, and retaining the leading isotropic positive gradient term gives the displayed quadratic-plus-quartic functional. The stiffness has been absorbed into the [normalization](../../../../normalization.md) of length and field. Higher powers and higher derivatives are neglected in this long-wavelength approximation.

The quartic truncation is stable at large $|\mathbf m|$ when $u>0$. The coefficient $t$ changes sign at the mean-field critical point; close to it one commonly writes $t\propto T-T_c$. If $u<0$, higher stabilizing powers are essential, so the displayed truncation alone cannot determine a stable first-order phase diagram. For $u=0$ and $t<0$ it is likewise unbounded.

For $u>0$, spatially uniform stationary points obey $(t+4u\rho)\mathbf m=0$. The global minima are

$$
\boxed{\mathbf m=0\quad(t\geq0),\qquad
|\mathbf m|^2=-\frac{t}{4u}\quad(t<0).}
$$

The corresponding minimum free-energy density is zero and $-t^2/(16u)$, respectively. The disordered phase gives way continuously to a ferromagnet with an arbitrary direction of [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md). At this mean-field transition the amplitude vanishes as $(-t)^{1/2}$; long-wavelength critical fluctuations can modify mean-field exponents in three dimensions.[§4.2, vector order parameter and rotationally invariant Landau expansion](../../../../statistical-field-theory-by-david-tong.md)
