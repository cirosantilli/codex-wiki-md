<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For [longitudinal-vector annihilation through a scalar contact interaction](../../../../longitudinal-vector-annihilation-through-a-scalar-contact-interaction.md), first use the **supplied vertex** $2ig^2\eta_{\mu\nu}$. Let $s=(p_1+p_2)^2$ and work in the [center-of-mass frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md). The amplitude is $\mathcal M=2g^2\epsilon_1\cdot\epsilon_2$, up to its overall phase. For two longitudinal incoming vectors,

$$
\epsilon_L(p_1)\cdot\epsilon_L(p_2)=\frac{p_1\cdot p_2}{m_A^2}=\frac{s-2m_A^2}{2m_A^2},
\qquad
|\mathcal M_{LL}|^2=g^4\frac{(s-2m_A^2)^2}{m_A^4}.
$$

This grows as $s^2/m_A^4$, whereas transverse amplitudes stay of order $g^2$ and mixed longitudinal-transverse amplitudes vanish in this frame. Consequently the leading high-energy contribution is longitudinal.

For an **[unpolarized](../../../../unpolarized-cross-section.md)** total [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md), average over the $3\times3$ incoming [polarizations](../../../../polarization-waves.md). The massive-vector [polarization](../../../../polarization-waves.md) sum gives

$$
\sum_{i,j}|\epsilon_i(p_1)\cdot\epsilon_j(p_2)|^2
=2+\frac{(p_1\cdot p_2)^2}{m_A^4}.
$$

The identical final scalars require a factor $1/2!$ in the two-body [phase space](../../../../phase-space.md). With $\beta_A=\sqrt{1-4m_A^2/s}$ and $\beta_h=\sqrt{1-4m_h^2/s}$, the angle-independent result is

$$
\boxed{\sigma_{\mathrm{unpol}}(s)=\frac{\beta_h}{\beta_A}\frac{g^4}{288\pi s}
\left[\frac{(s-2m_A^2)^2}{m_A^4}+8\right]
\underset{s\gg m^2}{\sim}\frac{g^4s}{288\pi m_A^4}.}
$$

For a prepared longitudinal pair there is no incoming [polarization](../../../../polarization-waves.md) average, giving instead

$$
\boxed{\sigma_{LL}(s)\sim\frac{g^4s}{32\pi m_A^4}=9\sigma_{\mathrm{unpol}}(s).}
$$

There is no extra initial-state factor $1/2$: a [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md) uses the incoming beam flux, while the final-state factor removes double counting of indistinguishable outgoing states.

Define the [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) in the stated bound to be the total [center-of-mass energy](../../../../center-of-mass-energy.md), $E=\sqrt s$. Equating the leading expressions to $4\pi/E^2$ gives

$$
\boxed{E_{\mathrm{unpol}}=(1152\pi^2)^{1/4}\frac{m_A}{g}\simeq2.58\ \mathrm{TeV},\qquad
E_{LL}=(128\pi^2)^{1/4}\frac{m_A}{g}\simeq1.49\ \mathrm{TeV},}
$$

for $m_A=100\ \mathrm{GeV}$ and $g=0.4$. The second value shows that a longitudinal channel reaches the supplied perturbative bound earlier. If the source's unspecified $E$ denotes the [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) of _one_ incoming particle and the bound is literally $4\pi/E^2$, substitute $s=4E^2$ instead: the corresponding values are $1.83\ \mathrm{TeV}$ [unpolarized](../../../../unpolarized-cross-section.md) and $1.05\ \mathrm{TeV}$ longitudinal. Stating the [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) and [polarization](../../../../polarization-waves.md) conventions is necessary for a numerical answer.

**Interaction-normalization qualification:** the written $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}=g^2 A_\mu A^\mu h^2$ differentiates to $4ig^2\eta_{\mu\nu}$, because there are $2!$ vector assignments and $2!$ scalar assignments. For example, differentiating $g^2(A^0)^2h^2$ twice in each variable gives $4g^2$. The supplied $2ig^2\eta_{\mu\nu}$ would instead follow from $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}=g^2 A_\mu A^\mu h^2/2$. Thus all [cross sections](../../../../cross-section-physics.md) above follow the explicitly requested rule. Using the literal written interaction multiplies them by four and divides every quoted threshold [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) by $\sqrt2$. The [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) growth itself is the physical issue: a contact interaction of massive longitudinal vectors needs additional dynamics before its perturbative high-energy extrapolation becomes reliable.
