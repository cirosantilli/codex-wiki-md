<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Varying the [vector field](../../../../vector-field.md) gives the [Proca equation](../../../../proca-equation.md),

$$
\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}+m_A^2A^\nu=0.
$$

Its divergence uses antisymmetry of $F^{\mu\nu}$ to cancel the first term. Since $m_A\ne0$,

$$
\boxed{\partial_\nu A^\nu=0,\qquad(\Box+m_A^2)A^\nu=0.}
$$

For an on-shell [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) this imposes $p_\nu\epsilon^\nu=0$ on the four [polarization](../../../../polarization-waves.md) components, leaving three independent solutions. In the rest frame, $p=(m_A,\mathbf0)$ makes $\epsilon^0=0$, leaving precisely the three spatial [polarizations](../../../../polarization-waves.md) of a massive spin-one particle.

There is no fourth independent oscillator hidden in $A^0$. Its canonical [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) is zero because the [Lagrangian](../../../../lagrangian.md) has no $\dot A^0$, and the temporal equation constrains $A^0$ in terms of the spatial data. Equivalently the primary and secondary [second-class constraints](../../../../second-class-constraint.md) remove two variables from the eight-dimensional [phase space](../../../../phase-space.md), leaving six phase-space variables, or three physical degrees of freedom.
