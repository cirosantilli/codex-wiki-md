<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Introduce three independent local parameters $\alpha_j(x)$. A consistent convention for the three Abelian [gauge transformations](../../../../gauge-transformation.md) is

$$
\boxed{B_\mu^j\longmapsto B_\mu^j-\frac1g\partial_\mu\alpha_j,\qquad
\phi_1\longmapsto e^{i(\alpha_1-q\alpha_2)}\phi_1,\qquad
\phi_2\longmapsto e^{i(\alpha_2-q\alpha_3)}\phi_2.}
$$

Indeed, $g(B_\mu^k-qB_\mu^{k+1})$ shifts by $-\partial_\mu(\alpha_k-q\alpha_{k+1})$, cancelling the derivative of the scalar phase. Thus $D_\mu\phi_k$ transforms by the same phase as $\phi_k$. The two [kinetic terms](../../../../kinetic-term.md), the absolute-value potentials and all Abelian [field](../../../../field.md) strengths are invariant.

In units where the gauge [coupling](../../../../coupling.md) is $g$, the charge vectors under the three factors are

$$
\boxed{Q(\phi_1)=(1,-q,0),\qquad Q(\phi_2)=(0,1,-q).}
$$

Locally this is the gauge algebra of $U(1)^3$. To interpret it globally as three compact circle groups, the charges must belong to a compatible charge lattice: in the stated unit-charge convention integer $q$ is sufficient, while arbitrary irrational $q$ is not a single-valued compact-group representation. The local transformations and [mass](../../../../mass.md) calculation remain meaningful for general positive real $q$ as a Lie-algebra calculation, or with noncompact Abelian factors.
