<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Set the time derivatives in the [master equation](../../../../master-equation.md) to zero. The first and third rows give $f_1p_0=f_3p_B$ and $f_2p_A=(f_3+f_4)p_B$. Normalization then gives, with $\mathcal N=f_2f_3+f_1(f_3+f_4)+f_1f_2$,

$$
\boxed{p_0^{\rm st}=\frac{f_2f_3}{\mathcal N},\qquad p_A^{\rm st}=\frac{f_1(f_3+f_4)}{\mathcal N},\qquad p_B^{\rm st}=\frac{f_1f_2}{\mathcal N}.}
$$

These expressions assume positive rates, or a limiting choice for which $\mathcal N\ne0$ and the [stationary distribution](../../../../stationary-distribution.md) is unique. If rates vanish so that multiple closed classes appear, the initial condition can matter.

The word “equilibrium” in this part must mean a [stationary distribution](../../../../stationary-distribution.md). With positive $f_1,f_2,f_3$ the one-way contact transitions support a nonzero stationary [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md), so the process is not in thermodynamic equilibrium and does not satisfy [detailed balance](../../../../detailed-balance.md). In particular, $0\to A$ carries positive probability flow while the direct reverse rate is zero.
