<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Let $R=\sqrt{J^2+D^2}$ and $k_0=\arctan(D/J)$. Since $J\cos k+D\sin k=R\cos(k-k_0)$, the continuum minimum of the [dispersion relation](../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) is

$$
\Delta=2S[J(1+u)-R],\qquad \boxed{u_c=\sqrt{1+(D/J)^2}-1.}
$$

For $D>0$, the [energy gap](../../../../energy-gap.md) closes at a positive $u_c$, at [wave vector](../../../../wavevector.md) $k_0$. Below it, the quadratic expansion about the polarized state has a negative mode and is no longer a stable [ground state](../../../../ground-state.md) approximation; higher-order terms and a different ordered reference state are needed.

Two qualifications are required by the actual assumptions. First, $D=0$ is permitted, in which case $u_c=0$; it is a concrete counterexample to the printed strict inequality $u_c>0$. Second, a finite ring only has discrete [momenta](../../../../momentum-split.md). Its [finite-ring spin-wave threshold with antisymmetric exchange](../../../../finite-ring-spin-wave-threshold-with-antisymmetric-exchange.md) is

$$
\boxed{u_c(L)=\max_{\ell=0,\ldots,L-1}\left[\cos\frac{2\pi\ell}{L}+\frac DJ\sin\frac{2\pi\ell}{L}\right]-1.}
$$

This approaches $u_c$ in the [thermodynamic limit](../../../../thermodynamic-limit.md) and equals it when $k_0$ is allowed. For example, $L=4$ and $0<D<J$ give $u_c(L)=0$, since the maximum of $1,D/J,-1,-D/J$ is one. Thus the continuum formula and its positive threshold require the large-ring interpretation. The two symmetry-related polarized [ground states](../../../../ground-state.md) already have equal exact [energy](../../../../energy-split.md); the calculated [energy gap](../../../../energy-gap.md) refers to excitations about one chosen orientation.
