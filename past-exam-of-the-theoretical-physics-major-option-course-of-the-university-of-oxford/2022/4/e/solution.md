<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The naive equal-time [canonical commutation relations](../../../../canonical-commutation-relation.md) would be

$$
[B^\mu(t,\mathbf x),\pi_\nu(t,\mathbf y)]=i\delta^\mu{}_{\nu}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf y),\qquad[B^{*\mu}(t,\mathbf x),\pi_\nu^*(t,\mathbf y)]=i\delta^\mu{}_{\nu}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf y),
$$

with all coordinate-coordinate, momentum-momentum and mixed conjugate-sector commutators zero before enforcing [constraints](../../../../constraint-mechanics.md). But the $\mu=\nu=0$ relation contradicts $\pi_0=0$ as an operator identity: its left side would vanish and its right side would not.

The primary zero-momentum [constraints](../../../../constraint-mechanics.md) and the secondary massive [Gauss laws](../../../../gauss-s-law.md) form [second-class constraints](../../../../second-class-constraint.md); their [Poisson bracket](../../../../poisson-bracket.md) matrix contains nonzero terms proportional to $M^2$. They must be eliminated, or treated using a [Dirac bracket](../../../../dirac-bracket.md), before quantization. In a reduced description the three complex spatial coordinates and their momenta are canonical, while

$$
B_0=\frac{\partial_i\pi_i^*}{M^2},\qquad B_0^*=\frac{\partial_i\pi_i}{M^2}
$$

are dependent operators. Substituting them in the integrated [Hamiltonian](../../../../hamiltonian.md) gives the positive density, up to a surface term,

$$
\mathcal H_{\rm red}=\sum_i|\pi_i|^2+\frac{|\partial_i\pi_i|^2}{M^2}+\frac12G_{ik}^*G^{ik}+M^2\sum_i|B^i|^2.
$$

Thus the problem is a constrained choice of canonical variables, not an absence of a consistent free [quantum field theory](../../../../quantum-field-theory-split.md).
