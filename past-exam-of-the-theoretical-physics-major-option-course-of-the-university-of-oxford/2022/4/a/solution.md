<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The free complex [Proca field](../../../../proca-field.md) has [Poincaré symmetry](../../../../poincare-symmetry.md), with $B_\mu$ transforming as a spacetime [four-vector](../../../../four-vector.md), and the global internal [U(1)](../../../../circle-group.md) [symmetry](../../../../symmetry-physics.md)

$$
B_\mu\longmapsto e^{i\alpha}B_\mu,\qquad B_\mu^*\longmapsto e^{-i\alpha}B_\mu^*.
$$

The free theory is also invariant under charge conjugation $B_\mu\leftrightarrow B_\mu^*$ and the usual parity and [time reversal](../../../../t-symmetry.md) transformations of a vector field. The internal real-field description consists of two equal-mass real [Proca fields](../../../../proca-field.md), with an $O(2)$ symmetry containing the phase rotations and charge conjugation.

A gradient shift of $B_\mu$ leaves $G_{\mu\nu}$ unchanged, because partial derivatives commute. It changes the mass term by

$$
\Delta\mathcal L=M^2\left[-B_\mu^*\partial^\mu\lambda-(\partial_\mu\lambda^*)B^\mu+(\partial_\mu\lambda^*)\partial^\mu\lambda\right].
$$

This is generally nonzero: for example, $B_\mu=0$ and real $\lambda=at$ produce $\Delta\mathcal L=M^2a^2$. Thus a massive [Proca field](../../../../proca-field.md) has no such [gauge symmetry](../../../../gauge-invariance.md). The massless kinetic term alone would have it.
