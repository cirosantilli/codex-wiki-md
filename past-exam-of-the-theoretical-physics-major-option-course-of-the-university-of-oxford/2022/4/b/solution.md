<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Treat $B_\mu$ and $B_\mu^*$ as independent variables in the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md). Varying $B_\nu^*$ gives the [Proca equation](../../../../proca-equation.md)

$$
\partial_\mu G^{\mu\nu}+M^2B^\nu=0.
$$

The divergence of its first term vanishes by antisymmetry and commuting partial derivatives. Since $M\ne0$, it follows that $\partial_\nu B^\nu=0$. Substitution into the original equation gives

$$
\boxed{(\Box+M^2)B^\nu=0,\qquad\partial_\nu B^\nu=0.}
$$

The complex-conjugate variation gives $\partial_\mu G^{*\mu\nu}+M^2B^{*\nu}=0$, hence $(\Box+M^2)B^{*\nu}=0$ and $\partial_\nu B^{*\nu}=0$.

For an on-shell [plane wave](../../../../plane-wave.md) $B^\mu=\varepsilon^\mu e^{-ipx}$, the [Lorenz constraint in Proca theory](../../../../lorenz-constraint-in-proca-theory.md) is $p_\mu\varepsilon^\mu=0$. In the rest frame it sets $\varepsilon^0=0$, leaving three complex [particle polarizations](../../../../particle-polarization.md). Thus the complex [Proca field](../../../../proca-field.md) has **three independent complex dynamical components**, or six real ones; its quantized particle and antiparticle each have three physical [particle polarizations](../../../../particle-polarization.md). The conjugate field is not another unrelated classical field.
