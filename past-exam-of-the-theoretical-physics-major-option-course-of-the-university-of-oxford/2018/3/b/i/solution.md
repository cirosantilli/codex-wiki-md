<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Counting the [mass dimensions](../../../../../mass-dimension.md) of each interaction operator gives

$$
\boxed{[\lambda_a]=-1,\qquad[\lambda_b]=-3,\qquad[\lambda_\gamma]=-1.}
$$

Indeed $\Phi(\partial a)^2$ and $\Phi F^2$ have dimension five, whereas $\Phi b^2(\partial b)^2$ has dimension seven. These are interactions of an [effective field theory](../../../../../effective-field-theory.md) with dimensionful [couplings](../../../../../coupling.md).

The direct one-vertex [decay channels](../../../../../decay-channel.md) are $\Phi\to aa$, $\Phi\to bbbb$ and $\Phi\to\gamma\gamma$. Each leading [scattering amplitude](../../../../../scattering-amplitude.md) is linear in its corresponding [coupling](../../../../../coupling.md), and a [decay width](../../../../../decay-width.md) is quadratic in the amplitude. Therefore, holding the parent [mass](../../../../../mass.md) fixed,

$$
\boxed{\Gamma_{aa}\propto|\lambda_a|^2,\qquad\Gamma_{bbbb}\propto|\lambda_b|^2,\qquad\Gamma_{\gamma\gamma}\propto|\lambda_\gamma|^2.}
$$

There are no tree-level interference terms between these different final states.
