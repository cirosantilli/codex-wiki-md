<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [spectral theorem](../../../../spectral-theorem.md) for a [Hermitian matrix](../../../../hermitian-operator.md) gives a unitary diagonalizer $V$ with $V\Phi V^\dagger=\operatorname{diag}(x_1,\ldots,x_5)$ and real $x_i$. Similarity preserves the [trace](../../../../matrix-trace.md), so $\sum_i x_i=0$.

To ensure that the diagonalizer belongs to [SU(5)](../../../../su-5.md) rather than only the [unitary group](../../../../unitary-group.md) $U(5)$, write $\det V=e^{i\alpha}$ and put $U=e^{-i\alpha/5}V$. Its determinant is one, and the scalar phase cancels from its conjugation action. Thus

$$
\boxed{U\Phi U^\dagger=\Phi_d=\operatorname{diag}(x_1,\ldots,x_5),\qquad
x_i\in\mathbb R,\quad\sum_i x_i=0,\quad U\in SU(5).}
$$

The same argument shows why no additional reality condition beyond Hermiticity is needed. Permuting the real [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md) merely changes their order in the diagonal form.
