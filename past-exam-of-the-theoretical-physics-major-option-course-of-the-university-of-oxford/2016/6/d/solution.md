<h1 id="6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

With outgoing physical photon [polarization vectors](../../../../polarization-vector.md), the [Feynman rule](../../../../feynman-rule.md) gives

$$
\mathcal A_{\lambda_1\lambda_2}=i g_a\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}q_1^\rho q_2^\sigma
\epsilon_1^{\mu *}\epsilon_2^{\nu *}.
$$

The overall phase does not affect the [decay width](../../../../decay-width.md). Replacing either polarization vector by its own [four-momentum](../../../../four-momentum.md) gives zero by antisymmetry. Thus the [Ward identity](../../../../ward-identity.md) permits replacement of each physical [photon polarization sum](../../../../photon-polarization-sum.md) by $-\eta^{\mu\nu}$ inside this amplitude. The printed sum is not an equality of free-standing physical two-polarization tensors: for a photon moving along $z$, its physical $00$ component is zero whereas $-\eta_{00}=-1$. Gauge-dependent longitudinal terms cancel only after contraction with the transverse amplitude.

The contraction of the two [Levi-Civita symbols](../../../../levi-civita-symbol.md) gives

$$
\sum_{\lambda_1,\lambda_2}|\mathcal A|^2
=2g_a^2\left[(q_1\cdot q_2)^2-q_1^2q_2^2\right]
=\frac{g_a^2m_a^4}{2},
$$

since $q_1^2=q_2^2=0$ and $2q_1\cdot q_2=m_a^2$. No initial spin average is needed for the spin-zero [axion](../../../../axion.md). The result is independent of direction in its rest frame. Including the identical-particle factor explained in [solution](../c/solution.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\Gamma(a\to\gamma\gamma)=\frac1{2!}\frac{4\pi}{64\pi^2m_a}\frac{g_a^2m_a^4}{2}
=\frac{g_a^2m_a^3}{64\pi}.}
$$

Omitting $1/2!$ would overestimate the physical rate by two. The normalization agrees with the standard axion-photon convention.[Axions and Other Similar Particles, page 4, equation (90.8): two-photon decay width for the coupling multiplying a F dual-F divided by four](../../../../review-of-particle-physics-by-particle-data-group-2022.md)
