<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Evaluate the [two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space](../../../../two-body-lorentz-invariant-phase-space.md) in the parent's rest frame, $p=(m,\mathbf0)$. The spatial [Dirac delta function](../../../../dirac-delta-function.md) sets $\mathbf q_2=-\mathbf q_1$. For massless daughters $E_1=E_2=q$, so

$$
d\Pi_2=\frac1{(2\pi)^2}\frac{q^2dq\,d\Omega}{4q^2}\delta(m-2q)
=\frac{d\Omega}{32\pi^2}.
$$

The factor $1/2$ in the last step is the derivative of the delta-function argument. Substituting into the [decay width](../../../../decay-width.md) gives, for labelled or distinct daughters,

$$
\boxed{\Gamma=\frac1{64\pi^2m}\int d\Omega\,|\mathcal A|^2.}
$$

The angular domain is one sphere: $0\le\varphi<2\pi$ and $-1\le\cos\theta\le1$, equivalently $0\le\theta\le\pi$. The printed negative-to-positive polar-angle interval is not a second independent coverage of the sphere.

For identical daughters, integration over the full labelled two-body [phase space](../../../../phase-space.md) counts each physical final state twice. One must therefore include an additional factor $1/2!$, or equivalently integrate over an unordered half-domain. This distinction is necessary for the two identical [photons](../../../../photon.md) in [solution](../d/solution.md).
