<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Hamiltonian](../../../../hamiltonian.md) has the discrete [symmetry](../../../../symmetry-physics.md) described in [solution](../a/solution.md), whereas either selected easy-axis [ground state](../../../../ground-state.md) has a nonzero [order parameter](../../../../order-parameter.md) $\langle S^x\rangle$ and is not invariant under it. This is [spontaneous symmetry breaking](../../../../spontaneous-symmetry-breaking.md): the equations preserve the symmetry while the selected macroscopic [quantum state](../../../../quantum-state.md) does not.

An infinitesimal longitudinal [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) selects one orientation. The defining order of limits is

$$
\boxed{m_x=\lim_{h\to0^+}\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac1L\sum_j\langle S_j^x\rangle_h\ne0}
$$

in the ordered zero-temperature phase, with the opposite sign for $h\to0^-$. A symmetric finite-volume [quantum state](../../../../quantum-state.md) can instead have zero [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md). Quantum ordered states are generally dressed by fluctuations rather than exact fully polarized product states, because unequal transverse exchange can create pairs of [spin waves](../../../../spin-wave.md). Discrete easy-axis symmetry does not imply a gapless [Goldstone mode](../../../../goldstone-boson.md); indeed [solution](../e/solution.md) finds a nonzero [spin wave](../../../../spin-wave.md) gap. Nor does this argument assert positive-temperature order in a one-dimensional short-range chain.[Section 1.2.1, spontaneous symmetry breaking and the order of thermodynamic and zero-field limits](../../../../statistical-field-theory-by-david-tong.md)
