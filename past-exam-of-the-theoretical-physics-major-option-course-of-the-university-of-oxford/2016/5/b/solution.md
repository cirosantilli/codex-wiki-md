<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Allow a freely chosen center $x_0$, set $u=k(x-x_0)$ and $z=e^u$. Direct differentiation of the proposed [Sine-Gordon kink](../../../../sine-gordon-kink.md) gives

$$
\phi'=\frac{4kz}{b(1+z^2)}=\frac{2k}{b}\operatorname{sech}u,
\qquad
\phi''=\frac{4k^2z(1-z^2)}{b(1+z^2)^2}.
$$

The angle identity gives

$$
\sin(b\phi)=\sin(4\arctan z)=\frac{4z(1-z^2)}{(1+z^2)^2}.
$$

Since $k^2/b=a$, these formulas show $\boxed{\phi''=a\sin(b\phi)}$, precisely the static [equation of motion](../../../../equation-of-motion.md).

For $x\to-\infty$ and $x\to+\infty$, the [field](../../../../field.md) approaches $0$ and $2\pi/b$ respectively. These are neighboring minima of the [scalar potential](../../../../scalar-potential.md) when $ab>0$, and $\phi'$ vanishes exponentially at either end. The [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is therefore finite and the [topological charge](../../../../topological-charge.md) $Q=b[\phi(+\infty)-\phi(-\infty)]/(2\pi)$ is one. Reversing $x-x_0$ gives the corresponding antikink. If $ab<0$, the printed real exponential profile is unavailable; its formal imaginary scale is not a real finite-energy solution of the stated form.
