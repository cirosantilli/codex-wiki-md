## Educational psychology

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

The study of psychological processes involved in [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning), instruction and educational participation. Mechanistic plausibility, predictive association and evidence that a teaching intervention improves outcomes are different levels of support.

### Learning styles

↑ **Parent:** [Educational psychology](#educational-psychology)

Proposed categories of learners associated with preferred or supposedly optimal ways of [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning). Preferences should be distinguished from the stronger [learning-style matching hypothesis](#learning-style-matching-hypothesis) that teaching to the preference improves [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning).

#### Learning-style matching hypothesis

↑ **Parent:** [Learning styles](#learning-styles)

The hypothesis that matching instructional format to a learner's assessed style improves [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) relative to mismatching. It requires an appropriate preference-by-format comparison, not merely a preference questionnaire or a format main effect.

##### Crossover criterion for learning-style matching

↑ **Parent:** [Learning-style matching hypothesis](#learning-style-matching-hypothesis)

Let $\mu_{p,f}$ be the expected score for learner-preference group $p$ under randomly assigned format $f$. A two-style visual/auditory matching claim requires $\mu_{V,V}>\mu_{V,A}$ and $\mu_{A,A}>\mu_{A,V}$. Thus the within-group format contrast changes sign. An [interaction](statistical-model.md#interaction-statistics) contrast $I=(\mu_{V,V}-\mu_{V,A})-(\mu_{A,V}-\mu_{A,A})$ is positive under this pattern, but $I>0$ alone is insufficient: one format might remain better in both groups. With preference group on the horizontal axis and format as the plotted line, the matching prediction is a crossover. This is a criterion for the stated hypothesis, not evidence that fixed styles exist.

### Health literacy

↑ **Parent:** [Educational psychology](#educational-psychology)

Knowledge and skills used to access, understand, evaluate and use health information. Factual knowledge, causal understanding, [emotion](#emotion) and behavior are related but distinct possible outcomes.

### Opportunity to learn

↑ **Parent:** [Educational psychology](#educational-psychology)

Access to relevant content, time, instruction and participation. [Opportunity to learn](#opportunity-to-learn) can differ after an attainment placement and should be measured rather than assumed equivalent across groups.

### Peer effect

↑ **Parent:** [Educational psychology](#educational-psychology)

An influence of classmates or peers on a pupil's outcome. Peer composition, instructional targeting and teacher allocation can change together, so their effects require an appropriate design to separate them.

### Teacher expectation

↑ **Parent:** [Educational psychology](#educational-psychology)

A teacher's prediction or assumption about a learner's performance or potential. To establish a self-fulfilling effect, research must show how expectations change treatment or opportunity and subsequent [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning), rather than infer causation from a correlation.

### Formative assessment

↑ **Parent:** [Educational psychology](#educational-psychology)

Using evidence about current understanding to choose and revise instruction. Assessment is formative through its use in the [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) process, not merely because another test is administered.

### Mathematical difficulty

↑ **Parent:** [Educational psychology](#educational-psychology)

Difficulty acquiring or using mathematical knowledge relative to the relevant task and educational context. Similar low attainment can reflect different component profiles, instructional opportunities, language demands or other constraints.

#### Developmental dyscalculia

↑ **Parent:** [Mathematical difficulty](#mathematical-difficulty)

Persistent difficulty with mathematical [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) in an appropriate developmental and educational assessment context. The label does not establish one identical cognitive deficit in every child or justify diagnosis from a single low test score.

### Procedural knowledge

↑ **Parent:** [Educational psychology](#educational-psychology)

Knowledge of how to carry out a sequence or method. Procedural fluency can reduce processing demands, but successful execution on familiar items does not alone establish understanding of every underlying relation.

### Numeracy

↑ **Parent:** [Educational psychology](#educational-psychology)

Knowledge and skill for using [numbers](arithmetic.md#number) and quantitative relationships. [Numeracy](#numeracy) includes components that can develop unevenly, rather than one score uniquely specifying a learner's cognitive mechanism.

### Arithmetic development

↑ **Parent:** [Educational psychology](#educational-psychology)

[Learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) the meanings, relations and procedures used in [arithmetic](arithmetic.md). A correct answer can arise through different strategies, so assessment should combine outcomes with informative observations of the process.

#### Strategy variability

↑ **Parent:** [Arithmetic development](#arithmetic-development)

Use of different procedures across problems or occasions. Variation can be informative about [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) rather than mere error; a single correct answer or verbal report may not identify the underlying strategy.

#### Arithmetic strategy

↑ **Parent:** [Arithmetic development](#arithmetic-development)

A procedure for solving an [arithmetic](arithmetic.md) problem, including [counting](#counting), retrieval and decomposition. Different strategies can coexist and be selected according to the problem and available knowledge.

##### Arithmetic decomposition

↑ **Parent:** [Arithmetic strategy](#arithmetic-strategy)

Reexpressing a calculation through useful numerical relations, such as $8+5=8+2+3=13$. Decomposition can connect practiced facts with [conceptual knowledge](neuroscience.md#conceptual-knowledge) instead of requiring direct retrieval of every whole problem.

##### Counting-on strategy

↑ **Parent:** [Arithmetic strategy](#arithmetic-strategy)

Starting from a known quantity and [counting](#counting) only the added units. The strategy requires a link between the starting [number](arithmetic.md#number) and its represented quantity, rather than simply omitting words from a memorized sequence.

###### Min strategy

↑ **Parent:** [Counting-on strategy](#counting-on-strategy)

For [addition](arithmetic.md#addition) of nonnegative [natural numbers](arithmetic.md#natural-number) $a$ and $b$, start at $\max(a,b)$ and count $\min(a,b)$ further units. [Commutativity](algebraic-operation.md#commutativity) guarantees the same sum while minimizing the increments among the two possible choices of starting addend.

##### Count-all strategy

↑ **Parent:** [Arithmetic strategy](#arithmetic-strategy)

Representing the relevant collections and [counting](#counting) the combined items to obtain a sum. Count-all can be accurate while requiring more increments and tracking than a suitable [counting-on strategy](#counting-on-strategy).

#### Counting

↑ **Parent:** [Arithmetic development](#arithmetic-development)

Coordinating ordered [number](arithmetic.md#number) words or symbols with items or successive units. Reciting the sequence is distinct from [counting](#counting) a collection and from understanding what its final count represents.

##### Counting principle

↑ **Parent:** [Counting](#counting)

A constraint on valid [counting](#counting), such as stable ordering of [number](arithmetic.md#number) words, one-to-one correspondence with items and the [cardinality principle](#cardinality-principle). Understanding and coordinating these constraints should be distinguished from rehearsing a count routine.

###### Cardinality principle

↑ **Parent:** [Counting principle](#counting-principle)

The principle that the final [number](arithmetic.md#number) word in a valid count represents the [cardinality](cardinal-number.md#cardinality) of the whole collection. A child's knowledge of this relationship is different from merely repeating the last word after being prompted.

###### Number-word knower level

↑ **Parent:** [Cardinality principle](#cardinality-principle)

A description of which exact [number](arithmetic.md#number)-word meanings a child demonstrates in tasks such as giving a requested collection. Early knowledge of particular small quantities can precede a general use of the [counting](#counting) system; task context and errors matter when assigning the description.

## Comparative psychology

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

The comparison of behavior and cognition across species, using tasks whose sensory, motivational and motor demands are appropriate to each species. Similar performance need not establish identical subjective experience.

## Innate behavior

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

Behavior with a substantial inherited developmental basis. Innateness should not be equated with being present at birth, immutable, independent of every environmental input, or identical across individuals. A useful claim states the proposed inherited constraints and the developmental experiences allowed.

## Psychophysiology

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

The study of relationships between psychological processes and bodily measurements. In [emotion](#emotion) research it combines [self-report](#self-report), behavior and physiological recordings; agreement among these measures is an empirical question.

### Autonomic emotion classification

↑ **Parent:** [Psychophysiology](#psychophysiology)

[Statistical classification](artificial-intelligence.md#statistical-classification) of [emotion](#emotion) categories using measurements of the [autonomic nervous system](neuroscience.md#autonomic-nervous-system). It requires an independently justified target label, appropriate [cross-validation](statistical-learning.md#cross-validation), and evaluation in the population and context where the [classifier](foundations-of-mathematics.md#classifier) will be used. Predictive success does not imply that every instance of a category has one invariant physiological pattern.

#### Base-rate dependence of autonomic emotion classification

↑ **Parent:** [Autonomic emotion classification](#autonomic-emotion-classification)

Fix a true-positive [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) $s>0$ and false-positive [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) $f>0$. The [positive predictive value](artificial-intelligence.md#positive-predictive-value) at [base rate](bayesian-statistics.md#base-rate) $p$ is $v(p)=sp/(sp+f(1-p))$. Differentiation gives $v'(p)=sf/[f+(s-f)p]^2>0$, while $v(p)\to0$ as $p\to0$. Thus a [classifier](foundations-of-mathematics.md#classifier) can retain the same conditional performance yet become unreliable among its positive classifications when the target [emotion](#emotion) is rare. This is a mathematical implication of the specified binary model, not an empirical performance estimate.

### Electrodermal activity

↑ **Parent:** [Psychophysiology](#psychophysiology)

Changes in the electrical properties of skin associated with [sweat gland](physiology.md#sweat-gland) activity. Recording electrodes can measure tonic levels and event-related changes in [skin conductance](#skin-conductance). Such changes inform [psychophysiology](#psychophysiology) but do not uniquely identify an [emotion](#emotion).

#### Skin conductance

↑ **Parent:** [Electrodermal activity](#electrodermal-activity)

The [electrical conductance](electric-charge.md#electrical-conductance) of skin, often measured between two recording electrodes. [Sweating](physiology.md#sweating) changes the conductive properties of the skin. Baseline, movement and recording conditions must be considered before interpreting an event-related response as evidence about [emotion](#emotion).

## Motivation

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

Processes governing the initiation, direction and persistence of behaviour. Reward expectation, effort, bodily state and action opportunity can influence [motivation](#motivation); a performance change need not reflect a change in [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) or experienced pleasure.

### Incentive motivation

↑ **Parent:** [Motivation](#motivation)

Processes through which anticipated outcomes influence the initiation, direction or vigor of behavior. [Reward](animal-behaviour.md#reward)-driven effort can be impaired despite preserved ability to produce a movement on instruction.

### Incentive salience

↑ **Parent:** [Motivation](#motivation)

The motivational attractiveness assigned to a represented outcome or its cue, often described as cue-triggered wanting. It can vary without a corresponding change in experienced pleasure or explicit [belief](#belief) about value.[Journal of Neuroscience 20, 8122–8130; separate instrumental and cue training, extinction test and taste reactivity](psychology-bibliography.md#intra-accumbens-amphetamine-increases-the-conditioned-incentive-salience-of-sucrose-reward-by-cindy-l-wyvell-and-others)

## Mental simulation

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

Internally constructing or evaluating possible situations, events or action sequences. It may recombine remembered elements rather than reproduce one prior episode exactly. Evidence for imagined content is distinct from evidence that the simulation causally controls a decision.

## Emotion

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

A coordinated, context-sensitive pattern involving appraisal, subjective feeling, bodily changes and action tendencies. These components can dissociate; a [facial expression](#facial-expression) is evidence about [emotion](#emotion) rather than an infallible definition of it.

### Alexithymia

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

Difficulty identifying and describing one’s own [emotions](#emotion), often accompanied by an externally oriented thinking style. It is not synonymous with absence of [emotion](#emotion), [autism](psychiatry.md#autism) or inability to care about others.

#### Alexithymia hypothesis of emotional difficulties in autism

↑ **Parent:** [Alexithymia](#alexithymia)

The proposal that some emotion-processing differences attributed to [autism](psychiatry.md#autism) instead track co-occurring [alexithymia](#alexithymia). It predicts dimensional relationships across diagnostic groups, not that [alexithymia](#alexithymia) explains every [autistic](psychiatry.md#autism) experience.

### Affect

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

A broad label for feeling-related states, often described along valence and [arousal](physiology.md#arousal) dimensions. Shared [affect](#affect), [emotion](#emotion) identification and compassionate concern are different experimental targets.

### Excitement

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

### Shame

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

### Interpersonal emotion transfer

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

A change in one person's [emotion](#emotion) attributable to information or influence from another person. It includes matching and complementary responses. Evidence must distinguish such influence from two people independently reacting to a shared event.

### Emotional contagion

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

An interpersonal influence in which a recipient comes to experience affect resembling another person's affect. [Facial mimicry](#facial-mimicry), shared circumstances and [social appraisal](#social-appraisal) are possible contributors, but the presence of one does not establish the others.

### Facial feedback hypothesis

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

The hypothesis that feedback arising from facial activity contributes to felt [emotion](#emotion). Weak versions predict modulation; stronger versions predict initiation, category-specific effects or necessity. These claims require different experimental tests.

#### Facial feedback manipulation does not identify its mediator

↑ **Parent:** [Facial feedback hypothesis](#facial-feedback-hypothesis)

Let $X$ be randomized assignment, $F$ facial activity, $M$ sensory feedback and $Y$ a report. One possible model is $F=X$, $M=F$, $Y=M$. A second is $F=X$, $M=F$, $D=X$, $Y=D$, where $D$ is an expectancy route. Both models predict the same observed $X,F,Y$ even though intervening on $M$ changes $Y$ only in the first. Therefore randomizing $X$ and measuring $F,Y$ does not distinguish these mechanisms. Identifying [causal mediation](causal-inference.md#causal-mediation) requires further assumptions or interventions. The binary models are a logical counterexample, not participant data.

#### Facial feedback experiment with a pen held in the mouth

↑ **Parent:** [Facial feedback hypothesis](#facial-feedback-hypothesis)

The 1988 experiment associated with [Fritz Strack](psychologist.md#fritz-strack) used different ways of holding a pen to facilitate or inhibit facial activity associated with smiling. The manipulation was intended to avoid explicitly asking participants to act happy. Its interpretive importance lies in separating bodily feedback from explicit labeling; its limitation is that different postures can also change effort and task experience.[1988, experimental manipulation and humor judgments](psychology-bibliography.md#inhibiting-and-facilitating-conditions-of-the-human-smile-by-fritz-strack-and-others)

#### Facial feedback

↑ **Parent:** [Facial feedback hypothesis](#facial-feedback-hypothesis)

Information about facial activity available to the organism, including bodily sensory consequences and information used in interpreting its own state. A manipulation of [facial movement](#facial-movement) can change several other things as well; its total effect on a report does not uniquely identify this route.

### Appraisal theory

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

A family of accounts in which evaluation of events in relation to goals, well-being and coping possibilities shapes [emotion](#emotion). [Cognitive appraisal](#cognitive-appraisal) can be fast and nonverbal. A theory requiring appraisal for every emotional episode must specify the appraisal process independently enough to permit a counterexample.

#### Social appraisal theory

↑ **Parent:** [Appraisal theory](#appraisal-theory)

The account that another person's reaction changes an observer's evaluation of an event, and thereby the observer's [emotion](#emotion). The process can be rapid and need not involve consciously naming the other's feeling. It permits complementary outcomes, such as another person's [anger](#anger) contributing to the observer's [fear](#fear).

##### Social appraisal

↑ **Parent:** [Social appraisal theory](#social-appraisal-theory)

Using another person's emotional reaction as information about an event or relationship. Establishing this mechanism requires evidence about the recipient's evaluation, beyond a [correlation](variance.md#pearson-correlation-coefficient) between the two people's reports.

### Surprise

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

### Sadness

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

### Happiness

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

### Anger

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

### Emotion component

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

An aspect of an emotional episode, such as [cognitive appraisal](#cognitive-appraisal), felt experience, bodily regulation, [facial movement](#facial-movement) or readiness to act. Components can vary partly independently. A theory must state which components its evidence actually measures.

### Basic emotion theory

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

A family of theories proposing a limited set of biologically grounded [basic emotions](#basic-emotion). Versions differ in how strongly they predict distinctive facial, physiological and behavioral patterns. A claim of fixed one-to-one patterns is stronger than a claim of inherited response biases that interact with context.

#### Basic emotion

↑ **Parent:** [Basic emotion theory](#basic-emotion-theory)

An [emotion](#emotion) proposed as a biologically primary category in a [basic emotion theory](#basic-emotion-theory). The status of a category is a theoretical and empirical claim, not something established merely by giving observers a category word in a [forced-choice emotion recognition](#forced-choice-emotion-recognition) task.

### Distress

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

An aversive subjective experience that can accompany [anxiety](#anxiety), [intrusive memories](neuroscience.md#intrusive-memory) or other difficulties. Its intensity is distinct from the conviction of a [belief](#belief), a physiological response or [functional impairment](#functional-impairment).

### Emotional arousal

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

The degree of activation associated with an emotional state. [Emotional arousal](#emotional-arousal) is distinct from [emotional valence](#emotional-valence): both positive and negative states can be highly activating.

### Emotional valence

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

The positive or negative evaluative quality of an experience or stimulus. Agreement about [emotional valence](#emotional-valence) is weaker than agreement about a particular discrete [emotion](#emotion) category.

### Facial expression

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

A configuration or movement of the face that can contribute to communication and sensory regulation. Similar production across populations does not imply identical [emotion recognition](#emotion-recognition), and an expression can be posed or socially regulated.

#### Facial mimicry

↑ **Parent:** [Facial expression](#facial-expression)

Facial reactions congruent with another person's observed [facial movements](#facial-movement). [Facial electromyography](physiology.md#facial-electromyography) can measure small congruent responses. Congruent movement alone does not establish [emotional contagion](#emotional-contagion) or a necessary route to [emotion recognition](#emotion-recognition).

#### Facial Action Coding System

↑ **Parent:** [Facial expression](#facial-expression)

A system for describing visible facial actions in terms of anatomically grounded action units. Coding an action unit documents a movement; assigning an [emotion](#emotion) to the movement is a separate inference.

#### Facial movement

↑ **Parent:** [Facial expression](#facial-expression)

Movement of facial [muscle](anatomy.md#muscle) groups, described without assuming its psychological cause. Calling a movement an expression of an [emotion](#emotion) adds an interpretation that requires independent evidence.

#### Facial action coding

↑ **Parent:** [Facial expression](#facial-expression)

Description of visible facial movements in terms of anatomically informed action components. Coding the movement keeps an observation separate from an inferred [emotion](#emotion) label; a code by itself does not prove the observer's emotional interpretation.

#### Display rule

↑ **Parent:** [Facial expression](#facial-expression)

A socially learned convention regulating when and how a [facial expression](#facial-expression) is shown. [Display rules](#display-rule) can alter a visible signal without implying that every underlying feeling or production mechanism is culture-specific.

### Disgust

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

An [emotion](#emotion) associated with rejection or aversion. A facial configuration labeled [disgust](#disgust) can have sensory consequences without uniquely identifying the feeling of its producer.

### Fear

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion](#emotion)

An emotional response to perceived threat. The experience of [fear](#fear), defensive behavior and a [facial expression](#facial-expression) interpreted as [fear](#fear) should be distinguished when measuring it.

## Cross-cultural psychology

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

The study of psychological variation and commonality across cultural settings. Equivalent translations do not alone establish [measurement invariance](#measurement-invariance), and observed cultural-group differences do not make culture a randomized cause.

## Social psychology

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

The study of how people understand, evaluate and influence one another within social settings. A social explanation can involve individual processing, relationships, group membership and institutions; the appropriate level depends on the question.

### Criminal behavior

↑ **Parent:** [Social psychology](#social-psychology)

Actions classified as offences in a specified legal setting. Their causes and consequences are heterogeneous; a legal classification is not itself a psychiatric diagnosis. Behavioural incidence, arrests, convictions and severity of harm are different outcomes.

#### Recidivism

↑ **Parent:** [Criminal behavior](#criminal-behavior)

Repetition of offending after an earlier offence or intervention, operationalised over a specified follow-up period. Rearrest is one imperfect indicator affected by detection and policing as well as behaviour.

### Social support

↑ **Parent:** [Social psychology](#social-psychology)

Resources available through relationships, including emotional, practical or informational assistance. Perceived support, received support and network availability are different measurements. A capacity to seek help does not guarantee responsive help exists.

### Intergroup contact

↑ **Parent:** [Social psychology](#social-psychology)

Interaction between people associated with different social groups. Contact provides an opportunity for learning, relationship formation and [recategorization](#recategorization); its association with lower [prejudice](#prejudice) must be distinguished from selection into contact.

### Discrimination

↑ **Parent:** [Social psychology](#social-psychology)

Differential treatment based on social-group membership. Evidence about [prejudice](#prejudice) or [stereotype](#stereotype) endorsement does not alone establish how a person behaves in a consequential interaction.

### Prejudice

↑ **Parent:** [Social psychology](#social-psychology)

An evaluative attitude toward people on the basis of social-group membership. [Prejudice](#prejudice), [stereotype](#stereotype) endorsement and [discrimination](#discrimination) are related but distinguishable targets for measurement and [intervention](causal-inference.md#treatment).

### Social identity

↑ **Parent:** [Social psychology](#social-psychology)

The aspect of a person's self-understanding associated with group memberships and their significance. A common [social identity](#social-identity) can coexist with meaningful subgroup identities.

#### Identity threat

↑ **Parent:** [Social identity](#social-identity)

A perceived challenge to the value, recognition or continuity of an identity. An ostensibly inclusive category can threaten a subgroup when inclusion is understood to require abandoning its distinctive identity.

#### Dual identity

↑ **Parent:** [Social identity](#social-identity)

Concurrent representation of a shared superordinate identity and distinct subgroup identities. For example, different departments can remain recognizable within one university. [Dual identity](#dual-identity) can combine inclusion with distinctiveness, but its effects depend on whether both memberships are credible and valued.

### Media effects

↑ **Parent:** [Social psychology](#social-psychology)

Changes associated with exposure to mediated content. [Causal inference](causal-inference.md) requires separating influence from selection and distinguishing the content of exposure from accompanying features such as duration, competition or frustration.

### Aggression

↑ **Parent:** [Social psychology](#social-psychology)

Behavior directed toward harming another who is motivated to avoid that harm. Anger, [emotional arousal](#emotional-arousal), aggressive thoughts and scores on experimental tasks should not automatically be equated with enacted harm. Measurement must specify which component is observed.

#### Violence

↑ **Parent:** [Aggression](#aggression)

Severe physical [aggression](#aggression), with a threshold defined for the research question. Evidence about mild [aggression](#aggression) or a laboratory proxy does not by itself identify an effect on serious [violence](#violence).

### Social cognition

↑ **Parent:** [Social psychology](#social-psychology)

The processes through which people represent other individuals, relationships and groups. [Social cognition](#social-cognition) includes [social attribution](#social-attribution), [emotion recognition](#emotion-recognition) and [theory of mind](animal-behaviour.md#theory-of-mind), which need not rely on identical mechanisms.

#### Spontaneous and elicited social cognition

↑ **Parent:** [Social cognition](#social-cognition)

The distinction between what a person does without explicit instruction and what they can do when a relevant strategy is prompted. Differences can reflect [attention](#attention), [motivation](#motivation), familiarity or control rather than absolute absence of capacity.

#### Social communication

↑ **Parent:** [Social cognition](#social-cognition)

Exchange of information, [intentions](#intention) and [affect](#affect) through interaction. Communication success depends on both participants, their shared conventions and the environment.

##### Double empathy problem

↑ **Parent:** [Social communication](#social-communication)

A proposal associated with [Damian Milton](README.md#damian-milton) that mutual understanding can be difficult across different experiential perspectives. It treats interaction as reciprocal rather than assigning every failure to one participant.

#### Action understanding

↑ **Parent:** [Social cognition](#social-cognition)

Recognizing or interpreting an observed action, its goal or its significance. Identifying a grasp, inferring a hidden object interaction, judging physical effort and attributing a [false belief](#false-belief) are different tasks.

##### Action prediction

↑ **Parent:** [Action understanding](#action-understanding)

Anticipating what an agent will do next using observed movement, context and prior experience. Successful prediction need not require attributing a conscious [intention](#intention).

#### Self-other distinction

↑ **Parent:** [Social cognition](#social-cognition)

Representing the difference between one's own state and another person's state. Affective sharing without this distinction is insufficient to establish [empathy](#empathy) as defined here.

#### Empathy

↑ **Parent:** [Social cognition](#social-cognition)

Understanding or sharing another person's state while maintaining a [self-other distinction](#self-other-distinction). [Affective empathy](#affective-empathy), [cognitive empathy](#cognitive-empathy), [compassion](#compassion) and [personal distress](#personal-distress) should be separated when operationalizing the construct: neural evidence for one component does not automatically explain the others.

##### Personal distress

↑ **Parent:** [Empathy](#empathy)

A self-focused aversive response to witnessing another's suffering. [Personal distress](#personal-distress) and concern for the other may have different behavioral consequences, even when both accompany an empathic situation.

##### Compassion

↑ **Parent:** [Empathy](#empathy)

Concern for another's suffering with an orientation toward helping. [Compassion](#compassion) should not be inferred merely from unpleasant arousal or from regional [brain](neuroscience.md#brain) activation.

##### Cognitive empathy

↑ **Parent:** [Empathy](#empathy)

Understanding another person's emotional state or perspective. [Cognitive empathy](#cognitive-empathy) can recruit [theory of mind](animal-behaviour.md#theory-of-mind) without necessarily producing [affective empathy](#affective-empathy) or [compassion](#compassion).

##### Affective empathy

↑ **Parent:** [Empathy](#empathy)

An affective response that shares aspects of another person's state while preserving awareness that the state belongs to the other. This is distinct from simply becoming distressed and from understanding a state without sharing it.

###### Empathy for pain

↑ **Parent:** [Affective empathy](#affective-empathy)

Responses to another person’s [pain](neuroscience.md#pain). Shared [affect](#affect), inference about the person, self-directed distress and [motivation](#motivation) to help require separate measures.

#### Stereotype

↑ **Parent:** [Social cognition](#social-cognition)

A belief or expectation about characteristics of members of a social group. Its application to a person can ignore relevant individuating information; a [stereotype](#stereotype) is not identical to an evaluative attitude or discriminatory action.

#### Attribution theory

↑ **Parent:** [Social cognition](#social-cognition)

An account of how observers explain conduct or events by assigning causal importance to personal and situational factors. The diagnosticity of behavior depends on the alternatives available in the actual situation.

##### Conditional diagnosticity in attribution

↑ **Parent:** [Attribution theory](#attribution-theory)

Behavior $B$ is evidence for a disposition $D$, given a situation $S$, to the extent that its conditional [likelihood ratio](statistical-hypothesis-test.md#likelihood-ratio) $P(B\mid D,S)/P(B\mid\neg D,S)$ differs from one. By [Bayes' theorem](probability-theory.md#bayes-theorem) this ratio multiplies the prior odds to give the [posterior odds](bayesian-statistics.md#posterior-odds). If a constraint forces the same behavior regardless of the disposition, the ratio is one and the behavior leaves those odds unchanged. This does not exclude diagnostic information in other features, such as wording or voluntary choices. The argument identifies the assumptions needed to call a [dispositional attribution](#dispositional-attribution) biased.

##### Attributional correction

↑ **Parent:** [Attribution theory](#attribution-theory)

Revising an initial impression to incorporate additional information about why conduct occurred. A cognitive-load manipulation can test the processing requirements of correction, but a final judgment alone does not establish a mandatory sequence of mental stages.

##### Fundamental attribution error

↑ **Parent:** [Attribution theory](#attribution-theory)

Underweighting situational determinants relative to personal determinants when explaining behavior. [Correspondence bias](#correspondence-bias) concerns the more specific inference of a corresponding disposition from observed conduct. Neither term establishes that a particular inference is mistaken without a justified comparison.

##### Correspondence bias

↑ **Parent:** [Attribution theory](#attribution-theory)

A tendency to infer personal characteristics corresponding to observed conduct more strongly than is warranted after relevant situational constraints are considered. Establishing [correspondence bias](#correspondence-bias) requires a justified benchmark for what the behavior reveals under those constraints. It is not a claim that every [dispositional attribution](#dispositional-attribution) is an error.

##### Situational attribution

↑ **Parent:** [Attribution theory](#attribution-theory)

An explanation of conduct in terms of circumstances, constraints or incentives. Situational and dispositional contributions can interact rather than constituting mutually exclusive explanations.

##### Dispositional attribution

↑ **Parent:** [Attribution theory](#attribution-theory)

An explanation of conduct in terms of a relatively enduring characteristic of the actor, such as an attitude or tendency. A [dispositional attribution](#dispositional-attribution) can be accurate; [correspondence bias](#correspondence-bias) concerns excessive inference from conduct whose circumstances make it weak evidence.

##### Social attribution

↑ **Parent:** [Attribution theory](#attribution-theory)

An inference about the causes of another person's conduct or a social event. [Social attribution](#social-attribution) can assign different weights to dispositional and situational explanations; its accuracy depends on available evidence and justified causal assumptions.

#### Social perception

↑ **Parent:** [Social cognition](#social-cognition)

Forming impressions and interpretations of other people from behavior, appearance and context. Observing a movement is different from establishing its intention, cause or emotional meaning.

##### Emotion recognition

↑ **Parent:** [Social perception](#social-perception)

Inferring an emotional state from cues such as [facial expression](#facial-expression), voice, behavior and context. Accuracy depends on a justified criterion for the state and on the response alternatives supplied by the task.

###### Fear recognition

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion recognition](#emotion-recognition)

Identifying [fear](#fear) from available information, such as [facial expressions](#facial-expression). Performance depends on which diagnostic features are sampled as well as on how they are interpreted, and need not measure the observer's own subjective [fear](#fear).[Nature 433, 68–72; patient SM, eye-region information and explicit fixation instructions](psychology-bibliography.md#a-mechanism-for-impaired-fear-recognition-after-amygdala-damage-by-ralph-adolphs-and-others)

###### Forced-choice emotion recognition

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion recognition](#emotion-recognition)

A procedure in which an observer selects an [emotion](#emotion) interpretation from specified alternatives. Above-chance performance supports [discrimination](#discrimination) among those alternatives under that task; it does not establish that the same categories would be produced spontaneously without prompting.

##### Social salience

↑ **Parent:** [Social perception](#social-perception)

The degree to which a person, feature or category captures [attention](#attention) or is readily available for interpretation in a social setting. Salience helps explain what enters a [social attribution](#social-attribution), but does not by itself determine its causal validity.

## Individual differences

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

Differences among people in measured characteristics, abilities, experiences or behavior. Their explanation requires [attention](#attention) to the population, context and measurement process rather than assuming every observed score difference reflects one stable underlying trait.

### Temperament

↑ **Parent:** [Individual differences](#individual-differences)

Characteristic differences in reactivity and regulation that emerge early in development. They can affect and be affected by social experience; an early appearance does not establish immutability or a purely inherited origin.

### Personality

↑ **Parent:** [Individual differences](#individual-differences)

Patterns of [individual differences](#individual-differences) in characteristic thinking, feeling and behavior. Typical behavioral tendencies should be distinguished from maximal performance on an ability test.

## Clinical psychology

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

The scientific study, assessment and psychological treatment of distress, impairment and [mental disorders](psychiatry.md#mental-disorder). A [clinical formulation](#clinical-formulation) proposes processes relevant to a particular presentation, while a diagnosis groups observed features; the two serve different purposes.

### Language disorder

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

A pattern of [language](README.md#language) difficulties associated with persistent functional impact. A [language](README.md#language) difference related to experience or multilingual context is not by itself a disorder.

#### Specific language impairment

↑ **Parent:** [Language disorder](#language-disorder)

A historical classification used with differing inclusion and exclusion criteria. It should not be assumed identical to every current [DLD](animal-behaviour.md#developmental-language-disorder) definition when comparing studies.

#### CATALISE

↑ **Parent:** [Language disorder](#language-disorder)

A multinational Delphi project developing consensus on identification and terminology for developmental [language](README.md#language) difficulties. Its terminology agreement should be distinguished from an experiment establishing a natural diagnostic boundary.[Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry 58, 1068–1080; 57-member terminology consensus, impairment, prognosis and biomedical associations](psychology-bibliography.md#phase-2-of-catalise-a-multinational-and-multidisciplinary-delphi-consensus-study-of-problems-with-language-development-terminology-by-dorothy-v-m-bishop-and-others)

### Psychological trauma

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

An experience with potentially severe psychological consequences, interpreted within the person's circumstances and available support. [Trauma](#psychological-trauma) [exposure](#exposure-therapy) does not imply that everyone develops a persistent disorder.

### Mechanism of psychological treatment

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

A process through which an intervention produces an outcome. Temporal [mediation](statistical-modelling.md#statistical-mediation), experimental target manipulation and active treatment comparisons supply different constraints; a successful package does not prove its complete explanatory theory.

### Clinical supervision

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

Structured review supporting clinical reasoning, competence and treatment delivery. The content of supervision can matter when an [outcome feedback](#outcome-feedback) intervention depends on clinicians responding to an alert.

### Psychotherapy

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

Clinical intervention using psychological methods to address [distress](#distress), impairment or a specified psychological difficulty. Treatment packages can include several techniques; demonstrating benefit from the package does not establish the necessity of each.

### Virtual reality therapy

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

Use of a computer-generated interactive environment in [psychological treatment](#psychotherapy) or experiments. Head tracking and a head-mounted display can standardise social encounters, while real-world transfer, accessibility and tolerability remain empirical questions.

#### Virtual-reality delusion experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Virtual reality therapy](#virtual-reality-therapy)

Freeman and colleagues compared graded virtual social [exposure](#exposure-therapy) with threat testing that included [safety behavior](animal-behaviour.md#safety-behavior) reduction, assessing [belief](#belief) conviction and later real-world [distress](#distress). The brief small-sample experiment probes a combined causal manipulation rather than establishing durable effects of every component.[British Journal of Psychiatry 209, 62–67; 30 participants, graded social environments, safety reduction versus exposure and real-world assessment](psychology-bibliography.md#virtual-reality-in-the-treatment-of-persecutory-delusions-randomised-controlled-experimental-study-testing-how-to-reduce-delusional-conviction-by-daniel-freeman-and-others)

### Contributory-causal treatment development

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

A research approach that identifies a potentially modifiable maintaining process, changes it experimentally and tests consequences for a clinical difficulty. Changing one contributory cause need not eliminate the difficulty, and a multicomponent manipulation does not identify one exclusive pathway.[Lancet Psychiatry 2, 305–313; 150 patients, six worry sessions plus usual care versus usual care, eight-/24-week outcomes](psychology-bibliography.md#effects-of-cognitive-behaviour-therapy-for-worry-on-persecutory-delusions-in-patients-with-psychosis-wit-a-parallel-single-blind-randomised-controlled-trial-with-a-mediation-analysis-by-daniel-freeman-and-others)

#### OASIS insomnia trial

↑ **Parent:** [Contributory-causal treatment development](#contributory-causal-treatment-development)

A trial of digital [CBT](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy) for insomnia versus usual practice in university students with insomnia. Changes in psychotic experiences support investigation of sleep as a contributory process, but the population and comparator constrain application to clinical [delusions](#delusion).[Lancet Psychiatry 4, 749–758; 3755 university students with insomnia, digital CBT versus usual practice and population limits](psychology-bibliography.md#the-effects-of-improving-sleep-on-mental-health-oasis-a-randomised-controlled-trial-with-mediation-analysis-by-daniel-freeman-and-others)

#### Worry Intervention Trial

↑ **Parent:** [Contributory-causal treatment development](#contributory-causal-treatment-development)

A [randomised](causal-inference.md#randomization) trial of a brief worry-focused intervention added to standard care for persistent [persecutory delusions](#persecutory-delusion). The standard-care comparator permits inference about the added package, while unequal therapeutic contact limits a more ingredient-specific claim.[Lancet Psychiatry 2, 305–313; 150 patients, six worry sessions plus usual care versus usual care, eight-/24-week outcomes](psychology-bibliography.md#effects-of-cognitive-behaviour-therapy-for-worry-on-persecutory-delusions-in-patients-with-psychosis-wit-a-parallel-single-blind-randomised-controlled-trial-with-a-mediation-analysis-by-daniel-freeman-and-others)

### Multisystemic therapy

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

An intensive family- and community-oriented intervention for serious adolescent behavioural difficulties. [Treatment](causal-inference.md#treatment) addresses maintaining factors across home, peers and school. Its comparative effectiveness depends on population, delivery and the services available to the control group.[Lancet Psychiatry 5, 119–133; Methods, primary outcome and Findings at 18 months](psychology-bibliography.md#multisystemic-therapy-versus-management-as-usual-in-the-treatment-of-adolescent-antisocial-behaviour-start-by-peter-fonagy-and-others)

### Therapeutic alliance

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

A collaborative treatment relationship involving shared goals, tasks and trust. A measure of reported [empathy](#empathy) or agreement is not by itself an observation of all aspects of the [therapeutic alliance](#therapeutic-alliance).

### Psychopathology

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

The study and description of mental difficulties and the processes associated with their development and persistence. A [symptom](#symptom) description, a diagnostic category, an etiological explanation and a maintaining mechanism answer different questions.

#### Psychopathy

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopathology](#psychopathology)

A construct involving characteristic affective, interpersonal and behavioural features. Findings in a selected offender sample should not be generalized to everyone with antisocial behaviour or used to infer an individual’s moral character from a brain signal.

#### Psychosis

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopathology](#psychopathology)

A clinical description involving marked disturbances in perception, belief, thought or related functioning, assessed in context. [Psychosis](#psychosis) is not synonymous with [schizophrenia](psychiatry.md#schizophrenia), dangerousness or loss of all capacities.

##### Hallucination

↑ **Parent:** [Psychosis](#psychosis)

A perception-like experience without a corresponding external stimulus, distinguished from a mistaken interpretation of an actual stimulus. The modality, context and impact matter for clinical interpretation.

##### Aberrant salience

↑ **Parent:** [Psychosis](#psychosis)

A theoretical account in which inappropriate assignment of significance to events contributes to psychotic experience and its interpretation. It links valuation, [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) and belief formation without making them identical.

###### Salience Attribution Test

↑ **Parent:** [Aberrant salience](#aberrant-salience)

A task using multidimensional cues for which one feature predicts [reinforcement](evolution.md#reinforcement) and another does not. Explicit ratings and response times assess adaptive and aberrant cue effects separately. An absolute rating difference also increases with measurement noise.[Psychological Medicine 39, 199–209; Salience Attribution Test, Results and study limitations](psychology-bibliography.md#do-patients-with-schizophrenia-exhibit-aberrant-salience-by-jonathan-p-roiser-and-others)

##### Delusion

↑ **Parent:** [Psychosis](#psychosis)

A conviction assessed in relation to the evidence, its responsiveness to alternatives and the person's cultural and clinical context. Disagreement, an unfamiliar cultural explanation or unusual content alone is not a sufficient assessment.

###### Delusional conviction

↑ **Parent:** [Delusion](#delusion)

The degree of confidence in a delusional [belief](#belief), often measured dimensionally. A change in conviction is not automatically identical to categorical [recovery](probability-and-statistics.md#clinical-recovery), reduced [distress](#distress) or restored functioning.

###### Persecutory delusion

↑ **Parent:** [Delusion](#delusion)

A [belief](#belief) that others intend harm, assessed as a [delusion](#delusion) in its clinical and contextual setting. Psychological formulations examine processes such as [worry](#worry), felt vulnerability and [safety behaviors](animal-behaviour.md#safety-behavior) without assuming a single cause of every presentation.

#### Psychotic experience

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopathology](#psychopathology)

An experience resembling phenomena assessed in [psychosis](#psychosis), such as a hallucination or unusual conviction. [Psychotic experiences](#psychotic-experience) can occur with or without a clinical disorder; a research definition must specify persistence, context and consequences.

##### Psychosis continuum hypothesis

↑ **Parent:** [Psychotic experience](#psychotic-experience)

The hypothesis that some [psychosis](#psychosis)-related experiences vary dimensionally across clinical and nonclinical populations. Overlap in a dimension does not imply that all people share the same cause, prognosis or [need for care](#need-for-care).

#### Hoarding

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopathology](#psychopathology)

Persistent retention or accumulation of possessions. In clinical research, the observable accumulation must be distinguished from the reasons for retaining objects, difficulty discarding, environmental constraints and associated impairment.

##### Attachment to possessions

↑ **Parent:** [Hoarding](#hoarding)

Emotional significance assigned to objects, including their relation to memory, identity or anticipated use. A research account must distinguish attachment from inability to categorize or act on a decision.

##### Discarding difficulty

↑ **Parent:** [Hoarding](#hoarding)

Difficulty making or carrying out decisions to relinquish possessions. Measuring [discarding difficulty](#discarding-difficulty) separately from clutter helps distinguish a proposed process from an outcome also influenced by space, resources and other household members.

#### Symptom equivalence does not establish mechanistic identity

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopathology](#psychopathology)

Let $Z$ index either of two candidate pathways and suppose $P(Y=1\mid Z=1)=P(Y=1\mid Z=2)=p$ for a measured [symptom](#symptom) $Y$. For any mixing [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) $\pi=P(Z=1)$, marginalization gives $P(Y=1)=\pi p+(1-\pi)p=p$. An independent sample with $k$ positive observations out of $n$ therefore has [likelihood](maximum-likelihood-estimation.md#likelihood-function) $p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$, independent of $\pi$. The [symptom](#symptom) alone does not identify the mixture proportion or pathway. This is an [identifiability](statistical-model.md#identifiability) counterexample, not an empirical claim that real clinical pathways necessarily have identical [symptom](#symptom) distributions. Additional indicators or [interventions](causal-inference.md#treatment) may distinguish them.

#### Equifinality

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopathology](#psychopathology)

Convergence of different starting conditions or processes on a similar outcome. [Equifinality](#equifinality) cautions against inferring a unique mechanism from a [symptom](#symptom) alone; it does not show that a syndrome-level classification is useless.

#### Etiology

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopathology](#psychopathology)

The origins or causes of a condition. An etiological factor can differ from the process that currently maintains a difficulty, so changing a maintainer need not establish the original cause.

#### Nosology

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopathology](#psychopathology)

The classification of disorders. A useful [nosology](#nosology) supports reliable communication and informative predictions; reliability of a category is different from validity of a proposed cause.

#### Symptom

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopathology](#psychopathology)

An experience or difficulty relevant to assessment. The same [symptom](#symptom) can occur in different conditions or outside a disorder; interpreting it requires its wider context, course and consequences.

#### Clinical syndrome

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopathology](#psychopathology)

A recognizable pattern of associated experiences or signs with clinical significance. A coherent [clinical syndrome](#clinical-syndrome) need not have a single cause, and covariance among its indicators does not alone establish their causal organization.

### Diathesis-stress model

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

An account in which vulnerability and stressful circumstances jointly influence an outcome. A [psychological stressor](#psychological-stressor) can increase risk without being universally necessary or sufficient, and the vulnerability can itself have developmental and environmental causes.

#### Stress sensitization

↑ **Parent:** [Diathesis-stress model](#diathesis-stress-model)

A pattern in which prior [adversity](#adversity) increases sensitivity to later [psychological stress](#psychological-stress), such as a lower subsequent [psychological stress](#psychological-stress) level accompanying [depression](#depression). Demonstrating the pattern does not by itself identify its mechanism or exclude differences in [exposure](causal-inference.md#exposure) measurement and prior liability.[2000, sensitization hypothesis and prospective test](psychology-bibliography.md#depression-and-sensitization-to-stressors-among-young-women-as-a-function-of-childhood-adversity-by-constance-hammen-and-others)

### Psychological resilience

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

Successful adaptation in the context of significant [adversity](#adversity), specified by outcome domain, developmental context and time. It is not simply the absence of disorder in an unexposed person, a universally favorable [personality](#personality) profile or proof of invulnerability. Capacities and resources proposed to explain adaptation should be measured separately from the outcome they are meant to explain.[2000, definition and multidimensionality](psychology-bibliography.md#the-construct-of-resilience-by-suniya-s-luthar-and-others)

#### Stress inoculation

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological resilience](#psychological-resilience)

A proposed protective effect of manageable prior challenges on later responses. A curved association between lifetime [adversity](#adversity) and current functioning is not sufficient to establish that effect causally or identify a safe beneficial [exposure](causal-inference.md#exposure) for an individual. Human observational and animal experimental evidence answer different questions.[2010, nonmonotonic longitudinal associations](psychology-bibliography.md#whatever-does-not-kill-us-by-mark-d-seery-and-others)

#### Promotive factor

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological resilience](#psychological-resilience)

A resource associated with more favorable functioning across the [exposure](causal-inference.md#exposure) levels under study. This describes a general favorable association, distinguished here from an [exposure](causal-inference.md#exposure)-dependent [stress-buffering interaction](biology.md#stress-buffering-interaction). Terminology varies across the literature, so a study should specify its statistical and causal meaning.

#### Measurement of psychological resilience

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological resilience](#psychological-resilience)

Measurement relating documented [adversity](#adversity) to subsequent adaptation. A design specifies [exposure](causal-inference.md#exposure), outcome, baseline functioning and follow-up; capacity [questionnaires](#questionnaire), trajectories and [exposure](causal-inference.md#exposure)-conditioned [regression residual](probability-and-statistics.md#regression-residual) scores operationalize different questions.

##### Residualized psychological resilience score

↑ **Parent:** [Measurement of psychological resilience](#measurement-of-psychological-resilience)

An [exposure](causal-inference.md#exposure)-conditioned adaptation measure. With distress $D_i$, [exposure](causal-inference.md#exposure) $S_i$ and predicted distress $\widehat m(S_i)$, the displayed convention makes higher scores mean less distress than predicted. The result depends on [exposure](causal-inference.md#exposure) measurement, reference population and model. A [regression residual](probability-and-statistics.md#regression-residual) can include [measurement error](probability-and-statistics.md#measurement-error) and omitted causes rather than a distinct protective capacity.

For [ordinary least squares](normal-linear-model.md#ordinary-least-squares) $D=X\widehat\beta+e$, the [normal equation](normal-linear-model.md#normal-equation) gives $X^Te=0$. Thus $R=-e$ is also orthogonal to the fitted predictors; with an intercept its sample mean is zero. A zero in-sample linear association between [exposure](causal-inference.md#exposure) and this score can therefore be an algebraic consequence of its construction, not evidence that [exposure](causal-inference.md#exposure) has no effect. Independent validation is needed before assigning substantive meaning to the [regression residual](probability-and-statistics.md#regression-residual).

##### Connor-Davidson Resilience Scale

↑ **Parent:** [Measurement of psychological resilience](#measurement-of-psychological-resilience)

A [self-report](#self-report) measure of [coping](#coping)-related qualities developed by [Kathryn M. Connor](psychiatry.md#kathryn-m-connor) and [Jonathan R. T. Davidson](psychiatry.md#jonathan-r-t-davidson). The original version has 25 items scored from zero to four. [Measurement reliability](#measurement-reliability) and treatment-associated change concern the measured [self-reports](#self-report); they do not substitute for prospective evidence of adaptation to a documented [adversity](#adversity).[2003, original scale and validation](psychology-bibliography.md#development-of-a-new-resilience-scale-by-kathryn-m-connor-and-jonathan-r-t-davidson)

##### Psychological resilience trajectory

↑ **Parent:** [Measurement of psychological resilience](#measurement-of-psychological-resilience)

A pattern of functioning across time around [adversity](#adversity). Stable adaptation and [recovery after adversity](#recovery-after-adversity) can produce similar scores at a late follow-up while reflecting different histories. Estimated trajectory classes depend on measurement and statistical assumptions; they should not automatically be treated as discrete natural types.

###### Delayed psychological distress

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological resilience trajectory](#psychological-resilience-trajectory)

Increasing difficulty that becomes evident later rather than immediately after an event. Sparse follow-up or selective attrition can hide such patterns or make their apparent onset misleading.

###### Recovery after adversity

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological resilience trajectory](#psychological-resilience-trajectory)

A return toward earlier or adequate functioning after disruption. [Recovery after adversity](#recovery-after-adversity) differs from maintaining low disturbance throughout, and its evaluation depends on the baseline and observation window.

### Access to mental health care

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

The practical possibility of recognising need, seeking and receiving appropriate support, and continuing to effective treatment. Referral counts, treatment starts, treatment completion and clinical benefit are different stages. Equity assessment must include eligible people who never enter or discontinue care.[European Child and Adolescent Psychiatry 27, 1023–1031; interviews with parents of sixteen school-identified children aged 7–11](psychology-bibliography.md#barriers-and-facilitators-to-parents-seeking-and-accessing-professional-support-for-anxiety-disorders-in-children-qualitative-interview-study-by-tessa-reardon-and-others)

#### Improving Access to Psychological Therapies

↑ **Parent:** [Access to mental health care](#access-to-mental-health-care)

The historical English programme expanding evidence-based psychological care for [depression](#depression) and [anxiety](#anxiety). Sessional outcome measurement made service results unusually complete and publicly inspectable. The clinical effects of particular feedback arrangements should be distinguished from the feasibility and transparency of collecting data.[Lancet 391, 679–686; 98% outcome coverage among treated patients, organisational predictors and between-year analyses](psychology-bibliography.md#transparency-about-the-outcomes-of-mental-health-services-iapt-approach-an-analysis-of-public-data-by-david-m-clark-and-others)

#### Stepped care

↑ **Parent:** [Access to mental health care](#access-to-mental-health-care)

An arrangement offering an appropriate initial level of intervention with planned monitoring and escalation when necessary. Lower-intensity care must not become an indefinite holding pathway for nonresponders or replace immediate specialist assessment where indicated.

#### Barrier to mental health care

↑ **Parent:** [Access to mental health care](#access-to-mental-health-care)

A condition that obstructs recognising need, seeking support, entering care or receiving effective intervention. Cost, scheduling, language, service navigation and digital exclusion may operate at different points and require different responses.

### Psychoeducation

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

An explanation of a difficulty, its proposed maintaining processes and the rationale for intervention. Understanding can support informed participation, but knowledge gained from an explanation is not itself proof of symptom [remission](probability-and-statistics.md#diagnostic-remission).

### Child and adolescent clinical psychology

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

The study, assessment and psychological treatment of clinically significant difficulties during childhood and [adolescence](organism.md#adolescence). Developmental understanding, family relationships, school contexts and the young person's perspective affect both assessment and intervention. Evidence from one age range must not be assumed to establish effectiveness across all children and [adolescents](organism.md#adolescent).

#### Youth anxiety treatment

↑ **Parent:** [Child and adolescent clinical psychology](#child-and-adolescent-clinical-psychology)

Psychological intervention for [anxiety disorders](psychiatry.md#anxiety-disorder) in children and [adolescents](organism.md#adolescent). Treatment studies should distinguish [diagnostic remission](probability-and-statistics.md#diagnostic-remission), symptom change, global improvement and everyday functioning, as well as immediate outcomes and maintenance. A successful multicomponent intervention establishes an effect of that package under the tested conditions, not the necessity of each included technique.

##### Anxiety management

↑ **Parent:** [Youth anxiety treatment](#youth-anxiety-treatment)

Strategies intended to recognise, tolerate or regulate [anxiety](#anxiety), including [coping](#coping) routines, relaxation and [breathing exercises](#breathing-exercise). These should be distinguished from targeted tests of threat predictions. Whether a technique supports approach or becomes a perceived safety requirement depends on its use, and efficacy of a [CBT](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy) package does not isolate the technique's effect.[Clinical Child and Family Psychology Review 23, 102–121; 75 studies, 111 CBT conditions and component meta-regressions](psychology-bibliography.md#a-meta-analysis-to-guide-the-enhancement-of-cbt-for-childhood-anxiety-exposure-over-anxiety-management-by-stephen-p-h-whiteside-and-others)

###### Emotion identification

↑ **Parent:** [Anxiety management](#anxiety-management)

Recognising and describing emotional experiences. Performance on an emotion-label task and ability to use that understanding in everyday situations are distinct outcomes.

###### Breathing exercise

↑ **Parent:** [Anxiety management](#anxiety-management)

Deliberate alteration or [attention](#attention) to breathing. In an [anxiety](#anxiety) intervention its proposed function should be specified and assessed rather than presuming that any reduction in [arousal](physiology.md#physiological-arousal) changes the meaning assigned to bodily sensations.

###### Relaxation technique

↑ **Parent:** [Anxiety management](#anxiety-management)

An activity intended to reduce muscular tension or [physiological arousal](physiology.md#physiological-arousal). A reduction in immediate [arousal](physiology.md#physiological-arousal) is a different endpoint from durable change in an [anxiety disorder](psychiatry.md#anxiety-disorder).

##### Parent involvement in youth anxiety treatment

↑ **Parent:** [Youth anxiety treatment](#youth-anxiety-treatment)

Caregivers may facilitate access and practice, receive information, change [accommodation](psychiatry.md#family-accommodation), or receive treatment for their own difficulties. These roles imply different mechanisms and should be compared against the same adequate [child](organism.md#child) treatment when testing augmentation. Parent-only treatment and adding parent sessions to [child](organism.md#child) treatment answer different questions.[Child and Adolescent Mental Health 24, 242–250; randomised parent-session allocation, primary remission and follow-up](psychology-bibliography.md#a-randomized-controlled-trial-of-internet-delivered-cognitive-behaviour-therapy-for-adolescent-anxiety-disorders-in-a-routine-clinical-care-setting-with-and-without-parent-sessions-by-polly-waite-and-others)

###### Guided parent-delivered cognitive behavioral therapy

↑ **Parent:** [Parent involvement in youth anxiety treatment](#parent-involvement-in-youth-anxiety-treatment)

A therapist supports a caregiver in delivering structured [CBT](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy) activities with their [child](organism.md#child). Guidance includes reviewing practice and overcoming barriers, rather than merely distributing information. Evidence for a specified guided programme does not establish that an unsupported manual has the same effect.[British Journal of Psychiatry 203, 436–444; 194 children, full/brief parent guidance and waiting list; primary-carer eligibility](psychology-bibliography.md#treatment-of-child-anxiety-disorders-via-guided-parent-delivered-cognitive-behavioural-therapy-by-kerstin-thirlwall-and-others)

###### Supportive Parenting for Anxious Childhood Emotions

↑ **Parent:** [Parent involvement in youth anxiety treatment](#parent-involvement-in-youth-anxiety-treatment)

A parent-based treatment that targets [accommodation](psychiatry.md#family-accommodation) and supportive responses to a [child](organism.md#child)'s [anxiety](#anxiety). A [randomised](causal-inference.md#randomization) trial compared it with [child CBT](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy) using prespecified [noninferiority](probability-and-statistics.md#noninferiority-trial) criteria; the tested age range and margins constrain the conclusion.[Journal of the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry 59, 362–372; SPACE noninferiority trial, ages 7–14 and accommodation outcomes](psychology-bibliography.md#parent-based-treatment-as-efficacious-as-cognitive-behavioral-therapy-for-childhood-anxiety-a-randomized-noninferiority-study-of-supportive-parenting-for-anxious-childhood-emotions-by-eli-r-lebowitz-and-others)

###### Parental anxiety

↑ **Parent:** [Parent involvement in youth anxiety treatment](#parent-involvement-in-youth-anxiety-treatment)

[Anxiety](#anxiety) experienced by a parent, distinguished from the [child](organism.md#child)'s symptoms and from parental [accommodation](psychiatry.md#family-accommodation). It may affect burden and participation, but association with [child](organism.md#child) difficulty does not establish that treating it necessarily augments [child](organism.md#child) treatment.[Health Technology Assessment 19(38); 211-family trial, maternal CBT and mother–child interaction arms](psychology-bibliography.md#treatment-of-childhood-anxiety-disorder-in-the-context-of-maternal-anxiety-disorder-a-randomised-controlled-trial-and-economic-analysis-by-cathy-creswell-and-others)

### Coping

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

Cognitive and behavioral efforts to manage relevant demands or distress. A response that reduces distress immediately can nevertheless maintain a difficulty if it prevents useful [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) or functioning.

#### Coping flexibility

↑ **Parent:** [Coping](#coping)

Adjusting [coping](#coping) responses to situational demands and available options. A response effective under a controllable short-term challenge may be less useful under prolonged uncontrollable [adversity](#adversity).

### Psychological stress

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

Responses to demands or threats appraised as taxing or exceeding relevant resources. Stressful experiences may contribute to risk without determining that every exposed person develops a disorder.

#### Stress generation

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological stress](#psychological-stress)

A process in which symptoms or related characteristics increase the probability of subsequent stressful experiences. It differs from sensitization, which concerns a changed response to [psychological stress](#psychological-stress), although the two can participate in a feedback loop.

#### Psychological stressor

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological stress](#psychological-stress)

An event or condition placing demands on adaptation, distinguished from appraisal and the resulting psychological or physiological response. Comparable event labels can hide differences in severity, duration and controllability.

### Relapse prevention

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

Intervention intended to reduce return of an established disorder or its symptoms after improvement. It is important care, but its evidence should not automatically be reported as prevention of a first-ever disorder.

### Depression

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

Depressive mood and related symptoms, or a context-dependent shorthand for a depressive disorder. Research should specify whether the outcome is symptom severity, a current diagnosed episode, first onset or recurrence.

### Cognitive behavioral therapy

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

A family of psychological treatments using explicit formulations and structured cognitive and behavioral procedures. Treatment tests and modifies relevant [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning), interpretations and behavior; its content depends on the problem and the evidence rather than on one universal technique.

#### Cognitive bias modification

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy)

Experimental or intervention procedures intended to change biased [information processing](#information-processing). Demonstrating changed responses on the training task does not automatically demonstrate durable transfer or effective clinical treatment.

#### Cognitive behavioral therapy for psychosis

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy)

[Psychological treatment](#psychotherapy) using an individual [formulation](#clinical-formulation) and collaborative examination of difficulties associated with psychotic experiences. Symptom-specific protocols, general support and usual care provide different comparisons for assessing incremental benefit.

##### SlowMo

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy for psychosis](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy-for-psychosis)

A blended digital and face-to-face reasoning intervention for [paranoia](#paranoia). Its 2021 trial did not establish a significant benefit on the prespecified total [paranoia](#paranoia) score at 24 weeks, despite favourable secondary results. This illustrates why endpoint, timing and process engagement matter when evaluating innovation.[JAMA Psychiatry 78, 714–725; 361 in analysis, prespecified 24-week GPTS total endpoint and secondary outcomes](psychology-bibliography.md#effects-of-slowmo-a-blended-digital-therapy-targeting-reasoning-on-paranoia-among-people-with-psychosis-a-randomized-clinical-trial-by-philippa-garety-and-others)

##### Feeling Safe Programme

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy for psychosis](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy-for-psychosis)

A personalised intervention for persistent [persecutory delusions](#persecutory-delusion), combining modules selected to address relevant maintaining processes. Its [active control](design-of-experiments.md#active-control) trial compared it with same-therapist befriending alongside usual care. The continuous conviction result and categorical [recovery](probability-and-statistics.md#clinical-recovery) comparison require separate interpretation.[Lancet Psychiatry 8, 696–707; 130 patients, same-therapist befriending, six-month continuous conviction and categorical recovery distinction](psychology-bibliography.md#comparison-of-a-theoretically-driven-cognitive-therapy-the-feeling-safe-programme-with-befriending-for-the-treatment-of-persistent-persecutory-delusions-a-parallel-single-blind-randomised-controlled-trial-by-daniel-freeman-and-others)

#### Becoming a Man

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy)

A structured youth programme using discussion, rehearsal and examination of automatic interpretations to support better decisions in provocative situations. Randomised school-based evaluations in Chicago tested outcomes including arrests and school engagement; participation-effect estimates and effects of a randomised offer should be distinguished.[Quarterly Journal of Economics 132, 1–54; two Becoming a Man randomized trials and mechanism analyses](psychology-bibliography.md#thinking-fast-and-slow-some-field-experiments-to-reduce-crime-and-dropout-in-chicago-by-sara-b-heller-and-others)

#### Cognitive restructuring

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy)

Examining and revising interpretations or assumptions that influence [emotion](#emotion) and action, typically with explicit alternatives and tests rather than unsupported reassurance. Its contribution to a [treatment](causal-inference.md#treatment) package requires component-level evidence.

#### Stress inoculation training

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy)

Structured [coping](#coping)-skills training with practice and application to appropriate challenges. The intervention is distinct from the hypothesis that naturally occurring [adversity](#adversity) causes later protection; it does not mean deliberately imposing harmful experiences.

#### Internet-delivered cognitive behavioral therapy

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy)

Delivery of structured [CBT](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy) content through an internet platform, potentially with clinician guidance, parent modules and live contact. Efficacy is a property of the specified programme, population and support arrangement, rather than the delivery medium alone.[Lancet Child and Adolescent Health 2, 792–801; 131 children aged 8–12, internet CBT versus child-directed play](psychology-bibliography.md#efficacy-and-cost-effectiveness-of-therapist-guided-internet-cognitive-behavioural-therapy-for-paediatric-anxiety-disorders-a-single-centre-single-blind-randomised-controlled-trial-by-maral-jolstedt-and-others)

#### Transdiagnostic cognitive behavioral therapy

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy)

An approach addressing processes shared across diagnostic categories. It can still use an individual [clinical formulation](#clinical-formulation) and disorder-relevant behavioural experiments; transdiagnostic delivery need not mean identical exercises for every patient.

#### Disorder-specific cognitive behavioral therapy

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy)

A protocol organised around mechanisms proposed to maintain a particular disorder. Its comparative value requires an adequate alternative with comparable delivery, competence and assessment; superiority to waiting list alone does not establish superiority to a flexible generic programme.

#### Video feedback

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy)

Use of a recording to compare a concrete prediction about observable behavior with recorded evidence. Preparation matters: selective flaw-searching can reproduce the original bias rather than provide a fair test of the prediction.

#### Exposure therapy

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy)

Planned contact with appropriate feared cues or situations to support new [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) and reduce maladaptive avoidance. Its rationale requires informed, collaborative tasks and [attention](#attention) to the behaviors that could block [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning); exposure does not mean deliberately creating genuine danger.

##### Inhibitory-learning exposure strategy

↑ **Parent:** [Exposure therapy](#exposure-therapy)

A strategy intended to strengthen [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) that competes with a threat association and improve its later retrieval. Expectancy testing and varied contexts are examples. A coherent [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) rationale does not itself demonstrate the optimal clinical implementation for every developmental group.[Behaviour Research and Therapy 58, 10–23; theoretical framework and illustrative implementation strategies](psychology-bibliography.md#maximizing-exposure-therapy-an-inhibitory-learning-approach-by-michelle-g-craske-and-others)

##### One-session treatment of specific phobia

↑ **Parent:** [Exposure therapy](#exposure-therapy)

A concentrated intervention combining approach practice and [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) about a phobic stimulus. A youth [randomised](causal-inference.md#randomization) trial compared this package with education-support and waiting list. Package efficacy is distinct from proof that one session is the optimal dose for all presentations.[Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology 77, 504–516; 196 youths, exposure, education-support and waiting-list conditions](psychology-bibliography.md#one-session-treatment-of-specific-phobias-in-youth-a-randomized-clinical-trial-in-the-united-states-and-sweden-by-thomas-h-ollendick-and-others)

##### Exposure expectancy testing

↑ **Parent:** [Exposure therapy](#exposure-therapy)

Specifying a feared prediction, arranging an informative and appropriately safe approach task, and comparing the observed outcome with the prediction. The review should include uncertainty and alternative explanations rather than compel a reassuring conclusion. Necessary protections and accessibility adjustments are retained.

##### Exposure and response prevention

↑ **Parent:** [Exposure therapy](#exposure-therapy)

Exposure to appropriate [obsession](psychiatry.md#obsession)-related triggers while refraining from [compulsions](psychiatry.md#compulsion) and functionally equivalent reassurance or neutralization. The target is [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) to respond differently to the cue and uncertainty, rather than first eliminating every intrusive thought.

##### Expectancy violation

↑ **Parent:** [Exposure therapy](#exposure-therapy)

A discrepancy between a predicted outcome and the outcome experienced. During exposure, explicit predictions can make such discrepancies informative; a [safety behavior](animal-behaviour.md#safety-behavior) may prevent revision if the person attributes the benign outcome to that behavior.

##### Interoceptive exposure

↑ **Parent:** [Exposure therapy](#exposure-therapy)

Exposure to appropriate bodily sensations relevant to a feared prediction. Assessment and supervision determine suitable exercises; the inference concerns a specific tested prediction, not the claim that all bodily symptoms are harmless.

#### Behavioral experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy)

A planned test of a specific prediction relevant to a psychological difficulty. The design specifies what would be expected under competing interpretations and reviews the observed outcome, including alternative explanations; it is not simply repeating a feared activity without a [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) question.

#### Cognitive therapy

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive behavioral therapy](#cognitive-behavioral-therapy)

Treatment examining and modifying interpretations, assumptions and predictions that contribute to distress and impairment. [Behavioral experiments](#behavioral-experiment) can generate new evidence rather than relying only on verbal argument or reassurance.

### Threat appraisal

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

Interpretation of an event or sensation in terms of its danger, likelihood, consequences and available [coping](#coping). An appraisal becomes a proposed maintaining process when it predicts continuing fear and changes in it alter the relevant response.

#### Threat expectancy

↑ **Parent:** [Threat appraisal](#threat-appraisal)

An expectation that a specified adverse outcome will occur under particular conditions. An informative [behavioral experiment](#behavioral-experiment) states the prediction and what observed outcome would count against it.

#### Intolerance of uncertainty

↑ **Parent:** [Threat appraisal](#threat-appraisal)

A difficulty accepting unresolved possibilities, often accompanied by demands for certainty. It can contribute to checking or [reassurance seeking](animal-behaviour.md#reassurance-seeking), but the function of those responses depends on context and objectively relevant risks.

#### Catastrophic misinterpretation

↑ **Parent:** [Threat appraisal](#threat-appraisal)

Interpreting an ambiguous or relatively benign event as evidence of imminent disaster. In cognitive accounts of [panic disorder](psychiatry.md#panic-disorder), [anxiety](#anxiety)-induced bodily sensations can appear to confirm the threatening interpretation, forming a feedback loop.

### Anxiety

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

Apprehension and associated cognitive, bodily and behavioral responses to anticipated threat. [Anxiety](#anxiety) can be adaptive; its presence alone does not imply an [anxiety disorder](psychiatry.md#anxiety-disorder).

#### Contamination fear

↑ **Parent:** [Anxiety](#anxiety)

[Fear](#fear) concerning contact with perceived contaminants or a sense of contamination. Perceived contamination, disgust, danger and compulsive responses are related but distinct measurements.

#### Worry

↑ **Parent:** [Anxiety](#anxiety)

Repetitive thought about possible adverse outcomes. It can increase the availability of threat explanations, but occurrence alongside another problem does not by itself establish a causal contribution.

#### Health anxiety

↑ **Parent:** [Anxiety](#anxiety)

Concern about having or acquiring illness. Its intensity, interpretation, persistence and effects on functioning vary; [health anxiety](#health-anxiety) can coexist with physical illness, and concern alone does not establish a disorder.

##### Severe health anxiety

↑ **Parent:** [Health anxiety](#health-anxiety)

Persistent and disabling concern about illness, often involving threatening interpretations, checking, [reassurance seeking](animal-behaviour.md#reassurance-seeking) or avoidance. Its [psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment) is distinct from deciding whether a physical illness exists; a [clinical formulation](#clinical-formulation) must allow both to be relevant.

#### Anxiety sensitivity

↑ **Parent:** [Anxiety](#anxiety)

Concern that anxiety-related sensations will have harmful consequences. It is conceptually distinct from the current intensity of [anxiety](#anxiety) and from objective interoceptive accuracy.[Behaviour Research and Therapy 42, 439–448; 79 children aged 8–11, mental tracking and symptom/sensitivity measures](psychology-bibliography.md#heart-beat-perception-panic-somatic-symptoms-and-anxiety-sensitivity-in-children-by-thalia-c-eley-and-others)

#### Social anxiety

↑ **Parent:** [Anxiety](#anxiety)

[Anxiety](#anxiety) concerning social encounters or evaluation by others. Symptom-selected [social anxiety](#social-anxiety) samples should not be described as diagnosed [social anxiety disorder](psychiatry.md#social-anxiety-disorder) unless that [diagnosis](#clinical-diagnosis) was assessed.

### Psychological assessment

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical psychology](#clinical-psychology)

Systematic collection and interpretation of information about psychological functioning. Interviews, observations, records and tests provide complementary evidence; a [questionnaire](#questionnaire) score does not by itself determine diagnosis, mechanism or treatment.

#### Practice effect

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

A change in performance resulting from prior exposure to the assessment or related procedures. Repeated measurements can support developmental inference while also changing task familiarity.

#### Reading comprehension assessment

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

Measurement of understanding under specified text, question, response and timing conditions. An inference about general comprehension requires adequate sampling and evidence that the score is not dominated by an unintended demand.[Scientific Studies of Reading 12, 281–300; five measures, differential decoding/listening dependence and developmental changes](psychology-bibliography.md#reading-comprehension-tests-vary-in-the-skills-they-assess-differential-dependence-on-decoding-and-oral-comprehension-by-janice-m-keenan-and-others)

##### Peabody Individual Achievement Test

↑ **Parent:** [Reading comprehension assessment](#reading-comprehension-assessment)

##### Qualitative Reading Inventory

↑ **Parent:** [Reading comprehension assessment](#reading-comprehension-assessment)

##### Gray Oral Reading Test

↑ **Parent:** [Reading comprehension assessment](#reading-comprehension-assessment)

##### Cloze test

↑ **Parent:** [Reading comprehension assessment](#reading-comprehension-assessment)

A task requiring restoration of omitted text elements. The amount of surrounding context and the response format determine whether local word knowledge, [grammar](README.md#grammar) or wider discourse integration is needed.

#### Clinical caseness

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

Meeting a specified [symptom](#symptom) or diagnostic threshold used to define eligibility or outcome. Crossing a questionnaire threshold and satisfying a diagnostic interview are different operational definitions.

#### Routine outcome monitoring

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

Repeated measurement of outcomes during ordinary clinical care. It can reveal trajectories, deterioration and lack of progress; an additional design is required to establish that acting on the information improves outcomes.

##### Clinical outcome coverage

↑ **Parent:** [Routine outcome monitoring](#routine-outcome-monitoring)

The proportion of a clearly specified clinical population with usable outcome measurements. High coverage among treated patients does not establish coverage among all referrals, all people needing care or a common long-term endpoint.

##### Outcome feedback

↑ **Parent:** [Routine outcome monitoring](#routine-outcome-monitoring)

Providing clinicians or patients with interpretable progress information, sometimes including alerts for trajectories at risk of poor outcome. A trial of feedback linked to supervisory action tests that arrangement, rather than measurement in isolation.[Lancet Psychiatry 5, 564–572; 77 participating therapists, 2233 patients, cluster allocation and not-on-track outcomes](psychology-bibliography.md#feedback-informed-treatment-versus-usual-psychological-treatment-for-depression-and-anxiety-a-multisite-open-label-cluster-randomised-controlled-trial-by-jaime-delgadillo-and-others)

###### Not-on-track treatment trajectory

↑ **Parent:** [Outcome feedback](#outcome-feedback)

A trajectory classified by a specified rule as unlikely to reach an expected outcome. An alert should prompt clinical review; its validity, false alarms and feasible responses require evaluation.

##### Session-by-session outcome monitoring

↑ **Parent:** [Routine outcome monitoring](#routine-outcome-monitoring)

Obtaining outcome measures at successive attended sessions. It can supply a last available score when a planned final contact is missed, while differing endpoint timing and people who never enter treatment remain important limitations.[Behaviour Research and Therapy 47, 910–920; Doncaster and Newham prospective service cohorts, sessional monitoring and ten-month follow-up](psychology-bibliography.md#improving-access-to-psychological-therapy-initial-evaluation-of-two-uk-demonstration-sites-by-david-m-clark-and-others)

#### Green et al. Paranoid Thoughts Scale

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

A [self-report](#self-report) measure used to assess paranoid thinking, including ideas of reference and persecution. The total score and a persecution subscale should not be substituted for each other after a trial has specified its [primary outcome](probability-and-statistics.md#primary-outcome).[JAMA Psychiatry 78, 714–725; 361 in analysis, prespecified 24-week GPTS total endpoint and secondary outcomes](psychology-bibliography.md#effects-of-slowmo-a-blended-digital-therapy-targeting-reasoning-on-paranoia-among-people-with-psychosis-a-randomized-clinical-trial-by-philippa-garety-and-others)

#### Psychotic Symptoms Rating Scale

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

A structured clinical rating instrument assessing dimensions of psychotic [symptoms](#symptom). A specified delusion-conviction item and an aggregate delusion-severity score are different endpoints.[Lancet Psychiatry 8, 696–707; 130 patients, same-therapist befriending, six-month continuous conviction and categorical recovery distinction](psychology-bibliography.md#comparison-of-a-theoretically-driven-cognitive-therapy-the-feeling-safe-programme-with-befriending-for-the-treatment-of-persistent-persecutory-delusions-a-parallel-single-blind-randomised-controlled-trial-by-daniel-freeman-and-others)

#### Paranoia

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

Suspicious or persecutory thinking, varying in severity and conviction. A dimensional [paranoia](#paranoia) score in a nonclinical sample should not be equated with a [diagnosis](#clinical-diagnosis) or with a persistent [persecutory delusion](#persecutory-delusion).

#### Clinical insight

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

Recognition or interpretation of one's experiences and their implications for help. [Clinical insight](#clinical-insight) is multidimensional and variable rather than a complete, all-or-none measure of a person's reasoning or agency.

#### Health Anxiety Inventory

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

A [questionnaire](#questionnaire) developed to measure [health anxiety](#health-anxiety) across relevant clinical and medical contexts. A score quantifies aspects of the psychological response to illness-related concerns; it does not determine the presence of physical disease or replace [clinical diagnosis](#clinical-diagnosis).[2002; original clinical contrasts, sensitivity-to-change studies and medical samples](psychology-bibliography.md#the-health-anxiety-inventory-development-and-validation-of-scales-for-the-measurement-of-health-anxiety-and-hypochondriasis-by-paul-salkovskis-and-others)

#### Strange situation procedure

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

A structured observational procedure using episodes of caregiver presence, separation and reunion to assess attachment-related behavior. Interpretation concerns the organized pattern of behavior in context rather than assuming that either distress or absence of distress alone establishes security.

#### Clinical diagnosis

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

Classification of a presentation using specified diagnostic criteria. A [diagnosis](#clinical-diagnosis) groups observed features; it does not, by itself, identify the causal processes maintaining a particular person's difficulties.

#### Comorbidity

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

Co-occurrence of more than one disorder or clinically relevant condition. Selecting one [principal diagnosis](#principal-diagnosis) does not make additional difficulties irrelevant to formulation, outcomes or access.

#### Principal diagnosis

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

The [diagnosis](#clinical-diagnosis) designated primary under a stated assessment rule. Losing the [principal diagnosis](#principal-diagnosis) does not necessarily mean losing all comorbid disorders.

#### Qualitative interview

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

A structured or flexible interview used to investigate experiences, meanings and processes. It can identify candidate barriers and explain implementation without by itself estimating their population prevalence or the [causal effect](causal-inference.md#causal-effect) of an intervention.

#### Behavioral approach task

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

A structured opportunity to approach a feared stimulus or situation, with behavioural performance and [fear](#fear) measured according to a specified protocol. Novel-context tasks can assess transfer; willingness, safety constraints and task demands still affect the result.

#### Somatic symptom

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

A bodily symptom, without presupposing its cause. Describing a symptom as somatic does not establish whether it arises from medical, psychological or interacting processes.

#### Clinical Global Impression scale

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

Clinician-rated global severity and improvement measures. A rating of much or very much improved on the improvement scale is a response endpoint, not automatically freedom from the original [diagnosis](#clinical-diagnosis).[New England Journal of Medicine 359, 2753–2766; CAMS, Table 2 and twelve-week CGI-I response](psychology-bibliography.md#cognitive-behavioral-therapy-sertraline-or-a-combination-in-childhood-anxiety-by-john-t-walkup-and-others)

<h5 id="clinical-global-impression-improvement-scale">Clinical Global Impression–Improvement scale</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical Global Impression scale](#clinical-global-impression-scale)

#### Functional impairment

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

Interference with everyday activities, relationships or participation. Symptom reduction can occur without full restoration of functioning, so both outcomes are informative.

#### Performance test

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

A task scored by observed responses under specified conditions. Choosing task content, response format and a scoring criterion determines which abilities and method demands can influence the score.

#### Self-report

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

Information a person provides about their own experiences, characteristics or behavior. Response style, self-knowledge and interpretation affect scores; self-perceived ability is not equivalent to demonstrated ability.

#### Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

A clinician-rated measure of obsessive-compulsive symptom severity used to monitor outcomes. Its interpretation depends on assessment and the trial's specified response or remission criterion; a symptom score is not identical to functioning or quality of life.

#### Clinical formulation

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological assessment](#psychological-assessment)

A testable account linking a person's difficulties to relevant precipitating, maintaining and protective processes. A formulation guides intervention and should be revised when predicted effects do not occur; it is more informative than merely repeating the diagnosis.

##### Need for care

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical formulation](#clinical-formulation)

A requirement for support or treatment assessed through difficulties, distress, impairment, risk, context and preferences. Actual service contact is an imperfect proxy because access, willingness and [discrimination](#discrimination) affect who receives care.

##### Treatment component

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical formulation](#clinical-formulation)

A distinguishable element of an [intervention](causal-inference.md#treatment), such as exposure, attentional training or organizing skills. Evidence that a package works is different from evidence that one component is necessary or sufficient.

##### Transdiagnostic process

↑ **Parent:** [Clinical formulation](#clinical-formulation)

A process relevant across more than one diagnostic category. A shared [safety behavior](animal-behaviour.md#safety-behavior) or attentional process can justify common [intervention](causal-inference.md#treatment) principles while its feared meaning and context differ between individuals.

## Attributional style

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

A tendency to explain outcomes using particular kinds of causes. The helplessness reformulation distinguishes internal from external, stable from unstable, and global from specific explanations to address self-esteem, persistence and generalization.

## Affective processing bias

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

A systematic difference in processing emotional information, such as preferential recognition or later recall of negative over positive material. A change in this measure may precede a change in subjective mood.

## Depressed mood

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

A sustained or recurring low affective state. It is one component of [major depressive disorder](psychiatry.md#major-depressive-disorder), not by itself equivalent to that diagnosis.

## Neuropsychology

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

Study of relations between [brain](neuroscience.md#brain) function and cognition or behaviour, including patterns of impairment and preservation after [brain lesions](neuroscience.md#brain-lesion).

### Neurological research participant

↑ **Parent:** [Neuropsychology](#neuropsychology)

A person whose participation in research on neurological function provides evidence about cognition or [brain](neuroscience.md#brain) mechanisms. Case studies may be published under initials or patient codes.

#### List of neurological research participants

### Amnesia

↑ **Parent:** [Neuropsychology](#neuropsychology)

A pathological impairment of memory. Specify the affected time period, task and process rather than assuming loss of all forms of learning or all prior knowledge.

#### Diencephalic amnesia

↑ **Parent:** [Amnesia](#amnesia)

Memory impairment associated with diencephalic damage, including thalamic or mammillary pathways. Connected cortical dysfunction and accompanying pathology complicate precise localization.

##### Korsakoff syndrome

↑ **Parent:** [Diencephalic amnesia](#diencephalic-amnesia)

A severe amnesic syndrome associated with thiamine deficiency, often but not exclusively in the context of alcohol misuse. Its eponym refers to [Sergei Korsakoff](psychiatry.md#sergei-korsakoff). Co-occurring damage varies across patients, making comparison with non-amnesic pathological controls valuable.

#### Retrograde amnesia

↑ **Parent:** [Amnesia](#amnesia)

Loss or impaired retrieval of information acquired before onset of the condition. Its temporal extent and detail can differ from deficits in new learning.

#### Anterograde amnesia

↑ **Parent:** [Amnesia](#amnesia)

Impaired formation or retention of new memories after onset of the condition, distinct from a general loss of sensory perception or intelligence.

### Double dissociation

↑ **Parent:** [Neuropsychology](#neuropsychology)

Two interventions or patient groups show opposite relative impairments on two tasks. This constrains a simple account based only on general task difficulty, but does not prove that the underlying [neural circuits](neuroscience.md#neural-circuit) are independent: task sensitivity, lesion extent and alternative strategies still matter.

#### Crossed performance rejects a single monotone resource

↑ **Parent:** [Double dissociation](#double-dissociation)

Suppose performances on fixed tasks $A$ and $B$ are $f_A(r)$ and $f_B(r)$, where both [functions](function.md) are [nondecreasing](calculus.md#nondecreasing-function) in the same [scalar](vector-space.md#scalar) resource $r$. If patient $P$ performs strictly better than $Q$ on $A$, then $r_P>r_Q$: the reverse ordering would contradict monotonicity. If $Q$ performs strictly better on $B$, the same argument gives $r_Q>r_P$, a contradiction. Thus a genuine crossover rejects this single-resource model.

The conclusion depends on the fixed mappings and task comparability. It does not prove isolated anatomical stores, and it need not apply when patients differ in strategy, sensory access or other task parameters.

### Cognitive development

↑ **Parent:** [Developmental psychology](#developmental-psychology)

Changes in cognitive capacities, representations and their deployment with experience and maturation. A change in test performance may reflect a change in understanding, a change in access or response demands, or both.

#### Teleological reasoning

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive development](#cognitive-development)

Explaining something in terms of a purpose or what it is for. Appropriate functional questions about an artifact or decision must be distinguished from claims about the physical causes of a natural event.

#### Intuitive biology

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive development](#cognitive-development)

Everyday expectations and explanations concerning living things and biological events. [Intuitive biology](#intuitive-biology) can contain structured insights alongside incomplete or mistaken mechanisms; factual [vocabulary](README.md#vocabulary) does not alone establish an integrated theory.

##### Contagion reasoning

↑ **Parent:** [Intuitive biology](#intuitive-biology)

Reasoning about whether and why illness can pass between individuals. A response based on a symptom label differs from an explanation that tracks the relevant cause; such judgments also depend on the child's interpretation of the task.

## Cognitive psychology

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

The study of how information is acquired, represented and used in tasks such as remembering, understanding and reasoning. Behavioral experiments constrain proposed processes; a plausible explanation must predict performance and errors, not merely name an internal faculty.

### Numerical cognition

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Representation and processing of numerical information. [Numerical cognition](#numerical-cognition) includes exact symbolic skills and nonsymbolic quantity processing; an association between them does not show that training one necessarily improves every other skill.

#### Subitizing

↑ **Parent:** [Numerical cognition](#numerical-cognition)

Rapid exact recognition of a small quantity without serial [counting](#counting). [Subitizing](#subitizing) small collections does not imply direct exact representation of arbitrarily large [numbers](arithmetic.md#number) or knowledge of the symbolic [counting](#counting) system.

#### Approximate number system

↑ **Parent:** [Numerical cognition](#numerical-cognition)

A system proposed to represent approximate nonsymbolic quantity. Such representations should be distinguished from exact [number](arithmetic.md#number)-word meanings and symbolic [arithmetic](arithmetic.md); causal transfer from training requires direct evidence.

#### Symbolic magnitude comparison

↑ **Parent:** [Numerical cognition](#numerical-cognition)

Judging relative quantity from numerical symbols. Comparing a symbol pair requires relevant symbol knowledge as well as a magnitude decision; the task should not be treated as a process-pure measure.

#### Number-line estimation

↑ **Parent:** [Numerical cognition](#numerical-cognition)

Placing a [number](arithmetic.md#number) on a line with specified endpoints. Performance depends on numerical representation, spatial interpretation and task strategy; an intervention effect on this task is not automatically a general [arithmetic](arithmetic.md) effect.

#### Number sense

↑ **Parent:** [Numerical cognition](#numerical-cognition)

A broad term for intuitions and skills concerning [numbers](arithmetic.md#number) and quantities. Studies use the term for different tasks and representations, so a [number](arithmetic.md#number)-sense score or training effect should not be assumed to identify one process or general mathematical ability.

### Belief flexibility

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Capacity to consider alternatives and the possibility of being mistaken. It should be distinguished from whether a particular [belief](#belief) is true and from speed of drawing a conclusion.

### Problem solving

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Identifying a goal, generating possible actions and evaluating their consequences. Addressing a realistic remediable problem differs from repeatedly attempting to obtain certainty about hypothetical threats.

### Introspection

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Examination or reporting of one's own mental states. A report of an experience, an accurate prediction of performance and an explanation of the process causing a response are different claims. Experiments can test them separately.[Distinction between reports and access to higher-order processes](psychology-bibliography.md#telling-more-than-we-can-know-verbal-reports-on-mental-processes-by-richard-e-nisbett-and-timothy-d-wilson)

#### Introspection illusion

↑ **Parent:** [Introspection](#introspection)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introspection_illusion)

##### Choice blindness

↑ **Parent:** [Introspection illusion](#introspection-illusion)

Failure to notice a mismatch between a choice and the outcome presented as that choice, sometimes followed by an explanation of the substituted outcome. Face-choice experiments provide an operational example; not every failure to detect a substitution has the same cause.[2005, abstract](psychology-bibliography.md#failure-to-detect-mismatches-between-intention-and-outcome-in-a-simple-decision-task-by-petter-johansson-and-others)

### Cognitive load

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Demand placed on limited processing resources by a task or concurrent activity. A manipulation of [cognitive load](#cognitive-load) may affect [attention](#attention), [working memory](neuroscience.md#working-memory) and strategy together; interpreting a difference requires checking which task information remained available.

### Modularity of mind

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Theoretical accounts in which some processing systems have specialised functions or restricted informational access. Claims about input selectivity, representations, [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) algorithms, developmental origins and mature processing architecture should be distinguished: evidence against one does not automatically settle the others.

#### Linguistic module

↑ **Parent:** [Modularity of mind](#modularity-of-mind)

A proposed specialised system for aspects of [language](README.md#language). Domain-general sequence [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) can contribute to [language acquisition](animal-behaviour.md#language-acquisition) while leaving open whether particular representations or other processing stages are specialised.

### Competence-performance distinction

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

The distinction between a proposed underlying ability or representation and its expression under a particular task’s demands. An easier response format may reveal an ability missed by another format, while scaffolded success need not establish independent, fully general deployment.

### Self-regulation

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Regulation of [attention](#attention), behaviour or emotion in relation to goals. Measures of [executive function](#executive-function), temperament, waiting behaviour and classroom engagement can overlap without being interchangeable.

#### Emotion regulation

↑ **Parent:** [Self-regulation](#self-regulation)

Processes influencing the course, intensity or expression of emotional responses. What is useful depends on the situation and goal; successful regulation does not require suppression of every unpleasant emotion.

##### Cognitive reappraisal

↑ **Parent:** [Emotion regulation](#emotion-regulation)

Changing the interpreted meaning of an event in a way that changes its emotional implications. An experiment that alters interpretation can support a causal role for [cognitive appraisal](#cognitive-appraisal); it does not by itself show that appraisal is necessary for every [emotion](#emotion).

#### Delay of gratification

↑ **Parent:** [Self-regulation](#self-regulation)

Foregoing an immediate outcome in favour of a preferred later one. Waiting can depend on control, strategy, current motivation and expectations that the later outcome will occur.[Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 21, 204–218; experimental manipulations of attention during waiting](psychology-bibliography.md#cognitive-and-attentional-mechanisms-in-delay-of-gratification-by-walter-mischel-and-others)

##### Marshmallow test

↑ **Parent:** [Delay of gratification](#delay-of-gratification)

A task offering an immediate edible reward or a larger or preferred reward after waiting. Reward visibility, instructions, experimenter [reliability](#measurement-reliability) and the maximum permitted waiting time affect its interpretation; a duration is not automatically a pure measure of self-control.

### Reading

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

#### Morphological awareness

↑ **Parent:** [Reading](#reading)

Awareness of the meaningful components of words and their relationships. [Morphological awareness](#morphological-awareness) should be distinguished from biological morphology and from merely recognizing a word's visual shape.

#### Rapid automatized naming

↑ **Parent:** [Reading](#reading)

Rapidly naming a sequence of familiar items such as colors, objects, letters or digits. This coordinated task predicts some literacy outcomes but does not isolate one cognitive or neural process.

#### Reading development

↑ **Parent:** [Reading](#reading)

Development of the knowledge and processes supporting identification and understanding of [written language](README.md#written-language). [Word recognition](neuroscience.md#word-recognition), fluency, [language](README.md#language) comprehension and their coordination can change at different rates; one endpoint can conceal different trajectories.

##### Home literacy environment

↑ **Parent:** [Reading development](#reading-development)

Experiences and resources associated with early literacy, including shared books and direct teaching. These activities can relate to different [language](README.md#language) and [reading](#reading) pathways; observational associations are not prescriptions guaranteed to cause the same outcomes.[Child Development 73, 445–460; 168 middle/upper-middle-class children, home activities and distinct language/literacy pathways](psychology-bibliography.md#parental-involvement-in-the-development-of-children-s-reading-skill-a-five-year-longitudinal-study-by-monique-senechal-and-jo-anne-lefevre)

##### Reading disorder

↑ **Parent:** [Reading development](#reading-development)

Persistent difficulty in an aspect of [reading](#reading) with functional consequences, assessed in context. Word-level difficulty and poor comprehension despite adequate [decoding](#word-decoding) are distinct possible profiles.

###### Multiple-risk account of reading difficulty

↑ **Parent:** [Reading disorder](#reading-disorder)

An account in which several probabilistic risks and protective influences combine rather than one deficit being necessary and sufficient for every case. Shared risks can produce overlap while differing profiles retain practical meaning.[Journal of Speech, Language, and Hearing Research 48, 1378–1396; 527-child longitudinal sample and separate phonological-profile subsample](psychology-bibliography.md#are-specific-language-impairment-and-dyslexia-distinct-disorders-by-hugh-w-catts-and-others)

###### Comorbid dyslexia and developmental language disorder

↑ **Parent:** [Reading disorder](#reading-disorder)

Co-occurring word-reading and broader [oral language](README.md#oral-language) difficulties. Studies selecting nonoverlapping groups are useful contrasts but do not describe the full distribution of mixed profiles.[Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry 61, 672–680; dyslexia-only, DLD-only and comorbid groups at eight, comprehension at eight/nine](psychology-bibliography.md#dyslexia-and-developmental-language-disorder-comorbid-disorders-with-distinct-effects-on-reading-comprehension-by-margaret-j-snowling-and-others)

###### Specific reading-comprehension difficulty

↑ **Parent:** [Reading disorder](#reading-disorder)

Poor understanding of text despite relatively adequate word-reading accuracy. Assessment should examine [oral language](README.md#oral-language), fluency and higher-level processes rather than infer a single cause from the profile.[Psychological Science 21, 1106–1116; text/oral/combined interventions, untreated comparison and eleven-month follow-up](psychology-bibliography.md#ameliorating-children-s-reading-comprehension-difficulties-a-randomized-controlled-trial-by-paula-j-clarke-and-others)

###### Dyslexia

↑ **Parent:** [Reading disorder](#reading-disorder)

A developmental pattern centred on difficulties in accurate or fluent word [reading](#reading) and [spelling](README.md#spelling). It is not synonymous with every comprehension problem, nor necessarily an isolated phonological deficit.

##### Reading instruction

↑ **Parent:** [Reading development](#reading-development)

Teaching intended to develop [written language](README.md#written-language) skills. A [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) principle must be evaluated through specified content, exposure, support, assessment and follow-up rather than inferred from an instructional label.

###### Phonics

↑ **Parent:** [Reading instruction](#reading-instruction)

Teaching relations between written units and speech sounds, including their use in [decoding](#word-decoding) and [spelling](README.md#spelling). The actual mappings, practice and integration with text [reading](#reading) define the intervention.

###### Phonological linkage hypothesis

↑ **Parent:** [Phonics](#phonics)

The proposal that phonological work benefits [reading](#reading) most when linked to [reading instruction](#reading-instruction). A matched-group intervention found greater [reading](#reading) progress for integrated work, even though phonology-only work particularly improved phonological tasks.[Child Development 65, 41–57; matched groups of seven-year-old poor readers, reading/phonology/combined/control conditions](psychology-bibliography.md#ameliorating-early-reading-failure-by-integrating-the-teaching-of-reading-and-phonological-skills-the-phonological-linkage-hypothesis-by-peter-j-hatcher-and-others)

###### Print-to-meaning training

↑ **Parent:** [Reading instruction](#reading-instruction)

Instruction or practice emphasising relations between written forms and meanings. Its effects must be assessed separately on trained items, unfamiliar-word [reading](#reading) and comprehension.

###### Print-to-sound training

↑ **Parent:** [Reading instruction](#reading-instruction)

Instruction or practice emphasising relations between written forms and pronunciations. It can organise the input available for [statistical learning](animal-behaviour.md#distributional-learning), rather than being opposed to [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) from regularities.[Journal of Experimental Psychology: General 146, 826–858; 24 adults, two novel scripts, eight-day training, generalisation and fMRI](psychology-bibliography.md#comparing-and-validating-methods-of-reading-instruction-using-behavioural-and-neural-findings-in-an-artificial-orthography-by-joanne-s-h-taylor-and-others)

#### Reading comprehension

↑ **Parent:** [Reading](#reading)

Constructing an integrated interpretation of a text, including its explicit content and relevant inferences. Comprehension can depend on [word decoding](#word-decoding), vocabulary, [working memory](neuroscience.md#working-memory) and monitoring; the contribution of one should not be inferred by ignoring the others.[Journal of Educational Psychology 96, 31–42; longitudinal assessments at eight, nine and eleven years, concurrent prediction models](psychology-bibliography.md#children-s-reading-comprehension-ability-concurrent-prediction-by-working-memory-verbal-ability-and-component-skills-by-kate-cain-and-others)

##### Background knowledge in comprehension

↑ **Parent:** [Reading comprehension](#reading-comprehension)

Knowledge brought to interpreting a text. It can support integration but can also produce answers not warranted by the passage, so text-topic sampling matters.

##### Inference in text comprehension

↑ **Parent:** [Reading comprehension](#reading-comprehension)

Deriving information needed to connect or interpret a text beyond what is explicitly stated. Useful inference should be distinguished from unsupported guessing based on background knowledge.

##### Comprehension monitoring

↑ **Parent:** [Reading comprehension](#reading-comprehension)

Detection and evaluation of whether a representation of a text is coherent or understood. Noticing an inconsistency and repairing it are distinguishable from correctly answering a literal question.

##### Reading comprehension pressure point

↑ **Parent:** [Reading comprehension](#reading-comprehension)

A component or task demand that limits successful understanding for a particular reader and text. Print access, [language](README.md#language) knowledge, inference and monitoring are candidate bottlenecks, not mutually exclusive [diagnoses](#clinical-diagnosis).

##### Simple view of reading

↑ **Parent:** [Reading comprehension](#reading-comprehension)

A model distinguishing [decoding](#word-decoding) and [linguistic comprehension](#linguistic-comprehension) as necessary contributors to [reading comprehension](#reading-comprehension). The product formulation motivates different profiles with similar aggregate performance, but does not license multiplying arbitrary raw test scores or treating the two components as indivisible mechanisms.[Reading and Writing 2, 127–160; decoding, linguistic comprehension and product predictions in a longitudinal bilingual sample](psychology-bibliography.md#the-simple-view-of-reading-by-wesley-a-hoover-and-philip-b-gough)

###### Component complementarity in the simple view of reading

↑ **Parent:** [Simple view of reading](#simple-view-of-reading)

In the idealised model $R=DC$, changing [decoding](#word-decoding) by $h$ with comprehension fixed changes the product by $hC$, since $(D+h)C-DC=hC$. Similarly, changing comprehension by $k$ changes it by $kD$. Thus

$$
\frac{\partial R}{\partial D}=C,\qquad \frac{\partial R}{\partial C}=D.
$$

If either component is zero, increasing only the other leaves the product zero. Equal products can arise from different profiles: $0.3\times0.9=0.9\times0.3=0.27$. These algebraic properties explain the model's complementarity; they are not estimates of treatment response or diagnostic cutoffs.

##### Linguistic comprehension

↑ **Parent:** [Reading comprehension](#reading-comprehension)

Understanding linguistic content, including word meanings, sentence relations and discourse. The construct is broader than any one listening test.

###### Discourse comprehension

↑ **Parent:** [Linguistic comprehension](#linguistic-comprehension)

Understanding connected [language](README.md#language) beyond an isolated word or sentence, including reference, coherence and relations between events.

###### Listening comprehension

↑ **Parent:** [Linguistic comprehension](#linguistic-comprehension)

Understanding spoken linguistic material. It can help assess [language](README.md#language) demands separately from print [decoding](#word-decoding), but also depends on [attention](#attention), [memory](neuroscience.md#memory-in-animals) and the temporal presentation of speech.

#### Word decoding

↑ **Parent:** [Reading](#reading)

Identifying written words using orthographic and phonological information. Decoding accuracy or fluency should be distinguished from understanding the propositions and inferences in a text.

##### Phonological recoding

↑ **Parent:** [Word decoding](#word-decoding)

Deriving a pronunciation from print. Successful recoding can supply a [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) opportunity for the particular written form, rather than being only a temporary alternative to recognition.[Journal of Experimental Child Psychology 72, 95–129; second-grade pseudoword reading, delayed spelling-choice/naming/spelling and recoding controls](psychology-bibliography.md#phonological-recoding-and-orthographic-learning-a-direct-test-of-the-self-teaching-hypothesis-by-david-l-share)

<h5 id="grapheme-phoneme-correspondence">Grapheme–phoneme correspondence</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Word decoding](#word-decoding)

A relation between a written unit and a speech-sound unit. Its usefulness can depend on context and competing mappings.

### Executive function

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Processes supporting goal-directed control, including maintaining and updating relevant information, inhibiting competing responses and changing task sets. Their importance depends on what a task requires: an initially effortful procedure may need less executive control after practice. The unity-and-diversity framework distinguishes correlated components without equating a single test score with a pure executive capacity.[Cognitive Psychology 41, 49–100; latent-variable analyses](psychology-bibliography.md#the-unity-and-diversity-of-executive-functions-and-their-contributions-to-complex-frontal-lobe-tasks-by-akira-miyake-and-others)

#### Goal neglect

↑ **Parent:** [Executive function](#executive-function)

Failure to implement an intended or understood goal when the situation does not adequately trigger it. Preserved performance on highly structured tests can coexist with poor organization of open-ended activities.[1991, open-ended multiple-subgoal performance](psychology-bibliography.md#deficits-in-strategy-application-following-frontal-lobe-damage-in-man-by-tim-shallice-and-others)

#### Wisconsin Card Sorting Test

↑ **Parent:** [Executive function](#executive-function)

A rule-discovery and switching task using feedback about [Wisconsin Card Sorting Test](#wisconsin-card-sorting-test). Performance also depends on learning, [human memory](neuroscience.md#human-memory), [attention](#attention) and general task demands.

#### Executive task impurity

↑ **Parent:** [Executive function](#executive-function)

An [executive function](#executive-function) task necessarily recruits nonexecutive processes as well as its intended control demand. A verbal [inhibition](#inhibitory-control) task, for example, requires word comprehension and a response. Multiple indicators with different incidental demands can help separate common executive [variance](variance.md), but only under a defensible measurement model.[Cognitive Psychology 41, 49–100; latent-variable analyses](psychology-bibliography.md#the-unity-and-diversity-of-executive-functions-and-their-contributions-to-complex-frontal-lobe-tasks-by-akira-miyake-and-others)

#### Cognitive flexibility

↑ **Parent:** [Executive function](#executive-function)

Changing a task set, representation or strategy in response to changing goals or evidence. Switching costs can also reflect [working memory](neuroscience.md#working-memory), speed and comprehension of the new rule.

##### Task switching

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive flexibility](#cognitive-flexibility)

Alternating between task rules or response sets. Switch costs compare changing with repeating a rule, but also depend on preparation, competing mappings, cue processing and baseline performance.

#### Inhibitory control

↑ **Parent:** [Executive function](#executive-function)

Control over prepotent or competing responses. Its operation is task-dependent: following an opposite tapping rule and suppressing irrelevant numerical information share a broad control demand but include different perceptual, linguistic and domain-specific processes.

##### Imitation inhibition

↑ **Parent:** [Inhibitory control](#inhibitory-control)

Control of the influence of an observed action when a different action is required. It can be studied through compatible/incompatible response tasks with separate controls for spatial interference.

##### Stop-signal task

↑ **Parent:** [Inhibitory control](#inhibitory-control)

A task in which an occasional signal instructs cancellation of an otherwise required response. Successful performance requires detection and interpretation of the signal as well as stopping.

###### Stop-signal reaction time

↑ **Parent:** [Stop-signal task](#stop-signal-task)

A model-derived estimate of the latency of stopping. It is not directly observed as a movement time, and its interpretation depends on assumptions about the go and stop processes.

##### Opposite tapping task

↑ **Parent:** [Inhibitory control](#inhibitory-control)

An experimenter taps once or twice and the [child](organism.md#child) must produce the opposite count. The task taxes [inhibition](#inhibitory-control) of [imitation](animal-behaviour.md#imitation) together with maintenance of the response rule; its score is not a process-pure measure.[Measures: peg-tapping inhibitory-control task](psychology-bibliography.md#relating-effortful-control-executive-function-and-false-belief-understanding-to-emerging-math-and-literacy-ability-in-kindergarten-by-clancy-blair-and-rachel-peters-razza)

### Intelligence

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

#### Fluid intelligence

↑ **Parent:** [Intelligence](#intelligence)

The capacity measured by tasks emphasizing novel reasoning rather than acquired factual knowledge. Statistical overlap with executive-test performance does not by itself establish a unique causal direction.

#### Social intelligence

↑ **Parent:** [Intelligence](#intelligence)

Proposed abilities involved in understanding and using information about people and social situations. Competing definitions emphasize [social understanding](#social-understanding), knowledge, memory, effective behavior or self-perceived skill; measures of these are not automatically interchangeable indicators of one factor.

<h5 id="tromso-social-intelligence-scale">Tromsø Social Intelligence Scale</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Social intelligence](#social-intelligence)

A 21-item [self-report](#self-report) instrument developed with dimensions labeled social information processing, [social skills](#social-skills) and [social awareness](#social-awareness). Its internal structure is evidence about this operationalization; it does not by itself settle the organization of all social abilities.

##### Social awareness

↑ **Parent:** [Social intelligence](#social-intelligence)

Sensitivity to relevant social cues and consequences. Its operational meaning depends on the instrument; a factor with this label in a [self-report](#self-report) scale is not automatically identical to a performance ability.

##### Social skills

↑ **Parent:** [Social intelligence](#social-intelligence)

Behaviors supporting effective interaction in a specified context. Typical real-world behavior, maximal ability and self-perceived competence are related but distinct measurement targets.

##### Social knowledge

↑ **Parent:** [Social intelligence](#social-intelligence)

Knowledge concerning people, relationships or conventions. Cultural and contextual specificity can affect what a test treats as an appropriate response.

##### Social memory

↑ **Parent:** [Social intelligence](#social-intelligence)

Memory for socially relevant information. A distinct label does not establish a separate memory mechanism; social and nonsocial tasks must be compared while controlling method and general memory demands.

##### Social understanding

↑ **Parent:** [Social intelligence](#social-intelligence)

Interpreting socially relevant information such as intentions or implications of behavior. Performance depends on the information available, the scoring criterion and potentially general verbal or memory abilities.

### Mental state

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

#### Desire

↑ **Parent:** [Mental state](#mental-state)

A motivational attitude toward an outcome. Attributing a [desire](#desire) is distinct from establishing what an agent believes about how the outcome can be achieved.

#### Intention

↑ **Parent:** [Mental state](#mental-state)

A state directed toward bringing about an action or outcome. Inferring an intended goal from behaviour need not imply representing every [belief](#belief) or reason of the agent.

#### Belief

↑ **Parent:** [Mental state](#mental-state)

A state with representational content taken to be true. Attributing another person’s [belief](#belief) requires distinguishing its content from one’s own knowledge of the actual situation.

##### False belief

↑ **Parent:** [Belief](#belief)

A [belief](#belief) whose content differs from the actual state of affairs. A [false belief](#false-belief) is distinct from ignorance: an ignorant agent need not have any particular mistaken representation. [False-belief understanding](animal-behaviour.md#false-belief-understanding) is the capacity to represent and reason about this difference.

### Dual-process theory

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

A family of accounts distinguishing relatively automatic or intuitive processing from more controlled, demanding processing. Specific theories differ about conflict detection, engagement and correction; a biased final response does not establish that statistical information was never processed.

### Cognitive bias

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

A systematic tendency for judgments or behavior to depart from a stated benchmark. The benchmark and task interpretation must be justified; a departure is not by itself a complete psychological mechanism.

#### Seductive allure of neuroscience explanations

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive bias](#cognitive-bias)

An increase in the apparent quality of an explanation associated with [neuroscience](neuroscience.md) information that does not improve its logic. The original text-based experiment distinguishes this effect from a general claim that every brain image or biological explanation is misleading.[2008; explanation quality, irrelevant information and expertise](psychology-bibliography.md#the-seductive-allure-of-neuroscience-explanations-by-deena-skolnick-weisberg-and-others)

#### Interpretation bias

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive bias](#cognitive-bias)

A systematic tendency to resolve ambiguous information in a particular direction, such as threat. Task performance, current [anxiety](#anxiety) and durable clinical vulnerability are distinct variables.[Journal of Abnormal Psychology 109, 602–615; five experiments, ambiguity training, active meaning generation and state anxiety](psychology-bibliography.md#induced-emotional-interpretation-bias-and-anxiety-by-andrew-mathews-and-bundy-mackintosh)

#### Dunning-Kruger effect

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive bias](#cognitive-bias)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunning–Kruger_effect)

A pattern in which low observed performers overestimate their performance, often accompanied by hypotheses about deficits in evaluating their own answers. Task skill, comparison-group beliefs, measurement noise and grouping can all affect the observed relation; the pattern alone does not identify one cause.[1999, original empirical proposal](psychology-bibliography.md#unskilled-and-unaware-of-it-by-justin-kruger-and-david-dunning)

##### Selection on noisy performance can produce apparent overconfidence

↑ **Parent:** [Dunning-Kruger effect](#dunning-kruger-effect)

Let $X=A+\varepsilon$ and $S=A+\eta$, where latent score $A$ has [normal distribution](probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution) with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma_A^2$, and the centred normal errors are independent of each other and $A$. Since $\operatorname{Cov}(S,X)=\sigma_A^2$ and $\operatorname{Var}(X)=\sigma_A^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2$, [Gaussian conditional expectation](statistical-modelling.md#gaussian-conditional-expectation) gives $\mathbb E[S\mid X=x]=\mu+\lambda(x-\mu)$ with $\lambda=\sigma_A^2/(\sigma_A^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2)$. Subtracting $x$ proves the formula. For $0<\lambda<1$, selecting scores below the mean creates positive expected self-assessment errors relative to those observed scores. This is a mathematical possibility, not an empirical estimate of any particular study's contribution from measurement error.

#### Representativeness heuristic

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive bias](#cognitive-bias)

Judging [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) through resemblance to a typical case or generating process. Resemblance need not incorporate prevalence or evidence reliability, so it can yield plausible-seeming but poorly calibrated [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) judgments.

#### Base-rate neglect

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive bias](#cognitive-bias)

Underweighting an applicable class prevalence when evaluating case-specific evidence. Sensitivity to whether descriptions and prevalence conflict can survive even when a final answer underweights the statistical information.

### Probabilistic reasoning

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Reasoning about uncertain events, evidence and predictions. Normative evaluation requires explicit assumptions about sampling and evidential reliability, as well as the mathematical rule used for updating.

#### Natural frequencies in probabilistic reasoning

↑ **Parent:** [Probabilistic reasoning](#probabilistic-reasoning)

Counts preserving how cases from a common population fall into nested classes and evidence subsets. [Bayesian inference](bayesian-statistics.md) can then use a ratio of joint counts, $n(H\cap E)/n(E)$. For uniform sampling from those cases, [conditional probability](probability-theory.md#conditional-probability) is the number satisfying both class and evidence divided by the number satisfying the evidence, so the ratio follows directly without reconstructing separately normalized rates. Separately normalizing each class to the same artificial denominator does not preserve the original prevalence automatically.

### Semantic priming

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

A change in processing caused by a preceding meaning-related cue. Contrasting related and unrelated cues can reveal how representations influence later performance, but task expectations and response strategies must be controlled. Category-name cues can test whether [category typicality](#category-typicality) affects processing beyond explicit ratings.

### Categorization

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Assigning objects or events to psychologically relevant classes. Evidence about classification speed, typicality and generalization constrains representations and decision processes, but no single behavioral effect uniquely identifies a storage format.

#### Psychological essentialism

↑ **Parent:** [Categorization](#categorization)

A tendency to treat a category as having a stable underlying nature that explains its nonobvious properties. This can support expectations beyond appearance without establishing that the assumed essence is biologically real.

#### Social categorization

↑ **Parent:** [Categorization](#categorization)

Representing people as members of socially meaningful classes. [Social categorization](#social-categorization) can organize expectations and identity; a category label alone does not determine whether its consequences are inclusive or prejudicial.

##### Crossed categorization

↑ **Parent:** [Social categorization](#social-categorization)

An arrangement in which group boundaries on different dimensions do not coincide. A former [outgroup](#social-outgroup) member may share another valued identity with the observer. This can soften a single division, but need not eliminate exclusion of people outside both categories.

##### Recategorization

↑ **Parent:** [Social categorization](#social-categorization)

Changing the category structure through which people represent an interaction, for example by replacing two opposed groups with one inclusive group. [Recategorization](#recategorization) should be assessed through perceived membership and behavior rather than inferred from labels alone.

###### Common ingroup identity model

↑ **Parent:** [Recategorization](#recategorization)

An account of [prejudice](#prejudice) reduction in which former [outgroup](#social-outgroup) members become included in a common [ingroup](#ingroup). The proposed mechanism extends favorable [ingroup](#ingroup) responses across a previous boundary. Whether the common identity recognizes or suppresses subgroup identity is a substantive boundary condition.

##### Decategorization

↑ **Parent:** [Social categorization](#social-categorization)

Reducing the relevance of a group boundary by individuating interaction partners. A central limitation is generalization: liking a particular person need not change [prejudice](#prejudice) toward other members of that person's [outgroup](#social-outgroup).

##### Ingroup favoritism

↑ **Parent:** [Social categorization](#social-categorization)

More favorable evaluation or treatment of an [ingroup](#ingroup) than an [outgroup](#social-outgroup). It should be distinguished from absolute hostility toward an [outgroup](#social-outgroup) and measured separately from consequential [discrimination](#discrimination).

##### Social outgroup

↑ **Parent:** [Social categorization](#social-categorization)

A group represented as excluding the self in a relevant comparison. A person can belong to a [social outgroup](#social-outgroup) on one dimension and share an [ingroup](#ingroup) membership on another.

##### Ingroup

↑ **Parent:** [Social categorization](#social-categorization)

A group represented as including the self in a relevant context. Which group counts as an [ingroup](#ingroup) can change with the dimension of [social categorization](#social-categorization).

#### Conceptual combination

↑ **Parent:** [Categorization](#categorization)

Forming and interpreting a concept from other concepts. A theory of representation must explain how combined concepts acquire constraints and typical instances, including cases not captured by a simple average of their separate typical examples.

#### Classical theory of concepts

↑ **Parent:** [Categorization](#categorization)

An account emphasizing defining conditions for category membership. Such criteria can coexist with graded identification or typicality effects; those effects alone do not prove the absence of a definitional representation.

#### Exemplar theory

↑ **Parent:** [Categorization](#categorization)

An account in which classification depends on stored examples and their similarity to a new case. An unseen central case can resemble many stored examples and receive strong aggregate support, so prototype-like behavior need not imply an explicitly stored average.

#### Prototype theory

↑ **Parent:** [Categorization](#categorization)

An account in which category judgments rely on similarity to an abstract or representative summary. It naturally explains centrality and generalization effects, but must be distinguished from [exemplar theory](#exemplar-theory) and from a descriptive typicality gradient.

##### Family resemblance

↑ **Parent:** [Prototype theory](#prototype-theory)

A pattern of overlapping attributes among members without requiring one attribute set shared identically by every member. The distribution of attributes in competing categories matters as well as their occurrence within one category. [Family resemblance](#family-resemblance) can describe a category structure without specifying how it is learned or stored.

##### Concept prototype

↑ **Parent:** [Prototype theory](#prototype-theory)

A central or representative summary used in a [categorization](#categorization) account. It need not be a previously encountered individual, and its selection can depend on the modeled feature distribution, comparison classes and goals.

#### Category typicality

↑ **Parent:** [Categorization](#categorization)

How representative a member seems or behaves relative to its category. Graded typicality is distinct from graded membership: well-defined mathematical categories can show unequal familiarity or identification speed despite exact membership criteria.

### Cognitive control

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Processes that configure and regulate behavior according to goals, including selecting relevant information and resolving competing responses. A control-related contribution to a [language](README.md#language) task need not mean that all [language](README.md#language) representations are domain-general.

#### Attention Network Test

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive control](#cognitive-control)

An experimental task combining cueing and response-conflict conditions to estimate aspects of attentional processing. A robust experimental effect does not automatically yield reliable [individual differences](#individual-differences) or a group advantage.

#### Conflict cost

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive control](#cognitive-control)

A performance contrast between conditions with greater and smaller response conflict. For [reaction time](#reaction-time), $C=\overline{RT}_{\rm incongruent}-\overline{RT}_{\rm congruent}$. A group advantage specific to conflict requires a difference of such contrasts, not just faster responses in both conditions.

#### Simon task

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive control](#cognitive-control)

A response-conflict task in which a relevant feature determines a response while an irrelevant spatial feature can favour a competing response. Congruent versus incongruent trials define a within-person interference contrast.

##### Simon effect

↑ **Parent:** [Simon task](#simon-task)

The performance difference between spatially incompatible and compatible trials in a [Simon task](#simon-task). A group difference in overall [reaction time](#reaction-time) is distinct from a group difference in this [conflict cost](#conflict-cost).

#### Domain-general control

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive control](#cognitive-control)

Control processes contributing across different task contents. Recruitment near language-selective cortex does not make those processes syntactic, and different control tasks need not be reliable measures of one unitary ability.

### Attention

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Selection and prioritization of information for processing and action. [Working memory](neuroscience.md#working-memory) can retain information outside the current focus of [attention](#attention), so attentional priority should not be equated with all retained content.

#### Visual extinction

↑ **Parent:** [Attention](#attention)

Failure to report a contralesional visual event during simultaneous bilateral stimulation despite better detection when it appears alone. The contrast points to competition and selection, while sensory imbalance and task demands remain possible contributors.

#### Attentional bias

↑ **Parent:** [Attention](#attention)

Preferential allocation of [attention](#attention) toward or away from a stimulus class. A group-level effect and reliable measurement of individual differences are different requirements.

##### Dot-probe task

↑ **Parent:** [Attentional bias](#attentional-bias)

A task presenting competing stimuli followed by a probe, with responses used to investigate [attention](#attention). Altering the relation between emotional stimuli and probe location can train a contingency; transfer to emotional vulnerability and clinical functioning must be tested.[Journal of Abnormal Psychology 111, 107–123; modified dot-probe training and responses to a standardised stress task](psychology-bibliography.md#selective-attention-and-emotional-vulnerability-assessing-the-causal-basis-of-their-association-through-the-experimental-manipulation-of-attentional-bias-by-colin-macleod-and-others)

#### Mind-wandering

↑ **Parent:** [Attention](#attention)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind-wandering)

Thought disengaged from the current external task. Its content, intentionality, associated awareness and effect on task performance should be measured separately. An off-task thought can occur with or without [meta-awareness](animal-behaviour.md#meta-awareness).

#### Self-focused attention

↑ **Parent:** [Attention](#attention)

[Attention](#attention) directed toward one's own sensations, performance or imagined appearance. In [social anxiety disorder](psychiatry.md#social-anxiety-disorder), internal sensations may be mistaken for reliable evidence about how an observer sees the person, while [attention](#attention) to the actual interaction is reduced.

##### Somatic hypervigilance

↑ **Parent:** [Self-focused attention](#self-focused-attention)

Sustained monitoring of bodily sensations for signs of danger. Monitoring can change which sensations are noticed and how they are interpreted; its proposed maintaining role must be tested rather than used to dismiss the original sensation.

#### Feature integration theory

↑ **Parent:** [Attention](#attention)

A theory proposing early registration of separable visual features and a role for focused [attention](#attention) in correctly binding their conjunctions into objects. Visual-search slopes and binding errors are complementary tests, not interchangeable proofs of one serial architecture. The original model already recognized possible top-down constraints on unattended feature combinations.[Cognitive Psychology 12, pp. 97–136](psychology-bibliography.md#a-feature-integration-theory-of-attention-by-anne-treisman-and-others)

##### Illusory conjunction

↑ **Parent:** [Feature integration theory](#feature-integration-theory)

A reported or measured binding of features into a combination not present in the display, such as assigning one item's [color](optics.md#color) to another item's shape. Such errors support a distinction between feature availability and correct assignment, while their perceptual, memory and response contributions require task-specific controls.

#### Selective attention

↑ **Parent:** [Attention](#attention)

Preferential processing of some information relative to competing information. A task can manipulate relevant location, sensory feature, object or modality; these manipulations need not all select the same underlying unit.

### Cognition

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive psychology](#cognitive-psychology)

Processes involved in acquiring, retaining, transforming and using information. [Cognitive neuroscience](neuroscience.md#cognitive-neuroscience) investigates how these processes depend on biological mechanisms.

#### Predictive processing

↑ **Parent:** [Cognition](#cognition)

A family of accounts relating [perception](neuroscience.md#perception) and action to predictions and incoming information. Explanations require a specified representation, error weighting and [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) rule; the general vocabulary alone does not identify a mechanism.

#### Cognitive appraisal

↑ **Parent:** [Cognition](#cognition)

The significance assigned to an event or experience. The same bodily sensation or reminder may have different emotional consequences depending on its meaning, while real-world danger still requires assessment.

##### Secondary appraisal

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive appraisal](#cognitive-appraisal)

Evaluation of coping possibilities, control and available responses. In [appraisal theory](#appraisal-theory), evaluating the same event as controllable or uncontrollable can help explain differences in [emotion](#emotion).

##### Primary appraisal

↑ **Parent:** [Cognitive appraisal](#cognitive-appraisal)

Evaluation of whether an event matters to well-being or goals, and whether it involves benefit, harm or threat. The distinction from [secondary appraisal](#secondary-appraisal) concerns evaluative content; it need not imply two consciously experienced, strictly successive stages.

#### Information processing

↑ **Parent:** [Cognition](#cognition)

Operations that select, transform, retain or use information. A definition broad enough to include all [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) does not, by itself, identify a mechanism distinguishing one clinical disorder from another.

#### Creativity

↑ **Parent:** [Cognition](#cognition)

##### Incubation effect

↑ **Parent:** [Creativity](#creativity)

Improvement on a problem after an interval spent away from it. A causal effect of an interval condition does not by itself identify which mental events during that interval produced the improvement.

#### Reasoning

↑ **Parent:** [Cognition](#cognition)

##### Jumping to conclusions

↑ **Parent:** [Reasoning](#reasoning)

Drawing a conclusion after comparatively limited evidence under a specified task. A [beads task](#beads-task) measures information sampling under particular demands, not a direct readout of every real-world [belief](#belief) process.

###### Beads task

↑ **Parent:** [Jumping to conclusions](#jumping-to-conclusions)

An information-sampling task in which observations from a source are used to infer which source generated them. Decisions about when to stop sampling can investigate reasoning tendencies, with comprehension and task incentives affecting interpretation.

#### Consciousness

↑ **Parent:** [Cognition](#cognition)

## Psychometrics

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

The study of psychological measurement, including [questionnaire](#questionnaire) construction, [measurement reliability](#measurement-reliability) and [construct validity](design-of-experiments.md#construct-validity). A score can be repeatable without supporting the intended interpretation; consistency and validity answer different questions.

### Ceiling effect (statistics)

↑ **Parent:** [Psychometrics](#psychometrics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceiling_effect_(statistics))

Clustering at the upper limit of a measurement instrument can prevent it from distinguishing higher underlying levels. A bounded score need not exhibit a [ceiling effect](#ceiling-effect-statistics), and a sample maximum alone does not establish one. The issue concerns what variation the instrument can measure, rather than a proof that a treatment has no effect.

### Psychometric reliability

↑ **Parent:** [Psychometrics](#psychometrics)

Consistency or precision of a psychological measure under specified conditions. Unreliable measures can weaken observed associations; high [reliability](#psychometric-reliability) alone does not establish [construct validity](design-of-experiments.md#construct-validity).

### Response bias

↑ **Parent:** [Psychometrics](#psychometrics)

A systematic tendency in responding that can alter a score independently of the target ability. Its interpretation depends on the task; a willingness to endorse questionnaire items is not automatically an accuracy difference.

### Reliable change index

↑ **Parent:** [Psychometrics](#psychometrics)

A change score scaled by its expected measurement error. With equal marginal standard deviation $s$, reliability $r$ and independent measurement errors at the two assessments, the difference has error standard deviation $s\sqrt{2(1-r)}$, giving

$$
\mathrm{RCI}=\frac{X_{\mathrm{post}}-X_{\mathrm{pre}}}{s\sqrt{2(1-r)}}.
$$

A conventional normal-error criterion compares its magnitude with 1.96. The assumptions and instrument-specific reliability matter; reliable change is not itself [diagnostic remission](probability-and-statistics.md#diagnostic-remission) or proof that treatment caused the change.

#### Reliable improvement

↑ **Parent:** [Reliable change index](#reliable-change-index)

Improvement exceeding a specified measurement-error criterion, with any additional no-deterioration rule made explicit. It differs from crossing a [symptom](#symptom) cutoff and from improvement attributable to treatment.

##### Reliable recovery

↑ **Parent:** [Reliable improvement](#reliable-improvement)

A service outcome combining a [reliable improvement](#reliable-improvement) criterion with movement from baseline [caseness](#clinical-caseness) below the relevant clinical cutoffs. The instruments and denominator must be specified; this is not automatically [remission](probability-and-statistics.md#diagnostic-remission) established by diagnostic interview.

### Common method variance

↑ **Parent:** [Psychometrics](#psychometrics)

Covariation attributable to shared measurement procedures rather than solely to the intended traits. Item format, response style or a common informant can make measures correlate even when their substantive contents differ.

### Multitrait-multimethod design

↑ **Parent:** [Psychometrics](#psychometrics)

A measurement design applying multiple methods to multiple proposed traits. Cross-method agreement can support [convergent validity](design-of-experiments.md#convergent-validity), while comparison among traits supports [discriminant validity](design-of-experiments.md#discriminant-validity). Trait and method must not be completely confounded in the design.

### Measurement invariance

↑ **Parent:** [Psychometrics](#psychometrics)

The extent to which a measurement model has the same relevant interpretation across groups or occasions. Equal factor patterns, loadings and intercepts or thresholds are distinct restrictions. Comparing latent means generally needs stronger evidence than merely fitting similar factor patterns.

### Measurement reliability

↑ **Parent:** [Psychometrics](#psychometrics)

Consistency or precision of measurements under stated sources of variation. Reliability depends on the population, conditions and intended score; it is not a permanent property of an instrument alone. [Inter-rater reliability](#inter-rater-reliability) and [test-retest reliability](#test-retest-reliability) address different sources of variation. Reliability does not establish [construct validity](design-of-experiments.md#construct-validity).

#### Internal consistency

↑ **Parent:** [Measurement reliability](#measurement-reliability)

The degree of association among items or components of a measure under a specified model. High [internal consistency](#internal-consistency) can arise from overlapping content or [common method variance](#common-method-variance); it does not by itself establish one substantive dimension or [construct validity](design-of-experiments.md#construct-validity).

#### Test-retest reliability

↑ **Parent:** [Measurement reliability](#measurement-reliability)

Consistency of scores obtained from the same units on different occasions, under a justified interval and conditions. A low value may reflect measurement error, genuine change or both; the intended stability of the construct matters.

#### Inter-rater reliability

↑ **Parent:** [Measurement reliability](#measurement-reliability)

Consistency or agreement among raters assessing the same units. The statistic should match the scale and whether consistency or absolute agreement matters. Repeated ratings improve information about a unit without creating additional independently sampled units.

### Questionnaire

↑ **Parent:** [Psychometrics](#psychometrics)

A structured set of items used to obtain reports or judgments. Item wording, response options, respondent perspective and scoring determine what the resulting measurements mean. [Content validity](design-of-experiments.md#content-validity), [measurement reliability](#measurement-reliability) and [construct validity](design-of-experiments.md#construct-validity) require evidence beyond a convenient scoring rule.

#### Generalised Anxiety Disorder-7

↑ **Parent:** [Questionnaire](#questionnaire)

A seven-item [anxiety](#anxiety) [symptom](#symptom) questionnaire used in [routine outcome monitoring](#routine-outcome-monitoring). It should not be assumed to capture every disorder-specific difficulty or replace a clinical assessment.[Lancet Psychiatry 5, 564–572; 77 participating therapists, 2233 patients, cluster allocation and not-on-track outcomes](psychology-bibliography.md#feedback-informed-treatment-versus-usual-psychological-treatment-for-depression-and-anxiety-a-multisite-open-label-cluster-randomised-controlled-trial-by-jaime-delgadillo-and-others)

#### Patient Health Questionnaire-9

↑ **Parent:** [Questionnaire](#questionnaire)

A nine-item [depression](#depression) [symptom](#symptom) questionnaire. A score, a change score, a [caseness](#clinical-caseness) threshold and a [clinical diagnosis](#clinical-diagnosis) are distinct uses or interpretations.[Lancet Psychiatry 5, 564–572; 77 participating therapists, 2233 patients, cluster allocation and not-on-track outcomes](psychology-bibliography.md#feedback-informed-treatment-versus-usual-psychological-treatment-for-depression-and-anxiety-a-multisite-open-label-cluster-randomised-controlled-trial-by-jaime-delgadillo-and-others)

#### Cognitive interviewing

↑ **Parent:** [Questionnaire](#questionnaire)

A method for investigating how intended respondents understand an item and construct their answer, using prompts about their reasoning. It helps diagnose ambiguous terms, mismatched assumptions and unsuitable response options before large-scale data collection.

## Psychological research methods

↑ **Parent:** [Psychology](psychology.md)

Methods for defining questions, obtaining observations and evaluating claims in [psychology](psychology.md). [Design of experiments](design-of-experiments.md) can identify effects of assigned conditions; an [observational study](causal-inference.md#observational-study) measures existing variation. [Qualitative research methods](#qualitative-research-methods) examine meanings and processes in context. The method must fit the question and the intended inference.

### Microgenetic method

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological research methods](#psychological-research-methods)

Dense repeated observation around a period of [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) or change. Trial-level behavior can reveal transitions missed by widely separated tests, although the measurements themselves may influence [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning).

### Bucharest Early Intervention Project

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological research methods](#psychological-research-methods)

A randomized study of a supported foster-care intervention for young children already living in institutions. The randomized offer supplies evidence about changing care; subsequent care changes, adherence and attrition affect the estimand and analysis. Mediator claims about attachment need assumptions beyond randomization of the whole package.[2012, design and attachment analysis](psychology-bibliography.md#attachment-security-as-a-mechanism-linking-foster-care-placement-to-improved-mental-health-outcomes-in-previously-institutionalized-children-by-katie-a-mclaughlin-and-others)

### Experience sampling method

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological research methods](#psychological-research-methods)

Collecting repeated reports about ongoing experience in ordinary life. Frequent reports reduce some retrospective demands, but are still measurements with selection, response and reactivity issues. An observed association is not automatically a randomized causal effect.

### Placebo effect

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological research methods](#psychological-research-methods)

A change associated with treatment context or expectation rather than the specific active component being tested. A comparison with no treatment and appropriate masking is needed to distinguish a [placebo effect](#placebo-effect) from spontaneous change or reporting differences.

#### Treatment expectation

↑ **Parent:** [Placebo effect](#placebo-effect)

A [belief](#belief) about the likely consequences of [treatment](causal-inference.md#treatment) that can influence [attention](#attention), interpretation, behaviour and symptom experience. Experimental changes in expectation at constant drug exposure can distinguish contextual modulation from differences in administered dose.[Science Translational Medicine 3, 70ra14; fixed remifentanil concentration, heat pain and expectation manipulation](psychology-bibliography.md#the-effect-of-treatment-expectation-on-drug-efficacy-by-ulrike-bingel-and-others)

#### Open-label placebo

↑ **Parent:** [Placebo effect](#placebo-effect)

An intervention transparently described as inert rather than concealed as an active medication. Trials require an appropriate comparator and must address unblinded reporting, explanatory rationale and duration; an effect for one symptomatic outcome is not a universal disease-modifying benefit.[PLoS ONE 5, e15591; 80-patient three-week randomized open-label placebo trial and Limitations](psychology-bibliography.md#placebos-without-deception-a-randomized-controlled-trial-in-irritable-bowel-syndrome-by-ted-j-kaptchuk-and-others)

#### Placebo response

↑ **Parent:** [Placebo effect](#placebo-effect)

The observed change after placebo administration, including possible contextual effects, spontaneous change, [regression to the mean](statistical-modelling.md#regression-to-the-mean) and concurrent care. A placebo group's improvement is not by itself an estimate of the causal [placebo effect](#placebo-effect).

#### Nocebo effect

↑ **Parent:** [Placebo effect](#placebo-effect)

Adverse change attributable to a negative [treatment](causal-inference.md#treatment) context or expectation. It can involve genuine discomfort and physiological modulation as well as reporting and attribution. Its presence does not exclude concurrent disease or a direct [adverse drug reaction](pharmacology.md#adverse-drug-reaction).[Science Translational Medicine 3, 70ra14; fixed remifentanil concentration, heat pain and expectation manipulation](psychology-bibliography.md#the-effect-of-treatment-expectation-on-drug-efficacy-by-ulrike-bingel-and-others)

#### Placebo analgesia

↑ **Parent:** [Placebo effect](#placebo-effect)

A reduction in [pain](neuroscience.md#pain) associated with a placebo manipulation. Its influence on responses to others' [pain](neuroscience.md#pain) can test shared modulation, without showing that the two experiences have identical [neural representations](neuroscience.md#neural-representation).

### Reverse correlation

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological research methods](#psychological-research-methods)

Estimating which stimulus variations systematically accompany an observer's responses. Randomly varying facial movements and correlating them with [emotion](#emotion) judgments can reconstruct response-relevant features, conditional on the stimulus space and judgment task.

### Treatment fidelity

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological research methods](#psychological-research-methods)

The extent to which an intervention is delivered as intended, including relevant procedures, dose and competence. A trial's success under expert support does not by itself establish the same effects under routine delivery.

### Research ethics

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological research methods](#psychological-research-methods)

Principles and practices for responsible research, including respect for participants, scientific integrity, proportionate risk assessment and independent review. [Informed consent](biology.md#informed-consent), privacy and honest reporting are part of the research design rather than substitutes for its scientific quality.[2021, Sections 2–4 and 13](psychology-bibliography.md#code-of-human-research-ethics-by-british-psychological-society)

#### Safeguarding

↑ **Parent:** [Research ethics](#research-ethics)

#### Confidentiality

↑ **Parent:** [Research ethics](#research-ethics)

#### Participant burden

↑ **Parent:** [Research ethics](#research-ethics)

Time, effort, discomfort or disruption associated with research or intervention. A low therapist-time programme can still impose substantial burdens on caregivers and children.

#### Research participant privacy

↑ **Parent:** [Research ethics](#research-ethics)

Protection against inappropriate identification or disclosure of participant information. Data sharing should reflect consent and the identifying content of the material; identifiable [child](organism.md#child) video may require controlled access rather than public release.

#### Research debriefing

↑ **Parent:** [Research ethics](#research-ethics)

An explanation after participation of the study's purpose and relevant procedures, including justified deception, with an opportunity for questions and appropriate follow-up. Its form should fit what participants experienced and the study's risks.

#### Research deception

↑ **Parent:** [Research ethics](#research-ethics)

Intentionally misleading participants or withholding relevant information about a study. Its necessity and risks require justification and appropriate review; less deceptive alternatives should be considered. [Research debriefing](#research-debriefing) can explain what was withheld but does not automatically remove all consequences.

### Qualitative research methods

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological research methods](#psychological-research-methods)

Approaches to investigating meaning, experience and processes through material such as interviews, observations and documents. Explain the sampling rationale, analytic approach and path from evidence to interpretation. Quality criteria should fit the approach rather than mechanically importing every criterion of a statistical survey.

#### Researcher reflexivity

↑ **Parent:** [Qualitative research methods](#qualitative-research-methods)

Critical consideration of how the researcher's position, assumptions and interactions influence the questions, material and interpretations. A reflexive record helps make these contributions inspectable; it does not claim that the researcher can remove every influence.

#### Purposive sampling

↑ **Parent:** [Qualitative research methods](#qualitative-research-methods)

Selecting cases because their characteristics or experiences are informative for a specified research question. It can deliberately include contrasting roles or experiences; it does not imply probability-based population representation.

#### Qualitative content analysis

↑ **Parent:** [Qualitative research methods](#qualitative-research-methods)

Systematic interpretation of recorded material using an explicit categorization process. Categories can be developed from theory, the material or both. State how categories were developed and applied and how evidence supports the resulting claims.

#### Thematic analysis

↑ **Parent:** [Qualitative research methods](#qualitative-research-methods)

Analysis identifying and interpreting patterns of meaning in qualitative material. The researcher should state the theoretical and analytic approach, explain the development of themes, and support interpretive claims with contextualized evidence.

### Reaction time

↑ **Parent:** [Psychological research methods](#psychological-research-methods)

The elapsed time between a specified event and a response. The event, response criterion and time units must be defined; trial exclusion and aggregation affect its analysis. Repeated trials from one participant are not independent participant-level observations.

#### Speed-accuracy trade-off

↑ **Parent:** [Reaction time](#reaction-time)

A relationship in which changes in response caution or time allocation affect both speed and error rate. A faster mean response is not automatically better performance if accuracy also falls; both measures and their uncertainty should be examined.

#### Redundant-signals race model

↑ **Parent:** [Reaction time](#reaction-time)

A model in which separate sensory channels can each trigger a response, and the first completed channel determines response time. Context invariance means that a channel's marginal completion distribution is unchanged between its single-signal and redundant-signal conditions. [Independence](random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) is a separate, stronger assumption.

##### Race model inequality

↑ **Parent:** [Redundant-signals race model](#redundant-signals-race-model)

Under context-invariant channel distributions, $P(\min(T_A,T_V)\leq t)=P(T_A\leq t\ \mathrm{or}\ T_V\leq t)\leq F_A(t)+F_V(t)$ by the [union bound](probability-inequality.md#boole-s-inequality); [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) is also at most one. No [independence](random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) assumption is needed. A reliable violation rejects this model with those marginals, not every possible separate-channel or decision account.
