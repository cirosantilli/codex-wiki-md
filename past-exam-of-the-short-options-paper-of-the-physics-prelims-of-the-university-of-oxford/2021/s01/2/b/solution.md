<h1 id="s01/2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [poles](../../../../../pole.md) are the four roots of $z^4=-1$. The upper [half-plane](../../../../../half-plane.md) contains $a=e^{i\pi/4}$ and $b=e^{3i\pi/4}$, both enclosed when $R>1$. At a [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md) $q$,

$$
\operatorname{Res}\left(\frac{z^2}{z^4+1},q\right)=\frac{q^2}{4q^3}=\frac1{4q}.
$$

The sum of the two [residues](../../../../../residue.md) is

$$
\frac14(e^{-i\pi/4}+e^{-3i\pi/4})=-\frac{i}{2\sqrt2}.
$$

The [residue theorem](../../../../../residue-theorem.md), with the real segment traversed from $-R$ to $R$ and the upper arc from $R$ to $-R$, yields

$$
\boxed{\oint_{\Gamma_R}\frac{z^2}{z^4+1}\,dz=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt2}.}
$$
