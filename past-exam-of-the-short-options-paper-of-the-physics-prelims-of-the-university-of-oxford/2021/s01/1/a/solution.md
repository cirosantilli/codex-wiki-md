<h1 id="s01/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The apparent [singularity](../../../../../complex-singularity.md) at zero cancels: the [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) of the [sine](../../../../../sine.md) gives

$$
\frac{\sin z}{z^2}-\frac1z=-\frac z6+\frac{z^3}{120}-\cdots.
$$

Thus zero is a [removable singularity](../../../../../removable-singularity.md), with extension $f(0)=1/2$. At $z=-2$, the other terms are [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md), whereas $(z+2)^{-1}$ has a [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{z=0:\ \text{removable},\qquad z=-2:\ \text{simple pole with residue }1.}
$$

There are no other finite [singularities](../../../../../complex-singularity.md). If “all” includes the point at infinity on the [Riemann sphere](../../../../../riemann-sphere.md), it is an [essential singularity](../../../../../essential-singularity.md): $f(1/w)=w/(1+2w)+w^2\sin(1/w)-w$ has infinitely many negative powers in its [Laurent series](../../../../../laurent-series.md) at $w=0$.
