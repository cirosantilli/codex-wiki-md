<h1 id="s01/1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Assume positive, counterclockwise [orientation of a contour](../../../../../orientation-of-a-contour.md). Factor the [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) denominator as

$$
z^4-10z^2+1=\bigl(z^2-(5-2\sqrt6)\bigr)\bigl(z^2-(5+2\sqrt6)\bigr).
$$

Only $z=\pm(\sqrt3-\sqrt2)$ lie inside the [unit circle](../../../../../complex-unit-circle.md). These are [simple poles](../../../../../simple-pole.md). Writing the denominator as $P(z)$, their [residues](../../../../../residue.md) are

$$
\frac{z}{P'(z)}=\frac1{4z^2-20}=-\frac1{8\sqrt6}.
$$

The [residue theorem](../../../../../residue-theorem.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{I=2\pi i\left(-\frac1{4\sqrt6}\right)=-\frac{\pi i}{2\sqrt6}.}
$$

Reversing [orientation of a contour](../../../../../orientation-of-a-contour.md) changes the sign.
