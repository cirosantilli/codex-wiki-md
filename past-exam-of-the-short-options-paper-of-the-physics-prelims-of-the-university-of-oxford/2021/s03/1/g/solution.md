<h1 id="s03/1/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

The estimate assumes the entire $2\,\mathrm{m^2}$ surface remains exposed at $35\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ continuously. Clothing reduces direct exposure and presents a cooler outer emitting surface; the skin [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) also varies across the body and decreases in cooler surroundings through changes in blood flow. Body posture and nearby surfaces reduce the effective exchange [area](../../../../../surface-area.md), while blankets and warm surrounding surfaces reduce net [radiative heat transfer](../../../../../radiative-heat-transfer.md). These effects make the full-area, constant-[temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) daily radiative estimate too large for ordinary clothed conditions.

The unit [emissivity](../../../../../emissivity.md) approximation is already fairly good for skin in the thermal [infrared](../../../../../infrared.md), so reducing [emissivity](../../../../../emissivity.md) alone is not the main correction. Also, [convection](../../../../../convection-split.md), [thermal conduction](../../../../../thermal-conduction.md), and evaporation are additional channels of [heat transfer](../../../../../heat-transfer.md); their existence does not subtract from the calculated radiative term. The computed number is a simplified **radiative** loss, not a complete metabolic [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) budget.
