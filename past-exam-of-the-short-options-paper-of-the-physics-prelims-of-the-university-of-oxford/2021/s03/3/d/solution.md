<h1 id="s03/3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The emitted [photon energy](../../../../../photon-energy.md) is the difference of the two [energy levels](../../../../../energy-level.md):

$$
\Delta E=E_3-E_2=\frac{(9-4)\hbar^2}{2mr^2}=\frac{5\hbar^2}{2mr^2}.
$$

Using $E_\gamma=hc/\lambda$ gives

$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{2hcmr^2}{5\hbar^2}=\frac{4\pi cmr^2}{5\hbar}.}
$$

No numerical [mass](../../../../../mass.md) or [radius](../../../../../radius.md) is supplied, so this is the determinate result. It is the wavelength conditional on the transition occurring. An isolated [particle on a circle](../../../../../particle-in-a-ring.md) with no coupling would remain in its [energy eigenstate](../../../../../energy-eigenstate.md); a charged particle coupled to [electromagnetic radiation](../../../../../electromagnetic-radiation-split.md) can emit subject to its transition [selection rules](../../../../../selection-rule.md). The $3\to2$ change has $\Delta n=-1$, compatible with an electric-dipole angular factor for a charged ring particle.
