<h1 id="s03/2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The incident [photon energy](../../../../../photon-energy.md) is

$$
E_\gamma=\frac{hc}{\lambda}=\frac{1239.841984\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}}{456\,\mathrm{nm}}=2.71895\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

Subtracting the [work function](../../../../../work-function.md) gives $K_{\max}=0.768952\,\mathrm{eV}=1.2320\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm J$. This is much less than the electron rest [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), so nonrelativistic [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is adequate:

$$
\boxed{v_{\max}=\sqrt{\frac{2K_{\max}}{m_e}}\simeq5.20\times10^5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The magnitude of the retarding [stopping potential](../../../../../stopping-potential.md) satisfies $eV_s=K_{\max}$, so

$$
\boxed{V_s\simeq0.769\,\mathrm V.}
$$

The collector must be negative relative to the emitting surface to retard the [electrons](../../../../../electron.md); the quoted number is the magnitude. Contact-potential corrections are neglected.
