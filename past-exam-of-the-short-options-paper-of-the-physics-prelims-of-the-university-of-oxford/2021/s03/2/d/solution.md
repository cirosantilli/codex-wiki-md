<h1 id="s03/2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

An [electron](../../../../../electron.md) accelerated from negligible initial [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) through $V$ gains $eV$. The largest possible single [photon energy](../../../../../photon-energy.md) in an X-ray tube is this entire [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md), so the [Duane-Hunt law](../../../../../duane-hunt-law.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\lambda_{\min}=\frac{hc}{eV}=\frac{1239.841984\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}}{25000\,\mathrm{eV}}=0.0496\,\mathrm{nm}=4.96\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm m.}
$$

The massive anode can absorb the required recoil [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) with negligible recoil [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md); most actual events produce lower-[energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) [bremsstrahlung](../../../../../bremsstrahlung.md) [photons](../../../../../photon.md). The acceleration [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $eV$ is valid without approximating the electron speed nonrelativistically.
