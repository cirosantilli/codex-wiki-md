<h1 id="s03/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the [photoelectric effect](../../../../../photoelectric-effect.md), incident light ejects [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) from a material when each absorbed [photon](../../../../../photon.md) supplies sufficient [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) to overcome its [work function](../../../../../work-function.md) $W$. [Conservation of energy](../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) gives the maximum emitted [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md),

$$
\boxed{K_{\max}=h\nu-W,\qquad \nu\ge\nu_0=W/h.}
$$

At fixed [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) above threshold, increasing light [intensity](../../../../../intensity-physics.md) primarily increases the electron emission rate; it does not increase $K_{\max}$ in the single-[photon](../../../../../photon.md) regime. Emission occurs without the long accumulation delay predicted for weak classical illumination.

Classical [electromagnetism](../../../../../electromagnetism-split.md) distributes incident [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) continuously. By itself, that picture suggests that sufficiently intense or sufficiently long illumination could eject [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) at any [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md), and that emitted [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [energies](../../../../../energy-split.md) should increase with light [intensity](../../../../../intensity-physics.md). It does not account for the observed threshold [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) and the linear dependence of $K_{\max}$ on [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) with slope $h$. Quantized [photon absorption](../../../../../photon-absorption.md) accounts for all three features.
