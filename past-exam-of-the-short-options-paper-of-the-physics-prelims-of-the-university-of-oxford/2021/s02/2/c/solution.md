<h1 id="s02/2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**Transit photometry.** A planet crossing the stellar disc produces a periodic reduction in [flux](../../../../../flux.md), approximately $\delta=(R_p/R_*)^2$ for a small, dark planet away from the limb. The [orbital period](../../../../../orbital-period.md) and [stellar radius](../../../../../stellar-radius.md) allow an inferred planetary [radius](../../../../../radius.md), but not its [mass](../../../../../mass.md) by this method alone. The geometric [geometric transit probability](../../../../../geometric-transit-probability.md) is roughly $(R_*+R_p)/a$ for a circular orbit, so the method strongly favours close orbits and large planets. Small signals compete with instrumental noise and [stellar activity](../../../../../stellar-activity.md); eclipsing stellar binaries and blended sources can mimic a planetary [exoplanet transit](../../../../../exoplanet-transit.md), and long [orbital periods](../../../../../orbital-period.md) require long observing baselines.

**Doppler spectroscopy.** Periodic shifts of stellar [spectral lines](../../../../../spectral-line.md) reveal the stellar [radial velocity](../../../../../radial-velocity.md) caused by motion around the [centre of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md). For a light companion, $K\propto M_p\sin i\,P^{-1/3}M_*^{-2/3}/\sqrt{1-e^2}$, favouring massive, short-period companions and low-mass host stars. Unknown [orbital inclination](../../../../../orbital-inclination.md) gives a minimum [mass](../../../../../mass.md); [stellar activity](../../../../../stellar-activity.md), line broadening, and instrumental calibration limit precision. Long [orbital periods](../../../../../orbital-period.md) require years of measurements.

**Direct imaging.** Spatially resolved reflected light or thermal emission separates a planet from its host. The large star–planet brightness contrast and small [angular separation](../../../../../angular-separation.md) demand starlight suppression and precise correction of optical aberrations. The method favours wide orbits around nearby stars, and young, hot, massive planets with stronger thermal emission. Translating brightness to [mass](../../../../../mass.md) depends on planetary cooling models and age; it is difficult to image an Earth analogue close to a bright star.
