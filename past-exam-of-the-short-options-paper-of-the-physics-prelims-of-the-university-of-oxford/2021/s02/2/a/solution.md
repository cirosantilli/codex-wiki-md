<h1 id="s02/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The intact source figure gives [orbital period](../../../../../orbital-period.md) $P=6.0\,\mathrm{yr}$, stellar [radial velocity](../../../../../radial-velocity.md) semiamplitude $K=30.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, and [orbital eccentricity](../../../../../orbital-eccentricity.md) $e=0$. A planetary [exoplanet transit](../../../../../exoplanet-transit.md) implies a nearly edge-on orbit, so $\sin i\simeq1$. Adopt a [solar mass](../../../../../solar-mass.md) for the [Solar-type star](../../../../../solar-analog.md) and initially neglect the companion's [mass](../../../../../mass.md) relative to it.

For a circular solution of the [two-body problem](../../../../../two-body-problem.md), the star's distance from the [centre of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md) is $a_*=aM_p/(M_*+M_p)$, and the measured semiamplitude is $K=2\pi a_*\sin i/P$. [Kepler's third law](../../../../../kepler-s-third-law.md) gives

$$
a^3=\frac{G(M_*+M_p)P^2}{4\pi^2},\qquad
\frac{M_p^3\sin^3 i}{(M_*+M_p)^2}=\frac{PK^3}{2\pi G}.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{a\simeq\left(\frac{GM_\odot P^2}{4\pi^2}\right)^{1/3}=3.30\,\mathrm{AU},\qquad
M_p\simeq K\left(\frac{P}{2\pi G}\right)^{1/3}M_\odot^{2/3}=3.64\times10^{27}\,\mathrm{kg}\simeq1.92M_J.}
$$

The inferred [binary mass ratio](../../../../../binary-mass-ratio.md) is only $0.00183$, validating the approximation. Since $e=0$, the star–planet separation is constant and equals the orbital [semimajor axis](../../../../../semi-major-axis.md). Without the [exoplanet transit](../../../../../exoplanet-transit.md), [Doppler spectroscopy](../../../../../doppler-spectroscopy.md) would generally give only a minimum companion [mass](../../../../../mass.md) through $M_p\sin i$.
