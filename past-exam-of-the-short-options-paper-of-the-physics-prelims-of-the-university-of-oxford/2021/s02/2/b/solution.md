<h1 id="s02/2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Assume uniform reradiation over the planetary surface, unit thermal [emissivity](../../../../../emissivity.md), and zero [Bond albedo](../../../../../bond-albedo.md). The planet absorbs over its projected disc and emits over its full sphere. [Radiative equilibrium](../../../../../radiative-equilibrium.md) and the [Stefan–Boltzmann law](../../../../../stefan-boltzmann-law.md) give

$$
\pi R_p^2\frac{L_\odot}{4\pi a^2}=4\pi R_p^2\sigma T_p^4,\qquad
T_p=T_\odot\sqrt{\frac{R_\odot}{2a}}.
$$

Using the liquid-water range $273.15\le T_p\le373.15\,\mathrm K$ as the crude thermal criterion gives

$$
\boxed{a_{\rm in}\simeq0.562\,\mathrm{AU},\qquad a_{\rm out}\simeq1.05\,\mathrm{AU}.}
$$

For nonzero [Bond albedo](../../../../../bond-albedo.md) $A_B$, both radii acquire the factor $\sqrt{1-A_B}$. For example $A_B=0.3$ gives approximately $0.470$ to $0.877\,\mathrm{AU}$. An airless planet need not have uniform surface [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md); inefficient heat redistribution makes the dayside hotter and the nightside colder, so a single equilibrium [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) is an averaging approximation.

This simple [habitable zone](../../../../../circumstellar-habitable-zone.md) contains the Earth's orbit under the zero-[albedo](../../../../../albedo.md) assumption, but also contains Venus's orbit, although its [greenhouse effect](../../../../../greenhouse-effect.md) produces an uninhabitably hot surface. Mars lies beyond the calculated outer boundary. These comparisons show the usefulness of the orbital scale and the limitations of a [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md)-only criterion. The stipulated airless model also omits the [atmospheric pressure](../../../../../atmospheric-pressure.md) needed for stable surface liquid water: this is an effective thermal window, not a sufficient habitability condition.
