<h1 id="s02/3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the estimate implied by the supplied [Hubble constant](../../../../../hubble-constant.md), use the low-[redshift](../../../../../redshift.md) [Hubble law](../../../../../hubble-s-law.md) $D\simeq cz/H_0$. Then

$$
D_L\simeq800\,\mathrm{Mpc},\qquad D_S\simeq2000\,\mathrm{Mpc},\qquad D_{LS}\simeq1200\,\mathrm{Mpc}.
$$

With $\theta_E=\pi/10800$ radians, inversion of the [Einstein angular radius](../../../../../einstein-angular-radius.md) relation gives

$$
\boxed{M_E=\frac{c^2\theta_E^2}{4G}\frac{D_LD_S}{D_{LS}}\simeq5.89\times10^{14}M_\odot.}
$$

The corresponding projected [Einstein radius](../../../../../einstein-radius.md) is $R_E=D_L\theta_E\simeq233\,\mathrm{kpc}$.

This is the projected enclosed [mass](../../../../../mass.md) of the [galaxy cluster](../../../../../galaxy-cluster.md), including all matter, not just its stars. Strictly, the [gravitational lensing](../../../../../gravitational-lensing-split.md) formula uses [angular diameter distances](../../../../../angular-diameter-distance.md), and at $z=0.5$ the low-[redshift](../../../../../redshift.md) approximation is only an estimate. An exact value requires a specified cosmological expansion history and a consistent lens-to-source [angular diameter distance](../../../../../angular-diameter-distance.md); it cannot be determined from $H_0$ alone. The later estimates use the same distance approximation consistently.
