<h1 id="s02/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Assume [virial equilibrium](../../../../../virial-equilibrium.md), structurally homologous [early-type galaxies](../../../../../early-type-galaxy.md), a fixed relation between the measured central [velocity dispersion](../../../../../velocity-dispersion.md) $\sigma_c$ and the global random stellar motions, and a constant total [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) $\Upsilon$. The [virial theorem](../../../../../virial-theorem.md) gives $M=k_v\sigma_c^2R_e/G$, where the dimensionless structure factor $k_v$ is common to the sample. With $\mathcal I_e$ denoting mean **linear** [surface brightness](../../../../../surface-brightness.md) inside the [effective radius](../../../../../effective-radius.md), $L=2\pi R_e^2\mathcal I_e$. Thus

$$
\boxed{R_e=\frac{k_v}{2\pi G\Upsilon}\frac{\sigma_c^2}{\mathcal I_e},\qquad \log R_e=2\log\sigma_c-\log\mathcal I_e+\mathrm{constant}.}
$$

The usual observed [fundamental plane of elliptical galaxies](../../../../../fundamental-plane-elliptical-galaxies.md) has a tilt relative to these exponents because [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) varies with [stellar population](../../../../../stellar-population.md), chemical composition, and [dark matter](../../../../../dark-matter.md) fraction; structural and dynamical nonhomology can also change $k_v$.

**Printed convention.** The source places the logarithm of a value expressed in $\mathrm{mag\,arcsec^{-2}}$ in the empirical fit. This differs from the usual logarithm of linear [surface brightness](../../../../../surface-brightness.md). If $\mu_e$ is the quoted magnitude [surface brightness](../../../../../surface-brightness.md),

$$
\log_{10}\frac{\mathcal I_e}{L_{V,\odot}\,\mathrm{pc^{-2}}}=-0.4(\mu_e-M_{V,\odot}-21.572).
$$

A coefficient $-0.8$ on $\log\mathcal I_e$ therefore becomes $+0.32$ on $\mu_e$, with an adjusted intercept; it does **not** become $-0.8\log\mu_e$. For a concrete distinction, changing $\mu_e$ from 20 to 25 decreases linear [surface brightness](../../../../../surface-brightness.md) by a factor 100, so the linear-intensity fit changes $R_e$ by $100^{0.8}\simeq39.8$, whereas the literal printed logarithm changes it by $(25/20)^{-0.8}\simeq0.837$.

The following part evaluates the printed numerical fit literally and labels that convention. If the fit was intended for linear [surface brightness](../../../../../surface-brightness.md), its calibration must be specified; a conditional result for using the same coefficients with linear units is also given. No equality between these two conventions is assumed.
