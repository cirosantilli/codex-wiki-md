[Compton scattering](../../../../../compton-scattering.md) transfers [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) from a photon to an electron. For an initially stationary, approximately free [electron](../../../../../electron.md), a photon scattered through a nonzero angle generally has lower [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) and longer [wavelength](../../../../../wavelength.md); the recoiling electron carries the difference. The effect demonstrates the photon [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) $p_\gamma=h\nu/c=h/\lambda$, beyond a purely elastic classical scattering description.

The middle panel of the figure in [solution](../../1/b/solution.md) shows the incident photon along $+x$, the outgoing photon at angle $\theta$ above that direction, and the recoiling [electron](../../../../../electron.md) at deflection angle $\phi$ below it. Each arrow labels its particle and [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md); the initial electron is at rest at the interaction point. The scattered photon and electron have opposite transverse momentum components, while their forward components sum to the incident photon [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md).
