<h1 id="s3/2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The ordinary electron [Compton wavelength](../../../../../compton-wavelength.md) is

$$
\boxed{\lambda_C=\frac{h}{m_ec}=2.4263\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm m.}
$$

A photon of that [wavelength](../../../../../wavelength.md) has [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) equal to the electron rest [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), $hc/\lambda_C=m_ec^2$. It therefore sets the characteristic scale at which photon recoil cannot generally be treated nonrelativistically, and it sets the magnitude of the [Compton wavelength shift](../../../../../compton-wavelength-shift.md). The reduced [Compton wavelength](../../../../../compton-wavelength.md), $\bar\lambda_C=\hbar/(m_ec)=\lambda_C/(2\pi)$, is a related but different convention. Localization on this reduced scale needs momenta of order $m_ec$, so a nonrelativistic single-particle description becomes inadequate. Neither wavelength is a measured geometrical radius of the electron.
