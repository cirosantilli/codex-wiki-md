<h1 id="s3/3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The two [probabilities](../../../../../probability.md) concern different [quantum measurements](../../../../../quantum-measurement-split.md), so there is no requirement that they match or that one bound the other. The spatial interval $[0,L/3]$ contains little of the state: its [probability density](../../../../../probability-density.md) is smallest near zero and increases towards $L$. By contrast, the positive [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) extends across the whole well. It has substantial same-sign overlap with the positive linear [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md), with no cancellation between lobes in the overlap [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md). The right panel of the figure in [solution](../../1/b/solution.md) illustrates these two amplitudes and marks the small position interval.

Consequently a large global [ground-state probability](../../../../../ground-state-probability.md) can coexist with a small probability of finding the particle in the left third. This is not because the [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) is spatially confined to that third; a ground-state particle can be found throughout the well. In operator language the two events are projections onto a spatial subspace and onto one [energy eigenstate](../../../../../energy-eigenstate.md), respectively.
