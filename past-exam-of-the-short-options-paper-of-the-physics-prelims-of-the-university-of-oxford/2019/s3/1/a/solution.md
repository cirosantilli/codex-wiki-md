<h1 id="s3/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [blackbody](../../../../../blackbody.md) is an ideal body that absorbs all incident [electromagnetic radiation](../../../../../electromagnetic-radiation-split.md), at every [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) and direction. Its [absorptivity](../../../../../absorptivity.md) is one, so [Kirchhoff's law of thermal radiation](../../../../../kirchhoff-s-law-of-thermal-radiation.md) makes its equilibrium [emissivity](../../../../../emissivity.md) one as well. At a specified [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) its thermal [spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-functional-analysis.md) is universal: it depends on [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), not material or shape. This does not mean it looks black when hot; it can glow brightly while remaining a perfect absorber.

A small hole in a nearly isothermal opaque cavity approximates a [blackbody](../../../../../blackbody.md), because radiation entering the hole undergoes many interactions before it could escape. In [thermal equilibrium](../../../../../thermal-equilibrium-split.md) the cavity contains an approximately isotropic [Planck spectrum](../../../../../blackbody-spectrum.md). The emitted [radiance](../../../../../radiance.md) follows [Planck's law](../../../../../planck-s-law.md), its integrated outward [radiant exitance](../../../../../radiant-exitance.md) follows the [Stefan–Boltzmann law](../../../../../stefan-boltzmann-law.md), and its wavelength peak follows [Wien's displacement law](../../../../../wien-s-displacement-law.md). A real surface can have wavelength- and direction-dependent [emissivity](../../../../../emissivity.md) below this ideal limit.
