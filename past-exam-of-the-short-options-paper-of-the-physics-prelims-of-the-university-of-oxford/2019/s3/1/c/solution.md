<h1 id="s3/1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For $x=h\nu/(k_BT)\ll1$, the [Taylor expansion](../../../../../taylor-expansion.md) $e^x-1=x+O(x^2)$ gives the [Rayleigh-Jeans law](../../../../../rayleigh-jeans-law.md) for equilibrium radiation,

$$
\boxed{U_\nu\simeq\frac{8\pi k_BT\nu^2}{c^3}.}
$$

This is the classical limit: each electromagnetic mode has mean thermal [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) approximately $k_BT$. Extending it to arbitrarily large [frequencies](../../../../../frequency.md) would give a divergent integrated [energy density](../../../../../energy-density.md), the [ultraviolet catastrophe](../../../../../ultraviolet-catastrophe.md).

For $x\gg1$, $e^x-1\simeq e^x$, giving the [Wien approximation](../../../../../wien-approximation.md)

$$
\boxed{U_\nu\simeq\frac{8\pi h\nu^3}{c^3}e^{-h\nu/(k_BT)}.}
$$

[Planck's quantum hypothesis](../../../../../planck-s-quantum-hypothesis.md) assigns equally spaced mode energies $E_n=nh\nu$. When the spacing is small relative to $k_BT$, many levels are occupied and the classical approximation works. When $h\nu\gg k_BT$, exciting even one quantum is exponentially unlikely, suppressing the high-frequency tail. The single expression $\langle E\rangle=h\nu/(e^x-1)$ contains both limits. Comparing [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) directly to $h\nu$ would be dimensionally incorrect; the low-temperature condition means $k_BT\ll h\nu$.
