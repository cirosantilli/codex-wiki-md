<h1 id="s2/3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Assume an isotropic, stationary spherical [galaxy cluster](../../../../../galaxy-cluster.md). Its three-dimensional [velocity dispersion](../../../../../velocity-dispersion.md) obeys $\langle v^2\rangle=3\sigma_{\rm los}^2$. For a uniform sphere, the [virial theorem](../../../../../virial-theorem.md) gives $3M\sigma_{\rm los}^2=3GM^2/(5R)$, hence

$$
\boxed{M_{\rm dyn}\simeq\frac{5R\sigma_{\rm los}^2}{G}=9.4\times10^{14}M_\odot.}
$$

A different [density profile](../../../../../density-profile.md) changes the coefficient, so $10^{15}M_\odot$ is the meaningful scale. For isothermal [X-ray](../../../../../x-ray.md) gas in [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md),

$$
M(r)=-\frac{k_BTr}{G\mu m_p}\frac{d\ln\rho_g}{d\ln r}.
$$

Using $\mu=0.6$ and a representative slope $-d\ln\rho_g/d\ln r=2$–$3$ at $1\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ gives $M_X\simeq(1.3$–$1.9)\times10^{15}M_\odot$. The [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and extent alone cannot fix this coefficient without a [density profile](../../../../../density-profile.md). The total [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) is $5\times10^{12}L_\odot$, so the previously inferred [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) gives

$$
\boxed{M_\star\simeq4.50(5\times10^{12})M_\odot=2.25\times10^{13}M_\odot.}
$$
