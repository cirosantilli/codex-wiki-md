<h1 id="s2/2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [free-fall timescale](../../../../../free-fall-timescale.md) estimate follows from $R/t^2\sim GM/R^2$:

$$
\boxed{t_{\rm dyn}\sim\sqrt{\frac{R_\odot^3}{GM_\odot}}\simeq1.6\times10^3\,\mathrm s.}
$$

Falling to a central [point mass](../../../../../point-mass.md) gives the more precise factor $\pi/(2\sqrt2)$, or $1.77\times10^3\,\mathrm s$; a distributed star changes an order-one coefficient. For any spherical [mass distribution](../../../../../mass-distribution.md) with radius $R$,

$$
|\Omega|=\int_0^M\frac{Gm}{r(m)}\,dm\ge\frac GR\int_0^M m\,dm=\frac{GM^2}{2R}.
$$

The inequality is strict for an extended star; equality is approached by a thin shell at $R$. For an [monatomic gas](../../../../../monatomic-gas.md) the [virial theorem](../../../../../virial-theorem.md) is $2U+\Omega=0$, so half the [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../gravitational-energy.md) released by contraction heats the star and half can be radiated. The [Kelvin-Helmholtz cooling time](../../../../../kelvin-helmholtz-cooling-time.md) is therefore of order

$$
\boxed{t_{\rm KH}\sim\frac{GM_\odot^2}{2R_\odot L_\odot}\simeq1.6\times10^7\,\mathrm{yr}.}
$$

A uniform-[density](../../../../../density.md) model gives $3GM^2/(10RL)\simeq9.4\times10^6\,\mathrm{yr}$. The given bound fixes a scale, rather than an exact contraction history; both estimates are far shorter than the Sun's [main-sequence lifetime](../../../../../main-sequence-lifetime.md).
