<h1 id="s2/2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The right panel of the figure in [solution](../../1/b/solution.md) has [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) increasing upwards and [effective temperature](../../../../../effective-temperature.md) decreasing to the right. An old [globular cluster](../../../../../globular-cluster.md) contains a low-mass [main sequence](../../../../../main-sequence.md), a [main-sequence turnoff](../../../../../main-sequence-turnoff.md), [subgiants](../../../../../subgiant.md), a [red giant branch](../../../../../red-giant-branch.md), a [horizontal branch](../../../../../horizontal-branch.md), an [Asymptotic giant branch](../../../../../asymptotic-giant-branch.md) and a faint hot [white dwarf](../../../../../white-dwarf.md) sequence. Its high-mass [main sequence](../../../../../main-sequence.md) is absent because those stars have evolved.

If a fraction $f_c=0.1$ of the star participates in [hydrogen burning](../../../../../hydrogen-burning.md), with initial [hydrogen mass fraction](../../../../../hydrogen-mass-fraction.md) $X=0.7$, the available [nuclear binding energy](../../../../../nuclear-binding-energy.md) is $0.007f_cXM c^2$. Assuming a roughly constant [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) and coeval stars,

$$
\boxed{t\simeq\frac{0.007f_cX(0.8M_\odot)c^2}{1.6\times10^{26}\,\mathrm W}\simeq1.39\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{yr}.}
$$

Taking the quoted core fraction as hydrogen fuel itself, $f_cX=0.1$, gives $1.98\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{yr}$. Stating the fuel convention matters: burning the whole stellar mass overestimates the age by about an order of magnitude. Evolving [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) and detailed [stellar structure](../../../../../stellar-structure-split.md) are needed for an accurate age.
