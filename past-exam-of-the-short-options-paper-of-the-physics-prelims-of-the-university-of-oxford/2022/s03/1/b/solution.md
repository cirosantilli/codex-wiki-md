<h1 id="s03/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take equal arm lengths, no plate, and coherent illumination. At a coated [beam splitter](../../../../../beam-splitter.md), choose the real-amplitude convention where transmission has amplitude $1/\sqrt2$ and reflection has amplitude $+1/\sqrt2$ from one side and $-1/\sqrt2$ from the other; which reflection carries the minus sign is a basis convention. The two paths interfere constructively at detector 1 and destructively at detector 2 in the diagram's labeling. Thus

$$
\boxed{I_1/I_{\rm in}=1,\qquad I_2/I_{\rm in}=0.}
$$

For example, writing the [beam splitter](../../../../../beam-splitter.md) as $B=2^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ and the two equal-reflection [mirrors](../../../../../mirror.md) as $M=-\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, one obtains $BMB(1,0)^T=(0,-1)^T$. The first output component propagates downwards to detector 2; the nonzero second component propagates upwards to detector 1. The overall minus sign is an unobservable global phase. The redistribution is [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) of amplitudes, rather than two successive independent coin tosses.
