<h1 id="s03/2/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Let $\theta_j=j\pi/N$ and retain the positive [spin](../../../../../spin.md) beam at every stage. The overlap of successive positive [eigenstates](../../../../../eigenstate.md) is

$$
\langle\psi_{\theta_j}^+|\psi_{\theta_{j-1}}^+\rangle
=\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2N}\right).
$$

Thus the unconditional survival [probability](../../../../../probability.md), relative to the original intensity, is

$$
\boxed{p_N=\left[\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2N}\right)\right]^N.}
$$

The last retained [quantum state](../../../../../quantum-state.md) is $|\psi_\pi^+\rangle=|\downarrow\rangle$, so its final negative-$z$ result is certain conditional on survival and adds no further factor. In particular $p_2=1/4$, $p_{10}\simeq0.7805$, and $p_{100}\simeq0.9756$. Since $\log\cos x=-x^2/2+O(x^4)$,

$$
\log p_N=-\frac{\pi^2}{4N}+O(N^{-3}),\qquad \boxed{\lim_{N\to\infty}p_N=1.}
$$

This is a changing-measurement-axis form of the [quantum Zeno effect](../../../../../quantum-zeno-effect.md): frequent selections steer the surviving [spin](../../../../../spin.md) through a continuous succession of nearby [eigenstates](../../../../../eigenstate.md).
