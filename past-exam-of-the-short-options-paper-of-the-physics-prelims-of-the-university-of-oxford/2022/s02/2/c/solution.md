<h1 id="s02/2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The same gas-pressure [stellar homology](../../../../../stellar-homology.md) estimates give $T\propto\mu M/R_*$ and $\rho\propto M/R_*^3$. With [Kramers' opacity law](../../../../../kramers-opacity-law.md) $\kappa\propto\rho T^{-7/2}$, [radiative diffusion](../../../../../radiative-diffusion.md) gives

$$
L\propto\frac{R_*T^4}{\kappa\rho}
\propto\frac{R_*T^{15/2}}{\rho^2}
\propto\mu^{15/2}M^{11/2}R_*^{-1/2}.
$$

Consequently the claimed exponent five additionally requires the approximate low-mass [main-sequence mass-radius relation](../../../../../main-sequence-mass-radius-relation.md) $R_*\propto M$ and fixed composition:

$$
\boxed{R_*\propto M\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L\propto M^5.}
$$

The supplied [opacity](../../../../../opacity.md) and pressure assumptions alone do not establish that [mass-radius relation](../../../../../mass-radius-relation.md). For example, a homologous fixed-radius family obeys those scaling assumptions but gives $L\propto M^{11/2}$, not $M^5$. More generally $R_*\propto M^q$ gives $L\propto M^{(11-q)/2}$. Very low-mass [stars](../../../../../star.md) can be convective, where this radiative model ceases to apply.
