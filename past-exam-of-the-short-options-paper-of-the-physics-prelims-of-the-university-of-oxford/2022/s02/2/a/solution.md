<h1 id="s02/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $r$ be radial position, $\mathcal F=L/(4\pi r^2)$ the outward radiative energy [flux](../../../../../flux.md), and $P_r=aT^4/3$ the [radiation pressure](../../../../../radiation-pressure.md). A thin layer absorbs a fraction $\kappa\rho\,dr$ of an incident directed beam. Since a [photon](../../../../../photon.md) carries [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) $E/c$, the outward force per unit volume deposited by the [radiation](../../../../../radiation.md) is $\kappa\rho\mathcal F/c$. Balance it against the [radiation pressure](../../../../../radiation-pressure.md) gradient:

$$
\frac{dP_r}{dr}=-\frac{\kappa\rho}{c}\mathcal F
=-\frac{\kappa\rho L}{4\pi r^2c}.
$$

Substituting $dP_r/dr=(4a/3)T^3dT/dr$ yields the [radiative diffusion](../../../../../radiative-diffusion.md) equation

$$
\boxed{L=-4\pi r^2\frac{4ac}{3\kappa\rho}T^3\frac{dT}{dr}.}
$$

The sign expresses outward energy [flux](../../../../../flux.md) down the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) gradient. The approximation assumes optically thick material and near local thermodynamic equilibrium, with an appropriate mean [opacity](../../../../../opacity.md).

The printed attenuation law describes the loss from an incident beam; it is not generally the radial derivative of the net [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md), since absorption is accompanied by emission. For a concrete counterexample, in a steady envelope without local energy generation the net [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) is constant even though $\kappa\rho>0$ and $dT/dr<0$. Applying $dL/dr=-\kappa\rho L$ to that net [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) would contradict energy conservation. The local [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) deposition argument supplies the physically correct derivation.
