<h1 id="s02/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume spherical [stars](../../../../../star.md), central eclipses, uniform disc [surface brightness](../../../../../surface-brightness.md), and negligible third light. Let $s$ denote the smaller [star](../../../../../star.md) and $\ell$ the larger one, with $r=R_s/R_\ell\leq1$ and $\ell_s=L_s/L_\ell$. Normalize the out-of-eclipse [flux](../../../../../flux.md) to one, and denote the deep and shallow fractional losses by $D$ and $d$. The graph gives approximately $F_{\rm out}\simeq0.98$, $F_{\rm deep}\simeq0.57$, $F_{\rm shallow}\simeq0.90$, so $D\simeq0.42$, $d\simeq0.08$; the last digits are graphical estimates.

At total occultation of the smaller [star](../../../../../star.md), the lost [flux](../../../../../flux.md) is $L_s/(L_s+L_\ell)$. At its central transit across the larger [star](../../../../../star.md), the lost [flux](../../../../../flux.md) is $r^2L_\ell/(L_s+L_\ell)$. There are two identifications:

- **Smaller star hidden at the deep eclipse.** Then $D=\ell_s/(1+\ell_s)$ and $d=r^2/(1+\ell_s)$, giving $\ell_s=D/(1-D)$ and $r=\sqrt{d/(1-D)}$. Thus $\boxed{R_s/R_\ell\simeq0.37,\quad L_s/L_\ell\simeq0.72}$. The smaller [star](../../../../../star.md) has the higher [surface brightness](../../../../../surface-brightness.md); the deep eclipse hides it and the shallow eclipse is its transit.
- **Smaller star hidden at the shallow eclipse.** Then $d=\ell_s/(1+\ell_s)$ and $D=r^2/(1+\ell_s)$, giving $\ell_s=d/(1-d)$ and $r=\sqrt{D/(1-d)}$. Thus $\boxed{R_s/R_\ell\simeq0.67,\quad L_s/L_\ell\simeq0.089}$. The smaller [star](../../../../../star.md) has the lower [surface brightness](../../../../../surface-brightness.md); the deep eclipse is its transit across the hotter, larger [star](../../../../../star.md).

These are ratios of [luminosities](../../../../../luminosity.md) in the observed band. Treating them as bolometric [luminosities](../../../../../luminosity.md) requires similar band corrections or an explicitly bolometric [light curve](../../../../../light-curve.md). [Limb darkening](../../../../../limb-darkening.md) and finite exposure times limit the precision of these elementary estimates.
