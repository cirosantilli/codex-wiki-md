<h1 id="s02/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Stefan–Boltzmann law](../../../../../stefan-boltzmann-law.md) gives $L=4\pi R^2\sigma T_{\rm eff}^4$ for bolometric [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{\frac{T_s}{T_\ell}=\left[\frac{L_s/L_\ell}{(R_s/R_\ell)^2}\right]^{1/4}.}
$$

With the central values above, this is approximately $1.51$ for the first geometry and $0.67$ for the second. If only one-band [luminosities](../../../../../luminosity.md) are measured, [bolometric corrections](../../../../../bolometric-correction.md) are needed before using this formula for [effective temperatures](../../../../../effective-temperature.md).

Two direct methods are fitting the continuum [stellar spectrum](../../../../../stellar-spectrum.md) or several photometric colors to a [stellar atmosphere](../../../../../stellar-atmosphere.md) model, and measuring temperature-sensitive [absorption lines](../../../../../absorption-line.md) or line ratios in a [stellar spectrum](../../../../../stellar-spectrum.md). The former includes the approximate [black-body radiation](../../../../../black-body-radiation.md) estimate $\lambda_{\max}T=b$ from [Wien's displacement law](../../../../../wien-s-displacement-law.md); the latter uses ionization and excitation populations. Observations during total eclipse or spectral disentangling help separate the two [stellar spectra](../../../../../stellar-spectrum.md).

For comparable composition and accessible fuel fractions, the [main-sequence lifetime](../../../../../main-sequence-lifetime.md) is proportional to available nuclear fuel divided by [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md), $t_{\rm MS}\propto M/L$. Estimate the masses from a calibrated [mass-luminosity relation](../../../../../mass-luminosity-relation.md) $L\propto M^\alpha$ in the appropriate mass range. Then

$$
\boxed{\frac{t_s}{t_\ell}\simeq\frac{M_s/M_\ell}{L_s/L_\ell}=\left(\frac{L_s}{L_\ell}\right)^{1/\alpha-1}.}
$$

For illustration $\alpha=3.5$ gives about $1.27$ and $5.6$ for the two graphical [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) ratios. The common [main sequence](../../../../../main-sequence.md) approximation is additional physics; eclipse depths alone do not determine masses or ages. A [main sequence](../../../../../main-sequence.md) consistency check would also favor the second geometry because ordinary [main-sequence stars](../../../../../main-sequence-star.md) have radius and [effective temperature](../../../../../effective-temperature.md) increasing with mass.
