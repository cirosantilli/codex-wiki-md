<h1 id="s02/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In the prescribed matter-dominated approximation, neglect curvature, [radiation](../../../../../radiation.md), and the [cosmological constant](../../../../../cosmological-constant.md) throughout the calculation. The [Friedmann equation](../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) and $\rho_m\propto R^{-3}$ give $\dot R\propto R^{-1/2}$, hence $R\propto t^{2/3}$ and $H=2/(3t)$. For $H_0=70\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}=2.269\times10^{-18}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$,

$$
\boxed{t_0=\frac{2}{3H_0}\simeq9.31\times10^9\ \mathrm{yr}.}
$$

The [particle horizon](../../../../../particle-horizon.md) is a proper distance on a constant-time slice, not simply $ct$:

$$
r_p(t)=ct^{2/3}\int_0^t(t')^{-2/3}\,dt'=3ct.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{r_p(t_0)=\frac{2c}{H_0}\simeq8.57\times10^3\ \mathrm{Mpc}\simeq27.9\times10^9\ \mathrm{light\ years}.}
$$

These are estimates within a spatially flat, matter-only model, rather than the measured age and [particle horizon](../../../../../particle-horizon.md) of the actual late-time universe.
