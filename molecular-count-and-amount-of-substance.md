# Molecular count and amount of substance

↑ **Parent:** [Stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md)

The molecular count $N$ is a dimensionless integer; the [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) $n$ has unit [mole](mole-unit.md), symbol $\mathrm{mol}$. They satisfy

$$
N=nN_{\mathrm A},\qquad N_{\mathrm A}=6.02214076\times10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol}^{-1}.
$$

The [Avogadro constant](avogadro-constant.md) is exact by the modern definition of the [mole](mole-unit.md): see [SI base unit: mole by Bureau International des Poids et Mesures](si-base-unit-mole-by-bureau-international-des-poids-et-mesures.md). Thus two [water](water.md) [molecules](molecule-split.md) correspond to $2/N_{\mathrm A}$ [moles](mole-unit.md), whereas two [moles](mole-unit.md) of [water](water.md) contain $1.204428152\times10^{24}$ [molecules](molecule-split.md). A [mole](mole-unit.md) is a unit for counting specified entities, which may also be [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), [ions](ion.md) or [electrons](electron.md).
