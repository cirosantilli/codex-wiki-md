# Higgs cancellation in longitudinal vector scattering

↑ **Parent:** [Goldstone-boson equivalence theorem](goldstone-boson-equivalence-theorem.md)

The [Goldstone-boson equivalence theorem](goldstone-boson-equivalence-theorem.md) reduces high-energy electroweak longitudinal scattering to scalar scattering. Without the radial [Higgs boson](higgs-boson.md), a nonlinear sigma-model description gives an amplitude of order $s/v^2$. In the corresponding linear scalar model, the contact and Higgs-exchange terms give, for the charged-to-neutral Goldstone channel,

$$
\mathcal M=-\frac{m_H^2}{v^2}-\frac{m_H^4}{v^2(s-m_H^2)}
=\frac{s}{v^2}-\frac{s^2}{v^2(s-m_H^2)}.
$$

The growing terms cancel, leaving $-m_H^2/v^2$ at high energy. Thus the [Higgs mechanism](higgs-mechanism.md) supplies both vector masses and the interactions required for controlled longitudinal scattering. The tree expression is not valid as an unresummed approximation arbitrarily close to the Higgs pole.
