# Causal Green function for a repeated-root Euler operator

↑ **Parent:** [Green's function](green-s-function-split.md)

For $L=y''+\alpha y'/x+(\alpha-1)^2y/(4x^2)$ on $x>0$, the repeated [indicial root](indicial-root.md) is $r=(1-\alpha)/2$ and the homogeneous solutions are $x^r,x^r\log x$. Their [Wronskian](wronskian.md) is $x^{-\alpha}$. The displayed [causal Green function](causal-green-function.md) is continuous at each source $\xi>0$, has a unit jump in its first [derivative](derivative.md), and satisfies $L_xG=\delta(x-\xi)$. It is zero near the origin, so its value and first derivative there vanish. Those origin conditions alone fail to give uniqueness when $\alpha<-1$: then both homogeneous solutions and their first derivatives vanish as $x\downarrow0$. For forcing $f(x)=x$, source integration from zero converges when $\alpha>-5$ and gives $y_p=4x^3/(\alpha+5)^2$. All zero-origin-data solutions in $-5<\alpha<-1$ are $y_p+x^r(C_1+C_2\log x)$; for $\alpha\geq-1$ the solution is unique. Causality selects the displayed kernel even when the bare singular-endpoint conditions are insufficient.
