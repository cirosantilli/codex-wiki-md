# Measuring refraction with a rectangular block

↑ **Parent:** [Refraction](refraction.md)

Use a slit [light](light.md) source, a rectangular transparent block on plain paper, pencil, ruler and protractor. Trace the block outline, keep it fixed, and mark widely separated points on incident and emergent [light rays](light-ray.md). Remove the block and join the entry and exit points to reconstruct its internal [light ray](light-ray.md). Draw the surface [normal vector](normal-vector.md) at entry; measure $i$ outside and $r$ inside from that normal line. Choose a path exiting the opposite parallel face rather than a side edge. Repeat for a range of $i$ with the same block and source colour; record $i,r,\sin i,\sin r$ and repeat-reading spread. Plot $\sin i$ vertically against $\sin r$ horizontally: [Snell's law](snell-s-law.md) predicts slope $n_{\mathrm{block}}/n_{\mathrm{air}}$, approximately the block's [refractive index](refractive-index.md). The emergent [light ray](light-ray.md) should be parallel to the incident one. Beam width, pencil marks, protractor resolution, block movement and colour spread limit precision; a narrow-colour source reduces spread from [optical dispersion](dispersion-optics.md). These are measured angular readings, not a direct timing of [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md).[International GCSE (Modular) Core Practical Guide, Issue 1, Physics core practicals 8–9, printed pp. 171–172 and 178–179: slit source, internal angle convention, pencil crosses, side-edge avoidance and sine-plot axes](international-gcse-modular-core-practical-guide.md)

A [normal incidence](normal-incidence.md) trial checks the drawn normal and the absence of bending, but the ratio $\sin i/\sin r$ is then $0/0$. Small nonzero [angles](angle.md) also make that ratio sensitive to reading errors. Compare several trials through the slope rather than relying on one nearly axial pair. This rectangular-block measurement complements the existing [total internal reflection](total-internal-reflection.md) experiment, which probes a different boundary condition.
