# Optical theorem at second Born order

↑ **Parent:** [Optical theorem](optical-theorem.md)

For a real [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md) potential the first Born forward amplitude is real, while the leading total [scattering cross-section](scattering-cross-section.md) is quadratic in the potential. The second Born amplitude supplies an imaginary part from its outgoing [Green function](green-s-function-split.md). Symmetrizing the two interaction positions makes its imaginary part equal to $k/(4\pi)$ times the integrated squared first Born amplitude. The identity therefore compares quantities at the same perturbative order; a real first Born amplitude alone cannot test it against a nonzero [scattering cross-section](scattering-cross-section.md).
