# Factorwise separability of a polynomial

↑ **Parent:** [Separable polynomial](separable-polynomial.md)

A nonzero [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) is factorwise separable if every irreducible factor is a [separable polynomial](separable-polynomial.md). Unlike the convention that all roots must be distinct, this condition allows repeated irreducible factors. Its [splitting field](splitting-field.md) is a [separable field extension](separable-extension.md) precisely when the polynomial is factorwise separable: in one direction every root has a separable minimal polynomial; in the other, adjoining finitely many roots with separable minimal polynomials gives a separable extension. Thus the implication from a separable splitting extension to a polynomial with distinct roots needs the extra squarefree hypothesis. The example $(X-1)^2$ already distinguishes the conventions.
