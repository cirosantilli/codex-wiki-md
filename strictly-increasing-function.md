# Strictly increasing function

↑ **Parent:** [Monotonic function](monotonic-function.md)

A [real-valued function](real-valued-function.md) $f:D\to\mathbb R$ is a [strictly increasing function](strictly-increasing-function.md) if every pair $x<y$ in its [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) satisfies $f(x)<f(y)$. It is therefore [injective](injective-function.md), and each output has at most one [preimage](preimage.md). Its [inverse function](inverse-function.md) on its [image of a function](image-of-a-function.md) is also [strictly increasing](strictly-increasing-function.md). Every [strictly increasing function](strictly-increasing-function.md) is a [nondecreasing function](nondecreasing-function.md); the converse fails when outputs repeat.

The [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) $f(x)=x^3$ is a [strictly increasing function](strictly-increasing-function.md) on the [real line](real-line.md), although its [derivative](derivative.md) $f'(x)=3x^2$ is zero at zero. Indeed, for $x<y$,

$$
y^3-x^3=(y-x)(x^2+xy+y^2)>0,
$$

because $x^2+xy+y^2=(x+y/2)^2+3y^2/4$ can vanish only when $x=y=0$. Thus a positive [derivative](derivative.md) everywhere is sufficient on a [real interval](interval-mathematics.md), but is not necessary for a [strictly increasing function](strictly-increasing-function.md).

Strict [monotonicity](monotonic-function.md) does not ensure [continuity](continuous-function.md): $g(x)=x$ for $x<0$ and $g(x)=x+1$ for $x\geq0$ is a [strictly increasing function](strictly-increasing-function.md) with a [jump discontinuity](jump-discontinuity.md). Its [image of a function](image-of-a-function.md) omits $[0,1)$, so the [inverse function](inverse-function.md) exists only on the actual [image of a function](image-of-a-function.md), rather than on all the [real numbers](real-number.md).
