# Symmetric-square proof of the naive height parallelogram estimate

↑ **Parent:** [Height parallelogram identity](height-parallelogram-identity.md)

The [biquadratic morphism for elliptic sums and differences](biquadratic-morphism-for-elliptic-sums-and-differences.md) has no common zero, so the local coefficient norms give

$$
h([A:B:C])=2h(x(P))+2h(x(Q))+O(1),
$$

uniformly in $P,Q$. Upper bounds use the fixed coefficients. Lower bounds follow from the absence of common zeros over every [local field](local-field.md); the constants are $1$ outside finitely many places, where the integral morphism still has no base point after reduction.

For the binary quadratic with roots $u,v\in\mathbb P^1(K)$, the [projective height](projective-height.md) of its coefficient vector is $h(u)+h(v)+O(1)$. At nonarchimedean places the coefficient maximum norm of a product of linear [polynomials](polynomial-split.md) is the product of their maximum norms by the [multiplicativity of the nonarchimedean polynomial norm](multiplicativity-of-the-nonarchimedean-polynomial-norm.md). At archimedean places the same norms are comparable up to a fixed factor for degree two. This also includes roots at infinity. Combining the comparisons yields $h_x(P+Q)+h_x(P-Q)=2h_x(P)+2h_x(Q)+O(1)$. After dividing the identity for $2^nP,2^nQ$ by $4^n$ and taking limits, it gives the exact [height parallelogram identity](height-parallelogram-identity.md) for the [canonical height of an elliptic curve](canonical-height-of-an-elliptic-curve.md).
