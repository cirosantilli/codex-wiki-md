# Local-unitary equivalence

↑ **Parent:** [Local unitary operation](local-unitary-operation.md)

Two bipartite [pure states](pure-state.md) on fixed subsystem spaces are [local-unitary equivalent](local-unitary-equivalence.md) when related by the displayed product of [unitary operators](unitary-operator.md). [Identity operators](identity-operator.md) give [reflexivity](reflexive-relation.md), inverse operators give symmetry, and products of local operators give [transitivity](transitive-relation.md). Thus this is an [equivalence relation](equivalence-relation.md), whose classes are the orbits under local unitaries.

Every pair of normalized [product states](product-state.md) is [local-unitary equivalent](local-unitary-equivalence.md): extend each factor to an [orthonormal basis](orthonormal-basis.md) and use a [unitary operator](unitary-operator.md) carrying one [basis](basis.md) to the other. Local operators preserve a product factorization, and their inverses do too, so [quantum entanglement](entangled-state.md) cannot be created or removed this way. More generally, the [tensor-product coefficient matrix](tensor-product-coefficient-matrix.md) changes as $C\mapsto UCV^T$, which preserves its [singular values](singular-value.md) and therefore its [Schmidt coefficients](schmidt-coefficient.md). The transpose on the second factor is ordinary, not a [conjugate transpose](conjugate-transpose.md), because coefficients of a [tensor product](tensor-product.md) depend bilinearly on the two input [vectors](vector.md).
