# Thermodynamics

↑ **Parent:** [Statistical physics](statistical-physics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamics)

Thermodynamics studies [energy](energy.md) transfers and the constraints on macroscopic changes. Its vocabulary connects [internal energy](internal-energy.md), [entropy](entropy.md), [temperature](temperature.md), [pressure](pressure.md) and [volume](volume.md) to [heat](heat.md) and [work](work.md). The [first law of thermodynamics](first-law-of-thermodynamics.md) balances [energy](energy.md); the [Second law of thermodynamics](second-law-of-thermodynamics.md) decides which changes can occur spontaneously and limits how much [work](work.md) a [heat engine](heat-engine.md) can deliver. These principles let us bound performance before choosing a material or a detailed machine design. Applications include power generation, propulsion and [refrigerators](refrigerator.md). [MIT, What thermodynamics describes](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node10.html)

Start by choosing a system boundary. A [closed system](closed-system.md) can exchange [energy](energy.md) but no matter; an [isolated system](isolated-system.md) exchanges neither. A [state function](state-function.md), such as [internal energy](internal-energy.md) or [entropy](entropy.md), depends on the equilibrium state. A process specifies how that state changes. Thus a body's [temperature](temperature.md) is a property it has, whereas [heat](heat.md) records [energy](energy.md) crossing its boundary during a process. A body contains [internal energy](internal-energy.md), rather than a stored quantity of [heat](heat.md).

Two insulated bodies initially at different positive [temperatures](temperature.md) approach [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium.md) when brought into thermal contact. Their combined [energy](energy.md) is conserved, but their combined [entropy](entropy.md) increases. They do not spontaneously separate again into a hotter body and a colder one. This familiar asymmetry makes [thermodynamics](thermodynamics.md) interesting: conserving [energy](energy.md) leaves many imaginable changes available, and [entropy](entropy.md) supplies a further constraint. The same distinction underlies both [entropy maximization under thermal contact](entropy-maximization-under-thermal-contact.md) and the [thermal efficiency](thermal-efficiency.md) limit in [Carnot's theorem](carnot-s-theorem.md).
