# Infinite-cardinal categoricity forces completeness

↑ **Parent:** [Categorical theory](categorical-theory.md)

If a [theory](first-order-theory.md) without finite [models](model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) is categorical in an infinite cardinal at least the language size, it is complete. Otherwise its two [consistent](consistent-first-order-theory.md) extensions deciding a sentence oppositely have infinite [models](model-of-a-first-order-theory.md). The [Löwenheim-Skolem theorems](lowenheim-skolem-theorem.md) produce [models](model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of both of the specified [cardinality](cardinality.md), contradicting their forced [isomorphism](structure-isomorphism.md). Categoricity includes existence at that [cardinality](cardinality.md), so it is not vacuous.
