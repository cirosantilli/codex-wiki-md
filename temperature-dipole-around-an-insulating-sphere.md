# Temperature dipole around an insulating sphere

↑ **Parent:** [Thermal conduction](thermal-conduction.md)

In the absence of heat advection, an insulating [sphere](sphere.md) of radius $a$ in a uniform remote [temperature](temperature.md) gradient $\mathbf H$ has the displayed exterior [harmonic function](harmonic-function.md) as its temperature field. The reflected dipole term makes $\partial_rT=0$ at $r=a$. The surface temperature is $T_0+(3/2)a\mathbf H\cdot\mathbf n$, so the tangential gradient is amplified by $3/2$. If the sphere has [thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md) ratio $K$ relative to its surroundings, temperature and heat-flux continuity instead give surface gradient factor $3/(2+K)$.
