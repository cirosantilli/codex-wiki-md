# Tetrahedron volume under sliding opposite edges

↑ **Parent:** [Opposite edges of a tetrahedron](opposite-edges-of-a-tetrahedron.md)

Place one pair of opposite [tetrahedron](tetrahedron.md) vertices on the line $b+\lambda a$ and the other pair on $d+\mu c$, with fixed nonzero direction [vectors](vector.md) $a,c$. Let $\Delta=d-b$ and let $\delta\lambda,\delta\mu$ be the parameter differences within the two pairs. The [scalar triple product](scalar-triple-product.md) of the three edge vectors from one vertex gives the displayed [volume](volume.md). Terms parallel to $a$ or $c$ vanish in the triple product, leaving only $\Delta\cdot(a\times c)$. Sliding either pair along its own line while preserving its separation leaves this volume unchanged. When the two lines are [skew lines](skew-lines.md), it can also be written $V=d_\perp\ell_1\ell_2|\sin\alpha|/6$, where $d_\perp$ is their shortest distance, $\ell_1,\ell_2$ the two edge lengths and $\alpha$ their direction angle. Intersecting or parallel lines give zero volume because all four vertices are coplanar.
