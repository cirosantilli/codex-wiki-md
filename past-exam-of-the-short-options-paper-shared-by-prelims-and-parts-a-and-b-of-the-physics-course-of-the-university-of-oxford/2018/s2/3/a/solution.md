<h1 id="s2/3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The curvature constant specifies the intrinsic spatial curvature of homogeneous constant-time slices. Positive curvature has spherical geometry, zero curvature Euclidean geometry, and negative curvature hyperbolic geometry. A conventional coordinate [normalization](../../../../../normalization.md) uses $k=+1,0,-1$, with the [scale factor](../../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md) carrying the curvature [radius](../../../../../radius.md); rescaling that factor changes the numerical magnitude assigned to a dimensional curvature coefficient.

The sign alone does not determine the future expansion once a [cosmological constant](../../../../../cosmological-constant.md) is admitted. Nor does negative or zero local curvature by itself determine the global topology: quotient spaces can share the same local curvature.
