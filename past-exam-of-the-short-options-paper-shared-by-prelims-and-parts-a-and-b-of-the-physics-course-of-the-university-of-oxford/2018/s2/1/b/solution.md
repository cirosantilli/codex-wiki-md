<h1 id="s2/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Integrating $dP/dr=-GM_r\rho/r^2$ in order of magnitude, with $\rho\sim M_*/R_*^3$, gives

$$
P_c\sim\frac{GM_*^2}{R_*^4}.
$$

The [ideal gas law](../../../../../ideal-gas-law.md) independently gives $P_c\sim M_*k_BT_c/(\mu m_HR_*^3)$. Equating the two estimates yields

$$
\boxed{R_*\sim\frac{G\mu m_H}{k_BT_c}M_*.}
$$

For similar composition and an approximately fixed hydrogen-burning [central temperature](../../../../../central-stellar-temperature.md), $R_*\propto M_*$. Calibrating this rough relation on the Sun and using part [a](../a.md) gives a nominal [mass](../../../../../mass.md) range of about $0.05$ to $5\,M_\odot$.

The endpoint [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) ratio is $10^8$, whereas the inferred [mass](../../../../../mass.md) ratio is about $10^2$, giving $L\propto M^4$ across that range. The _exponent_ is therefore consistent at this schematic level. The solar [normalization](../../../../../normalization.md) and individual endpoint [masses](../../../../../mass.md) are much less reliable: using the [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) relation itself gives approximately $0.1$ to $10\,M_\odot$. [Central temperatures](../../../../../central-stellar-temperature.md) are not truly constant, low-mass stellar structure differs, and the uncorrected $V$ [luminosities](../../../../../luminosity.md) in the diagram are not bolometric. These limitations prevent treating the rough radius-based [masses](../../../../../mass.md) as a contradictory precision measurement.
