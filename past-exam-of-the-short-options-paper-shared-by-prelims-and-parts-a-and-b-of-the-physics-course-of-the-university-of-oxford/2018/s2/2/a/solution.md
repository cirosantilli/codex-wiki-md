<h1 id="s2/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At this small [redshift](../../../../../redshift.md), $D\simeq cz/H_0\simeq20.0$ Mpc. The outer angular [radius](../../../../../radius.md) gives $R_{\rm out}\simeq D\theta\simeq21.3$ kpc. Approximating the dynamical [mass](../../../../../mass.md) by a spherical equivalent,

$$
\boxed{M_{\rm dyn}(<R_{\rm out})\simeq\frac{V_{\rm rot}^2R_{\rm out}}G
\simeq4.9\times10^{11}M_\odot.}
$$

The supplied [Tully-Fisher relation](../../../../../tully-fisher-relation.md) gives $M_V\simeq-22.49$, and hence $L_V\simeq9.9\times10^{10}L_{V,\odot}$. Treating the population as solar-like gives the estimate $\Upsilon_V\simeq1\,M_\odot/L_{V,\odot}$, so

$$
\boxed{M_{\rm luminous}\simeq1.0\times10^{11}M_\odot.}
$$

The dynamical estimate exceeds that by about a factor of five; a massive extended dark-matter component is the natural interpretation. Solar-like spectra do not exactly fix the population [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md), and a disk is not spherical, but order-unity corrections do not remove this large outer [mass](../../../../../mass.md) excess under the stated estimate.
