<h1 id="s2/2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let $I$ denote a characteristic [surface brightness](../../../../../surface-brightness.md) and $\Upsilon=M/L$ the relevant dynamical [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md). With comparable profiles, $L\propto IR^2$ and $M\propto V^2R/G$. Eliminating $R$ gives

$$
\boxed{L\propto\frac{V^4}{G^2\Upsilon^2 I}.}
$$

If both $I$ and $\Upsilon$ are independent of [speed](../../../../../speed.md), converting [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) to magnitude gives $M_V=\text{constant}-10\log_{10}V$. The empirical coefficient $-8$ instead means $L\propto V^{3.2}$, and therefore

$$
\boxed{\Upsilon^2 I\propto V^{0.8}.}
$$

For constant [surface brightness](../../../../../surface-brightness.md) this requires $\Upsilon\propto V^{0.4}$; for constant [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) it requires $I\propto V^{0.8}$. A mixture of these changes, including varying stellar populations or dark-matter fractions, can give the same observed slope. The simple derivation assumes comparable characteristic [radii](../../../../../radius.md) and structural coefficients.
