<h1 id="s2/2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Using the same [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) estimate, the [distance modulus](../../../../../distance-modulus.md) is $12.5-(-22.49)=34.99$, implying $D\simeq99.4$ Mpc. Pure Hubble expansion at that distance predicts about $7450\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. The measured [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) therefore implies

$$
\boxed{v_{\rm pec,los}\simeq6500-7450\simeq-950\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

Its line-of-sight [peculiar velocity](../../../../../peculiar-velocity.md) is toward us, of the order expected for orbital motions in a rich [galaxy](../../../../../galaxy-split.md) cluster. A [galaxy](../../../../../galaxy-split.md) falling toward a cluster centre from its far side could have this sign. Its actual front/back location or three-dimensional orbit is not determined without the cluster's mean [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md), distance and projected [position](../../../../../position.md). Tully-Fisher scatter and the assumption of identical [luminosities](../../../../../luminosity.md) also limit that inference.
