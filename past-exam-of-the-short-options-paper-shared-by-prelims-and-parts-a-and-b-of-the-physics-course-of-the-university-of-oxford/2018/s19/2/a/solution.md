<h1 id="s19/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The accelerating supply transfers $I_eV=50$ kW into the beam, but the [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) need not be discarded with that [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md). An electrostatic recovery system decelerates the used beam before collection and returns most of its [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) to the [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) or supply. The net required [power](../../../../../power.md) then covers unrecovered beam [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and losses, rather than the whole circulating kinetic-energy flux.

For example, $99\%$ recovery leaves $0.01(50\,\mathrm{kW})\simeq500$ W of unrecovered beam [power](../../../../../power.md), plus auxiliary loads. This explains the stated two-order-of-magnitude scale. It does not imply that gun heating, vacuum equipment, magnets and all electrical losses are automatically included in a $500$ W total plant budget.
