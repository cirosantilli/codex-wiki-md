<h1 id="s29/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

In [core accretion](../../../../core-accretion.md), solid material grows from dust and aggregates into planetesimals or rapidly accreted pebbles. A sufficiently large core binds an atmosphere; beyond the snow line, additional condensed volatiles aid solid growth. Cooling allows the envelope to contract and acquire more [gas](../../../../gas.md). Once [gas](../../../../gas.md) self-gravity becomes important, rapid [gas](../../../../gas.md) accretion can build a giant [planet](../../../../planet.md) until disk supply, gap formation or disk dispersal limits growth. Migration and interactions with other bodies can alter its final orbit.

Heavy-element enrichment in giant [planets](../../../../planet.md), evidence for substantial interior heavy material, the prevalence of smaller solid [planets](../../../../planet.md) and the correlation of giant-planet occurrence with host-star metallicity all support an important role for solid accumulation. They do not uniquely establish one growth pathway. A classic difficulty is forming cores and cooling envelopes before a [gas](../../../../gas.md) disk disperses, especially far from the [star](../../../../star.md); migration, envelope [opacity](../../../../opacity.md), solid-delivery rates and fragmentation complicate the calculation. Pebble-assisted growth can change the timescale, and disk gravitational instability remains a distinct possible route in some environments.

[Planetary luminosity](../../../../planetary-luminosity.md) is the total emitted [power](../../../../power.md). For the Kelvin-Helmholtz budget one needs the **intrinsic** [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md), excluding reradiated absorbed starlight. With orbital distance $r_p$, [Bond albedo](../../../../bond-albedo.md) $A_B$, solar [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md) $L_\odot$ and present-like [radius](../../../../radius.md) $R_J$,

$$
P_{\rm abs}=(1-A_B)\frac{L_\odot R_J^2}{4r_p^2}.
$$

The given ratio of emitted to absorbed [power](../../../../power.md) is $1.67$, so $L_{\rm int}=0.67P_{\rm abs}$. Using the supplied [radius](../../../../radius.md), $r_p=5.2$ AU and a solar [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md) around $3.8\times10^{26}$ W gives

$$
\boxed{P_{\rm abs}\simeq5.1\times10^{17}\,\mathrm W,\qquad
L_{\rm int}\simeq3.4\times10^{17}\,\mathrm W,}
$$

consistent with the quoted $3.42\times10^{17}$ W. Taking $1.67P_{\rm abs}$ as intrinsic [power](../../../../power.md) would incorrectly include reradiated sunlight.

The Hill [radius](../../../../radius.md) is the scale at which stellar tidal [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) competes with the [planet](../../../../planet.md)'s attraction. It bounds the approximate region in which [gas](../../../../gas.md) can remain gravitationally associated with the [planet](../../../../planet.md) and helps set feeding and envelope boundary scales. With a Jupiter [mass](../../../../mass.md),

$$
\boxed{R_H\simeq5.2\,\mathrm{AU}\left(\frac{M_J}{3M_\odot}\right)^{1/3}
\simeq0.355\,\mathrm{AU}\simeq5.3\times10^{10}\,\mathrm m.}
$$

Using this as the stipulated extended proto-planet [radius](../../../../radius.md) and the present-like intrinsic [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md) gives

$$
\boxed{t_{\rm KH}\sim\frac{GM_J^2}{R_HL_{\rm int}}
\simeq4.2\times10^8\,\mathrm{yr}.}
$$

Using the compact present [radius](../../../../radius.md) instead would give an even longer value, of order $3\times10^{11}$ years at that same [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md). Neither number is a complete formation [time](../../../../time-in-physics.md): [mass](../../../../mass.md), [radius](../../../../radius.md), [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md) and envelope structure evolve together. The Hill [radius](../../../../radius.md) is not automatically the [radius](../../../../radius.md) holding most of the binding [energy](../../../../energy-split.md).

A constant present-like [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md) with this simple [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) budget is too slow compared with a gas-disk lifetime of a few million years. At the Hill-radius [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) scale, radiating in $3$ Myr would require an average intrinsic [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md) of order $5\times10^{19}$ W, much brighter than the adopted value. This diagnoses a failure of the constant-current-Jupiter assumptions, not a general disproof of [core accretion](../../../../core-accretion.md). Formation models must compute a much more luminous, evolving accretion and contraction history.
