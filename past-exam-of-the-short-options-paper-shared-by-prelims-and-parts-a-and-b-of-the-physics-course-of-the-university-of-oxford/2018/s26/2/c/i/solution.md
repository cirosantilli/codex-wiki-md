<h1 id="s26/2/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For fully ionized [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) with a small metal fraction, there is approximately one [proton](../../../../../../proton.md) and one [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) per [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) [mass](../../../../../../mass.md), so $\mu_H\simeq1/2$. The Kramers coefficient is proportional to $Z(1+X)\simeq2Z$. Denote this [star](../../../../../../star.md)'s [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) by $L_H$; the supplied proportionality law alone does not fix its absolute [normalization](../../../../../../normalization.md).

Relative to the helium-dominated comparison [star](../../../../../../star.md), the calculation in part [ii](../ii.md) gives

$$
\boxed{L_H\simeq L_{\rm He}/155.}
$$

If one uses a roughly solar [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) as an illustrative calibration for this roughly solar-mass, solar-central-temperature reference, the [helium](../../../../../../helium.md) [star](../../../../../../star.md) would be of order a hundred solar [luminosities](../../../../../../luminosity.md). That calibration is only approximate because a mostly [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) composition with $\mu\simeq1/2$ is not the actual solar composition.
