<h1 id="s26/2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Fully ionized [helium](../../../../../../helium.md) supplies three particles per four [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) [masses](../../../../../../mass.md), so $\mu_{\rm He}\simeq4/3$. The same small $Z$, but negligible [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) fraction, gives $\kappa_{0,\rm He}\propto Z$, half the hydrogen-star coefficient. The relative [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) is

$$
\frac{L_{\rm He}}{L_H}
=\frac{\kappa_{0,H}}{\kappa_{0,\rm He}}
\left(\frac{4/3}{1/2}\right)^7
\left(\frac{0.5}{1}\right)^5
\left(\frac{10^8}{1.5\times10^7}\right)^{1/2}.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{L_{\rm He}/L_H\simeq2\left(\frac83\right)^7\frac1{32}\sqrt{\frac{20}3}
\simeq155.}
$$

The very strong molecular-weight dependence outweighs the reduction in [mass](../../../../../../mass.md). Treating the two [opacities](../../../../../../opacity.md) as identical would miss a factor two, and using neutral-gas molecular weights would be inconsistent with the stated hot ionized [stars](../../../../../../star.md).
