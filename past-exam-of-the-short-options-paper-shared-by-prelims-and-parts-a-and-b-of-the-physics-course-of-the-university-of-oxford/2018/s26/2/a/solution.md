↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Opacity](../../../../../opacity.md) is the extinction cross section per unit [mass](../../../../../mass.md) for radiation; a frequency-dependent value $\kappa_\nu$ gives $dI_\nu/ds=-\kappa_\nu\rho I_\nu$ when emission is considered separately. Stellar radiative diffusion generally uses an appropriately weighted [Rosseland mean opacity](../../../../../rosseland-mean-opacity.md). The associated local [mean free path](../../../../../mean-free-path.md) is of order $1/(\kappa\rho)$.

At sufficiently high [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) the material is highly ionized, and scattering by free [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) is often dominant for ordinary stellar [densities](../../../../../density.md) and photon energies well below the [electron](../../../../../electron.md) rest [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). Bound-bound and bound-free absorption weaken as bound [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) disappear; free-free absorption can still matter depending on [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and [density](../../../../../density.md). Thus “high [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md)” does not fix the dominant [opacity](../../../../../opacity.md) at every possible [density](../../../../../density.md). The next part explicitly imposes a Kramers absorption law as a separate scaling model.
