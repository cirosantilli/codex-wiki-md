↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A useful empirical profile is the Sérsic law,

$$
I(R)=I_e\exp\left\{-b_n\left[\left(\frac R{R_e}\right)^{1/n}-1\right]\right\},
$$

where $b_n$ is chosen so that $R_e$ encloses half the light. Classical bright [elliptical galaxies](../../../../../elliptical-galaxy.md) are often approximated by the [de Vaucouleurs's law](../../../../../de-vaucouleurs-s-law.md) case $n=4$, with $b_4\simeq7.67$. Real ellipticals span a range of Sérsic indices, so $R^{1/4}$ is not universal.

Their classical luminosity-dispersion scaling is the [Faber-Jackson relation](../../../../../faber-jackson-relation.md),

$$
\boxed{L\propto\sigma^4}
$$

as an approximate empirical trend with intrinsic scatter and sample-dependent slope. Combining size, [surface brightness](../../../../../surface-brightness.md) and dispersion gives the more informative [fundamental plane of elliptical galaxies](../../../../../fundamental-plane-elliptical-galaxies.md).
