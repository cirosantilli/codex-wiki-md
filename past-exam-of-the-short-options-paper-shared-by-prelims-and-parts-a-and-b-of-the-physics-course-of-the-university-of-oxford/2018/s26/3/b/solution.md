<h1 id="s26/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A K-star-like integrated spectrum indicates that old or intermediate-age cool [stars](../../../../../star.md), often luminous [red giants](../../../../../red-giant.md), dominate the observed optical/near-infrared light. It suggests little ongoing formation of very luminous hot O and B [stars](../../../../../star.md). It does not imply that every [star](../../../../../star.md) is K type: faint low-mass dwarfs and remnants can contribute substantial [mass](../../../../../mass.md) while contributing little light.

Using the reference wavelength printed in the problem,

$$
\boxed{z=\frac{874.0-854.4}{854.4}=0.02294.}
$$

The low-redshift Hubble estimate is

$$
\boxed{D\simeq\frac{cz}{H_0}\simeq96.9\,\mathrm{Mpc}.}
$$

[Peculiar velocities](../../../../../peculiar-velocity.md) and the distinction between [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) and angular-diameter distance introduce percent-level or larger corrections to this simple estimate. The numerical result intentionally uses the supplied calcium reference rather than replacing it with a different tabulated line wavelength.
