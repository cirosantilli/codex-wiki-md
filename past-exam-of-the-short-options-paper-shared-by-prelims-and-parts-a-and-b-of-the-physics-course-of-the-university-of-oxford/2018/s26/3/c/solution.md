<h1 id="s26/3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The integrated spectrum combines [stars](../../../../../star.md) moving toward and away from us within the [galaxy](../../../../../galaxy-split.md), broadening their absorption by [Doppler shifts](../../../../../doppler-effect.md). Assuming instrumental and individual-stellar widths have been removed or are negligible, the line-of-sight [velocity dispersion](../../../../../velocity-dispersion.md) is

$$
\boxed{\sigma_v\simeq c\frac{0.4}{874.0}\simeq137\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The angular [half-light radius](../../../../../effective-radius.md) at the low-redshift distance is $R_e\simeq2.35$ kpc. For a pressure-supported, approximately isotropic equilibrium spheroid with a standard structural virial coefficient of order five, use

$$
\boxed{M_{\rm dyn}\sim\frac{5R_e\sigma_v^2}G\simeq5.1\times10^{10}M_\odot.}
$$

The coefficient represents assumed [density](../../../../../density.md) profile, aperture and orbital structure; a different reasonable [mass](../../../../../mass.md) estimator changes the result by an order-unity factor. Treating $D$ as a [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) distance and using $D_A=D_L/(1+z)^2$ would reduce the [radius](../../../../../radius.md) and [mass](../../../../../mass.md) by about four percent here.

The [distance modulus](../../../../../distance-modulus.md) is about $34.93$. The magnitude **within** $R_e$ therefore corresponds to $M_{V,\rm half}\simeq-19.23$ and

$$
L_{\rm half}/L_\odot=10^{0.4(4.7-M_{V,\rm half})}\simeq3.74\times10^9.
$$

Doubling the half-light [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) gives

$$
\boxed{L_{V,\rm total}\simeq7.5\times10^9L_{V,\odot},\qquad
\frac{M_{\rm dyn}}{L_{V,\rm total}}\simeq6.9\,\frac{M_\odot}{L_{V,\odot}}.}
$$

This ratio is compatible with an old stellar population plus possible [dark matter](../../../../../dark-matter.md). It is larger than a solar-like [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md), but does not by itself isolate a unique dark-matter fraction: stellar age, initial [mass](../../../../../mass.md) function, anisotropy and the virial coefficient are not specified. Counting the quoted half-light magnitude as the total would incorrectly double the inferred ratio.
