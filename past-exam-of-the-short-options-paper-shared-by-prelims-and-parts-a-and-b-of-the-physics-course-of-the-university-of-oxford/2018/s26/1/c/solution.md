<h1 id="s26/1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [radiation pressure](../../../../../radiation-pressure.md) at a photospheric [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) near $5800$ K is only about $0.3$ Pa. For an illustrative photospheric [gas](../../../../../gas.md) [density](../../../../../density.md) $3\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and [mean molecular weight](../../../../../mean-molecular-weight.md) near $1.3$, the [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) is about $10^4$ Pa. Thus [gas pressure](../../../../../gas-pressure.md) dominates those outer layers by many orders of magnitude. Both the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and [density](../../../../../density.md) are needed: low [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) alone is not a complete comparison.

For homologous [stars](../../../../../star.md), hydrostatic and ideal-gas estimates give $T\sim\mu m_HGM/(k_BR)$ and $\rho\sim M/R^3$. Therefore

$$
\frac{P_{\rm rad}}{P_{\rm gas}}=\frac{a\mu m_HT^3}{3\rho k_B}
\propto\boxed{\mu^4M^2},
$$

with a structural coefficient independent of [mass](../../../../../mass.md) only under the homology assumption. As [mass](../../../../../mass.md) rises, radiation becomes increasingly important. A rough solar interior [normalization](../../../../../normalization.md) of $10^{-3}$-$10^{-2}$ suggests equality after a [mass](../../../../../mass.md) increase by tens, while realistic radiation-dominated structure and composition shift the scale; [stars](../../../../../star.md) of order $10^2M_\odot$ are a useful estimate for strong radiation support. The very small _photospheric_ ratio must not be used as a universal [normalization](../../../../../normalization.md) for their whole interiors.

Radiation also transfers outward [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md). Comparing radiative [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) $\kappa L/(4\pi r^2c)$ with [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) gives the Eddington [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md)

$$
\boxed{L_{\rm Edd}=\frac{4\pi cGM}{\kappa}.}
$$

Near this scale, radiatively driven winds and envelope instabilities can remove matter or impede further accretion. [Opacity](../../../../../opacity.md), composition, [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md), [mass](../../../../../mass.md) loss and formation history affect the limiting stellar [mass](../../../../../mass.md). The Eddington condition is a luminosity-to-mass constraint, not a unique composition-independent maximum stellar [mass](../../../../../mass.md).
