<h1 id="s18/3/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The zero [trace](../../../../../../matrix-trace.md) and unit [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) imply equal numbers of $+1$ and $-1$ [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md), so $N$ is even. A two-dimensional representation is impossible: all four matrices $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3,\beta$ would be traceless $2\times2$ matrices. Anticommutation gives $\operatorname{Tr}(AB)=0$ for distinct matrices, whereas $\operatorname{Tr}(A^2)=2$. They would therefore be four linearly independent members of the three-dimensional complex space of traceless $2\times2$ matrices, a contradiction.

$$
\boxed{N\text{ is even and }N\ge4.}
$$

The usual irreducible $3+1$ dimensional Dirac representation has $N=4$. Larger representations can contain multiple copies; the requested lower bound is not itself a proof of irreducibility.
