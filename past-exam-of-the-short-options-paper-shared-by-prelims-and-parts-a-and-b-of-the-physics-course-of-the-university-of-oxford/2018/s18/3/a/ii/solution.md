<h1 id="s18/3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $Av=\lambda v$ for $A=\alpha_i$ or $\beta$, then $A^2=I$ implies $\lambda^2=1$. The polynomial $x^2-1$ has distinct roots, so such a matrix is diagonalizable and

$$
\boxed{\lambda=+1\text{ or }-1.}
$$

Anticommutation with another invertible Dirac matrix maps a $+1$ eigenspace to a $-1$ eigenspace, so both signs occur with equal multiplicities in a nontrivial representation. A Hermitian operator is appropriate for the physical [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) and additionally guarantees an [orthonormal](../../../../../../orthonormal-set.md) [eigenbasis](../../../../../../eigenbasis.md).
