<h1 id="s18/3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use $\mathbf p=-i\hbar\nabla$ and regard $e$ as the signed charge in the printed [minimal coupling](../../../../../minimal-coupling.md). Multiplying the first [Dirac equation](../../../../../dirac-equation.md) by $c\gamma^0$ gives $H=c\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol\pi+\beta mc^2+e\Phi$, with $\boldsymbol\pi=\mathbf p-e\mathbf A/c$. Removing the rest-energy [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) yields

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
i\hbar\partial_t\tilde\varphi&=e\Phi\tilde\varphi+c\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\boldsymbol\pi\,\tilde\chi,\\
i\hbar\partial_t\tilde\chi&=c\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\boldsymbol\pi\,\tilde\varphi+(e\Phi-2mc^2)\tilde\chi.
\end{aligned}}
$$

For the alternative equation use its specified field conventions literally. They give

$$
F_{\mu\nu}\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu
=\frac2c\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf E+2i\boldsymbol\Sigma\cdot\mathbf B,
\qquad \boldsymbol\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol\sigma,\boldsymbol\sigma).
$$

Consequently its [Hamiltonian](../../../../../hamiltonian.md) is $c\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf p+\beta mc^2-\kappa\beta\boldsymbol\Sigma\cdot\mathbf B+(i\kappa/c)\beta\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf E$. The two shifted equations are

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
i\hbar\partial_t\tilde\varphi&=-\kappa\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B\,\tilde\varphi
+\left(c\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf p+\frac{i\kappa}{c}\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf E\right)\tilde\chi,\\
i\hbar\partial_t\tilde\chi&=\left(c\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf p-\frac{i\kappa}{c}\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf E\right)\tilde\varphi
+\left(-2mc^2+\kappa\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B\right)\tilde\chi.
\end{aligned}}
$$

These electric-field factors depend on the source's convention $E_i=cF_{0i}$; substituting a different [Gaussian](../../../../../normal-distribution.md) or SI definition without changing the equation would give incorrect [powers](../../../../../power.md) of $c$.
