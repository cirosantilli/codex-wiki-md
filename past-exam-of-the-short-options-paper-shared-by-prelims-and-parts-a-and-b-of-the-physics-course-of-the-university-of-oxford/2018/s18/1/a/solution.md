<h1 id="s18/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume a real, time-independent potential with the same zero [asymptote](../../../../../asymptote.md) on both sides, so $k=\sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$ is the same incident and transmitted wave number. For unit incoming amplitudes,

$$
\psi_L\sim\begin{cases}e^{ikx}+r_Le^{-ikx},&x\to-\infty,\\t_Le^{ikx},&x\to+\infty,\end{cases}
\qquad
\psi_R\sim\begin{cases}t_Re^{-ikx},&x\to-\infty,\\e^{-ikx}+r_Re^{ikx},&x\to+\infty.\end{cases}
$$

Multiplying the two [Schrödinger equations](../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) crosswise and subtracting shows that the [Wronskian](../../../../../wronskian.md) $W=\psi_L\psi_R'-\psi_L'\psi_R$ is constant. Its two asymptotic values are $-2ikt_R$ and $-2ikt_L$, hence $t_L=t_R$.

For a real potential the [probability current](../../../../../probability-current.md) $j=(\hbar/m)\operatorname{Im}(\psi^*\psi')$ is conserved, giving $|r_L|^2+|t_L|^2=|r_R|^2+|t_R|^2=1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{T_L=T_R=|t|^2,\qquad R_L=R_R=1-T.}
$$

The reflection _amplitudes_ can have different [phases](../../../../../phase-waves.md). Reality or [self-adjointness](../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md) is essential for the reflection-probability conclusion: a complex absorbing potential need not have equal left and right reflectances. Equal asymptotic [speeds](../../../../../speed.md) are also used in identifying transmission with the squared amplitude.
