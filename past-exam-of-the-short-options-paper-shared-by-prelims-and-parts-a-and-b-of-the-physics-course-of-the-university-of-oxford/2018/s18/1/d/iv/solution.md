<h1 id="s18/1/d/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For a nonzero everywhere attractive weak potential, $\gamma<0$, the pole has $k=i\kappa$ with $\kappa=m|\gamma|/\hbar^2>0$. The exterior waves decay as $e^{-\kappa|x|}$, giving one shallow [bound state](../../../../../../bound-state.md) to leading weak-coupling order:

$$
\boxed{E_b\simeq-\frac{m}{2\hbar^2}\left(\int U(x)\,dx\right)^2.}
$$

Its size $\kappa^{-1}$ is much larger than the potential range because $\kappa a=O(\lambda)\ll1$. For an everywhere repulsive potential there is no negative-energy [normalizable](../../../../../../normalizable-wavefunction.md) [bound state](../../../../../../bound-state.md): the kinetic and potential [quadratic forms](../../../../../../quadratic-form-split.md) are both nonnegative. Its lower-half-plane pole is a [virtual state](../../../../../../virtual-state.md), not a stable [bound state](../../../../../../bound-state.md). More complicated sign-changing potentials with a vanishing [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md) require higher-order analysis and are not covered by this simple leading result.
