<h1 id="s3/2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) is real and vanishes continuously at both endpoints. Hence

$$
\langle p\rangle=-i\hbar\int\Psi\Psi'\,dx
=-\frac{i\hbar}{2}[\Psi^2]_{-\infty}^{\infty}=0.
$$

The [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) second moment is most safely evaluated from its [quadratic form](../../../../../quadratic-form-split.md):

$$
\langle p^2\rangle=\hbar^2\int|\Psi'|^2dx
=4\hbar^2A^2\int_{-a}^ax^2dx.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\langle p\rangle=0,\qquad\langle p^2\rangle=\frac{5\hbar^2}{2a^2}.}
$$

The [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) jumps where the [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) joins zero. On the whole real line the strong second-derivative operator therefore produces endpoint delta distributions, so the state is not in the usual operator domain of $p^2$. The finite [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) second moment, equivalently the kinetic-energy [quadratic form](../../../../../quadratic-form-split.md) or the [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) of $p^2$ against the [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) [probability density](../../../../../probability-density.md), remains well defined and gives the value above.
