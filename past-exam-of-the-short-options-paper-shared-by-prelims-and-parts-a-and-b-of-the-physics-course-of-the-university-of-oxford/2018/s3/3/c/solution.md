<h1 id="s3/3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

At a separation of $1$ fm, the [proton](../../../../../proton.md) Coulomb [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is approximately $V=e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r)\simeq1.44$ MeV, whereas $k_BT\simeq0.862$ keV at the stated solar-core [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). As a deliberately crude rectangular-barrier estimate, take a width of $1$ fm and use the two-proton [reduced mass](../../../../../reduced-mass.md) $\mu=m_p/2$. Then

$$
2\alpha L\simeq\frac{2(1\,\mathrm{fm})\sqrt{2(469\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2)(1.44\,\mathrm{MeV})}}{197.3\,\mathrm{MeV\,fm}/c}
\simeq0.37,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{P_{\rm rectangular}\sim0.69.}
$$

Treating one [proton](../../../../../proton.md) as fixed and using $m_p$ instead gives about $0.59$. Both estimates only say that this arbitrarily shortened barrier would be fairly transparent; the opaque-barrier exponential is itself uncontrolled for such a small exponent.

There is an important conditioning issue. If the [protons](../../../../../proton.md) have **already** reached a separation of one femtometre, most of the long-range Coulomb suppression has already been overcome. The rectangular estimate cannot represent the probability for thermal [protons](../../../../../proton.md) starting far apart. Their classical turning separation at this [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is about $1.7\times10^3$ fm, not one femtometre.
