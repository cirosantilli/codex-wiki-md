↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [de Broglie hypothesis](../../../../../de-broglie-hypothesis.md) associates a wave [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) with a material particle. Its [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) and [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) set

$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac hp,\qquad \omega=\frac E\hbar,\qquad \mathbf k=\frac{\mathbf p}{\hbar}.}
$$

This is not a literal extended classical lump of matter oscillating at every point. A [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) gives amplitudes for measurements, and a localized particle is represented by a superposition of [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) waves. [Electron](../../../../../electron.md) [diffraction](../../../../../diffraction.md), for example, exhibits [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) at angles set by the same wavelength relation.
