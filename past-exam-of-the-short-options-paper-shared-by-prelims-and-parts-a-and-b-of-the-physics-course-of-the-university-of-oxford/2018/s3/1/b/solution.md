<h1 id="s3/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Using the given one-component equipartition estimate, $p^2=Mk_BT$. Substituting into the de Broglie relation gives

$$
\boxed{\lambda(T)\simeq\frac h{\sqrt{Mk_BT}}.}
$$

Comparison with a confinement [length](../../../../../length.md) or interparticle spacing $d$ gives the criterion $T\lesssim h^2/(Mk_Bd^2)$. Numerical factors depend on the convention: the statistical [thermal de Broglie wavelength](../../../../../thermal-de-broglie-wavelength.md) is usually $h/\sqrt{2\pi Mk_BT}$, while a three-component root-mean-square [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) introduces a factor $\sqrt3$. The question's specified one-component convention is used consistently here. Once degeneracy becomes important, classical equipartition itself ceases to describe the [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) accurately.
