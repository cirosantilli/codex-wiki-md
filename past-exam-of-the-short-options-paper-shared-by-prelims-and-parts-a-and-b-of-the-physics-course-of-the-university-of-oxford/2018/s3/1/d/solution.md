<h1 id="s3/1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At the same [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), momenta increase as the [square root](../../../../../square-root.md) of [mass](../../../../../mass.md), so wavelengths decrease as its inverse [square root](../../../../../square-root.md). For a nucleus of [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $M_N\simeq A m_p$,

$$
\boxed{\frac{\lambda_N}{\lambda_e}=\sqrt{\frac{m_e}{M_N}}
\simeq\frac1{\sqrt{1836A}}.}
$$

At the [electron](../../../../../electron.md) overlap threshold, $\lambda_e=d$, the nuclear wavelength is about $7.0\,\mathrm{pm}/\sqrt A$, far below the $0.3$ nm spacing. Nuclear exchange effects are consequently much less important than [electron](../../../../../electron.md) exchange effects. This statement about nuclear overlap does not mean lattice vibrations are always classical.
