<h1 id="s3/1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

At room [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) require [quantum mechanics](../../../../../quantum-mechanics-split.md), including their occupied orbitals or bands and [Fermi statistics](../../../../../fermi-dirac-statistics.md). Heavy nuclei can often be treated as distinguishable particles near classical equilibrium lattice sites, because their exchange wavelengths are short. Their coupled displacements form [phonons](../../../../../phonon.md), however, and the occupation of a vibrational mode is classical only if $k_BT\gg\hbar\omega$ for that mode. Even at room [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) some high-frequency modes remain quantum mechanical.

At $1$ K, quantum lattice vibrations, [zero-point motion](../../../../../zero-point-motion.md) and the scarcity of thermally excited [phonons](../../../../../phonon.md) are essential. Many heavy nuclei still do not have overlapping exchange [wavefunctions](../../../../../wave-function-split.md); quantum vibration and quantum exchange are distinct criteria. For the same spacing, the nuclear overlap threshold from the rough estimate is $T_N\simeq211/A$ K, illustrating why the nuclear [mass](../../../../../mass.md) cannot be omitted from a blanket low-temperature claim.
