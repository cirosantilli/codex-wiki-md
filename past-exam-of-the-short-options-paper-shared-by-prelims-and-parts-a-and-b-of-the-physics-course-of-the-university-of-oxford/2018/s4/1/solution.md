<h1 id="s4/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The Shockley-Queisser calculation treats a [single-junction solar cell](../../../../single-junction-solar-cell.md) with a single [band gap](../../../../band-gap.md), in steady state, with one collected [electron-hole pair](../../../../electron-hole-pair.md) per absorbed above-gap photon, an ideal absorption edge, thermalized carriers and [radiative recombination](../../../../radiative-recombination.md) as the limiting [recombination](../../../../recombination-cosmology.md) process. The source spectrum, cell [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), angular acceptance and optical concentration must be specified. Below-gap photons are transmitted; [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) above the gap is lost during carrier [thermalization](../../../../thermalization.md); the cell must emit photons by [detailed balance](../../../../detailed-balance.md), producing a [recombination](../../../../recombination-cosmology.md) current and an entropy-related voltage loss. Delivering [power](../../../../power.md) at finite current also puts the operating voltage below the [open-circuit voltage](../../../../open-circuit-voltage.md). [Nonradiative recombination](../../../../nonradiative-recombination.md), resistance and imperfect collection worsen a real cell but are not required for the ideal limit.

Three ways to change those assumptions are [tandem solar cells](../../../../tandem-solar-cell.md), which divide the spectrum among gaps and reduce both transmission and [thermalization](../../../../thermalization.md) losses; [hot-carrier solar cells](../../../../hot-carrier-solar-cell.md), which aims to collect excess carrier [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) before [thermalization](../../../../thermalization.md); and [carrier multiplication](../../../../carrier-multiplication.md) or spectral conversion, which aims to use a high-energy photon for more than one excitation or combine otherwise unusable sub-gap photons. These concepts have additional practical losses and do not automatically outperform an optimized conventional device. The usual unconcentrated single-junction optimum is approximately one third under a terrestrial solar spectrum, rather than a universal efficiency independent of illumination.

For the geometrical estimate, take the stated horizontal equinox irradiance to set a constant direct-normal reference. The equinox solar-noon elevation is $\alpha_e=90^\circ-51.75^\circ=38.25^\circ$, so this reference is $I_n=1/\sin\alpha_e\simeq1.62\,\mathrm{kW\,m^{-2}}$. A south-facing panel with tilt $\beta=41.1^\circ$ has noon incidence factor $\cos(51.75^\circ-\delta-41.1^\circ)$, where $\delta=\pm23.44^\circ$. Relative to the $1\,\mathrm{kW\,m^{-2}}$ plane-of-array rating,

$$
\boxed{P_{\rm June}\simeq5\frac{\cos12.79^\circ}{\sin38.25^\circ}=7.9\,\mathrm{kW},\qquad
P_{\rm December}\simeq5\frac{\cos34.09^\circ}{\sin38.25^\circ}=6.7\,\mathrm{kW}.}
$$

These are the results of that **idealized geometrical [normalization](../../../../normalization.md)**, not credible seasonal production forecasts. Its inferred direct-normal irradiance already exceeds the approximately $1.36\,\mathrm{kW\,m^{-2}}$ [solar irradiance](../../../../solar-irradiance.md) outside the atmosphere, so the supplied horizontal benchmark cannot describe a purely direct noon beam literally. Real direct and diffuse fractions, atmospheric attenuation at low winter elevation, cell [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and inverter clipping are unspecified. An alternative assumption of $1\,\mathrm{kW\,m^{-2}}$ direct-normal irradiance gives approximately $4.88$ and $4.14$ kW. A physically unique maximum cannot be inferred without an irradiance model.

The stated annual output corresponds to $4100/5=820$ full-rated hours, an average [power](../../../../power.md) of $0.468$ kW and a [capacity factor](../../../../capacity-factor.md) of $9.4\%$. Night, cloud, seasonal day [length](../../../../length.md) and other losses explain the large difference from an instantaneous sunlit estimate.

For a national scale estimate, take UK area $2.44\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, population $6.6\times10^7$, and annual mean solar input $100\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. Covering five percent with twenty-percent-efficient modules gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{E}{N\,\mathrm{day}}\sim
\frac{0.05(2.44\times10^{11})(0.20)(100)(24)}{1000(6.6\times10^7)}
\simeq89\,\mathrm{kWh\,person^{-1}\,day^{-1}}.}
$$

This is comparable with an order-$100$ kWh daily per-person primary-energy budget, and much larger than electricity use alone, of order $10$-$15$ kWh per person per day. It is a gross area-and-flux estimate: access land, shading, seasonal storage, curtailment and conversion between [primary energy](../../../../primary-energy.md) and electrical end use all affect its interpretation.
