<h1 id="s7/3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

With the apsidal origin chosen as specified, $G=0$. For $p_\phi\ne0$, rearrangement gives

$$
p_r=\left(\frac{p_\phi}{r}-\frac{mk}{p_\phi}\right)\tan\phi.
$$

Let $u=1/r$. Since $du/d\phi=-p_r/p_\phi$, the equation is $u'=-(u-mk/p_\phi^2)\tan\phi$. Its solution, continued through points where the intermediate division fails, is

$$
\boxed{\frac1r=\frac{mk}{p_\phi^2}+e\cos\phi.}
$$

As printed, $e$ has units of inverse [length](../../../../../length.md) and is not the usual dimensionless [eccentricity](../../../../../eccentricity-of-a-conic.md). Writing $\ell=p_\phi^2/(mk)$ gives the standard form $r=\ell/(1+\varepsilon\cos\phi)$ with $\varepsilon=e\ell$. The orbit is an [ellipse](../../../../../ellipse.md) for $|\varepsilon|<1$, parabolic for unit magnitude and hyperbolic for magnitude greater than one on its allowed angular interval. The purely radial case $p_\phi=0$ must be handled separately and is excluded by these divisions.
