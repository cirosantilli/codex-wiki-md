<h1 id="s7/3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

In the equatorial plane,

$$
\dot r=p_r/m,\quad\dot\phi=p_\phi/(mr^2),\quad
\dot p_r=p_\phi^2/(mr^3)-k/r^2,\quad\dot p_\phi=0.
$$

Differentiating the given combination produces a cosine term

$$
-\frac{p_\phi}{m}\left(\frac{p_\phi^2}{mr^3}-\frac{k}{r^2}\right)\cos\phi
+\left(\frac{p_\phi^2}{mr}-k\right)\frac{p_\phi}{mr^2}\cos\phi=0
$$

and a [sine](../../../../../sine.md) term $p_rp_\phi\dot\phi\sin\phi/m-p_\phi^2\dot r\sin\phi/(mr^2)=0$. Thus $\boxed{\dot G=0}$. The expression is a Cartesian component of the [Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector](../../../../../laplace-runge-lenz-vector.md) with a conventional overall [normalization](../../../../../normalization.md), reflecting the additional [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) of the inverse-square-force problem.
