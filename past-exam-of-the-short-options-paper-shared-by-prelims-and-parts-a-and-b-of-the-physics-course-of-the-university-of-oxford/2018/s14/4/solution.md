<h1 id="s14/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

[Energy](../../../../energy-split.md) conservation is a strong example of convergent discovery in the 1840s, but only if “simultaneous” means overlapping contributions rather than identical statements made at the same instant. Different researchers reached related conclusions from physiology, engineering, electricity and mechanics, with different evidence and terminology.

[Julius Robert Mayer](../../../../julius-von-mayer.md) argued in 1842 for an indestructible, interconvertible agency underlying natural processes and related mechanical work to [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) using thermal properties of [gases](../../../../gas.md). His physiological motivations and theoretical reasoning differed from a direct precision measurement of a mechanical equivalent. [James Prescott Joule](../../../../james-prescott-joule.md) investigated electrical heating and mechanical production of [heat](../../../../heat-split.md), including fluid-friction experiments. By comparing measured work with measured [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) rises, he supplied increasingly persuasive numerical evidence that a reproducible quantity of work corresponded to a quantity of [heat](../../../../heat-split.md). His later paper describes controls on thermometers and different working fluids, showing that the result depended on experimental metrology, not simply the observation that friction warms things.[description of calibrated thermometers and fluid-friction comparisons](../../../../on-the-mechanical-equivalent-of-heat-by-james-prescott-joule.md)

[Ludvig Colding](../../../../ludwig-a-colding.md) pursued related mechanical and thermal ideas and experiments in the same decade. [Hermann von Helmholtz](../../../../hermann-von-helmholtz.md)'s 1847 synthesis connected the impossibility of perpetual motion with the interrelation of mechanical, thermal, electrical and physiological processes. These contributions shared a broad conservation programme without having identical conceptual foundations. A modern historian must also distinguish an invariant quantity, its numerical equivalence across processes, and the general claim that the conservation applies to all physical processes.[research abstract comparing the contemporaneous contributions; accompanying translation of Mayer's 1842 paper](../../../../mayer-and-energy-conservation-by-roberto-de-andrade-martins.md)

The convergence was historically intelligible. Engines made the relation of [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) to work economically important; electrical experiments revealed conversions between previously separate domains; physiology raised questions about animal [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) and mechanical effort. Measurement practices and interrelated scientific communities supplied common problems. Earlier work on frictional heating and mechanical conservation provided resources, while the caloric account of [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) and uncertainty about reversible [heat engines](../../../../heat-engine.md) remained obstacles.

The differences also limit a simple multiple-discovery story. Recognition was uneven, communication changed later formulations, and priority disputes retrospectively compressed a developing research programme into a single discovery. The first law's mature formulation required further clarification of internal [energy](../../../../energy-split.md), [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) and work. [Energy](../../../../energy-split.md) conservation is therefore simultaneously an example of independent convergence, cumulative experimental confirmation and later conceptual unification; assigning the entire achievement to one person or saying everyone discovered precisely the same law would conceal that process.
