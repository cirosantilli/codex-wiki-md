<h1 id="s12/2/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A [base pair](../../../../../base-pair.md) has four possible sequence identities, hence two bits, not four bits from treating complementary strands as independent. B-DNA has diameter approximately 2 nm, axial rise 0.34 nm per [base pair](../../../../../base-pair.md) and about ten [base pairs](../../../../../base-pair.md) per helical turn. The tightly packed-cylinder upper [density](../../../../../density.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{2}{\pi(1\ \mathrm{nm})^2(0.34\ \mathrm{nm})}\simeq1.9\times10^{27}\ \mathrm{bits\,m^{-3}}.}
$$

A haploid human genome has about $6\times10^9$ raw bits; in a nucleus of several hundred cubic micrometres its bulk [density](../../../../../density.md) is order $10^{25}$ bits m$^{-3}$, far lower. Cellular [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) must allow hydration, packaging, regulatory access and copying/repair machinery, and cannot fill a cell as parallel bare cylinders. Diploidy and repeated or constrained sequence also reduce _independent functional information_ compared with the raw four-letter capacity; those concepts should not be confused with geometric storage [density](../../../../../density.md).

The original molecular figure in question 3 shows the approximate helical dimensions.
