<h1 id="s19/1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A uniform nonrelativistic [electron](../../../../../electron.md) column has radial [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) $E_r=-n_ee r/(2\epsilon_0)$ inside its [radius](../../../../../radius.md). A relativistic [proton](../../../../../proton.md) therefore has focusing strength $K=e^2n_e/(2\epsilon_0pv)$ per unit length. Electron-current magnetic effects are small under the stated nonrelativistic assumption. To change the nominal doublet strength $1/L^*$ by $10^{-4}$ requires

$$
\boxed{n_eL_e=\frac{2\epsilon_0pv}{e^2}\frac{10^{-4}}{L^*}\simeq1.1\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}}.}
$$

One illustrative choice is a one-metre lens with $n_e\simeq1.1\times10^{16}$ m$^{-3}$; other density-length combinations [work](../../../../../work.md). The [electron](../../../../../electron.md) beam must cover the [proton](../../../../../proton.md) beam and have a sufficiently uniform profile. Its placement changes the detailed transfer-map correction; the estimate treats the required additional integrated focusing at the entrance and does not claim a uniquely specified electron-lens engineering design.
