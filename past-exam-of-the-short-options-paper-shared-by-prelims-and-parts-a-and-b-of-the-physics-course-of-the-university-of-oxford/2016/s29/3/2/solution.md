<h1 id="s29/3/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Rotation rate, oblateness and measured external gravitational multipoles provide constraints beyond [mass](../../../../../mass.md) and [radius](../../../../../radius.md); spin-axis precession/nutation and satellite dynamics can add moment-related information. For a giant planet the deep rotation rate and [hydrostatic approximation](../../../../../hydrostatic-approximation.md) matter. These measurements require an interior model or supplementary constraints for an accurate moment: a [spherically symmetric](../../../../../spherical-symmetry.md) body's exterior monopole alone cannot reveal its radial [density](../../../../../density.md) distribution. An exact moment cannot generally be inferred just by labeling a measured $J_2$ as $C$.
