<h1 id="s29/3/5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

[Helium](../../../../../helium.md) becoming partially immiscible in dense [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) can form helium-rich droplets that settle inward. [Gravitational energy](../../../../../gravitational-energy.md) release and associated thermal evolution can supply extra [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md), while the upper [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) envelope becomes [helium](../../../../../helium.md) depleted. Different interior temperature-pressure histories can make separation stronger in Saturn than in Jupiter.

**The source's ratio is reversed for this intended explanation.** Depleting [helium](../../../../../helium.md) lowers He/H and raises H/He. For a concrete example, keeping ten [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) units while reducing one [helium](../../../../../helium.md) unit to one half changes H/He from ten to twenty, not to a lower value. Thus the literal statement that Saturn has _lower H/He_ cannot simultaneously be used as evidence for the stated upper-envelope helium-depletion mechanism. If the intended comparison is lower He/H, the observations and extra [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) are mutually consistent with [helium](../../../../../helium.md) rain. Composition measurements and interior modeling are still needed; excess [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) alone does not uniquely prove that process.[Background on Saturn: helium-to-molecular-hydrogen ratio and helium migration as a heat source](../../../../../cassini-mission-press-kit-by-nasa.md)
