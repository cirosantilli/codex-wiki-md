<h1 id="s29/2/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

At fixed projectile [mass](../../../../../mass.md) and the assumed common quarter-power law, changing diameter from 30 to 75 m increases [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) by $(75/30)^4$ and [speed](../../../../../speed.md) by $(75/30)^2=6.25$. The corresponding primary impact [speed](../../../../../speed.md) is about 15.6 km s$^{-1}$. Five percent transferred to secondary-forming ejecta gives $v_{ej}\simeq781$ m s$^{-1}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{r_{\max}\simeq\frac{781^2}{1.622}\simeq3.76\times10^5\ \mathrm m\approx380\ \mathrm{km}.}
$$

This assumes a 45-degree launch and similar target properties/coupling, as well as the source's fixed [mass](../../../../../mass.md) and velocity-transfer rules. Such a range is a noticeable fraction of the lunar [radius](../../../../../radius.md), so [curvature](../../../../../curvature.md) would produce a correction; the supplied flat-surface formula is an estimate rather than exact lunar [orbit](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) dynamics.
