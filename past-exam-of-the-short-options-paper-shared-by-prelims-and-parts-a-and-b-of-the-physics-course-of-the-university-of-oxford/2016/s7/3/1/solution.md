<h1 id="s7/3/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Vary $S=\int L(q,\dot q,t)dt$ with fixed endpoints. [Integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) gives $\delta S=\int\sum_i[L_{q_i}-dL_{\dot q_i}/dt]\delta q_i\,dt$, hence the [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md). Define $p_i=L_{\dot q_i}$ and, assuming an invertible velocity-momentum relation, $H=p\cdot\dot q-L$. The equivalent action is $\int(p\cdot\dot q-H)dt$. Independent variations of $p$ and $q$ yield

$$
\boxed{\dot q_i=H_{p_i},\qquad\dot p_i=-H_{q_i}.}
$$

[Boundary terms](../../../../../boundary-term.md) vanish for fixed endpoint coordinates; constraining [momenta](../../../../../momentum-split.md) independently at the same endpoints is unnecessary. Singular [Lagrangians](../../../../../lagrangian.md) require constraint analysis instead of this unconstrained [Legendre transform](../../../../../convex-conjugate.md).
