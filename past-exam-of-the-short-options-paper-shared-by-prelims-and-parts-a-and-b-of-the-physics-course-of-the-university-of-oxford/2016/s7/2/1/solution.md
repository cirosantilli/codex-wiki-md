<h1 id="s7/2/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

A [constant of motion](../../../../../conserved-quantity.md) has zero [total derivative](../../../../../total-derivative.md) on every solution, and may explicitly depend on time. For an infinitesimal transformation $\delta q_i=\epsilon\xi_i$ with $\delta L=\epsilon dF/dt$, the on-shell [first variation](../../../../../first-variation.md) is $\delta L=\epsilon d(\sum_i p_i\xi_i)/dt$. Therefore $\boxed{I=\sum_i p_i\xi_i-F}$ is conserved. Including a time shift $\delta t=\epsilon\tau$ gives the corresponding $I=\sum_i p_i\xi_i-H\tau-F$. This proves [Noether's theorem](../../../../../noether-conserved-quantity-for-a-mechanical-point-symmetry.md) in its usual point-transformation form. A [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) of the action up to a [boundary term](../../../../../boundary-term.md) is sufficient; exact invariance of the displayed [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md) is stronger than necessary. Discrete transformations by themselves do not give this continuous-charge construction.
