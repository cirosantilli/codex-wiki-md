<h1 id="s7/2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For equal masses $m$, the newly conserved relative $y$ [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is

$$
\boxed{E_{y,\mathrm{rel}}=\sum_i\frac{p_{y_i}^2}{2m}-\frac{(\sum_i p_{y_i})^2}{6m}+U_{12}(y_1-y_2)+U_{13}(y_1-y_3).}
$$

It is [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) relative to the free $y$ [center of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md) plus the two interactions. Adding its constant center [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) and the already conserved $x,z$ energies gives the total [Hamiltonian](../../../../../hamiltonian.md). The equal-mass oscillator rotations simplify to $J_{ij}=x_i p_{x_j}-x_jp_{x_i}$, but are not new: for unequal masses their conserved predecessors contain the mass-weight factors. As a concrete check, independent transformations of $X_i=\sqrt{m_i}x_i$ rotate an isotropic oscillator at _any_ positive masses. Complete [mass](../../../../../mass.md) equality does not magically make generic $U_{12}(y_1-y_2)+U_{13}(y_1-y_3)+V(z_1-z_2)$ invariant under rotations of physical space.
