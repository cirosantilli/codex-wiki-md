<h1 id="s4/2/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

A Pelton wheel splits and turns a free jet through nearly 180 degrees in moving buckets, transferring [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) to the runner. For ideal bucket [speed](../../../../../speed.md) $V$, the absolute exit [speed](../../../../../speed.md) is $2V-u$ and [force](../../../../../force-split.md) is $2\rho Q(u-V)$. Power $2\rho QV(u-V)$ is maximized at $V=u/2$, giving $P_{\max}=\rho Qu^2/2$. If the same inflow must now pass a slit of height $d$, continuity gives $u=Q/d$. Using the preceding flow rate with $H=y_{\min}$,

$$
\boxed{P_{\max}=\frac{\rho Q^3}{2d^2}=\frac{\rho g^{3/2}}{2d^2}\left(\frac23y_{\min}\right)^{9/2}.}
$$

The head must actually be sufficient to supply this jet [speed](../../../../../speed.md): $u^2/(2g)$ cannot exceed the available hydraulic drop. An arbitrarily small slit cannot produce infinite power from a fixed-height reservoir; it would reduce discharge or raise water until another outlet, including overtopping, operates. The printed scaling presupposes compatible head and fixed-flow conditions.
