<h1 id="s4/3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a generous illustrative roof area 100 m$^2$, annual rain depth one metre and five-metre usable fall, maximum [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) is $\rho A h_{\mathrm{rain}}gH\simeq4.9$ MJ, only 1.36 kWh per year before conversion losses. Even 25 million roofs give only about 34 GWh annually, around $10^{-4}$ of a 300 TWh electricity system. Tiny intermittent flows and installation embodied [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) make the practical result worse. Household roof-rain generation is therefore a very small carbon-saving resource under realistic domestic heights; the small gravitational drop and water flux explain why, without needing to assume an inefficient turbine.
