<h1 id="s26/3/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The 21.1 cm line is neutral [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md)'s hyperfine spin-flip transition, observed with [radio telescopes](../../../../../radio-telescope.md) and interferometers in the cool/warm neutral gas disk, often beyond the bright stellar disk. The mean wavelength gives $z=22.0/21.1-1\simeq0.0427$ and, using the stated low-redshift Hubble approximation, $d\simeq180$ Mpc. A 16-arcsecond angular [radius](../../../../../radius.md) gives $R\simeq14$ kpc. For a thin circular disk, $\cos i=8/32$, so $i\simeq75.5^\circ$ from face-on.

The original [spectrum](../../../../../frequency-spectrum.md) reaches roughly 21.98 and 22.02 cm: $v_{\mathrm{los,max}}\simeq c(0.02/22.0)\simeq273$ km s$^{-1}$. Deprojecting gives $\boxed{v_{\max}\simeq282\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}}$. A spherical dynamical estimate $M(<R)\simeq v^2R/G$ gives $\boxed{M\sim2.6\times10^{11}M_\odot}$. A thin massive disk is not spherical: its exact rotation-to-total-mass coefficient depends on radial [mass](../../../../../mass.md) profile. The no-exterior-matter assumption does not uniquely fix that coefficient, so the quoted result is the conventional order-of-magnitude dynamical estimate. Cosmological [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) and angular-diameter distances differ at this redshift; the source's simple Hubble-distance convention is being used consistently.
