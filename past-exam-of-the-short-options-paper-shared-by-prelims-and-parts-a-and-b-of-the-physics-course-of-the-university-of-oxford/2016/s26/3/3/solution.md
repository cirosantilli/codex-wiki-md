↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The low-redshift distance gives modulus about 36.28, so $M_V\simeq-20.28$ and $\boxed{L_V\simeq10^{10}L_\odot}$. A stellar [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) of order one to a few would imply only $10^{10}$–several $10^{10}M_\odot$ in stars, below the dynamical estimate. Gas and a dark component can supply additional [mass](../../../../../mass.md); the precise stellar estimate requires a population-dependent [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) and extinction correction.

An exponential luminous disk has most of its [mass](../../../../../mass.md) within a few scale lengths. Outside that concentration a mass-traces-light model tends toward a declining, approximately Keplerian [speed](../../../../../speed.md), not an extended flat curve. Adding a spatially extended [mass distribution](../../../../../mass-distribution.md) explains the mismatch more naturally than merely rescaling the same concentrated light profile.
