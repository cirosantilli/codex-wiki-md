↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

An isochrone is the locus of stars of a common age and composition over a range of initial masses. A real cluster diagram approximates it, with scatter from binaries, [measurement errors](../../../../../measurement-error.md), composition spread and differential reddening. Region A is the hydrogen-core-burning [main sequence](../../../../../main-sequence.md). Region B contains evolved subgiant/red-giant stars, typically with hydrogen-shell burning around an increasingly helium-rich core. Region C is the [horizontal branch](../../../../../horizontal-branch.md), occupied by core-helium-burning stars; RR Lyrae lie in its [instability strip](../../../../../instability-strip.md).

Point D is the [main-sequence turnoff](../../../../../main-sequence-turnoff.md): core [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) exhaustion drives structural readjustment toward shell burning and expansion. Point E is the tip of the red-giant branch, where a low-mass star ignites [helium](../../../../../helium.md) in its degenerate core, the [helium flash](../../../../../helium-flash.md), and subsequently moves toward horizontal-branch conditions. The two labeled events are different evolutionary transitions, not merely changes in apparent color from dust.
