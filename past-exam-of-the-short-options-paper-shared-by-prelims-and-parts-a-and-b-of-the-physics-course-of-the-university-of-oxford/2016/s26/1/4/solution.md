<h1 id="s26/1/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

At D, $M_V=20.3-15.09=5.21$. Ignoring differential [bolometric corrections](../../../../../bolometric-correction.md), $L/L_\odot\simeq10^{-0.4(5.21-4.74)}\simeq0.65$, and $L\propto M^4$ gives $\boxed{M_D\simeq0.90M_\odot}$. If accessible fuel fraction is similar, lifetime scales as $M/L\propto M^{-3}$. Normalizing to a solar [main-sequence lifetime](../../../../../main-sequence-lifetime.md) about 10 Gyr gives an age near $\boxed{14\ \mathrm{Gyr}}$.

This is much older than the Sun's current approximately 4.6 Gyr and close to the cosmological age scale. The approximate estimate is not evidence for a cluster older than the Universe: [bolometric corrections](../../../../../bolometric-correction.md), abundance, accessible [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) fraction and the crude [mass-luminosity](../../../../../mass-luminosity-relation.md) law limit precision. The stated 26.7 MeV conversion and an assumed core fuel fraction can supply the order-of-magnitude solar lifetime; they do not by themselves specify the burned fraction or composition precisely.
