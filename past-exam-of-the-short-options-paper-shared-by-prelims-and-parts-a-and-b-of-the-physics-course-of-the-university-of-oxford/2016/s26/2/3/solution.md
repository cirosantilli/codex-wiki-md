<h1 id="s26/2/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Degenerate [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) conduct [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) efficiently, making most of the interior nearly isothermal while an outer nondegenerate insulating envelope sustains a large [temperature gradient](../../../../../temperature-gradient.md). Nuclei have much larger masses and much smaller [thermal de Broglie wavelengths](../../../../../thermal-de-broglie-wavelength.md) than [electrons](../../../../../electron.md), so their [quantum degeneracy](../../../../../degenerate-energy-levels.md) is weak at these [temperatures](../../../../../temperature-split.md)/densities, although [Coulomb coupling](../../../../../coulomb-coupling.md) and crystallization can matter. Under the stipulated classical ideal-carbon-ion approximation,

$$
\boxed{U=\frac32\frac{M}{12m_u}k_BT_c=\frac{Mk_BT_c}{8m_u}.}
$$

This excludes crystallization, [latent heat](../../../../../latent-heat.md) and low-temperature heat-capacity changes; nondegenerate does not automatically mean weakly interacting.
