<h1 id="s26/2/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

A [white dwarf](../../../../../white-dwarf.md) is a compact [stellar remnant](../../../../../stellar-remnant.md) supported primarily by [electron degeneracy pressure](../../../../../electron-degeneracy-pressure.md), radiating stored [thermal energy](../../../../../thermal-energy.md) rather than sustained [main-sequence](../../../../../main-sequence.md) fusion. At zero-temperature leading order, filling [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) states gives $n_e=p_F^3/(3\pi^2\hbar^3)$ and $P=p_F^5/(15\pi^2\hbar^3m_e)$. Fully ionized [hydrogen mass fraction](../../../../../hydrogen-mass-fraction.md) $X$, with other nuclei approximately one [electron](../../../../../electron.md) per two nucleons, implies $n_e=(1+X)\rho/(2m_u)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P=K_1(1+X)^{5/3}\rho^{5/3},\quad K_1=\frac{\hbar^2(3\pi^2)^{2/3}}{5m_e(2m_u)^{5/3}}.}
$$

This is an $n=3/2$ polytrope. Solving hydrostatic structure with a zero-pressure surface gives $R=2.357K_1(1+X)^{5/3}M^{-1/3}/G$; the numerical factor uses $\xi_1=3.65375$ and $-\xi_1^2\theta'(\xi_1)=2.71406$ of that Lane-Emden solution. Thus $R\propto M^{-1/3}$. For $X=0$, $M=M_\odot$, $R$ is about $9\times10^3$ km. A uniform-density scaling gives the same [mass](../../../../../mass.md) dependence but a different order-one coefficient and should be identified as such.

Independently, $R/R_\odot=\sqrt{0.056}(T_\odot/25000)^2\simeq0.0126$, or about $8.8\times10^3$ km. Agreement is good at this level; relativistic degeneracy becomes relevant for accurate modeling near one [solar mass](../../../../../solar-mass.md) and eventually invalidates an unlimited nonrelativistic mass-radius law.
