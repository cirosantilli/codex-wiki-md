<h1 id="s1/1/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

On the square, $|z|\geq N$. The identity $|\sin\pi(x+\alpha+iy)|^2=\sin^2\pi(x+\alpha)+\sinh^2\pi y$ bounds the sine away from zero on the vertical sides because $x=\pm N$; on horizontal sides it grows exponentially. Therefore the whole [contour integral](../../../../../contour-integral.md) is $O(N^{-1})$ or smaller and vanishes. The [residue theorem](../../../../../residue-theorem.md) then gives

$$
0=2\pi i\left[-\pi\csc\pi\alpha\cot\pi\alpha+\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\frac{(-1)^n}{\pi(n-\alpha)^2}\right],
$$

which proves $\boxed{\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^n/(n-\alpha)^2=\pi^2\csc\pi\alpha\cot\pi\alpha}$. [Absolute convergence](../../../../../absolute-convergence.md) justifies the limiting [residue](../../../../../residue.md) sum.
