<h1 id="s1/3/b/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Cauchy's theorem says that a [holomorphic function](../../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) integrates to zero around a closed [contour](../../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) homologous to zero in its domain. Thus deforming one [contour](../../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) into another without crossing a singularity preserves the [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md). More generally for a [meromorphic](../../../../../../meromorphic-function.md) $f$, the exact condition is

$$
\boxed{\sum_a[n_\Gamma(a)-n_{\Gamma'}(a)]\operatorname{Res}_a f=0.}
$$

Equal [winding numbers](../../../../../../winding-number.md) about every singularity are sufficient. They are not necessary for one particular function because [residues](../../../../../../residue.md) can cancel; even arbitrary [contours](../../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) have equal [integrals](../../../../../../integral-split.md) for $f=0$. A topological deformation criterion must not be confused with a necessary condition for accidental equality.
