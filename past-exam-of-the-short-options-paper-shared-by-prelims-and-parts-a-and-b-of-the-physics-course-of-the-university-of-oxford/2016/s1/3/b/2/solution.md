<h1 id="s1/3/b/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The finite singularities are square-root [branch points](../../../../../../branch-point.md) at $z=\pm1$. Choose the cut between them and the branch $f(z)\sim z$ at infinity. The two banks then have values $+i\sqrt{1-x^2}$ above and $-i\sqrt{1-x^2}$ below. A counterclockwise loop around the cut traverses its upper bank right-to-left, so its [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md) is $-2iI$, where $I$ is the required real [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md). At large [radius](../../../../../../radius.md),

$$
f(z)=z-\frac1{2z}+O(z^{-3}),\qquad\oint f(z)dz=-\pi i.
$$

Therefore $\boxed{I=\pi/2}$. Infinity is not another square-root [branch point](../../../../../../branch-point.md): this branch has an ordinary [pole](../../../../../../pole.md) there on its sheet. Small detours about the finite endpoints vanish as their [radius](../../../../../../radius.md) to power $3/2$.
