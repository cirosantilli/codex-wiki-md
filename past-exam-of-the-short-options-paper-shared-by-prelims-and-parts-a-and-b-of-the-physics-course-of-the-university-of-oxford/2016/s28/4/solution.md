<h1 id="s28/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For local dichotomic outcomes define $A(a,\lambda),B(b,\lambda)=\pm1$ and a setting-independent normalized distribution $\rho(\lambda)$. At each $\lambda$,

$$
A(a)[B(b)+B(b')]+A(a')[B(b)-B(b')]=\pm2,
$$

because exactly one bracket vanishes and the other is $\pm2$. Integrating and using the [triangle inequality](../../../../triangle-inequality.md) yields

$$
\boxed{|E(a,b)+E(a,b')+E(a',b)-E(a',b')|\leq2.}
$$

For stochastic local response functions the same bound follows by replacing outcomes with [conditional means](../../../../conditional-expectation.md) in $[-1,1]$, or by including local randomness in $\lambda$. The important locality condition is factorization of joint conditional outcome probabilities, together with independence of the setting distribution and $\lambda$.

The [spin singlet](../../../../spin-singlet-state.md) instead predicts $E(a,b)=-a\cdot b$. Two orthogonal directions at one wing and directions halfway between them at the other give $|S|=2\sqrt2$. Therefore reproducing the quantum predictions requires giving up at least one of the stated premises. In the perfect-anticorrelation version, $B(a,\lambda)=-A(a,\lambda)$ gives Bell's three-setting form $|E(a,b)-E(a,c)|\leq1+E(b,c)$ by the same algebra.

This does not establish that all deterministic or hidden-variable theories are impossible: nonlocal guidance is a counterexample to that reading. Nor does it equate Bell locality with no-signalling; averaging quantum outcomes can preserve setting-independent local marginals despite nonfactorizing correlations. The theorem is significant because it turns a local-completeness dispute into incompatible quantitative correlation bounds, not because it refutes every possible meaning of physical reality.[1964, assumptions and three-setting inequality](../../../../on-the-einstein-podolsky-rosen-paradox-by-john-stewart-bell.md)
