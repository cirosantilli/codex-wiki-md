<h1 id="s16/2/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Room [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) corresponds to about 0.026 eV, or $2.6\times10^{-5}$ keV. Read the left-hand branch there and draw a horizontal line to the rising hot branch. The original graph gives a return [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) of order $\boxed{k_BT'\sim1\text{–}2\ \mathrm{keV}}$, with graph-reading uncertainty, not the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) of minimum conductivity.

At full ionization $n_e=13\rho/(27m_u)\simeq7.8\times10^{29}$ m$^{-3}$. Inserting the inferred [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) into $N_D=(4\pi/3)n_e[\epsilon_0k_BT'/(n_ee^2)]^{3/2}$ gives a Debye-sphere occupancy of order $60$–$170$. This is much greater than one and supports a collective weak-coupling description more than the conductivity-minimum region does. The approximate collision-time formula and omitted [Coulomb logarithm](../../../../../coulomb-logarithm.md) do not warrant forcing the entire plotted conductivity curve to have its exact prefactor.
