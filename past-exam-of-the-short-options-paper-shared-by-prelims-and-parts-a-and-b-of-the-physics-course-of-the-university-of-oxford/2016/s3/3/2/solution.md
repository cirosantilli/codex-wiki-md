<h1 id="s3/3/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

A classically accelerating Coulomb [electron](../../../../../electron.md) radiates and loses [orbital energy](../../../../../orbital-energy.md), so a stable nonradiating [orbit](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) is impossible within that unmodified theory. Bohr instead postulates stationary nonradiating [orbits](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md), quantized [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) $\mu vr=n\hbar$ and [photon emission](../../../../../photon-emission.md)/absorption with $h\nu=E_i-E_f$. Here $\mu=m_eM/(m_e+M)$ describes [relative motion](../../../../../relative-motion.md). The angular-momentum condition is the standing-wave relation $\boxed{2\pi r=n\lambda_{\mathrm{dB}}}$ for the relative [orbit](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md). These are additional quantum postulates, not consequences of classical radiation theory.
