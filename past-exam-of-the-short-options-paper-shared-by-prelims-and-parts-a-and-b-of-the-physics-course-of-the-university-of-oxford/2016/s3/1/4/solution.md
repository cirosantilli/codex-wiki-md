<h1 id="s3/1/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For an aligned narrower slit placed immediately after the first, uniform illumination gives [transmission probability](../../../../../transmission-coefficient.md) $\boxed{d/w}$. Conditional on passage the same normalized formula applies with $w$ replaced by $d$ and central-lobe [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) scale $h/d$. Position rms is $d/\sqrt{12}$, while the exact hard-edge rms $\Delta p_x$ is infinite. Thus the source's finite “uncertainty” can only mean a characteristic diffraction width; the rigorous Heisenberg inequality is not violated, and smoothing the edges restores finite rms moments satisfying it.

A 10 keV X-ray has $\lambda\simeq0.124$ nm. The ideal position-filter [probability](../../../../../probability.md) and [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) formula at fixed aperture widths are unchanged, since the [Fourier transform](../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) involves $h$ and the aperture geometry. Its larger total [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) makes the angular diffraction width smaller, approximately $\lambda/d$. Real slit-material transmission and absorption are additional experimental issues.
