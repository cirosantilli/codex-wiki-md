<h1 id="s3/2/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

For a uniformly illuminated [single slit](../../../../../single-slit.md) of width $w$, the far-field amplitude is proportional to $\sin u/u$, with $u=\pi w\sin\vartheta/\lambda$. Its first zeros occur at $\sin\vartheta=\pm\lambda/w$. If the quoted peak width is the full width between those zeros, the small-angle geometry gives

$$
\Delta y\simeq\frac{2L\lambda}{w},\qquad\boxed{w\simeq\frac{2L\lambda}{\Delta y}\simeq2.3\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm m.}
$$

The [Fraunhofer diffraction](../../../../../fraunhofer-diffraction.md) approximation is appropriate since $w^2/(\lambda L)\ll1$. If the supplied width instead meant a half-width or full width at half maximum, its numerical conversion factor would differ; the displayed answer states the first-zero convention explicitly.
