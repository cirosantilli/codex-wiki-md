<h1 id="s3/1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A stationary evanescent wave does not assign a classical trajectory or a real local speed inside the forbidden region. The [dwell time in a semi-infinite potential step](../../../../../dwell-time-in-a-semi-infinite-potential-step.md) measures probability stored in $x>0$ divided by incident [probability current](../../../../../probability-current.md). For incident amplitude one,

$$
\tau_D=\frac{\int_0^\infty|c|^2e^{-2\kappa x}dx}{\hbar k/m}
=\boxed{\frac{2mk}{\hbar\kappa(k^2+\kappa^2)}}.
$$

This gives a time in terms of $m,E,V_0$ through the definitions of $k,\kappa$. A simpler dimensional estimate is the penetration length divided by the incident speed, $\Delta x/v_{\rm in}=m/(\hbar k\kappa)=\hbar/[2\sqrt{E(V_0-E)}]$. The dwell time equals $(2E/V_0)$ times that estimate. Different operational timing definitions need not agree; treating the imaginary under-barrier momentum as a real particle velocity would not justify a unique traversal time.
