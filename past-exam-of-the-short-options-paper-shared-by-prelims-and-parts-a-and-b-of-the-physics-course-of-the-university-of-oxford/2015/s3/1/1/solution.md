<h1 id="s3/1/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The one-dimensional [Time-dependent Schrödinger equation](../../../../../time-dependent-schrodinger-equation.md) is

$$
i\hbar\partial_t\psi=\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\partial_x^2+V(x)\right]\psi.
$$

The left side generates time evolution; the two terms on the right are the kinetic and potential parts of the [Hamiltonian operator](../../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md). For a time-independent potential and an [energy eigenstate](../../../../../energy-eigenstate.md), set $\psi(x,t)=\phi(x)e^{-iEt/\hbar}$. Cancelling the time factor gives the [time-independent Schrödinger equation](../../../../../time-independent-schrodinger-equation.md), $-\hbar^2\phi''/(2m)+V\phi=E\phi$.

By the [Born rule](../../../../../born-rule.md), $|\psi|^2dx$ is the position probability in an interval of width $dx$, with $\int|\psi|^2dx=1$ for a normalized particle. Therefore $\boxed{[\psi]=\mathrm{length}^{-1/2}}$ in one dimension. An ideal scattering plane wave instead uses flux normalization and is not itself a normalizable single-particle state on the whole line.
