<h1 id="s25/3/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Insert the pulse response into $D=\beta(T-T_0)$ and write $u=t'-t_e$. For a positive discount rate $k_D$,

$$
\mathrm{PV}=\frac{\beta\alpha}{\lambda}\int_0^\infty(1-e^{-k_Tu})e^{-k_Du}\,du
=\frac{\beta\alpha}{\lambda}\left(\frac1{k_D}-\frac1{k_D+k_T}\right).
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{PV}=\frac{A}{k_D(k_D+k_T)},\qquad
A=\frac{\alpha\beta k_T}{\lambda}=\frac{\alpha\beta}{C}.}
$$

With $k_D=0.03\,\mathrm{year^{-1}}$, $k_T=0.05\,\mathrm{year^{-1}}$ and the supplied one-tonne normalization of $A$,

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{PV}=\frac{0.4}{0.03(0.03+0.05)}\simeq167\,\mathrm{USD\,tonne^{-1}}.}
$$

This is valued at the time of emission because the discount factor is relative to that date. Valuing an emission at a future date from an earlier reference date would require an additional discount factor. The infinite-horizon integral converges for $k_D>0$ under the assumed persistent forcing.
