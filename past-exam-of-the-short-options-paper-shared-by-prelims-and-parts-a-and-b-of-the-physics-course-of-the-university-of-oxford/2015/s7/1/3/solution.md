<h1 id="s7/1/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

For an infinitesimal continuous symmetry $\delta q_i=\varepsilon\xi_i(q,t)$, allow the [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md) to change by a total derivative, $\delta L=\varepsilon\,dF/dt$. Direct variation gives

$$
\frac{\delta L}{\varepsilon}=\sum_i\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}-\frac d{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\right)\xi_i+\frac d{dt}\sum_i p_i\xi_i.
$$

On a solution of the [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md), the first term vanishes. This proves [Noether's theorem](../../../../../noether-conserved-quantity-for-a-mechanical-point-symmetry.md) in the fixed-time form:

$$
\boxed{Q=\sum_i p_i\xi_i-F,\qquad\dot Q=0.}
$$

Spatial translations yield total [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md), and rotations yield [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md). Time translations yield [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) when the Lagrangian has no explicit time dependence; this follows either from the extended time-transformation form of the theorem or by differentiating $H=\sum_ip_i\dot q_i-L$ to obtain $\dot H=-\partial L/\partial t$.
