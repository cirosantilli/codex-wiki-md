<h1 id="s7/1/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

[Hamilton's principle](../../../../../principle-of-stationary-action.md) makes the [action](../../../../../action.md) $S[q]=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}L(q,\dot q,t)dt$ stationary under variations vanishing at the endpoints. It need not be a strict minimum. Varying the action and integrating the velocity variation by parts gives

$$
\delta S=\left[\sum_i\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\delta q_i\right]_{t_1}^{t_2}+\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\sum_i\left[\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}-\frac d{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\right]\delta q_i\,dt.
$$

The endpoint term vanishes. Since the interior variations are arbitrary, the [fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations](../../../../../fundamental-lemma-of-the-calculus-of-variations.md) yields

$$
\boxed{\frac d{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}=0.}
$$
