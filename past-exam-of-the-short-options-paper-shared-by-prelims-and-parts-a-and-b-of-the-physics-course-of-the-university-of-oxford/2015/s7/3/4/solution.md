<h1 id="s7/3/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For $L_i=\epsilon_{ijk}q_jp_k$, the canonical [Poisson brackets](../../../../../poisson-bracket.md) give $\{L_i,L_j\}=\epsilon_{ijk}L_k$. Consequently projections onto fixed axes $n,m$ obey

$$
\{n\cdot L,m\cdot L\}=(n\times m)\cdot L.
$$

If the two original projections are conserved and the axes are linearly independent, the preceding [Jacobi identity](../../../../../jacobi-identity.md) argument makes the projection along $n\times m$ conserved as well. The vectors $n,m,n\times m$ form a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) of three-dimensional space, so every fixed-axis projection is a [linear combination](../../../../../linear-combination.md) of these conserved quantities. **Angular momentum is therefore conserved along every axis.**
