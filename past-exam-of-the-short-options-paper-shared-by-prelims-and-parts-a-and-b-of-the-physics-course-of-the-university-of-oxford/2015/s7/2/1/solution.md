<h1 id="s7/2/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The [canonical momentum](../../../../../canonical-momentum.md) is $p_i=\partial L/\partial\dot q_i$. If the velocity-momentum relation can be locally inverted, the [Legendre transform](../../../../../convex-conjugate.md) defines $H(q,p,t)=\sum_ip_i\dot q_i-L$, with the velocities expressed in terms of $q,p,t$. Its differential is

$$
dH=\sum_i\dot q_i\,dp_i-\sum_i\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}dq_i-\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}dt.
$$

The [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) then yield [Hamilton's equations](../../../../../hamilton-s-equations.md),

$$
\boxed{\dot q_i=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i},\qquad\dot p_i=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_i}.}
$$

A singular velocity Hessian instead requires a constrained-Hamiltonian treatment.
