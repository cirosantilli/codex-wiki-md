<h1 id="s27/1/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

[Newton's first law](../../../../../newton-s-first-law.md) says that a body retains uniform rectilinear motion, including rest as the zero-velocity case, unless impressed [forces](../../../../../force-split.md) change that motion. Its physical content includes the existence of a common class of [inertial frames](../../../../../inertial-frame.md): free bodies do not each require an unrelated frame chosen just to straighten their own trajectory. Uniformity refers to the inertial time parameter, and straightness to the spatial structure of those frames. Everyday slowing under friction is compatible with this claim because it involves an interaction, not an exception to inertia.

[Newton's second law](../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) relates the impressed resultant [force](../../../../../force-split.md) to change of [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md). In modern continuous-force notation,

$$
\frac{d\mathbf p}{dt}=\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}},\qquad \mathbf p=m\mathbf v,
$$

so for constant [mass](../../../../../mass.md) it becomes $m\mathbf a=\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}$. An impulsive version is $\Delta\mathbf p=\int\mathbf F\,dt$. The change is directed along the impressed resultant, not necessarily along the body's current [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md). The original wording concerns change of quantity of motion; the differential equation is its modern continuous-time formulation.[1687, Definition II and Axioms, or Laws of Motion, Laws I–II; distinguish momentum from velocity](../../../../../philosophiae-naturalis-principia-mathematica-by-isaac-newton.md)

These are substantive physical claims only when [mass](../../../../../mass.md), interactions and the inertial structure are constrained independently. If “force” merely abbreviates whatever equals $m\mathbf a$ in any arbitrarily chosen coordinates, the second law by itself predicts nothing. Actual applications combine the law with independently testable interaction laws, vector addition of forces, initial conditions and a specification of the inertial frames.
