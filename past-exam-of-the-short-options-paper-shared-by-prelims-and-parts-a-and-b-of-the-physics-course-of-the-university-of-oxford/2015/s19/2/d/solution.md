<h1 id="s19/2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Below the critical photon energy, radiation is emitted over a wider angular cone. The usual low-frequency characteristic-angle estimate is

$$
\sigma_\theta(\varepsilon)\sim\frac1\gamma
\left(\frac{\varepsilon_c}{\varepsilon}\right)^{1/3},
$$

up to an order-one angular-width convention. At $\varepsilon=0.012\,\mathrm{eV}$ this gives

$$
\boxed{\sigma_\theta\sim1.49\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{rad},\qquad
\sigma_y\sim0.149\,\mathrm m.}
$$

The aperture should therefore be enlarged **vertically**. A full width of order $0.30\,\mathrm m$ covers $\pm1$ characteristic width; approximately $0.90\,\mathrm m$ covers $\pm3$ and gives containment comparable with the preceding X-ray estimate in the same Gaussian model. The original $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ width would pass only roughly $\operatorname{erf}[0.005/(\sqrt2\,0.149)]\simeq2.7\%$ in that model. Equivalent optics could instead reduce the spot at a relocated aperture, at the expense of angular acceptance. These estimates concern geometric acceptance; THz coherence and bunch structure require further beam data.
