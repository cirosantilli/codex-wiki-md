<h1 id="s19/1/a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

At fixed gradient and magnet length, $f\propto p$. If $E$ is total [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md),

$$
\frac{d\ln p}{d\ln E}=\frac{E^2}{E^2-m_e^2c^4}=\frac1{\beta_0^2},\qquad
\boxed{f(E)=f_0\left(1+\frac\delta{\beta_0^2}\right)+O(\delta^2).}
$$

Here $\beta_0=v_0/c$, not one of the [Courant-Snyder parameters](../../../../../../courant-snyder-parameters.md). For ultrarelativistic electrons this becomes $f\simeq f_0(1+\delta)$, or $1/f\simeq(1-\delta)/f_0$: higher-energy particles are bent and focused less strongly. If the symbol $E$ instead denotes [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) $K$, the coefficient is $(K+mc^2)/(K+2mc^2)$, approaching $1/2$ in the nonrelativistic limit. Stating the energy convention avoids applying the ultrarelativistic result to a slow beam.
