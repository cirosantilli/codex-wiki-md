<h1 id="s19/1/a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Use transverse coordinates $(x,x')$ and define a positive signed focal length as focusing in that plane. A thin [quadrupole magnet](../../../../../../quadrupole-magnet.md) of length $\ell$ and signed gradient $G$ has integrated strength $1/f=|e|G\ell/p_0$; the electron charge and chosen field orientation are absorbed into the sign of $G$. Its [beam transfer matrix](../../../../../../beam-transfer-matrix.md) is

$$
\boxed{Q(f)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1/f&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad f=\frac{p_0}{|e|G\ell}.}
$$

For total electron [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) $E_0$, $p_0=\sqrt{E_0^2-m_e^2c^4}/c$, so $f\simeq E_0/(|e|cG\ell)$ in the ultrarelativistic limit. The other transverse plane has the opposite signed focal length. A [quadrupole magnet](../../../../../../quadrupole-magnet.md) therefore focuses one plane while defocusing the other; the longitudinal [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) component is negligible in this paraxial idealization.
