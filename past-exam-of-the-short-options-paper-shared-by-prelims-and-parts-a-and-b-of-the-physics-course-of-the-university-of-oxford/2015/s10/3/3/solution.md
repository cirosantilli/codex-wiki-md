↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

There are three physical controls: alter the energy splitting, alter the temperature, or drive the spin populations away from thermal equilibrium.

Increasing the static [magnetic flux density](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) increases $\Delta E=\hbar\gamma B$, and hence the equilibrium [nuclear spin polarization](../../../../../nuclear-spin-polarization.md) approximately in proportion to $B/T$. High-field [magnetic resonance imaging](../../../../../magnetic-resonance-imaging.md) exploits this for greater available magnetization and potentially finer spatial or temporal resolution. The disadvantages include more demanding magnets, stronger susceptibility-related distortions, and harder [radio frequency](../../../../../radio-frequency.md) field uniformity and power management. A direct experimental comparison of head imaging at 7 T and 4 T measured both the signal gain and the RF penalties.[head-imaging measurements of signal-to-noise, RF power and field homogeneity](../../../../../7t-vs-4t-rf-power-homogeneity-and-signal-to-noise-comparison-in-head-images-by-j-thomas-vaughan-jr-and-others.md)

Lowering the _spin sample's_ temperature increases its equilibrium polarization at fixed $B$. Cryogenic preparation of an imaging agent can exploit this, often assisted by [dynamic nuclear polarization](../../../../../dynamic-nuclear-polarization.md), followed by rapid dissolution and transfer for [hyperpolarized magnetic resonance imaging](../../../../../hyperpolarized-magnetic-resonance-imaging.md). This offers a much larger signal from dilute nuclei than room-temperature equilibrium, but requires preparation hardware, rapid transfer and acquisition before [spin-lattice relaxation](../../../../../spin-lattice-relaxation.md) erases the polarization. Cooling an RF receiver reduces electronic noise, but does not itself change the nuclei's population ratio; nor does this argument propose cooling living tissue to cryogenic temperatures. The demonstrated dissolution method combines low temperature, magnetic field and microwave-assisted polarization transfer, so its gain must not be attributed to cooling alone.[solid-state polarization and rapid dissolution preserving enhanced nuclear polarization](../../../../../increase-in-signal-to-noise-ratio-of-greater-than-10-000-times-in-liquid-state-nmr-by-jan-henrik-ardenkjaer-larsen-and-others.md)

Driving transitions with a resonant [radio frequency](../../../../../radio-frequency.md) field changes the longitudinal populations without changing $B$ or the bath temperature. A $\pi$ pulse interchanges the populations; saturation equalizes them. [Inversion recovery](../../../../../inversion-recovery.md) then measures or exploits their return toward equilibrium, $M_z(t)=M_0(1-2e^{-t/T_1})$ after an ideal inversion, providing tissue contrast and signal nulling. Advantages are selective contrast and control within an ordinary scanner; costs include reduced signal, extra acquisition time, RF energy deposition and sensitivity to pulse errors. Such RF pulses alone cannot create a polarization magnitude greater than the available equilibrium value. Genuine nonthermal enhancement instead transfers polarization from another spin reservoir, for example electron spins in [dynamic nuclear polarization](../../../../../dynamic-nuclear-polarization.md) or optically polarized noble gases in lung imaging; its transient and preparation constraints differ from ordinary RF inversion.[introductory account distinguishing optical pumping, spin-order transfer and DNP from thermal polarization](../../../../../increase-in-signal-to-noise-ratio-of-greater-than-10-000-times-in-liquid-state-nmr-by-jan-henrik-ardenkjaer-larsen-and-others.md)
