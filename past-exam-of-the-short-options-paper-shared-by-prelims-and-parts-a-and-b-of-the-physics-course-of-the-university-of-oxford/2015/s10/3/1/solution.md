<h1 id="s10/3/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Take room temperature as $T=300\,\mathrm K$. With the supplied [gyromagnetic ratio](../../../../../gyromagnetic-ratio.md), [reduced Planck constant](../../../../../reduced-planck-constant.md) and [Boltzmann constant](../../../../../boltzmann-constant.md),

$$
\Delta E=(1.054\times10^{-34})(2.675\times10^8)(1.5)=4.23\times10^{-26}\,\mathrm J,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{\frac{\Delta E}{k_{\rm B}T}=\frac{4.23\times10^{-26}}{(1.380\times10^{-23})(300)}=1.02\times10^{-5}.}
$$

At $293\,\mathrm K$ the ratio is $1.05\times10^{-5}$; the room-temperature convention makes only a small difference. The corresponding fractional [nuclear spin polarization](../../../../../nuclear-spin-polarization.md) has magnitude about five parts per million. The source's negative sign convention assigns the label up to the higher-energy state; it should not be confused with the lower-energy magnetic moment aligned with a positive proton [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md).
