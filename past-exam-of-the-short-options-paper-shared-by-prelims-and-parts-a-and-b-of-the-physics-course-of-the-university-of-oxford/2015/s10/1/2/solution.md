For [photons](../../../../../photon.md), the total [mass attenuation coefficient](../../../../../mass-attenuation-coefficient.md) sums contributions from [photoelectric absorption](../../../../../photoelectric-absorption.md), [Compton scattering](../../../../../compton-scattering.md), coherent [Rayleigh scattering](../../../../../rayleigh-scattering.md), and [pair production](../../../../../pair-production.md) in nuclear or electronic fields when above their thresholds. At sufficiently high [photon energies](../../../../../photon-energy.md), [photonuclear reactions](../../../../../photonuclear-reaction.md) can also contribute. Attenuation counts removal from the incident narrow beam, including scattering without complete local deposition of the original energy.

In water at both specified energies, [Compton scattering](../../../../../compton-scattering.md) dominates. At $140\,\mathrm{keV}$, [photoelectric absorption](../../../../../photoelectric-absorption.md) and coherent [Rayleigh scattering](../../../../../rayleigh-scattering.md) make smaller contributions; at $511\,\mathrm{keV}$ these are still less important. Nuclear-field [pair production](../../../../../pair-production.md) is impossible below $2m_ec^2=1.022\,\mathrm{MeV}$, so it occurs in neither example. The positrons used to produce the fluorine-18 annihilation radiation do not change that threshold for interactions of its $511\,\mathrm{keV}$ [photons](../../../../../photon.md) in water.

Strictly, $\mu$ is a [linear attenuation coefficient](../../../../../linear-attenuation-coefficient.md), whereas $\mu/\rho$ is a [mass attenuation coefficient](../../../../../mass-attenuation-coefficient.md); the source's wording blurs these units. The [mass energy-absorption coefficient](../../../../../mass-energy-absorption-coefficient.md) $\mu_{\rm en}/\rho$ weights the fraction of incident [photon energy](../../../../../photon-energy.md) transferred to charged particles and retained after radiative losses, rather than simply counting all removals. Coherent scattering contributes attenuation with negligible energy transfer. A [photon](../../../../../photon.md) deflected by [Compton scattering](../../../../../compton-scattering.md) can carry much of the original energy away. One often writes $\mu_{\rm en}=(1-g)\mu_{\rm tr}$, with $g$ the fraction of secondary charged-particle kinetic energy lost radiatively. Under [charged-particle equilibrium](../../../../../charged-particle-equilibrium.md), multiplying [photon energy fluence](../../../../../photon-energy-fluence.md) by $\mu_{\rm en}/\rho$ gives the [absorbed dose](../../../../../absorbed-dose.md); that equilibrium condition matters near interfaces and in buildup regions.[§§2–3, definitions of total attenuation, energy transfer and energy absorption](../../../../../tables-of-x-ray-mass-attenuation-coefficients-and-mass-energy-absorption-coefficients-by-john-h-hubbell-and-stephen-m-seltzer.md)
