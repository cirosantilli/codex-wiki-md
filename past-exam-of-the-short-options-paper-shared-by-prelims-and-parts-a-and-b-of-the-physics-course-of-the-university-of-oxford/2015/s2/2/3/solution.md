↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A first distance-independent method fits the continuum shape or a calibrated [colour index](../../../../../color-index.md) to an atmosphere model. A black-body approximation uses [Wien's displacement law](../../../../../wien-s-displacement-law.md), $\lambda_{\max}T\simeq2.90\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m\,K}$, or ratios of the [Planck spectrum](../../../../../blackbody-spectrum.md) at two wavelengths. It requires a known extinction correction and sufficient treatment of molecular absorption and line blanketing; a cool supergiant need not have a black-body colour temperature equal to its [effective temperature](../../../../../effective-temperature.md).

A second method fits temperature-sensitive [spectral lines](../../../../../spectral-line.md) or molecular bands. [Boltzmann excitation](../../../../../boltzmann-excitation.md) and the [Saha equation](../../../../../saha-ionization-equation.md) explain why excitation and ionization ratios change with temperature; detailed atmosphere calculations also include surface gravity, composition and departures from [local thermodynamic equilibrium](../../../../../local-thermodynamic-equilibrium.md). TiO molecular bands are particularly useful for cool stars. Line ratios remove the overall distance normalization, but their calibration remains model dependent.

The direct flux-radius method can be made distance independent as well: an independently measured angular diameter $\Theta$ gives $T_{\rm eff}=[4F_{\rm bol}/(\sigma_{\rm SB}\Theta^2)]^{1/4}$. For a large nearby supergiant with a resolved photosphere, good bolometric flux and a limb-darkened [interferometric angular diameter](../../../../../interferometric-angular-diameter.md), this is often the most direct determination. Its advantage depends on those measurement uncertainties and a consistent radius definition. The radius inferred in the preceding part cannot itself provide an independent temperature measurement, because the supplied temperature was already used to calculate it.
