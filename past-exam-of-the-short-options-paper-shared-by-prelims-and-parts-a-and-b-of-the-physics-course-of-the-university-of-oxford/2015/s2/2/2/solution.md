<h1 id="s2/2/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The [parallax](../../../../../parallax.md) gives $d=1/0.00763\simeq131\,\mathrm{pc}$. Ignoring extinction, the [distance modulus](../../../../../distance-modulus.md) gives

$$
M_{\rm bol}=m_{\rm bol}-5\log_{10}(d/10\,\mathrm{pc})\simeq-6.29.
$$

The [bolometric magnitude](../../../../../bolometric-magnitude.md) relation then yields $L/L_\odot=10^{0.4(4.72-M_{\rm bol})}\simeq2.53\times10^4$. With $T_\odot\simeq5772\,\mathrm K$,

$$
\boxed{\frac R{R_\odot}=\sqrt{\frac L{L_\odot}}\left(\frac{T_\odot}{3500\,\mathrm K}\right)^2\simeq4.3\times10^2.}
$$

A star this cool and luminous belongs to the **red-supergiant region** of the [Hertzsprung-Russell diagram](../../../../../hertzsprung-russell-diagram.md). Extinction and the adopted [effective temperature](../../../../../effective-temperature.md) are important systematic inputs; this calculation uses the supplied temperature to infer the radius.
