<h1 id="s2/3/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

**Nonbaryonic dark matter** can provide the additional gravitating mass. [Gravitational lensing](../../../../../gravitational-lensing-split.md) independently maps cluster mass, while the [cosmic microwave background](../../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-split.md) and [Big Bang nucleosynthesis](../../../../../big-bang-nucleosynthesis.md) constrain the cosmic matter and baryon budgets. The separation of lensing mass from strongly interacting X-ray gas in merging clusters is evidence that much of the gravitating component is not that hot gas.

**Uncounted baryonic matter**, including the observed hot gas, faint stars and compact remnants, certainly adds to the stellar estimate. X-ray emission directly establishes a large gas reservoir. However, baryon-budget constraints and [microlensing](../../../../../gravitational-microlensing.md) constraints on compact objects prevent arbitrarily filling the entire discrepancy with ordinary hidden objects.

**Modified gravity** could change the inference from a measured acceleration to a Newtonian mass. Such a theory must also reproduce the observed lensing and cosmological evidence; explaining some rotation curves alone is insufficient, and cluster mass discrepancies remain a demanding test. Departures from equilibrium and projection can bias individual mass estimates, but would have to explain the independent observations systematically rather than merely modify one virial prefactor.
