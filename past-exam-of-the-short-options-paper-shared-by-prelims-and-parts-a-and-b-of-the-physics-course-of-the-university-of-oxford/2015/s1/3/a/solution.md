<h1 id="s1/3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For $t>0$, close the real-axis [contour](../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) in the upper half-plane: $|e^{izt}|=e^{-t\operatorname{Im}z}$ supplies decay. The only enclosed [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md) is $z=i$, with [residue](../../../../../residue.md) $e^{-t}/(2i)$. Thus $h(t)=2\pi i\,e^{-t}/(2i)=\pi e^{-t}$.

For $t<0$, close downwards. The clockwise orientation contributes $-2\pi i$ times the [residue](../../../../../residue.md) $e^t/(-2i)$ at $z=-i$, giving

$$
\boxed{h(t)=\pi e^{-|t|}.}
$$

At $t=0$, direct integration of $1/(1+p^2)$ gives $\pi$, consistent with the continuous extension. The large arc tends to zero by [Jordan's lemma](../../../../../jordan-s-lemma.md) for nonzero $t$, or the denominator estimate when $t=0$.
