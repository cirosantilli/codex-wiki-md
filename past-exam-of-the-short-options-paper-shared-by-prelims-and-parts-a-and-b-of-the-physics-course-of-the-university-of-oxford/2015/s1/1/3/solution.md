<h1 id="s1/1/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Take $g(z)=L(z)^3/z^3$ on the same [logarithm branch](../../../../../branch-of-the-complex-logarithm.md). At zero, $L(z)^3=-z^3+O(z^4)$, so the apparent singularity is removable and the closed [contour integral](../../../../../contour-integral.md) is zero. The bank difference is

$$
(\ell-i\pi)^3-(\ell+i\pi)^3=-6i\pi\ell^2+2i\pi^3.
$$

The circular contributions vanish by the previous estimates with the third power of the logarithm. Consequently

$$
0=-6i\pi\int_1^\infty\frac{\log^2(x-1)}{x^3}\,dx+2i\pi^3\int_1^\infty\frac{dx}{x^3}.
$$

The last [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) is $1/2$, giving

$$
\boxed{\int_1^\infty\frac{\log^2(x-1)}{x^3}\,dx=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.}
$$

Increasing the logarithm power has converted the desired real [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) into a jump across a [branch cut](../../../../../branch-cut.md), while removing the pole inside the contour.
