<h1 id="s1/1/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Choose the [principal logarithm](../../../../../principal-complex-logarithm.md), so $L(z)=\operatorname{Log}(1-z)$ has [branch cut](../../../../../branch-cut.md) $[1,\infty)$. Near zero its [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) gives

$$
L(z)=-z-\frac{z^2}2-\frac{z^3}3+O(z^4),\qquad\frac{L(z)^2}{z^3}=\frac1z+1+O(z).
$$

Thus **zero is a simple pole of residue one**, and **one is a logarithmic branch point**. The function is [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on $\mathbb C\setminus(\{0\}\cup[1,\infty))$ with this branch. Infinity is also a branch point in the extended-plane description; the function's decay there along the cut plane does not make it a single-valued holomorphic function through infinity.
