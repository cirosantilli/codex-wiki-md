<h1 id="s16/1/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Increasing the conserved nucleus density to $10N$ multiplies the low-temperature neutral-gas slope and high-temperature ionized-gas slope by ten. However, at a fixed intermediate temperature, $x^2/(1-x)=S(T)/(10N)$ is smaller, so a smaller fraction is ionized. The curved transition therefore moves to higher temperature; the new graph is not simply ten times the old graph throughout the crossover.

At a fixed chosen [ionization fraction](../../../../../ionization-fraction.md), $S(T)$ must increase tenfold. Locally this requires

$$
\Delta T\simeq\frac{\ln10}{3/(2T)+R/(k_BT^2)},
$$

which is of order $10^3\,\mathrm K$ in this region. Directly evaluating the same [Saha equation](../../../../../saha-ionization-equation.md) at $10^{21}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$ moves the maximum pressure slope from about $9250$ to $10560\,\mathrm K$. The two asymptotic slopes still have ratio two, because each hydrogen nucleus still contributes one particle when neutral and two when fully ionized.
