↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The [circumstellar habitable zone](../../../../../circumstellar-habitable-zone.md) is the range of stellar irradiation at which a suitable rocky planet could maintain liquid water at its surface under specified atmospheric conditions. It is not a guarantee of life or even of liquid water on every planet at that distance. Its boundaries depend on greenhouse gases, clouds, humidity, pressure, albedo, rotation and the stellar spectrum; stellar evolution, ultraviolet activity, atmospheric loss and tidal locking can complicate applications to cool stars.

Approximating both stars as [blackbodies](../../../../../blackbody.md), the host luminosity relative to the Sun is

$$
\frac{L_*}{L_\odot}=0.21^2\left(\frac{3000}{5778}\right)^4\simeq0.00320.
$$

The Earth-irradiance orbit therefore has

$$
\boxed{a_{\rm E}=\sqrt{L_*/L_\odot}\,\mathrm{AU}\simeq0.0566\,\mathrm{AU},\qquad
\theta_{\max}\simeq\frac{a_{\rm E}}{14.6\,\mathrm{pc}}=3.88\,\mathrm{mas}.}
$$

Use the largest projected separation and a visible reference wavelength of $550\,\mathrm{nm}$. The [Rayleigh criterion](../../../../../rayleigh-criterion.md) for a circular aperture gives

$$
\boxed{D\gtrsim\frac{1.22\lambda}{\theta_{\max}}\simeq35.7\left(\frac\lambda{550\,\mathrm{nm}}\right)\mathrm m.}
$$

This is only a diffraction-based minimum: detecting a faint planet beside its star additionally requires contrast suppression and adequate photon counts. An interferometer baseline, rather than its collecting-aperture diameter, sets its angular resolution.

For reflected light the [reflected-light planetary phase curve](../../../../../reflected-light-planetary-phase-curve.md) gives the flux ratio $\epsilon=A_g\Phi(\alpha)(R_\oplus/a_{\rm E})^2$, where $A_g$ is [geometric albedo](../../../../../geometric-albedo.md) and $\Phi(0)=1$. Numerically,

$$
\boxed{\epsilon_{\rm full}\simeq5.66\times10^{-7}A_g.}
$$

A unit-geometric-albedo reference gives about $6\times10^{-7}$; an Earth-like chosen $A_g=0.3$ gives $1.7\times10^{-7}$. Albedo and phase function were not specified, so an exact contrast cannot be inferred just from the radius. Full-phase brightness occurs close to zero projected separation for an edge-on orbit; at the best-resolved quadrature, a Lambert sphere has $\Phi(\pi/2)=1/\pi$, reducing the Earth-albedo example to $5.4\times10^{-8}$. Thus the best angular separation and maximum reflected contrast are not generally achieved at the same phase.
