<h1 id="s30/2/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The collision [cross section](../../../../../cross-section-physics.md) in the incoming impact-parameter plane is

$$
\boxed{\sigma_{\rm col}=\pi b_{\rm col}^2
=\pi(r_M+r_m)^2(1+f).}
$$

Without [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) it is only $\sigma_{\rm geom}=\pi(r_M+r_m)^2$, so [gravitational focusing](../../../../../gravitational-focusing.md) increases the cross-section by $1+f$. Trajectories that would miss geometrically can be bent into contact, especially for slow relative encounters. Gravity does not itself guarantee sticking: the following accretion estimate assumes collisions agglomerate rather than fragment, rebound or erode the target.

A uniform incoming projectile mass density $\rho_p$ has mass flux $\rho_pv_0$, so

$$
\boxed{\frac{dM}{dt}=\rho_pv_0\sigma_{\rm col}.}
$$

The formula counts mass swept per unit time and assumes an adequate continuing projectile supply, the stated speed, and negligible many-body or stellar-tidal modification of the two-body cross-section.
