<h1 id="s30/2/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The source expression $E=\tfrac12\mu v^2-GMm/r$ is **total mechanical energy** in the centre-of-mass frame, not kinetic energy alone. Take $v_0$ as the relative speed at a large initial separation where the mutual potential is negligible. At closest approach, the radial relative velocity is zero, so [conservation of angular momentum](../../../../../conservation-of-angular-momentum.md) and [conservation of energy](../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) give

$$
\mu b v_0=\mu r_cv_c,\qquad
\frac12\mu v_0^2=\frac12\mu v_c^2-\frac{GMm}{r_c}.
$$

Using $Mm/\mu=M+m$ eliminates $v_c$:

$$
b^2=r_c^2+\frac{2G(M+m)r_c}{v_0^2}.
$$

An impact occurs when $r_c\leq R$, where $R=r_M+r_m$. Since the right side increases with positive $r_c$, the collision threshold is

$$
\boxed{b_{\rm col}^2=R^2\left[1+\frac{2G(M+m)}{Rv_0^2}\right],\qquad
f=\frac{2G(M+m)}{Rv_0^2}.}
$$

The mutual [escape velocity](../../../../../escape-velocity.md) is the relative speed at contact required to reach infinite separation with zero residual speed. Setting total mechanical energy to zero gives

$$
\boxed{v_{\rm esc}=\sqrt{\frac{2G(M+m)}{r_M+r_m}},\qquad f=v_{\rm esc}^2/v_0^2.}
$$

This includes target recoil. If the given initial speed were instead at a finite non-negligible separation, its potential energy would have to be retained; $v_0$ would then not equal the asymptotic speed used in the focusing formula.
