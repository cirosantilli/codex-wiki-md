<h1 id="s18/2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the _[amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md)_ continue to $k=i\kappa$ on the decaying physical sheet. Then $\zeta=q$ and $D=\cos(qa)+(\kappa^2-q^2)\sin(qa)/(2\kappa q)$. The double-angle identity factors its zero condition into the even and odd [matching conditions](../../../../../asymptotic-matching-condition.md) $\kappa=q\tan(qb)$ and $\kappa=-q\cot(qb)$. Thus physical upper-half-plane [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) [poles](../../../../../pole.md) are exactly the bound energies.

**The literal statement for the [probability](../../../../../probability.md) requires correction.** The displayed real-axis expression continued algebraically is $1/[D(k)D(-k)]$, and includes [poles](../../../../../pole.md) of the opposite-boundary-condition [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) too. A concrete counterexample uses $\hbar^2/(2m)=1$, $a=2$, $q=1$, $\kappa=\cot1$ and $U_0=1+\cot^2 1$. Its continued [probability](../../../../../probability.md) denominator vanishes at $k=i\cot1$, because $D(-k)=0$. But neither $\kappa=\tan1$ nor $\kappa=-\cot1$ holds, so $E=-\cot^2 1$ is not a bound [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). Keeping the [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) and the decaying [boundary condition](../../../../../boundary-condition.md) removes this spurious identification.
