<h1 id="s18/3/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A simpler physical coordinate is $Y=y+p_xc/(eB)$. For $\sigma_3$ [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $s_z=\pm1$, the equation becomes an oscillator of [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $m$, [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) $\omega_c=|eB|/(mc)$, and auxiliary [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md)

$$
\varepsilon=\frac{E^2-m^2c^4-p_z^2c^2+e\hbar Bc\,s_z}{2mc^2}.
$$

If the source's variable $\xi=(p_xc+eBy)/(\hbar c)=b_B Y$ is used literally, $b_B=eB/(\hbar c)$, then the same canonical oscillator form has $\boxed{M=m/b_B^2,\ \omega=\omega_c}$ and the same $\varepsilon$. Because $\xi$ is an inverse length, this auxiliary $M$ is not a physical particle [mass](../../../../../mass.md) with ordinary [mass dimensions](../../../../../mass-dimension.md); the rescaling must be carried through both kinetic and potential terms.
