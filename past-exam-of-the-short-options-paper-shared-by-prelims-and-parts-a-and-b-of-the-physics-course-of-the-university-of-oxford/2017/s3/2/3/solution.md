<h1 id="s3/2/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A sudden wall displacement leaves the initial [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) unchanged: it is $\sqrt{2/L}\sin(\pi x/L)$ on $[0,L]$ and zero on $(L,2L]$. It is not an [eigenfunction](../../../../../eigenfunction.md) of the new [Hamiltonian](../../../../../hamiltonian.md), so there is **no single certain new [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md)**. Its [energy expectation](../../../../../energy-expectation.md) immediately afterward remains $E_1^{\mathrm{old}}$, as seen from $\hbar^2\int|\psi'|^2/(2m)$; the measurement outcomes are $E_n^{\mathrm{new}}=n^2E_1^{\mathrm{old}}/4$.

The new [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) is $L^{-1/2}\sin(\pi x/(2L))$. Its overlap is $4\sqrt2/(3\pi)$, so

$$
\boxed{P_{\mathrm{new\ ground}}=\frac{32}{9\pi^2}\simeq0.360.}
$$

The discontinuity of the [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) at the former wall does not invalidate the finite quadratic-form expectation, although it matters when applying the differential [Hamiltonian](../../../../../hamiltonian.md) pointwise. [Adiabatic expansion](../../../../../adiabatic-expansion.md) would instead keep the state in the ground level and reduce its [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) by four.
