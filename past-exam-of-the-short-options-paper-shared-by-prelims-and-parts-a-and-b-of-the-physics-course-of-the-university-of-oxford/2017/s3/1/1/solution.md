↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The [Bohr model](../../../../../bohr-model.md) assumes Coulomb attraction and classical [circular motion](../../../../../circular-motion.md) between transitions; selected stationary [orbits](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) do not radiate; their [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) is $mvr=n\hbar$; and a transition exchanges a [photon](../../../../../photon.md) of [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $h\nu=E_i-E_f$. The first two [dynamical equations](../../../../../dynamical-equation.md) give

$$
\frac{mv^2}r=\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2},\qquad mvr=n\hbar
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{r_n=\frac{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2n^2}{me^2}.}
$$

Here $n=1,2,\ldots$, $m$ is the [electron mass](../../../../../electron-mass.md) under the fixed-proton approximation, $e$ its [charge](../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) magnitude, and $\epsilon_0$ the [vacuum permittivity](../../../../../vacuum-permittivity.md). The stationary-orbit postulate deliberately suspends the classical radiation prediction; this is a historical model, not a consistent deduction from classical electrodynamics.
