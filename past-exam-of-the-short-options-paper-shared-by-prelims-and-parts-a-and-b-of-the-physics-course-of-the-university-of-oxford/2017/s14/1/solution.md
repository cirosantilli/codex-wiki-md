<h1 id="s14/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Instruments changed both what natural philosophers could observe and what counted as a credible observation. A [telescope](../../../../telescope.md) enlarged access to celestial detail; a [microscope](../../../../microscope-satellite.md) opened a small-scale domain; an [air pump](../../../../air-pump.md) created conditions unlike ordinary open-air experience. These were interventions with assumptions about manufacture and operation, not transparent windows through which nature spoke without interpretation.

The telescopic observations of [Galileo Galilei](../../../../galileo-galilei.md) illustrate the distinction between a novel sight and a warranted claim. Lunar relief and the phases of Venus challenged an unchanging heaven and a simple Earth-centered arrangement, but the phases did not by themselves discriminate every rival cosmological system. Critics could question optical artifacts, observer skill or the reliability of an unfamiliar device. Repeated observations, geometrical [arguments](../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) and demonstrations helped stabilize the evidence. [Robert Boyle](../../../../robert-boyle.md)'s air-pump [work](../../../../work.md) similarly made [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md) variation experimentally manageable while making the apparatus, seals, leakage and the credibility of witnesses part of the [argument](../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md).

Instruments also reorganized knowledge quantitatively. Scales, clocks and calibrated [thermometers](../../../../thermometer.md) enabled measurements to be compared, corrected and transported between sites. Reproducibility depended on written procedures and skilled craftsmanship, so it could initially be limited to particular laboratories rather than automatic across observers. Precision could reveal discrepancies that ordinary sensation could not discriminate; it also created new questions about [systematic error](../../../../systematic-error.md) and standards.

The transformation was therefore epistemic and social as well as visual. Specialist makers and trained operators became important contributors, and public demonstrations or correspondence could extend trust beyond direct witnessing. Older reasoning and unaided observations continued to matter. A strong historical conclusion is that instrumental practice expanded possible phenomena and reshaped standards of evidence, rather than instantly replacing speculative [natural philosophy](../../../../natural-philosophy.md) with a single modern experimental method.
