<h1 id="s19/2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Doubling the number of identical-angle bends halves each angle. The minimum emittance scales as $\theta^3$, so it becomes $\boxed{0.240\ \mathrm{nm\,rad}}$. At unchanged [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and unchanged individual magnet length, the bending [radius](../../../../../radius.md) doubles and field halves, giving $B=0.70$ T and $\boxed{E_c\simeq4.19\ \mathrm{keV}}$.

At fixed stored current the loss per turn, proportional to $E^4\oint ds/\rho^2$, halves: twice as many equal-length bends each have one quarter of the original contribution. Total radiation power therefore halves. The [spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-topology.md) softens by the factor-two critical-energy change. The characteristic high-energy vertical opening angle remains of order $1/\gamma$ at fixed [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), while each magnet's horizontal sweep halves. At a _fixed absolute_ [photon energy](../../../../../photon-energy.md) the normalized spectral position changes, so a claim that every [angular distribution](../../../../../angular-distribution.md) is identical would be too strong. Lower emittance also reduces the [electron](../../../../../electron.md) source size/divergence subject to the optical functions.
