<h1 id="s1/3/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Put $z=e^{i\theta}$, so $d\theta=dz/(iz)$, $\cos\theta=(z+z^{-1})/2$ and $\sin\theta=(z-z^{-1})/(2i)$. Rational expressions in these [trigonometric functions](../../../../../trigonometric-function.md) become rational [contour](../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) integrands on the [unit circle](../../../../../complex-unit-circle.md). The method is especially useful when the resulting denominator has a small number of identifiable [poles](../../../../../pole.md). A [pole](../../../../../pole.md) on the [integration](../../../../../integral-split.md) circle requires a separately specified [principal value](../../../../../cauchy-principal-value.md) or indentation; the [residue theorem](../../../../../residue-theorem.md) does not automatically assign an ordinary [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) there.
