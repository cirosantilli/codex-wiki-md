<h1 id="s1/2/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Taking the difference of the two bank [integrals](../../../../../integral-split.md) at $n=2$ gives

$$
\int_0^\infty\frac{(\log x)^2-(\log x+2\pi i)^2}{1+x^2}\,dx
=4\pi^2 I_0-4\pi i I_1=2\pi^3.
$$

The $I_j$ are real and convergent. Comparing real and [imaginary parts](../../../../../imaginary-part.md) proves $\boxed{I_0=\pi/2,\ I_1=0}$. Independently, $x\mapsto1/x$ changes the sign of each odd logarithmic moment and preserves the measure, so every odd $I_j$ vanishes.
