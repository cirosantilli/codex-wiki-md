<h1 id="s1/1/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Choose a continuous [argument](../../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) $\theta$ on a [simply connected domain](../../../../../simply-connected-domain.md) avoiding zero, and put $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$. With $u=x\log r-y\theta$, direct [differentiation](../../../../../differentiation.md) gives

$$
u_x=1+\log r,\quad u_y=-\theta,\quad u_{xx}=x/r^2,\quad u_{yy}=-x/r^2.
$$

Here $\theta_x=-y/r^2$, $\theta_y=x/r^2$; these identities specify the branch needed in the written [arctangent](../../../../../inverse-tangent.md). Integrating the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) gives

$$
\boxed{f(z)=z\operatorname{Log}z+iC,\qquad v=y\log r+x\theta+C,\quad C\in\mathbb R.}
$$

The literal principal $\arctan(y/x)$ works only separately on suitable half-planes. It cannot stand for a single continuous [argument](../../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) across the [imaginary axis](../../../../../imaginary-axis.md).
