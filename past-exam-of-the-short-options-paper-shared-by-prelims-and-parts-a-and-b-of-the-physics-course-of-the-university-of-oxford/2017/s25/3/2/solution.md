<h1 id="s25/3/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

For a small additional carbon [mass](../../../../../mass.md), logarithmic forcing has [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) inversely proportional to the background atmospheric carbon inventory. With other conversion factors fixed, $b_0$ therefore decreases as background concentration rises. Solve the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) equation using an [integrating factor](../../../../../integrating-factor.md) and the pulse burden:

$$
T(t)=\frac{b_0}{2}\left[\frac{1-e^{-b_1t}}{b_1}+\frac{e^{-2kt}-e^{-b_1t}}{b_1-2k}\right].
$$

At $b_1=2k$ the second term is its continuous limit $te^{-b_1t}$. For $0<k\ll b_1$, [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) initially rises over $b_1^{-1}$ toward roughly $b_0/b_1$, then relaxes over $(2k)^{-1}$ toward half that value. This produces an overshoot, rather than permanent growth. The two numerical limits requested separately below also follow directly from the [differential equation](../../../../../differential-equation-split.md).
