<h1 id="s27/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Homogeneity](../../../../../homogeneity.md) makes the inertial transformation linear. Align origins at $t=t'=0$, let the second origin have [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) $v$, and impose [isotropy](../../../../../isotropy.md) and reciprocity. Then $x'=\gamma(v)(x-vt)$, $y'=y$, $z'=z$. If the same limiting signal [speed](../../../../../speed.md) $c$ has trajectories $x=\pm ct$ in both systems, substituting these two trajectories fixes $t'=\gamma(v)(t-vx/c^2)$. Requiring the inverse to have the same form with $-v$ fixes $\gamma^2(1-v^2/c^2)=1$. Continuity at rest selects the positive [square root](../../../../../square-root.md):

$$
\boxed{x'=\frac{x-vt}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}},\quad t'=\frac{t-vx/c^2}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}},\quad y'=y,\ z'=z.}
$$

Light enters this derivation through the empirical/in-principle operational identification of the invariant [speed](../../../../../speed.md) and [Einstein synchronization](../../../../../einstein-synchronisation.md). Group, [homogeneity](../../../../../homogeneity.md) and [isotropy](../../../../../isotropy.md) [arguments](../../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) can instead derive a family with a universal parameter $K$, $t'=\gamma(t-Kvx)$ and $\gamma=(1-Kv^2)^{-1/2}$. They do not by themselves determine whether $K=0$ or a positive finite value. Empirical light propagation or other relativistic dynamics identifies $K=1/c^2$ for our world. Consequently a derivation need not assume light as its first premise, but it must acquire the physical invariant-speed structure somewhere rather than extract it from linearity alone.[1905, section 3, derivation of the coordinate transformation](../../../../../on-the-electrodynamics-of-moving-bodies-by-albert-einstein.md)
