↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Define the [plasma parameter](../../../../../plasma-parameter.md) as the number in a [Debye sphere](../../../../../debye-sphere.md), $N_D=(4\pi/3)n_e\lambda_D^3$; some conventions omit $4\pi/3$. A good classical [plasma](../../../../../plasma-physics.md) has many particles in a screening volume, $N_D\gg1$, and a system size much larger than $\lambda_D$, so collective fields dominate individual encounters.

A collision here means cumulative Coulomb deflection that significantly changes a particle's [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md), not necessarily a literal hard-sphere impact. A collisionless description requires the phenomenon's timescale to be short compared with the appropriate collision time, and its length scale short compared with the [mean free path](../../../../../mean-free-path.md). Parametrically $\boxed{\omega_p t_c\sim N_D/\log\Lambda}$ up to convention-dependent constants, with [Coulomb logarithm](../../../../../coulomb-logarithm.md) $\log\Lambda$ representing the range of [impact parameters](../../../../../impact-parameter.md). Many [oscillations](../../../../../oscillation.md) can then occur before collisional relaxation. The formula itself presupposes the weak-coupling regime.
