↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The parallax gives $d=20$ pc and the [distance modulus](../../../../../distance-modulus.md) gives $M_{\mathrm{bol}}=-0.641-5\log_{10}2=-2.146$. Therefore $L/L_\odot=10^{(4.72-M_{\mathrm{bol}})/2.5}\simeq558$. Taking $T_\odot\simeq5772$ K,

$$
\boxed{\frac{R_*}{R_\odot}=\sqrt{L/L_\odot}(T_\odot/3900)^2\simeq52.}
$$

This is a cool luminous giant, in the red-giant region of the [Hertzsprung-Russell diagram](../../../../../hertzsprung-russell-diagram.md). On the usual first-ascent interpretation, [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) comes predominantly from [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) shell burning around an inert [helium](../../../../../helium.md) core, with gravitational adjustment also contributing. The two measured quantities alone do not uniquely determine evolutionary history: a later giant phase can overlap this region, so they do not prove the precise core-burning state.
