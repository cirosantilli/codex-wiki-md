<h1 id="s2/2/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A continuum peak gives a color [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) through Wien's law, provided the continuum is approximately Planckian and extinction is corrected. Broad-band colors or a fitted [spectral energy distribution](../../../../../spectral-energy-distribution.md) compare flux ratios with model atmospheres; they require a reddening estimate, abundance and gravity assumptions, and calibrated passbands. Spectroscopic line ratios or excitation/ionization balances provide another [thermometer](../../../../../thermometer.md), requiring atomic data and a suitable atmosphere model, often [local thermodynamic equilibrium](../../../../../local-thermodynamic-equilibrium.md).

For this cool giant, line blanketing and molecular absorption substantially distort a simple continuum peak, so Wien's law is the least reliable of these approximations. A detailed spectroscopic analysis, checked against a multi-band atmosphere fit, would generally be the strongest of the three if good data and model parameters are available. There is no unconditional accuracy ranking without [measurement errors](../../../../../measurement-error.md) and model systematics; an accurately measured [angular diameter](../../../../../angular-diameter.md) plus bolometric flux would offer a more direct effective-temperature determination, if obtainable.
