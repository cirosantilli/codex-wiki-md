<h1 id="s2/1/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

For two [isolated point](../../../../../isolated-point.md) masses in a Newtonian [bound orbit](../../../../../bound-orbit.md), $\boxed{P^2=4\pi^2a^3/[G(M_s+M_p)]}$, where $a$ is the relative [orbit](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md)'s [semimajor axis](../../../../../semi-major-axis.md). For a negligible planet [mass](../../../../../mass.md), using years, [astronomical units](../../../../../astronomical-unit.md) and solar masses gives $(P/\mathrm{yr})^2=(a/\mathrm{AU})^3/(M_s/M_\odot)$. The [mass](../../../../../mass.md) in the denominator is the total gravitating [mass](../../../../../mass.md), not the planet's [mass](../../../../../mass.md) alone.
