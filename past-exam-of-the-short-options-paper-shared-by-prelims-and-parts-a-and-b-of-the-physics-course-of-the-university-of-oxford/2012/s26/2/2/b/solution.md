↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A $5M_\odot$ star has a much greater luminosity and exhausts central hydrogen in roughly $10^8$ to $2\times10^8$ years, mainly through the [CNO cycle](../../../../../../cno-cycle.md). It crosses the [Hertzsprung gap](../../../../../../hertzsprung-gap.md), develops shell hydrogen burning and ignites core helium without the strongly degenerate helium flash of the solar-mass case. Core helium burning lasts roughly $10^7$ years, with possible blue-loop evolution depending on the model.

It subsequently develops a carbon-oxygen core and double-shell burning on the [Asymptotic giant branch](../../../../../../asymptotic-giant-branch.md), with thermal pulses, winds and envelope loss. For ordinary isolated evolution at this mass, the core does not reach sustained carbon ignition and the final remnant is a carbon-oxygen [white dwarf](../../../../../../white-dwarf.md). The broad boundary between white-dwarf and core-collapse outcomes depends on composition, overshooting, rotation and mass loss; the quoted mass is comfortably in the usual intermediate-mass regime.
