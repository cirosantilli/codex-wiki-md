↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A $15M_\odot$ star burns core hydrogen chiefly by the [CNO cycle](../../../../../../cno-cycle.md) for about $10^7$ years. It commonly evolves towards a [red supergiant](../../../../../../red-supergiant.md), though winds and mixing alter its track. Core helium burning lasts of order $10^6$ years. Continued contraction then permits carbon, neon, oxygen and silicon burning, producing successively heavier nuclei up to an iron-group core. Neutrino cooling makes the advanced phases much shorter: carbon burning can last thousands of years, while the final burning stages fall to years or days, with substantial model dependence.

An iron-group core cannot gain energy by ordinary fusion into heavier nuclei. Electron captures, photodisintegration and loss of pressure support lead to [stellar core collapse](../../../../../../stellar-core-collapse.md). A [core-collapse supernova](../../../../../../core-collapse-supernova.md) can eject the envelope and leave a [neutron star](../../../../../../neutron-star.md); strong fallback or a failed explosion can instead produce a [black hole](../../../../../../black-hole-split.md). **The massive-star endpoint involves core collapse, rather than the quiet carbon-oxygen white-dwarf cooling of the two lower-mass examples.**
