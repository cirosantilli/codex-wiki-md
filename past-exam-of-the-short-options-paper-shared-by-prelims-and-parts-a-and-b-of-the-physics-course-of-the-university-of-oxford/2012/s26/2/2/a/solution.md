↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A $1M_\odot$ star spends roughly $10^{10}$ years on the [main sequence](../../../../../../main-sequence.md), deriving most of its [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) from the [proton–proton chain](../../../../../../proton-proton-chain.md). After central hydrogen depletion, shell hydrogen burning around a contracting helium core expands it into a [red giant](../../../../../../red-giant.md). The low-mass helium core becomes electron-degenerate before the [helium flash](../../../../../../helium-flash.md). Subsequent core helium burning by the [Triple-alpha process](../../../../../../triple-alpha-process.md), with alpha capture producing oxygen, lasts of order $10^8$ years.

After central helium exhaustion, hydrogen and helium shells burn around a carbon-oxygen core on the [Asymptotic giant branch](../../../../../../asymptotic-giant-branch.md). Strong mass loss removes the envelope, potentially making a [planetary nebula](../../../../../../planetary-nebula.md), and the remnant becomes a carbon-oxygen [white dwarf](../../../../../../white-dwarf.md). It cools using stored thermal energy and does not ignite sustained carbon burning. Detailed durations depend on composition and mass loss; **the nuclear endpoint of the isolated solar-mass model is a white dwarf**.
