<h1 id="s26/2/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Assume homologous, gas-pressure-supported [main sequence](../../../../../main-sequence.md) stars with nearly constant composition and mean molecular weight $\mu$, radiative energy transport and approximately constant opacity $\kappa$. Hydrostatic and mass scalings give

$$
P_c\sim\frac{GM^2}{R^4},\qquad \rho_c\sim\frac M{R^3},\qquad T_c\sim\frac{\mu m_uGM}{k_{\rm B}R}.
$$

The [radiative diffusion](../../../../../radiative-diffusion.md) equation gives the luminosity scaling

$$
L\sim\frac{ac}{\kappa}\frac{RT_c^4}{\rho_c}\propto\frac{\mu^4}{\kappa}M^3.
$$

Therefore, under these explicit assumptions,

$$
\boxed{L\propto M^k,\qquad k=3.}
$$

The radius cancels. Radiation-pressure dominance in the most massive stars changes the relation towards the [Eddington luminosity](../../../../../eddington-luminosity.md) scaling $L\propto M$, so the cubic law is an idealized gas-pressure homology result rather than an exact universal massive-star law.
