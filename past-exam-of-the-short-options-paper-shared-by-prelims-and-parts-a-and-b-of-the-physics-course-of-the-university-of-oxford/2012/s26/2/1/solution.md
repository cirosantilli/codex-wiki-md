An [absorption line](../../../../../absorption-line.md) depends on the population of its lower atomic level, ionization state, transition strength, pressure and broadening, not just elemental abundance. [Boltzmann distribution](../../../../../boltzmann-distribution.md) changes excitation populations with [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), while the [Saha equation](../../../../../saha-ionization-equation.md) changes ionization with temperature and electron pressure. Thus similar overall composition can produce very different spectra. Molecules survive preferentially in cool atmospheres.

The [Harvard spectral classification](../../../../../harvard-spectral-classification.md) orders the main stellar classes by decreasing effective temperature: **O, B, A, F, G, K, M**, from blue to red. Numerical subclasses run approximately from 0, the hotter end of a class, to 9, its cooler end. An A0 star, near $10^4\,\mathrm K$, has very strong [Balmer series](../../../../../balmer-series.md) lines; hotter stars ionize much of the hydrogen and cooler stars underpopulate its $n=2$ level. A G2 star, near $5800\,\mathrm K$, has weaker Balmer absorption, many neutral and ionized metal lines, prominent Ca II H and K lines and the CH G band. An M5 star, near $3000\,\mathrm K$, has strong TiO molecular bands, neutral-metal and alkali features, and weak photospheric Balmer absorption.[temperature sequence and characteristic atomic or molecular features](https://voyages.sdss.org/expeditions/expedition-to-the-milky-way/spectral-types/absorption-and-emission-lines/)

For giant and dwarf stars of the same colour, compare gravity-sensitive line widths and ratios. The giant's larger radius gives lower surface gravity and atmospheric pressure, typically narrower pressure-broadened lines; high-pressure dwarf atmospheres have stronger broad alkali wings and different molecular and ionization ratios. These diagnostics assign a [luminosity class](../../../../../luminosity-class.md), such as III for a giant and V for a dwarf. A line ratio or calibrated gravity indicator is stronger evidence than colour alone.
