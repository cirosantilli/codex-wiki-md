<h1 id="s26/1/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the Sun's cool outer layers, partial ionization lowers the adiabatic temperature gradient, while large opacity makes radiative transport inefficient. The required radiative gradient

$$
\nabla_{\rm rad}=\frac{3\kappa L_rP}{16\pi acGm_rT^4}
$$

can exceed $\nabla_{\rm ad}$, so [convection](../../../../../../convection-split.md) carries part of the outward [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md). The Sun therefore has an outer [convective envelope](../../../../../../convective-envelope.md), above its primarily radiative interior. Here $a$ is the radiation constant, $\kappa$ opacity per unit mass and $m_r,L_r$ the enclosed mass and luminosity. Near the visible surface, radiative losses are again important and the flow need not be perfectly adiabatic.
