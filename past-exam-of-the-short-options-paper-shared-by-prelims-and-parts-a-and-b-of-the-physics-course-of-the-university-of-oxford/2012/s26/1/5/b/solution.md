<h1 id="s26/1/5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Near a red giant's compact core, intense nuclear burning in a small region can produce a large $L_r/m_r$ and a steep required radiative gradient. Where this exceeds the adiabatic gradient, [convection](../../../../../../convection-split.md) transports the released [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) and mixes fuel. Core helium burning is a clear example: the strongly temperature-sensitive [Triple-alpha process](../../../../../../triple-alpha-process.md) commonly produces a convective burning core; shell flashes can also make convective regions.[helium-burning core convection and its temperature-sensitive energy source](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201527171)

The qualification matters: an inert degenerate helium core and a steady hydrogen-burning shell on the first-ascent [red giant branch](../../../../../../red-giant-branch.md) are not universally convective; conduction or radiation may dominate there. Red giants also commonly have deep [convective envelopes](../../../../../../convective-envelope.md). **The physical condition is an excessive required radiative gradient, not proximity to the centre alone.**
