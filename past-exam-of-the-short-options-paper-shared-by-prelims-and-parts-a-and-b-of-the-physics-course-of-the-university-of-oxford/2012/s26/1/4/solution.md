<h1 id="s26/1/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Displace a small parcel upward, with negligible heat exchange, rapid pressure balance, and the same composition as its surroundings. Define the environmental gradient $\nabla=d\log T/d\log P$. An adiabatic [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) parcel obeys $P\rho^{-\gamma}=\mathrm{constant}$ and hence $T\propto P^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}$, giving $\nabla_{\rm ad}=(\gamma-1)/\gamma$.

The upward displacement has $\delta\log P<0$. If $\nabla<\nabla_{\rm ad}$, the parcel's temperature changes more negatively than the environment's. At their common [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) it is cooler, denser and driven back down. Therefore the [Schwarzschild criterion](../../../../../schwarzschild-criterion.md) for stability is

$$
\boxed{\nabla=\frac PT\frac{dT}{dP}<\nabla_{\rm ad}=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}.}
$$

The reversed inequality produces buoyant growth, and equality is neutrally stable in this approximation. A composition gradient requires the [Ledoux criterion](../../../../../ledoux-criterion.md); pressure equilibrium alone does not ensure equal mean molecular weight.
