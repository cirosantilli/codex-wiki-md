<h1 id="s26/1/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

For a fully ionized, nonrelativistic monatomic [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md), $\gamma=5/3$ and the [virial theorem](../../../../../virial-theorem.md) becomes

$$
\boxed{\Omega=-2U,\qquad E=U+\Omega=-U.}
$$

Since $U$ increases with its particle-weighted mean [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), the equilibrated self-gravitating object has [negative heat capacity](../../../../../negative-heat-capacity.md): adding total [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) allows expansion, makes $\Omega$ less negative, and reduces $U$. Conversely, radiating energy permits contraction and raises the internal temperature. This refers to a sequence of gravitational equilibria, not to the positive local heat capacity of a parcel at fixed volume.

In an ordinary nondegenerate nuclear-burning star, a rise in nuclear heating tends to expand and cool the burning region, lowering its temperature-sensitive reaction rate. Reduced heating leads to contraction and reheating. This expansion feedback is the nuclear thermostat; it can fail in degenerate matter where pressure responds weakly to temperature. During contraction of a [pre-main-sequence star](../../../../../pre-main-sequence-star.md), gravitational binding supplies the radiated [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md) and heats the interior. For a fixed-structure scaling, half the released gravitational energy increases $U$ and half escapes as radiation, until hydrogen burning can support a long-lived [main sequence](../../../../../main-sequence.md) equilibrium.
