<h1 id="s26/1/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

For a spherical star in [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md), $dP/dr=-Gm(r)\rho/r^2$. Multiply by $4\pi r^3dr$ and integrate to its radius $R$:

$$
\int_0^R4\pi r^3\frac{dP}{dr}\,dr=-\int_0^R\frac{Gm(r)}r\,dm=\Omega.
$$

Integration by parts gives $4\pi R^3P_s-3\int P\,dV=\Omega$. Neglecting the external surface pressure $P_s$, an [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) with constant [heat capacity ratio](../../../../../heat-capacity-ratio.md) $\gamma$ has thermal [energy density](../../../../../energy-density.md) $u=P/(\gamma-1)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{3(\gamma-1)U+\Omega=0,\qquad U=\int u\,dV.}
$$

This is the hydrostatic [virial theorem](../../../../../virial-theorem.md). A nonzero confining pressure retains the right-hand side $4\pi R^3P_s$; varying composition, radiation contributions or a spatially varying effective exponent require the integral relation, rather than pulling one $\gamma$ outside it.
