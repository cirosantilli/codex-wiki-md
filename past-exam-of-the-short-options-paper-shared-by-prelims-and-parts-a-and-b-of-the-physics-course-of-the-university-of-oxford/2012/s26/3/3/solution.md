<h1 id="s26/3/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Use the low-redshift [Hubble law](../../../../../hubble-s-law.md), neglecting peculiar velocity and cosmological distance corrections:

$$
\boxed{d\simeq\frac{cz}{H_0}\simeq211\,\mathrm{Mpc},\qquad
R\simeq d\frac{15}{206265}\simeq15.35\,\mathrm{kpc}.}
$$

The angular radius is $15$ arcseconds, half the stated major-axis diameter. A spherical dynamical estimate gives

$$
\boxed{M(<R)\simeq\frac{v_{\max}^2R}{G}\simeq2.59\times10^{11}M_\odot.}
$$

It estimates mass inside the observed disk radius, not the entire unobserved halo. Disk geometry changes the exact coefficient.

Using the same approximate distance for the [distance modulus](../../../../../distance-modulus.md),

$$
M_V=16-5\log_{10}\left(\frac{211\times10^6\,\mathrm{pc}}{10\,\mathrm{pc}}\right)\simeq-20.62,
$$



$$
\boxed{\frac{L_V}{L_{V,\odot}}=10^{0.4(4.8-M_V)}\simeq1.47\times10^{10},\qquad
\frac{M_{\rm dyn}}{L_V}\simeq17.6\frac{M_\odot}{L_{V,\odot}}.}
$$

For an illustrative stellar [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) of $1$--$5$ in solar $V$-band units, $M_*\sim1.5\times10^{10}$--$7.4\times10^{10}M_\odot$, below the dynamical estimate. This suggests substantial nonluminous matter under the adopted assumptions. Gas, stellar-population uncertainty, inclination, extinction and geometry also affect the comparison; luminosity alone cannot uniquely fix a stellar mass. The single-distance approximation at $z\simeq0.05$ is the intended introductory estimate.
