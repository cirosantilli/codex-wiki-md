↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Post-combustion [carbon capture and storage](../../../../../carbon-capture-and-storage.md) separates [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) from an already formed flue gas, often by absorption in a regenerable solvent. The recovered gas must be purified, dehydrated, compressed, transported and injected into a suitable geological store. Caprock integrity, trapping mechanisms, monitoring and long-term liability matter as much as capture at the chimney.

Dilute [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) mixed with nitrogen requires a large gas-processing plant. Solvent regeneration consumes heat; compression consumes power; impurities can cause solvent degradation and corrosion. Retrofitting adds equipment, cooling demand and cost, and lowers net electrical output per unit fuel. Storage capacity, injection performance, leakage risk and public acceptance must be demonstrated, while upstream fuel-cycle emissions remain.

A capture fraction is not the same as the reduction per delivered kilowatt-hour. For illustrative capture $\chi=0.9$ and a plant-efficiency decrease from $0.4$ to $0.3$, residual stack emissions per delivered unit scale as $(1-\chi)(0.4/0.3)=0.133$, corresponding to an $86.7\%$ reduction rather than $90\%$. A full [life-cycle assessment](../../../../../life-cycle-assessment.md) includes the additional fuel and upstream emissions. These figures illustrate the accounting and are not claimed as universal plant performance.
