<h1 id="s4/1/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Annual conversion is reduced by several multiplicative losses. Only roughly $40$–$50\%$ of incident sunlight lies in the photosynthetically useful visible band. Reflection and transmission prevent complete absorption; the photon budget and unavoidable relaxation losses limit conversion of absorbed radiation into chemical energy. [Photosynthesis](../../../../../photosynthesis.md) also has to support respiration and maintenance, while seasonal growth, incomplete canopy cover, water and nutrient limitations reduce the annual harvested yield.

An illustrative loss budget, not a universal biological constant, is $0.45\times0.8\times0.25\times0.5\times0.11\simeq0.005$. The factors represent useful spectrum, absorption, photochemical conversion, retained net production, and combined annual interception and growing-condition limitations, respectively. Thus $\boxed{\eta_{\rm annual}\sim0.5\%}$ is plausible for a land-averaged crop yield even though a leaf under favourable illumination can be more efficient. The result depends on crop, climate, harvested fraction and the definition of dry [biomass energy](../../../../../bioenergy.md). When the input is already annual mean insolation, night-time must not be counted as a second independent loss.
