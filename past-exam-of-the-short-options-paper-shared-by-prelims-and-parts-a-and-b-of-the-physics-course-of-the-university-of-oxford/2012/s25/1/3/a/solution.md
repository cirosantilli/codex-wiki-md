<h1 id="s25/1/3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the linear [Planck function](../../../../../../planck-function.md) profile, direct integration gives

$$
I_0=\pi B_0+\pi\alpha(1-e^{-\tau_s})\longrightarrow\boxed{\pi(B_0+\alpha)}
$$

in the optically thick limit. A sudden doubling of absorber abundance doubles [optical depth](../../../../../../optical-depth.md) at every physical height while initially leaving the [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) profile fixed. With $\widetilde\tau=2\tau$, the same profile is $B=B_0+(\alpha/2)\widetilde\tau$, not $B_0+\alpha\widetilde\tau$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\widetilde I_0=\pi(B_0+\alpha/2),\qquad \Delta I_0=-\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}.}
$$

If temperature increases downward, $\alpha>0$ and outgoing [spectral irradiance](../../../../../../spectral-irradiance.md) decreases immediately because the effective emitting region moves upward into cooler gas. Subsequent thermal adjustment is outside this instantaneous comparison.
