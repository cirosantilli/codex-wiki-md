<h1 id="s25/2/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

[Hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) balances the vertical [pressure gradient](../../../../../pressure-gradient.md) against weight, $dP/dz=-\rho g$. For a dry [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) parcel displaced slowly enough to maintain pressure balance but rapidly enough to exchange negligible [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md), the first law gives

$$
c_p\,dT-\frac1\rho\,dP=0.
$$

Combining the two equations yields the [dry adiabatic lapse rate](../../../../../dry-adiabatic-lapse-rate.md)

$$
\boxed{\Gamma_d=-\frac{dT}{dz}=\frac g{c_p}\simeq9.8\,\mathrm{K\,km^{-1}},}
$$

using $c_p\simeq1004\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$. Here [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) in the thermodynamic differential is absolute, $g$ and $c_p$ are treated as constant, composition is fixed, and condensation is excluded. Hydrostatic balance alone does not force the atmosphere to be adiabatic; the parcel assumption supplies the extra relation.

Water condensation releases [latent heat](../../../../../latent-heat.md), reducing the cooling of a rising saturated parcel and giving a smaller [moist adiabatic lapse rate](../../../../../moist-adiabatic-lapse-rate.md). Radiation, mixing, large-scale motion and incomplete convective adjustment also shape the environmental lapse rate. Thus an observed $6$--$7\,\mathrm{K\,km^{-1}}$ need not equal the dry parcel value and is broadly consistent with a moist, radiatively influenced atmosphere.
