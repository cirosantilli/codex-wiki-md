[Protein primary structure](../../../../../protein-primary-structure.md) is the ordered [amino acid](../../../../../amino-acid.md) sequence of a [polypeptide](../../../../../polypeptide.md), linked by covalent [peptide bonds](../../../../../peptide-bond.md). The approximately planar [peptide bond](../../../../../peptide-bond.md) restricts local geometry, while backbone torsion angles give conformational freedom subject to steric exclusion. [Protein secondary structure](../../../../../protein-secondary-structure.md) comprises local backbone arrangements, especially the [alpha helix](../../../../../alpha-helix.md), with backbone [hydrogen bonds](../../../../../hydrogen-bond.md) between residues $i$ and $i+4$, and the [beta sheet](../../../../../beta-sheet.md), whose strands are joined by backbone [hydrogen bonds](../../../../../hydrogen-bond.md). Turns and loops connect these elements. [Protein tertiary structure](../../../../../protein-tertiary-structure.md) is the full three-dimensional organization of a single chain, often including several [protein domains](../../../../../protein-domain.md). [Protein quaternary structure](../../../../../protein-quaternary-structure.md) is the arrangement of multiple chains in a [protein complex](../../../../../protein-complex.md).

[Protein folding](../../../../../protein-folding.md) is governed by the [Gibbs free energy](../../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md) of the chain together with its solvent, not by the chain's [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) alone. Folding reduces the chain's [conformational entropy](../../../../../conformational-entropy.md), but can gain solvent [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) through the [hydrophobic effect](../../../../../hydrophobic-effect.md), burying nonpolar side chains away from water. Favorable [hydrogen bonds](../../../../../hydrogen-bond.md), [Van der Waals forces](../../../../../van-der-waals-force.md), electrostatic interactions and sometimes covalent [disulfide bonds](../../../../../disulfide-bond.md) stabilize particular structures. Steric repulsion prevents atomic overlap. At fixed [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md), the native ensemble is favored when its total $\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta S$ is lower than competing ensembles. The balance depends on the solvent, [pH](../../../../../ph.md), salt and ligands. [Molecular chaperones](../../../../../chaperone-protein.md) help avoid aggregation or kinetic traps; a stable fold is not a perfectly rigid conformation, and some proteins have disordered regions.

For a soluble globular [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md), a typical pattern is a nonpolar core and a polar, hydrated surface. A [transmembrane protein](../../../../../transmembrane-protein.md) instead presents nonpolar side chains to the hydrocarbon interior of the [lipid bilayer](../../../../../lipid-bilayer.md). Membrane-spanning [alpha helices](../../../../../alpha-helix.md) satisfy their polar backbone groups through internal [hydrogen bonds](../../../../../hydrogen-bond.md); a roughly membrane-thickness helix commonly contains about twenty residues, depending on tilt and bilayer thickness. Multiple helices can enclose polar channels or binding sites. Membrane-spanning [beta barrels](../../../../../beta-barrel.md) likewise expose mainly nonpolar groups outward towards lipid while arranging backbone [hydrogen bonds](../../../../../hydrogen-bond.md) between strands and often lining an aqueous pore with polar groups. Exposed loops and domains contacting water can resemble soluble [proteins](../../../../../protein-split.md). Not every membrane-associated [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) spans the membrane: peripheral attachment and lipid anchoring are distinct possibilities.

**The organizing distinction is the environment of each surface: water favors accessible polar groups, while the membrane core favors outward-facing nonpolar groups.** Buried polar groups can still be essential to a transport pathway or catalytic site.
