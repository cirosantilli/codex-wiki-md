[Landau damping](../../../../../landau-damping.md) is the collisionless decay of a plasma wave through resonant energy exchange with particles whose velocity is close to the wave's [phase velocity](../../../../../phase-velocity.md) $v_\phi=\omega/k$. Particles slightly slower than the wave tend to gain energy from it; particles slightly faster can give energy to it. For the decreasing resonant part of a [Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution](../../../../../maxwell-boltzmann-distribution.md), the net transfer is from the wave to the electrons, so the wave amplitude decays.

In the linear [Vlasov equation](../../../../../vlasov-equation.md) description this is [collisionless phase mixing](../../../../../collisionless-phase-mixing.md), not a requirement for frequent particle collisions. Fine-scale structure develops in the velocity dependence of the perturbation even while the macroscopic electric field decreases. The resonant contribution to the growth rate has the sign of $\partial f/\partial v$ at a positive wave phase velocity: a negative slope produces damping, whereas a sufficiently positive slope can drive an instability. A population comparison must be local to the resonance; merely counting all particles faster and slower than the wave is not the criterion.
