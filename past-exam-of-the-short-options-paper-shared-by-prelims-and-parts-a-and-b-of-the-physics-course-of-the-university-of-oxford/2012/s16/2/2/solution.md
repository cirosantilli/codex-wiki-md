<h1 id="s16/2/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The [Debye length](../../../../../debye-length.md) is the distance over which a small, slowly varying electrostatic potential is screened by mobile thermal charges. For classical electrons in a weak potential $\phi$, their [Boltzmann distribution](../../../../../boltzmann-distribution.md) gives

$$
n_e(\phi)=n_0e^{e\phi/(k_{\rm B}T)}\simeq n_0\left(1+\frac{e\phi}{k_{\rm B}T}\right).
$$

Take fixed singly charged ions of density $n_0$. Then $\rho=e(n_0-n_e)\simeq-n_0e^2\phi/(k_{\rm B}T)$, and [Poisson's equation](../../../../../poisson-s-equation.md) outside a test charge becomes $\nabla^2\phi=\phi/\lambda_D^2$, with

$$
\boxed{\lambda_D=\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_0k_{\rm B}T}{n_ee^2}}.}
$$

The screened point-charge potential is proportional to $e^{-r/\lambda_D}/r$. The derivation assumes nondegenerate statistics and $|e\phi|\ll k_{\rm B}T$. Mobile ions would add their own contribution to $\lambda_D^{-2}$; the expression here is the electron screening length.
