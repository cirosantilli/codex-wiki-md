<h1 id="s16/3/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Low excited states lie many thermal energies above the ground state at the inferred temperature, so their contributions to the atomic internal partition function are exponentially suppressed. For hydrogen, $E_2-E_1=10.2$ eV and the level degeneracy ratio is $g_2/g_1=4$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\frac{N_{n=2}}{N_{n=1}}=4e^{-10.2\,\mathrm{eV}/(k_{\rm B}T)}\simeq6.8\times10^{-15}.}
$$

The ground-state approximation is therefore excellent for this level under the assumed thermal-equilibrium conditions. A formal sum over infinitely many isolated-atom Rydberg states needs a physical high-level cutoff in a plasma; it should not be used to overturn the finite-level Boltzmann estimate. Radiation-driven nonequilibrium populations would require a different model.
