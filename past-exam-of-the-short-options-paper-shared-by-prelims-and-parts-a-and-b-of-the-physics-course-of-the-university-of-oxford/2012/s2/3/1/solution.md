<h1 id="s2/3/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Measure the absolute [spectral radiance](../../../../../spectral-radiance.md) over a range of [frequencies](../../../../../frequency.md) and fit the [Planck spectrum](../../../../../blackbody-spectrum.md)

$$
B_\nu(T)=\frac{2h\nu^3}{c^2}\frac1{e^{h\nu/(k_BT)}-1}.
$$

For example, the [COBE FIRAS blackbody spectrum experiment](../../../../../cobe-firas-blackbody-spectrum-experiment.md) used a differential [Michelson interferometer](../../../../../michelson-interferometer.md) to compare the sky with an internal [blackbody](../../../../../blackbody.md); an external calibrated [blackbody](../../../../../blackbody.md) could cover the sky entrance. Cold [bolometers](../../../../../bolometer.md) measured the difference spectrum, and foreground separation distinguished [Milky Way](../../../../../milky-way.md) emission from the nearly isotropic background. The fitted background temperature is approximately $\boxed{T_0=2.73\,\mathrm K}$.[Instrument overview: differential internal reference, external calibrator and absolute CMB temperature](../../../../../the-cobe-far-infrared-absolute-spectrophotometer-by-nasa-lambda.md) The result relies on an absolute spectral comparison, rather than merely measuring a map's hot-to-cold contrast.
