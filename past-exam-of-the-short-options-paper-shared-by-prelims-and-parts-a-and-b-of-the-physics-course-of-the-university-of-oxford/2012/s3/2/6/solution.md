<h1 id="s3/2/6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

For Coulomb binding, the [virial theorem](../../../../../virial-theorem.md) gives a typical [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) of the order of the binding energy. Taking $p_{\rm typ}\simeq\sqrt{2m_e|E_1|}$ and using the [Heisenberg uncertainty relation](../../../../../heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md) estimate $r_{\rm typ}\sim\hbar/p_{\rm typ}$ gives

$$
\boxed{r_{\rm typ}\sim\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m_e(54.4\,\mathrm{eV})}}
=2.65\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm m.}
$$

The [Bohr model](../../../../../bohr-model.md) gives $r_1=a_0/Z=a_0/2=2.65\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm m$ for a heavy helium nucleus, agreeing with the characteristic scale. Equivalently, minimizing the localization estimate $E(r)\sim\hbar^2/(2m_er^2)-Ze^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0r)$ gives that same scale.

This is an order-of-magnitude radius estimate, not an exact deduction of the cloud's [expected value](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) from the inequality alone. Numerical coefficients depend on which spread is used; $\Delta x\Delta p\ge\hbar/2$ does not assert $\langle r\rangle=\hbar/p$. For the exact hydrogenic $1s$ state, $\langle r\rangle=3a_0/(2Z)=3.97\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm m$ at $Z=2$, one and a half times the Bohr orbital radius. An electron cloud has a radial distribution, whereas the [Bohr model](../../../../../bohr-model.md) assigns a definite radius.
