↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

For a [photon](../../../../../photon.md), $p_\gamma=E/c$ and $\lambda_\gamma=hc/E$. The [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) is nonrelativistic here because $18.8\,\mathrm{keV}\ll m_nc^2$, so $p_n=\sqrt{2m_nE}$ and $\lambda_n=h/p_n$. Using the supplied common energy gives

$$
\boxed{p_\gamma=1.005\times10^{-23}\,\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}},\quad
\lambda_\gamma=6.595\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm m,}
$$



$$
\boxed{p_n=3.176\times10^{-21}\,\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}},\quad
\lambda_n=2.086\times10^{-13}\,\mathrm m.}
$$

Equal [kinetic energies](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) do not imply equal momenta: the massive particle obeys $E=p^2/(2m_n)$, while the massless [photon](../../../../../photon.md) obeys $E=pc$. The [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) momentum is about $316$ times larger, so its [de Broglie wavelength](../../../../../de-broglie-wavelength.md) is about $316$ times smaller.

To give the [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) the photon wavelength, set its momentum to $E/c$, not its [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) to $E$ again. Then

$$
\boxed{v_n\simeq\frac{E}{m_nc}=5.999\times10^3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

Its [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) would then be about $0.188\,\mathrm{eV}$, connecting this comparison with the next calculation.
