<h1 id="s18/1/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Let $U(x)=e^{ie\alpha(x)}$, $\psi'=U\psi$, and $A'_\mu=A_\mu+\partial_\mu\alpha$. Direct differentiation in the [gauge covariant derivative](../../../../../gauge-covariant-derivative.md) gives

$$
D'_\mu\psi'=(\partial_\mu-ieA'_\mu)(U\psi)
=U\left[\partial_\mu+ie\partial_\mu\alpha-ieA_\mu-ie\partial_\mu\alpha\right]\psi
=UD_\mu\psi.
$$

The gradient of the local phase is exactly cancelled by the potential transformation. Since $U$ is scalar in spinor space and commutes with the [Dirac matrices](../../../../../dirac-matrices.md),

$$
\boxed{(i\gamma^\mu D'_\mu-m)\psi'=U(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu-m)\psi.}
$$

Consequently a solution is mapped into a solution: the minimally coupled [Dirac equation](../../../../../dirac-equation.md) is covariant under the local [U(1) gauge symmetry](../../../../../u-1-gauge-symmetry.md). The operator and wavefunction both transform; ordinary differentiation of the transformed wavefunction alone would leave an extra phase-gradient term.
