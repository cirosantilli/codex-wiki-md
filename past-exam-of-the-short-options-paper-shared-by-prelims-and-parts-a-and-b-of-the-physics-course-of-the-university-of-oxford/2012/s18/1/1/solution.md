<h1 id="s18/1/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Work with $\eta={\rm diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ and $\not\!\partial=\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu$. Multiplying the free [Dirac equation](../../../../../dirac-equation.md) by the operator with the opposite mass sign gives

$$
(i\not\!\partial+m)(i\not\!\partial-m)\psi
=\left[-\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\partial_\mu\partial_\nu-m^2\right]\psi=0.
$$

The ordinary derivatives commute, so only the symmetric part of $\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu$ contributes. Requiring this operator to factor the [Klein-Gordon equation](../../../../../klein-gordon-equation.md), $(\partial^\mu\partial_\mu+m^2)\psi=0$, for arbitrary free momenta gives

$$
\boxed{\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}I,\qquad\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}=2\eta_{\mu\nu}I.}
$$

Thus the [Dirac matrices](../../../../../dirac-matrices.md) represent the corresponding [Clifford algebra](../../../../../clifford-algebra.md). The inference uses factorization for the general free equation; the fact that one specially chosen wavefunction happens to solve both equations would not alone determine every matrix anticommutator.
