<h1 id="s18/2/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Far from a short-range potential, the particle is locally free. The imposed incoming boundary condition is a unit-amplitude plane wave travelling along $+z$, while an outgoing free spherical wave has radial dependence $e^{ikr}/r$. The scattering coefficient can depend on direction, giving

$$
\psi\sim e^{ikz}+f_k(\theta,\phi)\frac{e^{ikr}}r.
$$

For the Schrödinger [probability current](../../../../../probability-current.md), $\mathbf j=(\hbar/m){\rm Im}(\psi^*\boldsymbol\nabla\psi)$, the incident flux is $j_{\rm in}=\hbar k/m$. The outgoing scattered radial flux, separated from the incident beam, is $j_{\rm sc,r}\sim(\hbar k/m)|f_k|^2/r^2$. Thus the outgoing rate through $r^2d\Omega$, divided by incident flux, gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{j_{\rm sc,r}r^2}{j_{\rm in}}=|f_k(\theta,\phi)|^2.}
$$

This elastic-scattering result uses the same incoming and outgoing wavenumber and the usual outgoing-wave boundary condition.
