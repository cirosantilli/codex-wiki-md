<h1 id="s18/3/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Assume the scalar potential is real, so the [Weyl equation](../../../../../weyl-equation.md) has a Hermitian Hamiltonian $H=\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf p+V$. Multiplying the equation and its adjoint by $\psi^\dagger$ and $\psi$ respectively gives

$$
\partial_t(\psi^\dagger\psi)=-\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot(\psi^\dagger\boldsymbol\sigma\psi).
$$

Thus with $\rho=\psi^\dagger\psi$ and $\mathbf J=\psi^\dagger\boldsymbol\sigma\psi$, the lower-index four-current $j_\mu=(\rho,-\mathbf J)$ obeys $\boxed{\partial^\mu j_\mu=0}$.

For $K=\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf p$,

$$
[K,H]=[K,V]=-i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\boldsymbol\nabla V.
$$

Consequently $K$ is conserved for spatially constant $V$; a spatially uniform time-dependent scalar offset would also commute with it. For general nonuniform $V$ it is not conserved. At nonzero momentum, $(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf p})^2=I$, so the [helicity](../../../../../helicity.md) eigenvalues are $\boxed{+1,-1}$. In the chosen constant-potential Weyl Hamiltonian, the positive $E-V$ branch has helicity $+1$; the negative branch has $-1$. An expectation value in a superposition need not itself be an eigenvalue.
