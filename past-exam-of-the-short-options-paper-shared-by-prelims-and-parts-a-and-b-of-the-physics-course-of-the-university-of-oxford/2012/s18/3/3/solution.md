<h1 id="s18/3/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Evaluate the envelopes at their displayed arguments and set $a=\eta^*\rho$. The common phase $e^{-iV_0t}$ cancels from every bilinear. Direct use of the [Pauli matrices](../../../../../pauli-matrices.md) gives

$$
\boxed{j_\mu=\left(|\eta|^2+|\rho|^2,\;-2\operatorname{Re}a,\;-2\operatorname{Im}a,\;|\rho|^2-|\eta|^2\right).}
$$

The physical longitudinal current is $J_z=|\eta|^2-|\rho|^2$: the two density profiles move in opposite directions. Their separate transport identities give $\partial_t(|\eta|^2+|\rho|^2)+\partial_z(|\eta|^2-|\rho|^2)=0$.

For a coherent superposition, the transverse currents $J_x=2\operatorname{Re}(\eta^*\rho)$ and $J_y=2\operatorname{Im}(\eta^*\rho)$ generally do not vanish. They are independent of $x,y$, so they contribute no divergence in this one-dimensional construction. Omitting them would give an incorrect local four-current. Pointwise, $|\mathbf J|^2=(|\eta|^2+|\rho|^2)^2$, so the pure two-component Weyl current is future null wherever the density is nonzero. If only one envelope is present, the current points entirely along its propagation direction; an incoherent average can remove the transverse interference terms.
