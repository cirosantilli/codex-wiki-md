<h1 id="s19/2/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Relativistic radiation is concentrated into an angle of order $1/\gamma$ around the instantaneous [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md). The emission direction sweeps past a tangent observer over an orbital interval of order $\rho/(\gamma c)$. Since the charge moves toward the observer, arrival times are compressed by an additional factor of order $1/\gamma^2$, giving the spectral scale $\omega\sim c\gamma^3/\rho$.

The conventional numerical factor follows from the radiation Fourier phase. Near the tangent direction, circular motion gives

$$
\omega\left(t-\frac{\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf r(t)}c\right)\simeq\omega\left(\frac{t}{2\gamma^2}+\frac{c^2t^3}{6\rho^2}\right).
$$

With $u=c\gamma t/\rho$, this becomes $[\omega\rho/(2c\gamma^3)](u+u^3/3)$. The resulting Airy-type integrals are expressible through modified Bessel functions with tangent-plane argument $\xi=\omega\rho/(3c\gamma^3)$. The standard angle-integrated [synchrotron radiation](../../../../../synchrotron-radiation.md) spectrum has shape

$$
F(x)=x\int_x^\infty K_{5/3}(z)\,dz,\qquad x=\frac\omega{\omega_c},\qquad\xi=\frac{x}{2}.
$$

It therefore identifies

$$
\boxed{\omega_c=\frac{3c\gamma^3}{2\rho},\qquad\epsilon_c=\hbar\omega_c=\frac32\frac{\hbar c}{\rho}\gamma^3.}
$$

The beaming argument supplies the physical scaling; the spectral integral fixes the conventional factor $3/2$. Critical energy is a broad spectral scale, not a hard photon-energy cutoff.[Critical energy and critical mode number](../../../../../synchrotron-radiation-in-the-geant4-physics-reference-manual-by-geant4-collaboration.md)
