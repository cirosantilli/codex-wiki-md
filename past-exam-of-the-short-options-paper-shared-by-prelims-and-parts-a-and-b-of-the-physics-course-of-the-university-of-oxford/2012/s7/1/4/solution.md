<h1 id="s7/1/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

It can describe the motion per unit mass of a charged particle in the azimuthal [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) of a long straight current-carrying wire. Choose magnetic [vector potential](../../../../../vector-potential.md)

$$
\mathbf A=-\frac{mC}{q}\log(R/R_0)\,\hat{\mathbf z}.
$$

Then $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A=(mC/(qR))\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}$ and $(T+q\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf v)/m$ is the given [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md). In terms of a straight-wire current $I$, $B_\phi=\mu_0I/(2\pi R)$, so $C=q\mu_0I/(2\pi m)$ with signs fixed by charge and current direction.

A static magnetic force does no work, consistent with the conserved [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md). Its radial component is $-q\dot zB_\phi$ and its axial component is $q\dot RB_\phi$, reproducing the radial and axial couplings above. The reference length in the logarithm is a gauge convention, not a new physical wire radius in the ideal exterior-field model.
