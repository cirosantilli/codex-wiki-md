<h1 id="s7/3/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Repeat the expansion in the strict-invariance proof, but now use $\delta L/\epsilon=dF/dt$. Along Euler-Lagrange trajectories,

$$
\frac d{dt}\left(\sum_ip_iK_i\right)=\frac{dF}{dt}.
$$

It follows that the conserved Noether quantity is

$$
\boxed{Q=\sum_ip_iK_i-F.}
$$

The minus sign matters. It subtracts the boundary term created by the symmetry transformation. More explicitly, before using the equations of motion one has

$$
\frac{\delta L}{\epsilon}
=\frac d{dt}(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf K)
+\sum_i\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}-\dot p_i\right)K_i.
$$

The final sum vanishes on shell, proving the result for arbitrary coordinate-dependent $\mathbf K(\mathbf q)$ under the source's fixed-time assumptions. It is this boundary-term form, rather than the uncorrected canonical momentum alone, that applies to translations in the magnetic example.
