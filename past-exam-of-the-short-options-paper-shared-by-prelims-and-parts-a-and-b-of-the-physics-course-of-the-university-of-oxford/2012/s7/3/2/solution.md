<h1 id="s7/3/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The canonical momentum for the stated symmetric-gauge [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md) is

$$
\mathbf p=m\dot{\mathbf x}+\frac q2\mathbf B\times\mathbf x,
\qquad
\frac{\partial L}{\partial\mathbf x}=\frac q2\dot{\mathbf x}\times\mathbf B.
$$

Since $\mathbf B$ is uniform and time independent, its [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) becomes

$$
m\ddot{\mathbf x}+\frac q2\mathbf B\times\dot{\mathbf x}
-\frac q2\dot{\mathbf x}\times\mathbf B=0,
\qquad\boxed{m\ddot{\mathbf x}=q\dot{\mathbf x}\times\mathbf B.}
$$

This is the magnetic [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md), which is unchanged by constant spatial translations. The [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md) itself changes by

$$
\delta L=\epsilon\frac q2\dot{\mathbf x}\cdot(\mathbf B\times\hat{\mathbf n})
=\epsilon\frac{dF_{\mathbf n}}{dt},\qquad
\boxed{F_{\mathbf n}=\frac q2\mathbf x\cdot(\mathbf B\times\hat{\mathbf n}).}
$$

A total time derivative changes the action only by $\epsilon[F_{\mathbf n}(t_f)-F_{\mathbf n}(t_i)]$. For variations with fixed endpoints this boundary contribution has zero variation, so the equations of motion are unchanged. The translation is therefore a symmetry of the dynamics even though this particular gauge makes the [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md) only quasi-invariant.
