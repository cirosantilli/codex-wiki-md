[Hamilton's equations](../../../../../hamilton-s-equations.md) are $\dot q_i=H_{p_i}$ and $\dot p_i=-H_{q_i}$. Using the elementary brackets from the previous part gives

$$
\boxed{\dot q_i=\{q_i,H\},\qquad\dot p_i=\{p_i,H\}.}
$$

A [constant of motion](../../../../../conserved-quantity.md) is a function that stays unchanged along each physical trajectory. For a function $C(\mathbf q,\mathbf p)$ with no explicit time dependence, the chain rule and Hamilton's equations give

$$
\frac{dC}{dt}=\sum_i(C_{q_i}\dot q_i+C_{p_i}\dot p_i)
=\{C,H\}.
$$

Thus $\boxed{C\text{ is conserved}\iff\{C,H\}=0}$ on the accessible phase-space domain. If a function explicitly depends on time, the criterion is instead $\partial_tC+\{C,H\}=0$; the simpler statement uses the source's time-independent $C$ assumption.
