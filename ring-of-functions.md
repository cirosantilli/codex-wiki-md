# Ring of functions

↑ **Parent:** [Direct product of rings](direct-product-of-rings.md)

For a [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md) $k$ and a [set](set-split.md) $X$, the set $k^X$ of all [functions](function-split.md) $X\to k$ is a [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md) under pointwise addition and multiplication. It is the [direct product of rings](direct-product-of-rings.md) with one copy of $k$ for each $x\in X$.

If $k$ is a [field](field.md) and $X$ is finite, every [ideal](ideal.md) is generated by some of the singleton [indicator functions](indicator-function.md) $e_x$. Indeed, whenever $f$ in the [ideal](ideal.md) has $f(x)\ne0$, multiplication by $e_x/f(x)$ puts $e_x$ in the [ideal](ideal.md). Every [function](function-split.md) in that [ideal](ideal.md) is a finite sum of its coordinate multiples of these $e_x$.

If $X$ is infinite and $k$ is a nonzero [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md), the [functions](function-split.md) with finite [support](support.md) form an [ideal](ideal.md) without a finite [generating set of an ideal](generating-set-of-an-ideal.md). A finite collection of such [functions](function-split.md) vanishes outside one finite subset, as does the [ideal](ideal.md) it generates, whereas a singleton [indicator function](indicator-function.md) outside that subset belongs to the [ideal](ideal.md) of all finitely supported [functions](function-split.md). Thus, for a [field](field.md) $k$, $k^X$ is a [Noetherian ring](noetherian-ring.md) exactly when $X$ is finite.
