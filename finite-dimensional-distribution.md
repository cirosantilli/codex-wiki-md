# Finite-dimensional distribution

↑ **Parent:** [Stochastic process](stochastic-process-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-dimensional_distribution)

For times $t_1,\ldots,t_n$, the corresponding finite-dimensional distribution of a [stochastic process](stochastic-process-split.md) $X$ is the [joint probability distribution](joint-probability-distribution.md) of the [random vector](random-vector.md) $(X_{t_1},\ldots,X_{t_n})$. The collection of these distributions records every finite set of coordinates of the process.

Knowing only the one-time [marginal distributions](marginal-distribution.md) is insufficient. Let $X_n$ be independent [standard normal random variables](standard-normal-random-variable.md), and let $Y_n=Z$ for every $n$, where $Z$ is one [standard normal random variable](standard-normal-random-variable.md). Every $X_n$ and $Y_n$ has the same [standard normal distribution](standard-normal-distribution.md). Nevertheless, for $m\ne n$, the difference $X_n-X_m$ has a [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md) with [variance](variance-split.md) two, whereas $Y_n-Y_m=0$. The [joint probability distribution](joint-probability-distribution.md) of $(Y_m,Y_n)$ is concentrated on the diagonal; that of $(X_m,X_n)$ is spread across the plane. A model for tomorrow's value therefore needs the relation between observations as well as their separate distributions.

![](covariance-process-comparison.png)

**[Figure 2](#image-identical-standard-normal-marginals-can-give-independent-histories-or-perfectly-shared-histories). Identical standard normal marginals can give independent histories or perfectly shared histories**.

For a real-valued [stochastic process](stochastic-process-split.md), its [finite-dimensional distributions](finite-dimensional-distribution.md) determine its [probability law](probability-distribution-split.md) on the [product sigma-algebra](product-sigma-algebra.md). With an uncountable [index set](index-set.md), a path property such as [continuity](continuous-function.md) need not be measurable in that [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md); specifying a suitable space of [sample paths](sample-path.md) and a regular [modification of a stochastic process](modification-of-a-stochastic-process.md) is a separate step. This distinction is explained in [MIT, The basics of stochastic processes, section 1.1](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-436j-fundamentals-of-probability-fall-2018/b9342e718ec4c58d98ec97dcfcb0d56e_MIT6_436JF18_lec20.pdf).
