# Nuclear elastic electron scattering cross-section

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear charge form factor](nuclear-charge-form-factor.md)

For an ultrarelativistic [electron](electron.md) scattered elastically by a static, spherical, spin-zero [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md), a plane-wave [Born approximation](born-approximation.md) retains a single electromagnetic interaction. The [Fourier transform](fourier-transform-split.md) of its [Coulomb potential](coulomb-potential-energy.md) is proportional to $ZeF(q)/q^2$. Relative to the same point charge, the scattering amplitude is therefore multiplied by $F(q)$ and the [differential scattering cross-section](differential-scattering-cross-section.md) by $|F(q)|^2$:

$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\simeq
\left(\frac{Z\alpha\hbar c}{2E}\right)^2
\frac{\cos^2(\theta/2)}{\sin^4(\theta/2)}|F(q)|^2,
\qquad q\simeq\frac{2E}{\hbar c}\sin\frac\theta2.
$$

Here $E\gg m_ec^2$ is the incident total [electron](electron.md) [energy](energy-split.md), $\theta$ the [scattering angle](scattering-angle.md), $d\Omega$ a [solid angle](solid-angle.md) element, and $\alpha$ the [fine-structure constant](fine-structure-constant.md). The cosine factor already accounts for the projectile's [spin](spin.md); a spinless projectile would give a different point-charge factor. The formula neglects [nuclear recoil](nuclear-recoil.md), electron rest-mass corrections, [radiative corrections](radiative-correction.md) and [Coulomb distortion in electron scattering](coulomb-distortion-in-electron-scattering.md). The latter can be substantial at large $Z\alpha$: electron waves then require treatment in the target's [Coulomb potential](coulomb-potential-energy.md), rather than undistorted plane waves.

Nonzero target [spin](spin.md) can introduce magnetic and additional electric contributions, so division by this point-charge expression does not generally isolate one charge form factor for a [proton](proton.md) or [deuteron](deuteron.md). Even for spin zero, finite [energy](energy-split.md) resolution, recoil and [Coulomb distortion in electron scattering](coulomb-distortion-in-electron-scattering.md) can change a measured minimum. A zero of the uniform-sphere model is a clear illustration of amplitude cancellation, not a universal prediction of a zero experimental counting rate.
