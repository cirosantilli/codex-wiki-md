# Differential geometry of surfaces

↑ **Parent:** [Differential geometry](differential-geometry-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_geometry_of_surfaces)

The differential geometry of [smooth surfaces](smooth-surface.md) studies their induced [Riemannian metrics](riemannian-metric.md) and intrinsic or extrinsic [curvature](curvature.md). The [first fundamental form](first-fundamental-form.md) measures [lengths](length.md), while the [second fundamental form](second-fundamental-form-split.md) and [shape operator](shape-operator.md) describe bending in an ambient [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md). A regular [parametric surface](parametric-surface.md) $X(u,v)$ must have linearly independent [tangent vectors](tangent-vector.md) $X_u,X_v$: otherwise its parameters cannot provide a [manifold chart](manifold-chart.md) with two independent directions.

For a [sphere](sphere.md) of radius $R>0$, use colatitude $0<\theta<\pi$ and longitude $\phi$:

$$
X(\theta,\phi)=R(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta),\qquad I=R^2d\theta^2+R^2\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2.
$$

Thus a change $d\phi$ has [arc length](arc-length.md) $R\sin\theta\,|d\phi|$: equally spaced longitudes draw closer together toward a pole. The coordinate degeneracy at the poles belongs to this parametrization, not to the [smooth surface](smooth-surface.md).

Both [principal curvatures](principal-curvature.md) of the [sphere](sphere.md) have magnitude $1/R$ and the same sign, giving [Gaussian curvature](gaussian-curvature.md) $K=1/R^2$. A [plane](plane.md) has $K=0$, and a [circular cylinder](circular-cylinder.md) also has $K=0$ because one [principal curvature](principal-curvature.md) vanishes. These three familiar shapes show why visible bending alone cannot measure intrinsic [Gaussian curvature](gaussian-curvature.md).
