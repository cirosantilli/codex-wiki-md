# Cell nucleus

↑ **Parent:** [Biological cell](biological-cell.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus)

The [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md) is a membrane-bounded compartment characteristic of [eukaryotic](eukaryote.md) [biological cells](biological-cell.md), containing most of the cellular [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) in a typical nucleated [biological cell](biological-cell.md). Its double [nuclear envelope](nuclear-envelope.md) separates this [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) from the surrounding [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md). Most does not mean all: [mitochondria](mitochondria.md) and [chloroplasts](chloroplast.md) retain their own [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md). Nor does every specialized [eukaryotic](eukaryote.md) [biological cell](biological-cell.md) retain a [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md): mature mammalian [red blood cells](red-blood-cell.md) have lost theirs. [Prokaryotes](prokaryote.md) organize their [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) in a [nucleoid](nucleoid.md) without a [nuclear envelope](nuclear-envelope.md), and still have [ribosomes](ribosome.md). See [NHGRI: nucleus](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Nucleus), [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 23.1: organelle DNA](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/23-1-eukaryotic-origins) and [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 40.2: mature mammalian red blood cells](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/40-2-components-of-the-blood).

Follow one nuclear [protein](protein.md)-coding [gene](gene.md): its [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) is used in [transcription](transcription-biology.md) to make [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md); after the appropriate processing, [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) is exported through a [nuclear pore complex](nuclear-pore-complex.md); a [ribosome](ribosome.md) in the [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md) then uses it in [biological translation](translation-biology.md) to build an [amino acid](amino-acid.md) chain. The [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) remains in the [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md). The [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) is a template, not the material converted into a [protein](protein.md). The [ribosome](ribosome.md) diagram below follows this route; [gene expression](gene-expression.md) gives a complementary [nucleotide sequence](nucleic-acid-sequence.md) example. See [Alberts et al.: selective RNA export and protein import](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26932/).

This architecture makes [gene expression](gene-expression.md) interesting in a spatial way: nuclear [transcription](transcription-biology.md) and cytoplasmic [biological translation](translation-biology.md) are separated by a selective boundary. A [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) can be processed before export and use, while [proteins](protein.md) needed for nuclear functions travel inward. Inside the [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md), the [cell nucleolus](nucleolus-biology.md) helps assemble [ribosome](ribosome.md) subunits for export. Thus the compartment housing most [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) also helps build the machinery that interprets its messages elsewhere.

Robert Brown described and named the [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md) in his account of [plant](plant.md) [biological cells](biological-cell.md), read to the Linnean Society in 1831 and published in its Transactions in 1833. This historical observation identified a recurring structure, without establishing its later-understood role in [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) storage or [gene expression](gene-expression.md). See [Brown, On the Organs and Mode of Fecundation in Orchideæ and Asclepiadeæ, Transactions 16, 685–738 (1833)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1095-8339.1829.tb00158.x). This is a documented contribution rather than a claim that no one had observed the structure earlier.
