# Euler equation in a uniformly rotating frame

↑ **Parent:** [Euler equations for an inviscid fluid](euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid-split.md)

For constant [mass density](density.md) $\rho_0$, a frame with constant [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) $\boldsymbol\Omega$ absorbs [centrifugal acceleration](centrifugal-acceleration.md) into reduced [pressure](pressure-split.md) $\Pi=p/\rho_0-|\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r|^2/2$. Indeed $\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r)=-\nabla|\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r|^2/2$. Transforming the [material acceleration](material-acceleration.md) in the [Euler equations](euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid-split.md) gives the displayed equation. The [Coriolis acceleration](coriolis-acceleration.md) remains velocity dependent and cannot be absorbed into a scalar potential. A conservative physical [body force](body-force.md) can be included by adding its potential to $\Pi$.
