# Term test for divergence

↑ **Parent:** [Series (mathematics)](series-mathematics.md)

The [term test for divergence](term-test-for-divergence.md) says that a [series](series-mathematics.md) cannot converge if its terms fail to tend to zero. Indeed, if its [partial sums](partial-sum.md) satisfy $S_n\to S$, then $a_n=S_n-S_{n-1}\to S-S=0$. This is a necessary condition for a [convergent series](convergent-series.md).

The converse fails: $1/n\to0$, but the [harmonic series](harmonic-series.md) diverges. For a quick successful test, $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n/(n+1)$ diverges because its terms tend to one. The test gives no conclusion for $\sum1/n$ or $\sum1/[n(n+1)]$: both have terms tending to zero, yet only the second is a [convergent series](convergent-series.md). The [telescoping series](telescoping-series.md) calculation and the [harmonic series](harmonic-series.md) block estimate resolve those cases.
