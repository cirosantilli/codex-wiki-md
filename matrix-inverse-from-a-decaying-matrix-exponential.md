# Matrix inverse from a decaying matrix exponential

↑ **Parent:** [Matrix exponential](matrix-exponential.md)

If every [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) of a finite square [matrix](matrix.md) $A$ has negative [real part](real-part.md), its [matrix exponential](matrix-exponential.md) decays and its integral converges. The [Jordan normal form](jordan-normal-form.md) shows that polynomial factors in $t$ are dominated by exponential decay. Integrating $d(e^{tA})/dt=Ae^{tA}$ gives $A\int_0^\infty e^{tA}dt=-I$, proving the displayed [matrix inverse](matrix-inverse-split.md) formula. In particular, for a [Hermitian matrix](hermitian-operator.md) with negative [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md), the [spectral theorem](spectral-theorem.md) reduces the integral to scalar exponentials with integrals $-1/\lambda_j$. The minus sign is essential: the integral is positive definite in this Hermitian case, while $A^{-1}$ has negative [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md).
