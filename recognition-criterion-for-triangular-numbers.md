# Recognition criterion for triangular numbers

↑ **Parent:** [Triangular number](triangular-number.md)

A [nonnegative integer](natural-number.md) $M$ is a [triangular number](triangular-number.md) exactly when $8M+1$ is a [perfect square](square-number.md). Its [square root](square-root.md) is then an [odd integer](odd-integer.md), and the row index is

$$
n=\frac{\sqrt{8M+1}-1}{2}.
$$

Completing the square in $M=n(n+1)/2$ proves necessity; reversing the calculation proves sufficiency. For example, $8\cdot21+1=169=13^2$, giving $n=6$. In contrast, $8\cdot20+1=161$ lies strictly between $12^2$ and $13^2$, so twenty is not a [triangular number](triangular-number.md). The criterion turns an apparently open-ended search through a [sequence](sequence.md) into one exact [perfect square](square-number.md) check.
