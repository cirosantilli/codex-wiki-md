# Bound-electron scattering without a Thomson frequency window

↑ **Parent:** [Compton scattering](compton-scattering.md)

A classical [Thomson scattering](thomson-scattering.md) frequency window for a harmonically bound [electron](electron.md) requires $\omega_0\ll\omega\ll\omega_C$, with $\omega_0=\sqrt{k/m}$ and recoil scale $\omega_C=mc^2/\hbar$. There is no parametrically separated window if $\omega_0\gtrsim\omega_C$, equivalently $k\gtrsim m^3c^4/\hbar^2$. This is a scale comparison, not a prediction of a sharp transition between scattering mechanisms.

At that binding scale, an oscillator quantum has [energy](energy-split.md) comparable with the [electron](electron.md) rest [energy](energy-split.md). The nonrelativistic [Lorentz oscillator model](lorentz-oscillator-model.md) is then inadequate, so a physical cross-section requires a relativistic quantum description. [Compton scattering](compton-scattering.md) itself does not switch on discontinuously at $\hbar\omega=mc^2$; its recoil corrections become substantial near that scale and tend to the [Thomson scattering](thomson-scattering.md) limit at low photon [energy](energy-split.md) for a free target.
