# Reflection-symmetric trials for a linearly weighted Rayleigh quotient

↑ **Parent:** [Rayleigh quotient](rayleigh-quotient.md)

For a nonzero [trial function](trial-function.md) with $y(0)=y(L)=0$ and $y(L-x)=y(x)$, substituting $L-x$ for $x$ proves $\int_0^Lxy^2dx=(L/2)\int_0^Ly^2dx$. This gives the displayed [Rayleigh quotient](rayleigh-quotient.md). The [Poincaré inequality](poincare-inequality.md) on an interval bounds it below by $2\pi^2/L^3$, with equality for a nonzero multiple of $\sin(\pi x/L)$. Thus this sine is the best reflection-symmetric trial for $-y''=\lambda xy$, while the unrestricted lowest [eigenfunction](eigenfunction.md) need not be reflection-symmetric. The polynomial $x(L-x)$ gives $20/L^3$, only a factor $10/\pi^2$ above the sine's value. Both are upper bounds on the unrestricted lowest [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) by the [Rayleigh-Ritz method](rayleigh-ritz-method.md).
