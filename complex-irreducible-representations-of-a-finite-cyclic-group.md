# Complex irreducible representations of a finite cyclic group

↑ **Parent:** [Irreducible representation of a finite abelian group](irreducible-representation-of-a-finite-abelian-group.md)

For $C_n=\langle g:g^n=e\rangle$, the complex [irreducible representations](irreducible-representation.md) are precisely the $n$ one-dimensional maps $g\mapsto e^{2\pi ij/n}$, with $0\leq j<n$. Their [characters](character-of-a-representation.md) have the displayed values, forming the discrete Fourier character table. Indeed a representing matrix $A$ satisfies $A^n=I$. Applying the [fundamental theorem of algebra](fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md) to its [characteristic polynomial](characteristic-polynomial.md) gives an [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) and an [eigenvector](eigenvector.md) over the [complex numbers](complex-number.md). Its line is an [invariant subspace](invariant-subspace.md) under every power of $A$; irreducibility therefore forces the entire space to be that line. Its [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) is an $n$th [root of unity](root-of-unity.md). Different roots give inequivalent one-dimensional representations, and there are exactly $n$ possibilities.

All [conjugacy classes](conjugacy-class.md) are singletons because the [group](group-split.md) is [Abelian](abelian-group.md). The number of the displayed irreducibles equals the group order, and their squared dimensions sum to $n$, in agreement with the [sum of squares of irreducible representation dimensions](sum-of-squares-of-irreducible-representation-dimensions.md). Over the real numbers, conjugate nonreal characters instead combine into the planes described by the [irreducible real representations of a finite cyclic group](irreducible-real-representations-of-a-finite-cyclic-group.md).
