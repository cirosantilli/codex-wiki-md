For example, choose $h=1/4$ and $k=1/2$ around $(a,b)=(0,0)$. On the rectangle, $1+y\geq1/2$, so $f=-\sqrt{1+y}$ is continuous, $|f|\leq\sqrt{3/2}$ and $|\partial f/\partial y|\leq1/\sqrt2$. The [mean value theorem](../../../../../mean-value-theorem.md) therefore supplies a [Lipschitz constant](../../../../../lipschitz-constant.md) $L=1/\sqrt2$. These choices even satisfy $Mh<k$ and $Lh<1$.

While $y>-1$, [separation of variables](../../../../../separation-of-variables.md) gives $2\sqrt{1+y}=2-x$. Therefore the uniquely determined branch up to its first arrival at $-1$ is

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\left(1-\frac x2\right)^2-1,\qquad 0\leq x\leq2.}
$$

It reaches $y=-1$ at $x=2$, with derivative zero. The restriction $x\leq2$ is essential: extending the same quadratic beyond two would make its derivative positive and violate the original differential equation. Beyond that time the [waiting-time continuation at a non-Lipschitz equilibrium](../../../../../waiting-time-continuation-at-a-non-lipschitz-equilibrium.md) is nonunique.
