↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

One useful form of the [Bendixson-Dulac theorem](../../../../../bendixson-dulac-theorem.md) is this: let $D\subseteq\mathbb R^2$ be open and [simply connected](../../../../../simply-connected-space.md), and let $X,Y,\psi$ be continuously differentiable on $D$. If

$$
\frac{\partial(\psi X)}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial(\psi Y)}{\partial y}
$$

has one strict sign throughout $D$, then **there is no nonconstant [periodic orbit](../../../../../periodic-orbit.md) lying entirely in $D$**. More generally the divergence may have a weak sign provided it is not identically zero on any nonempty open subset. The multiplier $\psi$ is a [Dulac function](../../../../../dulac-function.md). [Green's theorem](../../../../../green-theorem.md) proves the criterion: the vector field is tangent to a hypothetical closed orbit, so its scaled flux across that boundary is zero, whereas the signed area integral of its [divergence](../../../../../divergence.md) would be nonzero.
