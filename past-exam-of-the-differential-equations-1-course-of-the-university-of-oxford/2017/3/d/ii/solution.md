<h1 id="3/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

As in case (i), the initial curve is characteristic and its displacement data force $g'=1$. The new derivative data would therefore require

$$
1=\frac{\sqrt y}{2}\bigl(1-h'(0)\bigr),\qquad h'(0)=1-\frac2{\sqrt y}.
$$

The right-hand side varies along the initial segment. A single number $h'(0)$ cannot satisfy those conditions, so **no classical solution exists**.

Characteristic [Cauchy data](../../../../../cauchy-data.md) impose a compatibility condition on transverse derivatives without determining the missing independent wave family. Compatible data can leave nonuniqueness, as in case (i); incompatible data have no solution, as here. The distinction follows from [compatibility of Cauchy data on a characteristic curve](../../../../../compatibility-of-cauchy-data-on-a-characteristic-curve.md), rather than a noncharacteristic existence-and-uniqueness theorem.
