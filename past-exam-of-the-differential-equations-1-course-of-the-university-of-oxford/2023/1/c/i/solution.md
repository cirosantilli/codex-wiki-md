<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $y$ be any [continuous function](../../../../../continuous-function.md) solving the [integral equation](../../../../../integral-equation.md) on $[0,\infty)$, and retain $B=|b|>0$. A [first-exit argument](../../../../../first-exit-argument.md) avoids assuming in advance that its [supremum norm](../../../../../supremum-norm.md) is finite. If $|y|$ ever reached $2B$, continuity would give a first such time $T>0$. On $[0,T]$ we would have $|y|\leq2B$, so the kernel estimate would imply

$$
2B=|y(T)|\leq B+\frac{4B^2}{C_1}=B(1+\gamma)<2B,
$$

a contradiction. Therefore $|y(t)|<2B$ for every finite $t$.

That pointwise strict inequality alone would not give a strict bound on the [supremum norm](../../../../../supremum-norm.md). Apply the [integral equation](../../../../../integral-equation.md) once more, now using $|y|\leq2B$ globally:

$$
\boxed{\|y\|_\infty\leq B(1+\gamma)<2B.}
$$
