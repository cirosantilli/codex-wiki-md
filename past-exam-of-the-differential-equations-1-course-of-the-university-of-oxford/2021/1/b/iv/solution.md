<h1 id="1/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For $\beta=1$, $q=(y+x-x^2)/x^2$. On the two sides of the corner-carrying [characteristic curve](../../../../../characteristic-curve.md), the [partial derivative](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) is

$$
\boxed{u_y(x,y)=\begin{cases}x^{-2},&-x<y<x^2-x,\\0,&x^2-x<y<2x^2-x,\end{cases}\qquad x>0.}
$$

At every point $(x,x^2-x)$ with $x>0$, the two one-sided [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) are $x^{-2}$ and $0$. Therefore $u_y$ does not exist on this curve; its piecewise values have a [jump discontinuity](../../../../../jump-discontinuity.md) of magnitude $x^{-2}$. There are no other interior discontinuities. The values on the outer boundaries are interpreted from within $\mathcal D_1$.

This is exactly [propagation of a derivative jump along a characteristic](../../../../../propagation-of-a-derivative-jump-along-a-characteristic.md). Already on $x=1$, the data have slopes $1$ and $0$ on the two sides of $y=0$. The [transport equation](../../../../../transport-equation-split.md) transports this corner along the [characteristic curve](../../../../../characteristic-curve.md) from $(1,0)$, which is $y=x^2-x$; it provides no smoothing. This is consistent with [Noncharacteristic Cauchy data](../../../../../noncharacteristic-cauchy-data.md) and does not contradict the smooth-data theorem, whose differentiability hypothesis fails here.
