<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Parametrize the initial curve by $(x_0(r),y_0(r))=(1,r)$, $-1\leq r\leq1$. Its tangent is $(0,1)$, whereas the projected [characteristic vector field](../../../../../characteristic-vector-field.md) there is $(1,2r+\beta)$. The [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md)

$$
\boxed{\det\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2r+\beta&1\end{pmatrix}=1\ne0}
$$

proves that these are [Noncharacteristic Cauchy data](../../../../../noncharacteristic-cauchy-data.md): the initial curve is everywhere transverse to the [characteristic curves](../../../../../characteristic-curve.md).

There is a regularity qualification. The prescribed [function](../../../../../function-split.md) $g(r)=\min\{r,0\}$ is [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) and [Lipschitz continuous](../../../../../lipschitz-continuity.md), but is not differentiable at $r=0$. Thus the data are Cauchy in this geometric sense, with data given by a [smooth function](../../../../../smooth-function.md) on each of $r<0$ and $r>0$; the usual theorem giving a global [classical solution](../../../../../classical-solution.md) from continuously differentiable data does not apply across $r=0$.
