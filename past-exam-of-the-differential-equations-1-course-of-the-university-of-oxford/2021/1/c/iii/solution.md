<h1 id="1/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The second-order [differential operator](../../../../../differential-operator.md) factors as

$$
\boxed{xv_{xx}+(2y+\beta x)v_{xy}=\bigl(x\partial_x+(2y+\beta x)\partial_y\bigr)(v_x).}
$$

Therefore setting $u=v_x$ turns the second-order [partial differential equation](../../../../../partial-differential-equation-split.md) into exactly the first-order [partial differential equation](../../../../../partial-differential-equation-split.md) in part (a). Conversely, given a sufficiently smooth [solution of a differential equation](../../../../../solution-of-a-differential-equation.md) $u$ of that equation on a coordinate rectangle, define

$$
v(x,y)=\int_{x_*}^{x}u(\xi,y)\,d\xi+F(y).
$$

The [fundamental theorem of calculus](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) gives $v_x=u$, so this solves the second-order equation, with an arbitrary twice continuously differentiable [function](../../../../../function-split.md) $F$. This classical correspondence is local on smooth patches; for the cornered solution of part (b), it can instead be interpreted using [distributional derivatives](../../../../../distributional-derivative.md).
