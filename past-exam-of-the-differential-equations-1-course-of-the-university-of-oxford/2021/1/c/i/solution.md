<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In the convention $Av_{xx}+2Bv_{xy}+Cv_{yy}=\cdots$, the coefficients of the [principal symbol](../../../../../principal-symbol-of-a-partial-differential-equation.md) are

$$
A=x,\qquad B=y+\frac{\beta x}{2},\qquad C=0,\qquad B^2-AC=\frac{(2y+\beta x)^2}{4}.
$$

Thus the [second-order partial differential equation](../../../../../second-order-partial-differential-equation.md) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{ll}
\text{hyperbolic},&2y+\beta x\ne0,\\
\text{parabolic},&2y+\beta x=0,\quad x\ne0.
\end{array}}
$$

There is no region of [elliptic partial differential equation](../../../../../elliptic-partial-differential-equation.md) type. The terms hyperbolic, parabolic and elliptic refer to the [pointwise classification of a second-order partial differential equation](../../../../../pointwise-classification-of-a-second-order-partial-differential-equation.md). At $(0,0)$ every second-order coefficient vanishes, so the [principal symbol](../../../../../principal-symbol-of-a-partial-differential-equation.md) has rank zero and the equation is degenerate rather than genuinely parabolic there. Moreover the right side is $2$, so no twice continuously differentiable [classical solution](../../../../../classical-solution.md) can satisfy the equation at the origin.
