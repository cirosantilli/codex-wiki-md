# Continuous vacuum selection by an external source

↑ **Parent:** [Spontaneous symmetry breaking](spontaneous-symmetry-breaking.md)

Consider two [real scalar fields](real-scalar-field.md) $\boldsymbol\phi=(\phi_1,\phi_2)$ with positive $\lambda,v$, standard [kinetic terms](kinetic-term.md), and the displayed [scalar potential](scalar-potential.md). At $h=0$, the [circle group](circle-group.md) rotates one field into the other: this is a [global symmetry in field theory](global-symmetry-in-field-theory.md), with the same rotation at every point in space. The [vacuum manifold](vacuum-manifold.md) is the circle $\boldsymbol\phi^2=v^2$. Choose $(v,0)$ and differentiate the [scalar potential](scalar-potential.md) twice:

$$
M^2=\begin{pmatrix}2\lambda v^2&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The [scalar mass matrix](scalar-mass-matrix.md) assigns a restoring force to the radial displacement, whereas its tangent is one of the [Goldstone directions in the scalar mass matrix](goldstone-directions-in-the-scalar-mass-matrix.md). Moving around the circle costs no [potential energy](potential-energy.md); moving between neighbouring points in space can still cost [gradient energy](gradient-energy.md). Flat angular [potential energy](potential-energy.md) therefore means a soft collective excitation, not that every spatial pattern is free.

A positive source $h$ explicitly selects the positive $\phi_1$ direction. The minimum is $(w,0)$, where $w>v$ is the positive solution of $\lambda w(w^2-v^2)=h$. The two [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) of the [scalar mass matrix](scalar-mass-matrix.md) there are

$$
m_{\rm radial}^2=2\lambda w^2+\frac hw,\qquad m_{\rm angular}^2=\frac hw.
$$

For example, $\lambda=v=1$ and $h=0.15$ give $w\simeq1.06792$, $m_{\rm radial}^2\simeq2.42138$ and $m_{\rm angular}^2\simeq0.140459$. The light angular excitation becomes a [Goldstone boson](goldstone-boson.md) as $h\downarrow0$, while the radial [mass](mass.md) stays finite. The source changes both the preferred direction and the radius; expanding about the old point $(v,0)$ at nonzero $h$ would miss that shift.
