# Point interaction in a second-order differential equation

↑ **Parent:** [Second-order linear differential equation](second-order-linear-differential-equation.md)

A point interaction concentrates a coefficient of a [linear differential equation](linear-differential-equation.md) at a single interior point. On an [interval](interval-mathematics.md) $0<x<\ell$, with $0<c<\ell$, consider $L_\alpha y=y''+\alpha y(c)\delta_c$. The domain consists of [continuous functions](continuous-function.md) that are twice differentiable away from $c$, with the required one-sided derivatives. Multiplication of the [Dirac delta](dirac-delta-function.md) by $y$ means the unambiguous point value $y(c)\delta_c$. It does not mean an undefined product of a delta with an arbitrary discontinuous function.

The equation is interpreted using [distributional derivatives](distributional-derivative.md). Smooth forcing gives an ordinary equation on each side, with [transmission conditions for a delta coefficient](transmission-conditions-for-a-delta-coefficient.md) at the interaction. A [delta potential](delta-potential.md) in the one-dimensional [Schrödinger equation](schrodinger-equation.md) is a physical example after choosing the corresponding sign and constants.
