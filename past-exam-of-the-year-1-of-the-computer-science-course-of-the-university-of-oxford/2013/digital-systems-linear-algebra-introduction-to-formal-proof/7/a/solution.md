↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [conjunction introduction rule](../../../../../conjunction-introduction-rule.md) combines derivations of $\phi$ and $\psi$ into a derivation of $\phi\land\psi$. Both conclusions must be available under the current assumptions.

The [disjunction elimination rule](../../../../../disjunction-elimination-rule.md) is reasoning by cases: from $\phi\lor\psi$, a [subproof](../../../../../subproof.md) deriving the same conclusion $\kappa$ under $\phi$, and another [subproof](../../../../../subproof.md) deriving $\kappa$ under $\psi$, conclude $\kappa$. It discharges the two case assumptions, not any surrounding premises.

The [implication introduction rule](../../../../../implication-introduction-rule.md) derives $\phi\to\psi$ by temporarily assuming $\phi$ and deriving $\psi$; it then discharges that occurrence of the assumption $\phi$.

Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{the boxes delimit temporary assumptions, their dependent subproofs, and their discharge}.}
$$

A conclusion inside a box cannot simply be reused outside it without an appropriate [inference rule](../../../../../inference-rule.md). Any other undischarged assumptions continue to constrain the result.
