<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write each [set difference](../../../../../../set-difference.md) as an [intersection](../../../../../../set-intersection.md) with a [complement of a set](../../../../../../complement-of-a-set.md). Applying [De Morgan's laws](../../../../../../de-morgan-s-laws.md) and then the [distributive property](../../../../../../distributive-property.md) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
A\setminus(B\cup C)&=A\cap\overline{B\cup C}=A\cap(\overline B\cap\overline C)=(A\setminus B)\cap(A\setminus C),\\
A\setminus(B\cap C)&=A\cap\overline{B\cap C}=A\cap(\overline B\cup\overline C)=(A\setminus B)\cup(A\setminus C).
\end{aligned}
$$

The [complements](../../../../../../complement-of-a-set.md) turn the inner [union](../../../../../../set-union.md) into an [intersection](../../../../../../set-intersection.md) and vice versa. Thus

$$
\boxed{A\setminus(B\cup C)=(A\setminus B)\cap(A\setminus C),\quad A\setminus(B\cap C)=(A\setminus B)\cup(A\setminus C).}
$$
