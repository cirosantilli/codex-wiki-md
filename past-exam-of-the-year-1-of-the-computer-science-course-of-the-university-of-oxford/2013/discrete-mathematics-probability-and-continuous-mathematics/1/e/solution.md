<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

If $n$ is positive and odd, $n^2$ is odd. The [function](../../../../../function-split.md) $x\mapsto x^{n^2}$ is a [strictly increasing function](../../../../../strictly-increasing-function.md) on the [real numbers](../../../../../real-number.md), so it is [injective](../../../../../injective-function.md). Every real $y$ has the real odd root $\operatorname{sgn}(y)|y|^{1/n^2}$, so the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) is also [surjective](../../../../../surjective-function.md).

If $n$ is positive and even, the exponent is positive and even. Then $f(1)=f(-1)$, while $f(x)\geq0$ for all real $x$. These facts disprove [injectivity](../../../../../injective-function.md) and [surjectivity](../../../../../surjective-function.md), respectively. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f\text{ is onto iff }n\text{ is odd};\qquad f\text{ is one-to-one iff }n\text{ is odd}.}
$$

If the convention $0\in\mathbb N$ is used, $n=0$ gives exponent zero. Interpreting $x^0$ as the constant [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $1$ makes the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) neither [injective](../../../../../injective-function.md) nor [surjective](../../../../../surjective-function.md). If $0^0$ is left undefined, that case does not define the stated [function](../../../../../function-split.md) on all the [real numbers](../../../../../real-number.md). Neither convention adds a qualifying value of $n$.
