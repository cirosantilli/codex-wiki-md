↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An algebraic way to prove the [set identity](../../../../../../set-identity.md) is to show that $D=\overline A\cup\overline B$ is a [complement of a set](../../../../../../complement-of-a-set.md) for $A\cap B$. By the [distributive property](../../../../../../distributive-property.md),

$$
(A\cap B)\cap D=(A\cap B\cap\overline A)\cup(A\cap B\cap\overline B)=\varnothing,
$$

and

$$
(A\cap B)\cup D=(A\cup D)\cap(B\cup D)=\mathcal U\cap\mathcal U=\mathcal U.
$$

A [complement of a set](../../../../../../complement-of-a-set.md) is unique: if $D,E$ both complement $S$, then $D=D\cap(S\cup E)=D\cap E$, and similarly $E=D\cap E$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\overline{A\cap B}=\overline A\cup\overline B.}
$$
