<h1 id="3/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

In a [symmetric relation](../../../../../symmetric-relation.md), each of the $n$ diagonal pairs $(a,a)$ can be chosen independently. For each of the $\binom n2$ unordered pairs of distinct elements, either both ordered pairs $(a,b),(b,a)$ occur or neither occurs. Every such choice is free and produces a distinct [symmetric relation](../../../../../symmetric-relation.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{2^n2^{\binom n2}=2^{n(n+1)/2}.}
$$

For the [empty set](../../../../../empty-set.md) this correctly gives one [binary relation](../../../../../binary-relation.md), the empty one.
