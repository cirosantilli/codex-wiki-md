↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

This numbered item specifies the model used below. A convenient [sample space](../../../../../../sample-space.md) is the collection of $36$ ordered pairs $(s_1,s_2)\in\{1,\ldots,6\}^2$, each with [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) $1/36$. The corresponding [random variables](../../../../../../random-variable-split.md) are determined by the sum and difference. Inverting the two equations gives

$$
\boxed{S_1=\frac{X+Y}{2},\qquad S_2=\frac{X-Y}{2}.}
$$

Thus a candidate pair $(X,Y)=(i,j)$ is possible exactly when $(i+j)/2$ and $(i-j)/2$ are both [integers](../../../../../../integer-split.md) between $1$ and $6$. In particular, $i$ and $j$ must have the same parity. This inverse description will determine the [joint probability mass function](../../../../../../joint-probability-mass-function.md).
