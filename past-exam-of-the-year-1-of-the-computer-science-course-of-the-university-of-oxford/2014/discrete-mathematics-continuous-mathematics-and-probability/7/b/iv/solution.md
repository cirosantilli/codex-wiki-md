<h1 id="7/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

If the selected coin has bias $p$, the other two biases sum to $3/2-p$. A uniform choice between those two coins therefore has conditional head [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) $3/4-p/2$. Average over the [posterior distribution](../../../../../../bayesian-posterior.md), using the previous [posterior predictive probability](../../../../../../posterior-predictive-probability.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(\text{head on another coin}\mid H)
=\frac34-\frac12\frac{33}{58}
=\frac{27}{58}.}
$$

The observation makes a high-bias coin more likely to have been removed from the remaining pair.
