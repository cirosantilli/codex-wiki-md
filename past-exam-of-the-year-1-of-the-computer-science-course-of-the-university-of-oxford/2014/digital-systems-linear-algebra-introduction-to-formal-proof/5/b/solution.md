<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write the columns of $A$ as $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ and the coordinates of the [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $x$ as $x_1,\ldots,x_n$. By the definition of [matrix multiplication](../../../../../matrix-multiplication.md),

$$
\boxed{y=Ax=x_1a_1+\cdots+x_na_n\in\operatorname{span}\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}=\operatorname{Col}(A).}
$$

Indeed, its $i$th coordinate is $\sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}x_j$, exactly the $i$th coordinate of this [linear combination](../../../../../linear-combination.md). Since the [column space](../../../../../column-space.md) contains every [linear combination](../../../../../linear-combination.md) of the columns, the claim holds for every [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $x$.
