Here $v_3=2v_1-v_2$, so the [image of a linear map](../../../../../image-of-a-linear-map.md) is the [linear span](../../../../../linear-span.md) of $v_1,v_2$. These two [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md): if $av_1+bv_2=0$, the $e_1$ and $e_2$ coordinates give $a+b=0$ and $a-b=0$, hence $a=b=0$. Consequently the [rank of a linear map](../../../../../rank-of-a-linear-map.md) is two. By the [rank-nullity theorem](../../../../../rank-nullity-theorem.md), the [nullity](../../../../../nullity-of-a-linear-map.md) is $3-2=1$:

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\alpha=2,\qquad\operatorname{nullity}\alpha=1.}
$$

More explicitly, the relation $-2v_1+v_2+v_3=0$ gives the [kernel of a linear map](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md)

$$
\ker\alpha=\operatorname{span}\{-2e_1+e_2+e_3\}.
$$
