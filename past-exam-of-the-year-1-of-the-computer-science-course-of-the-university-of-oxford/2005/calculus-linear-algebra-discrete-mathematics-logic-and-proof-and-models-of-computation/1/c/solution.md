<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [basis](../../../../../basis.md) provides unique coordinates. Define

$$
\boxed{\alpha(a_1e_1+a_2e_2+a_3e_3)=a_1v_1+a_2v_2+a_3v_3.}
$$

The uniqueness of the coordinates makes this a well-defined [function](../../../../../function-split.md). Adding coordinates and multiplying them by a real [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md) shows directly that $\alpha(au+bw)=a\alpha(u)+b\alpha(w)$, so it is a [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md). Substituting $e_i$ gives $\alpha(e_i)=v_i$.

Conversely, every [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md) with those three values must send $\sum_i a_ie_i$ to $\sum_i a_iv_i$, by preservation of [linear combinations](../../../../../linear-combination.md). This determines its value on every element of $V$, proving uniqueness. No [linear independence](../../../../../linear-independence.md) of $v_1,v_2,v_3$ is needed.
