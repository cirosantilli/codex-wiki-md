<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In this [first-order structure](../../../../../first-order-structure.md), $R(x,z)\land R(y,z)$ says $x\leq z$ and $y\leq z$. Thus $z$ is an [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $x,y$. The universally quantified clause says that if $w$ is any other [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md), then $z\leq w$. Therefore $z$ is their [least upper bound](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). In the usual [total order](../../../../../total-order.md) on the [natural numbers](../../../../../natural-number.md), this is their [maximum](../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md):

$$
\boxed{\phi(x,y,z)\iff z=\max\{x,y\}.}
$$

This describes a condition on the values assigned to the free variables $x,y,z$; $\phi$ itself is not a closed [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md).
