<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The requested examples are [binary relations](../../../../../binary-relation.md) on [sets](../../../../../set-split.md), rather than [partially ordered sets](../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md): a [partially ordered set](../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md) necessarily has all three properties. This corrects the wording of the source.[Printed p. 5, Q7(b), requesting a “partially ordered set” without the third axiom](../../../../../calculus-linear-algebra-discrete-mathematics-logic-and-proof-and-models-of-computation-examination-paper-by-the-university-of-oxford-2005.md)

For an [antisymmetric relation](../../../../../antisymmetric-relation.md) and [transitive relation](../../../../../transitive-relation.md) that is not a [reflexive relation](../../../../../reflexive-relation.md), take $S=\{0,1\}$ and $R=\{(0,1)\}$. No distinct pair relates both ways, so the [antisymmetric relation](../../../../../antisymmetric-relation.md) condition holds. There are no composable pairs, so the [transitive relation](../../../../../transitive-relation.md) condition holds vacuously. Neither $(0,0)$ nor $(1,1)$ belongs to $R$, so the [reflexive relation](../../../../../reflexive-relation.md) condition fails.

For a [reflexive relation](../../../../../reflexive-relation.md) and [transitive relation](../../../../../transitive-relation.md) that is not an [antisymmetric relation](../../../../../antisymmetric-relation.md), take $S=\{0,1\}$ and $R=S\times S$. All diagonal pairs and all composite pairs belong to $R$, but $R(0,1)$ and $R(1,0)$ both hold with $0\ne1$.

For a [reflexive relation](../../../../../reflexive-relation.md) and [antisymmetric relation](../../../../../antisymmetric-relation.md) that is not a [transitive relation](../../../../../transitive-relation.md), take

$$
S=\{0,1,2\},\qquad R=\{(0,0),(1,1),(2,2),(0,1),(1,2)\}.
$$

All diagonal pairs are present, and no off-diagonal pair has its reverse present. However $R(0,1)$ and $R(1,2)$ hold while $R(0,2)$ fails. Thus each pair of axioms can hold without the third.
