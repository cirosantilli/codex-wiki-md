In [first-order logic](../../../../../first-order-logic.md) with equality, the three [first-order sentences](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) are

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\text{reflexivity: }&\forall x\,R(x,x),\\
\text{antisymmetry: }&\forall x\forall y\,((R(x,y)\land R(y,x))\to x=y),\\
\text{transitivity: }&\forall x\forall y\forall z\,((R(x,y)\land R(y,z))\to R(x,z)).
\end{aligned}}
$$

A [reflexive relation](../../../../../reflexive-relation.md) relates each element to itself. An [antisymmetric relation](../../../../../antisymmetric-relation.md) forbids distinct elements from relating in both directions. A [transitive relation](../../../../../transitive-relation.md) contains every composite of two successive related pairs. Together these axioms define a [partially ordered set](../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md).
