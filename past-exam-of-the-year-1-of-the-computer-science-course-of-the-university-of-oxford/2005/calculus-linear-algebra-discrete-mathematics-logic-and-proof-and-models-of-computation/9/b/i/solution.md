↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Every finite [formal language](../../../../../../formal-language.md) is a [regular language](../../../../../../regular-language.md). A single [string](../../../../../../string.md) $a_1\cdots a_k$ is described by the [regular expression](../../../../../../regular-expression.md) formed by concatenating its symbols; the empty [string](../../../../../../string.md) is described by $\varepsilon$. A finite [union](../../../../../../set-union.md) of these [regular expressions](../../../../../../regular-expression.md) describes any specified finite collection of [strings](../../../../../../string.md). The empty collection is described by $\varnothing$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{every finite subset of }\{0,1\}^*\text{ is regular}.}
$$

Equivalently, a [deterministic finite automaton](../../../../../../deterministic-finite-automaton.md) can remember which [prefix](../../../../../../prefix-of-a-list.md) of one of the finitely many accepted [strings](../../../../../../string.md) has been read, moving to a rejecting dead state when no such [prefix](../../../../../../prefix-of-a-list.md) remains possible.
