<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [continuous nonalgebraic unit interval](../../../../../continuous-nonalgebraic-unit-interval.md) is

$$
\boxed{P=[0,1]\text{ with its usual order}.}
$$

Every nonempty [directed set](../../../../../directed-set.md) has a [least upper bound](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) by completeness of the real numbers, and that [least upper bound](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) lies in $[0,1]$. Thus this is a [directed-complete partial order](../../../../../directed-complete-partial-order.md).

For $0\leq a<b\leq1$, we have $a\ll b$. Indeed, if $b\leq\sup D$ and no $d\in D$ were at least $a$, then $a$ would be an [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) for $D$, implying $\sup D\leq a<b$, a contradiction. If $a>b$, the singleton [directed set](../../../../../directed-set.md) $\{b\}$ excludes $a\ll b$. If $a=b>0$, the increasing [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $d_n=b(1-1/n)$, $n\geq1$, has [least upper bound](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $b$ and has no member at least $b$, so $b\not\ll b$.

Under the usual nonempty convention, $0\ll0$. Therefore the elements [way-below](../../../../../way-below-relation.md) $b>0$ form $[0,b)$, and those [way-below](../../../../../way-below-relation.md) $0$ form $\{0\}$. These are [directed sets](../../../../../directed-set.md) with [least upper bounds](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $b$ and $0$, respectively, proving that $[0,1]$ is a [continuous directed-complete partial order](../../../../../continuous-directed-complete-partial-order.md). Its only [compact element](../../../../../compact-element.md) is $0$. For any $b>0$, the [compact elements](../../../../../compact-element.md) below $b$ have [least upper bound](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $0$, rather than $b$, so it is not an [algebraic partially ordered set](../../../../../algebraic-partially-ordered-set.md).

Under the literal empty-set convention of the paper, $0\not\ll0$ as well. The elements [way-below](../../../../../way-below-relation.md) zero then form the empty [set](../../../../../set-split.md), which is directed in that convention and has [least upper bound](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) zero. There are no [compact elements](../../../../../compact-element.md), and their empty [set](../../../../../set-split.md) still fails to approximate any positive $b$. Thus both conventions give the same requested classification:

$$
\boxed{[0,1]\text{ is continuous but not algebraic}.}
$$
