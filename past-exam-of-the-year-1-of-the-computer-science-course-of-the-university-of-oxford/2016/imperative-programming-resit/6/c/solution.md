<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

[Class inheritance](../../../../../class-inheritance.md) defines a [software class](../../../../../class-programming.md) by extending another [software class](../../../../../class-programming.md), inheriting its available operations and optionally adding members or providing [method overriding](../../../../../method-overriding.md). In languages supporting it, [traits](../../../../../trait.md) and [mixins](../../../../../mixin.md) can contribute reusable behavior as well.

**Use inheritance for a sound specialization whose instances can satisfy the superclass's contract wherever its instances are expected.** For example, every specific gate in part (d) is usable through the common gate [object interface](../../../../../object-interface.md). [Subtyping](../../../../../subtyping.md) supports this substitution and [dynamic dispatch](../../../../../dynamic-dispatch.md) selects the specialized calculation. Merely noticing shared code does not establish the required behavioral relationship; [object composition](../../../../../object-composition.md) is often better for a replaceable component or policy that an object contains. A subclass must preserve the superclass's promised behavior, rather than just sharing its field names.
