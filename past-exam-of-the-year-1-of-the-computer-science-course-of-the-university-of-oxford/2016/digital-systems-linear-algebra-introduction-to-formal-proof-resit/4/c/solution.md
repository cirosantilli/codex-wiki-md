<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In the column convention used for [QR factorization](../../../../../qr-decomposition.md), an $m\times k$ real matrix has [orthonormal columns](../../../../../matrix-with-orthonormal-columns.md) when $Q^TQ=I_k$. This says that each column has unit norm and distinct columns have zero [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md). If $Q$ is square, then $Q^TQ=I$ provides a left inverse; a square matrix with a left inverse is invertible, so **$Q^{-1}=Q^T$**, and also $QQ^T=I$. Such a square matrix is an [orthogonal matrix](../../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md). A rectangular matrix with orthonormal columns generally has no two-sided inverse.
