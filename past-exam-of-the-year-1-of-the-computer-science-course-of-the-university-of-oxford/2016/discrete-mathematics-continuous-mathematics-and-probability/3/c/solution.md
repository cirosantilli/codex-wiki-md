<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The fifty [disjoint sets](../../../../../disjoint-sets.md) $\{1,100\},\{2,99\},\ldots,\{50,51\}$ form a [partition of a set](../../../../../partition-of-a-set.md) $\{1,\ldots,100\}$. If a chosen [subset](../../../../../subset.md) met each pair at most once, it would contain at most fifty elements. The [pigeonhole principle](../../../../../pigeonhole-principle.md) therefore forces a $51$-element [subset](../../../../../subset.md) to contain both elements of at least one pair. **Those two distinct elements sum to $101$.**
