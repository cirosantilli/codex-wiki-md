<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

We prove both [set inclusions](../../../../../../set-inclusion.md), as required by a [double inclusion proof](../../../../../../double-inclusion-proof.md). If $x\in A\setminus(B\cup C)$, then $x\in A$ and $x\notin B\cup C$. Thus $x\notin B$ and $x\notin C$, so $x\in A\setminus B$ and $x\in A\setminus C$. This proves the forward [set inclusion](../../../../../../set-inclusion.md).

Conversely, if $x\in(A\setminus B)\cap(A\setminus C)$, then $x\in A$, $x\notin B$, and $x\notin C$. Hence $x\notin B\cup C$, giving $x\in A\setminus(B\cup C)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{A\setminus(B\cup C)=(A\setminus B)\cap(A\setminus C).}
$$
