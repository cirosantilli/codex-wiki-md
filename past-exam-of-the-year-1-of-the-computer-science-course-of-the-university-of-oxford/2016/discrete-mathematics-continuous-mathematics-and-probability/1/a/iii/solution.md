<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Complements are relative to the same [universal set](../../../../../../universal-set.md) $\mathcal U$. Suppose $A\subseteq B$ and $x\in\overline B$. Then $x\in\mathcal U$ and $x\notin B$. If $x$ belonged to $A$, the assumed [set inclusion](../../../../../../set-inclusion.md) would imply $x\in B$, a contradiction. Thus $x\notin A$ and $x\in\overline A$, proving

$$
\boxed{A\subseteq B\ \Longrightarrow\ \overline B\subseteq\overline A.}
$$

There is only one [set inclusion](../../../../../../set-inclusion.md) in this conclusion; a reverse inclusion is neither needed nor generally true. The request for double inclusion applies to the equalities in (i) and (ii).
