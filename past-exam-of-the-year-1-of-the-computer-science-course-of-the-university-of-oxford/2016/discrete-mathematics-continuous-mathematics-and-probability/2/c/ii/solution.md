<h1 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose $m$ is the [greatest lower bound](../../../../../../greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of the [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) $X$ of all [upper bounds](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $S$. Every $s\in S$ satisfies $s\preceq x$ for every $x\in X$, so each such $s$ is a [lower bound](../../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $X$. Since $m$ is the greatest of those [lower bounds](../../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md), $s\preceq m$ for every $s\in S$. Thus $m$ itself is an [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $S$, meaning $m\in X$.

Also $m$, being a [lower bound](../../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $X$, satisfies $m\preceq x$ for every $x\in X$. It is therefore an [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $S$ below every other [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md), which proves **$m=\operatorname{lub}(S)$**. The argument also covers empty $S$ or $X$ whenever the assumed [greatest lower bound](../../../../../../greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) exists.
