↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [relation powers](../../../../../../power-of-a-relation.md) are $R^2=\{(a,c),(b,d)\}$, $R^3=\{(a,d)\}$, and $R^i=\varnothing$ for $i\geq4$. For $k=1$, $(a,b),(b,c)\in T$ but $(a,c)\notin T$, so [transitivity](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md) fails. For $k=2$, $(a,c),(c,d)\in T$ but $(a,d)\notin T$, so it still fails.

For $k=3$, $T$ consists of every pair in which the first endpoint precedes the second in $a,b,c,d$. Composing any two such pairs again gives such a pair, so $T$ is [transitive](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md). Thus **the smallest $k$ is $3$**; this $T$ is the [transitive closure of a relation](../../../../../../transitive-closure-relation.md) $R$.
