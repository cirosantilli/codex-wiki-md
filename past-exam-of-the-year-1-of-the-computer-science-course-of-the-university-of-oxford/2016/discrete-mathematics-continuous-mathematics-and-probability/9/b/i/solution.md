<h1 id="9/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

First $\mu>0$. If a nonnegative [random variable](../../../../../../random-variable-split.md) had zero [expected value](../../../../../../expected-value-split.md), it would equal zero almost surely, which is impossible for a distribution having a [probability density function](../../../../../../probability-density-function.md) on $[0,\infty)$: a singleton has probability zero. Thus the given division by $\mu$ is legitimate.

Because $x\geq0$ and $f(x)\geq0$, $g(x)=xf(x)/\mu\geq0$. Moreover

$$
\int_0^\infty g(x)\,dx=\frac1\mu\int_0^\infty xf(x)\,dx=\frac\mu\mu=1.
$$

Extend $g$ by zero for negative $x$. **It is a [probability density function](../../../../../../probability-density-function.md)**, defining the [size-biased distribution](../../../../../../size-biased-distribution.md) of $X$: larger observations receive weight proportional to their value.
