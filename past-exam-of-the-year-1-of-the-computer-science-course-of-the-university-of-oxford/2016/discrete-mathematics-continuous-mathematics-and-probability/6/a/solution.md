<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Under the hypotheses of [Taylor's theorem with Lagrange remainder](../../../../../taylor-theorem-with-lagrange-remainder.md), for example $f\in C^{n+1}$ on an interval containing $x$ and $x+h$, there is a point $\xi$ between them such that

$$
\boxed{f(x+h)=\sum_{r=0}^n\frac{f^{(r)}(x)}{r!}h^r+\frac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}h^{n+1}.}
$$

The [Lagrange remainder](../../../../../lagrange-remainder.md) makes this an exact equality, rather than just a truncated [Taylor polynomial](../../../../../taylor-polynomial.md). For $h=0$ the equality is immediate.
