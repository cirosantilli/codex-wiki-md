<h1 id="1/d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $h:B\to A$ be a two-sided [inverse function](../../../../../../inverse-function.md) of $f$. If $f(a)=f(a')$, apply $h$ and use $h\circ f=\operatorname{id}_A$ to obtain $a=a'$, proving [injectivity](../../../../../../injective-function.md). For any $b\in B$, the other identity gives $b=f(h(b))$, proving [surjectivity](../../../../../../surjective-function.md). Therefore **$f$ is a [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md)**. Both inverse identities matter: a one-sided inverse alone would establish only the corresponding one of these two properties.
