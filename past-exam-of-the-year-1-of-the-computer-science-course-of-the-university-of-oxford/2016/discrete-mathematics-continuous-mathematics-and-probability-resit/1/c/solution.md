↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [function](../../../../../function-split.md) $f:A\to B$ is **onto**, or a [surjective function](../../../../../surjective-function.md), when every $b\in B$ has a preimage: $\forall b\in B\ \exists a\in A:\ f(a)=b$. It is **one-to-one**, or an [injective function](../../../../../injective-function.md), when $f(a)=f(a')$ implies $a=a'$ for all $a,a'\in A$. It is **bijective**, a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md), when both properties hold.

An [inverse function](../../../../../inverse-function.md) is a [function](../../../../../function-split.md) $f^{-1}:B\to A$ satisfying

$$
\boxed{f^{-1}\circ f=\operatorname{id}_A,\qquad f\circ f^{-1}=\operatorname{id}_B}.
$$

For a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md), $f^{-1}(b)$ is the unique $a$ with $f(a)=b$: existence follows from [surjectivity](../../../../../surjective-function.md) and uniqueness from [injectivity](../../../../../injective-function.md). This is a two-sided [inverse function](../../../../../inverse-function.md), rather than just a left or right inverse.
