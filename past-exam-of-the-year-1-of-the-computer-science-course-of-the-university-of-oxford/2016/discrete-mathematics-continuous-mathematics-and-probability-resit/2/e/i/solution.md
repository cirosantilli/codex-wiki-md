<h1 id="2/e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The stated identity $k\binom nk=n\binom{n-1}{k-1}$ has an even right side because $n$ is even. Its left side is an odd integer $k$ times the integer [binomial coefficient](../../../../../../binomial-coefficient-split.md) $\binom nk$. An odd multiplier preserves [integer parity](../../../../../../parity-mathematics.md), so that coefficient must be even. Equivalently, reducing the identity in [modular arithmetic](../../../../../../modular-arithmetic-split.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\binom nk\equiv0\pmod2}.
$$
