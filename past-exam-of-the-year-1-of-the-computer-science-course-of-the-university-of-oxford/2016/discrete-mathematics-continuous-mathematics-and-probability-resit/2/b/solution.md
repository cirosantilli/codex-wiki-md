<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For positive integer pairs, [reflexivity](../../../../../reflexive-relation.md) is $ab=ba$. If $ad=bc$, reversing the equality gives $cb=da$, establishing [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md). For [transitivity](../../../../../transitive-relation.md), suppose $(a,b)R(c,d)$ and $(c,d)R(e,f)$. Then $ad=bc$ and $cf=de$, whence

$$
adf=bcf=bde.
$$

Since $d>0$, cancellation gives $af=be$. Thus $(a,b)R(e,f)$, and $R$ is an [equivalence relation](../../../../../equivalence-relation.md).

Membership in the [equivalence class](../../../../../equivalence-class.md) of $(4,6)$ means $4b=6a$, or $2b=3a$. Since $2$ and $3$ are [coprime](../../../../../coprime-integers.md), $a=2r$ and $b=3r$ for a positive integer $r$. Conversely every such pair satisfies the equality. Therefore

$$
\boxed{[(4,6)]_R=\{(2r,3r):r\in\mathbb Z_+\}}.
$$

These pairs are precisely the positive numerator-denominator representations of the [rational number](../../../../../rational-number.md) $2/3$.
