<h1 id="5/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For a [skew-symmetric matrix](../../../../../skew-symmetric-matrix.md), $A^T=-A$. By parts (c) and (d),

$$
\det(A)=\det(A^T)=\det(-A)=(-1)^n\det(A).
$$

If $n$ is odd, $\det(A)=-\det(A)$, hence $2\det(A)=0$ over $\mathbb R$. Therefore **$\det(A)=0$, so $A$ is a [singular matrix](../../../../../singular-matrix.md)**. The conclusion depends on odd dimension: in dimension two, $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$ is [skew-symmetric](../../../../../skew-symmetric-matrix.md) and has [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) one.
