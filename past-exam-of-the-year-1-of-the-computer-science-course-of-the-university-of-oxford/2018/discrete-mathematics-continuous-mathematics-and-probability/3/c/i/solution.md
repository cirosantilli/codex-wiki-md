↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An element $u\in A$ is a [least upper bound](../../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $S$ if it is an [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md), meaning $s\leq u$ for every $s\in S$, and **$u\leq v$ for every [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $v\in A$ of $S$**. The candidate need not belong to $S$. Because a [partial order](../../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md) is an [antisymmetric relation](../../../../../../antisymmetric-relation.md), two [least upper bounds](../../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) must be equal. A [least upper bound](../../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) is also called the [supremum](../../../../../../supremum.md).
