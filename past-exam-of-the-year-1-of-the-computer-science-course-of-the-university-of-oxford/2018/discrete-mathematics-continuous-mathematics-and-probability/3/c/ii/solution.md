<h1 id="3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

An element $m$ is a [maximum](../../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) of $S$ if **$m\in S$ and $s\leq m$ for every $s\in S$**. Thus it is an [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) that is itself a member. Every [maximum](../../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) is the [least upper bound](../../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md): any [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) must lie above $m$ because $m\in S$. A [supremum](../../../../../../supremum.md) outside $S$ need not be a [maximum](../../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md).
