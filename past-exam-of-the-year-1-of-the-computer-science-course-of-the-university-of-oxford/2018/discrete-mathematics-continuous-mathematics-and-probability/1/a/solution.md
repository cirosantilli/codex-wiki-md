<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

We prove both [set inclusions](../../../../../set-inclusion.md). If $x\in A\setminus(A\setminus B)$, then $x\in A$ and $x\notin A\setminus B$. Since $x\in A$, the latter condition forces $x\in B$, giving $x\in A\cap B$. Conversely, if $x\in A\cap B$, then $x\in A$ and $x\notin A\setminus B$, so $x\in A\setminus(A\setminus B)$. By [set extensionality](../../../../../axiom-of-extensionality.md), the two [sets](../../../../../set-split.md) are equal. This is a [double inclusion proof](../../../../../double-inclusion-proof.md); the implication involving the [set difference](../../../../../set-difference.md) uses membership in $A$ explicitly.
