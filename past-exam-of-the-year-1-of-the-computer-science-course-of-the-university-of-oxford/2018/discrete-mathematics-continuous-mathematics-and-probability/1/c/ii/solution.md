↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**False, even for two [equivalence relations](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md).** On $A=\{1,2,3\}$, let $R_1$ have [equivalence classes](../../../../../../equivalence-class.md) $\{1,2\}$ and $\{3\}$, and let $R_2$ have [equivalence classes](../../../../../../equivalence-class.md) $\{1\}$ and $\{2,3\}$. Both [binary relations](../../../../../../binary-relation.md) are [transitive](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md). For $S=R_2\circ R_1$, we have $3S2$, using the intermediate element $3$, and $2S1$, using the intermediate element $1$. However, $3S1$ fails: the only $R_1$-successor of $3$ is $3$, and $3R_21$ is false. Thus the composite fails [transitivity](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md). The order of composition here means first $R_1$, then $R_2$.
