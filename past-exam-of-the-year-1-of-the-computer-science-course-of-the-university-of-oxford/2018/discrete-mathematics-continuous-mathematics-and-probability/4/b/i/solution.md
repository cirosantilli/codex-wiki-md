<h1 id="4/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $x>0$, substitute $v=y/x$. Then $y'=v+xv'$, and cancellation reduces the [differential equation](../../../../../../differential-equation-split.md) to $v'+2v=e^{-2x}$. Multiplication by the [integrating factor](../../../../../../integrating-factor.md) $e^{2x}$ gives $(e^{2x}v)'=1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{y(x)=x(x+C)e^{-2x},\qquad C\in\mathbb R.}
$$

Direct substitution gives $y'+2y-y/x=xe^{-2x}$ and verifies the result. Dividing by $x$ is legitimate on the stated positive domain.
