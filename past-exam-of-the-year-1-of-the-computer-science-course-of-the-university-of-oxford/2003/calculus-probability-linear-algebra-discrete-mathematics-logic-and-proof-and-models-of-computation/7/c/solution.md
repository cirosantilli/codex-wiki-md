<h1 id="7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Take the [natural numbers](../../../../../natural-number.md) including zero with their usual [total order](../../../../../total-order.md):

$$
\boxed{P=(\{0,1,2,\ldots\},\le).}
$$

A nonempty subset with an [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $m\in P$ is contained in the finite [set](../../../../../set-split.md) $\{0,1,\ldots,m\}$. It therefore has a [greatest element](../../../../../greatest-element.md), which is its [least upper bound](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). The empty subset has [least upper bound](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $0$. Thus $P$ is a [bounded-complete partially ordered set](../../../../../bounded-complete-partially-ordered-set.md).

But $D=P$ is a [directed set](../../../../../directed-set.md), since $\max(x,y)\in P$ is above any two members. It has no [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) in $P$: for any proposed bound $m$, the member $m+1$ is larger. It therefore has no [least upper bound](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{P\text{ is bounded-complete but not directed-complete}.}
$$
