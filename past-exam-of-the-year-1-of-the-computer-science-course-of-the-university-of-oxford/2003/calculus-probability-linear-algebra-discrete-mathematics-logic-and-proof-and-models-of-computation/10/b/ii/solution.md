<h1 id="10/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [context-free grammar](../../../../../../context-free-grammar.md) is an [ambiguous grammar](../../../../../../ambiguous-grammar.md) if some terminal [string](../../../../../../string.md) has two distinct [parse trees](../../../../../../parse-tree.md), equivalently two distinct leftmost derivations. Different orders of expanding independent nonterminals do not by themselves constitute ambiguity.

The [context-free grammar](../../../../../../context-free-grammar.md) just given is **unambiguous**. It suffices to prove this for the reversed productions, because reversing a [parse tree](../../../../../../parse-tree.md) preserves whether two [parse trees](../../../../../../parse-tree.md) are distinct.

For $D$, the empty [string](../../../../../../string.md) forces the empty production. A nonempty [string](../../../../../../string.md) must use $D\to0D1D$. In any resulting nonnegative balanced path, the displayed one is the unique first return to zero. It therefore uniquely determines the two shorter substrings generated by $D$. [Mathematical induction](../../../../../../mathematical-induction.md) on length makes both smaller [parse trees](../../../../../../parse-tree.md) unique.

For $U$, the empty [string](../../../../../../string.md) again forces the empty production. A nonempty path with no return to zero must use $U\to0U$, since $0D1U$ always returns to zero. A path with a return must use $U\to0D1U$, since $0U$ stays positive after its first step. In the latter case, the first return uniquely determines the $D$ substring and the remaining $U$ substring. Applying [mathematical induction](../../../../../../mathematical-induction.md) to these shorter substrings proves uniqueness throughout. Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{The grammar in part (b)(i) is unambiguous}.}
$$
