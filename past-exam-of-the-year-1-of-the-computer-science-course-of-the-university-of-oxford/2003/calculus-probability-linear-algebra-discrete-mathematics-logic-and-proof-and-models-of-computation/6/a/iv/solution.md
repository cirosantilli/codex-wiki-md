<h1 id="6/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Inequality on a domain containing at least two people is **not** a [Euclidean relation](../../../../../../euclidean-relation.md). Choose distinct people $a,b$ and put $x=a$, $y=z=b$. Both premises $a\ne b$ hold, but the conclusion would be $b\ne b$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\ne\text{ is not Euclidean on the domain of people}.}
$$

The possibility $y=z$ matters: the defining [universal quantifiers](../../../../../../universal-quantification.md) do not require distinct arguments. On a domain with at most one element the inequality [binary relation](../../../../../../binary-relation.md) is empty and the condition holds by [vacuous truth](../../../../../../vacuous-truth.md).
