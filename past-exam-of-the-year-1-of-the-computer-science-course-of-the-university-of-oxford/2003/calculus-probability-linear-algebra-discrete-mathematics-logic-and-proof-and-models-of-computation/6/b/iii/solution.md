<h1 id="6/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose $R$ is a [reflexive relation](../../../../../../reflexive-relation.md) and a [Euclidean relation](../../../../../../euclidean-relation.md). To prove it is a [symmetric relation](../../../../../../symmetric-relation.md), assume $xRy$. Reflexivity gives $xRx$, so applying the Euclidean implication to $xRy$ and $xRx$ gives $yRx$.

To prove it is a [transitive relation](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md), assume $xRy$ and $yRz$. The symmetry just established gives $yRx$. Apply the Euclidean implication with common predecessor $y$ to $yRx$ and $yRz$; the conclusion is $xRz$. Thus all three requirements hold:

$$
\boxed{R\text{ reflexive and Euclidean}\Longrightarrow R\text{ an equivalence relation}.}
$$
