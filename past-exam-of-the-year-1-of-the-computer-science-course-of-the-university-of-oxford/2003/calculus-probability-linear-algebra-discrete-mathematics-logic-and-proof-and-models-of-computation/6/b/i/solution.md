A [binary relation](../../../../../../binary-relation.md) $R$ on a [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) $X$ is an [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) when it is a [reflexive relation](../../../../../../reflexive-relation.md), a [symmetric relation](../../../../../../symmetric-relation.md), and a [transitive relation](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md). In [first-order logic](../../../../../../first-order-logic.md), these three requirements are

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\forall x\in X\;xRx,\\
&\forall x,y\in X\;(xRy\Rightarrow yRx),\\
&\forall x,y,z\in X\;((xRy\land yRz)\Rightarrow xRz).
\end{aligned}}
$$
