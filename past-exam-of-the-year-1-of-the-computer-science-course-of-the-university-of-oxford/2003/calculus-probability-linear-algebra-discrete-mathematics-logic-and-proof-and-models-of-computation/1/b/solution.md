<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Multiplying the [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) by two suggests using the [dependent variable](../../../../../dependent-variable.md) $Y=y^2$, whose [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) is $Y'=2yy'$. On the interval through $x=1$, this gives the [linear ordinary differential equation](../../../../../linear-ordinary-differential-equation-split.md)

$$
xY'=Y+x^2,\qquad \left(\frac Yx\right)'=1.
$$

After [integration](../../../../../integral-split.md), $Y=x^2+Cx$. The [initial condition](../../../../../initial-condition.md) $Y(1)=4$ gives $C=3$. As $y(1)>0$, [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) selects the positive branch:

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\sqrt{x^2+3x}\quad(x>0),\qquad y(2)=\sqrt{10}.}
$$

The interval $x>0$ is the maximal real interval through the given [initial condition](../../../../../initial-condition.md): at $x=0$ the formula approaches $0$, but its [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) is unbounded, so it does not extend through zero as a differentiable [solution of a differential equation](../../../../../solution-of-a-differential-equation.md).
