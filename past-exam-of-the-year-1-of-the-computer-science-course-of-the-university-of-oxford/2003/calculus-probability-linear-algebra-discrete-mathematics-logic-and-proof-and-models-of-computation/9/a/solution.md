<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The assertion is **true**. Every single finite [string](../../../../../string.md) $w=b_1\cdots b_k$ over $\{0,1\}$ is denoted by the [regular expression](../../../../../regular-expression.md) $b_1\cdots b_k$. The empty [string](../../../../../string.md) is denoted by $\epsilon$. Hence if $F=\{w_1,\ldots,w_m\}$ is a finite [formal language](../../../../../formal-language.md), the [regular expression](../../../../../regular-expression.md)

$$
\boxed{w_1+w_2+\cdots+w_m}
$$

denotes precisely $F$, with each $w_j$ read as its concatenation of symbols. This uses the defining union and concatenation operations for [regular expressions](../../../../../regular-expression.md). For $F=\varnothing$, use the [regular expression](../../../../../regular-expression.md) $\varnothing$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{Every finite binary language is regular}.}
$$
