<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take the [linear map](../../../../../../linear-map.md)

$$
\boxed{T(x,y)=(y,0),\qquad [T]=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Its [kernel of a linear map](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) consists of all $(x,0)$, and its [image of a linear map](../../../../../../image-of-a-linear-map.md) is the same line:

$$
\ker T=\operatorname{im}T=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)\}.
$$

Their [set intersection](../../../../../../set-intersection.md) is nonzero and their sum is only one-dimensional. In particular, $(0,1)$ cannot be written as a sum of one [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) from each of these [vector subspaces](../../../../../../vector-subspace.md). The nonzero [set intersection](../../../../../../set-intersection.md) also prevents an internal [direct sum of vector spaces](../../../../../../direct-sum.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathbb R^2\ne\ker T\oplus\operatorname{im}T.}
$$

The [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) is a nonzero [nilpotent matrix](../../../../../../nilpotent-matrix.md); its failure of [diagonalizability](../../../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md) explains why the preceding result does not apply.
