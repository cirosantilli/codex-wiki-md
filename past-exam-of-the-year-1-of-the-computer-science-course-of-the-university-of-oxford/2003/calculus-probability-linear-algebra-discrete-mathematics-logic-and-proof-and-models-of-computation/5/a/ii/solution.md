<h1 id="5/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The partition theorem is the [law of total probability](../../../../../../law-of-total-probability.md). If $B_1,B_2,\ldots$ form a finite or countable [partition of a sample space](../../../../../../partition-of-a-sample-space.md) into pairwise [disjoint events](../../../../../../disjoint-events.md) with positive [probabilities](../../../../../../probability.md), then for any [event](../../../../../../event.md) $A$,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(A)=\sum_j\mathbb P(A\mid B_j)\mathbb P(B_j).}
$$

Indeed, $A$ is the [disjoint union](../../../../../../disjoint-union.md) of the [events](../../../../../../event.md) $A\cap B_j$, so [countable additivity](../../../../../../countable-additivity.md) gives the formula after applying the definition of [conditional probability](../../../../../../conditional-probability.md). Zero-probability members can be omitted.
