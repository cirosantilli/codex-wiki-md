<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take $V=\mathbb R^2$ and the [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md) $h(x,y)=(y,0)$. Then

$$
\mathcal R(h)=\mathcal N(h)=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)\}.
$$

Their [intersection](../../../../../set-intersection.md) is nonzero and their sum is only one-dimensional. Hence **$V\ne\mathcal R(h)\oplus\mathcal N(h)$**. This [nilpotent linear map](../../../../../nilpotent-linear-map.md) has $h^2=0$, illustrating why taking sufficiently high powers changes the situation.
