<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Apply [structural induction](../../../../../structural-induction.md) to the formation of a [propositional formula](../../../../../propositional-formula.md). Let $L(\theta)$ and $R(\theta)$ count its opening and closing parentheses, and use the usual grammar generated by propositional variables, any allowed truth constants, [negation](../../../../../negation.md), and parenthesized binary [logical connectives](../../../../../logical-connective.md).

An atomic variable or truth constant contains neither kind of parenthesis, so $L=R=0$. If $L(\alpha)=R(\alpha)$, prefixing [negation](../../../../../negation.md) leaves both counts unchanged. If the notation writes a negated formula with an extra pair of parentheses, it adds one to each and still preserves equality.

For a binary construction $(\alpha\circ\beta)$, where $\circ$ is an allowed binary [logical connective](../../../../../logical-connective.md),

$$
L((\alpha\circ\beta))=L(\alpha)+L(\beta)+1,
\qquad R((\alpha\circ\beta))=R(\alpha)+R(\beta)+1.
$$

The two induction hypotheses make these equal. Every grammar construction preserves the property, so **every [propositional formula](../../../../../propositional-formula.md) contains equally many left and right parentheses**. This is [balanced parentheses in propositional syntax](../../../../../balanced-parentheses-in-propositional-syntax.md).
