<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Split $B$ into its [symmetric part](../../../../../symmetric-part-of-a-matrix.md) and [skew-symmetric part](../../../../../skew-symmetric-part-of-a-matrix.md):

$$
\boxed{B=\underbrace{\frac{B+B^\top}{2}}_{S}+\underbrace{\frac{B-B^\top}{2}}_{K}.}
$$

Taking the [transpose](../../../../../transpose.md) gives $S^\top=S$ and $K^\top=-K$. Thus $S$ is a [symmetric matrix](../../../../../symmetric-matrix.md) and $K$ a [skew-symmetric matrix](../../../../../skew-symmetric-matrix.md). These formulas also show uniqueness, since a real [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) that is both symmetric and skew-symmetric is zero.
