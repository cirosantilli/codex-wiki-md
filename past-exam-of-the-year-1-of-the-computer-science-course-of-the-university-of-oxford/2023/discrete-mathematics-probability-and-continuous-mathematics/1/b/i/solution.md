<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For the [product order](../../../../../../product-order.md), [reflexivity](../../../../../../reflexive-relation.md) follows from $a_i\le a_i$ in every coordinate. If $\mathbf a\preceq\mathbf b$ and $\mathbf b\preceq\mathbf a$, the [antisymmetric relation](../../../../../../antisymmetric-relation.md) axiom for the usual real order gives $a_i=b_i$ for every $i$, hence $\mathbf a=\mathbf b$. If $\mathbf a\preceq\mathbf b\preceq\mathbf c$, [transitivity](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md) in each coordinate gives $a_i\le c_i$, hence $\mathbf a\preceq\mathbf c$. Thus this is a [partial order](../../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md).

For $n\ge2$, the [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) $(1,0,0,\ldots)$ and $(0,1,0,\ldots)$ are incomparable: each has a coordinate larger than the corresponding coordinate of the other. **It is not a [total order](../../../../../../total-order.md) for $n\ge2$.** The dimension qualification matters: for $n=1$ this is the usual [total order](../../../../../../total-order.md) on $\mathbb R$, so the printed non-totality claim needs the implicit assumption $n\ge2$.
