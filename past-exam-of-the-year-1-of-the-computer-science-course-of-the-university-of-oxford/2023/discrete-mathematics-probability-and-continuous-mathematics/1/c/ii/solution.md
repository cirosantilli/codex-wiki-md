<h1 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write each member as $3-2/(n+1)$. Every member is strictly below $3$, so $3$ is an [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). If $u<3$, the [Archimedean property](../../../../../../archimedean-property.md) supplies $n\in\mathbb N$ with $n+1>2/(3-u)$; then $3-2/(n+1)>u$. Thus no smaller number is an [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md), proving **$\sup S=3$**. Since $3\notin S$, there is **no [greatest element](../../../../../../greatest-element.md)**. Equivalently, each member is followed by a larger member because $2/(n+2)<2/(n+1)$.
