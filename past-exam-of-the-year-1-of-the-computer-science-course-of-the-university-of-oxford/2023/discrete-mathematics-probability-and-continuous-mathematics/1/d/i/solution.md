<h1 id="1/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For every $a$, both $aRa$ and $aSa$ hold, so the [intersection of equivalence relations](../../../../../../intersection-of-equivalence-relations.md) is [reflexive](../../../../../../reflexive-relation.md). If $a(R\cap S)b$, [symmetry](../../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) of both component [binary relations](../../../../../../binary-relation.md) gives $bRa$ and $bSa$, hence $b(R\cap S)a$. Finally, if $a(R\cap S)b$ and $b(R\cap S)c$, [transitivity](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md) in each component gives $aRc$ and $aSc$, hence $a(R\cap S)c$. These are precisely the three conditions for an [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md).
