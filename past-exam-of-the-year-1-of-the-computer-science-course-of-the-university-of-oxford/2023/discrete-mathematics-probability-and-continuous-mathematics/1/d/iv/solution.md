<h1 id="1/d/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Take the nonempty intersections of the length-three and length-four blocks. Within the first twelve [nonnegative integers](../../../../../../natural-number.md) the common cut points are $0,3,4,6,8,9,12$, giving

$$
\{0,1,2\},\quad\{3\},\quad\{4,5\},\quad\{6,7\},\quad\{8\},\quad\{9,10,11\}.
$$

The complete [partition of a set](../../../../../../partition-of-a-set.md) consists of these six blocks translated by $12j$ for every $j\in\mathbb N$. Indeed, adding $12$ increases the two floor quotients by $4$ and $3$ respectively, preserving equality of both quotients within each translated block. No adjacent displayed blocks can merge because at least one quotient changes at their boundary. This is the [periodic common refinement of floor-quotient partitions](../../../../../../periodic-common-refinement-of-floor-quotient-partitions.md), with period $\operatorname{lcm}(3,4)=12$.
