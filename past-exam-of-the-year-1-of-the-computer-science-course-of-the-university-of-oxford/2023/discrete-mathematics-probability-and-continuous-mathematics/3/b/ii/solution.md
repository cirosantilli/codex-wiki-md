<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $p\mid a$, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise the only possible positive common divisors of the [prime](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md) $p$ and $a$ show that $\gcd(p,a)=1$. By [Bézout's identity](../../../../../../bezout-s-identity.md), $up+va=1$ for some [integers](../../../../../../integer-split.md) $u,v$. Multiplying by $b$ gives $b=upb+vab$. Both terms on the right are divisible by $p$, the second because $p\mid ab$. Hence **$p\mid b$**. This proves [Euclid's lemma](../../../../../../euclid-s-lemma.md), including when either factor is zero or negative.
