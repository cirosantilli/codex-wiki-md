<h1 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose $n=p_1\cdots p_r=q_1\cdots q_s$ are two products of positive [primes](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md). If $r=0$, then $n=1$, and necessarily $s=0$, since any nonempty product of positive [primes](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md) is at least $2$. Otherwise [Euclid's lemma](../../../../../../euclid-s-lemma.md), applied repeatedly to the right-hand product, says $p_1$ divides one $q_j$. Since $q_j$ is [prime](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md), $p_1=q_j$. Reorder the right-hand factors and cancel this common nonzero factor. Repeating, or using [induction](../../../../../../mathematical-induction.md) on the number of factors, matches every factor. Neither side can have factors left over, because their product would then equal $1$. Thus **the [prime factorization](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md) is unique up to rearrangement**.
