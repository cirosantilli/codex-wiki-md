<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use [strong induction](../../../../../../strong-induction.md) on the positive [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) $n$. The case $n=1$ is represented by the [empty product](../../../../../../empty-product.md). For $n>1$, if $n$ is [prime](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md) it is already a product of one [prime](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md). Otherwise $n=ab$ with $1<a,b<n$; the [induction hypothesis](../../../../../../induction-hypothesis.md) gives [prime factorizations](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md) for both $a$ and $b$, and concatenating them gives one for $n$. This proves existence in the [fundamental theorem of arithmetic](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md) without presupposing uniqueness.
