<h1 id="9/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [events](../../../../../../event.md) $\{X=k\}$, for $k\in\mathbb Z$, are pairwise disjoint. Their union is $\{X\in\mathbb Z\}$, and the [integers](../../../../../../integer-split.md) form a [countable set](../../../../../../countable-set.md). By [countable additivity](../../../../../../countable-additivity.md),

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(X\in\mathbb Z)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb P(X=k)=0.}
$$

Countability is the necessary justification: one cannot apply this argument to an arbitrary uncountable union of zero-probability singletons. This is a special case of [countable sets have zero probability under a density](../../../../../../countable-sets-have-zero-probability-under-a-density.md).
