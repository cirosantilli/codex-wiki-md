<h1 id="4/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Since $\mathbf a\ne0$, the line $\mathbf x=t\mathbf a/\|\mathbf a\|^2$ lies in the domain and satisfies $\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x=t$. The restriction of $l$ to this line is $t^3$, whose second [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is $6t$: negative for $t<0$ and positive for $t>0$. It can be neither globally [convex](../../../../../../convex-function.md) nor globally [concave](../../../../../../concave-function.md). Hence **$l$ is neither**. Equivalently, its [Hessian matrix](../../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) is $6(\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x)\mathbf a\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}$, changing the sign of its nonzero directional curvature across $\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x=0$.
