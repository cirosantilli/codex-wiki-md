<h1 id="4/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The denominator is positive throughout the domain. Holding $y$ fixed, differentiate with respect to $x$:

$$
k_x(x,y)=\frac{1-y^2}{(1+xy)^2},\qquad k_{xx}(x,y)=\frac{2y(y^2-1)}{(1+xy)^3}.
$$

At the interior point $(1,1/2)$ this second [partial derivative](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md) is $-2/9<0$, so the restriction to a horizontal line is locally strictly [concave](../../../../../../concave-function.md), ruling out [convexity](../../../../../../convex-function.md). At the interior point $(1,2)$ it is $4/9>0$, ruling out [concavity](../../../../../../concave-function.md). Restrictions of a [convex function](../../../../../../convex-function.md) or a [concave function](../../../../../../concave-function.md) to every line segment must retain that property. Consequently **$k$ is neither [convex](../../../../../../convex-function.md) nor [concave](../../../../../../concave-function.md)** on its stated domain.
