<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For $q=1,\ldots,5$, choose the first $q$ elements of $\{1,2,4,8,16\}$. Every [sum of a subset](../../../../../sum-of-a-subset.md) has a unique [binary representation](../../../../../binary-number.md), so distinct [subsets](../../../../../subset.md) cannot have the same sum. To see this directly, take the largest power of two belonging to exactly one of two proposed [subsets](../../../../../subset.md). It exceeds the sum of all smaller powers, and therefore cannot be cancelled by their difference. Thus these chosen sets are [dissociated sets](../../../../../dissociated-set.md) and do not have the two-sum property.

If cardinality zero is included, the empty [set](../../../../../set-split.md) has only one [subset](../../../../../subset.md), so it cannot supply two distinct ones either. **There is a [counterexample](../../../../../counterexample.md) at every cardinality from $0$ through $5$.**
