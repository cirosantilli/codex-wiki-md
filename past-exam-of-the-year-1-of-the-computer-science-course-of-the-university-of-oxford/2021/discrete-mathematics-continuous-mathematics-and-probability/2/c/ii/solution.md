<h1 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Check each [partial order](../../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md) axiom. For every $(a,b)$, $a\leq a$ and $b\geq b$, so $S$ is a [reflexive relation](../../../../../../reflexive-relation.md). If both $(a,b)S(c,d)$ and $(c,d)S(a,b)$, the first coordinates satisfy $a\leq c\leq a$ and the second coordinates satisfy $b\geq d\geq b$. Therefore $a=c$ and $b=d$, which proves $S$ is an [antisymmetric relation](../../../../../../antisymmetric-relation.md).

Finally, if $(a,b)S(c,d)$ and $(c,d)S(e,f)$, then $a\leq c\leq e$ and $b\geq d\geq f$. Hence $(a,b)S(e,f)$, making $S$ a [transitive relation](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md). All three axioms hold, so **$S$ is a partial order**. It is a [product order](../../../../../../product-order.md), with the usual order on the first coordinate and the reversed order on the second.
