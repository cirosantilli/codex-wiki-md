<h1 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Suppose, for a [proof by contradiction](../../../../../../reductio-ad-absurdum.md), that an [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) $n$ is both an [even number](../../../../../../even-number.md) and an [odd number](../../../../../../odd-number.md). Then $n=2u$ and $n=2v+1$ for some [integers](../../../../../../integer-split.md) $u,v$. Subtracting yields $u-v=1/2$. The left side is an [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md), while $1/2$ is not. This contradiction proves that **no [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) is both even and odd**.
