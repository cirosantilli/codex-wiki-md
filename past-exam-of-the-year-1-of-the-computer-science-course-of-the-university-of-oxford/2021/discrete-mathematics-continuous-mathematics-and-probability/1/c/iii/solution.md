<h1 id="1/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Take **$(a,b,c,n)=(1,4,2,6)$**. These are four distinct positive [integers](../../../../../../integer-split.md) and $n\geq3$. Their products are $ac=2$ and $bc=8$, whose difference is $6$, so $ac\equiv bc\pmod6$. Here the correct reduced modulus is $6/\gcd(2,6)=3$, and indeed $1\equiv4\pmod3$. The example illustrates why cancelling a noninvertible factor may change the modulus in [modular arithmetic](../../../../../../modular-arithmetic-split.md).
