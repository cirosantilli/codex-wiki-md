<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $d=\gcd(c,n)$, and write $c=dc'$, $n=dn'$ with positive [integers](../../../../../../integer-split.md) $c',n'$. If an [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) $e>1$ divided both $c'$ and $n'$, then $de$ would divide both $c$ and $n$, contradicting the maximality of their [greatest common divisor](../../../../../../greatest-common-divisor.md) $d$. Thus **$\gcd(c',n')=1$**. Equivalently, [Bézout's identity](../../../../../../bezout-s-identity.md) for $c,n$ can be divided by $d$ to express $1$ as an [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) combination of $c',n'$.
