<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Divide the [divisibility](../../../../../../divisibility-split.md) relation $91\mid26x-39$ by $13$ to obtain $7\mid2x-3$. Since $2\cdot4\equiv1\pmod7$, multiplication by the [modular multiplicative inverse](../../../../../../modular-multiplicative-inverse.md) gives $x\equiv12\equiv5\pmod7$. Therefore all [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) solutions are $x=5+7q$, $q\in\mathbb Z$.

Modulo $91=13\cdot7$, the choices $q=0,\ldots,12$ give

$$
\boxed{x\equiv5,12,19,26,33,40,47,54,61,68,75,82,89\pmod{91}.}
$$

They are distinct [residue classes](../../../../../../residue-class.md): equality of two such classes would imply $91\mid7(q-q')$, hence $13\mid q-q'$. Every [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) $q$ is congruent to one of those thirteen choices, so the list is exhaustive.
