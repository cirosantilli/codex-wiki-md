<h1 id="8/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $R_1,R_2$ be the two red-card [events](../../../../../../event.md). Sampling without replacement gives

$$
\mathbb P(R_1\cap R_2)=\frac mN\frac{m-1}{N-1}.
$$

This differs from the product $m^2/N^2$, since

$$
\mathbb P(R_1\cap R_2)-\mathbb P(R_1)\mathbb P(R_2)
=-\frac{mn}{N^2(N-1)}<0.
$$

Thus **the events are not independent**, for every positive $m,n$. A first red card reduces the remaining red fraction, a basic instance of how [sampling without replacement need not be independent](../../../../../../sampling-without-replacement-need-not-be-independent.md).
