<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The coefficient assumptions imply a nonconstant [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) with $p(0)<0$ and a positive [leading coefficient](../../../../../leading-coefficient-of-a-polynomial.md). Thus $p(x)\to+\infty$ as $x\to+\infty$: dividing by its leading power of $x$ leaves the positive [leading coefficient](../../../../../leading-coefficient-of-a-polynomial.md) plus terms tending to zero. There is some $B>0$ with $p(B)>0$. A [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is continuous, so the [intermediate value theorem](../../../../../intermediate-value-theorem.md) supplies **at least one positive [root of a polynomial](../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md)** in $(0,B)$.

This gives neither uniqueness nor monotonicity. Multiple crossings are possible. If the [degree of a polynomial](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) is an [even integer](../../../../../even-number.md), the same leading-term argument gives $p(x)\to+\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$, so there is also a negative [root of a polynomial](../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md). For an [odd integer](../../../../../odd-integer.md) [degree of a polynomial](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) no negative [root of a polynomial](../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) is forced; a linear [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) with negative constant coefficient and positive slope is a counterexample.
