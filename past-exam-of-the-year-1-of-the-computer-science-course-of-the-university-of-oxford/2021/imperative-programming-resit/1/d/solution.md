<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Every positive [divisor](../../../../../divisor.md) has a unique [prime factorization](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md) $p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_k^{e_k}$ with $0\leq e_j\leq i_j$. Conversely every such exponent choice gives a [divisor](../../../../../divisor.md). The exponent choices are independent, so the [product rule](../../../../../product-rule.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\tau(n)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(i_j+1).}
$$

For $n=1$ the factorization is empty, and the [empty product](../../../../../empty-product.md) is one, corresponding to the [divisor](../../../../../divisor.md) one.
