<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

More [processor registers](../../../../../processor-register.md) can reduce [register spills](../../../../../spilling-a-register.md) and the associated loads and stores. A richer instruction set may also replace several old [machine instructions](../../../../../machine-instruction.md) by one new operation. Both effects can **decrease the number of instructions** in a compiled program, although a particular program might obtain no benefit.

Wider [instruction encodings](../../../../../instruction-encoding.md) increase the [bytes](../../../../../byte.md) per instruction. Consequently **the total machine-code size can increase or decrease**: approximately, it is instruction count times average encoded instruction width, with alignment and padding also relevant. There is no universal net direction without the program and revised encoding. The length of [assembly language](../../../../../assembly-language.md) source text is a separate matter and is not determined by instruction [bit](../../../../../bit.md) widths.
