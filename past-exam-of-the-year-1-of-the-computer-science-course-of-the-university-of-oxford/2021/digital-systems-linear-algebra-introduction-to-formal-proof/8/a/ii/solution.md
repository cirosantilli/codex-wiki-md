<h1 id="8/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Temporarily assume $Q(c)$ under label $a$. The implication premise and the [modus ponens rule](../../../../../../implication-elimination-rule.md) yield $\forall x\,(P(x)\leftrightarrow P(c))$. Instantiate it at $c'$ using the [universal quantifier elimination rule](../../../../../../universal-quantifier-elimination-rule.md), obtaining $P(c')\leftrightarrow P(c)$. Its right [biconditional elimination rule](../../../../../../biconditional-elimination-rule.md) gives $P(c)\to P(c')$.

Apply the [modus ponens rule](../../../../../../implication-elimination-rule.md) with the premise $P(c)$ to obtain $P(c')$. Together with $\neg P(c')$, [negation elimination rule](../../../../../../negation-elimination-rule.md) gives $\bot$. Discharge $a$ using the [negation introduction rule](../../../../../../negation-introduction-rule.md) to conclude **$\neg Q(c)$**. Nothing assumes that the two constant symbols denote different objects; if they denoted the same object, the stated premises would themselves already be inconsistent.
