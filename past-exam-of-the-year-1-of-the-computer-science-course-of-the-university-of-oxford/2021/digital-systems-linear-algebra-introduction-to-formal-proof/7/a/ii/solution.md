<h1 id="7/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Keep the four given premises in context and assume $P$ under label $a$. The [modus ponens rule](../../../../../../implication-elimination-rule.md) applied to the first two premises gives $Q\lor R$ and $\neg S$. Apply the [disjunction elimination rule](../../../../../../disjunction-elimination-rule.md) to $Q\lor R$ as follows.

Under a case assumption $Q$, the premise $Q\to S$ gives $S$ by the [modus ponens rule](../../../../../../implication-elimination-rule.md). Together with $\neg S$, the [negation elimination rule](../../../../../../negation-elimination-rule.md) gives $\bot$, and the supplied [logical falsity elimination rule](../../../../../../logical-falsity-elimination-rule.md) gives $R$. Under the other case assumption $R$, the required conclusion $R$ is already available. Discharge both case assumptions with the [disjunction elimination rule](../../../../../../disjunction-elimination-rule.md) to conclude $R$.

Finally discharge $a$ with the [implication introduction rule](../../../../../../implication-introduction-rule.md), giving **$P\to R$**. The premise $S\to Q$ is not needed: a derivation is permitted to leave a premise unused.
