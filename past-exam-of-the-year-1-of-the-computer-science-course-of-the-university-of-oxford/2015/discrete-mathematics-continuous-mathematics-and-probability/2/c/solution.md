<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For adjacent [binomial coefficients](../../../../../binomial-coefficient-split.md), the [ratio](../../../../../ratio.md)

$$
\frac{\binom n{k+1}}{\binom nk}=\frac{n-k}{k+1}
$$

is greater than $1$ when $k<(n-1)/2$, equal to $1$ at $k=(n-1)/2$, and less than $1$ afterwards. For odd $n$, the row is therefore increasing up to its two equal central entries and decreasing after them. The [maximum](../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) is attained exactly at

$$
\boxed{k^*=\frac{n-1}{2}\quad\text{or}\quad k^*=\frac{n+1}{2}.}
$$

They are distinct, including when $n=1$, so $k^*$ is **not unique**. This is the [unimodality of binomial coefficients](../../../../../unimodality-of-binomial-coefficients.md).
