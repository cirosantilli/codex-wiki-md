<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In the intended range $0\le k\le n$, division by the positive [binomial coefficient](../../../../../../binomial-coefficient-split.md) $\binom nk$ gives

$$
\frac{n-2k-1}{k+1}=1,
\qquad n=3k+2.
$$

Consequently the intended [remainder](../../../../../../remainder.md) is

$$
\boxed{n\bmod3=2.}
$$

There is a necessary qualification to the printed unrestricted [domain](../../../../../../domain-mathematical-analysis.md). If [binomial coefficients](../../../../../../binomial-coefficient-split.md) outside $0\le k\le n$ are extended by zero, then every $k>n$ satisfies $g(k)=\binom nk=0$, regardless of $n\bmod3$. For example, $n=3,k=4$ satisfies the literal equality but has $n\bmod3=0$. Thus the literal extended-domain premise alone does not determine a [remainder](../../../../../../remainder.md); the boxed conclusion requires $0\le k\le n$, or equivalently here $\binom nk\ne0$.
