<h1 id="2/e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a nonzero [geometric sequence](../../../../../../geometric-progression.md), substitute $H_n=cq^n$ into the [linear recurrence](../../../../../../linear-recurrence-relation.md) and cancel $cq^{n-1}$:

$$
q^2=q+1.
$$

The [characteristic equation of a linear recurrence](../../../../../../characteristic-equation-of-a-linear-recurrence.md) therefore has roots

$$
\boxed{q=\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\quad\text{or}\quad
q=\psi=\frac{1-\sqrt5}{2}.}
$$

The zero [sequence](../../../../../../sequence.md) $c=0$ is a degenerate solution for any $q$ for which the displayed expression is defined. A nonzero [sequence](../../../../../../sequence.md) with $q=0$ cannot satisfy the recurrence at $n=1$, since it would require $0=0+H_0$.
