<h1 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $f(x)=f(y)$, applying the [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) $f$ gives $f(f(x))=f(f(y))$. Since $f\circ f=\operatorname{id}_A$, this implies $x=y$, proving [injectivity](../../../../../../injective-function.md). Also every $y\in A$ is the image of $f(y)$, because $f(f(y))=y$, proving [surjectivity](../../../../../../surjective-function.md).

Thus $f$ is a [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md) and its [inverse function](../../../../../../inverse-function.md) is itself:

$$
\boxed{f^{-1}=f.}
$$

Such a [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) is an [involution](../../../../../../involution.md).
