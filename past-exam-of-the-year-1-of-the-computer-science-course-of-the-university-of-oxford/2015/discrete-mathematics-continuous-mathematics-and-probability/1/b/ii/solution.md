<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

We use a [double inclusion proof](../../../../../../double-inclusion-proof.md). First, let $x\in\overline{A\oplus B}$. If $x\in A$, then $x\in B$, since otherwise $x\in A\setminus B\subseteq A\oplus B$. Hence in this case $x\in A\cap B$. If $x\notin A$, then $x\notin B$, since otherwise $x\in B\setminus A\subseteq A\oplus B$. Hence in this case $x\in\overline{A\cup B}$. This proves the first [set inclusion](../../../../../../set-inclusion.md).

Conversely, an element of $A\cap B$ belongs to neither $A\setminus B$ nor $B\setminus A$. An element of $\overline{A\cup B}$ also belongs to neither [set difference](../../../../../../set-difference.md). Thus every element of $(A\cap B)\cup\overline{A\cup B}$ lies outside $A\oplus B$, proving the reverse [set inclusion](../../../../../../set-inclusion.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\overline{A\oplus B}=(A\cap B)\cup\overline{A\cup B}.}
$$
