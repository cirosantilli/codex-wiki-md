<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) of the [Jacobian matrix](../../../../../../jacobian-matrix.md) is $s^2+t^2$. Wherever $(s,t)\ne(0,0)$, the [inverse function theorem](../../../../../../inverse-function-theorem.md) permits a local inverse of the [parabolic coordinates](../../../../../../parabolic-coordinates.md) transformation, and

$$
\frac{\partial(s,t)}{\partial(x,y)}
=J^{-1}
=\frac1{s^2+t^2}\begin{pmatrix}t&-s\\s&t\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Reading off its entries gives

$$
\boxed{s_x=\frac t{s^2+t^2},\quad s_y=-\frac s{s^2+t^2},\quad
 t_x=\frac s{s^2+t^2},\quad t_y=\frac t{s^2+t^2}.}
$$

These are local formulas on a chosen branch: $(s,t)$ and $(-s,-t)$ have the same image, and at the origin the [Jacobian matrix](../../../../../../jacobian-matrix.md) is singular, so this inverse-derivative formula does not apply there.
