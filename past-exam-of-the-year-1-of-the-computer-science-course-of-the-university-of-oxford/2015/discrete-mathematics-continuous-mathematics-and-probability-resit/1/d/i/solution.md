<h1 id="1/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For every [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) $S$, the definition of a [power set](../../../../../../power-set.md) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
S\in\mathcal P(A\cap B)
&\Longleftrightarrow S\subseteq A\cap B\\
&\Longleftrightarrow (S\subseteq A\text{ and }S\subseteq B)\\
&\Longleftrightarrow S\in\mathcal P(A)\cap\mathcal P(B).
\end{aligned}
$$

Equality by identical membership therefore proves

$$
\boxed{\mathcal P(A\cap B)=\mathcal P(A)\cap\mathcal P(B).}
$$

The essential observation is that being a [subset](../../../../../../subset.md) of a [set intersection](../../../../../../set-intersection.md) means being a [subset](../../../../../../subset.md) of both factors.
