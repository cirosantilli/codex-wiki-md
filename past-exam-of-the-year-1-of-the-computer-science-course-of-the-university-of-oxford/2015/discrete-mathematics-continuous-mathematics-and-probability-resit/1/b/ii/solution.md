↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $0\leq x<y$, then $\sqrt x<\sqrt y$ and $x<y$, so $\sqrt x+x<\sqrt y+y$. Thus the [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) is [strictly increasing](../../../../../../strictly-increasing-function.md) on its [domain](../../../../../../domain-mathematical-analysis.md) and is [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md). Its values are nonnegative, so it is not [surjective](../../../../../../surjective-function.md) onto $\mathbb R$:

$$
\boxed{\text{one-to-one, but not onto}.}
$$

If $\mathbb R_+$ means $(0,\infty)$ its [image of a function](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) is $(0,\infty)$; if it includes zero its [image of a function](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) is $[0,\infty)$. Indeed, for any $u>0$, set $t=(\sqrt{1+4u}-1)/2>0$ and $x=t^2$; then $\sqrt x+x=t+t^2=u$. This convention does not change the classification.
