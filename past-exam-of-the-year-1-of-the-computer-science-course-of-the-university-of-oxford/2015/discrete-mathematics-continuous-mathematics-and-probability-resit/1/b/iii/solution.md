<h1 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

With the convention $\mathbb N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$, the even inputs $2j$ map to $j$ for $j\geq0$, while the odd inputs $2j+1$ map to $-(j+1)$ for $j\geq0$. These two [images of a function](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) are disjoint and together equal $\mathbb Z$. Within either class there are no repeated outputs. More explicitly the [inverse function](../../../../../../inverse-function.md) is

$$
\boxed{f^{-1}(z)=\begin{cases}2z,&z\geq0,\\-2z-1,&z<0.\end{cases}}
$$

Consequently $f$ is both [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md) and [surjective](../../../../../../surjective-function.md), hence a [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md). If instead $\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, the input zero is absent: $f$ remains [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md) but its [image of a function](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) is $\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, so it is not onto. The printed paper does not specify this convention; the zero-based convention gives the apparent intended [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md).
