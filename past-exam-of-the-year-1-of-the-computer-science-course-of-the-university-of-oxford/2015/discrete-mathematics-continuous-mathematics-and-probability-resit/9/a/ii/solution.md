<h1 id="9/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [cosine function](../../../../../../cosine.md) is positive on $(0,\pi/2)$ and negative on $(\pi/2,2)$. Thus any nonzero $\alpha$ makes $\alpha\cos x$ negative on an interval of positive length. Taking $\alpha=0$ makes the total [definite integral](../../../../../../definite-integral.md) zero. Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{No real value of }\alpha\text{ gives a probability density.}}
$$

Normalization alone would suggest $\alpha=1/\sin2$, but that candidate still violates nonnegativity and therefore is not a [probability density function](../../../../../../probability-density-function.md).
