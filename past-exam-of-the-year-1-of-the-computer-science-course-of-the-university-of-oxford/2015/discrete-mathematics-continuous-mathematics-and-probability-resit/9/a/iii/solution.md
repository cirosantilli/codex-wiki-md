<h1 id="9/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

We need $0<\alpha<2$. Indeed, if $\alpha<0$, the [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) is negative on $(\alpha,0)$; if $\alpha=0$, the [improper integral](../../../../../../improper-integral.md) diverges at zero; and if $\alpha\geq2$, its support interval is empty. For $0<\alpha<2$,

$$
\int_\alpha^2\frac{dx}{x}=\log\frac2\alpha.
$$

Setting this equal to one gives the unique admissible value

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac2e.}
$$

On this interval $1/x>0$, so both requirements for a [probability density function](../../../../../../probability-density-function.md) are met.
