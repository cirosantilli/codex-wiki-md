<h1 id="2/d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For each unordered pair $\{a,b\}$ with $a\ne b$, [antisymmetry](../../../../../../antisymmetry.md) allows three choices: neither direction, only $(a,b)$, or only $(b,a)$. This contributes $3^{\binom n2}$. The diagonal is unrestricted by [antisymmetry](../../../../../../antisymmetry.md), but being not [irreflexive](../../../../../../irreflexive-relation.md) requires a nonempty [subset](../../../../../../subset.md) of the $n$ diagonal pairs. There are $2^n-1$ such choices. Hence

$$
\boxed{(2^n-1)3^{\binom n2}.}
$$

This allows some diagonal pairs to be absent; “not [irreflexive](../../../../../../irreflexive-relation.md)” does not require a [reflexive relation](../../../../../../reflexive-relation.md).
