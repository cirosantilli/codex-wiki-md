<h1 id="6/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At $x=0$, $g''(0)=36>0$, so the [second-derivative test](../../../../../../second-derivative-test.md) gives a strict local [minimum](../../../../../../minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md). In fact $g'(x)=4x(x-3)^2$ is negative for $x<0$ and positive for $x>0$ except at $x=3$, so $g(0)=5$ is the global [minimum](../../../../../../minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) as well.

At $x=3$, the [second-derivative test](../../../../../../second-derivative-test.md) is inconclusive because $g''(3)=0$. But $g''(x)=12(x-1)(x-3)$ changes from negative to positive across $3$, while $g'(3)=0$. Hence this is a [stationary inflection point](../../../../../../stationary-inflection-point.md). Equivalently, the leading nonconstant term $4(x-3)^3$ in the [Taylor polynomial](../../../../../../taylor-polynomial.md) changes sign across $3$. The paper explicitly uses “saddle point” to mean an [inflection point](../../../../../../inflection-point.md), so in its terminology the classification is

$$
\boxed{x=0:\text{ minimum};\qquad x=3:\text{ saddle point};\qquad\text{no maxima}.}
$$

Here the “saddle point” is a one-dimensional [stationary inflection point](../../../../../../stationary-inflection-point.md), rather than the usual multivariable use of [saddle point of a scalar function](../../../../../../saddle-point-of-a-scalar-function.md).[Page 8, Question 6(b)(iii), explicit definition of “saddle point”](../../../../../../discrete-mathematics-continuous-mathematics-and-probability-resit-examination-by-the-university-of-oxford-2015.md)
