<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $c_1,\ldots,c_4$ be the original columns. The first three are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md): restricting them to rows 2, 3 and 4 gives the triangular [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md)

$$
\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
$$

whose [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) is one. Also $c_4=c_1-c_2+c_3$, so a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) for the [column space](../../../../../column-space.md) is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal B_{\rm col}=\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}\right\}.}
$$

These are columns of the original [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md). Taking columns of its [reduced row echelon form](../../../../../reduced-row-echelon-form.md) would generally give the wrong [column space](../../../../../column-space.md).
