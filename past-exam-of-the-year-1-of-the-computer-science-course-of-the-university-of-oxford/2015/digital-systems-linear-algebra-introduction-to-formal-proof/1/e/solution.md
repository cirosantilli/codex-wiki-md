<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The factorization of the [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md) shows that $\lambda=1$ is an [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md). To find its normalized [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md), solve $(P-I)v=0$ together with $v_C+v_H+v_B=1$. The first two independent [linear equations](../../../../../linear-equation.md) are

$$
-4v_C+v_H+v_B=0,\qquad v_C-2v_H+2v_B=0.
$$

The first equation and normalization give $5v_C=1$. The second gives $v_H-v_B=v_C/2=1/10$, while $v_H+v_B=4/5$. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{v=\begin{pmatrix}1/5\\9/20\\7/20\end{pmatrix},\qquad Pv=v.}
$$

All entries are positive and sum to one. This [probability vector](../../../../../probability-vector.md) is the [stationary distribution](../../../../../stationary-distribution.md).
