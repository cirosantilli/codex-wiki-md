<h1 id="6/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Define $f:W_1\cap W_2\to U$ by $f(u)=(u,-u)$. It is well-defined by the definition of $U$, and

$$
f(au+bv)=(au+bv,-au-bv)=af(u)+bf(v).
$$

Thus $f$ is a [linear map](../../../../../../linear-map.md). If $f(u)=f(v)$, comparing first components gives $u=v$, proving [injectivity](../../../../../../injective-function.md). Every element of $U$ has the form $(u,-u)=f(u)$, proving [surjectivity](../../../../../../surjective-function.md). Hence **$f$ is a [linear isomorphism](../../../../../../linear-isomorphism.md)**, with inverse $(u,-u)\mapsto u$, and $\dim U=\dim(W_1\cap W_2)$.
