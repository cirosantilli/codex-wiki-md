<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Work over the [field](../../../../../field.md) containing the stated $n$ distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $B$; in a real formulation these must be real to obtain real [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md). Choose one [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) for each distinct [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md). By [independence of eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues](../../../../../independence-of-eigenvectors-for-distinct-eigenvalues.md), these $n$ [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) are a [basis](../../../../../basis.md). Consequently each [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) $E_\lambda(B)$ has [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) one: in an expansion in this [eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenbasis.md), $Bw=\lambda w$ forces every coefficient with a different [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) to vanish.

Now let $v\ne0$ be any [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $B$. By part (d), $Av$ belongs to its one-dimensional [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md), so $Av=\mu v$ for some [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md) $\mu$. This proves that **$v$ is an [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $A$**, including the case $Av=0$, where $\mu=0$. This is the crucial one-dimensional step in [simple spectrum gives simultaneous diagonalization](../../../../../simple-spectrum-gives-simultaneous-diagonalization.md).
