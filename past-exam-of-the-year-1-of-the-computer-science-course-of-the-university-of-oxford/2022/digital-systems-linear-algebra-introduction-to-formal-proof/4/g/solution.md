<h1 id="4/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

For $n=2$, take

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=I_2.
$$

They are [commuting operators](../../../../../commuting-operators.md) because every [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) commutes with the [identity matrix](../../../../../identity-matrix.md). However $A$ has only the [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) zero, and $\ker A=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)^T\}$ has [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) one. It has no [eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenbasis.md), so it cannot appear in any [simultaneous diagonalization](../../../../../simultaneous-diagonalization.md). Equivalently, a [diagonal matrix](../../../../../diagonal-matrix.md) similar to this nonzero [nilpotent matrix](../../../../../nilpotent-matrix.md) would have to be zero, an impossibility. For any prescribed $n\ge2$, use $A=E_{12}$ and $B=I_n$; its zero [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) has [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) $n-1$. For $n=1$ no such example exists.
