<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $PAP^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)$ and $PBP^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_n)$. Then

$$
P(A+B)P^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1+\beta_1,\ldots,\alpha_n+\beta_n).
$$

By [matrix similarity](../../../../../matrix-similarity.md), **every [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $A+B$ is $\alpha_i+\beta_i$ for some $i$**. These are corresponding [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) on the common [eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenbasis.md), rather than every possible pairwise sum. This is [eigenvalue addition in a common eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenvalue-addition-in-a-common-eigenbasis.md).
