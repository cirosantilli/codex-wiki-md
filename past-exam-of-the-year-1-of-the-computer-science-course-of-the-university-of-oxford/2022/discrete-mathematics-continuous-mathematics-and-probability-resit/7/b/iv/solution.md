<h1 id="7/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**There is no such value of $p$ in the specified interval.** Indeed, $\mathbb P(Y=1)=pe^{-p}>0$, and the [joint probability mass function](../../../../../../joint-probability-mass-function.md) gives

$$
\mathbb P(X=1\mid Y=1)=1\ne p=\mathbb P(X=1).
$$

This contradicts [independence](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) for every $0<p<0.8$. Equivalently, $(1,2)$ has joint [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) zero but the product of its marginal [probabilities](../../../../../../probability.md) is $p\,e^{-p}p^2/2>0$.

At the suggested value $r=e^{-r}$, the [covariance](../../../../../../covariance.md) is zero, but the same [conditional probability](../../../../../../conditional-probability.md) contradiction still applies. This is an explicit example of how [uncorrelated Bernoulli and Poisson variables need not be independent](../../../../../../uncorrelated-bernoulli-and-poisson-variables-need-not-be-independent.md). Zero [covariance](../../../../../../covariance.md) controls one mixed [moment](../../../../../../moment.md), whereas [independence](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) requires the entire [joint probability mass function](../../../../../../joint-probability-mass-function.md) to factor.
