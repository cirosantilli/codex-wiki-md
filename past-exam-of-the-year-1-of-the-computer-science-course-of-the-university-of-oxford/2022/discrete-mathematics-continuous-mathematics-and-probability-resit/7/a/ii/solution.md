↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [random variables](../../../../../../random-variable-split.md) are [independent random variables](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) if the [events](../../../../../../event.md) determined by one are independent of the [events](../../../../../../event.md) determined by the other. For [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md)-valued variables, this is equivalent to the factorization

$$
\boxed{p_{X,Y}(x,y)=p_X(x)p_Y(y)\quad\hbox{for every }x,y\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

Indeed, [independence](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) gives the factorization by taking singleton [events](../../../../../../event.md). Conversely, summing it over $x\in A$ and $y\in B$, for any [subsets](../../../../../../subset.md) $A,B\subseteq\mathbb Z$, gives $\mathbb P(X\in A,Y\in B)=\mathbb P(X\in A)\mathbb P(Y\in B)$. Thus the singleton condition implies [independence](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of all [events](../../../../../../event.md) in question.
