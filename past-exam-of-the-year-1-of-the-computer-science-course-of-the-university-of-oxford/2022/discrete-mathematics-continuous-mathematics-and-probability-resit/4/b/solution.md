<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Assume $f$ and $g$ are continuously differentiable near the point. At a regular [feasible point](../../../../../feasible-point.md), meaning $g(x)=0$ and $\nabla g(x)\ne0$, a differentiable constrained local extremum satisfies the [Lagrange multiplier](../../../../../lagrange-multiplier.md) equations

$$
\nabla f(x)=\lambda\nabla g(x),\qquad g(x)=0.
$$

They arise by differentiating the [optimization Lagrangian](../../../../../optimization-lagrangian.md) $L(x,\lambda)=f(x)-\lambda g(x)$ with respect to all coordinates and the multiplier. Geometrically, the objective has zero [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) along the [tangent space](../../../../../tangent-space.md) of the [constraint](../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md) surface, so its [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) must lie in the normal direction.

Solve these equations for candidates, examine [feasible points](../../../../../feasible-point.md) where the regularity condition fails separately, and compare objective values or use a second-order test restricted to feasible directions. The equations locate [stationary points](../../../../../stationary-point.md); they do not by themselves establish which are minima or whether a [global minimum](../../../../../global-minimum.md) is attained.
