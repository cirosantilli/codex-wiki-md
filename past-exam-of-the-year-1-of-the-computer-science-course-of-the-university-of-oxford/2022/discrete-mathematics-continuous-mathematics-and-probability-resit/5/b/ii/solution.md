<h1 id="5/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) vanishes at $0$. Decompose any [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) as $x=tb+w$, with $w\perp b$. [Symmetry](../../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) of $A$ and $Ab=\tfrac12b$ imply $b^TAw=(Ab)^Tw=0$. Since $\|b\|=1$,

$$
g(tb+w)=-\tfrac12t^2+w^TAw.
$$

If $d\geq2$, choose a nonzero $w\perp b$. Arbitrarily close to zero, the direction $b$ gives negative values and the direction $w$ gives positive values. Thus **$0$ is a [saddle point](../../../../../../saddle-point.md) for $d\geq2$**. Equivalently, the [Hessian matrix](../../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) has one negative [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) and all its other [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) positive.

The dimension matters: if $d=1$, the [orthogonal complement](../../../../../../orthogonal-complement.md) is zero-dimensional and $g(tb)=-t^2/2$. In that case **$0$ is the unique [global maximum](../../../../../../global-maximum.md)**. This edge case is not excluded by the stated hypotheses. The decomposition is a [rank-one downdate along an eigenvector](../../../../../../rank-one-downdate-along-an-eigenvector.md).
