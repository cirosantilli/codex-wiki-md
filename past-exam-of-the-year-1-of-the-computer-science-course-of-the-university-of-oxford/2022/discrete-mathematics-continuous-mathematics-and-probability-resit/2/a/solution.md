<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For real-valued [functions](../../../../../function-split.md), use magnitude comparisons. The constants below are independent of $n$.

The statement $f(n)=O(g(n))$ in [Big O notation](../../../../../big-o-notation.md) means that some $C>0$ and [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) $N$ satisfy $|f(n)|\leq C|g(n)|$ for all $n\geq N$. The statement $f(n)=\Omega(g(n))$ in [big omega notation](../../../../../big-omega-notation.md) means that some $c>0$ and $N$ satisfy $|f(n)|\geq c|g(n)|$ for all $n\geq N$.

Finally, $f(n)=\Theta(g(n))$ in [big theta notation](../../../../../big-theta-notation.md) means that some $c,C>0$ and $N$ satisfy

$$
\boxed{c|g(n)|\leq|f(n)|\leq C|g(n)|\qquad(n\geq N).}
$$

Equivalently it is both an eventual [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) and an eventual [lower bound](../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). These definitions ignore a finite initial segment; they do not assert pointwise inequalities with constant one.
