Let $A$ and $B$ be the finite [sets](../../../../../set-split.md) of objects satisfying the two properties. The [rule of sum](../../../../../addition-principle.md) says that mutually exclusive alternatives have additive counts: if $A\cap B=\varnothing$, then $|A\cup B|=|A|+|B|$. Without disjointness, the corresponding [inclusion-exclusion principle](../../../../../inclusion-exclusion-principle.md) is

$$
|A\cup B|=|A|+|B|-|A\cap B|.
$$

The [rule of subtraction](../../../../../rule-of-subtraction.md) counts objects satisfying the first property but not the second:

$$
\boxed{|A\setminus B|=|A|-|A\cap B|.}
$$

In particular, if the objects with the second property form a [subset](../../../../../subset.md) of those with the first, the count is $|A|-|B|$. These conditions prevent either double counting an overlap or subtracting objects that were not counted initially.
