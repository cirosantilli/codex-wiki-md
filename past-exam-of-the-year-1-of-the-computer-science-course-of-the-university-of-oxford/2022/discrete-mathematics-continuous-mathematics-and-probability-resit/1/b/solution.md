<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [rule of product](../../../../../rule-of-product.md) says that if a construction has $r$ stages, with exactly $a_j$ choices at stage $j$ after every permitted earlier history, then it has $\prod_{j=1}^r a_j$ possible outcomes. The later choices need not be identical [sets](../../../../../set-split.md) after different histories; their numbers must have the stated values.

For a [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md) of $n$ distinct objects, choose the first object in $n$ ways, the second in $n-1$ ways, and continue until only one remains. Thus the number is

$$
\boxed{n(n-1)\cdots1=n!.}
$$

Every ordered arrangement is produced exactly once by its successive choices, so this counts [permutations](../../../../../permutation.md) without omissions or repetitions. For $n=0$, the unique empty arrangement agrees with $0!=1$.
