<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Rearrange the identity into

$$
A(B-A-I)=I.
$$

Taking [determinants](../../../../../determinant.md) gives $\det A\,\det(B-A-I)=1$. Hence $\det A\ne0$, so **$A$ is an [invertible matrix](../../../../../invertible-matrix.md)**, with $A^{-1}=B-A-I$. This uses that the [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md) are finite and square: a right inverse then implies a two-sided inverse. Applying the identity to a vector in the [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) without first establishing commutation would not justify cancelling an $A$ on the wrong side. The argument is a case of [invertibility forced by a polynomial product identity](../../../../../invertibility-forced-by-a-polynomial-product-identity.md).
