<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let $Av=\lambda v$ for a real [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$ and a nonzero real [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) $v$. Part (b) gives $\lambda\ne0$ and $A^{-1}v=\lambda^{-1}v$. The expression for $B$ in part (c) therefore gives

$$
Bv=(A+I+A^{-1})v=(\lambda+1+\lambda^{-1})v.
$$

Consequently **$\lambda+1+\lambda^{-1}$ is a real [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $B$**, with the same [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) $v$. For a real [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) and real [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md), a nonzero real vector can be chosen in the real [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) of $A-\lambda I$; no complex-vector assumption is needed.
