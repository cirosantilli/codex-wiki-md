↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**The [all-ones matrix](../../../../../../all-ones-matrix.md) is diagonalizable for every $n\geq1$.** The vector $u$ together with the $n-1$ vectors from part (ii) is an [eigenbasis](../../../../../../eigenbasis.md). Independence follows because a multiple of $u$ has coordinate sum $n$ times that multiple, whereas every other [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) vector has coordinate sum zero. In this [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) the [diagonal matrix](../../../../../../diagonal-matrix.md) is $\operatorname{diag}(n,0,\ldots,0)$. At $n=1$ the one-vector [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) suffices. Alternatively, the [spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices](../../../../../../spectral-theorem-for-real-symmetric-matrices.md) applies. If dimension zero is allowed by the convention for $\mathbb N$, the empty $0\times0$ [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) has no [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md), an empty [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) and a vacuous [diagonalization](../../../../../../diagonalization-of-a-matrix.md).
