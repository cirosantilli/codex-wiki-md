<h1 id="6/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Assume $n\geq1$. With $u=(1,\ldots,1)^T$, the [all-ones matrix](../../../../../../all-ones-matrix.md) satisfies $Av=(\sum_jv_j)u$. Hence **$n$ is an [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md), and for $n\geq2$ the other [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is $0$**. At $n=1$ the [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) is $[1]$, so 1 is its only [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md); zero is not an [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) with a nonzero [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) in that case.
