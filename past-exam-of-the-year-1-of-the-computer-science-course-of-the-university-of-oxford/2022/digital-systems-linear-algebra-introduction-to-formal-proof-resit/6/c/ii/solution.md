<h1 id="6/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write $B=QDQ^{-1}$ with $D$ a [diagonal matrix](../../../../../../diagonal-matrix.md). Then $0=B^k=QD^kQ^{-1}$, so each diagonal entry $\lambda_i$ satisfies $\lambda_i^k=0$ and therefore $\lambda_i=0$. Hence $D=0$ and **$B=0$**. Conversely the zero [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) is diagonalizable and nilpotent. This proves the existing result that [a diagonalizable nilpotent matrix is zero](../../../../../../a-diagonalizable-nilpotent-matrix-is-zero.md); nilpotence alone does not imply a zero [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md), because nonzero nilpotent Jordan blocks exist.
