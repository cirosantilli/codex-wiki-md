<h1 id="5/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A flattened Boolean [array](../../../../../../array-data-structure.md) stores the whole board in $N^2$ entries, with cell $(x,y)$ at position `y*N + x`. For the pictured board, its bottom-to-top rows are **`111111 | 111100 | 111100 | 110000 | 000000 | 000000`**.

A column-height [array](../../../../../../array-data-structure.md) uses only $N$ entries: column $x$ contains exactly the cells below height $h(x)$. The pictured board is **$h=(4,4,3,3,1,1)$**, with 16 occupied cells. The alternatives are essentially different because the first stores every cell and the second encodes the boundary of an [order ideal](../../../../../../order-ideal.md).
