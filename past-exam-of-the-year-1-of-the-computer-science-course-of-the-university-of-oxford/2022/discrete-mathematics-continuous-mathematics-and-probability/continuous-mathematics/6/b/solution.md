<h1 id="continuous-mathematics/6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [stationary point](../../../../../../stationary-point.md) equations give $y=x$ and $x^3=x$, hence $(0,0),(1,1),(-1,-1)$. At $(0,0)$ the [Hessian matrix](../../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) has negative [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) $-16$, so **$(0,0)$ is a [saddle point](../../../../../../saddle-point.md)**. At each of $(1,1)$ and $(-1,-1)$, the [Hessian matrix](../../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) has positive leading minors $12$ and $32$, proving a strict [local minimum](../../../../../../local-minimum.md). More strongly,

$$
f(x,y)=(x^2-1)^2+2(y-x)^2+3\ge3.
$$

Thus **$(1,1)$ and $(-1,-1)$ are exactly the [global minima](../../../../../../global-minimum.md), with value $3$**. There are no [local maxima](../../../../../../local-maximum.md), since any differentiable interior [local maximum](../../../../../../local-maximum.md) would be a [stationary point](../../../../../../stationary-point.md), and all three have been classified.
