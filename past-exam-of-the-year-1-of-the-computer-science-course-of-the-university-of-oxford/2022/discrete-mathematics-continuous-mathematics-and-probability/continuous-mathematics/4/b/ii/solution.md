<h1 id="continuous-mathematics/4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Writing $g=e^{x^{\mathsf T}x}$, the [gradient](../../../../../../../gradient.md) and [Hessian matrix](../../../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) are

$$
\nabla g(x)=2gx,\qquad H(g)(x)=g(2I+4xx^{\mathsf T}).
$$

Consequently, for any nonzero $v$,

$$
v^{\mathsf T}H(g)(x)v=g\left(2\|v\|^2+4(x^{\mathsf T}v)^2\right)>0.
$$

In particular the [Hessian matrix](../../../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) is a [positive semidefinite matrix](../../../../../../../positive-semidefinite-matrix.md) everywhere, so the [Hessian criterion for convexity](../../../../../../../hessian-criterion-for-convexity.md) proves **$g$ is [convex](../../../../../../../convex-function.md)**. The strict inequality along every nonconstant line also proves [strict convexity](../../../../../../../strictly-convex-function.md).
