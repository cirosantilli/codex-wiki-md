<h1 id="discrete-mathematics/1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Each of the $n$ elements is either included or excluded, so the [rule of product](../../../../../../../rule-of-product.md) gives $|\mathcal P(\{1,\ldots,n\})|=2^n$. Alternatively, partition the [power set](../../../../../../../power-set.md) into [disjoint](../../../../../../../disjoint-sets.md) families according to [cardinality](../../../../../../../cardinality.md). The family of $k$-element [subsets](../../../../../../../subset.md) has size equal to the [binomial coefficient](../../../../../../../binomial-coefficient-split.md) $\binom nk$. The [rule of sum](../../../../../../../addition-principle.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{2^n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk.}
$$
