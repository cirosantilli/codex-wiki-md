<h1 id="discrete-mathematics/2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Set $m=\lceil N/k\rceil$, using the [ceiling function](../../../../../../../ceiling-function.md). If no box contains at least $m$ objects, all boxes contain at most $m-1$, so the total number of objects is at most $k(m-1)$. Taking the [contrapositive](../../../../../../../contrapositive.md), a total greater than $k(m-1)$ forces some box to contain at least $m$ objects. But $m-1<N/k$, hence $k(m-1)<N$. This proves the [generalized pigeonhole principle](../../../../../../../generalized-pigeonhole-principle.md): **some box contains at least $\lceil N/k\rceil$ objects**.
