<h1 id="discrete-mathematics/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a [counterexample](../../../../../../counterexample.md) to closure under [union of antisymmetric relations](../../../../../../union-of-antisymmetric-relations.md), take $A=\{0,1\}$, $R=\{(0,1)\}$, and $S=\{(1,0)\}$. Each is an [antisymmetric relation](../../../../../../antisymmetric-relation.md): neither contains an off-diagonal pair together with its reverse. But $R\cup S$ contains both $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$ while $0\ne1$. Therefore **$R\cup S$ is not an [antisymmetric relation](../../../../../../antisymmetric-relation.md)**.
