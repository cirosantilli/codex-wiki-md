<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the first [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) choose its single independent column:

$$
\boxed{B_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Their product has every column equal to $B_1$, and both factors have rank one.

For the second [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md), the first and third columns are independent and the second equals the first, giving

$$
\boxed{B_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Multiplication reconstructs the stated [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md). The first factor has independent columns and the second has independent rows, so both have [matrix rank](../../../../../matrix-rank.md) two, as required for a [rank factorization](../../../../../rank-factorization.md).
