**Not unique in general.** For any invertible $r\times r$ [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) $S$,

$$
A=(BS)(S^{-1}C)
$$

is another [rank factorization](../../../../../../rank-factorization.md), with the same full ranks. This is a [change of basis](../../../../../../change-of-basis.md) in the intermediate $r$-dimensional space. For example, even $[1]=[1][1]=[2][1/2]$ has different full-rank factors. Once a particular ordered basis $B$ is fixed, its coordinate [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) $C$ is unique; it is the basis choice that creates the freedom.
