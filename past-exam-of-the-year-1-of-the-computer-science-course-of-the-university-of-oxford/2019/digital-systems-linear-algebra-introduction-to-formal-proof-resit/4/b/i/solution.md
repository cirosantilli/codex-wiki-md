<h1 id="4/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $c_1=(1,1,1)^T$ and $c_2=(2,-2,6)^T$. The remaining columns are $3c_1$ and $2c_2$, so these two [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) span the [column space](../../../../../../column-space.md). They are [linearly independent](../../../../../../linear-independence.md): the [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) of their first two coordinates is $-4\ne0$. Therefore **$\{(1,1,1)^T,(2,-2,6)^T\}$ is a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) of the range**, and the [matrix rank](../../../../../../matrix-rank.md) is two.

Equivalently the range is the plane $y_3=2y_1-y_2$: both displayed basis [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) satisfy this equation, and their two-dimensional span fills that plane.
