<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The independent-column representation gives $T(x)=c_1(x_1+3x_3)+c_2(x_2+2x_4)$. Thus a [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) lies in the [kernel of a linear map](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) represented by this [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) precisely when $x_1=-3x_3$ and $x_2=-2x_4$. Writing the two free coordinates separately gives

$$
x=x_3\begin{pmatrix}-3\\0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}
+x_4\begin{pmatrix}0\\-2\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The two displayed [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) are [linearly independent](../../../../../../linear-independence.md), so **they form a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) of the kernel**. Its dimension two also agrees with the [rank-nullity theorem](../../../../../../rank-nullity-theorem.md), $4=2+2$.
