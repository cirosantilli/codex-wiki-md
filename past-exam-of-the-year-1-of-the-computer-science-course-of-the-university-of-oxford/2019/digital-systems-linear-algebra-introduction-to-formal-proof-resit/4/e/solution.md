↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

**True for these square [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md).** The [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) multiplication identity gives $\det(XY)=\det X\det Y$. Since $XY$ is invertible, its [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) is nonzero; both factors are therefore nonzero, so both [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md) have [matrix inverses](../../../../../matrix-inverse-split.md).

Equivalently, $Y$ must be injective and $X$ must be surjective, and either property implies invertibility for an endomorphism of a finite-dimensional [vector](../../../../../vector.md) space. The equal square dimensions matter: a rectangular [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) and a right inverse can have identity product without being mutually invertible square [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md).
