- The [implication introduction rule](../../../../../implication-introduction-rule.md) assumes $\phi$ locally, derives $\psi$ under that assumption, then concludes $\phi\to\psi$ while discharging the temporary assumption.
- The [disjunction elimination rule](../../../../../disjunction-elimination-rule.md) uses $\phi\lor\psi$ and two local cases: one derives a common conclusion $\kappa$ from $\phi$, and the other derives that same $\kappa$ from $\psi$. It concludes $\kappa$ and discharges both case assumptions.
- The [negation introduction rule](../../../../../negation-introduction-rule.md) assumes $\phi$ locally and derives [logical falsity](../../../../../logical-falsity.md). Discharging that assumption gives $\neg\phi$.

The boxes delimit a [subproof](../../../../../subproof.md) and identify the lifetime of its temporary assumptions. A result depending on a boxed assumption is available inside that box and its nested boxes; it cannot simply be exported outside. The enclosing rule specifies what may be exported and which assumptions are discharged. Other premises on which the derivation depends remain undischarged. This [assumption discharge](../../../../../assumption-discharge.md) discipline distinguishes a conditional argument from an unconditional [proof](../../../../../mathematical-proof.md).
