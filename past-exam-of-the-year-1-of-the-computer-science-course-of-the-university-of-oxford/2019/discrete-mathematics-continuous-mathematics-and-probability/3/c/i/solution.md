<h1 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $J=\{k\in\mathbb N:k\le100\}$, avoiding any dependence on whether the convention includes $0$ among the [natural numbers](../../../../../../natural-number.md). Under [set inclusion](../../../../../../set-inclusion.md), the results are

| Family | Maximum | [Least upper bound](../../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) in $A$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $S_1$ | None | None |
| $S_2$ | $J$ | $J$ |
| $S_1\cap S_2$ | None | $J$ |

The [set union](../../../../../../set-union.md) of all three-element [subsets](../../../../../../subset.md) of $\mathbb N$ is $\mathbb N$. An [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) for $S_1$ under [set inclusion](../../../../../../set-inclusion.md) would have to contain this entire infinite [set](../../../../../../set-split.md), whereas members of $A$ are finite. Thus $S_1$ has no [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) and hence no maximum or [least upper bound](../../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md).

For $S_2$, every member is a [subset](../../../../../../subset.md) of the finite [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) $J$, and $J$ itself belongs to $S_2$, making it both maximum and [least upper bound](../../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). The union of the three-element [subsets](../../../../../../subset.md) of $J$ is again $J$, so $J$ is the [least upper bound](../../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $S_1\cap S_2$. It is not a member of that family, and no three-element member contains all the others, so there is no maximum.
