An [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $S$ is an element $u\in A$ with $s\preceq u$ for every $s\in S$. It need not belong to $S$. A maximum, or [greatest element](../../../../../greatest-element.md), is an $m\in S$ with that same property. A [least upper bound](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) is an [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $\ell\in A$ satisfying $\ell\preceq u$ for every [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $u$.

**A maximum belongs to $S$; a [least upper bound](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) need not.** In a [partially ordered set](../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md), an existing maximum is automatically the [least upper bound](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md), and the [antisymmetric relation](../../../../../antisymmetric-relation.md) condition makes each unique.
