↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**False.** It suffices to find a subsequence along which the ratio is unbounded. Set $n=4m$. The last $3m$ factors of $(4m)!$ are at least $m$, while the first $m$ factors have product at least one. Hence

$$
\frac{(4m)!}{(4m)^{2m}}
\ge\frac{m^{3m}}{16^m m^{2m}}
=\left(\frac m{16}\right)^m\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

This contradicts the eventual uniform bound required by [Big O notation](../../../../../../big-o-notation.md). The proof is an elementary [factorial](../../../../../../factorial.md) comparison, [independent](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of [Stirling's approximation](../../../../../../stirling-formula.md).
