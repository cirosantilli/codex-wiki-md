<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

**There are six [functions](../../../../../function-split.md): the four power maps $x\mapsto x^k$ for $k=1,2,3,4$, and the two constant maps $0,1$.** All calculations in this part are in the [finite field](../../../../../finite-field.md) $\mathbb Z_5$.

Put $e=f(1)$. Multiplicativity gives $e^2=e$, so the absence of [zero divisors](../../../../../zero-divisor.md) forces $e=0$ or $e=1$. If $e=0$, then $f(x)=f(x)f(1)=0$ for every $x$, giving only the constant-zero map.

Suppose $e=1$. For nonzero $x$, the equality $f(x)f(x^{-1})=1$ shows $f(x)\ne0$. The nonzero elements form a [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) generated by $2$, in the order $1,2,4,3$. Choosing $a=f(2)$ determines every value on this group by $f(2^j)=a^j$. Each $a\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ satisfies $a^4=1$, so all four choices give consistent [group homomorphisms](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md).

Finally, $z=f(0)$ satisfies $z^2=z$ and $z=zf(x)$ for every $x$. The choice $z=0$ works for all four group maps. The choice $z=1$ forces every nonzero value to be $1$, giving one extra map, the constant-one map. Thus the count is $1+4+1=6$. The four zero-preserving possibilities are exactly the four displayed power maps. This is the special case with five elements of [multiplicative self-maps of a finite field](../../../../../multiplicative-self-maps-of-a-finite-field.md).
