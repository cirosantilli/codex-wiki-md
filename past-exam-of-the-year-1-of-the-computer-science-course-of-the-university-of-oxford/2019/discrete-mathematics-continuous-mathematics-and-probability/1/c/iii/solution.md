<h1 id="1/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**It is [surjective](../../../../../../surjective-function.md) but not [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md); there is no global [inverse function](../../../../../../inverse-function.md).** Noninjectivity follows from

$$
f(3/2)=\frac3{\sqrt2}=f(3).
$$

For surjectivity, restrict to $x<1$, where

$$
f(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x}},\qquad
f'(x)=\frac{1-x/2}{(1-x)^{3/2}}>0.
$$

This restriction is a [continuous function](../../../../../../continuous-function.md), tends to $-\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$ and to $+\infty$ as $x\to1^-$. The [intermediate value theorem](../../../../../../intermediate-value-theorem.md) therefore supplies a preimage for every real number. The additional branch $x>1$ destroys global injectivity but cannot destroy surjectivity.
