<h1 id="7/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $I_B$ be the [indicator](../../../../../../indicator-function.md) that black bead $B$ belongs to the white bead's [connected component](../../../../../../connected-component.md). The number of such black beads is $\sum_B I_B$. By [linearity of expectation](../../../../../../linearity-of-expectation.md), no [independence](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) between these [indicators](../../../../../../indicator-function.md) is needed.

For $n=7$, the six oriented distances $1,\ldots,6$ give respective [probabilities](../../../../../../probability.md)

$$
\frac47,\ \frac27,\ \frac17,\ \frac17,\ \frac27,\ \frac47.
$$

Their sum is **$2$**, the expected number of black beads in the white component. As a check, summing the formula from (i) and using $\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom k3=\binom n4$ gives the general value $2\binom n4/\binom n3=(n-3)/2$, again equal to $2$ when $n=7$.
