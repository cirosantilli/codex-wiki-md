<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Four differentiations give

$$
f^{(4)}(x)=e^x-ex,\qquad f^{(5)}(x)=e^x-e.
$$

For $0<x<1$, the fifth [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) is negative, so the fourth [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) is strictly decreasing. Since $f^{(4)}(1)=0$, strict decrease towards that endpoint implies $f^{(4)}(x)>0$ for $x<1$. On the closed [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md), **$0\le f^{(4)}(x)\le1$**, with the maximum at $0$ and minimum at $1$.
