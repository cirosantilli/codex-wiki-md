<h1 id="9/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The disjoint decomposition into [sets](../../../../../../set-split.md) $A=(A\cap B)\cup(A\setminus B)$ uses only [events](../../../../../../event.md) in $\mathcal F$. [Finite additivity](../../../../../../finite-additivity-of-a-set-function.md) and nonnegativity give

$$
\mathbb P(A)=\mathbb P(A\cap B)+\mathbb P(A\setminus B)\geq\mathbb P(A\cap B).
$$

Thus **$\mathbb P(A\cap B)\leq\mathbb P(A)$** already follows from the two stated requirements. Equality is attained, for example, by choosing $B=A$, since then $A\cap B=A$. More generally it holds whenever $\mathbb P(A\setminus B)=0$. Neither normalization nor [countable additivity](../../../../../../countable-additivity.md) is needed for this particular inequality.
