<h1 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For each $y\in Y$, [surjectivity](../../../../../../surjective-function.md) makes the [fiber of a function](../../../../../../fiber-of-a-function.md) $f^{-1}(\{y\})$ nonempty. Choose one element $x_y$ of each such [fiber of a function](../../../../../../fiber-of-a-function.md) and define $g(y)=x_y$. Then $f(g(y))=y$ for every $y$, proving **$f\circ g=I_Y$**. For arbitrary [sets](../../../../../../set-split.md), the simultaneous selection invokes the usual [axiom of choice](../../../../../../axiom-of-choice.md); this is the [right-inverse characterization of the axiom of choice](../../../../../../right-inverse-characterization-of-the-axiom-of-choice.md). For finite $Y$ only finitely many choices are needed. If $Y$ is empty, the unique empty [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) gives the same conclusion.
