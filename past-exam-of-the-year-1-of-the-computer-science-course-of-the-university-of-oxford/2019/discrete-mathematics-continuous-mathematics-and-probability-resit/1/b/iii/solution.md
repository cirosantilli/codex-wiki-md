<h1 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**Not [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md); its [image of a function](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) is exactly $\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$.** For example, both $x=e^{-1}$ and $x=e^{-1/2}$ give the [ceiling function](../../../../../../ceiling-function.md) value $1$. Since $0<x<1$, $(\ln x)^2>0$, so the value $0$ is never attained. For every positive [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) $j$,

$$
f(x)=j\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e^{-\sqrt j}\leq x<e^{-\sqrt{j-1}}.
$$

Each such [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) is nonempty and contained in $(0,1)$, with the upper endpoint $1$ excluded when $j=1$. Thus the answer concerning [surjectivity](../../../../../../surjective-function.md) depends on the convention for the [natural numbers](../../../../../../natural-number.md): **onto if $\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and not onto if $0\in\mathbb N$**. The source does not settle that convention.
