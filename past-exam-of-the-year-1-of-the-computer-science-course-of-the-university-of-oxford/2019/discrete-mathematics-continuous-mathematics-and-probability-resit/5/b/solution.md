With the gap-at-one convention, [machine epsilon](../../../../../machine-epsilon.md) is the difference between $1$ and the next larger representable [floating-point number](../../../../../floating-point-number.md). The [double-precision floating-point format](../../../../../double-precision-floating-point-format.md) has 53 significant binary digits, including the implicit leading digit, so

$$
\boxed{\varepsilon_{\rm mach}=2^{-52}\approx2.22\times10^{-16}.}
$$

A related convention uses [unit roundoff](../../../../../unit-roundoff.md), the worst relative rounding scale for round-to-nearest: $u=2^{-53}\approx1.11\times10^{-16}$. Stating the convention distinguishes these two common values.[Chapter 2, IEEE Arithmetic Model; Double Format and Table 2-5](../../../../../ieee-arithmetic-in-oracle-s-numerical-computation-guide.md)
