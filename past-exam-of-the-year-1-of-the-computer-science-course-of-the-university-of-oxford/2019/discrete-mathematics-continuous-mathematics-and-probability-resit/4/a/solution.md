One standard finite form of [Taylor's theorem](../../../../../taylor-s-theorem.md) is the following. Suppose $f$ has continuous [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) through order $m+1$ on an [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) containing $a$ and $x$. Then, if $x\ne a$, there is some $\xi$ strictly between them such that

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^m\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k+\frac{f^{(m+1)}(\xi)}{(m+1)!}(x-a)^{m+1}.}
$$

The final term is the [Lagrange remainder](../../../../../lagrange-remainder.md). At $x=a$ the identity holds directly with zero [remainder](../../../../../remainder.md). The finite expansion requires no assumption that an infinite [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) converges to $f$.
