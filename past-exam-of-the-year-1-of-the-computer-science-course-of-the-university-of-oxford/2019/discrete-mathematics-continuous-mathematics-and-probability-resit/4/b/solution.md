<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $0<x<\pi$, apply [Taylor's theorem](../../../../../taylor-s-theorem.md) to the [sine](../../../../../sine.md) about zero, retaining terms through degree four. Its fourth-order coefficient is zero, and its fifth [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) is the [cosine function](../../../../../cosine.md), giving

$$
\sin x=x-\frac{x^3}{6}+\frac{x^5}{120}\cos\xi,\qquad 0<\xi<x.
$$

The [cosine function](../../../../../cosine.md) is decreasing on $[0,\pi]$, so $\cos x\leq\cos\xi\leq1$. Multiplying by the nonnegative factor $x^5/120$ and adding $x-x^3/6$ proves **both required bounds**. At $x=0$ both are equalities.
