<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let $q=x^2+4y^2$. The objective has the exact [sum of squares](../../../../../sum-of-squares.md) decomposition

$$
x^2+6xy+4y^2+3=3-\frac12q+\frac32(x+2y)^2.
$$

For every feasible point, $q\leq8$, so this is at least $3-8/2=-1$. Equality requires simultaneously $q=8$ and $x+2y=0$. Substitution gives $y^2=1$ and $x=-2y$. Both resulting points are feasible. Therefore **the global [minimum](../../../../../minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) is $-1$, attained exactly at $(2,-1)$ and $(-2,1)$**. The lower bound and its equality conditions prove global attainment of the [minimum](../../../../../minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md), rather than merely identifying [stationary points](../../../../../stationary-point.md) on the boundary.
