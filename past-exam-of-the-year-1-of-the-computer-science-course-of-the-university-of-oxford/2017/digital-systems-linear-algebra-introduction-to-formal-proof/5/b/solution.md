<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use multiplicativity of the [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) and the determinant criterion for an [invertible matrix](../../../../../invertible-matrix.md):

$$
0\ne\det A=\det L\det U.
$$

Neither factor can therefore be zero, so both matrices are nonsingular. The determinant of a [triangular matrix](../../../../../triangular-matrix.md) is the product of its diagonal entries:

$$
\det L=\prod_{i=1}^N L_{ii},\qquad\det U=\prod_{i=1}^N U_{ii}.
$$

Each product is nonzero, forcing every diagonal factor to be nonzero. These explicitly stated determinant properties justify both conclusions.
