One example is **the identity function**, $f(n)=n$. A second is the [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md)

$$
\boxed{f(1)=2,\quad f(2)=3,\quad f(3)=1,\quad f(n)=n\ \text{otherwise}.}
$$

Both are [bijections](../../../../../bijection.md) of $\mathbb N$, whichever convention includes or excludes zero. For the second, applying $f$ three times returns every input to itself. Thus $f^3=\operatorname{id}$, and composing with the [inverse function](../../../../../inverse-function.md) gives $f^2=f^{-1}$. This [permutation cube identity criterion](../../../../../permutation-cube-identity-criterion.md) also shows why three-cycles work whereas a nontrivial two-cycle would not.
