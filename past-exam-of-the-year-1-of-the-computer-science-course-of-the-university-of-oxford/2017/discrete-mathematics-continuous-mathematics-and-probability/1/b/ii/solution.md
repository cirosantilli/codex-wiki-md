<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $x\in A\oplus B$, the definition of [symmetric difference](../../../../../../symmetric-difference.md) puts it in exactly one of $A,B$. It is therefore in their [set union](../../../../../../set-union.md) and outside their [set intersection](../../../../../../set-intersection.md), giving $x\in(A\cup B)\setminus(A\cap B)$.

Conversely, an element of $(A\cup B)\setminus(A\cap B)$ lies in at least one of the two sets but not both. It consequently belongs to $A\setminus B$ or to $B\setminus A$, and hence to $A\oplus B$. Both [set inclusions](../../../../../../set-inclusion.md) hold.
