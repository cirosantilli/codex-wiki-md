<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The base cases are $13=3(2)+7(1)$, $14=3(0)+7(2)$ and $15=3(5)+7(0)$. Suppose a counterexample exists and choose the least integer $n\geq13$ failing the claim. It is not one of these base cases, so $n\geq16$ and $n-3\geq13$.

Minimality supplies nonnegative integers $x,y$ with $n-3=3x+7y$. Then $n=3(x+1)+7y$, a contradiction. This [proof by minimal counterexample](../../../../../proof-by-minimal-counterexample.md) establishes the claim. Three consecutive base cases are needed because the reduction subtracts three.
