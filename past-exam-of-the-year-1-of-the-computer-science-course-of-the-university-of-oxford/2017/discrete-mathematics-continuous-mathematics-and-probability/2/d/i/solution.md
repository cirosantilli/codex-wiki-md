<h1 id="2/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For one colour, the box occupancies form a [weak composition](../../../../../../weak-composition-of-an-integer.md) of $k$ into $n$ nonnegative parts. For $n\geq1$, [stars and bars](../../../../../../stars-and-bars-combinatorics.md) gives $\binom{k+n-1}{n-1}$ choices. The three colours' occupancies can be selected independently, so the number is

$$
\boxed{\binom{k+n-1}{n-1}^{\!3}.}
$$

Boxes are labelled, while equal-colour balls contribute only occupancy counts; no extra factorial for permuting such balls is appropriate.
