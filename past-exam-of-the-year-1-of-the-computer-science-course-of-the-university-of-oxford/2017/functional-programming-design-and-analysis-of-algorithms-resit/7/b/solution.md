Relative to the resulting [DFS edge classification](../../../../../dfs-edge-classification.md), a **[tree edge in depth-first search](../../../../../tree-edge-in-depth-first-search.md)** discovers a white vertex and becomes its parent edge. A **[forward edge in depth-first search](../../../../../forward-edge-in-depth-first-search.md)** goes to a proper descendant but is not that descendant's discovery edge. A **[back edge in depth-first search](../../../../../back-edge-in-depth-first-search.md)** goes to an active ancestor, with a self-loop also counted as a back edge when such edges are allowed. A **[cross edge in depth-first search](../../../../../cross-edge-in-depth-first-search.md)** joins vertices with no ancestor-descendant relationship in the forest, such as different completed branches or different trees.

The definitions refer to directed edges; an undirected graph is normally classified only with tree and back edges, accounting for the reverse copy of a parent edge separately.
