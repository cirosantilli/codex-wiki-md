↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Dynamic programming](../../../../../dynamic-programming.md) defines subproblem optima and combines their stored answers through a recurrence. It is useful when [optimal substructure](../../../../../optimal-substructure.md) and [overlapping subproblems](../../../../../overlapping-subproblems.md) allow repeated work to be shared. Several alternative choices can be compared before an optimum is selected.

A [greedy algorithm](../../../../../greedy-algorithm.md) instead commits to a locally selected choice and does not later reconsider it. To be correct, its choice needs a justification such as an [exchange argument](../../../../../exchange-argument.md) or a safe-cut property, beyond merely having optimal substructure. Neither method is automatically applicable to every optimization problem. [Prim's algorithm](../../../../../prim-s-algorithm.md) illustrates a justified greedy choice; the maximum-segment recurrence below illustrates dynamic programming.
