# No-log boundary condition at a linearly vanishing Sturm-Liouville endpoint

↑ **Parent:** [Sturm-Liouville problem](sturm-liouville-problem.md)

Suppose $p(x)=c(x-a)+O((x-a)^2)$ with $c>0$, and the other coefficients in $(py')'+(\lambda w-q)y=0$ extend continuously to $a$, with $w(a)>0$. A bounded [eigenfunction](eigenfunction.md) has $(py')'=O(1)$, hence $py'=C+O(x-a)$. A nonzero $C$ would give $y'\sim C/[c(x-a)]$ and an unbounded [logarithmic singularity](logarithmic-singularity.md). Thus $C=0$, and the regular branch has bounded first [derivative](derivative.md). The second local branch carries a logarithm. The title condition excludes that branch.

For two regular [eigenfunctions](eigenfunction.md), $p(\overline u v'-\overline{u'}v)\to0$. This removes the singular-end contribution to the [Lagrange identity for a Sturm-Liouville operator](lagrange-identity-for-a-sturm-liouville-operator.md). Together with a separated self-adjoint condition at the other endpoint, it selects the regular [self-adjoint differential operator](self-adjoint-differential-operator.md) realization. The precise operator domain uses the maximal differential-operator domain and the no-log endpoint condition; boundedness of classical eigenfunctions is its convenient local expression, not a replacement for specifying an operator domain.
