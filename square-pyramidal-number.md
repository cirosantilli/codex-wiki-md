# Square pyramidal number

↑ **Parent:** [Figurate number](figurate-number.md)

The [sum of squares](sum-of-squares.md) of the first $n$ [positive integers](positive-integer.md): $S_n=\sum_{k=1}^n k^2=n(n+1)(2n+1)/6$. It counts a square-based stack with layers containing $1^2,2^2,\ldots,n^2$ objects. Differences $S_b-S_{a-1}$ give sums over consecutive squares.

The number of objects in a square-based pyramid with successive square layers of sizes $1^2,2^2,\ldots,n^2$. If $F(n)=n(n+1)(2n+1)/6$, direct algebra gives $F(n)-F(n-1)=n^2$ and $F(0)=0$. [Mathematical induction](mathematical-induction.md) therefore proves the displayed [summation](summation.md) formula. Its leading term is $n^3/3$, so this [sum](sum.md) grows cubically, even though its largest individual summand grows quadratically.
