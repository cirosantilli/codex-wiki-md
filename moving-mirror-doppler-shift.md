# Moving-mirror Doppler shift

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic Doppler effect](relativistic-doppler-effect.md)

A mirror receding at [velocity](velocity.md) $u=\beta c$ from normally incident [light](light.md) produces two applications of the [relativistic Doppler effect](relativistic-doppler-effect.md), one into the mirror rest frame and one back to the observer. In that rest frame, reflection preserves [frequency](frequency.md). Consequently $\omega_r=\gamma^2(1-\beta)^2\omega_i=\omega_i(1-\beta)/(1+\beta)$. Equivalently, continuity of [wave phase](phase-waves.md) on the moving surface $z=ut$ gives $\omega_i-k_i u=\omega_r+k_r u$, with $k_i=\omega_i/c$ and $k_r=\omega_r/c$. For $|u|\ll c$, the fractional [frequency](frequency.md) change is $-2u/c$ to first order.
