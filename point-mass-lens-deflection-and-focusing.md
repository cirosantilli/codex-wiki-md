# Point-mass lens deflection and focusing

↑ **Parent:** [Point-mass gravitational lens](point-mass-gravitational-lens.md)

Deflection changes one [light ray](light-ray.md)'s direction; focusing concerns relative directions of neighboring [light rays](light-ray.md) and their bundle's cross section. To see the difference for a [point-mass gravitational lens](point-mass-gravitational-lens.md), send parallel incident [light rays](light-ray.md) past the lens. A ray at [impact parameter](impact-parameter.md) $b$ meets the [optical axis](optical-axis.md) approximately $f(b)=b/\widehat\alpha=c^2b^2/(4GM)$ beyond the lens. Doubling $b$ quadruples this crossing distance. Thus the lens does not have a common [focal length](focal-length.md) for all rays, although it can produce [lensing magnification](lensing-magnification.md) and an [Einstein ring](einstein-ring.md) for a specified source-observer geometry.

The [point-mass gravitational lens](point-mass-gravitational-lens.md) also illustrates why focusing must not be identified just with positive [lensing convergence](lensing-convergence.md). Away from the central [mass](mass.md), differentiate the reduced deflection $\boldsymbol\alpha=\theta_E^2\boldsymbol\theta/r^2$: its two-dimensional [divergence](divergence.md) is zero, so $\kappa=0$ there. Nevertheless its radial and tangential [derivatives](derivative.md) differ, giving [lensing shear](lensing-shear.md) of magnitude $\theta_E^2/r^2$ and $\det A=1-\theta_E^4/r^4$. A vacuum ray bundle can be distorted and magnified. In the same ideal transparent [geometrical optics](geometrical-optics-split.md) model, [surface brightness](surface-brightness.md) is preserved; the larger apparent [solid angle](solid-angle.md) supplies the increased received light.
