# Mixed inhibition

↑ **Parent:** [Reversible enzyme inhibition](reversible-enzyme-inhibition.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_inhibition)

In the branching model, [mixed inhibition](mixed-inhibition.md) retains inhibitor binding to both free [enzyme](enzyme-split.md) and the [enzyme-substrate complex](enzyme-substrate-complex.md). Unequal $K_i$ and $K_i'$ shift both apparent parameters; their equality gives [pure noncompetitive inhibition](pure-noncompetitive-inhibition.md). For the chosen unequal case, $I=30\,\mathrm{\mu M}$ gives $\alpha=4$ and $\alpha'=2$, hence $V_{\max,\rm app}=50\,\mathrm{\mu M\,min^{-1}}$ and $K_{M,\rm app}=40\,\mathrm{\mu M}$. Because $K_i<K_i'$, binding favours free [enzyme](enzyme-split.md), and the apparent [Michaelis constant](michaelis-constant.md) increases. Reversing that inequality decreases it.

The limiting behaviour provides a useful diagnostic within this model:

$$
\lim_{s\to0}\frac{v}{s}=\frac{V_{\max}}{\alpha K_M},\qquad
\lim_{s\to\infty}v=\frac{V_{\max}}{\alpha'}.
$$

Low [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) [concentration](concentration.md) isolates $\alpha$ through the initial [slope](slope.md); high [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) [concentration](concentration.md) isolates $\alpha'$ through the plateau. At $I=30\,\mathrm{\mu M}$, the chosen [competitive inhibition](competitive-inhibition.md) and unequal [mixed inhibition](mixed-inhibition.md) examples have the same low-$s$ [slope](slope.md), $1.25\,\mathrm{min^{-1}}$, but different plateaus, $100$ and $50\,\mathrm{\mu M\,min^{-1}}$. Measuring only low [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) [concentrations](concentration.md) would miss that distinction.
