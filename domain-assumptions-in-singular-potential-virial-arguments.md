# Domain assumptions in singular-potential virial arguments

↑ **Parent:** [Fall to the centre for a supercritical inverse-power potential](fall-to-the-centre-for-a-supercritical-inverse-power-potential.md)

For the formal one-dimensional operator $H=p^2/(2m)-a|x|^{-p}$ with $a>0$ and $p>2$, take a smooth normalized test function supported away from the origin. Its normalized [dilation](uniform-dilation.md) $\psi_\lambda(x)=\sqrt\lambda\psi(\lambda x)$ has

$$
\mathcal E(\lambda)=\lambda^2T+\lambda^pU,\qquad T>0,\quad U<0.
$$

As $\lambda\to\infty$, this tends to minus infinity. Thus the unregularized expression has [fall to the centre for a supercritical inverse-power potential](fall-to-the-centre-for-a-supercritical-inverse-power-potential.md) and cannot define a semibounded [Hamiltonian operator](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) on a domain containing these test functions.

If a putative [bound state](bound-state.md) has separately finite $T,U$ and admits norm-preserving dilation variations inside its [operator domain](operator-domain.md) or energy domain, stationarity gives $2T+pU=0$, hence $E=(1-2/p)T>0$, incompatible with a negative-energy bound state below the zero continuum threshold. For a putative ground state, the second scaling derivative is $2(2-p)T<0$, also incompatible with a minimum. The argument's finite-energy and domain assumptions are essential: it proves absence of a stable ground state and excludes bound states to which this ordinary virial calculation applies. An unqualified assertion about all formal square-integrable solutions at the singular origin needs a specified self-adjoint realization; adding a short-distance cutoff changes the problem.
