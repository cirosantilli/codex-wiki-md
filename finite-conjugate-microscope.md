# Finite-conjugate microscope

↑ **Parent:** [Optical microscope](optical-microscope.md)

An [optical microscope](optical-microscope.md) whose [microscope objective](microscope-objective.md) is designed to form a [real image](real-image.md) at a specified finite distance. The intended image distance and [optical aberration](optical-aberration.md) corrections belong together; mechanical tube length is a mounting specification, not automatically the image distance measured from a [principal plane](principal-plane.md).[Infinity Optical Systems, opening finite-system discussion and Fig. 2(a)](infinity-optical-systems-by-nikon-instruments-inc.md)

Why can a small displacement produce a large image? For a converging [thin lens](thin-lens.md), write $u_o=f_o+\epsilon$ with $epsilon>0$. Substitution into the [thin lens](thin-lens.md) equation gives

$$
v_o=\frac{f_o(f_o+\epsilon)}{\epsilon},\qquad m_o=-\frac{f_o}{\epsilon}.
$$

The [similar triangles](similar-triangle.md) construction supplies the minus sign. The example has $epsilon=2\,\mathrm{mm}$ and hence $m_o=-5$. Letting $epsilon$ decrease moves the [real image](real-image.md) farther away; it does not keep the [microscope eyepiece](microscope-eyepiece.md) in focus at a fixed position. Large [transverse magnification](transverse-magnification.md) therefore requires the correct conjugate geometry, not merely a short [focal length](focal-length.md).
