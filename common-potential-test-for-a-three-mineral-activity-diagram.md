# Common-potential test for a three-mineral activity diagram

↑ **Parent:** [Stability boundary in a mineral activity diagram](stability-boundary-in-a-mineral-activity-diagram.md)

Suppose two dissolution reactions to a reference [mineral](mineral.md) $C$ have been normalized to the same amount of a conserved reference component. After division by the same positive energy factor, let their [Gibbs energies of reaction](gibbs-energy-of-reaction.md) be $y-\ell_1(x)$ and $y-\ell_2(x)$. Their relative thermodynamic potentials can then be written as displayed. The stable [mineral](mineral.md) has the smallest potential.

It follows that $C$ is stable below $y=\min(\ell_1,\ell_2)$, mineral 1 is stable where $\ell_1<\ell_2$ and $y>\ell_1$, and mineral 2 is stable where $\ell_2<\ell_1$ and $y>\ell_2$. The two nonreference minerals coexist stably where $\ell_1=\ell_2<y$. Their equality below that height is a [metastable](metastability.md) continuation. This comparison proves the stable lower envelope and selects which portions of the pairwise mass-action lines belong on the [mineral activity diagram](mineral-activity-diagram.md).
