# Derivations of smooth functions are vector fields

↑ **Parent:** [Derivation of an algebra](derivation-of-an-algebra.md)

Every real-linear [derivation of an algebra](derivation-of-an-algebra.md) $D:C^\infty(M)\to C^\infty(M)$ on a [smooth manifold](smooth-manifold.md) has the form $Dh=Z(h)$ for a unique smooth [vector field](vector-field.md) $Z$. The product rule makes $D$ local: multiplying a function that vanishes near a point by a [smooth cutoff function](smooth-cutoff-function.md) supported there gives zero derivative at that point. Locally write $h(x)=h(p)+\sum_i(x^i-x^i(p))h_i(x)$, where $h_i(p)=\partial_i h(p)$. Applying $D$ at $p$ gives $Dh(p)=\sum_i D(x^i)(p)\partial_i h(p)$; the smooth coefficients $D(x^i)$ define the claimed [vector field](vector-field.md). No continuity hypothesis is needed. In dimension zero all such derivations vanish.
