# Signal peptide

↑ **Parent:** [Protein targeting](protein-targeting.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_peptide)

A [signal peptide](signal-peptide.md) is a short targeting segment of a newly made [polypeptide chain](polypeptide.md). A familiar [ER signal sequence](er-signal-sequence.md) lies near the [N-terminus](n-terminus.md) and is removed during entry; [Günter Blobel](gunter-blobel.md) and [Bernhard Dobberstein](bernhard-dobberstein.md)'s 1975 experiments linked a larger [immunoglobulin light chain](immunoglobulin-light-chain.md) precursor to a processed product.[Transfer of proteins across membranes. I, Introduction p. 836; Figures 5 and 14; Discussion pp. 845–847](transfer-of-proteins-across-membranes-i-by-gunter-blobel-and-bernhard-dobberstein.md)

Cleavage is a separate question from recognition. Not every targeting signal is a cleaved [signal peptide](signal-peptide.md): an internal [signal anchor](signal-anchor.md) can remain part of a [transmembrane protein](transmembrane-protein.md). Conversely, loss of a segment does not by itself prove membrane crossing. Compare the independent size and accessibility readouts in the [microsomal protein import experiment](microsomal-protein-import-experiment.md).
