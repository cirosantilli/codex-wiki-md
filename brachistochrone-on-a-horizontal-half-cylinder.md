# Brachistochrone on a horizontal half-cylinder

↑ **Parent:** [Brachistochrone problem](brachistochrone-problem.md)

Measure $\theta\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ from the vertically downward direction on the lower half of a cylinder of radius $R$, and let $z$ be axial distance. From rest at an edge, [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) gives speed $\sqrt{2gR\cos\theta}$. The [travel time](travel-time.md) is $T=(2gR)^{-1/2}\int\sqrt{R^2+z'^2}/\sqrt{\cos\theta}\,d\theta$. Since $z$ is a [cyclic coordinate](cyclic-coordinate.md), its conjugate quantity $z'/[\sqrt{\cos\theta}\sqrt{R^2+z'^2}]=K$ is constant, giving the displayed formula. For finite positive axial separation, $0<K<1$ is fixed uniquely by integrating $z'$ between the edges: the separation grows strictly from zero to infinity as $K$ increases from zero to one. The time integrand is strictly convex in $z'$, so its supporting-line inequality proves global minimality for the fixed endpoints. As the starting edge is approached, $z'\to0$: the initial direction is directly down the curved surface, with no axial component. This differs from the constant-pitch shortest [geodesic](geodesic.md) on the unrolled cylinder.
