# Compensated two-carrier magnetoresistance

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetoresistance](magnetoresistance.md)

Consider isotropic [electrons](electron.md) and [electron holes](electron-hole.md) with equal density $n$, charges $\mp e$, equal positive [charge carrier mobility](charge-carrier-mobility.md) $\mu$, and single-species zero-field [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) $\sigma_0=ne\mu$. In a transverse magnetic field, opposite Hall conductivity components cancel, while diagonal components add to $2\sigma_0/[1+(\mu B)^2]$. Inverting gives $\rho_{xy}=0$ and $\rho_{xx}=\rho_{yy}=[1+(\mu B)^2]/(2\sigma_0)$. Its large-field growth is $\sigma_0B^2/(2n^2e^2)$. The total zero-field resistivity is $1/(2\sigma_0)$, half the single-species resistivity; confusing these quantities introduces a factor-of-two normalization error.
