# Square-root reduction of a regular-singular differential equation

↑ **Parent:** [Frobenius method](frobenius-method.md)

With $s=\sqrt z$ and $Y(s)=y(s^2)$, the displayed [linear differential equation](linear-differential-equation.md) becomes $Y_{ss}+Y=0$ away from zero. Its [Frobenius solutions](frobenius-solution.md) have exponents $0$ and $1/2$: $\cos\sqrt z=\sum_{n\geq0}(-1)^nz^n/(2n)!$ and $\sin\sqrt z=z^{1/2}\sum_{n\geq0}(-1)^nz^n/(2n+1)!$. The first is an entire function of $z$; the second needs a local square-root branch. This illustrates how a [regular singular point](regular-singular-point.md) can arise from a degenerate change of independent variable.
