# Bethe stellar energy-production theory

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](stellar-nuclear-fusion.md)

Why can a [star](star.md) shine for so long, and why do hotter [stellar cores](stellar-core.md) favor different [nuclear reactions](nuclear-reaction.md)? A useful theory must supply both an adequate [energy](energy-split.md) reservoir and the right [stellar energy-generation rate](stellar-energy-generation-rate.md). [Nuclear binding energy](nuclear-binding-energy.md) supplies the reservoir; the [thermonuclear reaction rate](thermonuclear-reaction-rate.md), [stellar composition](stellar-chemical-abundance.md) and [stellar structure](stellar-structure-split.md) determine how quickly it is spent. The [proton–proton chain](proton-proton-chain.md) and [CNO cycle](cno-cycle.md) are two routes for [hydrogen burning](hydrogen-burning.md) with very different [temperature](temperature-split.md) sensitivities.

In his 1967 lecture, [Hans Albrecht Bethe](hans-albrecht-bethe.md) contrasted the short [Kelvin-Helmholtz cooling time](kelvin-helmholtz-cooling-time.md) with long ages inferred from terrestrial and stellar evidence.[1967 Nobel lecture, History, pp. 218–219](energy-production-in-stars-by-hans-albrecht-bethe-1967.md)

For an original solar-scale estimate, choose [mass](mass.md) $M=1.99\times10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg}$, [radius](radius.md) $R=6.96\times10^8\,\mathrm m$ and [luminosity](luminosity.md) $L=3.83\times10^{26}\,\mathrm W$. The characteristic [Kelvin-Helmholtz cooling time](kelvin-helmholtz-cooling-time.md) $GM^2/(RL)$ is about $31\,\mathrm{Myr}$; the precise gravitational reservoir has a structure-dependent coefficient. If a fraction $f=0.10$ of the [mass](mass.md) can burn its initial [hydrogen mass fraction](hydrogen-mass-fraction.md) $X=0.70$, with a chosen rest-energy conversion fraction $\eta=0.007$, the fuel estimate is

$$
t_{\rm fuel}\simeq\frac{\eta fXM c^2}{L}\simeq7.3\,\mathrm{Gyr}.
$$

This assumes fixed [luminosity](luminosity.md) and neglects [neutrino](neutrino.md) losses. It demonstrates the reservoir advantage, rather than predicting the [Sun](sun.md)'s age or its full [stellar evolution](stellar-evolution.md). Having enough fuel still does not identify a reaction: the fuel must release [energy](energy-split.md) at a rate compatible with [hydrostatic equilibrium](hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) and [radiative transfer](radiative-transfer.md).

[Hans Albrecht Bethe](hans-albrecht-bethe.md) and [Charles Louis Critchfield](charles-louis-critchfield.md) calculated [deuterium](deuterium.md) formation from two [protons](proton.md) in 1938, combining [quantum tunnelling](quantum-tunnelling.md) with a weak conversion of a [proton](proton.md) into a [neutron](neutron-split.md). Their calculated [nuclear reaction cross-section](nuclear-reaction-cross-section.md) was a theoretical input to [hydrogen burning](hydrogen-burning.md).[The Formation of Deuterons by Proton Combination, 15 August 1938, abstract and p. 248](the-formation-of-deuterons-by-proton-combination-by-hans-albrecht-bethe-and-charles-louis-critchfield.md) In 1939, [Hans Albrecht Bethe](hans-albrecht-bethe.md) analysed the catalytic carbon–nitrogen route and compared required [stellar core](stellar-core.md) [temperatures](temperature-split.md) with [stellar structure](stellar-structure-split.md) estimates in Table VIII. His reported solar agreement used historical abundance and structure assumptions; it should not be read as a modern demonstration that the [CNO cycle](cno-cycle.md) dominates the [Sun](sun.md).[Energy Production in Stars, 1939, abstract p. 434 and section 9, Table VIII, pp. 449–450](energy-production-in-stars-by-hans-albrecht-bethe-1939.md)

[Hans Albrecht Bethe](hans-albrecht-bethe.md)'s 1967 lecture credits [Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker](carl-friedrich-von-weizsacker.md)'s independent 1938 carbon–nitrogen cycle discovery, distinguishing it from calculation of rates and [temperature](temperature-split.md) dependence.[1967 Nobel lecture, p. 234, note 9](energy-production-in-stars-by-hans-albrecht-bethe-1967.md) The 1967 [Nobel Prize in Physics](nobel-prize-in-physics-split.md) recognized [Hans Albrecht Bethe](hans-albrecht-bethe.md)'s contributions to [nuclear reaction](nuclear-reaction.md) theory, especially stellar [energy](energy-split.md) production.[1967 entry, recipient and award motivation](all-nobel-prizes-in-physics-by-nobel-prize-outreach.md)

Laboratory [nuclear reaction cross-sections](nuclear-reaction-cross-section.md) were inputs, not measurements of an artificial [star](star.md). The 1939 paper converted thick-target yields to [nuclear reaction cross-sections](nuclear-reaction-cross-section.md) using a range–energy relation and tabulated capture data.[Section 2, p. 436, Tables II–III and discussion of thick-target yields](energy-production-in-stars-by-hans-albrecht-bethe-1939.md) A concrete apparatus example is the [Cockcroft-Walton lithium disintegration experiment](cockcroft-walton-lithium-disintegration-experiment.md), with an accelerated [proton](proton.md) beam, [lithium](lithium.md) target, [zinc sulfide](zinc-sulfide.md) screen and beam-blocking control. Its [nuclear reaction](nuclear-reaction.md) differs from both stellar burning routes. The 1967 lecture stresses extrapolation below laboratory access and tests for nearby nuclear levels.[1967 Nobel lecture, p. 224, Fig. 3](energy-production-in-stars-by-hans-albrecht-bethe-1967.md)
