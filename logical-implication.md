# Logical implication

↑ **Parent:** [Logical connective](logical-connective.md)

Logical implication forms the [proposition](proposition.md) $A\to B$, read “if $A$, then $B$”. In [classical propositional logic](classical-propositional-logic.md), it is false exactly when $A$ is true and $B$ is false. Equivalently, it has the same [truth table](truth-table.md) as $\neg A\vee B$, using [logical negation](negation.md) and [logical disjunction](logical-disjunction.md). A false hypothesis makes the [logical implication](logical-implication.md) true regardless of its conclusion; thus it asserts neither the hypothesis nor a causal connection.

For example, for every [natural number](natural-number.md) $n$, [divisibility](divisibility-split.md) by four implies that $n$ is an [even number](even-number.md): $n=4k=2(2k)$. At $n=8$, both statements are true. At $n=6$, the hypothesis is false and the conclusion true; at $n=3$, both are false. The only forbidden case is a multiple of four that is not an [even number](even-number.md).

In [Intuitionistic propositional logic](intuitionistic-propositional-logic.md), a proof of $A\to B$ consists of a construction transforming any proof of $A$ into a proof of $B$. The [implication introduction rule](implication-introduction-rule.md) proves $B$ under a temporary assumption $A$; the [modus ponens rule](implication-elimination-rule.md) applies the result when $A$ is available. The classical identification with $\neg A\vee B$ is not generally intuitionistically valid.
