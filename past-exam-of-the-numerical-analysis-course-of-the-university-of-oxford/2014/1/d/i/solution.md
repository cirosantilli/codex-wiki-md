<h1 id="1/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [QR factorization](../../../../../qr-decomposition.md) of a real $n\times n$ [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) is

$$
\boxed{A=QR,\qquad Q^TQ=I,\qquad R\text{ upper triangular}.}
$$

Thus $Q$ is an [orthogonal matrix](../../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) and $R$ is an [upper triangular matrix](../../../../../upper-triangular-matrix.md). It exists even if $A$ is singular; requiring positive diagonal entries of $R$ is a convenient uniqueness convention for nonsingular $A$, rather than part of the general existence statement. Over the complex numbers, replace [orthogonal](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) by [unitary](../../../../../unitary-connection.md) and transpose by [conjugate transpose](../../../../../conjugate-transpose.md).
