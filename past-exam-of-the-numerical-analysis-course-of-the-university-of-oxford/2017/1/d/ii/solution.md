<h1 id="1/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At $q=3$,

$$
\boxed{Q(g)=\frac{1+1/2}{36}=\frac1{24}=I(g).}
$$

The $y$ factor is now a [cubic polynomial](../../../../../cubic-polynomial.md). [Simpson's rule](../../../../../simpson-s-rule.md) still integrates it exactly because its [degree of precision of a quadrature rule](../../../../../degree-of-precision-of-a-quadrature-rule.md) is three. Exactness of [tensor-product quadrature](../../../../../tensor-product-quadrature.md) depends on the [polynomial degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) in each variable, rather than their sum.
