<h1 id="1/d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At $q=4$,

$$
\boxed{Q(g)=\frac{1+1/4}{36}=\frac5{144},\qquad I(g)=\frac1{30}.}
$$

This is an overestimate, with signed [quadrature error](../../../../../quadrature-error.md)

$$
Q(g)-I(g)=\frac{25-24}{720}=\frac1{720}.
$$

The [relative error](../../../../../relative-error.md) is $(1/720)/(1/30)=1/24$, approximately $4.17\%$. The [quartic polynomial](../../../../../quartic-polynomial.md) $y^4$ lies beyond the [degree of precision of a quadrature rule](../../../../../degree-of-precision-of-a-quadrature-rule.md) of [Simpson's rule](../../../../../simpson-s-rule.md), so the exactness of the previous cases no longer applies.
