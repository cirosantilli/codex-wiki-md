<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [trapezoidal rule](../../../../../trapezoidal-rule.md) is exact for every [affine function](../../../../../affine-function.md) $f(x)=\alpha+\beta x$, equivalently every [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) of [polynomial degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) at most one: the [linear interpolant](../../../../../linear-interpolant.md) is the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) itself. These [functions](../../../../../function-split.md) have $f''=0$, so the preceding upper bound is also zero. There is **no contradiction**: zero error is precisely what the bound predicts.

The [degree of precision of a quadrature rule](../../../../../degree-of-precision-of-a-quadrature-rule.md) is one, since the [quadratic polynomial](../../../../../quadratic-polynomial.md) in part (i) is not integrated exactly. This does not exclude exactness for particular higher-degree or nonpolynomial [functions](../../../../../function-split.md). For example $f(x)=(x-m)^3$, $m=(a+b)/2$, has zero [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) and opposite endpoint values, so the [trapezoidal rule](../../../../../trapezoidal-rule.md) also gives zero. Its [second derivative](../../../../../second-derivative.md) is generally nonzero, making the upper bound positive; an upper bound need not be attained. Signed [quadrature error](../../../../../quadrature-error.md) can cancel.
