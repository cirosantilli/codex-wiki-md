<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take the [identity matrix](../../../../../identity-matrix.md)

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Both $B_1$ and $B_2$ have [matrix rank](../../../../../matrix-rank.md) one, and

$$
\|A-B_1\|_2=\|A-B_2\|_2=1=\sigma_2(A).
$$

They are distinct and both attain the minimum [spectral norm](../../../../../matrix-2-norm.md) error from the preceding part. Hence **a best [low-rank approximation](../../../../../low-rank-approximation.md) of rank one need not be unique**. More generally, every [orthogonal projection](../../../../../orthogonal-projection.md) $uu^T$ with $\|u\|_2=1$ gives another such minimizer.
