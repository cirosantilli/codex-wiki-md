# Electron microscope

↑ **Parent:** [Electron optics](electron-optics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscope)

An [electron microscope](electron-microscope.md) forms a spatially enlarged representation of a specimen using [electrons](electron.md), [electron lenses](electron-lens.md), and a detector. Its appeal is access to structure too fine for an ordinary [optical microscope](optical-microscope.md), but a short [de Broglie wavelength](de-broglie-wavelength.md) alone does not guarantee fine [electron microscope resolution](electron-microscope-resolution.md). Increasing [magnification](magnification.md) can enlarge an existing blur without revealing any additional structure.

In [transmission electron microscopy](transmission-electron-microscopy.md), an [electron gun](electron-gun.md) illuminates a thin specimen; [magnetic electron lenses](magnetic-electron-lens.md) form and enlarge its transmitted image on a [fluorescent screen](fluorescent-screen.md) or detector. A [vacuum](vacuum.md) reduces collisions with gas. Lens [electric currents](electric-current.md) control focusing and [magnification](magnification.md).[JEOL, The difference between Electron Beam and Light: gun, vacuum, magnetic lenses and screen](transmission-electron-microscope-tem-by-jeol.md)
