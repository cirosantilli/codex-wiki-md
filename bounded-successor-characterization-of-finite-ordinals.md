# Bounded successor characterization of finite ordinals

↑ **Parent:** [Ordinal](ordinal.md)

A [Von Neumann ordinal](ordinal.md) $x$ is finite exactly when $x$ and every member of $x$ are either zero or [successor ordinals](successor-ordinal.md). Every [finite ordinal](finite-ordinal.md) has that property. Conversely, an infinite [ordinal](ordinal.md) is either $\omega$, which is a nonzero limit, or is greater than $\omega$ and contains that nonzero [limit ordinal](limit-ordinal.md). The test can be expressed by a bounded [first-order formula](first-order-formula.md): ordinality uses transitivity and membership comparability, and a nonempty [ordinal](ordinal.md) is a successor exactly when it has a greatest member. This defines the class of [natural numbers](natural-number.md) without quantifying over arbitrary infinite [inductive sets](inductive-set.md).
