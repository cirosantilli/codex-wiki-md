# Tidal locking

↑ **Parent:** [Planetary system dynamics](planetary-system-dynamics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_locking)

[Tidal locking](tidal-locking.md) settles a body's spin into a sustained relation with its orbital motion through [tidal forces](tidal-force.md) and [tidal dissipation](tidal-dissipation.md). The simplest case is [synchronous rotation](synchronous-rotation.md), a $1:1$ [spin-orbit resonance](spin-orbit-resonance.md). On an ideal [circular orbit](circular-orbit.md), with spin axis perpendicular to the [orbital plane](orbital-plane.md), the body keeps the same face toward its companion, as the [Moon](moon.md) approximately does toward [Earth](earth.md). [Tracy Vogel, NASA: tidal locking](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/tidal-locking/).

This article retains the broader sense of [tidal locking](tidal-locking.md) as capture into a [spin-orbit resonance](spin-orbit-resonance.md); the term is also commonly used specifically for [synchronous rotation](synchronous-rotation.md). Tidally evolved rotation need not be $1:1$: [Mercury](mercury-planet.md) has a $3:2$ [spin-orbit resonance](spin-orbit-resonance.md) with the [Sun](sun.md), turning three times in two [orbital periods](orbital-period.md). The distinction matters when inferring illumination or climate from a spin period. [Rodríguez et al. (2012): spin-orbit coupling](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.1580).
