<h1 id="4/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Choose $0<\rho<r$ and traverse the [circle](../../../../../circle.md) $|z|=\rho$ counterclockwise, so it has [positive orientation of a contour](../../../../../positive-orientation-of-a-contour.md). The [Cauchy coefficient formula](../../../../../cauchy-coefficient-formula.md) gives, for every [nonnegative integer](../../../../../natural-number.md) $n$,

$$
\boxed{c_n=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=\rho}\frac{f(z)}{z^{n+1}}\,dz,\qquad 0<\rho<r.}
$$

The smaller [radius](../../../../../radius.md) matters: the [holomorphic function](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) is only known on $|z|<r$, so its values on $|z|=r$ need not exist. To see directly why this [contour integral](../../../../../contour-integral.md) selects $c_n$, the [power series](../../../../../power-series.md) converges uniformly on $|z|=\rho$, allowing [uniform convergence and contour integration](../../../../../uniform-convergence-and-contour-integration.md) to justify integrating term by term. The [contour integral](../../../../../contour-integral.md) of $z^{k-n-1}$ is zero for $k\ne n$ and $2\pi i$ for $k=n$. Thus all other terms disappear.
