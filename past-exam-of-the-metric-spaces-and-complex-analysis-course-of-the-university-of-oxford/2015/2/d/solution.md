<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Define $g(p)=f(p)-f(-p)$ on the [sphere](../../../../sphere.md). It is [continuous](../../../../continuous-function.md) and satisfies $g(-p)=-g(p)$. If $g(p)=0$ at a chosen point, we are done. Otherwise choose a [unit vector](../../../../unit-vector.md) $q$ perpendicular to $p$ and follow the half [great circle](../../../../great-circle.md)

$$
\gamma(t)=\cos(\pi t)p+\sin(\pi t)q,\qquad0\leq t\leq1.
$$

The [Pythagorean theorem](../../../../pythagorean-theorem.md) shows that $\gamma(t)$ lies on the [sphere](../../../../sphere.md), and its endpoints are $p,-p$. The [continuous function](../../../../continuous-function.md) $g\circ\gamma$ has opposite signs at the endpoints. The [Intermediate value theorem](../../../../intermediate-value-theorem.md) therefore gives a zero $t_*$, so

$$
\boxed{f(\gamma(t_*))=f(-\gamma(t_*))}.
$$

This is a scalar equal-value assertion for an [antipodal pair](../../../../antipodal-pair.md); it uses only a [path](../../../../continuous-path.md) and the [Intermediate value theorem](../../../../intermediate-value-theorem.md).
