<h1 id="5/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The equation requires $\sinh z=\pm i\cosh a$. Write $z=x+iy$, with real $x,y$. Comparing [real parts](../../../../../real-part.md) and [imaginary parts](../../../../../imaginary-part.md) using part (b)(i) gives

$$
\sinh x\cos y=0,\qquad \cosh x\sin y=\pm\cosh a.
$$

If $x=0$, the second equality would require $|\sin y|=\cosh a>1$, impossible because $a>0$. Thus $\sinh x\ne0$, forcing $\cos y=0$ and

$$
y=\frac\pi2+k\pi,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
$$

Now $|\sin y|=1$, so $\cosh x=\cosh a$. The [hyperbolic cosine](../../../../../hyperbolic-cosine.md) is even and is a [strictly increasing function](../../../../../strictly-increasing-function.md) on $[0,\infty)$, hence $x=\pm a$. Conversely, substituting these values gives $\sinh(x+iy)=i(-1)^k\cosh a$, so every value found does satisfy the original equation. Therefore the complete set is

$$
\boxed{z=\pm a+i\left(\frac\pi2+k\pi\right),\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.}
$$
