<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

One standard form of [Cauchy's residue theorem](../../../../residue-theorem.md) is the following. Let $\Gamma$ be a [positively oriented contour](../../../../positive-orientation-of-a-contour.md) that is a [simple closed curve](../../../../simple-closed-curve.md), and let $f$ be a [holomorphic function](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on an [open set](../../../../open-set.md) containing $\Gamma$ and its interior except for finitely many interior [isolated singularities](../../../../isolated-singularity-split.md) $z_1,\ldots,z_m$. There are no [singularities](../../../../complex-singularity.md) on $\Gamma$. Then

$$
\boxed{\int_\Gamma f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^m\operatorname{Res}(f,z_j).}
$$

The [residue](../../../../residue.md) $\operatorname{Res}(f,z_j)$ is the coefficient of $(z-z_j)^{-1}$ in the [Laurent series](../../../../laurent-series.md) at $z_j$. Each interior [isolated singularity](../../../../isolated-singularity-split.md) contributes once because the [winding number](../../../../winding-number.md) of this [positively oriented contour](../../../../positive-orientation-of-a-contour.md) about each such point is one. Reversing the [orientation of a contour](../../../../orientation-of-a-contour.md) reverses the sign. This form of [Cauchy's residue theorem](../../../../residue-theorem.md) applies to the rectangle below.
