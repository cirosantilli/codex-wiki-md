<h1 id="5/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The two terms in the preceding formula are the [real part](../../../../../real-part.md) and [imaginary part](../../../../../imaginary-part.md). Their squared sum is the squared [complex modulus](../../../../../complex-modulus.md):

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\sinh(x+iy)|^2&=\sinh^2x\cos^2y+\cosh^2x\sin^2y\\
&=\sinh^2x(\cos^2y+\sin^2y)+\sin^2y\\
&=\sinh^2x+\sin^2y.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\cosh^2x-\sinh^2x=1$ follows directly from the [exponential function](../../../../../exponential-function.md) definitions. The [hyperbolic sine modulus identity](../../../../../hyperbolic-sine-modulus-identity.md) therefore yields

$$
\boxed{|\sinh(x+iy)|=\sqrt{\sinh^2x+\sin^2y}\geq|\sinh x|.}
$$

Equality holds exactly when $\sin y=0$, that is, when $y$ is an integer multiple of $\pi$. The lower bound is uniform in $y$, which will control whole vertical sides of the [contour](../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) rather than just their endpoints.
