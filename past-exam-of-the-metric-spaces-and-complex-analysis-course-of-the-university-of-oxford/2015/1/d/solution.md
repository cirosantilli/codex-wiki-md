<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

By the [Heine-Borel theorem](../../../../heine-borel-theorem.md), a noncompact [subset](../../../../subset.md) $X\subseteq\mathbb R$ is either [unbounded](../../../../unbounded.md) or not [closed](../../../../closed-set.md). If $X$ is [unbounded](../../../../unbounded.md), the restriction $f(x)=x$ is a [continuous function](../../../../continuous-function.md) with [unbounded](../../../../unbounded.md) range.

Otherwise $X$ is [bounded](../../../../boundedness.md) and not [closed](../../../../closed-set.md). Choose $a\in\overline X\setminus X$, and define

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\frac1{|x-a|},\qquad x\in X.}
$$

Its denominator is nonzero on $X$, so it is [continuous](../../../../continuous-function.md). Since $a$ is in the [closure](../../../../closure-topology.md), for every $n$ there is $x_n\in X$ with $0<|x_n-a|<1/n$. Hence $f(x_n)>n$, proving that this [continuous function](../../../../continuous-function.md) is [unbounded](../../../../unbounded.md). These two cases establish [unbounded continuous witnesses to noncompactness of a real subspace](../../../../unbounded-continuous-witnesses-to-noncompactness-of-a-real-subspace.md).
