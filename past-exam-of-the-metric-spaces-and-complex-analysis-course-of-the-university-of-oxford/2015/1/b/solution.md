<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [empty set](../../../../empty-set.md) is [bounded](../../../../boundedness.md). Otherwise choose $p$ in the [compact metric space](../../../../compact-metric-space.md) $X$. The [open balls](../../../../open-ball.md) $B(p,n)$, $n=1,2,\ldots$, form an [open cover](../../../../open-cover.md). A [finite subcover](../../../../finite-subcover.md) exists by [compactness](../../../../compact-space.md); because the balls are nested, the largest one already covers $X$. Hence $X\subseteq B(p,N)$ for some $N$.

For $x,y\in X$, the [triangle inequality](../../../../triangle-inequality.md) gives $d(x,y)\leq d(x,p)+d(p,y)<2N$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{diam}X\leq2N<\infty,\quad\text{so }X\text{ is bounded}.}
$$

The proof uses only [compactness](../../../../compact-space.md) and the [metric](../../../../metric.md), not a coordinate system.
