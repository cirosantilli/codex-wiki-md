↑ **Parent:** [i](../i.md)

Both properties are preserved by a [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md).

For [sequential compactness](../../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md), take any [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $(y_n)$ in $f(X)$ and choose $x_n\in X$ with $f(x_n)=y_n$. If $X$ is [sequentially compact](../../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md), some [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md) satisfies $x_{n_k}\to x\in X$. By [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md), $y_{n_k}=f(x_{n_k})\to f(x)\in f(X)$. Hence $f(X)$ is a [sequentially compact space](../../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md).

For [connectedness](../../../../../connected-space.md), suppose instead that $f(X)=U\cup V$ is a separation into disjoint nonempty relatively [open sets](../../../../../open-set.md). Then $f^{-1}(U)$ and $f^{-1}(V)$ are disjoint nonempty [open sets](../../../../../open-set.md) of $X$ whose union is $X$, contradicting [connectedness](../../../../../connected-space.md). Therefore $f(X)$ is a [connected space](../../../../../connected-space.md).
