<h1 id="2/d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [iii](../iii.md)

Apply the local bound from condition (1) separately to $f$ and $-f$. For any $a\in X$ and $\varepsilon>0$, there are $r_+,r_->0$ such that

$$
d(x,a)<r_+\implies f(x)<f(a)+\varepsilon,\qquad d(x,a)<r_-\implies -f(x)<-f(a)+\varepsilon.
$$

With $\delta=\min\{r_+,r_-\}$, both inequalities hold when $d(x,a)<\delta$, so

$$
f(a)-\varepsilon<f(x)<f(a)+\varepsilon.
$$

This is exactly [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) at $a$. Since $a$ was arbitrary, $f$ is [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) on $X$: [upper semicontinuity](../../../../../upper-semicontinuity.md) of $f$ and of $-f$ is equivalent to simultaneous [upper semicontinuity](../../../../../upper-semicontinuity.md) and [lower semicontinuity](../../../../../lower-semicontinuity.md) of $f$.
