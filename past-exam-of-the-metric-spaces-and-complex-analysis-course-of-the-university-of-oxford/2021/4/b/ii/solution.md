<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

The only possible finite [singularities](../../../../../complex-singularity.md) are $z_\pm=\pm i\pi/2$, the zeros of $4z^2+\pi^2$. They are simple zeros of the denominator, because its [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) $8z_\pm$ is nonzero. They are also simple zeros of the numerator: $\cosh z_\pm=0$ and $\sinh z_\pm=\pm i\ne0$.

The common simple factors cancel, so both points are [removable singularities](../../../../../removable-singularity.md), with extension values

$$
\boxed{\lim_{z\to z_\pm}\frac{\cosh z}{4z^2+\pi^2}=\frac{\sinh z_\pm}{8z_\pm}=\frac1{4\pi}.}
$$

There are no other finite [singularities](../../../../../complex-singularity.md); after inserting these two values the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) is [entire](../../../../../entire-function.md).
