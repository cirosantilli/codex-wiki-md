<h1 id="4/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [i](../i.md)

The expression is $F(z)=e^{z^2+1/z}=e^{z^2}e^{1/z}$, which is [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) for every $z\ne0$. At zero,

$$
e^{1/z}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{z^{-n}}{n!}
$$

has infinitely many nonzero negative-power terms, so it has an [essential singularity](../../../../../essential-singularity.md). Multiplication by the nonvanishing [entire function](../../../../../entire-function.md) $e^{z^2}$ preserves that classification: if $F$ had a [removable singularity](../../../../../removable-singularity.md) or a [pole](../../../../../pole.md), multiplying by $e^{-z^2}$ would give the same type for $e^{1/z}$, a contradiction. Hence

$$
\boxed{0\text{ is the only finite singularity, and it is essential}.}
$$
