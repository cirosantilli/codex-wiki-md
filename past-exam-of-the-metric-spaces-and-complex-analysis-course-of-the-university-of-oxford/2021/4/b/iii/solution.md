<h1 id="4/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [iii](../iii.md)

For $z\ne0$, the denominator vanishes exactly when $1/z=(2k+1)\pi i$. Thus set

$$
z_k=\frac1{(2k+1)\pi i},\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
$$

Each denominator zero is simple, since

$$
\left(e^{1/z}+1\right)'\big|_{z=z_k}=-\frac{e^{1/z_k}}{z_k^2}=\frac1{z_k^2}\ne0.
$$

Every $z_k$ is purely imaginary and nonzero. The identity $\sin(iy)=i\sinh y$ for real $y$ shows $\sin z_k\ne0$, so there is no cancellation. Each $z_k$ is consequently a [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md), with [residue](../../../../../residue.md) $z_k^2\sin z_k$.

These [poles](../../../../../pole.md) accumulate at zero. Therefore

$$
\boxed{z_k\text{ are simple poles, and }0\text{ is a non-isolated singularity}.}
$$

In particular, zero has no punctured [neighbourhood](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) on which the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) is [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md), so it cannot be classified as a [removable singularity](../../../../../removable-singularity.md), a [pole](../../../../../pole.md), or an isolated [essential singularity](../../../../../essential-singularity.md). There are no other finite [singularities](../../../../../complex-singularity.md).
