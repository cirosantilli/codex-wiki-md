<h1 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [iii](../iii.md)

The denominator vanishes exactly when $e^z=-1$, namely at $z=(2k+1)\pi i$, $k\in\mathbb Z$. Its [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) there is $e^z=-1\ne0$, so each such zero is simple and the numerator does not cancel it. Hence

$$
\boxed{\frac1{1+e^z}\text{ is holomorphic on }\mathbb C\setminus\{(2k+1)\pi i:k\in\mathbb Z\},}
$$

and every omitted point is a [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md).
