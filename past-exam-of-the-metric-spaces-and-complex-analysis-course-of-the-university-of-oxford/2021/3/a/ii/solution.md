<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

The zeros of the [hyperbolic sine](../../../../../hyperbolic-sine.md) are exactly $z=k\pi i$, $k\in\mathbb Z$, since $\sinh z=0$ is equivalent to $e^{2z}=1$. Each zero is simple because $\cosh(k\pi i)=(-1)^k\ne0$. The quotient is therefore [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) precisely on

$$
\boxed{\mathbb C\setminus\{k\pi i:k\in\mathbb Z\}.}
$$

At zero, the [Taylor expansion](../../../../../taylor-expansion.md) $\sinh z=z+O(z^3)$ gives $z/\sinh^2z=1/z+O(z)$, so zero is a [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md). At $k\pi i\ne0$, the denominator has a double zero and the numerator is nonzero, giving a [pole](../../../../../pole.md) of order two. Thus none of the omitted points admits a [holomorphic extension](../../../../../holomorphic-extension.md).
