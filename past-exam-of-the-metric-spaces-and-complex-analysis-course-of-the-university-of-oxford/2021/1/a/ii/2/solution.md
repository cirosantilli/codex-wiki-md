<h1 id="1/a/ii/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The answers are

$$
\boxed{\text{yes, no, no, no}.}
$$

Distinct [natural numbers](../../../../../../natural-number.md) have $d(x,y)>1$. Taking $\varepsilon=1$ in the [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) condition forces every sufficiently late pair of terms to be equal. Every [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) is therefore eventually constant and converges in $\mathbb N$, proving [completeness](../../../../../../completeness.md).

The [sequence](../../../../../../sequence.md) $x_n=n$ has no [convergent subsequence](../../../../../../convergent-subsequence.md), since convergence would force its sufficiently late terms to lie within distance $1$ of its [limit of a sequence](../../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md), hence to equal that limit. Thus [sequential compactness](../../../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md) fails.

Every [singleton](../../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md) is an [open ball](../../../../../../open-ball.md) of radius $1$, so the [metric topology](../../../../../../metric-topology.md) is the [discrete topology](../../../../../../discrete-space.md). For example, $\{1\}$ and $\mathbb N\setminus\{1\}$ form a separation into two nonempty [open sets](../../../../../../open-set.md), so $X$ is not [connected](../../../../../../connected-space.md). It is consequently not [path-connected](../../../../../../path-connected-space.md) either.
