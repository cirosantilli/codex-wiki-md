A [metric space](../../../../../metric-space.md) $(X,d)$ is a [complete metric space](../../../../../complete-metric-space.md) if every [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) in $X$ converges to a point of $X$. Explicitly, the hypothesis is

$$
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N\ \forall m,n\geq N:\ d(x_m,x_n)<\varepsilon,
$$

and the conclusion is that some $x\in X$ satisfies $d(x_n,x)\to0$.

It is a [sequentially compact space](../../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md) if every [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) in $X$ has a [convergent subsequence](../../../../../convergent-subsequence.md) whose [limit of a sequence](../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md) belongs to $X$.

It is a [connected space](../../../../../connected-space.md) if there are no disjoint nonempty [open subsets](../../../../../open-set.md) $U,V$ of $X$ with $X=U\cup V$. Here openness refers to the [metric topology](../../../../../metric-topology.md) of $X$.

It is a [path-connected space](../../../../../path-connected-space.md) if, for every $x,y\in X$, there is a [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md) $\gamma:[0,1]\to X$ with $\gamma(0)=x$ and $\gamma(1)=y$. Such a map is a [path](../../../../../continuous-path.md). Every [path-connected space](../../../../../path-connected-space.md) is a [connected space](../../../../../connected-space.md): a separation would pull back along a [path](../../../../../continuous-path.md) joining its two sides to a separation of the [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) $[0,1]$.
