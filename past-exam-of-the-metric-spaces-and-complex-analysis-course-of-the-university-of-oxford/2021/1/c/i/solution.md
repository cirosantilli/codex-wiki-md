<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [i](../i.md)

Extend the coefficient lists of each [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) by zeros. Then $\rho(p,q)=\sum_{k\geq0}|a_k-b_k|$ is finite because only finitely many terms are nonzero. It is nonnegative and symmetric. Moreover, $\rho(p,q)=0$ holds exactly when every coefficient agrees, which by uniqueness of [polynomial coefficients](../../../../../coefficient-of-a-polynomial.md) is equivalent to $p=q$.

If $r(t)=\sum c_kt^k$, the [triangle inequality](../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) for the [absolute value](../../../../../absolute-value.md) gives

$$
\rho(p,r)=\sum_k|a_k-c_k|\leq\sum_k\bigl(|a_k-b_k|+|b_k-c_k|\bigr)=\rho(p,q)+\rho(q,r).
$$

Thus $\rho$ satisfies all the [metric](../../../../../metric.md) axioms. It is the [metric](../../../../../metric.md) induced by the [polynomial coefficient sum norm](../../../../../polynomial-coefficient-sum-norm.md).
