<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

The [arctangent pullback metric](../../../../../arctangent-pullback-metric.md) is not [complete](../../../../../completeness.md). Take $x_n=n$. Since $\arctan n\to\pi/2$, its transformed [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) is [Cauchy](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md), and hence $(x_n)$ is a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) for $\rho$. If it converged in $\rho$ to $x\in\mathbb R$, then

$$
|\arctan n-\arctan x|\longrightarrow0
$$

would force $\arctan x=\pi/2$, which is impossible. The missing endpoint of $I$ explains the failure of [completeness](../../../../../completeness.md), even though part (i) shows that the [metric topologies](../../../../../metric-topology.md) agree.
