<h1 id="5/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [iii](../iii.md)

Suppose $w\in\mathbb C$ were omitted by $g$. Then

$$
F(z)=\frac1{g(z)-w}
$$

would be an [entire function](../../../../../entire-function.md). The [reverse triangle inequality](../../../../../reverse-triangle-inequality.md) gives $|g(z)-w|\geq|g(z)|-|w|\to\infty$ as $|z|\to\infty$, so $F(z)\to0$ there. On a sufficiently large closed [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md), $F$ is bounded by [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) and the [extreme value theorem](../../../../../extreme-value-theorem.md); outside it, the limit gives a bound. The [Liouville theorem](../../../../../liouville-theorem.md) therefore makes $F$ constant, and the limit forces $F\equiv0$.

That is impossible, since $1/(g(z)-w)$ is nonzero at every finite $z$. No value $w$ can be omitted, and hence

$$
\boxed{g\text{ is surjective onto }\mathbb C.}
$$
