<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the continuous argument $0<\arg z<2\pi$ on the positive-axis slit plane and define

$$
\boxed{L(z)=\log|z|+i\arg z.}
$$

This [analytic logarithm on the positive-axis slit plane](../../../../../analytic-logarithm-on-the-positive-axis-slit-plane.md) is holomorphic, satisfies $e^{L(z)}=z$ and $L'(z)=1/z$, and has $L(-1)=i\pi$. That value fixes the branch uniquely on the connected domain. For positive real $x$, the two boundary limits are

$$
L(x+i0)=\log x,\qquad L(x-i0)=\log x+2\pi i.
$$

The jump, rather than a pole of the integrand, is what the keyhole argument exploits.
