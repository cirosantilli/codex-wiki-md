<h1 id="5/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [Taylor expansions](../../../../../taylor-expansion.md) give

$$
e^{iaz}\sin(bz)=(1+iaz+O(z^2))(bz+O(z^3))=bz+iabz^2+O(z^3),
$$

and hence $f(z)=b/z+iab+O(z)$. Since $b>0$, zero is **a [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md) with [residue](../../../../../residue.md) $b$**. It is not a [removable singularity](../../../../../removable-singularity.md), despite the vanishing numerator, because the denominator has a zero of order two.
