<h1 id="4/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**No such [entire function](../../../../../entire-function.md) exists.** If it did, the entire function $h(z)^2-z$ would vanish at the distinct points $(n/(n+1))^2$, whose [accumulation point](../../../../../limit-point.md) is one. The [identity theorem](../../../../../identity-theorem.md) would give $h(z)^2=z$ on all of $\mathbb C$.

At zero this forces $h(0)=0$. Differentiating the identity gives $2h(z)h'(z)=1$, impossible at zero. Equivalently, a squared holomorphic function has an even-order zero, whereas $z$ has a simple zero. This is the obstruction expressed by [no entire square root of the identity function](../../../../../no-entire-square-root-of-the-identity-function.md); a square-root branch near one cannot extend holomorphically through zero.
