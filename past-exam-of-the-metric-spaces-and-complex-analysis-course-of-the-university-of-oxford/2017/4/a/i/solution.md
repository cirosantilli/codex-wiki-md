<h1 id="4/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [identity theorem](../../../../../identity-theorem.md) says that two holomorphic functions on a connected domain which agree on a set with an accumulation point inside that domain agree everywhere there.

**No such [entire function](../../../../../entire-function.md) exists.** On the [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md) $1/(2m)\to0$ indexed by [even numbers](../../../../../even-number.md), the proposed function equals $\sin z$. The [identity theorem](../../../../../identity-theorem.md) would force $f(z)=\sin z$ throughout $\mathbb C$. At the points indexed by [odd numbers](../../../../../odd-number.md) it is instead required to equal $-\sin z$, a contradiction since $\sin(1/n)>0$ for positive $n$. The accumulation point zero lies inside the assumed domain of holomorphy, which is essential to this argument.
