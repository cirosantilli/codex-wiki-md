<h1 id="6/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Treat a straight line together with infinity as a [generalized circle](../../../../../generalized-circle-under-a-mobius-transformation.md) on the [Riemann sphere](../../../../../riemann-sphere.md). Its finite points have a real equation

$$
A|z|^2+Bz+\overline B\,\overline z+C=0,\qquad A,C\in\mathbb R.
$$

Translations and nonzero complex scalings preserve circles and lines. Under $z=1/w$, multiplying the equation by $|w|^2$ gives

$$
C|w|^2+\overline B w+B\overline w+A=0,
$$

again the equation of a circle or line. Nondegeneracy is preserved because the reciprocal map is a bijection of the sphere. The zero and infinite points are interpreted there, rather than discarded when multiplying denominators.

Every Möbius map with $c\ne0$ factors as

$$
\frac{az+b}{cz+d}=\frac ac+\frac{bc-ad}{c^2}\frac1{z+d/c};
$$

if $c=0$ it is already affine. Thus [Möbius translation-scaling-inversion factorization](../../../../../mobius-translation-scaling-inversion-factorization.md) and the [reciprocal transformation of a generalized circle](../../../../../reciprocal-transformation-of-a-generalized-circle.md) prove **every generalized circle maps to another generalized circle**. A circle becomes a line exactly when its image contains infinity, that is, when the original circle contains the map's pole.
