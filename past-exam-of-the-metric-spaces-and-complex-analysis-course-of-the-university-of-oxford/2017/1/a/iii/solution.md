<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A [complete metric space](../../../../../complete-metric-space.md) is one in which **every [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) converges to a point of that space**. For an incomplete example, take $(0,1)$ with the usual [Euclidean metric](../../../../../euclidean-metric.md) and $x_n=1/(n+1)$. It is [Cauchy](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) because it converges to zero in $\mathbb R$. It cannot converge inside $(0,1)$: any such convergence would also hold in $\mathbb R$, contradicting uniqueness of the limit, since zero is excluded. Thus the ambient metric and the underlying set both matter to [completeness](../../../../../completeness.md).
