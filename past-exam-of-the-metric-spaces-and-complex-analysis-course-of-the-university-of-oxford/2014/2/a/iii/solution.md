<h1 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $(x_n,y_n)$ be a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) in the product. Since each coordinate distance is bounded by $\delta$, both $(x_n)$ and $(y_n)$ are Cauchy in their respective factors. By [completeness](../../../../../completeness.md) they have limits $x\in M$, $y\in N$. Part (ii) then gives convergence of the pairs to $(x,y)$.

Thus **the product of two [complete metric spaces](../../../../../complete-metric-space.md) is complete under the [maximum product metric](../../../../../maximum-product-metric.md)**. This checks every [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md), rather than assuming beforehand that it has a coordinate limit.
