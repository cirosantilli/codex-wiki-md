<h1 id="6/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $z=x+iy$ and $r^2=|z|^2$, solving the [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) and projection equations gives

$$
\pi^{-1}(z)=\left(\frac{2x}{1+r^2},\frac{2y}{1+r^2},\frac{r^2-1}{r^2+1}\right).
$$

For $z\ne0$, the reciprocal has real and imaginary parts $x/r^2$ and $-y/r^2$, and squared modulus $1/r^2$. Substituting these into the inverse formula produces

$$
\left(\frac{2x}{1+r^2},-\frac{2y}{1+r^2},-\frac{r^2-1}{r^2+1}\right).
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\pi^{-1}\circ(z\mapsto1/z)\circ\pi:(a,b,c)\longmapsto(a,-b,-c).}
$$

The reciprocal exchanges zero and infinity, corresponding to exchanging the south and north poles, so the formula holds there too. On the [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) this is a [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) through $\pi$ about the first coordinate axis, not the antipodal map.
