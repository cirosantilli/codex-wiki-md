↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In the [upper half-plane](../../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md), the argument lies strictly between zero and $\pi$, while $\log|z|$ can be any real number. Conversely, if $w=s+it$ with $0<t<\pi$, then $z=e^w$ is in the [upper half-plane](../../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md) and $L(z)=w$.

$$
\boxed{L(H)=\{w\in\mathbb C:0<\operatorname{Im}w<\pi\}.}
$$

Thus the [principal complex logarithm](../../../../../principal-complex-logarithm.md) is a [conformal bijection](../../../../../biholomorphism.md) from the [upper half-plane](../../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md) onto that horizontal strip, with inverse the [exponential function](../../../../../exponential-function.md) restricted to the strip.
