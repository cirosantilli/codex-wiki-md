<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At $z=x+iy$, compute the [complex derivative](../../../../complex-derivative.md) first along real increments and then along imaginary increments. [Holomorphy](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) gives

$$
f'(z)=u_x+iv_x=\frac{u_y+iv_y}{i}=v_y-iu_y.
$$

Equating real and imaginary parts proves the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md):

$$
\boxed{u_x=v_y,\qquad u_y=-v_x.}
$$

The partial derivatives exist because these directional difference quotients are limits of the [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) [derivative](../../../../derivative.md). This argument proves the necessary equations; it does not claim that their pointwise existence alone is sufficient for [holomorphy](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md).
