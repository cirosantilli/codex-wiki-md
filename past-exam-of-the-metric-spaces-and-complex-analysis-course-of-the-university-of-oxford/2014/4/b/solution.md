<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Fix any bounded disk avoiding the integers, and choose $R$ with $|z|\leq R$ on it. For sufficiently large $|n|$, say $|n|\geq2R$ and $|n|\geq1$,

$$
|n^2-z^2|\geq n^2-|z|^2\geq\frac34 n^2,
\qquad
\left|\frac1{n^2-z^2}\right|\leq\frac4{3n^2}.
$$

The convergent numerical series $\sum_{n\ne0}n^{-2}$ and the [Weierstrass M-test](../../../../weierstrass-m-test.md) give absolute and [uniform convergence](../../../../uniform-convergence.md) of the tails. The finitely many remaining summands are [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) away from their integer poles. Thus the partial sums converge locally uniformly on $\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Z$.

By part (a)(iii), **the sum is a [holomorphic function](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on $\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Z$**. The term $n=0$ is $-1/z^2$ and is harmless on this domain; it must not be omitted.
