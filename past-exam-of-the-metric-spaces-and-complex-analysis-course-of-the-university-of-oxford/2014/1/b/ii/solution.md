**False:** the target need not equal the image. Take the one-point [compact space](../../../../../compact-space.md) $M=\{0\}$, $N=\mathbb R$, and the [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md) $f(0)=0$. The target is not [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md), for example because it is unbounded.

The valid theorem is that a [continuous image of a compact space](../../../../../continuous-image-of-a-compact-space.md) is [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md). Thus $f(M)$ is [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md); [compactness](../../../../../compact-space.md) of $N$ follows if [surjectivity](../../../../../surjective-function.md) is added.
