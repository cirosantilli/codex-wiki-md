<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [simple zero](../../../../simple-zero.md) means $h(a)=0$ and $h'(a)\ne0$. The local factorization is $h(z)=(z-a)h_1(z)$, where $h_1$ is [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) and $h_1(a)=h'(a)\ne0$. Thus

$$
\frac{g(z)}{h(z)}=\frac{g(z)/h_1(z)}{z-a}.
$$

The numerator is [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) near $a$, so its constant Taylor coefficient is the coefficient of $(z-a)^{-1}$ in the quotient. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{res}\left(\frac gh;a\right)=\frac{g(a)}{h'(a)}.}
$$

If $g(a)=0$, the possible [pole](../../../../pole.md) cancels and the [residue](../../../../residue.md) is zero; the formula does not require $g/h$ actually to have a [pole](../../../../pole.md). This is the [residue formula for a quotient at a simple denominator zero](../../../../residue-formula-for-a-quotient-at-a-simple-denominator-zero.md).
