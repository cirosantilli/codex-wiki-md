A [metric space](../../../../../metric-space.md) $X$ is a [compact space](../../../../../compact-space.md) if every [open cover](../../../../../open-cover.md) $X=\bigcup_{\alpha\in I}U_\alpha$, with all $U_\alpha$ open in $X$, has a [finite subcover](../../../../../finite-subcover.md): finitely many indices $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r$ cover $X$.

It is a [sequentially compact space](../../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md) if every [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $(x_n)$ in $X$ has a [convergent subsequence](../../../../../convergent-subsequence.md) $(x_{n_k})$ whose [limit of a sequence](../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md) belongs to $X$. In formulas,

$$
\boxed{\forall(x_n)\subseteq X\ \exists x\in X,\ n_1<n_2<\cdots:\ d(x_{n_k},x)\to0}.
$$

A [finite subcover](../../../../../finite-subcover.md) is the defining test for [compactness](../../../../../compact-space.md); a [convergent subsequence](../../../../../convergent-subsequence.md) is the defining test for [sequential compactness](../../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md).
