<h1 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [i](../i.md)

The source prints $m\ne0$ in the zero case. Since the coordinates are indexed by positive integers, that overlaps its $m=n$ case and does not define a sequence. We use the intended coordinate vectors, with $e_m^{(n)}=1$ for $m=n$ and $e_m^{(n)}=0$ for $m\ne n$.

Every $e^{(n)}$ belongs to $X$, because its entries are bounded by one and its coordinate sequence eventually vanishes. If $n\ne k$, their [supremum norm](../../../../../supremum-norm.md) distance is

$$
\boxed{d(e^{(n)},e^{(k)})=1}.
$$

Indeed, the difference is $1$ at coordinate $n$, $-1$ at coordinate $k$, and zero elsewhere. No subsequence is a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md), since arbitrarily late distinct terms remain distance one apart. Every [convergent sequence](../../../../../convergent-sequence.md) is a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md), so no [convergent subsequence](../../../../../convergent-subsequence.md) exists. Therefore $\boxed{X\text{ is not sequentially compact}}$.
