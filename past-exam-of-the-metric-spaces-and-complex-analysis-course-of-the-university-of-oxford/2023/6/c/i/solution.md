<h1 id="6/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [i](../i.md)

Suppose $F:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C$ were a [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) bijection. By the allowed nonvanishing derivative result, $F'(z)\ne0$ throughout $\mathbb D$. The [holomorphic inverse function theorem](../../../../../holomorphic-inverse-function-theorem.md) gives a [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) inverse locally at every image point. Since $F$ is globally bijective, these local inverses agree with its global inverse $H:\mathbb C\to\mathbb D$.

Thus $H$ is an [entire function](../../../../../entire-function.md) with $|H|<1$. The [Liouville theorem](../../../../../liouville-theorem.md) forces $H$ to be constant, contradicting its being an inverse. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{there is no holomorphic bijection }\mathbb D\to\mathbb C}.
$$

This is the analytic obstruction behind the fact that the [complex plane and unit disc are homeomorphic but not conformally equivalent](../../../../../complex-plane-and-unit-disc-are-homeomorphic-but-not-conformally-equivalent.md).
