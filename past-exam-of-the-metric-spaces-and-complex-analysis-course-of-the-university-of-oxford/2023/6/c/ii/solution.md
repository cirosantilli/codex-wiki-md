<h1 id="6/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

Yes. An explicit [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) surjection is the [rational function](../../../../../rational-function.md)

$$
\boxed{F(z)=\left(\frac z{1-z}\right)^2,\qquad |z|<1}.
$$

Its denominator never vanishes inside the [unit disc](../../../../../unit-disc.md). To prove surjectivity, take any $w\in\mathbb C$ and choose a square root $r$ of $w$ with $\operatorname{Re}r\geq0$; one of the two opposite roots always has this property. Define $z=r/(1+r)$. Then $1+r\ne0$ and

$$
|1+r|^2-|r|^2=1+2\operatorname{Re}r>0,
$$

so $|z|<1$. Also $z/(1-z)=r$, and consequently $F(z)=r^2=w$.

This is a [holomorphic surjection from the unit disc onto the complex plane](../../../../../holomorphic-surjection-from-the-unit-disc-onto-the-complex-plane.md). It does not contradict part (i): it is not injective. For example, $F(i/(1+i))=F(-i/(1-i))=-1$, with two distinct arguments of modulus $1/\sqrt2$.
