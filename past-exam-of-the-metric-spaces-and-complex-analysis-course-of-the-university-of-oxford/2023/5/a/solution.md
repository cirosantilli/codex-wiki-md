<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [a](../a.md)

The [simple zero](../../../../simple-zero.md) assumption gives the local factorization

$$
h(z)=(z-a)k(z),\qquad k(a)=h'(a)\ne0,
$$

where $k$ is [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md). In a small disc $k$ stays nonzero, so $q=g/k$ is [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) there. Expanding $q$ to first order,

$$
\frac{g(z)}{h(z)}=\frac{q(z)}{z-a}=\frac{q(a)}{z-a}+\text{a function holomorphic near }a.
$$

The [residue](../../../../residue.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_a\left(\frac gh\right)=q(a)=\frac{g(a)}{h'(a)}}.
$$

If $g(a)=0$, the quotient has a [removable singularity](../../../../removable-singularity.md) rather than a [simple pole](../../../../simple-pole.md), and the same formula correctly gives zero. This is the [residue formula for a quotient at a simple denominator zero](../../../../residue-formula-for-a-quotient-at-a-simple-denominator-zero.md).
