<h1 id="4/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [iv](../iv.md)

A function has a [pole](../../../../../pole.md) of order $n$ at $a$ when, on a punctured neighbourhood,

$$
\boxed{f(z)=\frac{h(z)}{(z-a)^n},\qquad h\text{ holomorphic near }a,\quad h(a)\ne0}.
$$

Equivalently, $(z-a)^n f(z)$ has a [removable singularity](../../../../../removable-singularity.md) whose extension is nonzero at $a$. After shrinking the neighbourhood so $h\ne0$, its reciprocal $1/f$ extends with a zero of order $n$. In particular, $|f(z)|\to\infty$ at a [pole](../../../../../pole.md).
