# Singularity forced by an odd-dimensional transpose-inverse identity

↑ **Parent:** [Singular matrix](singular-matrix.md)

Let $B$ be an [invertible matrix](invertible-matrix.md) and $C$ a real square [matrix](matrix.md) of the same odd order. If the displayed identity holds, multiply it on the left by $B$ to obtain $BB^{\mathsf T}C^{\mathsf T}=-C$. Taking [determinants](determinant.md) gives

$$
(\det B)^2\det C=(-1)^d\det C=-\det C.
$$

Thus $((\det B)^2+1)\det C=0$. Since $\det B$ is real, the first factor is positive, forcing $C$ to be a [singular matrix](singular-matrix.md). Both odd dimension and reality matter: an invertible real [antisymmetric matrix](skew-symmetric-matrix.md) exists in even dimension, while over the complex numbers a squared determinant can equal $-1$.
