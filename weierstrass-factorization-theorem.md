# Weierstrass factorization theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Entire function](entire-function.md)

A nonzero [entire function](entire-function.md) can be described by its discrete zero locations and multiplicities, a product of [Weierstrass elementary factors](weierstrass-elementary-factor.md) and a zero-free exponential factor. Conversely, consistent prescribed multiplicities at distinct points escaping every compact subset can be realized by choosing factor orders large enough for [infinite product convergence from logarithmic tails](infinite-product-convergence-from-logarithmic-tails.md). Repeated locations with incompatible exact multiplicities are not admissible data, and a finite accumulation of distinct zeros is excluded by the [identity theorem](identity-theorem.md).
