# Three-time-scale cubic relaxation oscillator

↑ **Parent:** [Relaxation oscillation](relaxation-oscillation.md)

For $f(y)=y-y^2+\gamma y^3/3$ with $0<\gamma<1$, the [fold points of a critical manifold](fold-point-of-a-critical-manifold.md) are $y_\pm=(1\pm\sqrt{1-\gamma})/\gamma$. When $\delta\ll\epsilon\ll1$, the fastest variable satisfies $z\simeq f(y)$ even during jumps of the intermediate variable. At fixed $x$, the outer branches of $x=f(y)$ attract the fast $y$ dynamics because $f'(y)>0$; its middle branch repels. If $y_-<\alpha<y_+$, the slow equation $\dot x=\alpha-y$ drives the low branch to its [fold point of a critical manifold](fold-point-of-a-critical-manifold.md) and the high branch back to the other [fold point of a critical manifold](fold-point-of-a-critical-manifold.md). Fast jumps at almost fixed $x$ close a relaxation cycle. This explains why a reduction justified on the slow arcs must also be checked on the jump time scale. For $\gamma>1$, the cubic is strictly increasing; the reduced [energy](energy-split.md) $V=[x-f(\alpha)]^2/2+\epsilon(y-\alpha)^2/2$ has $\dot V=-(y-\alpha)[f(y)-f(\alpha)]\leq0$, precluding this oscillation mechanism.
