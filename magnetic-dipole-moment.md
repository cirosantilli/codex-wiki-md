# Magnetic dipole moment

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic vector potential](magnetic-vector-potential.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_dipole_moment)

A [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) is a [vector](vector.md) that summarizes the strength and orientation of a localized magnetic source. For a planar [current loop](current-loop.md), it is the [electric current](electric-current.md) times the [vector area](vector-area.md): $\mathbf m=IA\widehat{\mathbf n}$. The [right-hand rule](right-hand-rule.md) fixes the unit [normal vector](normal-vector.md) $\widehat{\mathbf n}$: curl the fingers of the right hand along the [conventional current](conventional-current.md), and the thumb points along $\mathbf m$. This single [vector](vector.md) predicts both the distant [magnetic dipole field](magnetic-dipole-field.md) and the turning effect of an imposed [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md).[§11.5, equations 11.20–11.22: loop moment, winding count and torque](university-physics-volume-2-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others/11-5-force-and-torque-on-a-current-loop.md)

The [SI](international-system-of-units.md) unit is [ampere](ampere.md) times square metre, $\mathrm{A\,m^2}$, equivalently [joule](joule.md) per [tesla](tesla-unit.md). A [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) is neither a [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) nor a [magnetic monopole](magnetic-monopole.md): a closed [current loop](current-loop.md) can have a nonzero [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) while its net outward [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md) through any closed surface remains zero, as required by [Gauss's law for magnetism](gauss-s-law-for-magnetism.md).

For a localized, steady [electric current density](current-density.md) $\mathbf J(\mathbf r)$, the more general definition is

$$
\mathbf m=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R^3}\mathbf r\times\mathbf J(\mathbf r)\,d^3r.
$$

Here the [cross product](cross-product.md) is integrated over the complete source, including return paths. Steadiness requires $\nabla\cdot\mathbf J=0$, and localization requires sufficiently rapid decay for the boundary terms to vanish. Under these assumptions the [localized steady-current moment identities](localized-steady-current-moment-identities.md) give $\int\mathbf J\,d^3r=0$: shifting the origin by $\mathbf a$ changes the proposed [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) by $-\tfrac12\mathbf a\times\int\mathbf J\,d^3r=0$. An isolated open wire segment does not satisfy these assumptions. [Localized Current Distribution](classical-electromagnetism-by-richard-fitzpatrick/localized-current-distribution.md).
