# Shrinking arc integral at a simple pole

↑ **Parent:** [Residue](residue.md)

At a [simple pole](simple-pole.md), write $f(z)=c/(z-a)+h(z)$ with $h$ holomorphic near $a$ and $c$ its [residue](residue.md). On the arc $z=a+\epsilon e^{it}$, the singular term integrates exactly to $ic(\beta-\alpha)$. The remainder has absolute integral at most $\epsilon(\beta-\alpha)\sup|h|$, which tends to zero. Reversing orientation reverses the sign. In particular a clockwise upper semicircle contributes $-i\pi c$ in the shrinking limit.

A higher-order [pole](pole.md) need not have such a limit: $(z-a)^{-2}$ has residue zero but integral $2/\epsilon$ on a positively oriented upper semicircle. Thus the simple-pole assumption controls all the potentially divergent terms of the [principal part of a Laurent series](principal-part-of-a-laurent-series.md).
