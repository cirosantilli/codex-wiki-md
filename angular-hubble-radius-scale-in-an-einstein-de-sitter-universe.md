# Angular Hubble-radius scale in an Einstein-de Sitter universe

↑ **Parent:** [Angular diameter distance](angular-diameter-distance.md)

In an [Einstein-de Sitter universe](einstein-de-sitter-universe.md), $H(z)=H_0(1+z)^{3/2}$ and the radial [comoving distance](comoving-radial-distance.md) is $2H_0^{-1}[1-(1+z)^{-1/2}]$. A physical [Hubble radius](hubble-radius.md) $H(z)^{-1}$ has comoving size $(1+z)/H(z)$. Dividing by the radial distance gives the displayed small angle, in radians. A Hubble diameter has twice this angle; the matter-era particle-horizon radius also equals twice the [Hubble radius](hubble-radius.md). These distinctions matter when comparing approximate [CMB](cosmic-microwave-background.md) angular scales.
