# DC Josephson effect

↑ **Parent:** [Josephson effect](josephson-effect.md)

At zero [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md), $\dot\phi=0$ permits a nonzero constant [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md) and hence a steady pair [electric current](electric-current.md). Under an imposed [electric current](electric-current.md) $I_b$ with $|I_b|<I_c$, a static solution satisfies $\sin\phi=I_b/I_c$. The low-energy branch has $\cos\phi>0$. For example, a stipulated $I_b=I_c/2$ gives $\phi=\pi/6$ modulo $2\pi$; it does not require a voltage drop. In the ideal current-biased [energy](energy-split.md) $-E_J\cos\phi-(\hbar I_b/2e)\phi$, the positive second [derivative](derivative.md) on that branch explains its local stability.

The [superconducting critical current](superconducting-critical-current.md) $I_c$ is a maximum available static pair [electric current](electric-current.md), not the inevitable [electric current](electric-current.md) under a voltage bias. At $V=0$ the initial state and circuit select a phase and a current. Above $I_c$ this sinusoidal model has no static phase solution, but its two relations alone do not predict the complete resistive [current-voltage characteristic](current-voltage-characteristic.md), switching dynamics or hysteresis. Those require the circuit and dissipative channels.
