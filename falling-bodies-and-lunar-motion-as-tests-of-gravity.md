# Falling bodies and lunar motion as tests of gravity

↑ **Parent:** [Inverse-square potential](inverse-square-potential.md)

One motivating laboratory demonstration compares two balls initially at the same height. A switch releases one vertically when the other leaves a horizontal track; arranging their paths to meet gives a collision as the readout of equal downward [displacement](displacement.md) over equal [time](time-in-physics.md). Equal initial height and synchronized release control the comparison. This tests the local independence of horizontal motion and [free fall](free-fall.md), under nearly constant [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md); it does not measure an [inverse-square law](inverse-square-law.md).[§7–4, figure 7–3: synchronized release and midair collision](the-feynman-lectures-on-physics-volume-i-by-richard-feynman-and-others/chapter-7-the-theory-of-gravitation.md)

A complementary astronomical comparison uses the [Moon](moon.md)'s distance and [orbital period](orbital-period.md) to infer inward [centripetal acceleration](centripetal-acceleration.md), $a=4\pi^2r/T^2$, and compares it with surface [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) scaled by $(R/r)^2$. The approximately sixtyfold distance ratio gives the approximately $1/3600$ prediction. The inference links terrestrial [free fall](free-fall.md) to a lunar [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md). Distance calibration matters; the [circular orbit](circular-orbit.md) approximation and the [Sun](sun.md)'s [tidal force](tidal-force.md) limit this elementary comparison.[§7–4: lunar distance, period, and the inverse-square comparison](the-feynman-lectures-on-physics-volume-i-by-richard-feynman-and-others/chapter-7-the-theory-of-gravitation.md)
