# X-ray tube cutoff

↑ **Parent:** [X-ray tube](x-ray-tube.md)

For an [electron](electron.md) starting with negligible [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and accelerated through a constant positive [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $V$, electrical work supplies $K=eV$. In the ideal single-[electron](electron.md) [bremsstrahlung](bremsstrahlung.md) model, [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) bounds any one emitted [photon](photon.md):

$$
E_\gamma=h\nu=\frac{hc}{\lambda}\le eV,\qquad
\nu_{\max}=\frac{eV}{h},\qquad
\lambda_{\min}=\frac{hc}{eV}.
$$

The endpoint corresponds to essentially all available [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) going into one [photon](photon.md). It is a limiting bound, not a claim that every [electron](electron.md) produces an endpoint [photon](photon.md). Neglect target recoil, initial [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md), and losses before the emitting interaction. If the accelerating [voltage](voltage.md) varies, its largest value sets the corresponding ideal upper endpoint; a mean [voltage](voltage.md) does not substitute for that value.

For a calculated constant-$V$ example, $50\,\mathrm{kV}$ gives $E_{\max}=50\,\mathrm{keV}$ and $\lambda_{\min}=0.0248\,\mathrm{nm}$. Doubling $V$ halves $\lambda_{\min}$. Producing a $0.0200\,\mathrm{nm}$ [photon](photon.md) requires at least $V=hc/(e\lambda)\simeq62.0\,\mathrm{kV}$ in this model. A continuous [frequency spectrum](frequency-spectrum.md) spans lower [photon](photon.md) [energies](energy-split.md); the cutoff calculation predicts neither its shape nor the efficiency of [photon emission](photon-emission.md).
