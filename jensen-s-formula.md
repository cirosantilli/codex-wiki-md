<h1 id="jensen-s-formula">Jensen's formula</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Holomorphic function](holomorphic-function-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen's_formula)

Let $R>0$ and let $f$ be a [holomorphic function](holomorphic-function-split.md) on an open neighbourhood of the closed [disk](disk-mathematics.md) $\{z:|z|\le R\}$. Assume $f(0)\ne0$ and that $f$ has no [zeros of a function](zero-of-a-function.md) on the boundary [circle](circle.md) $|z|=R$. List its interior [zeros of a function](zero-of-a-function.md) as $a_1,\ldots,a_N$, repeating each according to its [multiplicity of a zero](multiplicity-of-a-zero.md). With the natural [logarithm](logarithm.md), [Jensen's formula](jensen-s-formula.md) states

$$
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|f(Re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta
=\log|f(0)|+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\log\frac{R}{|a_j|}.
$$

The empty sum is zero. This boundary-zero-free version is the holomorphic special case of the general identity in the source.[§1, Theorem 2 and equation (6): neighbourhood of the closed disk, zeros counted with multiplicity, holomorphic special case](246b-notes-1-zeroes-poles-and-factorisation-of-meromorphic-functions-by-terence-tao.md)

Every interior [zero of a function](zero-of-a-function.md) makes a positive contribution; a zero nearer the centre contributes more. Consequently boundary growth relative to $|f(0)|$ limits how many [zeros of a function](zero-of-a-function.md) can lie well inside the [disk](disk-mathematics.md). The [Jensen zero-count bound](jensen-zero-count-bound.md) makes this quantitative, while the [integrated zero count](integrated-zero-count.md) retains the individual radial weights. The boundary statistic here is the [circle log-mean of a holomorphic function](circle-log-mean-of-a-holomorphic-function.md); exponentiating gives a [circle geometric mean of a holomorphic function](circle-geometric-mean-of-a-holomorphic-function.md).
