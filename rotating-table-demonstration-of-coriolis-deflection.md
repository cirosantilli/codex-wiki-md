# Rotating-table demonstration of Coriolis deflection

↑ **Parent:** [Coriolis force](coriolis-force.md)

The Technische Universität Darmstadt demonstration uses a steel ball launched outward from the centre of a rotating disc. A fixed lecture-room camera records a straight path, while a camera carried above the disc records a curved path. The instructions specify launching toward the fixed camera so that the laboratory motion is clearly visible. The rotating iPod transmits its view wirelessly through Apple TV; two vertical support rods of length $1\ \mathrm m$ are specified for the slow-motion arrangement. The paired video views are the readout: one physical motion is represented in two [reference frames](reference-frame.md).[Coriolis-Kraft, demonstration M C 13 2: Info; Beschreibung, Hinweis zur Durchführung and Hinweis zum Aufbau](coriolis-kraft-at-technische-universitat-darmstadt.md)

A useful preparation control appears in the Universität Freiburg instructions: with the disc stationary, check that the ball travels radially toward its edge. A camera is mounted on supports attached to the disc; turn the disc by hand as the ball leaves its centre and observe the camera view.[Kugel auf rotierender Scheibe: Durchführung, Material, Aufbau and Durchführung](kugel-auf-rotierender-scheibe-at-universitat-freiburg.md)

The inference is a dependence of the observed [trajectory](trajectory.md) on the rotating axes; a curved camera trace alone does not isolate the [Coriolis force](coriolis-force.md). To compare with the constant-[angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) [free particle in rotating coordinates](free-particle-in-rotating-coordinates.md), measure the disc [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md), calibrate lengths in the camera image, and compare launches at rest and with opposite rotation directions. These are checks of the ideal model, not reported measurements from the manuals. A rolling ball can exchange [forces](force-split.md) with the surface through [friction](friction.md), and hand rotation need not have constant [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md); an [Euler acceleration](euler-acceleration.md) then enters too. The ideal free-ball drawing deliberately omits those effects. Neither manual supplies a numerical uncertainty or a precision measurement of the [Coriolis acceleration](coriolis-acceleration.md).
