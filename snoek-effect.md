# Snoek effect

↑ **Parent:** [Anelasticity](anelasticity.md)

The Snoek effect is [anelasticity](anelasticity.md) caused by stress-induced redistribution of mobile [interstitial defects](interstitial-defect.md) among differently oriented sites in a crystal. An applied [stress](stress.md) favors orientations whose local distortion best accommodates it. At low frequency the atoms can track that bias and provide additional relaxed compliance; at high frequency their populations are nearly frozen. Dissipation is largest between these regimes. For a weak single relaxation, the [loss tangent](loss-tangent.md) has the approximate shape $\Delta\omega\tau/[1+(\omega\tau)^2]$, with $\tau=\tau_0e^{E_a/(k_BT)}$ for thermally activated hopping. The peak is near $\omega\tau=1$. This describes a standard mechanism for interstitial impurities in body-centred cubic metals, not every damping mechanism in every alloy.
