# Distance from a point to a line in three dimensions

↑ **Parent:** [Distance from a point to a line](distance-from-a-point-to-a-line.md)

For distinct points $\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_2$, the [cross product](cross-product.md) in the numerator has magnitude twice the area of the [triangle](triangle.md) with third vertex $\mathbf r_0$. The same double area is the base length $|\mathbf r_2-\mathbf r_1|$ times the perpendicular height from $\mathbf r_0$, proving the displayed [Euclidean distance](euclidean-distance.md). Equivalently, let $\mathbf u=\mathbf r_2-\mathbf r_1$ and project $\mathbf r_0-\mathbf r_1$ onto the line direction. The squared distance is $|\mathbf r_0-\mathbf r_1|^2-[\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf r_0-\mathbf r_1)]^2/|\mathbf u|^2$; [Lagrange's identity](lagrange-identity-for-the-cross-product.md) gives the same cross-product formula.
