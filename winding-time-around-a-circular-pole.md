# Winding time around a circular pole

↑ **Parent:** [Involute](involute.md)

A point [mass](mass.md) attached to a light inextensible string winds around a fixed smooth circular pole of radius $R$. Let the initial straight free [length](length.md) be $L$, and assume the string remains taut without slipping over the part already laid on the pole. Since its [tension](tension.md) is perpendicular to the point [mass](mass.md)'s [velocity](velocity.md), the [speed](speed.md) $v$ stays constant. If the contact point turns through $d\theta$, the free [length](length.md) $s$ obeys $ds=-R\,d\theta$, while the [involute](involute.md) geometry gives $v=s\dot\theta$. Thus $s\dot s=-Rv$, or $s^2=L^2-2Rvt$. First contact of the point [mass](mass.md) with the pole occurs at $s=0$, after $L^2/(2Rv)$. In the ideal model, the required [tension](tension.md) $mv^2/s$ diverges at contact; finite string strength or finite [particle](particle-physics.md) size limits that idealization.
