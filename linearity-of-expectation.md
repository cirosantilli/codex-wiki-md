# Linearity of expectation

↑ **Parent:** [Expected value](expected-value.md)

For [integrable random variables](integrable-random-variable.md) $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ on the same [probability space](probability-space.md) and fixed [real numbers](real-number.md) $a_1,\ldots,a_n$, [linearity of expectation](linearity-of-expectation.md) states

$$
\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^n a_iX_i\right]=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\mathbb E[X_i].
$$

The [random variables](random-variable-split.md) can be [dependent random variables](statistical-dependence.md). The formula follows by the [linearity of integration](linearity-of-integration.md); a finite [linear combination](linear-combination.md) of [integrable random variables](integrable-random-variable.md) remains [integrable](integrability.md). For example, if $X$ takes $-1,2,8$ with [probabilities](probability.md) $1/2,3/10,1/5$, then $X$ and $2X$ are [dependent random variables](statistical-dependence.md), yet $\mathbb E[X+2X]=3\mathbb E[X]=5.1$.

An especially useful application counts how many [events](event.md) occur. Put $I_i=\mathbf1_{A_i}$, an [indicator random variable](indicator-random-variable.md), and $N=\sum_iI_i$. Since $\mathbb E[I_i]=\mathbb P(A_i)$, we get $\mathbb E[N]=\sum_i\mathbb P(A_i)$. There is no need to compute the whole [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md) of $N$, or to assume [independence](independent-random-variables.md). For a [uniform random permutation](uniform-random-permutation.md) of $n\geq1$ objects, each object stays fixed with [probability](probability.md) $1/n$, so the [fixed point count of a uniform random permutation](fixed-point-count-of-a-uniform-random-permutation.md) has [expected value](expected-value.md) one for every $n$. [Lehman, Leighton and Meyer, Mathematics for Computer Science, §18.5: linearity and sums of indicators](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-042j-mathematics-for-computer-science-spring-2015/mit6_042js15_textbook.pdf).

[Linearity of expectation](linearity-of-expectation.md) concerns [addition](addition.md) and multiplication by fixed [scalars](scalar.md). Products instead satisfy $\mathbb E[XY]=\mathbb E[X]\mathbb E[Y]$ under the hypotheses of [expectation of a product of independent random variables](expectation-of-a-product-of-independent-random-variables.md). For the example above, $\mathbb E[X^2]=14.5$, whereas $(\mathbb E[X])^2=2.89$. Applying a nonlinear [function](function-split.md) before averaging can therefore change the answer substantially; use the [law of the unconscious statistician](law-of-the-unconscious-statistician.md) to compute that average.
