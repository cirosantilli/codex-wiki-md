# Improper line integral

↑ **Parent:** [Line integral](line-integral.md)

A [line integral](line-integral.md) can be defined as an [improper integral](improper-integral.md) of its parametrized integrand when that integrand is undefined or unbounded at isolated parameter values. At an interior singular parameter, split the integral and require both one-sided limits to exist separately; their cancellation in a symmetric [Cauchy principal value](cauchy-principal-value.md) is a different convention. For a scalar [arc length](arc-length.md) integral, use $f(\mathbf r(t))|\mathbf r'(t)|$ in place of the vector-field [dot product](dot-product.md).

If the restricted integrand has a removable singularity, integrating its continuous extension gives the same value. This does not require the original [vector field](vector-field.md) to extend continuously to a full neighbourhood of the curve. Likewise, a differential identity may hold only on the regular segments: a zero integrand there has improper integral zero, although its printed classical expression may not be defined at the omitted points.
