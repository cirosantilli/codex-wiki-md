# Dunford-Pettis theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform integrability](uniform-integrability.md)

On a finite-measure space, a bounded subset of $L^1$ is relatively weakly compact exactly when it is [uniformly integrable](uniform-integrability.md). In particular every [uniformly integrable](uniform-integrability.md) bounded sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence. This is the [compactness](compact-space.md) substitute for reflexivity at exponent one. A superlinear integral bound $\sup_j\int H(u_j)<\infty$, with $H\geq0$ and $H(t)/|t|\to\infty$, gives the required [uniform integrability](uniform-integrability.md): for $H(t)\geq A|t|$ outside $[-R_A,R_A]$,

$$
\int_E|u_j|\leq R_A|E|+A^{-1}\sup_j\int H(u_j).
$$

Choose $A$ large and then the measure of $E$ small. This is the direction used in the [direct method in the calculus of variations](direct-method-in-the-calculus-of-variations.md); a mere bounded $L^1$ [norm](norm.md) does not suffice, as concentrating spikes show.
