# Field

↑ **Parent:** [Algebra](algebra-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_(mathematics))

A field is a [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md) with $0\ne1$ in which every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse. Examples include the [rational numbers](rational-number.md), [real numbers](real-number.md), and [complex numbers](complex-number.md). The [integers](integer-split.md) are a [ring](ring.md) but not a field: dividing $1$ by $2$ leaves them. For a [prime number](prime-number-split.md) $p$, the [prime field](prime-field.md) $\mathbb F_p=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$ is a finite example, because every nonzero residue is [coprime](coprime-integers.md) to $p$ and hence has a [modular inverse](modular-multiplicative-inverse.md).

In $\mathbb F_5$, the nonzero inverse pairs are $1^{-1}=1$, $2^{-1}=3$, $3^{-1}=2$, and $4^{-1}=4$. Thus $2x=3$ has solution $x=3\cdot3=4$ in this field; the [real number](real-number.md) solution would be $3/2$. Dividing by a nonzero coefficient is valid in both settings, but it means division according to the chosen field's operations.

A field has no [zero divisors](zero-divisor.md): if $ab=0$ and $a\ne0$, multiplying by $a^{-1}$ gives $b=0$. This short argument supports [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) factorization arguments and pivot division in [Gaussian elimination](gaussian-elimination.md). It does not hold in every [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md): modulo $6$, the nonzero residues $2$ and $3$ multiply to zero. The distinction is visible in the figure under [algebraic structure](algebraic-structure.md).
