# Non-Archimedean part of Ostrowski theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Ostrowski's theorem](ostrowski-s-theorem.md)

For a nontrivial [Non-Archimedean absolute value](non-archimedean-absolute-value.md) on $\mathbb Q$, every integer has absolute value at most one. Some prime $p$ must have value less than one, or the value would be trivial on every rational number. Two distinct such primes are impossible by a [Bezout identity](bezout-identity.md). Prime factorization then gives the displayed formula. Distinct primes give inequivalent topologies, since powers of $p$ tend to zero only for the $p$-adic absolute value among these values.
