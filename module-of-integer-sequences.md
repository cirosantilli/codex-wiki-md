# Module of integer sequences

↑ **Parent:** [Module theory](module-theory-split.md)

The module of integer sequences consists of all [functions](function-split.md) $\mathbb N_0\to\mathbb Z$, with pointwise addition and integer scalar multiplication. Its [submodule](submodule.md) of finitely supported sequences is the [direct sum of modules](direct-sum-of-modules.md) $\bigoplus_{j\geq0}\mathbb Z$, a [free module](free-module.md) with coordinate [basis of a module](basis-of-a-module.md) $(e_j)$. The full product is not a [free module](free-module.md).

To prove this, suppose it had a [basis of a module](basis-of-a-module.md) $E$. The [countable basis support lemma](countable-basis-support-lemma.md) places every finitely supported sequence in a [submodule](submodule.md) $C$ generated by a [countable](countable-set.md) subset of $E$. The [basis partition gives a module direct sum](basis-partition-gives-a-module-direct-sum.md) lemma supplies a projection $\pi$ onto its complementary [direct summand](direct-summand.md), with [kernel](kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) $C$. By [countability of countably generated abelian groups](countability-of-countably-generated-abelian-groups.md), $C$ is [countable](countable-set.md). The [Cantor diagonal argument](cantor-s-diagonal-argument.md) makes the family of all sequences $w(j)=\pm2^j$ [uncountable](uncountable-set.md), so choose one with $\pi(w)\ne0$. Truncating $w$ before coordinate $i$ writes $w=f_i+2^ig_i$, where $f_i$ is finitely supported and $g_i$ is an integer sequence. Thus $\pi(w)=2^i\pi(g_i)$ for every $i$. The [vanishing of infinitely divisible integer sequences](vanishing-of-infinitely-divisible-integer-sequences.md) forces $\pi(w)=0$, a contradiction.
