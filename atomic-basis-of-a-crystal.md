# Atomic basis of a crystal

↑ **Parent:** [Crystal structure](crystal-structure.md)

An [atomic basis of a crystal](atomic-basis-of-a-crystal.md) is the motif of [chemical species](chemical-species.md) $s_j$ and relative positions $r_j$ attached identically to every [lattice point](lattice-point.md) $R$ of a [Bravais lattice](bravais-lattice.md) $\Lambda$. The ideal periodic structure is the labeled set

$$
\{(s_j,R+r_j):R\in\Lambda,\quad j=1,\ldots,N\}.
$$

The [primitive lattice translation vectors](primitive-lattice-translation-vector.md) generate $R$ by [integer](integer-split.md) combinations; the [atomic basis](atomic-basis-of-a-crystal.md) lists what is repeated at $R$. These are different meanings of the word [basis](basis.md). In particular, the offsets $r_j$ need not be [linearly independent vectors](linear-independence.md), and one of them may be zero. A [primitive unit cell](primitive-unit-cell.md) represents $N$ motif sites, even though it represents only one [lattice point](lattice-point.md).

Changing the origin, choosing another [primitive unit cell](primitive-unit-cell.md), or moving a motif site by a [Bravais lattice](bravais-lattice.md) translation changes the coordinates used to describe the same infinite [crystal structure](crystal-structure.md). The grouping into a motif does not, by itself, identify bonded [molecules](molecule-split.md). In solid [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md), grouping one [sodium](sodium.md) [cation](cation.md) and one [chlorine](chlorine.md) [anion](anion.md) into an [atomic basis](atomic-basis-of-a-crystal.md) does not create a collection of discrete NaCl [molecules](molecule-split.md); the [ionic crystal](ionic-crystal.md) has an extended network of equivalent neighboring [ions](ion.md).
