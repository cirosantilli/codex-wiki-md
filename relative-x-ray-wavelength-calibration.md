# Relative X-ray wavelength calibration

↑ **Parent:** [Crystal X-ray spectrometer](crystal-x-ray-spectrometer.md)

For two lines measured with the same [interplanar spacing](interplanar-spacing.md) at the same [temperature](temperature-split.md), division of [Bragg's law](bragg-s-law.md) gives

$$
\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}
=\frac{n_2\sin\theta_1}{n_1\sin\theta_2}.
$$

The common $d$ cancels. Consequently a precise [wavelength](wavelength.md) ratio can coexist with an uncertain conversion to [SI](international-system-of-units.md) [length](length.md). In contrast, angular calibration errors generally survive: a shared small zero offset $\epsilon$ changes the inferred ratio fractionally by approximately $(\cot\theta_1-\cot\theta_2)\epsilon$. Nearby lines partially cancel this effect, but widely separated angles need not. Different [crystals](crystal.md), [temperatures](temperature-split.md) or [refraction](refraction.md) corrections also prevent unqualified cancellation.
