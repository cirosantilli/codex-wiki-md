# Partial-sum norm on absolutely summable sequences

↑ **Parent:** [Sequence space](sequence-space.md)

On $\ell^1$, $q$ is a [norm](norm.md), bounded above by $\|x\|_1$. The partial-sum map $Jx=(\sum_{n\leq k}x_n)_k$ is an isometric linear embedding into the space $c$ of convergent sequences with the [supremum norm](supremum-norm.md). Its range contains every eventually constant sequence: take successive differences of such a sequence to obtain a finitely supported element of $\ell^1$. These sequences are dense in $c$, so the [completion of a normed space](completion-of-a-normed-space.md) is $c$.

The range is proper: partial sums of the alternating harmonic series converge but their successive differences do not belong to $\ell^1$. The finite truncations of these differences are $q$-Cauchy and cannot have a limit in $\ell^1$. Thus this normed version of $\ell^1$ is incomplete, despite the completeness of its usual norm.
