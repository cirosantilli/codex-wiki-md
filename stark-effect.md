# Stark effect

↑ **Parent:** [Mathematical physics](mathematical-physics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stark_effect)

An [electric field](electric-field-split.md) shifts and can split the [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue-split.md) of an [atom](atom-physics.md). This is the Stark effect; its [hydrogen atom](hydrogen-atom.md) ground-state case is quadratic in a weak field.

The observable in [Stark effect](stark-effect.md) [spectroscopy](spectroscopy.md) is a change in a [spectral line](spectral-line.md), rather than a direct reading of an individual [energy eigenvalue](energy-eigenvalue-split.md). If an upper [energy eigenstate](energy-eigenstate.md) $u$ emits a [photon](photon.md) into a lower [energy eigenstate](energy-eigenstate.md) $l$, the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md) $h$ relates its [frequency](frequency.md) to the difference:

$$
h\nu(\mathcal E)=E_u(\mathcal E)-E_l(\mathcal E),
\qquad
h\,\Delta\nu=\Delta E_u-\Delta E_l.
$$

Here $\mathcal E$ denotes the applied [electric field](electric-field-split.md) component along a chosen axis. Both levels can move, and [electric-dipole selection rules for hydrogen](electric-dipole-selection-rules-for-hydrogen.md) decide which pairs produce observable [spectral lines](spectral-line.md). This makes [Stark effect](stark-effect.md) [spectroscopy](spectroscopy.md) a probe of [energy eigenstates](energy-eigenstate.md) and their [electric dipole moments](electric-dipole-moment.md): the slope $-\partial E/\partial\mathcal E$ is the corresponding dipole component by the [Hellmann–Feynman theorem](hellmann-feynman-theorem.md). The comparison with [Zeeman splitting](zeeman-splitting.md) is useful: there an applied [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) supplies the perturbation.
