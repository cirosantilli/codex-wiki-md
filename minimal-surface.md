# Minimal surface

↑ **Parent:** [Mean curvature](mean-curvature.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_surface)

A [minimal surface](minimal-surface.md) has vanishing [mean curvature vector](mean-curvature-vector.md). For a [smooth surface](smooth-surface.md) in three-dimensional [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md), this means $H=0$, equivalently $k_1+k_2=0$. Thus [Gaussian curvature](gaussian-curvature.md) is $K=-k_1^2\leq0$, but neither [principal curvature](principal-curvature.md) needs to vanish. A [plane](plane.md) and a [catenoid](catenoid.md) both satisfy the condition, with very different [Gaussian curvatures](gaussian-curvature.md).

By the [first variation of area formula](first-variation-of-area-formula.md), a [minimal surface](minimal-surface.md) is a [stationary point](stationary-point.md) of [surface area](surface-area.md) for every smooth variation that fixes its boundary, or has [compact support](compact-support.md) in its interior. The word “minimal” specifies this differential equation; it does not guarantee a [global minimum](global-minimum.md) of [surface area](surface-area.md), or even a [local minimum](local-minimum.md). A [stable minimal surface](stable-minimal-surface.md) imposes a further condition on the [second variation](second-variation.md).
