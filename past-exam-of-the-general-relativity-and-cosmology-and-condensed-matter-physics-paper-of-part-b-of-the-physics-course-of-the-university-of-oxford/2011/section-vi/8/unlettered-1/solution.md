<h1 id="section-vi/8/unlettered-1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Unlettered 1](../unlettered-1.md)

For positive $\lambda$, a fluctuation of [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) produces a [Weiss molecular field](../../../../../weiss-molecular-field.md) in the same direction, reinforcing alignment. The [mean-field self-consistency equation](../../../../../self-consistency-equation.md) is

$$
M=M_sB_J\!\left[\frac{g\mu_BJ\mu_0}{k_BT}(H_{\rm applied}+\lambda M)\right].
$$

Linearizing in the paramagnetic phase gives

$$
M=\frac CT(H_{\rm applied}+\lambda M),\qquad
\boxed{\chi=\frac C{T-\lambda C}=\frac C{T-T_C},\qquad T_C=\lambda C=\frac{\mu_0\lambda n(g\mu_B)^2J(J+1)}{3k_B}.}
$$

At zero applied field, the right-hand side's slope at $M=0$ is $T_C/T$. Below $T_C$ this exceeds one, and the saturation of the [Brillouin function](../../../../../brillouin-function.md) allows stable nonzero solutions of opposite sign: spontaneous [ferromagnetism](../../../../../ferromagnetism.md). Above $T_C$, only the zero-field solution $M=0$ remains stable. The stated linear susceptibility is the paramagnetic branch for $T>T_C$; it must not be continued as a negative susceptibility below the transition.
