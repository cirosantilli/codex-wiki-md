<h1 id="section-vi/8/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The $1/x$ terms in the [Brillouin function](../../../../../brillouin-function.md) cancel. Keeping the linear terms gives

$$
B_J(x)=\frac{(2J+1)^2-1}{12J^2}x+O(x^3)=\frac{J+1}{3J}x+O(x^3).
$$

Consequently,

$$
M\simeq\frac{n(g\mu_B)^2J(J+1)}{3k_BT}B.
$$

Use the dimensionless SI [magnetic susceptibility](../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md) $\chi=\partial M/\partial H_{\rm applied}$ and $B_{\rm applied}=\mu_0H_{\rm applied}$, as in the source's molecular-field convention. The [Curie law](../../../../../curie-s-law.md) is

$$
\boxed{\chi=\frac CT,\qquad C=\frac{\mu_0n(g\mu_B)^2J(J+1)}{3k_B}.}
$$

If susceptibility is instead defined as $\partial M/\partial B$, its coefficient is $C/\mu_0$ and is dimensionful. The SI convention above makes the subsequent [Curie–Weiss law](../../../../../curie-weiss-law.md) and $\lambda$ consistent.
