# Removing a singular axis in a flux integral

↑ **Parent:** [Divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md)

A [vector field](vector-field.md) singular on an axis need not satisfy the classical [divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md) on the original closed region. Remove a thin tube $\rho<\varepsilon$, apply the theorem to the remaining smooth region, then examine the added cylindrical boundary. If its [flux](flux.md) tends to zero and the remaining [volume integral](volume-integral.md) converges, the improper boundary [flux](flux.md) equals the improper [integral](integral-split.md) of the [divergence](divergence.md). For $\mathbf F=(\log\rho^2,\log\rho^2,z/\rho)$ with $\rho=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, the cylindrical [unit normal](unit-normal.md) has zero $z$ component, so the added [flux](flux.md) is $O(\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|)$ on a bounded axial interval. The divergence $(2\cos\phi+2\sin\phi+1)/\rho$ is locally integrable under the [cylindrical coordinates](cylindrical-coordinate-system.md) measure $\rho\,d\rho\,d\phi\,dz$. Hence no finite line-source correction survives, despite the field itself being unbounded.
