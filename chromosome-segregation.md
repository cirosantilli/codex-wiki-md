# Chromosome segregation

↑ **Parent:** [Chromosome](chromosome.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome_segregation)

[Chromosome segregation](chromosome-segregation.md) allocates [chromosomes](chromosome.md) to the products of division. [Mitosis](mitosis.md) separates [sister chromatids](sister-chromatids.md), preserving [ploidy](ploidy.md) in completed daughter [cell nuclei](cell-nucleus.md). [Meiosis I](meiosis-i.md) separates [homologous chromosomes](homologous-chromosome.md) with their [sister chromatids](sister-chromatids.md) still associated, reducing [diploid](diploid.md) to [haploid](haploid.md) [ploidy](ploidy.md); [meiosis II](meiosis-ii.md) separates the [sister chromatids](sister-chromatids.md). Only the first of the two divisions reduces the number of complete sets.[Biology 2e by Mary Ann Clark and others; §11.1, introduction comparing mitosis and meiosis; Telophase I and Cytokinesis; Meiosis II](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/the-process-of-meiosis.md)

For example, correct [chromosome segregation](chromosome-segregation.md) of the stipulated three [homologous chromosome](homologous-chromosome.md) pairs gives each [meiosis I](meiosis-i.md) product one $A$, one $B$ and one $D$, each replicated. Giving one product both $A$ members and the other none would preserve the total [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) across products while failing to give each a complete set. Conservation of material alone does not prove correct [chromosome segregation](chromosome-segregation.md).
