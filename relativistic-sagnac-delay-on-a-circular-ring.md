# Relativistic Sagnac delay on a circular ring

↑ **Parent:** [Sagnac effect](sagnac-effect.md)

Consider an ideal circular vacuum path of laboratory radius $R$, a limit of closely spaced [mirrors](mirror.md) constraining [light](light.md) around the circle at laboratory [speed](speed.md) $c$. The source and detector rotate uniformly together with signed [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) $\Omega$, where $|\Omega|R<c$, in flat [spacetime](spacetime.md). Let $L=2\pi R$ be the laboratory circumference and $A=\pi R^2$ the positive [area](surface-area.md). The $+$ pulse travels counterclockwise and the $-$ pulse clockwise, independently of the sign of $\Omega$. Both depart from the detector at $t=0$.

Unwrap the circle into a laboratory arc coordinate. The two first-return conditions are

$$
ct_+=L+\Omega Rt_+,\qquad ct_-=L-\Omega Rt_-.
$$

Consequently the exact laboratory arrival times and their difference are

$$
t_+=\frac{L}{c-\Omega R},\qquad
t_-=\frac{L}{c+\Omega R},\qquad
\Delta t=\frac{4\Omega A}{c^2-\Omega^2R^2}.
$$

The denominators $c\mp\Omega R$ are closing rates in these laboratory equations; they are not local [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) measurements by a rotating observer. The two arrival [spacetime events](event-relativity.md) lie on the same detector [world line](world-line.md). Its constant [speed](speed.md) is $|\Omega|R$, so [proper time](proper-time.md) along that [world line](world-line.md) gives

$$
\Delta\tau=\sqrt{1-\frac{\Omega^2R^2}{c^2}}\,\Delta t
=\frac{4\Omega A}{c^2\sqrt{1-\Omega^2R^2/c^2}}.
$$

Thus laboratory coordinate-time and rotating-detector [proper time](proper-time.md) delays agree only to leading order at small $|\Omega|R/c$. The radius and circumference here are laboratory quantities, not lengths obtained by laying comoving rods around the rim. Finite mirror polygons, transparent media, and [optical dispersion](dispersion-optics.md) require their own path and propagation assumptions before using an exact formula.

An exact dimensionless check takes $\Omega R/c=1/5$. Relative to $L/c$, the laboratory arrival times are $5/4$ and $5/6$, giving $\Delta t/(L/c)=5/12$. The detector instead records $\Delta\tau/(L/c)=\sqrt6/6\simeq0.408248$. The leading [Sagnac area law](sagnac-area-law.md) would give $\Delta t/(L/c)\simeq2/5$. These are model values chosen to make the relativistic distinction visible, not attainable parameters of the historical apparatus.

The rotating loop does not constitute a universal measurement of the [one-way speed of light](one-way-speed-of-light.md). It compares completed circuits at a single readout; assigning a [velocity](velocity.md) to each remote leg additionally requires a clock synchronization convention. Local [Einstein synchronisation](einstein-synchronisation.md) around a rotating rim does not close into a single globally synchronized clock network: the accumulated mismatch is another expression of the rotating-path asymmetry. Local comoving vacuum measurements still give $c$ in both directions. [Special relativity](special-relativity-split.md) predicts a nonzero closed-loop delay because the receiver moves during propagation, while no single comoving [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md) covers the whole circuit.
