# Magnetic field

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field)

A [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) is a [vector field](vector-field.md) $\mathbf B(\mathbf r,t)$ whose local value determines the magnetic part of the [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md) on a body with [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $q$ and [velocity](velocity.md) $\mathbf v$. In [SI](international-system-of-units.md) units,

$$
\mathbf F_B=q\mathbf v\times\mathbf B,\qquad
|\mathbf F_B|=|q|vB\sin\alpha,
$$

where $v$ is the [speed](speed.md), $B=|\mathbf B|$, and $\alpha$ is the [angle](angle.md) between the [velocity](velocity.md) and the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md). The [cross product](cross-product.md) makes a nonzero magnetic [force](force-split.md) perpendicular to both the [velocity](velocity.md) and the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md). The [right-hand rule](right-hand-rule.md) gives its direction for positive [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md); negative [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) reverses it. [OpenStax, magnetic fields and forces](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/11-2-magnetic-fields-and-lines).

The [SI](international-system-of-units.md) unit of [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) is the [tesla](tesla-unit.md):

$$
1\,\mathrm T=\frac{1\,\mathrm N}{(1\,\mathrm C)(1\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})}.
$$

For a stipulated example in [SI](international-system-of-units.md) units, take [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $q=2\,\mathrm C$, [velocity](velocity.md) $\mathbf v=(3,0,0)\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, and [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) $\mathbf B=(0,0,4)\,\mathrm T$. The [cross product](cross-product.md) gives magnetic [force](force-split.md) $\mathbf F_B=(0,-24,0)\,\mathrm N$. With the same [speed](speed.md) directed along $+z$, the magnetic [force](force-split.md) is zero: motion parallel to a [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) produces no magnetic [acceleration](acceleration.md).

[Magnetic field lines](magnetic-field-line.md) visualize the direction of a [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) by being tangent to it at each point. They are not generally the path of a body with [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md): in a uniform [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) along $+z$, the [magnetic field lines](magnetic-field-line.md) are straight while transverse [velocity](velocity.md) produces [circular motion](circular-motion.md), and adding parallel [velocity](velocity.md) produces [helical motion in a uniform magnetic field](helical-motion-in-a-uniform-magnetic-field.md). The worked [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md) example below illustrates this distinction.

A [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) need not be static. [Magnetostatics](magnetostatics.md) is the restricted case with no dependence on [time](time-in-physics.md); an [electromagnetic wave](electromagnetic-wave-split.md) has changing [electric field](electric-field-split.md) and [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) even in vacuum. A changing [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) is coupled to the [electric field](electric-field-split.md) through [Faraday's law of induction](faraday-s-law-of-induction.md). [Feynman lectures, II.20: electromagnetic waves in free space](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_20.html). The useful separation is between local bending, described by the [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md), and the amount of [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) crossing a [surface](topological-surface-split.md), described by [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md).
