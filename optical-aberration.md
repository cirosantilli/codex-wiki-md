# Optical aberration

↑ **Parent:** [Geometrical optics](geometrical-optics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_aberration)

An [optical aberration](optical-aberration.md) is a departure from ideal [image formation](image-formation.md): [light rays](light-ray.md) from one object point may miss their intended common image point, or image positions may depart from the intended mapping. [Spherical aberration](spherical-aberration.md), [coma](coma-optics.md), and [astigmatism](astigmatism-optical-systems.md) describe different ways of spreading a point's [light rays](light-ray.md); [optical distortion](distortion-optics.md) can change image shape without spreading each point. In [physical optics](physical-optics.md), these departures can be described by [wavefront error](wavefront-error.md) relative to a reference [wavefront](wavefront.md). [Diffraction](diffraction.md) also gives a finite [point spread function](point-spread-function.md) in a perfect [optical instrument](optical-instrument.md), so an extended image alone does not establish an [optical aberration](optical-aberration.md).
