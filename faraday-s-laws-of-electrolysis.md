<h1 id="faraday-s-laws-of-electrolysis">Faraday's laws of electrolysis</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Electrolysis](electrolysis.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraday's_laws_of_electrolysis)

[Faraday's laws of electrolysis](faraday-s-laws-of-electrolysis.md) state that the [mass](mass.md) of material formed or consumed by a specified [electrode](electrode.md) reaction is proportional to the transported [electric charge](electric-charge.md), and that equal [electric charge](electric-charge.md) produces [masses](mass.md) proportional to $M/z$ for different reactions. Here $M$ is the product's [mass](mass.md) per [mole](mole-unit.md), and $z$ is the positive number of [Electrons](electron.md) transferred per product entity. For complete use of the [electric charge](electric-charge.md) in that reaction,

$$
n_{e^-}=\frac QF,\qquad n_{\mathrm{product}}=\frac{Q}{zF},\qquad m=\frac{MQ}{zF}.
$$

The first relation uses the [Faraday constant](faraday-constant.md); the second uses [half-reaction](half-reaction.md) [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md); the third converts [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) to [mass](mass.md). For equal transported [electric charge](electric-charge.md) in two ideal cells,

$$
\frac{m_1}{m_2}=\frac{M_1/z_1}{M_2/z_2}.
$$

The number $z$ belongs to the balanced [half-reaction](half-reaction.md), rather than to the metal's name alone: [copper](copper.md) deposited from $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$ needs $z=2$. These modern forms of both laws are given in KIT's practical.[Elektrolyse, Bestimmung von e by Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, Faculty of Physics teaching laboratory](elektrolyse-bestimmung-von-e-by-karlsruhe-institute-of-technology-faculty-of-physics-teaching-laboratory.md)

If other same-direction [electrode](electrode.md) reactions share the measured [electric current](electric-current.md), define an integrated current efficiency $\eta=Q_{\mathrm{product}}/Q$. Provided the product is neither subsequently consumed nor lost, the retained [mass](mass.md) is

$$
m=\eta\frac{MQ}{zF},\qquad
\eta=\frac{\int I_{\mathrm{product}}(t)\,dt}{\int I(t)\,dt}.
$$

Here $0\leq\eta\leq1$ follows from the specified same-direction partition of [electric current](electric-current.md). It is not a universal bound on every signed partial-current convention: IUPAC notes that simultaneous [oxidation](oxidation.md) and [reduction](reduction-chemistry.md) at one [electrode](electrode.md) can produce a current-efficiency ratio above one.[current efficiency](iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/current-efficiency.md) When efficiency varies during a run, use the charge-weighted ratio above, rather than an unweighted average over [time](time-in-physics.md). If deposition removes material from a multicomponent [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md), specify which [chemical species](chemical-species.md) is being counted and balance its [half-reaction](half-reaction.md) before choosing $z$.

Michael Faraday's accessible 1834 paper, Experimental Researches in Electricity, Seventh Series, §783 states proportionality between chemical action and the quantity of electricity passed. In §§789–791 he describes connecting a molten tin-chloride cell in series with a water-decomposition charge indicator, weighing the receiving [electrode](electrode.md) before and after, and comparing the deposited metal with the indicator's gas readout. The common series [electric current](electric-current.md) makes these two readouts comparable. This was a quantitative test of chemical equivalents, rather than merely an observation that a deposit appeared. It is historical evidence, not a suggested classroom procedure; the modern [Electron](electron.md)-counting derivation above uses concepts unavailable to Faraday.[§783; §§789–791; pp. 101–103](experimental-researches-in-electricity-seventh-series-by-michael-faraday.md)
