# Opposite propagation directions in the annular displacement equation

↑ **Parent:** [Forced annular displacement equation](forced-annular-displacement-equation.md)

With no forcing, the linear [dispersion relation](dispersion-relation.md) has positive [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) $\sigma k^2/(\beta\alpha_1)$ and [group velocity](group-velocity.md) three times this. A decaying permanent wave with $\xi=x+ct$ instead obeys $\sigma(A')^2=A^2(\beta\alpha_1c+\alpha_2A/3)$. For positive coefficients a nonzero homoclinic wave requires $c>0$ and is negative, travelling upstream. Its inverse width is $k_s=\frac12\sqrt{\beta\alpha_1c/\sigma}$. The opposite directions reflect the nonlinear amplitude-dependent propagation speed.
