# Partial ancillary statistic

↑ **Parent:** [Ancillary statistic](ancillary-statistic.md)

For a parameter of interest $\psi$ and [nuisance parameter](nuisance-parameter.md) $\lambda$, a partial ancillary for $\psi$ has a marginal law independent of $\psi$, possibly depending on $\lambda$. This differs from a fully [ancillary statistic](ancillary-statistic.md), whose law is independent of both. For independent Poisson counts with means $\lambda\psi$ and $\lambda(1-\psi)$, their total is Poisson with mean $\lambda$, while the first count conditional on the total is binomial with success probability $\psi$. Conditioning on the total eliminates $\lambda$. A partial ancillary does not automatically eliminate a [nuisance parameter](nuisance-parameter.md); the conditional law must actually be free of that parameter.
