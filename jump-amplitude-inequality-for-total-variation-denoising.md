# Jump-amplitude inequality for total variation denoising

↑ **Parent:** [Total variation denoising](total-variation-denoising.md)

For scalar [total variation denoising](total-variation-denoising.md) with $f\in BV(\Omega)\cap L^2(\Omega)$ on a bounded [Lipschitz domain](lipschitz-domain.md) in arbitrary dimension, the inequality holds with common oriented [BV traces on a hypersurface](bv-trace-on-a-hypersurface.md). It says that every surviving jump has the same direction as its data jump and no larger amplitude. Reversing the normal changes both differences and preserves the inequality.

Use [residual-preserving clipping of an ROF minimizer](residual-preserving-clipping-of-an-rof-minimizer.md) and the [jump-amplitude inequality for a bounded ROF minimizer](jump-amplitude-inequality-for-a-bounded-rof-minimizer.md). At each finite jump, choose an integer clipping level above both output traces. The clipped function has those same traces, and its residual-adjusted data have the same traces as $f$. Taking a countable union over the integer clipping levels proves the result. This gives the [no-new-jumps property of total variation denoising](no-new-jumps-property-of-total-variation-denoising.md) under the full $L^2\cap BV$ assumptions, without asserting regularity of unbounded-forcing perimeter [minimizers](global-minimizer.md).
