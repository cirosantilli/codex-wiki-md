# Cosmological constant problem

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmological constant](cosmological-constant.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_constant_problem)

The [cosmological constant problem](cosmological-constant-problem.md) is the unexplained smallness of the gravitationally observable [vacuum energy](vacuum-energy.md) compared with the contributions expected from [quantum field theory](quantum-field-theory-split.md). In units with $\hbar=c=1$, an [ultraviolet cutoff](ultraviolet-cutoff.md) $\Lambda_{\mathrm{UV}}$ generically gives contributions of order $\Lambda_{\mathrm{UV}}^4$, whereas the observed dark-energy density is of order $10^{-47}\,\mathrm{GeV}^4$. The [Einstein equation](einstein-field-equations.md) depends on the sum of a bare [cosmological constant](cosmological-constant.md) and these contributions; subtracting a constant from a nongravitational [Hamiltonian](hamiltonian.md) does not remove its gravitational effect. Exact global [supersymmetry](supersymmetry-split.md) gives zero energy to a [supersymmetric vacuum](supersymmetric-vacuum.md), but [supersymmetry breaking](supersymmetry-breaking.md) generally reintroduces a much larger positive scale.
