## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2025](2025.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Compact [support of a measure](../measure.md#support-of-a-measure) gives finite second [absolute moments](../expected-value.md#absolute-moment) for both [probability measures](../probability-space.md#probability-measure). Since the source satisfies [absolute continuity of measures](../measure-theory.md#absolute-continuity-of-measures) with respect to [Lebesgue measure](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure), [Brenier's theorem](../mathematical-optimization.md#brenier-theorem) identifies the quadratic [Kantorovich optimal transport problem](../mathematical-optimization.md#kantorovich-optimal-transport-problem) with the [Monge optimal transport problem](../mathematical-optimization.md#monge-optimal-transport-problem):

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}d_2^2(\mu,\nu)&=\min_{\pi\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}\int|x-y|^2\,d\pi(x,y)\\&=\min_{S_\#\mu=\nu}\int|x-S(x)|^2\,d\mu(x)=\int|x-T(x)|^2\,d\mu(x).\end{aligned}}
$$

Here $S_\#\mu$ is the [pushforward measure](../measure.md#pushforward-measure). The optimal [transport plan](../mathematical-optimization.md#transport-plan) is unique and equals $(\operatorname{Id},T)_\#\mu$. The optimal [transport map](../mathematical-optimization.md#transport-map) is **unique up to a $\mu$-null set**, with

$$
\boxed{T=\nabla u\quad\mu\text{-almost everywhere},\qquad u\text{ convex}.}
$$

The [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) of this [convex function](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) is [c-cyclically monotone](../mathematical-optimization.md#c-cyclical-monotonicity) for $c(x,y)=|x-y|^2$ on a full-measure set. In particular, summing the two supporting inequalities for the [convex function](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) gives $(T(x)-T(x'))\cdot(x-x')\geq0$ wherever both [gradients](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) exist. No regularity of the target beyond the stated [probability measure](../probability-space.md#probability-measure) assumptions is needed.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Write $P=\omega\omega^T$. Since $P^T=P$ and $P^2=P$, the [orthogonal reflection](../linear-algebra.md#reflection-in-a-hyperplane) $R_\omega=I-2P$ satisfies $R_\omega^TR_\omega=I$ and $R_\omega^{-1}=R_\omega$. Thus, for every [Borel set](../measure-theory.md#borel-set) $E$, invariance under the [orthogonal group](../linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-group) gives

$$
(R_\omega{}_\#\mu)(E)=\mu(R_\omega^{-1}E)=\mu(E),\qquad\boxed{R_\omega{}_\#\mu=\mu.}
$$

**The spherical average must use normalized surface measure without an additional area factor.** Let $d\sigma$ be the normalized [surface measure on a sphere](../euclidean-geometry.md#surface-measure-on-a-sphere), and set

$$
A\eta(r)=\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\eta(r\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta).
$$

The correct [radial disintegration of an orthogonally invariant measure](../measure.md#radial-disintegration-of-an-orthogonally-invariant-measure) is

$$
\boxed{\int\eta(x)\,d\mu(x)=\int_{[0,\infty)}A\eta(r)\,d(\Phi_\#\mu)(r).}
$$

Indeed, take normalized [Haar measure](../measure.md#haar-measure) $H$ on the compact [orthogonal group](../linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-group) $O(d)$. Invariance and [Fubini's theorem](../measure-theory.md#fubini-s-theorem) give

$$
\int\eta\,d\mu=\int\!\int_{O(d)}\eta(Qx)\,dH(Q)\,d\mu(x).
$$

Right invariance of [Haar measure](../measure.md#haar-measure) and transitivity of the [orthogonal group](../linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-group) on each [sphere](../euclidean-geometry.md#sphere) show that the inner average depends only on $r=|x|$. Averaging it once more over $x=r\theta$, using invariance of [surface measure on a sphere](../euclidean-geometry.md#surface-measure-on-a-sphere) and [Fubini's theorem](../measure-theory.md#fubini-s-theorem), identifies its value as $A\eta(r)$. At $x=0$ the same identity reads $\eta(0)=\eta(0)$. The definition of [pushforward measure](../measure.md#pushforward-measure) now proves the boxed formula. This argument also works for invariant [probability measures](../probability-space.md#probability-measure) with an [atom of a measure](../measure.md#atom-measure-theory) at the origin.

For the printed normalization, choose a [smooth cutoff function](../analysis.md#smooth-cutoff-function) $\eta$ equal to one on a ball containing the [support of a measure](../measure.md#support-of-a-measure) $\mu$. Its left integral is one, whereas the printed right integral is $\sigma_d$. Thus the extra $\sigma_d$ cannot be retained. Equivalently, with unnormalized area measure $dS$, the average is $\sigma_d^{-1}\int\eta(r\theta)\,dS(\theta)$.

**The reflection-averaging hint also fails in general dimension.** For $x=e_1$,

$$
\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}R_\omega e_1\,d\sigma(\omega)=\left(1-\frac2d\right)e_1,
\qquad\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\omega\,d\sigma(\omega)=0.
$$

The first equality uses $\int\omega_i\omega_j\,d\sigma=\delta_{ij}/d$, which follows from coordinate reflections, coordinate permutations, and $\sum_i\omega_i^2=1$. Taking a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) equal to the first coordinate near the unit [sphere](../euclidean-geometry.md#sphere) disproves the hint when $d\ne2$. Averaging over the full [orthogonal group](../linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-group), as above, proves the required corrected result without that hint.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Let $\alpha=\Phi_\#\mu_0=\Phi_\#\mu_1$. The corrected [radial disintegration of an orthogonally invariant measure](../measure.md#radial-disintegration-of-an-orthogonally-invariant-measure), whose proof did not require [absolute continuity of measures](../measure-theory.md#absolute-continuity-of-measures), gives for every [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $\eta$

$$
\int\eta\,d\mu_0=\int A\eta\,d\alpha=\int\eta\,d\mu_1.
$$

Finite [Borel measures](../measure.md#borel-measure) on [Euclidean space](../normed-vector-space.md#euclidean-norm) are determined by their integrals against [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function): the difference defines the zero [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis), or one can approximate continuous compactly supported functions uniformly by [smooth functions](../analysis.md#smooth-function) and use regularity of the [measures](../measure.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mu_0=\mu_1.}
$$

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

Write $d\mu=f(x)\,dx$, with $f\geq0$. Applying [polar coordinates](../multivariable-calculus.md#polar-coordinates) and [Fubini's theorem](../measure-theory.md#fubini-s-theorem) gives, for bounded nonnegative measurable $h$,

$$
\int h\,d\widetilde\mu=\int_0^\infty h(r)g(r)\,dr,\qquad g(r)=\sigma_d r^{d-1}\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}f(r\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta)\quad(r>0).
$$

Also $\widetilde\mu(\{0\})=\mu(\{0\})=0$ by [absolute continuity of measures](../measure-theory.md#absolute-continuity-of-measures). Extending $g$ by zero on the negative half-line proves

$$
\boxed{\widetilde\mu\ll\mathcal L^1.}
$$

Both radial [pushforward measures](../measure.md#pushforward-measure) have compact [support of a measure](../measure.md#support-of-a-measure), so [Brenier's theorem](../mathematical-optimization.md#brenier-theorem) on the line supplies a unique optimal [transport plan](../mathematical-optimization.md#transport-plan) and a [transport map](../mathematical-optimization.md#transport-map) $\tau$, unique $\widetilde\mu$-almost everywhere, with

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}d_2^2(\widetilde\mu,\widetilde\nu)&=\min_{s_\#\widetilde\mu=\widetilde\nu}\int_0^\infty|r-s(r)|^2\,d\widetilde\mu(r)\\&=\int_0^\infty|r-\tau(r)|^2\,d\widetilde\mu(r).\end{aligned}}
$$

In one dimension, the [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) of a [convex function](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) is a [nondecreasing function](../calculus.md#nondecreasing-function). Nonnegativity holds $\widetilde\mu$-almost everywhere because $\tau_\#\widetilde\mu=\widetilde\nu$ is carried by $[0,\infty)$. To choose a nonnegative, nondecreasing representative everywhere, let $F,G$ be the radial [cumulative distribution functions](../probability-distribution.md#cumulative-distribution-function) and use the [monotone rearrangement](../mathematical-optimization.md#monotone-rearrangement)

$$
\boxed{\tau(r)=G^{-1}(F(r)),\qquad G^{-1}(u)=\inf\{s\geq0:G(s)\geq u\}\quad(0<u<1).}
$$

At $F(r)=0$ set $\tau(r)=0$, and at $F(r)=1$ use the largest point of the [support of a measure](../measure.md#support-of-a-measure) $\widetilde\nu$. These choices preserve monotonicity and boundedness. Since $F$ is continuous, $F(R)$ has the [uniform distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution) on $(0,1)$ when $R$ has law $\widetilde\mu$, and the [quantile function](../probability-distribution.md#quantile-function) $G^{-1}$ sends this [uniform distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution) to $\widetilde\nu$. Thus this representative has the required [pushforward measure](../measure.md#pushforward-measure), even if the target has atoms.

<h3 id="1/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#1/e)

Choose the bounded, nonnegative [nondecreasing function](../calculus.md#nondecreasing-function) $\tau$ from part (d), and define

$$
h(r)=\int_0^r\tau(s)\,ds,\qquad U(x)=h(|x|).
$$

The [function](../function.md) $h$ is [convex](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) and nondecreasing. For $0\leq a\leq1$, the [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality), monotonicity, and [convexity](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) give

$$
U((1-a)x+ay)\leq h((1-a)|x|+a|y|)\leq(1-a)U(x)+aU(y).
$$

Hence $U$ is a [convex function](../real-analysis.md#convex-function). By the [gradient of a radial function](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient-of-a-radial-function) formula and the [fundamental theorem of calculus](../calculus.md#fundamental-theorem-of-calculus),

$$
\nabla U(x)=\tau(|x|)\frac{x}{|x|}
$$

for $x\ne0$ whenever $h'(|x|)=\tau(|x|)$. A [monotone function](../calculus.md#monotonic-function) has at most countably many discontinuities, so part (d) makes the exceptional radii $\widetilde\mu$-null. The origin is also $\mu$-null. Define $\mathcal T(0)=0$; thus $\mathcal T=\nabla U$ $\mu$-almost everywhere.

For every $Q$ in the [orthogonal group](../linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-group), $\mathcal T(Qx)=Q\mathcal T(x)$, including at zero. Consequently $\mathcal T_\#\mu$ is invariant under the [orthogonal group](../linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-group). Moreover $|\mathcal T(x)|=\tau(|x|)$ away from the null origin, and composition of [pushforward measures](../measure.md#pushforward-measure) gives

$$
\Phi_\#(\mathcal T_\#\mu)=\tau_\#(\Phi_\#\mu)=\widetilde\nu=\Phi_\#\nu.
$$

Part (c) therefore gives $\mathcal T_\#\mu=\nu$. The [Knott–Smith optimality criterion](../mathematical-optimization.md#knott-smith-optimality-criterion) makes a [transport map](../mathematical-optimization.md#transport-map) given by a [convex function](../real-analysis.md#convex-function)'s [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) optimal for the quadratic [transport cost](../mathematical-optimization.md#transport-cost-function). Uniqueness from [Brenier's theorem](../mathematical-optimization.md#brenier-theorem) yields

$$
\boxed{T(x)=\mathcal T(x)=\tau(|x|)\frac{x}{|x|}\quad\mu\text{-almost everywhere}.}
$$

Since directions agree, this also identifies the optimal value:

$$
\boxed{d_2^2(\mu,\nu)=\int_0^\infty|r-\tau(r)|^2\,d\widetilde\mu(r)=d_2^2(\widetilde\mu,\widetilde\nu).}
$$

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Put $D=d_2(\mu,\nu)$ and $z_t(x,y)=(1-t)x+ty$. The [marginal distributions](../probability-distribution.md#marginal-distribution) of the optimal [transport plan](../mathematical-optimization.md#transport-plan) $\Pi$ give $\mu_0=\mu$ and $\mu_1=\nu$. Also

$$
\int|z_t|^2\,d\Pi\leq(1-t)\int|x|^2\,d\mu+t\int|y|^2\,d\nu<\infty,
$$

so $\mu_t$ has a finite second [absolute moment](../expected-value.md#absolute-moment). For $0\leq s<t\leq1$, the [pushforward measure](../measure.md#pushforward-measure) $(z_s,z_t)_\#\Pi$ is a [coupling](../probability-and-statistics.md#coupling) of $\mu_s,\mu_t$. Therefore

$$
d_2(\mu_s,\mu_t)^2\leq\int|z_t-z_s|^2\,d\Pi=(t-s)^2D^2.
$$

Apply the [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) for the [second Wasserstein distance](../probability-and-statistics.md#second-wasserstein-distance) along $\mu_0,\mu_s,\mu_t,\mu_1$:

$$
D\leq d_2(\mu_0,\mu_s)+d_2(\mu_s,\mu_t)+d_2(\mu_t,\mu_1)\leq sD+(t-s)D+(1-t)D=D.
$$

All inequalities are equalities, including when $D=0$. Thus this [displacement interpolation](../probability-and-statistics.md#displacement-interpolation) is a constant speed [metric geodesic](../topological-analysis.md#metric-geodesic):

$$
\boxed{d_2(\mu_s,\mu_t)=|t-s|d_2(\mu,\nu).}
$$

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Set $q=y-x$ and $z_t=x+tq$. The definition of [pushforward measure](../measure.md#pushforward-measure) gives the [confinement energy](../mathematical-optimization.md#confinement-energy) $\mathcal V[\mu_t]=\int V(z_t)\,d\Pi$. The [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) and [differentiation under the integral sign](../analysis.md#differentiation-under-the-integral-sign), whenever justified, give

$$
\frac d{dt}\mathcal V[\mu_t]=\int\nabla V(z_t)\cdot q\,d\Pi,\qquad\boxed{\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\mathcal V[\mu_t]=\int q^TD^2V(z_t)q\,d\Pi.}
$$

For example, bounded [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) is sufficient: it gives $|\nabla V(z)|\leq C(1+|z|)$, and the first two differentiated integrands are bounded by $C'(1+|x|^2+|y|^2)$. The [dominated convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem) applies because $\Pi$ has finite second [absolute moments](../expected-value.md#absolute-moment). Compactly supported endpoints also suffice because all interpolating points lie in one compact set.

**The printed growth assumption alone does not ensure a classical second derivative.** Here is a counterexample within the stated assumptions. On the line take the bounded nonnegative [smooth function](../analysis.md#smooth-function)

$$
V(x)=1-\cos(2\pi x^3),\qquad p_n=cn^{-4}\quad(n\geq1),\qquad c^{-1}=\sum_{n\geq1}n^{-4},
$$

and the [probability measures](../probability-space.md#probability-measure) $\mu=\sum_np_n\delta_{n-1/2}$, $\nu=\sum_np_n\delta_{n+1/2}$. Both have finite second [absolute moments](../expected-value.md#absolute-moment). Their translation [transport plan](../mathematical-optimization.md#transport-plan) is optimal: its cost is one, while every [coupling](../probability-and-statistics.md#coupling) has cost at least the square of its mean displacement, also one. At $t_0=1/2$, $z_{t_0}=n$, so $F(t)=\mathcal V[\mu_t]$ satisfies $F(t_0)=0$. Each summand is nonnegative, and the [Fatou lemma](../measure-theory.md#fatou-s-lemma) gives

$$
\liminf_{h\to0}\frac{F(t_0+h)+F(t_0-h)-2F(t_0)}{h^2}\geq\sum_np_nV''(n)=36\pi^2c\sum_n1=\infty.
$$

Thus the requested classical identity requires an additional integrability assumption on the [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix); quadratic growth controls the energy but not its [second derivative](../calculus.md#second-derivative).

For a [confinement potential](../mathematical-optimization.md#confinement-potential) $V$ that is a $\lambda$-[strongly convex function](../real-analysis.md#strongly-convex-function), the derivative formula, where valid, yields

$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\mathcal V[\mu_t]\geq\lambda\int|q|^2\,d\Pi=\lambda D^2.}
$$

The underlying [displacement convexity](../probability-and-statistics.md#displacement-convexity) conclusion is valid under the printed assumptions without that extra differentiation hypothesis. Apply [convexity](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) of $V(x)-\lambda|x|^2/2$ pointwise and integrate:

$$
\boxed{\mathcal V[\mu_t]\leq(1-t)\mathcal V[\mu]+t\mathcal V[\nu]-\frac\lambda2t(1-t)D^2.}
$$

The same argument on every subinterval makes $t\mapsto\mathcal V[\mu_t]-\lambda D^2t^2/2$ [convex](../real-analysis.md#convex-function), so the second derivative lower bound also holds in the sense of [distributions](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis).

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Use an [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) copy $(x',y')$ of $(x,y)$ under the [product measure](../probability-theory.md#product-measure) $\Pi\otimes\Pi$. Write $q=y-x$, $q'=y'-x'$, and

$$
r_t=z_t-z'_t=(1-t)(x-x')+t(y-y'),\qquad a=q-q'.
$$

Since the [pushforward measure](../measure.md#pushforward-measure) of $\Pi\otimes\Pi$ under $(z_t,z'_t)$ is $\mu_t\otimes\mu_t$, the [interaction energy](../mathematical-optimization.md#interaction-energy) is

$$
\mathcal W[\mu_t]=\frac12\iint W(r_t)\,d\Pi(x,y)\,d\Pi(x',y').
$$

Two applications of the [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) give

$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\mathcal W[\mu_t]=\frac12\iint (q-q')^TD^2W(z_t-z'_t)(q-q')\,d\Pi\,d\Pi'.}
$$

The factor $1/2$ is retained. This classical identity requires [differentiation under the integral sign](../analysis.md#differentiation-under-the-integral-sign) to be justified, just as in part (b). Bounded [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) of $W$ is sufficient, since $|q-q'|^2\leq2|q|^2+2|q'|^2$ is integrable; compactly supported endpoints are another sufficient case. The printed quadratic-growth condition alone does not provide this domination.

The failure can occur for the [interaction energy](../mathematical-optimization.md#interaction-energy) too. Take the even [interaction potential](../mathematical-optimization.md#interaction-potential) $W(x)=1-\cos(2\pi x^3)$ and replace the endpoint [probability measures](../probability-space.md#probability-measure) in part (b) by $\frac12\delta_0+\frac12\mu$ and $\frac12\delta_0+\frac12\nu$. Keep the origin fixed and translate every other atom by one. This coupling is increasing on the line, hence is optimal: its assigned values extend to a nondecreasing [transport map](../mathematical-optimization.md#transport-map), which is the [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) of a [convex function](../real-analysis.md#convex-function). Differences between translated atoms remain integers, so their mutual interaction vanishes. Only interactions with the origin contribute, giving $\mathcal W[\mu_t]=F(t)/4$, with $F$ from part (b). It therefore also lacks a finite classical [second derivative](../calculus.md#second-derivative) at $t=1/2$.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

Define the [barycenter of a measure](../measure-theory.md#barycenter-of-a-measure-on-a-banach-space) by $m_\mu=\int x\,d\mu(x)$. Under $\Pi$, the mean displacement is $\bar q=m_\nu-m_\mu$, and optimality gives $\int|q|^2\,d\Pi=D^2$. Expanding the square under the [product measure](../probability-theory.md#product-measure) yields the [variance identity for independent copies](../variance.md#variance-identity-for-independent-copies):

$$
\frac12\iint|q-q'|^2\,d\Pi\,d\Pi'=\int|q|^2\,d\Pi-\left|\int q\,d\Pi\right|^2=D^2-|m_\nu-m_\mu|^2.
$$

In the classical regime of part (c), the lower bound on the [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) of the [interaction potential](../mathematical-optimization.md#interaction-potential) therefore gives

$$
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\mathcal W[\mu_t]\geq\lambda\left(D^2-|m_\nu-m_\mu|^2\right).
$$

For equal [barycenters](../topological-vector-space.md#barycenter), this becomes

$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\mathcal W[\mu_t]\geq\lambda D^2.}
$$

For the full stated class of [probability measures](../probability-space.md#probability-measure), prove the [displacement convexity](../probability-and-statistics.md#displacement-convexity) inequality directly. Pointwise strong [convexity](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) of $W$ gives

$$
W((1-t)(x-x')+t(y-y'))\leq(1-t)W(x-x')+tW(y-y')-\frac\lambda2t(1-t)|q-q'|^2.
$$

Integrating, including the $1/2$ in the [interaction energy](../mathematical-optimization.md#interaction-energy), and using the displayed [variance identity for independent copies](../variance.md#variance-identity-for-independent-copies) gives

$$
\mathcal W[\mu_t]\leq(1-t)\mathcal W[\mu]+t\mathcal W[\nu]-\frac\lambda2t(1-t)\left(D^2-|m_\nu-m_\mu|^2\right).
$$

All terms are finite by quadratic growth and finite second [absolute moments](../expected-value.md#absolute-moment). Equal [barycenters](../topological-vector-space.md#barycenter) give the claimed $\lambda$-[displacement convexity](../probability-and-statistics.md#displacement-convexity). Also $m_{\mu_t}=(1-t)m_\mu+tm_\nu$, so this [metric geodesic](../topological-analysis.md#metric-geodesic) remains in the prescribed fixed-mean class. Applying the same argument to subintervals proves the second derivative bound in the sense of [distributions](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) even when a classical derivative has not been justified. The mean restriction is natural: translating all particles together leaves their pairwise differences and hence the [interaction energy](../mathematical-optimization.md#interaction-energy) unchanged.

<h3 id="2/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#2/e)

Choose a uniform lower bound $\kappa\in\mathbb R$ for the [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) of the [confinement potential](../mathematical-optimization.md#confinement-potential), so $D^2V(x)\geq\kappa I$ for every $x$. A sufficient condition is

$$
\boxed{\kappa+\lambda>0,\qquad\widetilde\lambda=\kappa+\lambda.}
$$

Indeed, [convexity](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) of $V(x)-\kappa|x|^2/2$ gives the [confinement energy](../mathematical-optimization.md#confinement-energy) inequality from part (b), now with $\kappa$ possibly negative. Adding it to the equal-mean [interaction energy](../mathematical-optimization.md#interaction-energy) inequality from part (d) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathcal E[\mu_t]\leq(1-t)\mathcal E[\mu]+t\mathcal E[\nu]-\frac{\kappa+\lambda}{2}t(1-t)d_2^2(\mu,\nu).}
$$

This proves positive [displacement convexity](../probability-and-statistics.md#displacement-convexity) on every class with fixed [barycenter](../topological-vector-space.md#barycenter). In particular, if $D^2V\geq-KI$ with $K\geq0$, the sufficient condition is $\lambda>K$ and one may take $\widetilde\lambda=\lambda-K$. Wherever classical second derivatives exist, the equivalent differential calculation is $\mathcal E''[\mu_t]\geq(\kappa+\lambda)D^2$; the integrated proof also covers the printed growth assumptions.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2025](2025.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

Set

$$
K(z)=-J\nabla W(z)=(W_y(z),-W_x(z)),\qquad L=\max\left\{1,\sup_z\|D^2W(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\right\}.
$$

Symmetry here means $W(-z)=W(z)$, so $\nabla W(0)=0$ and $K(0)=0$. The bounded [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) and the fact that $J$ is an [orthogonal matrix](../linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-matrix) give

$$
|K(z)-K(z')|\leq L|z-z'|,\qquad |K(z)|\leq L|z|.
$$

Thus the particle system is $\dot X^i=N^{-1}\sum_jK(X^i-X^j)$. On the configuration space with norm $\|X\|_{\max}=\max_i|X^i|$, its vector field has [Lipschitz constant](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-constant) at most $2L$:

$$
|F_i(X)-F_i(Y)|\leq\frac L N\sum_j\left(|X^i-Y^i|+|X^j-Y^j|\right)\leq2L\|X-Y\|_{\max}.
$$

The [Picard-Lindelöf theorem](../differential-equation.md#picard-lindelof-theorem) gives local existence and uniqueness. Linear growth and the [Gronwall inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#gronwall-inequality) give $\|X(t)\|_{\max}\leq e^{2Lt}\|X(0)\|_{\max}$, which rules out escape to infinity in finite time and extends the solution to every $t\geq0$.

The associated [empirical measure](../probability-space.md#empirical-measure) is

$$
\boxed{\mu_t^N=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\delta_{X^i(t)}.}
$$

Here each $\delta_{X^i(t)}$ is a [Dirac measure](../measure.md#dirac-measure). The finite first [absolute moment](../expected-value.md#absolute-moment) follows from $\int|x|\,d\mu_t^N=N^{-1}\sum_i|X^i(t)|<\infty$. Pairing equal particle labels defines a [coupling](../probability-and-statistics.md#coupling), so

$$
d_1(\mu_t^N,\mu_s^N)\leq\frac1N\sum_i|X^i(t)-X^i(s)|\longrightarrow0\quad(t\to s).
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mu^N\in C([0,\infty),\mathcal P_1(\mathbb R^2)).}
$$

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

For a candidate curve $\rho$, define the [velocity field](../fluid-mechanics.md#velocity-field) by the [convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#convolution)

$$
b_\rho(t,x)=K*\rho_t(x)=-\int J\nabla W(x-z)\,d\rho_t(z).
$$

It is well defined with a finite first [absolute moment](../expected-value.md#absolute-moment), because $|K(x-z)|\leq L(|x|+|z|)$. Equality of mixed [partial derivatives](../multivariable-calculus.md#partial-derivative) gives

$$
\operatorname{div}K=\partial_xW_y-\partial_yW_x=0,\qquad\operatorname{div}b_\rho=0.
$$

The [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) bound justifies moving a spatial derivative through this [convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#convolution). For smooth densities, the [transport equation](../transport-equation.md) consequently becomes the [continuity equation](../mathematical-physics.md#continuity-equation)

$$
\boxed{\partial_t\rho+\operatorname{div}(b_\rho\rho)=0.}
$$

This form defines the equation for singular [probability measures](../probability-space.md#probability-measure) as well. With initial [probability measure](../probability-space.md#probability-measure) $\rho_0$, a [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) is a curve in $C([0,\infty),\mathcal P_1)$ such that every $\varphi\in C_c^1([0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^2)$ satisfies

$$
\boxed{\int_0^\infty\!\int\left[\partial_t\varphi(t,x)+b_\rho(t,x)\cdot\nabla\varphi(t,x)\right]d\rho_t(x)\,dt+\int\varphi(0,x)\,d\rho_0(x)=0.}
$$

Using smooth [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) gives the same definition by approximation on compact sets. Continuity in the [Wasserstein distance](../probability-and-statistics.md#wasserstein-distance) bounds first [absolute moments](../expected-value.md#absolute-moment) on compact time intervals, so the displayed integrals exist.

For the [empirical measure](../probability-space.md#empirical-measure), the [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) and the particle equations give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac d{dt}\int\varphi(t,x)\,d\mu_t^N(x)&=\frac1N\sum_i\left[\partial_t\varphi(t,X^i)+\nabla\varphi(t,X^i)\cdot\dot X^i\right]\\
&=\int\left[\partial_t\varphi+b_{\mu^N}\cdot\nabla\varphi\right]d\mu_t^N.
\end{aligned}
$$

Integrating in time, with zero terminal contribution because $\varphi$ has compact [support](../function.md#support), gives precisely the weak identity and the correct initial [empirical measure](../probability-space.md#empirical-measure). Thus **$\mu^N$ is a global weak solution**. The self-interaction term vanishes since $K(0)=0$.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

**A finite first moment suffices for the velocity, but may not suffice for the printed potential.** For example, $W(z)=|z|^2/2$ has all the stated properties. If $\mu$ is the law of $(S,0)$ with density $2s^{-3}$ on $s\geq1$, then $\mathbb ES=2$ but $\mathbb ES^2=\infty$, and $(W*\mu)(x)=\infty$ for every $x$. Thus the literal definition of $V$ needs a finite second [absolute moment](../expected-value.md#absolute-moment), or another hypothesis ensuring integrability of $W$.

For the specified class $\mathcal P_1$, define the [velocity fields](../fluid-mechanics.md#velocity-field) directly by $b_\mu=K*\mu$ and $b_{\widehat\mu}=K*\widehat\mu$. Equivalently, use the [renormalized interaction potential](../mathematical-optimization.md#renormalized-interaction-potential)

$$
V_{\mathrm{ren}}(t,x)=\int\left[W(x-z)-W(-z)\right]d\mu_t(z).
$$

The bounded [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) and $\nabla W(0)=0$ imply

$$
|W(x-z)-W(-z)|\leq L|x||z|+\frac L2|x|^2.
$$

Thus $V_{\mathrm{ren}}$ is finite, is twice continuously differentiable in space, and has $\nabla V_{\mathrm{ren}}=\nabla W*\mu$, by dominated [differentiation under the integral sign](../analysis.md#differentiation-under-the-integral-sign). Its rotated negative [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) is exactly $b_\mu$. Whenever $W*\mu$ is finite, the two potentials differ only by an $x$-independent constant and give the same [transport equation](../transport-equation.md).

We check the hypotheses for [characteristic representation of a measure-valued continuity equation](../transport-equation.md#characteristic-representation-of-a-measure-valued-continuity-equation). For every $x,y$,

$$
|b_\mu(t,x)-b_\mu(t,y)|\leq L|x-y|,\qquad |b_\mu(t,x)|\leq L\left(|x|+m_1(\mu_t)\right),\qquad m_1(\mu_t)=\int|z|\,d\mu_t(z).
$$

For any [coupling](../probability-and-statistics.md#coupling) $\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu_t,\widehat\mu_t)$, the [Lipschitz condition](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) on $K$ also gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
|b_\mu(t,x)-b_{\widehat\mu}(t,x)|&=\left|\int[K(x-z)-K(x-\widehat z)]\,d\gamma(z,\widehat z)\right|\\
&\leq L\int|z-\widehat z|\,d\gamma(z,\widehat z).
\end{aligned}
$$

Taking the infimum over [couplings](../probability-and-statistics.md#coupling) proves the uniform bound

$$
\boxed{\sup_x|b_\mu(t,x)-b_{\widehat\mu}(t,x)|\leq Ld_1(\mu_t,\widehat\mu_t).}
$$

Applying the same estimate at two times of one curve proves time continuity of $b_\mu$, uniformly in $x$. Also $|m_1(\mu_t)-m_1(\mu_s)|\leq d_1(\mu_t,\mu_s)$, so $m_1(\mu_t)$ is bounded on compact time intervals. Spatial [partial derivatives](../multivariable-calculus.md#partial-derivative) of $b_\mu$ are continuous and bounded by $L$, using dominated convergence in space and [weak convergence of probability measures](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#weak-convergence-of-probability-measures) in time for the bounded continuous kernel $DK$. All corresponding facts hold for $\widehat\mu$.

The [characteristic representation of a measure-valued continuity equation](../transport-equation.md#characteristic-representation-of-a-measure-valued-continuity-equation) says that a continuous [velocity field](../fluid-mechanics.md#velocity-field), continuously differentiable and globally [Lipschitz](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) in space and with locally uniform linear growth, has a global [characteristic flow map](../transport-equation.md#characteristic-flow-map) $X_\mu(t;s,a)$ solving

$$
\frac d{dt}X_\mu(t;s,a)=b_\mu(t,X_\mu(t;s,a)),\qquad X_\mu(s;s,a)=a.
$$

For each $f\in\mathcal P_1$, its [pushforward measure](../measure.md#pushforward-measure) is the unique [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) in $C([0,\infty),\mathcal P_1)$ of the associated [continuity equation](../mathematical-physics.md#continuity-equation). The hypotheses have all been verified above, and the fields have zero [divergence](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence), so these are also the two required [transport equations](../transport-equation.md). Explicitly,

$$
\boxed{\rho_t=X_\mu(t;0,\cdot)_\#f,\qquad\widehat\rho_t=X_{\widehat\mu}(t;0,\cdot)_\#f.}
$$

For completeness, the [Gronwall inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#gronwall-inequality) gives

$$
|X_\mu(t;0,a)|\leq e^{Lt}|a|+L\int_0^te^{L(t-s)}m_1(\mu_s)\,ds,
$$

so these [pushforward measures](../measure.md#pushforward-measure) have finite first [absolute moments](../expected-value.md#absolute-moment). Integrating $|X_\mu(t;0,a)-X_\mu(s;0,a)|$ against $f$ proves continuity in the [Wasserstein distance](../probability-and-statistics.md#wasserstein-distance). The [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) proves the weak identity. For uniqueness at a fixed terminal time $t$, use the backward [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $\varphi(s,x)=\psi(X_\mu(t;s,x))$, where $\psi$ is continuously differentiable with compact [support](../function.md#support). It satisfies $\partial_s\varphi+b_\mu\cdot\nabla\varphi=0$ and has compact spatial [support](../function.md#support) uniformly for $0\leq s\leq t$, by the backward linear-growth bound. The weak identity with temporal cutoffs therefore gives $\int\psi\,d\rho_t=\int\psi(X_\mu(t;0,a))\,df(a)$, identifying every [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) with the displayed [pushforward measure](../measure.md#pushforward-measure).

Finally compare the two [characteristic curves](../transport-equation.md#characteristic-curve) with the same initial point $a$. Put $\Delta_t=X_\mu(t;0,a)-X_{\widehat\mu}(t;0,a)$. The spatial [Lipschitz condition](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) and the boxed velocity estimate give

$$
\frac d{dt}|\Delta_t|\leq L|\Delta_t|+Ld_1(\mu_t,\widehat\mu_t),\qquad\Delta_0=0,
$$

for almost every time; the integral form gives the same conclusion at zeros of $\Delta$. The [Gronwall inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#gronwall-inequality) implies

$$
|\Delta_t|\leq L\int_0^te^{L(t-s)}d_1(\mu_s,\widehat\mu_s)\,ds.
$$

The [pushforward measure](../measure.md#pushforward-measure) $(X_\mu(t;0,\cdot),X_{\widehat\mu}(t;0,\cdot))_\#f$ is a [coupling](../probability-and-statistics.md#coupling) of $\rho_t,\widehat\rho_t$. Integrating the last bound against the [probability measure](../probability-space.md#probability-measure) $f$ proves

$$
\boxed{d_1(\rho_t,\widehat\rho_t)\leq L\int_0^te^{L(t-s)}d_1(\mu_s,\widehat\mu_s)\,ds.}
$$

The same constant $L$ depends only on the uniform bound for the [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) of $W$, and is independent of the prescribed curves and their common initial data.
