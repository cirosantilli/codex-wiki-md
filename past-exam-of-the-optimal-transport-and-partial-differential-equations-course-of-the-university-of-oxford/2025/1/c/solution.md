<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $\alpha=\Phi_\#\mu_0=\Phi_\#\mu_1$. The corrected [radial disintegration of an orthogonally invariant measure](../../../../radial-disintegration-of-an-orthogonally-invariant-measure.md), whose proof did not require [absolute continuity of measures](../../../../absolute-continuity-of-measures.md), gives for every [test function](../../../../test-function.md) $\eta$

$$
\int\eta\,d\mu_0=\int A\eta\,d\alpha=\int\eta\,d\mu_1.
$$

Finite [Borel measures](../../../../borel-measure.md) on [Euclidean space](../../../../euclidean-norm.md) are determined by their integrals against [test functions](../../../../test-function.md): the difference defines the zero [distribution](../../../../distribution-mathematical-analysis.md), or one can approximate continuous compactly supported functions uniformly by [smooth functions](../../../../smooth-function.md) and use regularity of the [measures](../../../../measure-split.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mu_0=\mu_1.}
$$
