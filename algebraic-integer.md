# Algebraic integer

↑ **Parent:** [Algebraic number theory](algebraic-number-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_integer)

An [algebraic number](algebraic-number.md) $\alpha$ is an algebraic integer if it is a [polynomial root](root-of-a-polynomial.md) of a [monic polynomial](monic-polynomial.md) with [integer](integer-split.md) coefficients:

$$
\alpha^m+c_{m-1}\alpha^{m-1}+\cdots+c_0=0,\qquad m\ge1,\quad c_j\in\mathbb Z.
$$

Equivalently, its [algebraic minimal polynomial](minimal-polynomial-of-an-algebraic-element.md) over $\mathbb Q$ has [integer](integer-split.md) coefficients. The requirement that the leading coefficient be one matters: $1/2$ satisfies $2X-1=0$ and is an [algebraic number](algebraic-number.md), but is not an algebraic integer. In fact the [rational numbers](rational-number.md) that are algebraic integers are exactly the [integers](integer-split.md).[Milne, Algebraic Number Theory, Chapter 2, integral elements](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf)

Some useful computed examples are:

- $\sqrt2$ satisfies $X^2-2=0$.
- $1+\sqrt2$ satisfies $X^2-2X-1=0$.
- $\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ satisfies $X^2-X-1=0$, although its displayed coefficients in the [basis](basis.md) $1,\sqrt5$ are not [integers](integer-split.md).
- $\sqrt2/2$ has [algebraic minimal polynomial](minimal-polynomial-of-an-algebraic-element.md) $X^2-1/2$, so it is not an algebraic integer even though it satisfies $2X^2-1=0$.

Algebraic integers are closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication. Thus they provide an arithmetic [ring](ring.md), while division is allowed only when the result is again an algebraic integer. The [algebraic integer module criterion](algebraic-integer-module-criterion.md) explains this closure without having to find a new [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) for every sum and product.
