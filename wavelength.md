# Wavelength

↑ **Parent:** [Wavenumber](wavenumber.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength)

The [wavelength](wavelength.md) $\lambda$ is the spatial [period](period-of-a-function.md) of a [monochromatic wave](monochromatic-wave.md), measured at a fixed time along the [wavevector](wavevector.md). For a [plane wave](plane-wave.md) with a [sinusoid](sinusoid.md) as its profile, it is the distance between consecutive [wave crests](wave-crest.md), or between any consecutive points with the same [wave phase](phase-waves.md) modulo $2\pi$:

$$
\lambda=\frac{2\pi}{|\mathbf k|}=\frac{v_p}{f}.
$$

Here $\mathbf k$ uses angular [wavenumber](wavenumber.md), $f$ is ordinary [frequency](frequency.md), and $v_p$ is [phase speed](phase-speed.md). A distance between arbitrary zero crossings need not be a [wavelength](wavelength.md): consecutive zeros of a [sinusoid](sinusoid.md) are only $\lambda/2$ apart, with opposite slopes. In a [standing wave](standing-wave.md), consecutive [nodes of a standing wave](node-physics.md) are also $\lambda/2$ apart; $\lambda$ refers to the constituent [travelling waves](travelling-wave.md), even though the [nodes of a standing wave](node-physics.md) do not travel.

Across a stationary interface between stationary linear media, the incident, reflected and transmitted [monochromatic waves](monochromatic-wave.md) have the same [frequency](frequency.md). The [boundary conditions](boundary-condition.md) must hold at every time: terms oscillating with different [angular frequencies](angular-frequency.md) cannot cancel throughout the entire time record. Each medium's [dispersion relation](dispersion-relation-split.md) then determines its own angular [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) and [wavelength](wavelength.md), so

$$
f_1=f_2,\qquad \frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1}=\frac{v_{p,2}}{v_{p,1}}.
$$

For [electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation-split.md) in a transparent medium with [refractive index](refractive-index.md) $n$, $v_p=c_0/n$ and $\lambda=\lambda_0/n$, where $c_0$ is the vacuum [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). A synthetic example with $\lambda_0=600\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $n=1.5$ gives $\lambda=400\,\mathrm{nm}$ at the same [frequency](frequency.md). This conservation argument assumes time-independent linear material response and a stationary interface in the chosen frame. A moving interface can produce a [Doppler effect](doppler-effect.md); time-dependent or nonlinear response can change or generate [frequencies](frequency.md).[27.1 The Wave Aspect of Light: Interference](college-physics-by-paul-peter-urone-and-others/27-1-the-wave-aspect-of-light-interference.md)

A broad [frequency spectrum](frequency-spectrum.md) generally supplies many component [wavelengths](wavelength.md), rather than one [wavelength](wavelength.md) for an entire pulse. Even two tones have both their individual [wavelengths](wavelength.md) and a different overall repetition distance: the $2$- and $8$-cycle-per-metre sum in [spatial sampling of a wave](spatial-sampling-of-a-wave.md) repeats after $0.50\,\mathrm m$, but contains the finer $0.125\,\mathrm m$ structure. A narrow [wave packet](wave-packet.md) can have an approximate carrier [wavelength](wavelength.md); the [wave envelope](envelope-waves.md) propagates approximately at [group velocity](group-velocity.md), determined by the slope of the [dispersion relation](dispersion-relation-split.md). Substituting that [group velocity](group-velocity.md) into $\lambda=v_p/f$ generally gives the wrong carrier [wavelength](wavelength.md). Where the medium varies slowly, [local wavelength](local-wavelength.md) gives a more useful description.
