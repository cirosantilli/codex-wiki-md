# Cosmological principle

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmology](cosmology-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_principle)

The [cosmological principle](cosmological-principle.md) assumes that the universe is statistically homogeneous and isotropic on sufficiently large spatial scales. It applies to a coarse-grained description, not to each [galaxy](galaxy-split.md) or local void. Combined with the [Copernican principle](copernican-principle.md), [cosmological isotropy](cosmological-isotropy.md) around us supports a homogeneous description rather than a specially centred model. It motivates an [FRW metric](friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md), whose expansion is governed by a few time-dependent quantities. Observations sample only a finite [past light cone](past-light-cone.md), so they support this hypothesis without proving global [spatial homogeneity](spatial-homogeneity.md) beyond the observed region.
