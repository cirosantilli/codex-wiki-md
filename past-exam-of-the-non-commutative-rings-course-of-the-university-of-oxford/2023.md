<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

A [left-primitive ideal](../associative-algebra.md#primitive-ideal) is a two-sided [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) of the form

$$
P=\operatorname{Ann}_R(V)=\{r\in R:rV=0\}
$$

for a nonzero [simple module](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module) $V$ on the left. Equivalently, the [quotient ring](../commutative-algebra.md#quotient-ring) $R/P$ has a [faithful module](../module-theory.md#faithful-module) that is a [simple module](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module), and is therefore a [primitive ring](../noncommutative-algebra.md#primitive-ring).

To prove that $P$ is a [prime ideal of a noncommutative ring](../noncommutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal-of-a-noncommutative-ring), let $A,B$ be two-sided [ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) with $AB\subseteq P$. Since $B$ is two-sided, $BV$ is a [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) of $V$: for $r\in R$, $b\in B$ and $v\in V$, one has $r(bv)=(rb)v\in BV$. If $B\not\subseteq P$, then $BV\ne0$, so simplicity gives $BV=V$. Consequently

$$
AV=A(BV)=(AB)V=0,
$$

and $A\subseteq P$. Thus $AB\subseteq P$ implies $A\subseteq P$ or $B\subseteq P$. Also $P\ne R$, since $1$ acts as the [identity element](../algebraic-operation.md#identity-element) on the nonzero [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) $V$. These are precisely the required [prime ideal of a noncommutative ring](../noncommutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal-of-a-noncommutative-ring) conditions.

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

The [Jacobson radical](../noncommutative-algebra.md#jacobson-radical) is

$$
J(R)=\bigcap_{L\text{ maximal left ideal}}L
=\bigcap_{V\text{ simple left module}}\operatorname{Ann}_R(V).
$$

Here the second intersection ranges over isomorphism classes of nonzero [simple modules](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module). For the equality, every nonzero $v$ in a [simple module](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module) gives a [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function) [module homomorphism](../module-theory.md#module-homomorphism) $R\to V$, $r\mapsto rv$, whose [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is a [maximal left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#maximal-left-ideal). Conversely, every [maximal left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#maximal-left-ideal) $L$ gives the [simple module](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module) $R/L$. This description by [annihilators](../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory) shows that $J(R)$ is a two-sided [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal).

Let $j\in J(R)$ and put $a=1+j$. If the [left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) $Ra$ were proper, it would lie in a [maximal left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#maximal-left-ideal) $L$. Both $a$ and $j$ would belong to $L$, giving $1\in L$, a contradiction. Therefore there is $u\in R$ with $ua=1$.

A left inverse alone need not be a two-sided inverse in a general [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring), so one more step is necessary. The equality $u(1+j)=1$ gives $u=1-uj\in1+J(R)$. Applying the same argument to $u$ supplies $v\in R$ with $vu=1$. Then

$$
v=v(ua)=(vu)a=a,
$$

and hence $au=vu=1$. Thus

$$
\boxed{1+J(R)\subseteq R^\times.}
$$

For the [zero ring](../commutative-algebra.md#zero-ring) the conclusion holds directly; the [maximal left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#maximal-left-ideal) argument concerns the nonzero case.

<h4 id="1/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/a/iii)

Let $L$ be any [maximal left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#maximal-left-ideal). If $I\not\subseteq L$, maximality gives $L+I=R$, because the two-sided [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $I$ is in particular a [left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal). Thus $1=\ell+i$ for some $\ell\in L$ and $i\in I$. But

$$
\ell=1-i\in1+I
$$

is a [unit](../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring) by hypothesis. A proper [left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) cannot contain a [unit](../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring), since left multiplication by its inverse would put $1$ in the [left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal). This contradiction proves $I\subseteq L$ for every [maximal left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#maximal-left-ideal) $L$, and therefore

$$
\boxed{I\subseteq J(R).}
$$

Together with [ii](#1/a/ii), this is the ideal form of the [unit criterion for the Jacobson radical](../noncommutative-algebra.md#unit-criterion-for-the-jacobson-radical).

Assume $A\ne0$, as is implicit in the claimed conclusion. Suppose that a [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function) [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $f:R\to M_2(A)$ exists. Choose $r_1,r_2\in R$ mapping to the [matrix units](../vector-space.md#matrix-unit) $E_{12}$ and $E_{21}$. Applying $f$ to the assumed relation gives integers $c_1,\ldots,c_n$ such that

$$
E_{22}=E_{21}E_{12}
=\sum_{k=1}^n c_kE_{12}^{\,k}E_{21}.
$$

But $E_{12}^2=0$ and $E_{12}E_{21}=E_{11}$, so the right side is $c_1E_{11}$. Its $(2,2)$ entry is zero, whereas the $(2,2)$ entry of $E_{22}$ is $1_A\ne0$. This contradiction proves that no such [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function) [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) exists.

If the convention for [rings](../commutative-algebra.md#ring) allows $A$ to be the [zero ring](../commutative-algebra.md#zero-ring), that case must be excluded from the assertion: $M_2(0)=0$, and the zero [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) is [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function). This is a convention issue in the printed question.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Put $B=R/I$. By the definition of a [left-primitive ideal](../associative-algebra.md#primitive-ideal), there is a [faithful module](../module-theory.md#faithful-module) $V$ that is a [simple module](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module) on the left over $B$. The assumed relation passes to the [quotient ring](../commutative-algebra.md#quotient-ring) $B$, since any pair of elements of $B$ can be lifted to $R$.

By [Schur's lemma](../representation-theory.md#schur-s-lemma), the [endomorphism ring](../module-theory.md#endomorphism-ring) $\operatorname{End}_B(V)$ is a [division ring](../commutative-algebra.md#division-ring): the [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) and [image](../set-theory.md#image-of-a-function) of a nonzero [module endomorphism](../module-theory.md#module-endomorphism) are [submodules](../module-theory.md#submodule), so simplicity makes that [module endomorphism](../module-theory.md#module-endomorphism) both [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function) and [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function). Set

$$
\Delta=\operatorname{End}_B(V)^{\mathrm{op}}.
$$

Evaluation makes $V$ a right [vector space over a division ring](../vector-space.md#vector-space-over-a-division-ring) $\Delta$, and the $B$-action is $\Delta$-linear. The [opposite ring](../commutative-algebra.md#opposite-ring) in the definition ensures the correct order for this right action.

The [Jacobson density theorem](../noncommutative-algebra.md#jacobson-density-theorem) says that, for any finite $\Delta$-[linearly independent](../vector-space.md#linear-independence) family $v_1,\ldots,v_m$ and any $w_1,\ldots,w_m\in V$, some $b\in B$ satisfies $bv_i=w_i$ for every $i$.

Suppose that $V$ contains two $\Delta$-[linearly independent](../vector-space.md#linear-independence) vectors $v,w$. Applying the [Jacobson density theorem](../noncommutative-algebra.md#jacobson-density-theorem) twice, choose $a,b\in B$ satisfying

$$
av=0,\qquad aw=v,\qquad bv=w,\qquad bw=0.
$$

The inherited relation gives

$$
ba=\sum_{k=1}^n c_ka^kb
$$

with integers $c_k$. Acting on $w$, the left side gives $baw=bv=w$, but every term on the right gives $a^kbw=0$. This contradicts $w\ne0$. Thus $\dim_\Delta V=1$.

The [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism)

$$
B\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_\Delta(V)
$$

given by the action is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function) because $V$ is a [faithful module](../module-theory.md#faithful-module). It is [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function) because the [Jacobson density theorem](../noncommutative-algebra.md#jacobson-density-theorem), applied to a single nonzero [basis vector](../vector-space.md#basis-vector), realizes every $\Delta$-linear [endomorphism](../algebra.md#endomorphism) of this one-dimensional [vector space over a division ring](../vector-space.md#vector-space-over-a-division-ring). Every nonzero such [endomorphism](../algebra.md#endomorphism) is invertible. Hence

$$
\boxed{R/I\cong\operatorname{End}_\Delta(V)\text{ is a division ring}.}
$$

The use of the [Jacobson density theorem](../noncommutative-algebra.md#jacobson-density-theorem) here only prescribes actions on finitely many vectors; it does not assume that an arbitrary [primitive ring](../noncommutative-algebra.md#primitive-ring) is already a full [endomorphism ring](../module-theory.md#endomorphism-ring).

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

A [multiplicative subset](../commutative-algebra.md#multiplicatively-closed-set) $S$ is a [left Ore set](../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-ore-set) if for every $s\in S$ and $r\in R$ there are $s'\in S$ and $r'\in R$ with

$$
\boxed{s'r=r's.}
$$

Equivalently, the subsets $Sr$ and $Rs$ intersect for each such pair. This is the common-left-multiple condition needed for [Ore localization](../noncommutative-algebra.md#ore-localization) with denominators on the left. In a [noncommutative domain](../noncommutative-algebra.md#noncommutative-domain), no extra condition concerning annihilation by denominators is needed, since all members of $S$ are [regular elements of a ring](../noncommutative-algebra.md#regular-element-of-a-ring).

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

We first prove that any two nonzero principal [left ideals](../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) $Ra$ and $Rb$ intersect nontrivially. Suppose instead that $Ra\cap Rb=0$. The nonzero [left ideals](../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal)

$$
Ra,\quad Rab,\quad Rab^2,\quad\ldots
$$

then form a [direct sum](../vector-space.md#direct-sum). Indeed, take a finite relation

$$
\sum_{i=0}^m u_iab^i=0.
$$

It implies

$$
u_0a=-\left(\sum_{i=1}^m u_iab^{i-1}\right)b\in Ra\cap Rb=0.
$$

As $R$ is a [noncommutative domain](../noncommutative-algebra.md#noncommutative-domain) and $a\ne0$, this gives $u_0=0$. Right cancellation of the nonzero element $b$ reduces the remaining relation to one of the same form with one fewer term. Repetition gives $u_1=\cdots=u_m=0$, proving the asserted [direct sum](../vector-space.md#direct-sum).

Consequently its finite partial sums give a strictly ascending chain of [left ideals](../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal), contradicting the [ascending chain condition](../algebra.md#ascending-chain-condition) in a [left Noetherian ring](../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-noetherian-ring). Thus $Ra\cap Rb\ne0$ whenever $a,b\ne0$. This is the [left Noetherian domain Ore lemma](../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-noetherian-domain-ore-lemma).

Now take $s\in S=R\setminus\{0\}$ and $r\in R$. If $r=0$, choose $s'=1$ and $r'=0$. Otherwise choose

$$
0\ne z\in Rr\cap Rs,\qquad z=s'r=r's.
$$

Since $z\ne0$, the coefficient $s'$ is nonzero and hence belongs to $S$. This proves the [left Ore set](../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-ore-set) condition in all cases.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Use $s=x_1$ and $r=x_2$. A putative [left Ore set](../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-ore-set) equality would be

$$
s'x_2=r'x_1,\qquad s'\in S,\quad r'\in R.
$$

The left side is a single [word](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#string) ending in $x_2$, with coefficient $1$. The right side is a finite [linear combination](../vector-space.md#linear-combination) of [words](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#string) ending in $x_1$. Distinct [words](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#string) are [linearly independent](../vector-space.md#linear-independence) in the [free associative algebra](../associative-algebra.md#free-associative-algebra), so equality is impossible. Therefore

$$
\boxed{S\text{ is not a left Ore set in }\mathbb Q\langle x_1,x_2\rangle.}
$$

The last letters matter because the denominators in the [left Ore set](../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-ore-set) equation occur on the right of the common left multiple.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

View $\hat R$ as a [noncommutative formal power series ring](../associative-algebra.md#noncommutative-formal-power-series-ring), and let $\varepsilon:\hat R\to\mathbb Q$ take the coefficient of the empty [word](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#string). This is a [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism), since the empty [word](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#string) has only the factorization $1\cdot1$. In particular the two-sided [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $I$ generated by $x_1,x_2$ lies in $\ker\varepsilon$.

In fact $I=\ker\varepsilon$. Every [noncommutative formal power series](../associative-algebra.md#noncommutative-formal-power-series) with zero [constant term](../polynomial.md#constant-term) can be partitioned according to the first letter of its [words](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#string) and written

$$
h=x_1h_1+x_2h_2,\qquad h_1,h_2\in\hat R.
$$

Both summands belong to the two-sided [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $I$.

For $a=1+h\in1+I$, define the formal [geometric series](../sequence-and-series.md#geometric-series)

$$
g=\sum_{k=0}^\infty(-h)^k.
$$

This defines an element of $\hat R$: since $h$ has zero [constant term](../polynomial.md#constant-term), every [word](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#string) in $h^k$ has length at least $k$. Thus the coefficient of a fixed [word](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#string) of length $d$ receives contributions only from $0\leq k\leq d$, each a finite sum.

For each finite partial sum $g_N=\sum_{k=0}^N(-h)^k$, [associativity](../algebraic-operation.md#associative-property) gives both identities

$$
(1+h)g_N=g_N(1+h)=1-(-h)^{N+1}.
$$

In every fixed [word](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#string) degree the last term vanishes once $N$ is large enough. It follows coefficient by coefficient that $ag=ga=1$. Hence

$$
\boxed{(1+h)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty(-h)^k\qquad(h\in I).}
$$

The argument is formal and uses no analytic convergence. More generally, every [noncommutative formal power series](../associative-algebra.md#noncommutative-formal-power-series) with a nonzero rational [constant term](../polynomial.md#constant-term) is a [unit](../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring), after factoring out that scalar. This is the [unit criterion for noncommutative formal power series](../associative-algebra.md#unit-criterion-for-noncommutative-formal-power-series).

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

Take $D=\hat R$. We first check that this [noncommutative formal power series ring](../associative-algebra.md#noncommutative-formal-power-series-ring) is a [noncommutative domain](../noncommutative-algebra.md#noncommutative-domain). For nonzero $F,G\in D$, let $p,q$ be the smallest lengths of [words](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#string) with nonzero coefficients in $F,G$, respectively. Their degree-$p$ and degree-$q$ parts $F_p,G_q$ are nonzero [homogeneous polynomials](../algebra.md#homogeneous-polynomial) in the [free associative algebra](../associative-algebra.md#free-associative-algebra).

Choose [words](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#string) $u,v$ with nonzero coefficients in $F_p,G_q$. In $F_pG_q$, the coefficient of $uv$ is the product of those two coefficients: a [word](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#string) of length $p+q$ has a unique splitting into a prefix of length $p$ and a suffix of length $q$. This product is nonzero in $\mathbb Q$. The degree-$p+q$ part of $FG$ is exactly $F_pG_q$, since all lower parts of $F$ and $G$ vanish. Thus $FG\ne0$, proving that $D$ is a [noncommutative domain](../noncommutative-algebra.md#noncommutative-domain).

By the defining property of the [free associative algebra](../associative-algebra.md#free-associative-algebra), there is a [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism)

$$
f:R\longrightarrow D,\qquad f(x_1)=1+x_1,\qquad f(x_2)=2+x_2.
$$

The same substitution defines a [ring automorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-automorphism) of $R$, with inverse $x_1\mapsto x_1-1$, $x_2\mapsto x_2-2$. Composing this [ring automorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-automorphism) with the natural inclusion $R\hookrightarrow D$ proves that $f$ is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function).

Finally,

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x_1+x_2)&=3+x_1+x_2,& f(x_1-x_2)&=-1+x_1-x_2,\\
f(x_1)&=1+x_1,&f(x_2)&=2+x_2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Their [constant terms](../polynomial.md#constant-term) are $3,-1,1,2$, respectively, all nonzero. By the [unit criterion for noncommutative formal power series](../associative-algebra.md#unit-criterion-for-noncommutative-formal-power-series) proved in [c](#2/c), all four elements are [units](../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring). Therefore $D$ and $f$ have every required property. The embedding changes the images of the generators, so the failure of the original monomial [left Ore set](../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-ore-set) in [b](#2/b) presents no obstruction.

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

Use the [Weyl algebra](../noncommutative-algebra.md#weyl-algebra) convention $[y_i,x_j]=\delta_{ij}$, with $[x_i,x_j]=[y_i,y_j]=0$, where the brackets are [commutators](../lie-algebra.md#commutator). Its [order filtration of a Weyl algebra](../noncommutative-algebra.md#order-filtration-of-a-weyl-algebra) is

$$
F_jR=\sum_{|\beta|\leq j}\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]y^\beta\quad(j\geq0),\qquad F_jR=0\quad(j<0).
$$

Thus $x_i$ has degree zero and $y_i$ degree one. By the [ordered monomial basis of a Weyl algebra](../noncommutative-algebra.md#ordered-monomial-basis-of-a-weyl-algebra), the [associated graded ring](../commutative-algebra.md#associated-graded-ring) is

$$
A=\operatorname{gr}R\cong\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n,\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n],\qquad \deg x_i=0,\quad\deg\xi_i=1,
$$

where $\xi_i$ is the symbol of $y_i$.

The [Rees ring of a filtered algebra](../module-theory.md#rees-ring-of-a-filtered-algebra) is the [graded ring](../commutative-algebra.md#graded-ring)

$$
\boxed{\widetilde R=\bigoplus_{j\geq0}(F_jR)t^j\subseteq R[t],}
$$

where $t$ is a central indeterminate. Here it is generated by $x_i$, $Y_i=y_it$, and $t$, with degrees $0,1,1$, respectively, and relations

$$
[Y_i,x_j]=\delta_{ij}t,\qquad [x_i,x_j]=[Y_i,Y_j]=[t,x_i]=[t,Y_i]=0.
$$

It interpolates between the [associated graded ring](../commutative-algebra.md#associated-graded-ring) and the original [filtered ring](../module-theory.md#filtered-ring):

$$
\widetilde R/(t)\cong\operatorname{gr}R,\qquad \widetilde R/(t-1)\cong R.
$$

A [good filtration for the order filtration](../module-theory.md#good-filtration-for-the-order-filtration) on $M$ is an increasing exhaustive family of complex [vector subspaces](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) $(M_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}$, zero for sufficiently negative $j$, with $F_iR\,M_j\subseteq M_{i+j}$, such that the [associated graded module](../commutative-algebra.md#associated-graded-module)

$$
\operatorname{gr}M=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb Z}M_j/M_{j-1}
$$

is a [finitely generated module](../module-theory.md#finitely-generated-module) over $A$. Equivalently, there are finitely many $m_1,\ldots,m_s$ and integer shifts $d_1,\ldots,d_s$ such that $M_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^s(F_{j-d_\ell}R)m_\ell$. In particular, choose any finite generating family $m_1,\ldots,m_s$ of $M$ and put

$$
\boxed{M_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^s(F_jR)m_\ell.}
$$

This is a [good filtration for the order filtration](../module-theory.md#good-filtration-for-the-order-filtration).

For $M\ne0$, its [dimension of a Weyl algebra module for the order filtration](../noncommutative-algebra.md#dimension-of-a-weyl-algebra-module-for-the-order-filtration) is

$$
\boxed{d(M)=\dim\operatorname{Supp}_A(\operatorname{gr}M)
=\dim_{\mathrm{Krull}}\bigl(A/\operatorname{Ann}_A(\operatorname{gr}M)\bigr).}
$$

Here [support of a module](../module-theory.md#support-of-a-module), [annihilator of a module](../module-theory.md#annihilator-of-a-module), and [Krull dimension](../commutative-algebra.md#krull-dimension) are taken over the commutative [polynomial ring](../commutative-algebra.md#polynomial-ring) $A$. The corresponding [algebraic set](../algebraic-geometry.md#algebraic-set) is the [order characteristic variety of a Weyl algebra module](../noncommutative-algebra.md#order-characteristic-variety-of-a-weyl-algebra-module). This dimension is independent of the chosen [good filtration for the order filtration](../module-theory.md#good-filtration-for-the-order-filtration); one may set $d(0)=-\infty$.

The pieces $F_jR$ generally have infinite complex [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space), since they contain all polynomials in the $x_i$. Thus a polynomial-growth definition based directly on $\dim_{\mathbb C}M_j$ would be inappropriate for this [order filtration of a Weyl algebra](../noncommutative-algebra.md#order-filtration-of-a-weyl-algebra).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

For the [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) $N\subseteq M$, define its [subspace filtration](../module-theory.md#subspace-filtration) and the [quotient filtration](../module-theory.md#quotient-filtration) on the [quotient module](../module-theory.md#quotient-module) $Q=M/N$ by

$$
N_j=N\cap M_j,\qquad Q_j=(M_j+N)/N.
$$

They are compatible [filtrations of a module](../module-theory.md#filtration-of-a-module), since multiplication by $F_iR$ sends $N_j$ into $N_{i+j}$ and $Q_j$ into $Q_{i+j}$.

In each degree, inclusion and the quotient map induce

$$
\iota_j:N_j/N_{j-1}\longrightarrow M_j/M_{j-1},\qquad
\pi_j:M_j/M_{j-1}\longrightarrow Q_j/Q_{j-1}.
$$

The first map is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function): if $n\in N_j$ belongs to $M_{j-1}$, then $n\in N\cap M_{j-1}=N_{j-1}$. The second map is [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function) by the definition of $Q_j$.

For exactness in the middle, a class $m+M_{j-1}$ lies in $\ker\pi_j$ precisely when $m\in M_j$ and $m+N\in Q_{j-1}$. This means $m=m'+n$ for $m'\in M_{j-1}$ and $n\in N$. Since $m,m'\in M_j$, one also has $n\in N_j$, and

$$
m+M_{j-1}=n+M_{j-1}\in\operatorname{im}\iota_j.
$$

Conversely, every class from $N_j$ maps to zero in $Q_j/Q_{j-1}$. Taking the [direct sum](../vector-space.md#direct-sum) over all degrees gives the [short exact sequence](../module-theory.md#short-exact-sequence) of [graded modules](../commutative-algebra.md#graded-module)

$$
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}N\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}M
\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}(M/N)\longrightarrow0.
$$

The maps respect the $\operatorname{gr}R$-action because they come from [module homomorphisms](../module-theory.md#module-homomorphism) compatible with the [filtrations of a module](../module-theory.md#filtration-of-a-module). Consequently

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{gr}M/\operatorname{gr}N\cong\operatorname{gr}(M/N)}
$$

as $\operatorname{gr}R$-[modules](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics). This proves [exactness of associated graded modules for induced filtrations](../commutative-algebra.md#exactness-of-associated-graded-modules-for-induced-filtrations).

Furthermore $A=\operatorname{gr}R$ is a [Noetherian ring](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring), and $\operatorname{gr}M$ is a [finitely generated module](../module-theory.md#finitely-generated-module). Its [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) $\operatorname{gr}N$ and its [quotient module](../module-theory.md#quotient-module) $\operatorname{gr}(M/N)$ are therefore [finitely generated modules](../module-theory.md#finitely-generated-module). Hence the induced [subspace filtration](../module-theory.md#subspace-filtration) and [quotient filtration](../module-theory.md#quotient-filtration) are both [good filtrations for the order filtration](../module-theory.md#good-filtration-for-the-order-filtration).

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/c/i)

Use the [quotient filtration](../module-theory.md#quotient-filtration) induced from the [order filtration of a Weyl algebra](../noncommutative-algebra.md#order-filtration-of-a-weyl-algebra). The key step is to identify the entire [associated graded module](../commutative-algebra.md#associated-graded-module) of the [left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) $I$, rather than merely take symbols of its displayed generators.

For every member $x^\alpha y^\beta$ of the [ordered monomial basis of a Weyl algebra](../noncommutative-algebra.md#ordered-monomial-basis-of-a-weyl-algebra), right multiplication by the pure $y$-[monomials](../polynomial.md#monomial) gives

$$
(x^\alpha y^\beta)y_1^3y_2^2=x^\alpha y^{\beta+(3,2,0,\ldots,0)},\qquad
(x^\alpha y^\beta)y_1^2y_3^3=x^\alpha y^{\beta+(2,0,3,0,\ldots,0)}.
$$

No commuting past an $x_i$ is necessary. It follows that $I$ is exactly the complex [linear span](../vector-space.md#linear-span) of the [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) [monomials](../polynomial.md#monomial) satisfying either

$$
\beta_1\geq3,\ \beta_2\geq2,
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\beta_1\geq2,\ \beta_3\geq3.
$$

Intersecting this [linear span](../vector-space.md#linear-span) with each filtration piece and then taking symbols therefore yields the [monomial ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#monomial-ideal)

$$
\operatorname{gr}I=K=(\xi_1^3\xi_2^2,\xi_1^2\xi_3^3)\subseteq A.
$$

By [b](#3/b),

$$
\operatorname{gr}(R/I)\cong A/K.
$$

To determine its [Krull dimension](../commutative-algebra.md#krull-dimension), compute the [radical of an ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#radical-of-an-ideal):

$$
\sqrt K=(\xi_1\xi_2,\xi_1\xi_3)
=(\xi_1)\cap(\xi_2,\xi_3).
$$

Indeed, $K$ lies in the displayed intersection, an intersection of [prime ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal). Conversely, $(\xi_1\xi_2)^3\in K$ and $(\xi_1\xi_3)^3\in K$, so both generators of that intersection lie in $\sqrt K$. The equality of the intersection with $(\xi_1\xi_2,\xi_1\xi_3)$ follows by factoring out $\xi_1$ and reducing modulo $(\xi_2,\xi_3)$.

The [order characteristic variety of a Weyl algebra module](../noncommutative-algebra.md#order-characteristic-variety-of-a-weyl-algebra-module) in this case has two [irreducible components](../algebraic-geometry.md#irreducible-component):

$$
V(K)=\{\xi_1=0\}\ \cup\ \{\xi_2=\xi_3=0\}.
$$

They are coordinate [affine spaces](../geometry-and-topology.md#affine-space) of dimensions $2n-1$ and $2n-2$, respectively, in the $2n$ coordinates $x_1,\ldots,x_n,\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n$. The [Krull dimension](../commutative-algebra.md#krull-dimension) is the larger of these component dimensions. Thus

$$
\boxed{d(R/I)=2n-1.}
$$

In particular, two displayed generators do not force a drop of two in [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space): their common factor creates the component $\xi_1=0$ of dimension $2n-1$.

<h4 id="3/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/c/ii)

In fact $J=R$, so the [quotient module](../module-theory.md#quotient-module) is zero. This requires an argument using [left ideals](../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal): a [left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) is not generally closed under multiplication on the right.

Write $x=x_1$, $y=y_1$, so that $yx-xy=1$. The [commutator product rule](../lie-algebra.md#commutator-product-rule) gives

$$
yx^2=x^2y+2x,\qquad y^2x^2=x^2y^2+4xy+2.
$$

Both $y^2x^2$ and $x^2y^2$ lie in $J=Rx^2+Ry^2$, so their difference

$$
h=y^2x^2-x^2y^2=4xy+2
$$

belongs to $J$. Multiplying on the left and subtracting another known element of the [left ideal](../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) gives

$$
yh-4xy^2=4(yx-xy)y+2y=6y\in J.
$$

Because the scalar $6$ is invertible in $\mathbb C$, it follows that $y\in J$, and hence $xy\in J$. Therefore $h-4xy=2\in J$, giving $1\in J$. We conclude

$$
\boxed{R/J=0,\qquad\dim_{\mathbb C}(R/J)=0.}
$$

Thus the answer is yes for every $n\geq1$. All ideal operations in the proof use left multiplication. In particular, simply replacing $J$ by the commutative [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $(x_1^2,\xi_1^2)$ of the [associated graded ring](../commutative-algebra.md#associated-graded-ring) would miss the lower-order consequences that force $J=R$.
