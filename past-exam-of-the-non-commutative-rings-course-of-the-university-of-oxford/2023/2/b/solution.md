<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use $s=x_1$ and $r=x_2$. A putative [left Ore set](../../../../left-ore-set.md) equality would be

$$
s'x_2=r'x_1,\qquad s'\in S,\quad r'\in R.
$$

The left side is a single [word](../../../../string.md) ending in $x_2$, with coefficient $1$. The right side is a finite [linear combination](../../../../linear-combination.md) of [words](../../../../string.md) ending in $x_1$. Distinct [words](../../../../string.md) are [linearly independent](../../../../linear-independence.md) in the [free associative algebra](../../../../free-associative-algebra.md), so equality is impossible. Therefore

$$
\boxed{S\text{ is not a left Ore set in }\mathbb Q\langle x_1,x_2\rangle.}
$$

The last letters matter because the denominators in the [left Ore set](../../../../left-ore-set.md) equation occur on the right of the common left multiple.
