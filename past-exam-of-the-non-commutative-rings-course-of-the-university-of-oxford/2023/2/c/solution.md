<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

View $\hat R$ as a [noncommutative formal power series ring](../../../../noncommutative-formal-power-series-ring.md), and let $\varepsilon:\hat R\to\mathbb Q$ take the coefficient of the empty [word](../../../../string.md). This is a [ring homomorphism](../../../../ring-homomorphism.md), since the empty [word](../../../../string.md) has only the factorization $1\cdot1$. In particular the two-sided [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) $I$ generated by $x_1,x_2$ lies in $\ker\varepsilon$.

In fact $I=\ker\varepsilon$. Every [noncommutative formal power series](../../../../noncommutative-formal-power-series.md) with zero [constant term](../../../../constant-term.md) can be partitioned according to the first letter of its [words](../../../../string.md) and written

$$
h=x_1h_1+x_2h_2,\qquad h_1,h_2\in\hat R.
$$

Both summands belong to the two-sided [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) $I$.

For $a=1+h\in1+I$, define the formal [geometric series](../../../../geometric-series.md)

$$
g=\sum_{k=0}^\infty(-h)^k.
$$

This defines an element of $\hat R$: since $h$ has zero [constant term](../../../../constant-term.md), every [word](../../../../string.md) in $h^k$ has length at least $k$. Thus the coefficient of a fixed [word](../../../../string.md) of length $d$ receives contributions only from $0\leq k\leq d$, each a finite sum.

For each finite partial sum $g_N=\sum_{k=0}^N(-h)^k$, [associativity](../../../../associative-property.md) gives both identities

$$
(1+h)g_N=g_N(1+h)=1-(-h)^{N+1}.
$$

In every fixed [word](../../../../string.md) degree the last term vanishes once $N$ is large enough. It follows coefficient by coefficient that $ag=ga=1$. Hence

$$
\boxed{(1+h)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty(-h)^k\qquad(h\in I).}
$$

The argument is formal and uses no analytic convergence. More generally, every [noncommutative formal power series](../../../../noncommutative-formal-power-series.md) with a nonzero rational [constant term](../../../../constant-term.md) is a [unit](../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md), after factoring out that scalar. This is the [unit criterion for noncommutative formal power series](../../../../unit-criterion-for-noncommutative-formal-power-series.md).
