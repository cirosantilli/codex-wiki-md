<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [left-primitive ideal](../../../../../primitive-ideal.md) is a two-sided [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) of the form

$$
P=\operatorname{Ann}_R(V)=\{r\in R:rV=0\}
$$

for a nonzero [simple module](../../../../../irreducible-module.md) $V$ on the left. Equivalently, the [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md) $R/P$ has a [faithful module](../../../../../faithful-module.md) that is a [simple module](../../../../../irreducible-module.md), and is therefore a [primitive ring](../../../../../primitive-ring.md).

To prove that $P$ is a [prime ideal of a noncommutative ring](../../../../../prime-ideal-of-a-noncommutative-ring.md), let $A,B$ be two-sided [ideals](../../../../../ideal.md) with $AB\subseteq P$. Since $B$ is two-sided, $BV$ is a [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) of $V$: for $r\in R$, $b\in B$ and $v\in V$, one has $r(bv)=(rb)v\in BV$. If $B\not\subseteq P$, then $BV\ne0$, so simplicity gives $BV=V$. Consequently

$$
AV=A(BV)=(AB)V=0,
$$

and $A\subseteq P$. Thus $AB\subseteq P$ implies $A\subseteq P$ or $B\subseteq P$. Also $P\ne R$, since $1$ acts as the [identity element](../../../../../identity-element.md) on the nonzero [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $V$. These are precisely the required [prime ideal of a noncommutative ring](../../../../../prime-ideal-of-a-noncommutative-ring.md) conditions.
