<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $L$ be any [maximal left ideal](../../../../../maximal-left-ideal.md). If $I\not\subseteq L$, maximality gives $L+I=R$, because the two-sided [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) $I$ is in particular a [left ideal](../../../../../left-ideal.md). Thus $1=\ell+i$ for some $\ell\in L$ and $i\in I$. But

$$
\ell=1-i\in1+I
$$

is a [unit](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) by hypothesis. A proper [left ideal](../../../../../left-ideal.md) cannot contain a [unit](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md), since left multiplication by its inverse would put $1$ in the [left ideal](../../../../../left-ideal.md). This contradiction proves $I\subseteq L$ for every [maximal left ideal](../../../../../maximal-left-ideal.md) $L$, and therefore

$$
\boxed{I\subseteq J(R).}
$$

Together with [ii](../ii.md), this is the ideal form of the [unit criterion for the Jacobson radical](../../../../../unit-criterion-for-the-jacobson-radical.md).
