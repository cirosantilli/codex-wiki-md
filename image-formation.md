# Image formation

↑ **Parent:** [Optics](optics-split.md)

[Image formation](image-formation.md) assigns each object point a corresponding image point: outgoing [light rays](light-ray.md) from that object point either converge there, giving a [real image](real-image.md), or diverge as though they originated there, giving a [virtual image](virtual-image.md). The requirement applies separately to each point. Bringing unrelated [light rays](light-ray.md) together makes a bright patch, but does not preserve the object's pattern. This point-by-point correspondence explains how an [optical camera](optical-camera.md) records a scene. [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, University Physics Volume 3, §2.4: Image formation by thin lenses](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/2-4-thin-lenses).

Use the same convention as [thin lens](thin-lens.md): place the [optical lens](lens-optics.md) at $x=0$, with incident [light](light.md) travelling to the right and a real object at $x=-u$, $u>0$. Write the image coordinate as $x=v$, so $v>0$ for a [real image](real-image.md) and $v<0$ for a [virtual image](virtual-image.md). Heights $h_o,h_i$ are positive above the [optical axis](optical-axis.md) and negative below it. In air, within the [paraxial approximation](paraxial-approximation.md), a [thin lens](thin-lens.md) obeys

$$
\frac1u+\frac1v=\frac1f,\qquad v=\frac{fu}{u-f},\qquad m=\frac{h_i}{h_o}=-\frac vu.
$$

Here $f$ is the signed [focal length](focal-length.md), positive for a converging [optical lens](lens-optics.md) and negative for a diverging [optical lens](lens-optics.md); the expression for $v$ assumes $u\ne f$. Object distance $u$ is positive even though the object's coordinate is negative. These coordinate rules describe transmission through the [optical lens](lens-optics.md); a mirror reverses the outgoing direction, so do not identify its image distance with this same rightward coordinate. [Urone and Hinrichs, College Physics 2e, §25.6: Lens equation and sign conventions](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/25-6-image-formation-by-lenses).

The beauty of [geometrical optics](geometrical-optics-split.md) is that two convenient [light rays](light-ray.md) can locate a point, while a whole admitted bundle forms its image. For a converging [thin lens](thin-lens.md), an incident [light ray](light-ray.md) parallel to the [optical axis](optical-axis.md) exits through the rear [optical focus](focus-optics.md); a [light ray](light-ray.md) through the centre continues undeviated. Real [optical lens](lens-optics.md) elements do not focus every admitted [light ray](light-ray.md) exactly: [optical aberrations](optical-aberration.md) and [diffraction](diffraction.md) limit the point correspondence. The diagrams below are original ideal-model constructions, not measured data. [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, §2.4: Ray tracing and imaging limits](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/2-4-thin-lenses).
