# Distance between parallel lines

↑ **Parent:** [Euclidean geometry](euclidean-geometry-split.md)

For two parallel [lines](straight-line.md) $\mathbf a_1+s\mathbf v$ and $\mathbf a_2+t\mathbf v$ in three-dimensional [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md), with $\mathbf v\ne0$ and $\mathbf d=\mathbf a_2-\mathbf a_1$, their shortest distance is

$$
\boxed{d=\left\|\mathbf d-\frac{\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf v}{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf v}\mathbf v\right\|=\frac{\|\mathbf d\times\mathbf v\|}{\|\mathbf v\|}.}
$$

Every joining displacement is $\mathbf d+(t-s)\mathbf v$. Its component perpendicular to $\mathbf v$ is fixed, and its parallel component can be cancelled by a suitable $t-s$. The [Pythagorean theorem](pythagorean-theorem.md) proves that this perpendicular component has the shortest [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md). The [cross product](cross-product.md) expression follows from $\|\mathbf d\times\mathbf v\|=\|\mathbf d\|\|\mathbf v\|\sin\theta$. For coincident [lines](straight-line.md) the distance is zero. The perpendicular-component formula also applies in any finite-dimensional [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md), where a three-dimensional [cross product](cross-product.md) need not be available.
