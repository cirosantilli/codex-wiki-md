# Statistical learning

↑ **Parent:** [Statistical modelling](statistical-modelling-split.md)

Statistical learning constructs predictive rules from data and studies their [prediction error](prediction-error.md), [generalization](generalization-in-statistical-learning.md), and computational fitting. In [supervised learning](supervised-learning.md), [training data](training-data.md) contain inputs $X$ and a [response variable](response-variable.md) $Y$: [regression analysis](regression-analysis.md) predicts a numerical response, while [classification in statistical learning](classification-in-statistical-learning.md) predicts a label. [Unsupervised learning](unsupervised-learning.md) instead seeks structure without supplied responses, as in [cluster analysis](cluster-analysis.md) and [principal component analysis](principal-component-analysis.md). These tasks connect learning to [probability](probability.md) and [mathematical optimization](mathematical-optimization-split.md). [James, Witten, Hastie and Tibshirani, ISLR chapter 2 slides](https://web.stanford.edu/~hastie/ISLR2/Slides/Ch2_Statistical_Learning.pdf)

For a fitted rule $\widehat f$ and [loss function](loss-function.md) $L$, the predictive goal is

$$
R(\widehat f)=\mathbb E\bigl[L(Y^*,\widehat f(X^*))\mid\mathcal D_n\bigr],
$$

where $(X^*,Y^*)$ is a fresh observation, [independent](independent-random-variables.md) of the [training data](training-data.md) $\mathcal D_n$, from the intended target [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md). This [prediction error](prediction-error.md) fixes the fitted rule and averages over future observations; averaging again over [training data](training-data.md) gives the learning procedure's average performance. Its practical beauty is that a rule compresses past observations into useful future predictions. Its central difficulty is that excellent [training error](training-error.md) need not imply good [generalization](generalization-in-statistical-learning.md): [cross-validation](cross-validation.md) supports [model selection](model-selection.md), and a reserved [test set](test-set.md) assesses the resulting choice.
