# Irrationality of e

↑ **Parent:** [e (mathematical constant)](e-mathematical-constant.md)

If [Euler's number](e-mathematical-constant.md) were $a/b$, choose $n\geq\max(b,2)$ and multiply its factorial-series tail by $n!$. This produces an [integer](integer.md), but the tail is positive and smaller than $\sum_{j\geq1}(n+1)^{-j}=1/n<1$. The contradiction proves that the number is an [irrational number](irrational-number.md). The argument illustrates how a rapidly convergent rational series can [force](force.md) a hypothetical rational remainder into an impossible [integer](integer.md) interval.
