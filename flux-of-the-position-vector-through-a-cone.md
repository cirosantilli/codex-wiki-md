# Flux of the position vector through a cone

↑ **Parent:** [Surface integral](surface-integral.md)

Let a solid [cone](cone-geometry.md) of [volume](volume.md) $V$ have its planar base in a plane through the origin, and orient its curved surface $C$ outwards. The [vector field](vector-field.md) $\mathbf F(\mathbf x)=\mathbf x$ has [divergence](divergence.md) three. Its [flux](flux.md) through the base vanishes because the [position vector](position-vector.md) lies in that plane and is [orthogonal](orthogonal-vectors.md) to its [normal vector](normal-vector.md). The [divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md) therefore gives the displayed curved-surface [flux](flux.md). For a circular base of radius $a$ and perpendicular height $b$, this is $\pi a^2b$.
