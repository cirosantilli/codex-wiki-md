# Gabor filter

↑ **Parent:** [Computational neuroscience](computational-neuroscience.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabor_filter)

A [Gabor filter](gabor-filter.md) has a kernel formed by multiplying a [Gaussian function](gaussian-function.md) by a [sinusoid](sinusoid.md) or a complex oscillation. In two-dimensional [image processing](image-processing.md), its orientation and [spatial frequency](spatial-frequency.md) select local image structure; evaluating the kernel's translated [dot product](dot-product.md) with an image is spatial filtering. The real even example under [signed spatial filter model of a simple cell](signed-spatial-filter-model-of-a-simple-cell.md) uses a [cosine](cosine.md).

There is experimental motivation for this family without making every [simple cell](simple-cell.md) an exact [Gabor filter](gabor-filter.md). Jones and Palmer fitted two-dimensional [Gabor filters](gabor-filter.md) to measured spatial and spectral response profiles of [simple cells](simple-cell.md) in cat cortex and compared independently estimated parameters as a test of spatial [linearity](linearity.md). Their reported residuals supported the approximation in that dataset. The chosen parameters and [orientation tuning curve](orientation-tuning-curve.md) in this article are not extracted from their measurements. [Jones and Palmer, An evaluation of the two-dimensional Gabor filter model of simple receptive fields in cat striate cortex (1987), primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3437332/)
