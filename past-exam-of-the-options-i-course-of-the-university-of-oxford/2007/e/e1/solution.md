<h1 id="e/e1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E1](../e1.md)

The sequence $x_n=1/(n+1)$ lies in the open interval and converges in the ambient real line to zero. Every [subsequence](../../../../subsequence.md) also converges to zero, and uniqueness of limits prevents it converging to any point of the interval. Thus **the interval is not [sequentially compact](../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md)**.

For a sequence $(x_n,y_n)$ in the product, [sequential compactness](../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md) of $X$ supplies indices $n_j$ with $x_{n_j}\to x\in X$. Apply [sequential compactness](../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md) of $Y$ to the corresponding $y_{n_j}$, extracting a further [subsequence](../../../../subsequence.md) $y_{n_{j_\ell}}\to y\in Y$. The first coordinate still tends to $x$. For any $\epsilon>0$, both coordinate distances are eventually less than $\epsilon/2$, so their sum is less than $\epsilon$. Hence the same [subsequence](../../../../subsequence.md) converges in the given product metric to $(x,y)$. This proves **the product is [sequentially compact](../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md)**.
