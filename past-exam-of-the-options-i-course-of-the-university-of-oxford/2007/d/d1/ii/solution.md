<h1 id="d/d1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the nonnegative increasing truncations $f_n=x^{-2}\mathbf1_{[1/n,1]}$. They increase almost everywhere to the specified function. On each [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) interval the [continuous function](../../../../../continuous-function.md) has equal Riemann and Lebesgue integrals, so the [fundamental theorem of calculus](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) gives

$$
\int f_n=\int_{1/n}^1x^{-2}\,dx=n-1.
$$

Monotone convergence yields $\int f=+\infty$. Since a nonnegative function is [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md) exactly when its integral is finite, **this function is not [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md)**.
