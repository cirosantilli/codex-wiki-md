<h1 id="b/1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Realize the cube's vertices as $(\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)$ and take the four with coordinate product $+1$. They form a [regular tetrahedron](../../../../../regular-tetrahedron.md), and their negatives form the other one. Every proper rotation of the first tetrahedron also permutes the negatives, hence is a cube rotation. This embeds $G_1$ as a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) of $G_2$.

The embedded [group](../../../../../group-split.md) preserves each of these two four-vertex sets and acts transitively on each. Thus the resulting action on all eight cube vertices has **two orbits, each of size four**.
