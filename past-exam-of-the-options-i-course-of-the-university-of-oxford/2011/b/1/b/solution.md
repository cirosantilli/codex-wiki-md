<h1 id="b/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a [regular tetrahedron](../../../../../regular-tetrahedron.md), rotations fixing a vertex rotate about the line from that vertex through the opposite face centre. Their angles are $0,2\pi/3,4\pi/3$, so the [stabilizer](../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) is a [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) of order three. Rotations act transitively on the four [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md), giving $|G_1|=4\cdot3=12$ by the [orbit-stabilizer theorem](../../../../../orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md).

For a [cube](../../../../../cube.md), the corresponding axis is the body diagonal through the fixed vertex and its opposite. The three incident edges can be cyclically permuted by the same three angles, so again the [stabilizer](../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) is cyclic of order three. The [orbit](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) has eight vertices. Hence

$$
\boxed{|G_1|=12,\qquad |G_2|=24.}
$$
