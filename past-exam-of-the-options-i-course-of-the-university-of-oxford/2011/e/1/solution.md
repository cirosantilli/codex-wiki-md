<h1 id="e/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

An [open cover](../../../../open-cover.md) of a [topological space](../../../../topological-space.md) is a collection of open subsets whose union is the whole space. A [compact space](../../../../compact-space.md) is one for which every [open cover](../../../../open-cover.md) admits a finite [subcover](../../../../subcover.md). The [subspace topology](../../../../subspace-topology.md) on $A\subseteq Y$ consists of sets $A\cap O$ with $O$ open in $Y$.

Let $\{U_j\}$ cover $f(X)$ by relatively open sets, and write $U_j=f(X)\cap O_j$ with $O_j$ open in $Y$. By [continuity](../../../../continuous-function.md), $\{f^{-1}(O_j)\}$ is an open cover of $X$. A finite subcollection covers $X$ by [compactness](../../../../compact-space.md), and its corresponding $U_j$ cover $f(X)$. Thus **a continuous image of a compact space is compact**.

For an image that is not closed, take $X$ to be a singleton and $Y=\{0,1\}$ with the [indiscrete topology](../../../../indiscrete-topology.md). Send the sole point to $0$. This map is continuous and $X$ is compact, while $f(X)=\{0\}$ is not closed in $Y$, whose only closed sets are $\varnothing$ and $Y$. A [Hausdorff](../../../../hausdorff-space.md) hypothesis on the target would exclude this example.
