<h1 id="e/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $x_n$ converges to $x$ if every open [neighbourhood](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of $x$ contains all sufficiently late terms. A [Hausdorff space](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) separates any two distinct points by disjoint open neighbourhoods.

If a sequence had distinct limits $x,y$ in a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md), choose disjoint open neighbourhoods $U$ of $x$ and $V$ of $y$. Convergence to each limit implies that all sufficiently late terms lie in both $U$ and $V$, contradicting $U\cap V=\varnothing$. Hence **a convergent sequence in a Hausdorff space has a unique limit**.
