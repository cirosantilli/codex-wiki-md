<h1 id="k/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Use the convention that positive vortex strength is counterclockwise and complex velocity is $u-iv$. A [complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow](../../../../complex-potential-of-a-two-dimensional-potential-flow.md) for the superposed [uniform flow](../../../../uniform-flow.md) and two [point vortices](../../../../line-vortex.md) is

$$
\boxed{W(z)=Uz+\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi i}\log(z-a)-\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi i}\log(z+a).}
$$

Local choices of logarithm branch change no velocity. Differentiation gives

$$
W'(z)=U-\frac{i\Gamma a}{\pi(z^2-a^2)}.
$$

For nonzero vortex strength and $U>0$, its two zeros are

$$
\boxed{z_\pm=\pm\sqrt{a^2+\frac{i\Gamma a}{\pi U}}.}
$$

The number under the square root is nonzero, so these are distinct [stagnation points](../../../../stagnation-point.md), neither at a vortex.

A free [point vortex](../../../../line-vortex.md) moves with the velocity induced by the other vortex and the background, omitting its own singular field. At either vortex, the other induces vertical velocity $-\Gamma/(4\pi a)$, while the background contributes $(U,0)$. Their velocities are therefore equal. Their separation stays $(2a,0)$, making this induced velocity constant for all time. Both travel along parallel straight lines with

$$
\boxed{\mathbf V=\left(U,-\frac{\Gamma}{4\pi a}\right),\qquad
|\mathbf V|=\sqrt{U^2+\frac{\Gamma^2}{16\pi^2a^2}}.}
$$
