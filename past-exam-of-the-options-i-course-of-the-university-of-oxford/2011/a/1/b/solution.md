<h1 id="a/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $\alpha$ be a [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) in a [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md). The preceding characteristic-$p$ identity gives $X^p-a=(X-\alpha)^p$, so there is only one distinct [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md). If the [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) were reducible over $F$, choose a monic proper factor $g\in F[X]$ of [degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) $d$, with $1\leq d<p$. In the splitting field it must be $g=(X-\alpha)^d$. Its coefficient of $X^{d-1}$ is $-d\alpha$, which belongs to $F$. Since $d$ is nonzero and thus invertible in the [field](../../../../../field.md) $F$, this forces $\alpha\in F$, contradicting the absence of a [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) there. Therefore **$X^p-a$ is irreducible over $F$**.
