<h1 id="h/h1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For the first particle, the derivative of its [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) about the origin is its [torque](../../../../../torque.md):

$$
\frac d{dt}\left(m_1\mathbf q_1\times\dot{\mathbf q}_1\right)
=\mathbf q_1\times\left(-\frac{f'(r)}r(\mathbf q_1-\mathbf q_2)\right)
=\frac{f'(r)}r\mathbf q_1\times\mathbf q_2.
$$

This need not vanish. For example take $f(r)=r^2/2$ and instantaneous positions $\mathbf q_1=(1,0,0)$, $\mathbf q_2=(0,1,0)$; the [torque](../../../../../torque.md) is $(0,0,1)$. Therefore **it is not conserved in general**, although it is in special configurations or for a constant potential.
