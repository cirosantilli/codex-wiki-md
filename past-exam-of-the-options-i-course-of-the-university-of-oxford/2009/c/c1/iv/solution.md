<h1 id="c/c1/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Because the primes are distinct, [Fermat's little theorem](../../../../../fermat-little-theorem.md) gives $q^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p$ and $p^{q-1}\equiv1\pmod q$. The other term in each modulus vanishes. Therefore

$$
p^{q-1}+q^{p-1}-1\equiv0\pmod p,\qquad
p^{q-1}+q^{p-1}-1\equiv0\pmod q.
$$

The distinct primes are [coprime](../../../../../coprime-integers.md), so their product divides this [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{p^{q-1}+q^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod{pq}}.
$$
