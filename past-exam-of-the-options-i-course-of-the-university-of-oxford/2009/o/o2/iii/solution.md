<h1 id="o/o2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The transformation $N=k/p$ is strictly decreasing for $p>0$. Therefore an [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) $[L,U]$ with $0<L<U\leq1$ transforms to

$$
\boxed{\left[\frac kU,\frac kL\right]}.
$$

The event $L\leq p\leq U$ is exactly the event $k/U\leq N\leq k/L$, so the approximate coverage is inherited. If the lower endpoint for $p$ reaches zero, the transformed upper endpoint for $N$ is $+\infty$; if the upper endpoint reaches one, the lower endpoint is $k$. Finally intersect with [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) values $N>k$, rounding the lower bound upward and the upper bound downward. This respects the original discrete population constraint.
