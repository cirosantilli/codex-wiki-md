<h1 id="e/e2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $m=\min A$, any [open cover](../../../../../../open-cover.md) contains a member containing $m$. Such a member is either $\mathbb R$ or a ray $(a,\infty)$ with $a<m$. It therefore contains every element of $A$, so that single member is already a [subcover](../../../../../../subcover.md).

Conversely suppose $A$ is nonempty and has no smallest element. Then

$$
\{(a,\infty):a\in A\}
$$

is an [open cover](../../../../../../open-cover.md) of $A$: for any $x\in A$, the lack of a minimum gives an $a\in A$ below $x$. A nonempty finite subcollection has [union](../../../../../../set-union.md) $(a_0,\infty)$, where $a_0$ is the smallest of its thresholds. It misses $a_0\in A$. The empty subcollection cannot cover nonempty $A$ either. Hence there is no finite [subcover](../../../../../../subcover.md), and $A$ is not [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md). Thus **[compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) is equivalent to having a smallest element**.
