<h1 id="e/e1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Both $\varnothing$ and $X$ satisfy the condition. For an arbitrary [intersection](../../../../../../set-intersection.md) of members, either every member contains $a$, in which case the [intersection](../../../../../../set-intersection.md) contains $a$, or one does not, in which case that member is countable and so is the [intersection](../../../../../../set-intersection.md). The empty [intersection](../../../../../../set-intersection.md) is $X$.

For a finite [union](../../../../../../set-union.md), either a member contains $a$, so the [union](../../../../../../set-union.md) does too, or all members omit $a$ and are countable, so their finite [union](../../../../../../set-union.md) is countable. Thus the [closed set](../../../../../../closed-set.md) axioms hold and define a [topology](../../../../../../topology-split.md). This is a [Fortissimo space](../../../../../../fortissimo-space.md): every point other than $a$ is isolated, while the [open neighbourhoods](../../../../../../open-neighbourhood.md) of $a$ have countable complement.
