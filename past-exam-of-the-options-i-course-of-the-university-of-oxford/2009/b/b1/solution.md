<h1 id="b/b1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B1](../b1.md)

A [solvable group](../../../../solvable-group.md) has a finite [subnormal series](../../../../subnormal-series.md) from the identity to the [group](../../../../group-split.md) with [abelian group](../../../../abelian-group.md) factors. An equivalent definition uses the [derived series](../../../../derived-series.md): set $G^{(0)}=G$ and $G^{(j+1)}=[G^{(j)},G^{(j)}]$; the series must reach the identity. For [completeness](../../../../completeness.md), an abelian-factor series forces successive derived subgroups into its successive terms, while a terminating [derived series](../../../../derived-series.md) itself supplies such a series.

If $H\leq G$, induction gives $H^{(j)}\leq G^{(j)}$: every [commutator](../../../../commutator.md) of elements of $H^{(j)}$ is also a [commutator](../../../../commutator.md) in $G^{(j)}$. Thus termination for $G$ implies termination for $H$, proving that every [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) of a [solvable group](../../../../solvable-group.md) is solvable.

In $S_4$, use

$$
\{1\}\triangleleft V_4\triangleleft A_4\triangleleft S_4,
\qquad V_4=\{1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}.
$$

The [Klein four-group](../../../../klein-four-group.md) is invariant under [conjugation](../../../../conjugation.md) in $S_4$; the other normality assertions follow from the [alternating group](../../../../alternating-group.md) being the kernel of the sign map. The [quotient group](../../../../quotient-group.md) factors are $V_4,C_3,C_2$, all abelian. Hence **$S_4$ is solvable**.

For the last example take $A_5\times C_2$. Its [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) $\{1\}\times C_2$ is nontrivial, proper and normal, so the product is not a [simple group](../../../../simple-group.md). Projection onto $A_5$ is surjective, and every quotient of a [solvable group](../../../../solvable-group.md) is solvable, because a homomorphism carries each [derived subgroup](../../../../commutator-subgroup.md) onto the [derived subgroup](../../../../commutator-subgroup.md) of its image. But the nonabelian [simple group](../../../../simple-group.md) $A_5$ has $A_5'=A_5$: its [derived subgroup](../../../../commutator-subgroup.md) is nontrivial and normal. Thus the product is **not solvable either**. One standard justification of the simplicity used here is that the nonidentity [conjugacy class](../../../../conjugacy-class.md) sizes of $A_5$ are $15,20,12,12$; no proper nontrivial [union](../../../../set-union.md) with the identity has size dividing $60$, as a [normal subgroup](../../../../normal-subgroup.md) would require by [Lagrange's theorem for finite groups](../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md).
