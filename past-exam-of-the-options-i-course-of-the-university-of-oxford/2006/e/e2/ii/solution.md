↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**False.** Take $X=\mathbb R$ and $A=B=\{0\}$. Both complements are disconnected: their negative and positive halves form a separation. But

$$
(X\times X)\setminus(A\times B)=\mathbb R^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}
$$

is [path-connected](../../../../../path-connected-space.md), hence [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md). To see [path connectedness](../../../../../path-connected-space.md) directly, move any nonzero point radially to its normalization on the unit circle; join the two normalized points by a circular arc; then reverse the radial path for the second point. Every segment of this path avoids the origin. Thus disconnected coordinate complements do not force a disconnected complement of their product.
