↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**False.** In $X=\mathbb R$, take

$$
A=[0,1)\cup\{2\},\qquad B=[1,2].
$$

Then $A\cap B=\{2\}$ and $A\cup B=[0,2]$ are [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md). Nevertheless $A$ is disconnected: $[0,1)$ and $\{2\}$ are disjoint nonempty relatively [open sets](../../../../../open-set.md) partitioning $A$. For instance, intersecting $A$ with $(-1,3/2)$ and $(3/2,3)$ produces this separation. The endpoint missing from $[0,1)$ is supplied by $B$, so the union becomes [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) without forcing $A$ itself to be [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md).
