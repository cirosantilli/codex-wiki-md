<h1 id="b/b1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Since $K$ is an $F$-[vector subspace](../../../../../vector-subspace.md) of the finite-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) $L$, it has a finite $F$-[basis](../../../../../basis.md) $u_1,\ldots,u_m$. Any finite $F$-spanning set for $L$ also spans $L$ over $K$, so choose a finite $K$-[basis](../../../../../basis.md) $v_1,\ldots,v_r$ of $L$.

Every $\ell\in L$ can be expanded first in the $v_j$ and then in the $u_i$, showing that the products $u_i v_j$ span $L$ over $F$. If $\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}u_i v_j=0$ with $c_{ij}\in F$, [independence](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of the $v_j$ over $K$ gives $\sum_i c_{ij}u_i=0$ for every $j$; [independence](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of the $u_i$ then gives every $c_{ij}=0$. The products form an $F$-[basis](../../../../../basis.md), proving the [tower law for field extensions](../../../../../tower-law-for-field-extensions.md):

$$
\boxed{[L:F]=rm=[L:K][K:F]}.
$$
