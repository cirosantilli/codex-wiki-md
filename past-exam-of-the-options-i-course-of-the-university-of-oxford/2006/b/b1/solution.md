<h1 id="b/b1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B1](../b1.md)

Put $E=F(a,b)$. The [tower law for field extensions](../../../../tower-law-for-field-extensions.md) shows that both $m$ and $n$ divide $[E:F]$. Since $m,n$ are [coprime](../../../../coprime-integers.md), their product divides $[E:F]$, so $[E:F]\geq mn$.

The [minimal polynomial](../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) of $b$ over $F$ has degree $n$ and is still an annihilating [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) over $F(a)$. The [minimal polynomial](../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) over $F(a)$ divides it, so

$$
[E:F(a)]\leq n,\qquad [E:F]=[E:F(a)]m\leq mn.
$$

The upper and lower bounds agree:

$$
\boxed{[F(a,b):F]=mn}.
$$

Coprimality is the step forcing the lower bound to equal the product; without it the two generated [fields](../../../../field.md) can overlap substantially.
