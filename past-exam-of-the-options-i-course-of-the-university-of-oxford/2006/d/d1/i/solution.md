<h1 id="d/d1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The Gaussian function is continuous and hence [Lebesgue measurable](../../../../../lebesgue-measurable-set.md). For $|x|\geq2$, $x^2/2\geq|x|$, and therefore

$$
0\leq e^{-x^2/2}\leq\mathbf1_{[-2,2]}(x)+e^{-|x|}.
$$

The right side has finite [Lebesgue integral](../../../../../lebesgue-integral.md): its first term has integral 4 and the second is [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md) by hypothesis. The comparison principle for the [Lebesgue integral](../../../../../lebesgue-integral.md) says that a measurable nonnegative function dominated by an [integrable function](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md) has finite integral. Consequently **the Gaussian function is [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md)**.
