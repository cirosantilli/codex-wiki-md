<h1 id="d/d1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A real measurable function is [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md) precisely when the integral of its absolute value is finite. For every integer $k\geq1$, on $I_k=[k+1/6,k+5/6]$ one has $|\sin\pi x|\geq1/2$ and $x\leq k+1$. Consequently

$$
\int_{I_k}\frac{|\sin\pi x|}{x}\,dx\geq\frac{1}{3(k+1)}.
$$

The intervals have disjoint interiors. Countable additivity of the nonnegative [Lebesgue integral](../../../../../lebesgue-integral.md), or the [monotone convergence theorem](../../../../../monotone-convergence-theorem.md) applied to their finite unions, gives

$$
\int_1^\infty\frac{|\sin\pi x|}{x}\,dx\geq\frac13\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac1{k+1}=\infty.
$$

The [harmonic series](../../../../../harmonic-series.md) diverges, so the function is **not [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md)**. Cancellation between adjacent oscillations cannot give absolute integrability.
