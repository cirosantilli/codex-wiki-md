<h1 id="o/o1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Set $s=S_D/\sqrt{17}$. Symmetry of $t_{16}$ gives $P(T>-1.746)=0.95$, so

$$
P_\mu(\mu<\overline D+1.746s)=0.95.
$$

The requested upper confidence bound is therefore

$$
\boxed{b=\overline D+1.746\frac{S_D}{\sqrt{17}}.}
$$

There is a direction inconsistency in connecting this particular [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) to the stated treatment alternative. Since $D$ is before minus after, a blood-[pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) reduction means $\mu>0$. An upper [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) $(-\infty,b)$ excludes the null value zero only when $b<0$, which instead supports $\mu<0$.

For the requested reduction alternative, use the lower 95% [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md)

$$
\left(\overline D-1.746\frac{S_D}{\sqrt{17}},\infty\right).
$$

It excludes zero in the appropriate direction exactly when

$$
\boxed{\frac{\overline D}{S_D/\sqrt{17}}>1.746.}
$$

This is the correct level-5% paired one-sided $t$ test of no mean change against a reduction. The requested upper bound is valid as a confidence statement, but its ordinary exclusion-of-zero test has the opposite direction.

The sign follows the [duality between confidence intervals and hypothesis tests](../../../../../duality-between-confidence-intervals-and-hypothesis-tests.md) for the [paired t-test](../../../../../paired-t-test.md).
