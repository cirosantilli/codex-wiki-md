<h1 id="e/e2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Take distinct classes $p(x)\neq p(y)$. The points are not related by $R$, so by the defining negation there is a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) $Z$ and a [continuous map](../../../../../../continuous-map.md) $f:X\to Z$ with $f(x)\neq f(y)$. Choose disjoint open neighborhoods $U$ and $V$ of those two values. Their inverse images $A=f^{-1}(U)$ and $C=f^{-1}(V)$ are disjoint open subsets of $X$ and are saturated by part (ii).

Hence $p^{-1}(p(A))=A$ and $p^{-1}(p(C))=C$, making $p(A)$ and $p(C)$ open in the [quotient topology](../../../../../../quotient-topology.md). They contain the chosen classes and are disjoint: an [equivalence class](../../../../../../equivalence-class.md) meeting both would contradict saturation and $A\cap C=\varnothing$. Thus **$X/R$ is Hausdorff**. This quotient is the [Hausdorff reflection by continuous maps](../../../../../../hausdorff-reflection-by-continuous-maps.md); every [continuous map](../../../../../../continuous-map.md) from $X$ to a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) factors uniquely through it.
