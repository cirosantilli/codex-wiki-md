<h1 id="e/e1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $(i)\Rightarrow(ii)$, suppose $B=U\cap F$ with $U$ open and $F$ closed. Since $B\subseteq F$, also $\overline B\subseteq F$. Therefore $U\cap\overline B=B$, showing that $B$ is relatively open in its [closure](../../../../../closure-topology.md).

For $(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)$, write $B=U\cap\overline B$ with $U$ open in $X$. Then $\overline B\setminus B=\overline B\cap(X\setminus U)$ is an intersection of closed sets, hence closed in $X$.

For $(iii)\Rightarrow(i)$, set $F=\overline B$ and $U=X\setminus(\overline B\setminus B)$. The assumed condition makes $U$ open; $F$ is closed and $U\cap F=B$. Thus **the three conditions are equivalent**. They characterize a [locally closed subset](../../../../../locally-closed-subset.md).
