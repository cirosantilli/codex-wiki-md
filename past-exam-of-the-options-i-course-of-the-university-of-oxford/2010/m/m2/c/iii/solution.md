<h1 id="m/m2/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

On the class $\{1,2\}$, the two-step [transition matrix](../../../../../../stochastic-matrix.md) is symmetric with positive entries, so its [stationary distribution](../../../../../../stationary-distribution.md) is $(1/2,1/2)$ and its distribution converges to that law. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}P(X_{2n}=2\mid X_0=1)=\frac12.}
$$

Explicitly the two eigenvalues are $1$ and $1/9$, giving $P(X_{2n}=2\mid X_0=1)=\tfrac12(1-9^{-n})$. The original chain has probability zero of being in state $2$ at odd times, consistent with its period two.
