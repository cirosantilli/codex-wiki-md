<h1 id="a/a1/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Reduce the monic [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) modulo the prime $p$. For every $x\in\mathbb F_p$, $x^p=x$, so

$$
x^p-x-1=-1\neq0.
$$

If the reduction were reducible, a proper monic factorization would, by part (2), force a [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) in $\mathbb F_p$, a contradiction. Its reduction is therefore irreducible of degree $p$. The monic reduction criterion, obtained from [Gauss lemma for polynomials](../../../../../gauss-lemma-for-polynomials.md) by reducing a hypothetical [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) factorization, now proves

$$
\boxed{X^p-X-1\text{ is irreducible in }\mathbb Q[X].}
$$
