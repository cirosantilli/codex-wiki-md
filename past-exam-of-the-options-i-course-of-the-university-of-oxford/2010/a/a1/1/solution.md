<h1 id="a/a1/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

In characteristic $p$, the binomial theorem gives $(\alpha+1)^p=\alpha^p+1$. Hence

$$
(\alpha+1)^p-(\alpha+1)-a=\alpha^p-\alpha-a=0.
$$

Iterating shows that all $\alpha+j$, with $j\in\mathbb F_p$, are roots. They are distinct, since the prime-[field](../../../../../field.md) elements $j$ are distinct. There are $p$ of them, equal to the [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md)'s degree. Thus if $\alpha\in F$,

$$
\boxed{X^p-X-a=\prod_{j\in\mathbb F_p}(X-\alpha-j)}
$$

splits into distinct linear factors in $F[X]$. Equivalently, its derivative is $-1$, so no repeated roots are possible.
