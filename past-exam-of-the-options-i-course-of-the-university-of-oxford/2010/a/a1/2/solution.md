<h1 id="a/a1/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Interpret the displayed factorization as a proper factorization, with $1\leq r\leq p-1$; that restriction is necessary for the conclusion. In a [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md), the monic degree-$r$ factor has $r$ of the distinct roots of the original [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md). Comparing its coefficient of $X^{r-1}$ gives

$$
-a_1=\sum_{j\in J}(\alpha+j)=r\alpha+\sum_{j\in J}j,
$$

for an $r$-element subset $J\subseteq\mathbb F_p$. Both $a_1$ and the final sum lie in $F$. Since $0<r<p$, the element $r$ is invertible in $F$, so

$$
\boxed{\alpha=r^{-1}\left(-a_1-\sum_{j\in J}j\right)\in F.}
$$

Part (1) then gives complete splitting into distinct linear factors. Thus an [Artin–Schreier polynomial](../../../../../artin-schreier-polynomial.md) of this form either is irreducible or splits completely; a trivial factorization with $r=0$ or $r=p$ would not imply a [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) in $F$.
