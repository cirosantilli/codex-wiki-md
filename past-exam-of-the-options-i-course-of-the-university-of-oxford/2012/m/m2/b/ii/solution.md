<h1 id="m/m2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $0\leq x\leq1$, integrate the [joint probability density function](../../../../../../joint-probability-density.md) over its allowed $y$-range:

$$
f_X(x)=\int_0^{1-x}24xy\,dy=12x(1-x)^2.
$$

Outside this interval it is zero. Therefore the [marginal density](../../../../../../marginal-density.md) is

$$
\boxed{f_X(x)=\begin{cases}12x(1-x)^2,&0\leq x\leq1,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}}
$$

Its integral is $12(1/2-2/3+1/4)=1$, also checking the original joint normalization. By symmetry $Y$ has the same marginal density.
