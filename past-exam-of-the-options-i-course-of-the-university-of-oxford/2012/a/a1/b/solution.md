<h1 id="a/a1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The notation $K=F(\delta)$ means that $K$ is the smallest [subfield](../../../../../subfield.md) containing both $F$ and $\delta$, namely the [simple field extension](../../../../../simple-field-extension.md) generated by $\delta$. Its elements can be expressed as rational functions in $\delta$ with coefficients in $F$ and nonzero denominator.

Write $d=\delta^2\in F$. The element $\delta$ is a root of $X^2-d$, so its [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) over $F$ has degree at most two. Since $\delta\notin F$, that degree cannot be one. The degree of the [simple field extension](../../../../../simple-field-extension.md) equals the degree of the [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md), giving

$$
\boxed{[F(\delta):F]=2.}
$$

In particular $1,\delta$ form an $F$-basis, so every element is uniquely $a+b\delta$. This argument also works in characteristic two; separability is not needed for this part.
