<h1 id="e/e2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let $\{B_i:i\in I\}$ and $\{C_j:j\in J\}$ be countable bases of the two factors. The family

$$
\mathcal D=\{B_i\times C_j:(i,j)\in I\times J\}
$$

is countable, since the Cartesian product of two countable sets is countable. It is a [basis of a topology](../../../../../basis-of-a-topology.md) for the [product topology](../../../../../product-topology.md): given a product-open neighbourhood of $(x,y)$, choose a contained open rectangle $U\times V$, then basis members with $x\in B_i\subseteq U$ and $y\in C_j\subseteq V$. Their product contains $(x,y)$ and is contained in the original neighbourhood. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{The product has a countable basis.}}
$$

In standard terminology, a finite product of [second-countable spaces](../../../../../second-countable-space.md) is second-countable.
