<h1 id="e/e2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [product topology](../../../../../product-topology.md) on $X\times Y$ has as a basis all open rectangles

$$
\boxed{U\times V,\qquad U\in\mathcal T_X,\quad V\in\mathcal T_Y.}
$$

Thus a subset $W\subseteq X\times Y$ is open precisely when, around every $(x,y)\in W$, there are open $U\ni x$ and $V\ni y$ with $U\times V\subseteq W$. These rectangles cover the product, and their pairwise intersections are rectangles because $(U\times V)\cap(U'\times V')=(U\cap U')\times(V\cap V')$. Hence they define a topology by part (b).

This is equivalently the coarsest [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md) making the two coordinate [projection maps](../../../../../projection-map.md) continuous: the preimages of factor-open sets are the open strips $U\times Y$ and $X\times V$, whose finite intersections are the basis rectangles.
