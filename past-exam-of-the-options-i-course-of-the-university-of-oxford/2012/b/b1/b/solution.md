↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Given a [group action](../../../../../group-action-split.md), define $\rho(g)(j)=g\cdot j$. Each $\rho(g)$ is a [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md), since $\rho(g^{-1})$ is its inverse. The action law gives, for every $j$,

$$
\rho(gh)(j)=(gh)\cdot j=g\cdot(h\cdot j)=(\rho(g)\rho(h))(j).
$$

Thus $\boxed{\rho:G\to S_n\text{ is a group homomorphism}}$.

Conversely, given a [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) $\rho:G\to S_n$, put $g\cdot j=\rho(g)(j)$. Since a homomorphism sends the identity to the identity permutation, $e\cdot j=j$. Also

$$
(gh)\cdot j=\rho(gh)(j)=\rho(g)(\rho(h)(j))=g\cdot(h\cdot j).
$$

These are exactly the two [group action](../../../../../group-action-split.md) axioms, so the construction defines the required action.
