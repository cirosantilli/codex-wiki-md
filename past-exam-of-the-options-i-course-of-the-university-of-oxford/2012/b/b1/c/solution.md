<h1 id="b/b1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The three nonzero [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) in $\mathbb F_2^2$ are $e_1$, $e_2$ and $e_1+e_2$. An invertible [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md) permutes this three-element set, so part (b) gives a [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md)

$$
\rho:GL_2(\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow S_3.
$$

If a matrix acts trivially on all three vectors, it fixes both basis vectors $e_1,e_2$, so it is the identity matrix. Thus the [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) is trivial and $\rho$ is injective.

To count the domain, its first column may be any of the three nonzero vectors. The second column must be outside the two-element span of the first, leaving two choices. Hence

$$
|GL_2(\mathbb F_2)|=3\cdot2=6=|S_3|.
$$

An injection between these finite sets of equal size is surjective, proving

$$
\boxed{GL_2(\mathbb F_2)\cong S_3.}
$$
