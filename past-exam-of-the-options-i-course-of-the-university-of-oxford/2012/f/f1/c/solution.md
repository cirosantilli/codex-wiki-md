<h1 id="f/f1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $L(x,y,z)=(x-y,y-z,z-x)$. This is a [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md), hence smooth; composing it with the given $C^1$ function proves that $g=f\circ L$ is [continuously differentiable](../../../../../continuously-differentiable-function.md) by the [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md). Write $f_1,f_2,f_3$ for the first [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md), evaluated at $L(p)$. Then

$$
g_x=f_1-f_3,\qquad g_y=-f_1+f_2,\qquad g_z=-f_2+f_3.
$$

Adding these identities gives

$$
\boxed{g_x(p)+g_y(p)+g_z(p)=0.}
$$

Geometrically, $L(p+t(1,1,1))=L(p)$, so $g$ is constant along every line in the diagonal direction; its [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) in that direction must vanish.
