<h1 id="p/p1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take any $q\in\Pi_n$. Write $f-q=(f-p_n)+(p_n-q)$. The second term belongs to $\Pi_n$, so the assumed orthogonality gives $\langle f-p_n,p_n-q\rangle=0$. Expanding the squared [norm](../../../../../norm.md) yields the [Pythagorean theorem](../../../../../pythagorean-theorem.md) in this [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md):

$$
\|f-q\|^2=\|f-p_n\|^2+\|p_n-q\|^2\geq\|f-p_n\|^2.
$$

Taking square roots proves

$$
\boxed{p_n\text{ is a best least-squares approximation}.}
$$

Equality can hold only when $\|p_n-q\|=0$, hence $p_n=q$, so the best polynomial is unique. Completeness of the ambient space is unnecessary for this orthogonality argument.
