↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [inverse function theorem](../../../../../inverse-function-theorem.md) states: if $f$ is continuously differentiable on an open subset of $\mathbb R^n$ and $Df(a)$ is invertible, then some [open neighbourhood](../../../../../open-neighbourhood.md) of $a$ is mapped bijectively onto an [open neighbourhood](../../../../../open-neighbourhood.md) of $f(a)$, with continuously differentiable inverse. Its derivative at $f(a)$ is $(Df(a))^{-1}$.

The present map is smooth, and at $a=(\pi/2,\pi)$,

$$
Df(a)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-e^{\pi/2}\\-e^{-\pi}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\det Df(a)=-e^{-\pi/2}\ne0.
$$

Also $f(a)=(0,0)$, so the theorem applies locally and gives

$$
\boxed{D(f^{-1})(0,0)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-e^{\pi}\\-e^{-\pi/2}&0\end{pmatrix}}.
$$

The inverse is defined near the image point $(0,0)$ and takes values near $a$; no assertion of a global inverse is required.
