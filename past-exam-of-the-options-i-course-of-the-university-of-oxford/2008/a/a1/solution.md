<h1 id="a/a1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A1](../a1.md)

Fix $\omega_1$. For any $u,v\ne\omega_1$, apply the given two-point transitivity property to $(\omega_1,u)$ and $(\omega_1,v)$. The resulting element fixes $\omega_1$ and sends $u$ to $v$, so the [stabilizer](../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) is transitive on the complement.

The whole group is transitive on $\Omega$: extend any desired first-coordinate pair by choosing a different second point on each side. By the [orbit-stabilizer theorem](../../../../orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md), $|G|=n|\operatorname{Stab}_G(\omega_1)|$. Applying the same theorem inside the [stabilizer](../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) to its [orbit](../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) of size $n-1$ shows that $n-1$ divides the [stabilizer](../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) order. Hence

$$
\boxed{n(n-1)\mid|G|}.
$$

In the action on ordered pairs there are exactly **two orbits**. One is the diagonal, transitive because the action on $\Omega$ is transitive. The other comprises distinct-coordinate pairs and is transitive by hypothesis. Equality of the two coordinates is preserved by every group element, so the two sets cannot merge into one [orbit](../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md). Faithfulness of the action is not required.
