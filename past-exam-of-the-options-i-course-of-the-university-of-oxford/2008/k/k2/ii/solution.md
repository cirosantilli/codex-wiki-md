<h1 id="k/k2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write the surface displacement and the perturbation [velocity potential](../../../../../velocity-potential.md) as small quantities about rest, and drop their quadratic products. Evaluating the remaining surface terms at the unperturbed level $y=0$ gives

$$
\boxed{\phi_{xx}+\phi_{yy}=0\quad(-H<y<0)},
$$



$$
\boxed{\eta_t=\phi_y,\qquad\phi_t+g\eta=0\quad(y=0),\qquad\phi_y=0\quad(y=-H)}.
$$

The velocity components are $u=\phi_x$, $v=\phi_y$. The shift of the evaluation point from zero to $\eta$ contributes a product of perturbations, as do $u\eta_x$ and the kinetic-[energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) term, so they disappear at this order.
