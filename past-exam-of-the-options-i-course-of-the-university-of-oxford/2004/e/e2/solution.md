<h1 id="e/e2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E2](../e2.md)

The [product topology](../../../../product-topology.md) is generated by the [basis of a topology](../../../../basis-of-a-topology.md) of open rectangles $U\times V$, with $U$ open in $X$ and $V$ open in $Y$. For a fixed $y$,

$$
 i_y^{-1}(U\times V)=\begin{cases}U,&y\in V,\\\varnothing,&y\notin V.\end{cases}
$$

Both possibilities are open; checking this [basis of a topology](../../../../basis-of-a-topology.md) proves that $i_y$ is [continuous](../../../../continuous-function.md). The analogous map $j_x:Y\to X\times Y$, $j_x(y)=(x,y)$, is [continuous](../../../../continuous-function.md) by the same argument.

If either factor is empty the product is empty and [connected](../../../../connected-space.md). Otherwise let $h:X\times Y\to\{0,1\}$ be [continuous](../../../../continuous-function.md). For each $y$, $h\circ i_y$ is constant since $X$ is [connected](../../../../connected-space.md). Thus $h(x,y)=h(x_0,y)$ for any fixed $x_0\in X$. The right-hand side is $h\circ j_{x_0}$ and is constant in $y$ since $Y$ is [connected](../../../../connected-space.md). Therefore $h$ is constant on the whole product. The given characterization proves **$X\times Y$ is [connected](../../../../connected-space.md)**.
