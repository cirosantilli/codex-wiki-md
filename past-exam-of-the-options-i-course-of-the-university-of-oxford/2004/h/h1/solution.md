<h1 id="h/h1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [H1](../h1.md)

The [inertia tensor](../../../../inertia-tensor.md) is $I_{ij}=\int(r^2\delta_{ij}-x_ix_j)\,dm$. The mixed moments vanish by reflection in $x$ or $y$. Every diagonal integrand is even in $z$, so its [integral](../../../../integral-split.md) over the upper hemisphere is half its [integral](../../../../integral-split.md) over the corresponding full ball of mass $2M$. A uniform ball's coordinate moments are each $(2M)a^2/5$, obtained by spherical symmetry and $\langle r^2\rangle=3a^2/5$. Hence the hemisphere has each coordinate [second moment](../../../../second-moment.md) $Ma^2/5$ and

$$
\boxed{I_O=\frac25Ma^2I.}
$$

The tensor [parallel axis theorem](../../../../parallel-axis-theorem.md) gives $I_O=I_C+M(|d|^2I-dd^t)$ with $d=(0,0,3a/8)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{I_C=Ma^2\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{83}{320},\frac{83}{320},\frac25\right).}
$$

Translation along the symmetry axis changes the two transverse moments but leaves the axial one unchanged.
