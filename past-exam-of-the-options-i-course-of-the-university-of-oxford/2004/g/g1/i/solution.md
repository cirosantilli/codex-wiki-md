<h1 id="g/g1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Multiply the equation for $y_i$ by $y_j$ and the equation for $y_j$ by $y_i$, then subtract. The $q$ terms cancel and the [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) terms combine into a [Wronskian](../../../../../wronskian.md) boundary [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md):

$$
\frac d{dx}[p(y_jy_i'-y_iy_j')]+(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)wy_iy_j=0.
$$

Integrating over the interval eliminates the boundary term because both [eigenfunctions](../../../../../eigenfunction.md) vanish at both endpoints. Since their [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) differ,

$$
\boxed{\int_0^1w(x)y_i(x)y_j(x)\,dx=0.}
$$

This is [orthogonality](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) in a [weighted inner product](../../../../../weighted-inner-product.md) in the regular Sturm-Liouville setting; the endpoint values must justify the [integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md).
