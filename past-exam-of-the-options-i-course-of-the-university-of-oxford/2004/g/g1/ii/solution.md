<h1 id="g/g1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $\lambda=0$, only a linear function can solve the equation, and zero endpoint values make it zero. If $\lambda<0$, the corresponding hyperbolic-sine solution also cannot vanish at both endpoints unless it is zero. For $\lambda=k^2>0$, the first endpoint gives $y=C\sin kx$, and the second requires $k=n\pi$ for a positive [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) $n$. Thus $\boxed{\lambda_{\min}=\pi^2}$.

The admissible trial function $y=\sin\pi x$ has quotient

$$
\frac{\int_0^1\pi^2\cos^2\pi x\,dx}{\int_0^1\sin^2\pi x\,dx}=\pi^2.
$$

Therefore the minimum, or infimum before its attainment is established, is $\boxed{\leq\pi^2}$ as requested. The sharp [Poincaré inequality](../../../../../poincare-inequality.md) supplies the reverse inequality on the finite-energy zero-trace class, so the exact minimum is also $\pi^2$ and is attained by this trial function. The one-sided assertion in the source does not require a full variational existence argument.
