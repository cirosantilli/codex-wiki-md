<h1 id="p/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If $p$ and $q$ both interpolate the data and have degree at most $n$, the [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $r=p-q$ has the $n+1$ distinct [roots](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) $x_0,\ldots,x_n$. A nonzero [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) of degree at most $n$ has at most $n$ roots: repeated use of the [factor theorem](../../../../../factor-theorem.md) would otherwise make a product of $n+1$ linear factors divide it. Hence $r=0$, proving **the interpolating [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is unique**.
