<h1 id="a/1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [tower law for field extensions](../../../../../tower-law-for-field-extensions.md) states that, for $F\subseteq K\subseteq L$ with both successive degrees finite,

$$
\boxed{[L:F]=[L:K][K:F].}
$$

Indeed, if $u_i$ form an $F$-[basis](../../../../../basis.md) of $K$ and $v_j$ form a $K$-basis of $L$, their products $u_iv_j$ form an $F$-basis of $L$: expansion gives spanning, and independence follows first over $K$ and then over $F$.

Starting from a unit segment, every [straightedge and compass construction](../../../../../straightedge-and-compass-construction-split.md) introduces coordinates by solving linear or quadratic equations over the coordinates already available. Thus a [constructible number](../../../../../constructible-number.md) belongs to a tower of [field extensions](../../../../../field-extension.md) whose successive degrees are one or two; the total degree is a power of two. Doubling the volume of a unit cube would require the edge length $\sqrt[3]2$. Its [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) over $\mathbb Q$ is $x^3-2$, irreducible by [Eisenstein's criterion](../../../../../eisenstein-criterion.md) at the [prime number](../../../../../prime-number-split.md) two. Thus $[\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2):\mathbb Q]=3$. If that number lay in a construction [field](../../../../../field.md) of degree $2^r$, the [tower law](../../../../../tower-law.md) would make three divide $2^r$, a contradiction. Therefore **doubling the cube by straightedge and compass is impossible**.
