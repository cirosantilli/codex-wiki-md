<h1 id="f/1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [total derivative](../../../../../../total-derivative.md) of $f$ at $x$ is a [linear map](../../../../../../linear-map.md) $Df(x):\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ satisfying

$$
\boxed{\lim_{h\to0}\frac{|f(x+h)-f(x)-Df(x)h|}{\|h\|}=0.}
$$

The [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) is the [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) of [partial derivatives](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md), $\nabla f(x)=(\partial_1f(x),\ldots,\partial_nf(x))$. When $f$ is totally differentiable, these [partial derivatives](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md) exist and

$$
\boxed{Df(x)h=\nabla f(x)\cdot h=\sum_{j=1}^n\partial_jf(x)h_j.}
$$

Thus the derivative is a linear functional represented by the [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) under the [Euclidean inner product](../../../../../../dot-product.md). Existence of [partial derivatives](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md) alone does not imply total differentiability.
