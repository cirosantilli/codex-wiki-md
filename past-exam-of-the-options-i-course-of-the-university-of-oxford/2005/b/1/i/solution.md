<h1 id="b/1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An element is algebraic over $F$ when some nonzero [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) in $F[x]$ vanishes at it. Its [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) is the [monic polynomial](../../../../../monic-polynomial.md) of least positive [degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) with this property; scaling a least-[degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) nonzero vanishing [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) makes it monic.

If $m_1,m_2$ are two such [polynomials](../../../../../polynomial-split.md), they have the same least [degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md). Their difference has smaller [degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) because both are monic, and it also vanishes at $a$. Minimality forces the difference to be zero. Hence **the monic [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) is unique**.
