<h1 id="e/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

A [topology](../../../../topology-split.md) on $X$ contains $\varnothing,X$, is closed under arbitrary unions and under finite intersections. The cofinite family has these properties: a nonempty union contains one cofinite member and therefore has finite complement; the complement of a finite intersection is a finite union of finite sets. The empty union and the sets $X,\varnothing$ are included explicitly.

Any two nonempty [open sets](../../../../open-set.md) intersect, because their intersection has finite complement in the infinite set $X$. Thus no two disjoint nonempty [open sets](../../../../open-set.md) can separate $X$, proving **[connectedness](../../../../connected-space.md)**. For [compactness](../../../../compact-space.md), take any [open cover](../../../../open-cover.md) and choose a nonempty member $U$. Only finitely many points lie outside $U$. Choose one covering member for each of those points. Together with $U$ these give a [finite subcover](../../../../finite-subcover.md). Thus **$X$ is compact**.
