↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Pearson's statistic is

$$
\boxed{X^2=\sum_{i=1}^4\sum_{j=1}^3\frac{(O_{ij}-E_{ij})^2}{E_{ij}}.}
$$

For [independent](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) observations under the [independence](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) [null hypothesis](../../../../../null-hypothesis.md), its large-sample null distribution is approximately $\chi^2$ with $(4-1)(3-1)=6$ [statistical degrees of freedom](../../../../../statistical-degrees-of-freedom.md). The fitted row and column probabilities account for the reduction in [statistical degrees of freedom](../../../../../statistical-degrees-of-freedom.md). All [expected counts](../../../../../expected-frequency.md) exceed five, so the usual approximation has adequate expected cell sizes here. Reject [independence](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) when the statistic exceeds the corresponding upper-tail [critical value](../../../../../critical-value.md).
