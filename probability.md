# Probability

↑ **Parent:** [Probability theory](probability-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability)

The probability of an [event](event.md) is its value under a [probability measure](probability-measure.md), a [real number](real-number.md) between zero and one. It compares the mass assigned to that [event](event.md) with the unit mass of the whole [sample space](sample-space.md). For a finite [sample space](sample-space.md) with equally likely outcomes, $\mathbb P(A)=|A|/|\Omega|$. For unequal outcome masses, add their individual [probabilities](probability.md) instead.

For example, selecting a coin with head [probability](probability.md) $1/2$ with chance $4/5$, or a coin with head [probability](probability.md) $3/4$ with chance $1/5$, gives head [probability](probability.md) $(4/5)(1/2)+(1/5)(3/4)=11/20$. This is the [law of total probability](law-of-total-probability.md): averaging over the hidden selection is part of calculating the outcome's [probability](probability.md).
