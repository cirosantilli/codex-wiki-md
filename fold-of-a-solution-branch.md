# Fold of a solution branch

↑ **Parent:** [Continuation method](continuation-method.md)

A nondegenerate [fold of a solution branch](fold-of-a-solution-branch.md) in a smooth scalar equation satisfies the displayed conditions. The [implicit function theorem](implicit-function-theorem.md) still gives $\lambda=\lambda(x)$ because $F_\lambda\ne0$. [Implicit differentiation](implicit-differentiation.md) gives $\lambda'(x_*)=0$ and $\lambda''(x_*)=-F_{xx}/F_\lambda\ne0$: the parameter has a genuine local extremum on the [curve](curve.md).

In the cubic [polynomial](polynomial-split.md), $F_x=0$ gives $x_*=\pm1/\sqrt3$, and substituting into $F=0$ gives $\lambda_*=2x_*/3$. Furthermore, $F_{xx}=6x_*\ne0$. Yet $\nabla F=(3x^2-1,1)$ never vanishes, so the full [solution branch](solution-branch.md) remains a [regular level set](regular-level-set.md) everywhere. The obstruction belongs to the projection onto $\lambda$, not to the [curve](curve.md) itself.

For systems, the cited continuation account uses a one-dimensional [kernel](kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) of $F_u$ and $F_\lambda$ outside its [image of a linear map](image-of-a-linear-map.md) to define a simple fold (its Definition 2.2). These conditions make $[F_u\ F_\lambda]$ have full [rank](rank-one-quadratic-form.md) and support a transverse correction. A nonzero second-order term along the [kernel](kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) is additionally needed to describe a nondegenerate quadratic turning point; in the scalar example this is $F_{xx}\ne0$.[§2.2, Definition 2.2, printed p. 265: one-dimensional kernel of the state Jacobian and parameter derivative outside its range](condition-estimates-for-pseudo-arclength-continuation-by-k-i-dickson-and-others.md)
