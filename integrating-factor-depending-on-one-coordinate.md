# Integrating factor depending on one coordinate

↑ **Parent:** [Integrating factor for a differential one-form](integrating-factor-for-a-differential-one-form.md)

For a [differential one-form](one-form.md) $P\,dx+Q\,dy$, seek a nonzero [integrating factor](integrating-factor.md) $\mu(x)$. Expanding $(\mu P)_y=(\mu Q)_x$ gives the displayed equation where $Q\ne0$. Such a factor exists locally when $(P_y-Q_x)/Q$ depends only on $x$, and is $\mu=\exp\int[(P_y-Q_x)/Q]dx$. Likewise, a factor depending only on $y$ obeys $\mu'/\mu=(Q_x-P_y)/P$ where $P\ne0$. The resulting product is an [exact differential form](exact-differential-form.md) on a sufficiently small regular domain; singular lines of the factor need separate treatment.
