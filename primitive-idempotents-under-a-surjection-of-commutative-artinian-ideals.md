# Primitive idempotents under a surjection of commutative Artinian ideals

↑ **Parent:** [Primitive idempotent](primitive-idempotent.md)

Let $A,B$ be finite-dimensional commutative [algebras](algebra-split.md), let $I\subset A$ and $J\subset B$ be [ideals](ideal.md), and let $f:I\to J$ be a surjective algebra homomorphism. The ideals need not have identities. Then $f$ gives a bijection between the [primitive idempotents](primitive-idempotent.md) of $A$ lying in $I$ with nonzero image and the primitive idempotents of $J$.

Decompose $A$ into its commutative [Artinian local rings](artinian-local-ring.md) $A_i$, with identities $e_i$. Each $I\cap A_i$ is either all of $A_i$, in which case $e_i\in I$, or a proper ideal and therefore a [nilpotent ideal](nilpotent-ideal.md). The sum of the latter ideals is nilpotent, so its image contributes no idempotents. Each nonzero $f(e_i)$ has local corner algebra $f(e_i)J=f(A_i)$, a quotient of $A_i$, so is primitive. These idempotents are orthogonal, and the remaining part of $J$ is nilpotent; hence they are exactly its primitive idempotents. This statement does not require the entire kernel of $f$ to be nilpotent.
