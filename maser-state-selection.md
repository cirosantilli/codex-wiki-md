# Maser state selection

↑ **Parent:** [Ammonia maser](ammonia-maser.md)

Maser state selection separates paths according to the [Stark effect](stark-effect.md), the dependence of a [molecule](molecule-split.md)'s [energy levels](energy-level.md) on an [electric field](electric-field-split.md). It prepares a transmitted population by sorting. [Optical pumping](optical-pumping.md), by contrast, changes populations through absorption of [light](light.md) and subsequent transitions. Both can produce a useful population imbalance, but they are different preparation mechanisms.

A simple [two-level system](two-level-system.md) explains the sign of the focusing force. Ignore additional splittings and let the magnitude of the relevant projected [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) be $d$. Model the two local [energy levels](energy-level.md) in an [electric field](electric-field-split.md) of magnitude $\mathcal E$ by

$$
W_\pm(\mathcal E)=\pm\sqrt{(h\nu_0/2)^2+d^2\mathcal E^2}.
$$

For an ideal quadrupolar [electrostatic potential](electric-potential.md) $\Phi=A(x^2-y^2)$, the [electric field](electric-field-split.md) magnitude is $\mathcal E=2|A|r$, where $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$. Since $W_+$ increases with $r$, the [force](force-split.md) $\boldsymbol F_+=-\nabla W_+$ points inward. The lower branch has the opposite sign and is defocused. The upper branch is thus low-field seeking. This argument uses the [gradient](gradient.md) of internal [energy](energy-split.md), rather than a net electric charge on the [molecule](molecule-split.md). The square-root splitting is the usual two-state description.[§9–2, Eq. (9.30)](chapter-9-the-ammonia-maser.md) The idealized [two-level system](two-level-system.md) omits the actual rotational and [hyperfine structure](hyperfine-structure.md) sublevels and their different transmission efficiencies.
