# IID does not imply finite moments

↑ **Parent:** [Independent and identically distributed random variables](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md)

The [IID sample](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) property places no integrability condition on its common [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md). An [IID sample](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) with the standard [Cauchy distribution](cauchy-distribution.md) has no finite [expected value](expected-value-split.md) or [variance](variance-split.md). The [Cauchy sample mean stability](cauchy-sample-mean-has-an-unchanged-distribution.md) result shows that its averages keep that same [Cauchy distribution](cauchy-distribution.md) for every sample size. Thus increasing $n$ need not concentrate an [IID sample](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) around a population [mean](expected-value-split.md) that does not exist. The finite-variance bound below applies only when its hypotheses hold.
