<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

If $p\nmid n$, the preceding part says $U=S_F^{(n-1,1)}$ is simple and has no nonzero [homomorphism](../../../../homomorphism.md) into $W$. Its only nonzero simple [quotient](../../../../quotient.md) is itself, so $W$ cannot contain a [submodule](../../../../submodule.md) isomorphic to such a [quotient](../../../../quotient.md).

If $p\mid n$, use the given nonzero [homomorphism](../../../../homomorphism.md) $\phi:U\to W$. The [submodule](../../../../submodule.md) chain shows that $T$ is the unique maximal proper [submodule](../../../../submodule.md) of $U$ and $U/T$ is simple. Moreover $\operatorname{Hom}_{S_n}(T,W)=0$: for odd [characteristic](../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) this follows from part (b), and in [characteristic two](../../../../characteristic-two.md) the divisibility condition makes $n$ even, where the corrected fixed-space calculation also gives zero. Thus $\phi$ kills $T$ and descends to a nonzero map $U/T\to W$. Simplicity makes this map [injective](../../../../injective-function.md).

Its [image](../../../../image-of-a-function.md) is the required simple [submodule](../../../../submodule.md), proving

$$
\boxed{W\text{ contains a simple quotient of }U\iff p\mid n.}
$$

In the divisible case the [quotient](../../../../quotient.md) is explicitly $U/T$, of [dimension](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) $n-2$. The argument uses the supplied existence of $\phi$ and only the fixed-space vanishing that remains valid in that case.
