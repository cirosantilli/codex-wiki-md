# Square-gradient theory of a liquid interface

↑ **Parent:** [Landau-Ginzburg theory](landau-ginzburg-theory.md)

A diffuse-interface [free energy](thermodynamic-free-energy.md) balances the bulk preference for coexisting compositions against a positive penalty for sharp composition gradients. For dimensionless composition $\phi$, $f_b$ is a bulk energy density and $K$ has units of energy per length. Subtract the bulk coexistence common tangent to define $\Delta\omega(\phi)$, with equal zero minima at the two compositions. A planar equilibrium profile obeys $K\phi''=\Delta\omega'(\phi)$ and $K(\phi')^2/2=\Delta\omega(\phi)$. Its [surface tension](surface-tension-split.md) is $\int K(\phi')^2dz$. As a [thermodynamic critical point](thermodynamic-critical-point.md) is approached, the composition contrast and bulk curvature vanish, the [correlation length](correlation-length.md) grows, and the intrinsic interface broadens. A [scalar quartic domain wall](scalar-quartic-domain-wall.md) gives the mean-field hyperbolic-tangent profile. Thermally excited [capillary waves](capillary-wave.md) can broaden a measured profile further.
