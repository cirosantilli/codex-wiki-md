<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For an incompressible Newtonian fluid at low [Reynolds number](../../../../reynolds-number.md), the quasi-steady [Stokes equations](../../../../stokes-equation.md) are

$$
\boxed{\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=0,\qquad
-\nabla p+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf u+\rho\mathbf g=0,\qquad \mu=\rho\nu.}
$$

The first equation expresses local conservation of volume for constant density; the second balances [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md), viscous stresses and body force with negligible inertia. Convective inertia is negligible when $\mathrm{Re}=U\ell/\nu\ll1$ at the relevant flow scale. Quasi-steady flow also requires the viscous diffusion time $\ell^2/\nu$ to be short compared with the evolution time. Low convective [Reynolds number](../../../../reynolds-number.md) alone does not justify neglecting a rapidly oscillating unsteady acceleration.

For a thin layer, slenderness further changes the comparison of longitudinal and transverse derivatives; these scalings, as well as low inertia, are needed for [lubrication theory](../../../../lubrication-theory.md).
