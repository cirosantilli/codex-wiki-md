[Intrinsic gene-expression noise](../../../../intrinsic-gene-expression-noise.md) is variability from the random reaction events of the expression process itself, conditional on the cell's shared state: transcript production and removal, translation events and [protein](../../../../protein-split.md) loss. It remains possible in nominally identical cells in the same environment and is particularly visible when molecule numbers are small.

[Extrinsic gene-expression noise](../../../../extrinsic-gene-expression-noise.md) comes from fluctuations in shared cellular conditions, such as cell size, growth state, expression machinery abundance or environmental signals. It can correlate otherwise distinct expression processes. In a matched two-reporter measurement, reporter differences reveal an intrinsic component while correlated cell-to-cell changes reveal a shared component, after reporter-specific measurement noise is accounted for.[Matched fluorescent reporters separate within-cell reporter differences from correlated cell-to-cell variation](../../../../stochastic-gene-expression-in-a-single-cell-by-michael-b-elowitz-and-others.md)

Intrinsic fluctuations can be beneficial when a population gains a diversity of temporary physiological states, or when a noisy threshold crossing helps a cell explore or select a state. They can be detrimental when an essential product transiently falls too low, when tightly coordinated components become imbalanced, or when a regulatory threshold is crossed at an inappropriate time. These are context-dependent effects: noise is not automatically adaptive, and increasing a [Fano factor](../../../../fano-factor.md) is not synonymous with improving a cellular function.
