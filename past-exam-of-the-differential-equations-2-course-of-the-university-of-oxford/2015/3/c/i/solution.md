<h1 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a positive [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) write $\mu=\sqrt\lambda>0$. The [general solution](../../../../../general-solution.md) of the limiting [constant-coefficient differential equation](../../../../../constant-coefficient-differential-equation.md) is $y=A\cos(\mu x)+B\sin(\mu x)$. The endpoint at zero gives $A=0$. A nonzero [eigenfunction](../../../../../eigenfunction.md) then requires $B\ne0$, and the endpoint at $\pi$ gives $\sin(\mu\pi)=0$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\lambda_k=k^2,\qquad y_k(x)=C_k\sin(kx),\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,\quad C_k\ne0.}
$$

For real [eigenfunctions](../../../../../eigenfunction.md) the constants may be any nonzero real amplitudes. Requiring unit [L2 norm](../../../../../l2-norm.md) would set $|C_k|=\sqrt{2/\pi}$, but that normalization is not required by the question. The lowest [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is $\lambda_1=1$ and its [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) is spanned by $\sin x$. For completeness, zero yields only an affine solution annihilated by both endpoints, and negative [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) yield [hyperbolic functions](../../../../../hyperbolic-function.md) whose two zero endpoint values also force the zero solution.
