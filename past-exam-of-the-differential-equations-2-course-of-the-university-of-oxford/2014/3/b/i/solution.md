<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Seek an order-one [outer expansion](../../../../../outer-expansion.md) as $\epsilon\to0^+$:

$$
y_{\rm out}(x)=y_0(x)+\epsilon y_1(x)+O(\epsilon^2).
$$

At leading order, $0=y_0-x^2$, so $y_0=x^2$. At order $\epsilon$,

$$
y_0''=y_1+2+y_0^2.
$$

Since $y_0''=2$, this gives $y_1=-x^4$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{y_{\rm out}(x)=x^2-\epsilon x^4+O(\epsilon^2).}
$$

This is an approximation away from any [boundary layer](../../../../../boundary-layer.md); the small coefficient of the highest [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) means the reduced equation cannot generally impose both endpoint conditions.
