<h1 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Move the expansion point to the origin with $z=x+1$. The transformed [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) is

$$
zy_{zz}+(z-1)y_z+cy=0.
$$

After division by $z$, the functions $z(1-1/z)=z-1$ and $z^2(c/z)=cz$ are [analytic functions](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) at zero, so this is a [regular singular point](../../../../../regular-singular-point.md). For a [Frobenius solution](../../../../../frobenius-solution.md) with leading term $a_0z^\rho$, $a_0\ne0$, the lowest power is $z^{\rho-1}$ and its coefficient is

$$
a_0\bigl[\rho(\rho-1)-\rho\bigr]=a_0\rho(\rho-2).
$$

Therefore the [indicial equation](../../../../../indicial-equation.md) and its [indicial exponents](../../../../../indicial-exponent.md) are

$$
\boxed{\rho(\rho-2)=0,\qquad\rho=0,\ 2.}
$$

The exponent $\rho$ denotes the quantity called $\alpha$ in the paper, and avoids confusion with other uses of that letter.
