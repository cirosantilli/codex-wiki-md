<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [Lagrange identity for a Sturm-Liouville operator](../../../../../lagrange-identity-for-a-sturm-liouville-operator.md) gives

$$
\int_\alpha^\beta\bigl(\overline u\,Mv-\overline{Mu}\,v\bigr)\,dx
=\bigl[a(\overline u\,v'-\overline{u'}\,v)\bigr]_\alpha^\beta.
$$

In the intended class of [functions](../../../../../function-split.md) whose values and first [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) stay finite at the endpoints, the endpoint factor $a$ makes this entire boundary term vanish. Thus **no separately prescribed endpoint values are needed to eliminate the boundary form**. The same [integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) argument then proves that the [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are real.

The admissible endpoint behaviour is part of this conclusion: it is the [vanishing endpoint coefficient and the Sturm-Liouville boundary form](../../../../../vanishing-endpoint-coefficient-and-the-sturm-liouville-boundary-form.md), together with endpoint regularity, that replaces explicit [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md). If singular [functions](../../../../../function-split.md) are allowed, a vanishing coefficient alone need not suffice: $a(x)=x(1-x)$ and $v(x)=\log x$ have $a(x)v'(x)\to1$ as $x\downarrow0$. A [self-adjoint differential operator](../../../../../self-adjoint-differential-operator.md) therefore still needs a specified domain of admissible [functions](../../../../../function-split.md).
