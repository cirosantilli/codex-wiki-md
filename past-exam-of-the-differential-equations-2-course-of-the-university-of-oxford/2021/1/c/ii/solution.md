<h1 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Putting both data at zero makes this an [initial value problem](../../../../../initial-value-problem.md). The [uniqueness theorem for ordinary differential equations](../../../../../uniqueness-theorem-for-ordinary-differential-equations.md) applies, and one can also prove uniqueness directly: the difference of two solutions is a [homogeneous solution](../../../../../homogeneous-solution.md) $A\cos x+B\sin x$ whose value and first [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) at zero are both zero, forcing $A=B=0$. In fact, for every prescribed [continuous function](../../../../../continuous-function.md) $f$,

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\int_0^x\sin(x-t)f(t)\,dt}
$$

satisfies both initial data and $y''+y=f$. Consequently non-uniqueness is impossible for every $l>0$, including $l=m\pi$. In this setting the [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) is trivial. The [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md) at a single endpoint do not give the same [self-adjoint operator](../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md) domain as the two separated [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../../../../../dirichlet-boundary-condition.md), so the earlier exceptional lengths do not apply.
