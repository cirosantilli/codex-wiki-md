<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

At $l=\pi$, the [Fredholm solvability condition](../../../../../fredholm-solvability-condition.md) is

$$
0=\int_0^\pi(a\sin x+b\sin2x)\sin x\,dx=\frac{a\pi}{2},
$$

by [orthogonality](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) of the [Fourier sine basis](../../../../../fourier-sine-basis.md). Thus a non-unique solution exists exactly when $a=0$, with any real $b$. Since $(d^2/dx^2+1)\sin2x=-3\sin2x$, the complete family is

$$
\boxed{a=0:\quad y(x)=C\sin x-\frac b3\sin2x,\quad C\in\mathbb R.}
$$

If $a\ne0$ there is no solution, for any $b$. In particular, when $a=b=0$, the family reduces to the [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) $C\sin x$; it is still non-unique. There is no choice of $a,b$ producing a unique solution for this length.
