# Missing-outcome bounds for a binary endpoint

↑ **Parent:** [Missing data](missing-data.md)

Suppose an endpoint is binary, $N$ people are in the target cohort, $r$ observed outcomes are successes and $m$ outcomes are missing. With no assumptions about the missing values, the cohort success proportion $p$ satisfies

$$
\frac{r}{N}\leq p\leq\frac{r+m}{N}.
$$

For the proof, let $u$ be the number of successes among the missing outcomes. Since $0\leq u\leq m$ and $p=(r+u)/N$, both [inequalities](inequality-mathematics.md) follow. Every integer $u$ in that range is a possible completion of the observed data, so the endpoints are sharp. These are [missing data](missing-data.md) bounds on a cohort proportion, not [confidence intervals](confidence-interval.md) or bounds on a causal treatment effect.
