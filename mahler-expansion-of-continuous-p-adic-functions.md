# Mahler expansion of continuous p-adic functions

↑ **Parent:** [p-adic measure](p-adic-measure.md)

Let $I$ be a complete [discrete valuation ring](discrete-valuation-ring.md) with a continuous $\mathbb Z_p$-algebra structure. Every [continuous function](continuous-function.md) on $\mathbb Z_p$ with values in $I$ has a unique uniformly convergent expansion in the [binomial polynomials](binomial-polynomial.md), with $a_n=\Delta^nf(0)$. This is the coefficient-ring version of the [Mahler theorem](mahler-s-theorem.md). To see that $a_n\to0$, approximate the function modulo any power of the [maximal ideal](maximal-ideal.md) by a function of period $p^k$. In the expansion of $(E-1)^{p^k}$, all intermediate [binomial coefficients](binomial-coefficient.md) are divisible by $p$, while $(E^{p^k}-1)$ kills the periodic function. Iterating makes its differences vanish modulo the prescribed ideal power. [Finite differences](finite-difference-split.md) do not increase the [uniform norm](supremum-norm.md), so the same conclusion holds for the original function. [Binomial inversion](binomial-inversion.md) gives agreement at the nonnegative [integers](integer.md), and their density in the [p-adic integers](p-adic-integer.md) gives the expansion everywhere. This gives the dual description of [p-adic measures](p-adic-measure.md) by arbitrary bounded sequences of binomial moments, and hence the [Amice transform](amice-transform.md).
