<h1 id="kuhn-s-theorem">Kuhn's theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Perfect recall](perfect-recall.md)

In a finite [extensive-form game](extensive-form-game.md) with [perfect recall](perfect-recall.md), each [mixed strategy](mixed-strategy.md) has a [realization-equivalent](realization-equivalent-strategies.md) [behavioural strategy](behavioural-strategy.md), and every [behavioural strategy](behavioural-strategy.md) has a [realization-equivalent](realization-equivalent-strategies.md) [mixed strategy](mixed-strategy.md). The latter direction samples all contingent actions independently in advance. For the former, condition the random complete plan on the player's own action and information history leading to an [information set](information-set.md); [perfect recall](perfect-recall.md) makes that own history common to its nodes. Conditional action [probabilities](probability.md) then reproduce the terminal path factors. At an [information set](information-set.md) reached with zero probability under that player's own plan distribution, choose any action distribution. The result does not assert equivalence without [perfect recall](perfect-recall.md).[original article, pp. 193–216, perfect-recall equivalence](extensive-games-and-the-problem-of-information-by-harold-w-kuhn.md)
