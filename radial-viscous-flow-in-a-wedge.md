# Radial viscous flow in a wedge

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous fluid flow](viscous-fluid-flow-split.md)

A steady two-dimensional purely radial [incompressible](incompressible-flow.md) [Newtonian fluid](newtonian-fluid.md) flow has $u_r=g(\theta)/r$ by the [continuity equation](continuity-equation.md). The polar [Navier-Stokes equation](navier-stokes-equation.md) gives

$$
p_r=\rho(g^2+\nu g'')/r^3,\qquad p_\theta=2\rho\nu g'/r^2.
$$

Equality of the mixed pressure derivatives yields the displayed third-order equation. On fixed walls $\theta=\pm\alpha$, [no-slip boundary conditions](no-slip-boundary-condition.md) require $g(\pm\alpha)=0$. A source with volume flux $Q$ per unit out-of-plane length requires $\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha}g\,d\theta=Q$. Consequently $g=QG$ leaves the single inertial parameter $\epsilon=Q/\nu$ in $G^{(3)}+4G'+2\epsilon GG'=0$. The origin is a source singularity; the radial ansatz describes the wedge away from that point, not the detailed source apparatus.
