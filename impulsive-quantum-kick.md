# Impulsive quantum kick

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum state continuity at a finite Hamiltonian step](quantum-state-continuity-at-a-finite-hamiltonian-step.md)

An [impulsive quantum kick](impulsive-quantum-kick.md) is the limit of a pulse with finite integrated [Hamiltonian operator](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md), rather than a bounded finite step. For a bounded [self-adjoint operator](self-adjoint-operator.md) $K$ with units of [action](action.md), a rectangular pulse $H_\tau=K/\tau$ lasting $\tau$ has exact [unitary time evolution](unitary-time-evolution.md) $e^{-iK/\hbar}$ for every $\tau$. In the limiting notation $H(t)=K\delta(t)$, $\delta$ is the [Dirac delta function](dirac-delta-function.md) and

$$
|\psi(0^+)\rangle=e^{-iK/\hbar}|\psi(0^-)\rangle.
$$

The integral estimate for [quantum state continuity at a finite Hamiltonian step](quantum-state-continuity-at-a-finite-hamiltonian-step.md) no longer tends to zero: the pulse height diverges. A bounded background [Hamiltonian operator](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) contributes vanishing evolution during this limit. A more general pulse with noncommuting generators can require [time ordering](time-ordering.md); integrated area alone then need not specify the result.

For example, $K=\hbar\phi\sigma_y/2$, with a $y$ [Pauli matrix](pauli-matrices.md), sends a [qubit](qubit.md) in $|0\rangle$ to $\cos(\phi/2)|0\rangle+\sin(\phi/2)|1\rangle$. At $\phi=\pi$, the [Born rule](born-rule.md) predicts $|1\rangle$ with [probability](probability.md) one, however short the regularized pulse is. Its finite rotation is precisely what a bounded finite step cannot accomplish at the switching instant.
