<h1 id="perron-frobenius-theorem">Perron–Frobenius theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Nonnegative matrix](nonnegative-matrix.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perron–Frobenius_theorem)

The strictly positive case states that a real square [matrix](matrix.md) $W$ with $W_{ij}>0$ has a positive [simple eigenvalue](simple-eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$ and positive left and right [eigenvectors](eigenvector.md), with all other [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) strictly smaller in [modulus](modulus.md). The [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](brouwer-fixed-point-theorem.md) applied to $v\mapsto Wv/(\mathbf1^TWv)$ on the nonnegative unit [simplex](simplex.md) gives $v>0$ and $Wv=\lambda v$. For another [eigenvector](eigenvector.md) $z$, maximize $|z_i|/v_i$; the [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md) gives $|\mu|\leq\lambda$. Equality forces every [modulus](modulus.md) ratio and [complex argument](argument-complex-analysis.md) to agree because every entry is positive, so $z$ is proportional to $v$ and $\mu=\lambda$. Apply the same argument to $W^T$ for a positive left [eigenvector](eigenvector.md); its positive pairing with $v$ rules out a [generalized eigenvector](generalized-eigenvector.md) at $\lambda$, establishing algebraic simplicity. For merely nonnegative matrices a nonnegative leading [eigenvector](eigenvector.md) exists. An [irreducible nonnegative matrix](irreducible-nonnegative-matrix.md) has a positive leading [eigenvector](eigenvector.md) and simple Perron [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md); a [primitive nonnegative matrix](primitive-nonnegative-matrix.md) has the strict [modulus](modulus.md) gap.
