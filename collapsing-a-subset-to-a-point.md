# Collapsing a subset to a point

↑ **Parent:** [Quotient topology](quotient-topology.md)

For a nonempty [subset](subset.md) $A$ of a [topological space](topological-space.md) $X$, identify all points of $A$ and leave each point of $X\setminus A$ in its own [equivalence class](equivalence-class.md). The resulting [quotient space](quotient-topological-space.md) is denoted $X/A$. For every [subset](subset.md) $K\subseteq X$, its [saturation of a subset](saturation-of-a-subset.md) is

$$
q_A^{-1}(q_A(K))=
\begin{cases}
K,&K\cap A=\varnothing,\\
K\cup A,&K\cap A\ne\varnothing.
\end{cases}
$$

The [quotient topology](quotient-topology.md) therefore gives a useful test: $q_A(K)$ is a [closed set](closed-set.md) if and only if the displayed [saturation of a subset](saturation-of-a-subset.md) is a [closed set](closed-set.md) of $X$. In particular, every [closed set](closed-set.md) disjoint from $A$ has [closed](closed-set.md) image, even if $A$ is not [closed](closed-set.md). When $A$ is [closed](closed-set.md), $q_A$ is a [closed map](closed-map.md), because both possibilities in the formula are then [closed sets](closed-set.md) for [closed](closed-set.md) $K$.
