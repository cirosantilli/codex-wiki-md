# Arithmetic series

↑ **Parent:** [Arithmetic progression](arithmetic-progression.md)

An [arithmetic series](arithmetic-series.md) adds the [sequence terms](sequence-term.md) of an [arithmetic progression](arithmetic-progression.md). Its $n$-th [partial sum](partial-sum.md) is $S_n=\sum_{k=1}^na_k$, with the [empty sum](empty-sum.md) convention $S_0=0$. For $n\geq1$,

$$
\boxed{S_n=\frac{n(a_1+a_n)}2=na_1+\frac{n(n-1)}2d.}
$$

Thus the total is the number of [sequence terms](sequence-term.md) multiplied by the [arithmetic mean](arithmetic-mean.md) of the two endpoints. The formula also covers decreasing and constant [arithmetic progressions](arithmetic-progression.md).[NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, §1.2(ii) Finite Series, equation 1.2.10: arithmetic progression sum and last-term definition](nist-digital-library-of-mathematical-functions/1-algebraic-and-analytic-methods.md)

For the eight-entry example, **the sum is $8(7+28)/2=140$**, and the [arithmetic mean](arithmetic-mean.md) of all eight entries is $17.5$. A decreasing example $11,9,7,5,3,1$ gives $6(11+1)/2=36$. Negative values cause no algebraic difficulty: $3,1,-1,-3,-5$ has sum $5(3-5)/2=-5$.

The [triangular number](triangular-number.md) $T_n=1+2+\cdots+n$ is the special case $a_1=d=1$, giving $n(n+1)/2$. More generally, an equal increase in each successive allocation makes the total grow as a [quadratic function](quadratic-function.md) of the number of allocations. The eight-entry example has

$$
a_k=3k+4,\qquad S_n=\frac{3n^2+11n}2.
$$

Its first four entries add to $46$, its first eight to $140$, and its first twelve to $282$. The [sequence terms](sequence-term.md) lie on a [straight line](straight-line.md) when plotted against their [sequence indices](sequence-index.md), while the [partial sums](partial-sum.md) follow a [quadratic function](quadratic-function.md).
