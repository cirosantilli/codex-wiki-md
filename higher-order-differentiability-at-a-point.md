# Higher-order differentiability at a point

↑ **Parent:** [Differentiability](differentiability.md)

For $n=1$, [differentiability](differentiability.md) at $a$ means that the [difference quotient](difference-quotient.md) has a finite two-sided [limit of a function](limit-of-a-function.md). For $n\geq2$, require [derivatives](derivative.md) through order $n-1$ throughout some open [neighbourhood](neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of $a$, and require the final [difference quotient](difference-quotient.md)

$$
f^{(n)}(a)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f^{(n-1)}(a+h)-f^{(n-1)}(a)}h
$$

to exist as a finite real number. The last [derivative](derivative.md) need not exist elsewhere or be continuous at $a$. In particular, existence of $f'(a)$ by itself does not justify taking a second [derivative](derivative.md): the function $f'$ must first be defined in a [neighbourhood](neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of $a$.
