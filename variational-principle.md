# Variational principle

↑ **Parent:** [Calculus of variations](calculus-of-variations-split.md)

A variational principle specifies a [function](function-split.md), path or field by requiring a [functional](functional.md) to be stationary under an allowed class of [variations](variation.md). The admissible class includes the prescribed [boundary conditions](boundary-condition.md) and [constraints](constraint-mechanics.md). A stationary value need not be a [minimum](minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md): the [Euler-Lagrange equation](euler-lagrange-equation.md) expresses the first-order stationarity condition, while a minimum requires additional analysis. [Fermat principle](fermat-principle.md) applies this idea to optical path length, and the [Rayleigh-Ritz method](rayleigh-ritz-method.md) approximates a variational problem in a chosen family of [trial functions](trial-function.md).
