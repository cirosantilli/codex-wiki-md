# Hanbury Brown and Twiss effect

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum optics](quantum-optics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanbury_Brown_and_Twiss_effect)

[Robert Hanbury Brown](robert-hanbury-brown.md) and [Richard Q. Twiss](richard-quentin-twiss.md) sought [angular sizes](angular-diameter.md) through correlations of detected [light intensities](light-intensity.md). Their method multiplies detector outputs after detection, whereas a [Michelson interferometer](michelson-interferometer.md) combines fields before detection.[1956, Nature 177, pp. 27–29, accessible opening paragraphs](correlation-between-photons-in-two-coherent-beams-of-light-by-robert-hanbury-brown-and-richard-quentin-twiss.md)

A monochromatic discharge source illuminated a half-silvered [beam splitter](beam-splitter.md) and two [photodetectors](photodetector.md). The averaged current product showed an excess near coincidence. Moving one detector introduced a delay that removed it, supplying a control. Slow response weakened the peak. This [photon bunching](photon-bunching.md) is compatible with fluctuating classical [light intensity](light-intensity.md).[2005 individual Nobel lecture, pp. 86–88, figures 3–4](one-hundred-years-of-light-quanta-by-roy-jay-glauber.md)
