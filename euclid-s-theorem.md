<h1 id="euclid-s-theorem">Euclid's theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Prime number](prime-number.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid's_theorem)

There are infinitely many [prime numbers](prime-number.md). Indeed, for any finite list of [prime numbers](prime-number.md) $p_1,\ldots,p_k$, the [integer](integer.md) $N=p_1\cdots p_k+1>1$ has a [prime factor](prime-factor.md), by the [Fundamental theorem of arithmetic](fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md). None of the listed [prime numbers](prime-number.md) divides $N$, since each divides $N-1$ and a common [divisor](divisor.md) would divide $1$. Thus no finite list contains all [prime numbers](prime-number.md). The constructed $N$ need not itself be a [prime number](prime-number.md).
