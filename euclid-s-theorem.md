<h1 id="euclid-s-theorem">Euclid's theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Prime number](prime-number-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid's_theorem)

There are infinitely many [prime numbers](prime-number-split.md). Indeed, for any finite list of [prime numbers](prime-number-split.md) $p_1,\ldots,p_k$, the [integer](integer-split.md) $N=p_1\cdots p_k+1>1$ has a [prime factor](prime-factor.md), by the [fundamental theorem of arithmetic](fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md). None of the listed [prime numbers](prime-number-split.md) divides $N$, since each divides $N-1$ and a common [divisor](divisor.md) would divide $1$. Thus no finite list contains all [prime numbers](prime-number-split.md). The constructed $N$ need not itself be a [prime number](prime-number-split.md).

For example, the first six [prime numbers](prime-number-split.md) give

$$
2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13+1=30031=59\cdot509.
$$

Both [prime factors](prime-factor.md) lie outside the original list, although $30031$ is a [composite number](composite-number.md). The construction defeats a proposed complete list; it is not a formula guaranteed to produce a [prime number](prime-number-split.md). Book IX, Proposition 20 of [Euclid's Elements](euclid-s-elements.md) explicitly considers both possibilities, prime and composite. [Euclid, Elements, Book IX, Proposition 20: a prime or composite successor](https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/elements/bookIX/propIX20.html).
