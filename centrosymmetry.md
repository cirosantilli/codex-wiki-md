# Centrosymmetry

↑ **Parent:** [Crystal symmetry](crystal-symmetry.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrosymmetry)

A crystal has centrosymmetry when its [crystallographic point group](crystallographic-point-group.md) contains spatial inversion. Its bulk electric-dipole polarization law must then satisfy $P(-E)=-P(E)$. Substituting a power expansion in E makes every even-order coefficient vanish, including the second-order [nonlinear optical susceptibility](nonlinear-optical-susceptibility.md). A surface, an external symmetry-breaking field or a response beyond the electric-dipole approximation can change the conclusion.
