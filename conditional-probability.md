# Conditional probability

↑ **Parent:** [Probability theory](probability-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_probability)

For [events](event.md) $A,B$ with $\mathbb P(B)>0$, conditional probability is

$$
\mathbb P(A\mid B)=\frac{\mathbb P(A\cap B)}{\mathbb P(B)}.
$$

Restrict attention to the outcomes in $B$, then renormalize their [probability measure](probability-measure.md) so its total mass becomes one. The resulting rule $A\mapsto\mathbb P(A\mid B)$ is itself a [probability measure](probability-measure.md). The definition and its connection to [independent events](independent-events.md) are developed in [MIT 18.05, Reading 3: Conditional Probability, Independence and Bayes' Theorem](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-05-introduction-to-probability-and-statistics-spring-2022/resources/mit18_05_s22_class03-prep_pdf/).

For the two-dice model, let $A$ mean the sum is at least nine and $B$ mean the first result is six. Of the six outcomes in $B$, four belong to $A$: the second die can be 3, 4, 5, or 6. Thus **$\mathbb P(A\mid B)=2/3$**. Reversing the information gives a different answer: four of the ten outcomes in $A$ have first result six, so **$\mathbb P(B\mid A)=2/5$**. Both ratios use the same overlap; their denominators describe different information.

![](conditional-probability-dice.png)

**[Figure 15](#image-conditioning-selects-a-different-denominator-in-the-same-dice-outcome-grid). Conditioning selects a different denominator in the same dice outcome grid**.

The definition requires positive conditioning mass. For a continuously distributed [random variable](random-variable-split.md) $X$, it cannot directly define $\mathbb P(A\mid X=x)$ when $\mathbb P(X=x)=0$. A [regular conditional distribution](regular-conditional-distribution.md) or [conditional density](conditional-density.md) supplies the appropriate extension, with the usual [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md) qualification. Nor does conditioning by itself justify [causal inference](causal-inference-split.md): changing which outcomes are considered is different from physically changing how the experiment is generated.
