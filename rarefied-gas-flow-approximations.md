# Rarefied gas flow approximations

↑ **Parent:** [Knudsen number](knudsen-number.md)

For $\mathrm{Kn}\ll1$, frequent gas–gas [collisions](collision.md) can establish a local [Maxwellian](local-maxwellian.md) before macroscopic conditions change appreciably. Under this additional near-equilibrium assumption, the [hydrodynamic limit of the Boltzmann equation](hydrodynamic-limit-of-the-boltzmann-equation.md) motivates a [continuum approximation](continuum-approximation.md) with [Navier-Stokes equations](navier-stokes-equation.md) and heat conduction. A [no-slip boundary condition](no-slip-boundary-condition.md) is a leading wall approximation, not a statement about individual molecular [velocities](velocity.md).

With a small but finite [Knudsen number](knudsen-number.md), a useful slip approximation retains continuum bulk equations and corrects wall [velocity](velocity.md) and [temperature](temperature-split.md). A [Navier slip boundary condition](navier-slip-boundary-condition.md) can represent the leading tangential [velocity](velocity.md) correction, but its [slip length](slip-length.md) must come from the relevant gas–wall interaction model. A nearby [Knudsen layer](knudsen-layer.md) still requires kinetic treatment if its detailed structure is wanted. Slip corrections are perturbations; extending them into order-one [Knudsen numbers](knudsen-number.md) has no general accuracy guarantee. At order-one [Knudsen number](knudsen-number.md), wall and intermolecular [collisions](collision.md) both matter; this transitional situation calls for a [kinetic theory](kinetic-theory-split.md) model. At $\mathrm{Kn}\gg1$, [free molecular flow](free-molecular-flow.md) becomes a useful limiting approximation. These are scale comparisons, with accuracy depending on geometry, flow conditions and the observable, rather than universal sharp numerical boundaries. [Frank G. Collins (1985), Low Density Gas Dynamic Wall Boundary Conditions, introduction](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19860015045/downloads/19860015045.pdf#page=3)
