# Integrability threshold for exponential powers of a Gaussian

↑ **Parent:** [Integrable random variable](integrable-random-variable.md)

Let $Z$ have [standard normal distribution](standard-normal-distribution.md), let $m\ge1$ be an integer and let $\alpha\in\mathbb R$. A finite [expected value](expected-value-split.md) of $Ze^{\alpha Z^{2m}}$ requires absolute [integrability](integrability.md), which reduces to finiteness of

$$
\int_0^\infty z\exp(\alpha z^{2m}-z^2/2)\,dz.
$$

For $m=1$ it is finite exactly when $\alpha<1/2$. For every $m\ge2$ it is finite exactly when $\alpha\le0$: the higher power dominates the negative quadratic when $\alpha>0$, and for $\alpha\le0$ the [integrand](integrand.md) is bounded by $ze^{-z^2/2}$. In the finite cases the signed [expected value](expected-value-split.md) is zero by [symmetry](symmetry-physics.md). Otherwise each signed part has infinite [integral](integral-split.md); a formal symmetric cancellation is only a [Cauchy principal value](cauchy-principal-value.md).
