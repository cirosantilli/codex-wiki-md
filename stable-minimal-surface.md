# Stable minimal surface

↑ **Parent:** [Minimal surface](minimal-surface.md)

A [stable minimal surface](stable-minimal-surface.md) has nonnegative [second variation](second-variation.md) of [surface area](surface-area.md) for every admissible normal variation $fN$ fixing the boundary. For a two-sided [minimal surface](minimal-surface.md) in three-dimensional [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md), the quadratic form is

$$
\delta^2\operatorname{Area}[f]=\int_M\bigl(|\nabla_M f|^2-(k_1^2+k_2^2)f^2\bigr)\,dA.
$$

Here $\nabla_M f$ is the [gradient](gradient.md) for the induced [Riemannian metric](riemannian-metric.md). The [gradient](gradient.md) term penalizes rapid changes in the displacement; the [principal curvature](principal-curvature.md) term can make the [second variation](second-variation.md) negative. A [plane](plane.md) has only the nonnegative [gradient](gradient.md) term. Nonnegativity alone, especially with zero modes, does not prove a strict [local minimum](local-minimum.md) or a [global minimum](global-minimum.md).

For a finite [catenoid](catenoid.md) between fixed rings, the answer depends on the ring separation, waist [radius](radius.md), and allowed variations. The [axisymmetric second variation of a catenoid](axisymmetric-second-variation-of-a-catenoid.md) tests a specified restricted class. Comparing with two disconnected disks tests a different class of competitors, including a change in [topology](topology-split.md). Consequently, a stationary [catenoid](catenoid.md) can have greater [surface area](surface-area.md) than two disks without immediately collapsing under every small perturbation.
