# First-order definition of exactly finitely many witnesses

↑ **Parent:** [First-order logic](first-order-logic.md)

For a fixed positive [integer](integer-split.md) $k$, the displayed [first-order logic](first-order-logic.md) sentence says that exactly $k$ distinct elements satisfy $R$. The pairwise inequalities and positive instances give at least $k$ witnesses; the universal exhaustiveness clause gives at most $k$. Additional parameters can remain fixed inside $R$. For $k=0$, use $\forall y\,\neg R(y)$ instead. Omitting distinctness or exhaustiveness changes exactly to a weaker counting condition.
