# Exponential of a bounded skew-adjoint operator is unitary

↑ **Parent:** [Operator exponential](operator-exponential.md)

For a bounded [skew-adjoint operator](skew-adjoint-generator.md) on a complex [Hilbert space](hilbert-space-split.md), the [operator exponential](operator-exponential.md) converges in [norm](norm.md) because its term [norms](norm.md) are bounded by $\|T\|^n/n!$. Continuity of the [adjoint operator](adjoint-operator.md) gives $(e^T)^*=e^{T^*}=e^{-T}$. [Absolute convergence](absolute-convergence.md) permits the Cauchy product of $e^T$ and $e^{-T}$: its degree-$n$ coefficient is $T^n\sum_{j=0}^n(-1)^j/[j!(n-j)!]$, zero for $n>0$ and the identity for $n=0$. Both multiplication orders therefore give the identity, proving unitarity. The boundedness hypothesis makes this elementary power-[series](series-mathematics.md) proof valid without domain issues for unbounded generators.
