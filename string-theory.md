## Fundamental string

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)

A fundamental string is the perturbative string of a string-theory description. Its [string tension](#string-tension) is the displayed expression in the string metric. It couples to the [Neveu–Schwarz two-form](#neveu-schwarz-two-form).

## Hagedorn temperature

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagedorn_temperature)

If a density of states has high-energy growth proportional to a power of energy times $e^{\beta_HE}$, its canonical Boltzmann sum fails for $\beta<\beta_H$. The Hagedorn temperature is the corresponding threshold. The [open bosonic string mass spectrum](#open-bosonic-string-mass-spectrum) and [large-level degeneracy of an open bosonic string](#large-level-degeneracy-of-an-open-bosonic-string) give $\beta_H=4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}$ in units with Boltzmann's constant, the speed of light and Planck's constant set to one. Polynomial prefactors affect behavior at the threshold itself; the growth alone does not distinguish a limiting temperature from a phase transition.

### Thermal winding instability of a closed bosonic string

↑ **Parent:** [Hagedorn temperature](#hagedorn-temperature)

Euclidean finite [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature) compactifies time to a circle of radius $R_\beta=\beta/(2\pi)$. The [string oscillator](#string-oscillator) ground states with zero [momentum](classical-mechanics.md#momentum) number and winding $w=\pm1$ have the displayed mass from the [bosonic string mass spectrum](#bosonic-string-mass-spectrum). They become massless at $\beta_H=4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}$ and tachyonic at higher [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature). This thermal winding mode diagnoses breakdown of the perturbative low-temperature string gas at the [Hagedorn temperature](#hagedorn-temperature). The endpoint and order of a transition require interactions and a suitable stable vacuum; the ordinary zero-winding bosonic [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon) is already unstable independently of this thermal threshold.

### Finite canonical partition function at the bosonic Hagedorn threshold

↑ **Parent:** [Hagedorn temperature](#hagedorn-temperature)

For a critical [closed bosonic string](#closed-string) in 25 noncompact spatial dimensions, the smoothed [string oscillator](#string-oscillator) rest-mass density behaves as $\rho(M)\sim C M^{-26}e^{\beta_H M}$, with $\beta_H=4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}$. At large $M$, the centre-of-mass [momentum](classical-mechanics.md#momentum) integral of $e^{-\beta\sqrt{M^2+p^2}}$ is asymptotic to $(2\pi M/\beta)^{25/2}e^{-\beta M}$, up to the convention for phase-space measure. Thus the high-mass tail of the one-string [canonical partition function](statistical-physics.md#canonical-partition-function) is proportional to $\int_{M_0}^\infty M^{-27/2}e^{-(\beta-\beta_H)M}\,dM$. This converges at $\beta=\beta_H$ but fails for $\beta<\beta_H$. Expanding about the endpoint gives twelve finite derivatives and a nonanalytic term proportional to $(\beta-\beta_H)^{25/2}$; the thirteenth derivative diverges. Consequently exponential state growth does not imply divergence of the [partition function](statistical-physics.md#canonical-partition-function) at the threshold itself. The discussion assumes the separate infrared instability of the bosonic [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon) has been regulated and does not claim a stable bosonic thermal vacuum.

## Brane

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)

A brane is an extended object with $p$ spatial dimensions. Its history is a $(p+1)$-dimensional [worldvolume](#worldvolume) embedded in target [spacetime](special-relativity.md#spacetime). A point particle has $p=0$, a string has $p=1$, and a membrane has $p=2$. Geometric brane actions depend on the [induced worldvolume metric](#induced-worldvolume-metric); [D-branes](#d-brane) also carry open-string endpoint and gauge-field data.

### Four-dimensional supermembrane

↑ **Parent:** [Brane](#brane)

The four-dimensional minimal [supersymmetric](supersymmetry.md) membrane has embedding coordinates $(X^m,\theta^\alpha)$ with four real odd components. Its area term uses the [supertranslation-invariant superspace coframe](supersymmetry.md#supertranslation-invariant-superspace-coframe), and a [Wess-Zumino brane coupling](#wess-zumino-brane-coupling) integrates a superspace three-form potential with closed four-form curvature. Equal charge magnitude and tension permit [kappa symmetry](supersymmetry.md#kappa-symmetry). After [worldvolume](#worldvolume) reparametrization and kappa fixing, one transverse scalar and one three-dimensional Majorana [fermion](quantum-mechanics.md#fermion) remain. This gives the long-wavelength description of the translation and [fermion](quantum-mechanics.md#fermion) zero modes of a [supersymmetric domain wall](supersymmetry.md#supersymmetric-domain-wall); it is distinct from restricting the eleven-dimensional M2-brane to a four-dimensional target.

#### Supermembrane closed four-form

↑ **Parent:** [Four-dimensional supermembrane](#four-dimensional-supermembrane)

The four-dimensional membrane [Fierz rearrangement](quantum-field-theory.md#fierz-identity) identity $(C\gamma_{mn})_{(\alpha\beta}(C\gamma^n)_{\gamma\delta)}=0$ makes the displayed supertranslation-invariant four-form closed. It is locally the [exterior derivative](differential-form.md#exterior-derivative) of a three-form potential, which can change by an exact form under target [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md). Pulling the potential back to a closed [worldvolume](#worldvolume) gives an invariant [Wess-Zumino brane coupling](#wess-zumino-brane-coupling). The charge magnitude and normalization of this form are fixed together by cancellation of the area variation under [kappa symmetry](supersymmetry.md#kappa-symmetry).

### Dirac membrane action

↑ **Parent:** [Brane](#brane)

A relativistic membrane sweeps out a three-dimensional [worldvolume](#worldvolume) with [induced worldvolume metric](#induced-worldvolume-metric) $h_{ij}=\partial_iX^m\partial_jX^n\eta_{mn}$. Its leading geometric [action](classical-mechanics.md#action) is tension times its invariant volume. In static gauge in four-dimensional spacetime, one transverse field $Y$ gives $h_{ij}=\eta_{ij}+\partial_iY\partial_jY$, and the [matrix determinant lemma](linear-algebra.md#matrix-determinant-lemma) gives $S=-T\int\sqrt{1+\partial_iY\partial^iY}$. Expanding supplies the translation-zero-mode kinetic term. A [domain wall](critical-phenomenon.md#domain-wall) of finite thickness also has higher-curvature corrections outside the long-wavelength limit.

### Wess-Zumino brane coupling

↑ **Parent:** [Brane](#brane)

A charged [brane](#brane) couples to an appropriate differential-form [gauge potential](relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-field) through its [pullback](category.md#pullback-category-theory). Under $A\mapsto A+d\Lambda$, a closed worldvolume changes the action by a boundary term, by [Stokes theorem](calculus.md#stokes-theorem). Reversing the brane charge reverses this coupling, which matters in probe-force and supersymmetry calculations.

#### Electric and magnetic dimensions of a form-coupled brane

↑ **Parent:** [Wess-Zumino brane coupling](#wess-zumino-brane-coupling)

A $(p+1)$-form [gauge potential](relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-field) couples electrically through its pullback to a $(p+1)$-dimensional [worldvolume](#worldvolume), whose object has $p$ spatial dimensions. Its field strength has degree $p+2$ and its magnetic dual potential has degree $d-p-3$, hence magnetic spatial dimension $d-p-4$. Electric and magnetic fluxes are integrated over linking spheres of dimensions $d-p-2$ and $p+2$, respectively. This dimensional dictionary applies when the indicated objects and dual descriptions exist; top forms need separate treatment and carry no ordinary propagating polarization.

#### Gauge invariance of an open-brane coupling

↑ **Parent:** [Wess-Zumino brane coupling](#wess-zumino-brane-coupling)

For an oriented [brane](#brane) worldvolume $W$, a [gauge transformation](electromagnetism.md#gauge-transformation) $C\mapsto C+d\Lambda$ changes the [Wess-Zumino brane coupling](#wess-zumino-brane-coupling) by $q\int_{\partial W}X^*\Lambda$, by the [Generalized Stokes theorem](differential-form.md#generalized-stokes-theorem). If the brane boundary lies on a manifold carrying a form $B$ of one lower degree, adding the displayed boundary term and transforming $B\mapsto B-\Lambda$ cancels this variation. The sign uses the induced boundary orientation. A closed worldvolume needs no boundary term; noncompact cases require appropriate support or decay conditions.

#### Field-strength force on a charged brane

↑ **Parent:** [Wess-Zumino brane coupling](#wess-zumino-brane-coupling)

For $F=dA$ and an embedding variation $V$, the pullback [Lie derivative](differential-form.md#lie-derivative-of-a-differential-form) and [Cartan's magic formula](differential-form.md#cartan-s-magic-formula) give the displayed variation. Consequently the interior force from the [Wess-Zumino brane coupling](#wess-zumino-brane-coupling) depends only on the gauge-invariant field strength. A change $A\mapsto A+d\Lambda$ changes the action only on the boundary, by the [Generalized Stokes theorem](differential-form.md#generalized-stokes-theorem); boundaries require the appropriate fixed boundary data or additional boundary coupling.

### Brane tension

↑ **Parent:** [Brane](#brane)

The brane tension is the energy per unit spatial [brane](#brane) volume and multiplies its geometric action. In units $\hbar=c=1$, a $p$-brane tension has mass dimension $p+1$. The $p=1$ case is the [string tension](#string-tension).

### Worldvolume

↑ **Parent:** [Brane](#brane)

The worldvolume is the history of a [brane](#brane). An embedding $X:\Sigma\to\mathcal M$ maps its local coordinates into target [spacetime](special-relativity.md#spacetime). A string [worldvolume](#worldvolume) is a [worldsheet](#worldsheet).

#### Auxiliary worldvolume metric

↑ **Parent:** [Worldvolume](#worldvolume)

An auxiliary worldvolume metric is an independent metric introduced in a [brane](#brane) action, distinct from the [induced worldvolume metric](#induced-worldvolume-metric). Its algebraic equation can eliminate it. For the [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action) of a string, it is determined only up to a [Weyl transformation](#weyl-transformation).

##### Auxiliary-metric brane action

↑ **Parent:** [Auxiliary worldvolume metric](#auxiliary-worldvolume-metric)

The auxiliary-metric brane action is a quadratic embedding action with an independent [worldvolume metric](#auxiliary-worldvolume-metric). Its embedding equation is $\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-\gamma}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu X^a)=0$. Its metric equation is $h_{\mu\nu}-\gamma_{\mu\nu}(\gamma^{\rho\sigma}h_{\rho\sigma}-(p-1))/2=0$. For $p\ne1$, tracing determines $\gamma^{\rho\sigma}h_{\rho\sigma}=p+1$ and hence $\gamma=h$; eliminating the auxiliary field gives twice the invariant [worldvolume](#worldvolume) volume, before including the overall [brane tension](#brane-tension). The [Weyl-invariance exception for the string among branes](#weyl-invariance-exception-for-the-string-among-branes) prevents unique elimination at $p=1$.

###### Weyl-invariance exception for the string among branes

↑ **Parent:** [Auxiliary-metric brane action](#auxiliary-metric-brane-action)

Under $\gamma_{\mu\nu}\mapsto e^{2\omega}\gamma_{\mu\nu}$, the quadratic kinetic density of a $p$-brane scales as $e^{(p-1)\omega}$ and its constant density scales as $e^{(p+1)\omega}$. At $p=1$ the first factor is one and the constant term in the [auxiliary-metric brane action](#auxiliary-metric-brane-action) vanishes. The traced metric equation is then an identity, leaving a [Weyl transformation](#weyl-transformation) freedom. For $p\ne1$, the metric equation instead identifies the auxiliary and [induced worldvolume metrics](#induced-worldvolume-metric).

#### Induced worldvolume metric

↑ **Parent:** [Worldvolume](#worldvolume)

The induced worldvolume metric is the pullback of the target [metric tensor](general-relativity.md#metric-tensor) by a [brane](#brane) embedding. For a nondegenerate timelike embedding, $\sqrt{-\det h}$ is its invariant [worldvolume](#worldvolume) volume density. The $p=1$ specialization is the [induced worldsheet metric](#induced-worldsheet-metric).

## M-theory

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-theory)

An eleven-dimensional nonperturbative framework suggested by superstring dualities, with eleven-dimensional supergravity as its low-energy limit. Type IIA at coupling $g_s$ has an interpretation on a circle of radius $R_{11}=g_s\sqrt{\alpha'}$, so an additional dimension becomes visible at strong coupling. Perturbative string descriptions represent particular limits of this framework.

### M-theory K3 duality

↑ **Parent:** [M-theory](#m-theory)

[M-theory](#m-theory) on a [K3 surface](complex-geometry.md#k3-surface) and the [heterotic string](#heterotic-string) on a three-torus have matching seven-dimensional moduli and charge lattices. The [K3 intersection lattice](complex-geometry.md#k3-intersection-lattice) supplies twenty-two vector charges; an [M5-brane](#m5-brane) wrapped on K3 becomes the heterotic [fundamental string](#fundamental-string). The strong-coupling interpretation and low-energy matching are derived in section 4.7 of [String Theory Dynamics in Various Dimensions](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9503124).

#### ADE gauge enhancement

↑ **Parent:** [M-theory K3 duality](#m-theory-k3-duality)

When appropriate negative-square two-cycles of a [K3 surface](complex-geometry.md#k3-surface) collapse, wrapped [M2-branes](#m2-brane) become massless. Their intersection pattern is an ADE root diagram, and the massless charged states enlarge the abelian gauge algebra to the corresponding simply laced [Lie algebra](lie-algebra.md).

#### Volume-coupling relation for M-theory on K3

↑ **Parent:** [M-theory K3 duality](#m-theory-k3-duality)

A wrapped [M5-brane](#m5-brane) fixes the heterotic [string tension](#string-tension) proportional to $V/\ell_p^6$. Reduction of the gravitational coupling gives $G_7\propto\ell_p^9/V$. Therefore $G_7/\ell_{s,H}^5\propto(V/\ell_p^4)^{3/2}$, yielding the displayed dimensionless coupling relation. This derivation separates the K3 volume modulus from its shape [Narain moduli space](#narain-moduli-space).

### M-theory torus duality

↑ **Parent:** [M-theory](#m-theory)

[M-theory](#m-theory) on a two-torus and [type IIB superstring theory](#type-iib-superstring-theory) on a circle describe the same nine-dimensional physics. Torus shape supplies the [axion-dilaton](#axion-dilaton); torus area supplies the circle-radius modulus after matching metrics. An [M2-brane](#m2-brane) on a one-cycle gives a [(p,q) string](#p-q-string); an [M5-brane](#m5-brane) on the whole torus gives a [D3-brane](#d3-brane). Matching brane charges and tensions establishes the dictionary in [The Power of M Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9510086).

### M-theory circle duality

↑ **Parent:** [M-theory](#m-theory)

Compactifying [M-theory](#m-theory) on a circle gives [type IIA superstring theory](#type-iia-superstring-theory). The circle radius sets its coupling. Multiplying [M2-brane](#m2-brane) and [M5-brane](#m5-brane) tensions by the circle circumference gives the [fundamental string](#fundamental-string) and [D4-brane](#d4-brane) tensions, while unwrapped objects give [D2-branes](#d2-brane) and [NS5-branes](#ns5-brane). Momentum and a [Kaluza-Klein monopole](physics.md#kaluza-klein-monopole) supply [D0-brane](#d0-brane) and [D6-brane](#d6-brane) charges. The circle dictionary and worldvolume reductions are developed in [Four Lectures on M-theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9612121).

#### M-theory origin of type IIA D-branes

↑ **Parent:** [M-theory circle duality](#m-theory-circle-duality)

[D0-branes](#d0-brane) are circle momentum states, [D2-branes](#d2-brane) are unwrapped [M2-branes](#m2-brane), [D4-branes](#d4-brane) are circle-wrapped [M5-branes](#m5-brane), and [D6-branes](#d6-brane) arise from [Kaluza-Klein monopoles](physics.md#kaluza-klein-monopole) with the M-circle as fibre. Wrapped M2-branes give [fundamental strings](#fundamental-string), and unwrapped M5-branes give [NS5-branes](#ns5-brane). The [D8-brane](#d8-brane) is a source for [Romans mass](supersymmetry.md#romans-mass) and is outside this ordinary massless-circle dictionary. Tension matching uses $R_{11}=g_s\ell_s$ and $\ell_p^3=g_s\ell_s^3$.

### M-brane

↑ **Parent:** [M-theory](#m-theory)

An [M-brane](#m-brane) is a [brane](#brane) in [M-theory](#m-theory). Its elementary membrane and five-brane carry electric and magnetic charges for the three-form potential of [eleven-dimensional supergravity](supersymmetry.md#eleven-dimensional-supergravity). Compactification and wrapping relate them to strings and [D-branes](#d-brane).

#### M5-brane

↑ **Parent:** [M-brane](#m-brane)

The [M5-brane](#m5-brane) has five spatial dimensions and carries the magnetic charge dual to the [M2-brane](#m2-brane)'s electric three-form charge. Wrapping it on a compact cycle lowers its effective spatial dimension and multiplies its [brane tension](#brane-tension) by the cycle volume.

#### M2-brane

↑ **Parent:** [M-brane](#m-brane)

The [M2-brane](#m2-brane) has two spatial dimensions and couples electrically to the supergravity three-form. Its supersymmetric worldvolume description is the [supermembrane](#supermembranes).

##### M2-brane supergravity solution

↑ **Parent:** [M2-brane](#m2-brane)

The transverse [harmonic function](partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function) $H$ solves the field equations away from membrane sources. Positive centres contribute $Q_a/|y-y_a|^6$ in eight dimensions. Its [Killing spinors](supersymmetry.md#killing-spinor) are $H^{-1/6}\epsilon_0$ with one oriented membrane projector, giving sixteen supersymmetries. The three-form's sign and membrane orientation must be chosen together.

###### M2-brane near-horizon geometry

↑ **Parent:** [M2-brane supergravity solution](#m2-brane-supergravity-solution)

For $H\sim R^6/r^6$, changing radial coordinate to $z=R^3/(2r^2)$ gives an AdS metric of radius $R/2$ times a round sphere of radius $R$. The [Freund-Rubin compactification](supersymmetry.md#freund-rubin-compactification) flux has magnitude $6/R$ in the AdS volume form. This is the regular positive-charge membrane throat, rather than the limit of an arbitrary singular harmonic multipole.

###### M2-brane probe no-force identity

↑ **Parent:** [M2-brane supergravity solution](#m2-brane-supergravity-solution)

For $F=\operatorname{vol}_{012}\wedge dH^{-1}$ and $F=dA$, choose $A=(1-H^{-1})\operatorname{vol}_{012}$. A parallel probe then has $q=-1$ in the [supermembrane action](#supermembrane-action), so its induced-volume term $-T_2H^{-1}$ and potential term $-T_2(1-H^{-1})$ add to a position-independent constant. This proves the cancellation without a weak-field approximation. An oppositely charged probe has no such cancellation.

##### Supermembranes

↑ **Parent:** [M2-brane](#m2-brane)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermembranes)

The supersymmetric [M2-brane](#m2-brane) worldvolume theory includes embedding bosons and fermions and has [kappa symmetry](supersymmetry.md#kappa-symmetry). In eleven dimensions its fermionic embedding coordinate is a thirty-two-component [Majorana spinor](relativistic-quantum-field.md#majorana-spinor).

###### Supermembrane action

↑ **Parent:** [Supermembranes](#supermembranes)

The first term uses the [induced worldvolume metric](#induced-worldvolume-metric) from the [supervielbein](supersymmetry.md#supervielbein); the second is the [Wess-Zumino brane coupling](#wess-zumino-brane-coupling) to the super-three-form. Its fermionic dependence is retained before bosonic truncation. The relative charge magnitude equals the [brane tension](#brane-tension), while $q=\pm1$ sets the orientation and matching [kappa symmetry projector](supersymmetry.md#kappa-symmetry-projector) sign. In an on-shell eleven-dimensional supergravity superspace, this action has [kappa symmetry](supersymmetry.md#kappa-symmetry).

## Orientifold

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientifold)

A string construction combining worldsheet orientation reversal with a suitable spacetime involution. It projects the spectrum and introduces fixed-locus sources whose charges must be treated together with branes and fluxes. In compactifications, such projections can reduce [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md) and modify the gauge and matter spectrum. Charge cancellation is part of consistency.

## Type I string theory

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_I_string_theory)

An unoriented theory containing open and closed superstrings. Consistent gauge and gravitational anomaly cancellation yields the familiar $\mathfrak{so}(32)$ gauge algebra. Open-string [Chan-Paton factors](#chan-paton-factor) supply the gauge degrees of freedom, while closed strings contain gravity. It is related by strong/weak duality to the corresponding heterotic theory.

## Heterotic string

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotic_string)

A [string theory](string-theory.md) combining a bosonic left-moving system with a supersymmetric right-moving system. Ten target coordinates leave sixteen left-moving internal degrees of freedom for gauge currents. Modular consistency allows the familiar gauge algebras $\mathfrak e_8\oplus\mathfrak e_8$ and $\mathfrak{so}(32)$. Suitable compactification can produce spacetime [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md) and chiral gauge matter.

### Heterotic Calabi-Yau compactification

↑ **Parent:** [Heterotic string](#heterotic-string)

Compactification of a heterotic string on a [Calabi-Yau threefold](complex-geometry.md#calabi-yau-threefold) with a consistent [holomorphic vector bundle](complex-geometry.md#holomorphic-vector-bundle). Suitable bundle and geometry choices preserve four-dimensional N=1 [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md) and generate chiral matter from internal Dirac zero modes. The index controls net chirality. In the standard embedding, the net generation index is half the internal [Euler characteristic](homology.md#euler-characteristic), up to chirality orientation conventions.

## Superstring theory

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstring_theory)

String theories with worldsheet fermionic degrees of freedom and the corresponding supersymmetric consistency conditions. In the [RNS string](#spinning-string) description, consistent supersymmetric [GSO projections](#gso-projection) produce spacetime [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md) and remove the NS tachyon. The flat critical dimension is ten. [Worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry) alone does not guarantee spacetime [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md) without a suitable projection.

## Compactification in string theory

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)

Compactification makes some target spatial dimensions compact and sufficiently small that lower-energy observations see an effective lower-dimensional spacetime. A simple geometry is a product of noncompact spacetime with a compact internal manifold; its [momentum](classical-mechanics.md#momentum) and winding sectors influence the effective spectrum. Reaching a four-dimensional model from a critical ten-dimensional superstring requires such additional geometric and field-theoretic data. [Worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry) alone does not choose the compactification or establish a realistic particle spectrum.

### Modulus of a string compactification

↑ **Parent:** [Compactification in string theory](#compactification-in-string-theory)

A parameter of an internal geometry, gauge background or [dilaton](#dilaton) configuration that becomes a scalar field in the lower-dimensional theory. A continuously undetermined parameter gives a flat direction and often a massless field. Fluxes and other effects can generate a potential, leading to [moduli stabilization](#moduli-stabilization). Shape, size and coupling moduli affect low-energy observables.

#### Narain moduli space

↑ **Parent:** [Modulus of a string compactification](#modulus-of-a-string-compactification)

For a toroidally compactified [heterotic string](#heterotic-string), the torus metric, antisymmetric tensor and gauge [Wilson lines](relativistic-quantum-field.md#wilson-line) determine a positive $d$-plane in a charge space of signature $(d,d+16)$, modulo lattice identifications. The continuous dimension is $d(d+16)$; the coupling is an additional modulus. The three-torus case has fifty-seven shape and Wilson-line parameters.

#### Moduli stabilization

↑ **Parent:** [Modulus of a string compactification](#modulus-of-a-string-compactification)

Generating a potential with an acceptable stable minimum for previously undetermined compactification scalar fields. It can involve fluxes, branes and nonperturbative effects, with [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md) and consistency restrictions. A plausible phenomenological model must determine or suitably control these fields rather than leave arbitrary massless scalars and varying couplings.

### Flux compactification

↑ **Parent:** [Compactification in string theory](#compactification-in-string-theory)

A compactification with quantized background form-field strengths on internal cycles. Fluxes can generate potentials for [moduli of a string compactification](#modulus-of-a-string-compactification) and affect [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md) and vacuum geometry. They must satisfy field equations, Bianchi identities and global charge constraints; choosing flux integers alone is not a complete consistency proof.

## Spinning string

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)

The spinning string adds [worldsheet Majorana fermions](#worldsheet-majorana-fermion) and local [worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry) to the embedding coordinates. Its gauge-fixed quantum description is the Ramond–Neveu–Schwarz formulation of the superstring.

### Unprojected low levels of the open RNS string

↑ **Parent:** [Spinning string](#spinning-string)

Before the [GSO projection](#gso-projection), the [Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector) has [string oscillator](#string-oscillator) levels $0,1/2,1,3/2$, with physical multiplicities $1,8,36,128$ in ten-dimensional [light-cone gauge in string theory](#light-cone-gauge-in-string-theory). The eight transverse [worldsheet Majorana fermions](#worldsheet-majorana-fermion) supply eight single $\psi_{-1/2}$ states. Level one has eight $\alpha_{-1}$ states and $\binom82=28$ pairs of distinct $\psi_{-1/2}$ modes. Level $3/2$ has $8$ single $\psi_{-3/2}$ modes, $8\cdot8$ mixed [boson](quantum-mechanics.md#boson)–[fermion](quantum-mechanics.md#fermion) modes and $\binom83=56$ triples. Their squared masses are respectively $-1/(2\alpha'),0,1/(2\alpha'),1/\alpha'$. In the [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector), the [Ramond zero-mode Clifford algebra](#ramond-zero-mode-clifford-algebra) gives a sixteen-dimensional ground space. At level one, $\alpha_{-1}^i$ and $\psi_{-1}^i$ each provide $8\cdot16$ states, hence multiplicities $16,256$ at squared masses $0,1/\alpha'$. No half-integer Ramond levels occur. The [GSO projection](#gso-projection) keeps odd NS [fermion](quantum-mechanics.md#fermion) number and one total Ramond [chirality](relativistic-quantum-field.md#chirality-physics), giving $8$ [bosons](quantum-mechanics.md#boson) and $8$ [fermions](quantum-mechanics.md#fermion) at zero mass, then $128$ of each at the first massive level.

### First massive level of the open RNS string

↑ **Parent:** [Spinning string](#spinning-string)

After the [GSO projection](#gso-projection), the first massive open-string level has NS oscillator number $3/2$ and Ramond oscillator number $1$. Eight transverse directions give $8+64+\binom83=128$ NS states and $8\cdot8+8\cdot8=128$ R states. They form the massive Spin(9) representations $44+84$ and a gamma-traceless vector-spinor of dimension $9\cdot16-16=128$. The identical chiral oscillator count in a [closed string](#closed-string) has the different mass normalization $\alpha'M^2=4$.

### GSO projection

↑ **Parent:** [Spinning string](#spinning-string)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSO_projection)

The Gliozzi–Scherk–Olive projection selects consistent worldsheet fermion-number and chirality sectors in an RNS string theory. In suitable supersymmetric choices it removes the tachyonic [Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector) ground state and keeps a chosen [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector) chirality. Together with the consistent sector combinations it produces spacetime [fermions](quantum-mechanics.md#fermion) and, for theories such as [Type II superstring theory](#type-ii-string-theory), a tachyon-free spectrum. The mere presence of classical [worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry) does not specify this projection or guarantee tachyon removal.

<h3 id="neveu-schwarz-sector">Neveu–Schwarz sector</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Spinning string](#spinning-string)

Antiperiodic [worldsheet Majorana fermions](#worldsheet-majorana-fermion) have half-integer modes in this sector.

<h4 id="half-level-neveu-schwarz-vector-state">Half-level Neveu–Schwarz vector state</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector)

The lowest fermionic excitation has level $1/2$. Its old-covariant physical conditions are $\alpha'p^2+1/2-a=0$ and $p\cdot A=0$. For nonzero real momentum, timelike $p$ gives a positive polarization space, null $p$ gives a nonnegative space with a longitudinal null direction, and spacelike $p$ permits a negative-norm time polarization. The limiting $a=1/2$ state is a massless gauge vector after quotienting null polarizations. This level-specific test does not prove positivity of every higher NS level.

##### Nonzero-momentum condition in the massless vector norm test

↑ **Parent:** [Half-level Neveu–Schwarz vector state](#half-level-neveu-schwarz-vector-state)

The usual half-level NS claim that all physical vector polarizations have nonnegative norm for $a\le1/2$ assumes nonzero particle momentum. At zero momentum and limiting intercept, all positive-mode [constraints](classical-mechanics.md#constraint-mechanics) and transversality allow a timelike polarization with negative norm, and $A\sim A+\kappa p$ removes nothing. Excluding this exceptional momentum restores the ordinary null-vector argument; retaining it makes the unrestricted elementary criterion strict $a<1/2$.

<h4 id="neveu-schwarz-fourier-phase-space-action">Neveu–Schwarz Fourier phase-space action</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector)

The open NS action has integer bosonic modes and half-integer fermionic matter modes, together with even Virasoro and odd supercurrent multipliers. Opposite [fermion](quantum-mechanics.md#fermion) signs at the two endpoints make the doubled [fermion](quantum-mechanics.md#fermion) antiperiodic and remove a matter zero mode. The $b_r$ matter variables are distinct from the $bc$ reparameterization [FP ghosts](relativistic-quantum-field.md#faddeev-popov-ghost). Canonical quantization yields $[\alpha_k^m,\alpha_l^n]=k\eta^{mn}\delta_{k+l,0}$ and $\{b_r^m,b_s^n\}=\eta^{mn}\delta_{r+s,0}$.

<h4 id="neveu-schwarz-level-one-massive-tensor">Neveu–Schwarz level-one massive tensor</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector)

The unprojected level-one states form a transverse [vector representation](representation-theory.md#vector-representation) and an [exterior square](linear-algebra.md#exterior-square). Fermionic anticommutation makes the two-fermion states antisymmetric. Together they restrict from an antisymmetric rank-two tensor of $SO(D-1)$, not a [massive spin-two field](relativistic-quantum-field.md#massive-spin-two-field).

<h4 id="neveu-schwarz-level-operator">Neveu–Schwarz level operator</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector)

Before the [GSO projection](#gso-projection), $N=\sum_{k>0}\alpha_{-k}\cdot\alpha_k+\sum_{r>0}r\,b_{-r}\cdot b_r$ takes nonnegative half-integer values. The first vector at $N=1/2$ is massless for [string intercept](#normal-ordering-constant-of-a-string) $a_{\mathrm{NS}}=1/2$.

<h4 id="neveu-schwarz-fermionic-oscillator">Neveu–Schwarz fermionic oscillator</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector)

Antiperiodic [worldsheet Majorana fermions](#worldsheet-majorana-fermion) give transverse modes $b_r^I$, $r\in\mathbb Z+\tfrac12$, with $\{b_r^I,b_s^J\}=\delta^{IJ}\delta_{r+s,0}$. Positive modes annihilate the [oscillator vacuum](#oscillator-vacuum); negative modes are [fermionic creation operators](relativistic-quantum-field.md#fermionic-creation-operator).

### Ramond sector

↑ **Parent:** [Spinning string](#spinning-string)

Periodic [worldsheet Majorana fermions](#worldsheet-majorana-fermion) have integer modes and fermionic zero modes in this sector.

#### RNS spin field

↑ **Parent:** [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector)

A worldsheet operator that changes fermion boundary conditions to the [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector) and creates a target spinor state. For $D$ real matter fermions its conformal weight is $D/16$. At $D=10$, combining it with the appropriate superghost factor gives a dimension-one spacetime-supersymmetry current.

##### Spacetime supercharge from an RNS spin field

↑ **Parent:** [RNS spin field](#rns-spin-field)

In the critical [RNS string](#spinning-string), a spacetime [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md) generator is $Q_A=\oint e^{-\phi_{\rm gh}/2}S_A$. The [RNS spin field](#rns-spin-field) has weight $5/8$ and the superghost exponential has weight $3/8$, making a weight-one current. The [GSO projection](#gso-projection) ensures the locality needed to act on retained vertex operators. Its operator products generate translations and relate [NS sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector) and [R sector](#ramond-sector) states.

#### Ramond zero-mode Clifford algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector)

The zero-mode anticommutator $\{d_0^I,d_0^J\}_+=\delta^{IJ}$ becomes the Euclidean [Clifford algebra](algebra.md#clifford-algebra) after $\gamma^I=\sqrt2d_0^I$. It acts within the oscillator-vacuum space. Eight transverse Gamma matrices give a minimal complex representation of dimension sixteen, split into two eight-dimensional [chirality](relativistic-quantum-field.md#chirality-physics) sectors.

#### Ramond level operator

↑ **Parent:** [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector)

For integer transverse string modes, $N_R=\sum_{k>0}(\alpha_{-k}\cdot\alpha_k+k d_{-k}\cdot d_k)$ with vacuum-annihilating normal ordering. Each nonzero-mode creation operator raises the level by $k$; fermionic zero modes commute with it. The open-string mass condition is $M^2=2\pi TN_R$, with zero Ramond intercept.

### Worldsheet supersymmetry

↑ **Parent:** [Spinning string](#spinning-string)

Local [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md) on a [string worldsheet](#worldsheet) relates embedding coordinates to their [worldsheet Majorana fermion](#worldsheet-majorana-fermion) partners. Fixing this fermionic gauge symmetry introduces commuting [superconformal ghosts](#superconformal-ghost).

#### On-shell closure of rigid worldsheet supersymmetry

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry)

For $\delta X=-\bar\epsilon\psi$ and $\delta\psi=\rho^\alpha\partial_\alpha X\epsilon$, with constant Grassmann-odd Majorana parameters, the commutator on $X$ is the displayed translation. Set $M=\epsilon_1\bar\epsilon_2-\epsilon_2\bar\epsilon_1$. In two dimensions $\{\rho^\alpha,M\}=2\bar\epsilon_1\rho^\alpha\epsilon_2$ times the identity, so the fermion commutator is the translation minus $M\rho^\alpha\partial_\alpha\psi$. The remainder vanishes on the fermion field equation. This distinguishes on-shell closure from the off-shell total-derivative invariance of the action.

#### Superconformal gauge

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry)

The local gauge choice $h_{\mu\nu}=e^{2\omega}\eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\chi_\mu=0$ for an [RNS string](#spinning-string). Diffeomorphisms and Weyl symmetry fix the metric, while local [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md) and [super-Weyl symmetry](#super-weyl-symmetry) fix the gravitino. Stress-tensor and supercurrent constraints survive the gauge choice and eliminate longitudinal degrees of freedom. Higher-genus surfaces retain metric and spin-structure moduli.

#### Super-Weyl symmetry

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry)

A local fermionic gauge symmetry shifting the gamma-trace part of the [worldsheet gravitino](#worldsheet-gravitino), schematically $\delta\chi_\mu=\gamma_\mu\rho$. Together with local [worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry) it permits the gravitino to be set to zero locally in [superconformal gauge](#superconformal-gauge). Global spin structures and moduli still remain.

#### Worldsheet gravitino

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry)

The worldsheet gravitino is the fermionic vector-spinor gauge field for local [worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry), paired with the worldsheet metric in two-dimensional [supergravity](supersymmetry.md#supergravity). In a locally supersymmetric string action it couples to the worldsheet supercurrent. Fixing its gauge and the bosonic [conformal gauge](#conformal-gauge) leaves the usual flat rigid transformations on embeddings and [worldsheet Majorana fermions](#worldsheet-majorana-fermion); it should not be silently omitted when claiming an arbitrary curved-worldsheet locally supersymmetric action.

#### Rigid worldsheet supersymmetry boundary term

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry)

In flat [conformal gauge](#conformal-gauge), take $\mathcal L=\partial X\cdot\partial X+i\alpha'\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi$ and rigid transformations $\delta X=iA\bar\epsilon\psi$, $\delta\psi=B\gamma^\nu\partial_\nu X\epsilon$, with $A=\alpha'B$. The [Majorana Grassmann bilinear interchange](relativistic-quantum-field.md#majorana-grassmann-bilinear-interchange) and [Clifford algebra](algebra.md#clifford-algebra) combine the variations into

$$
\delta\mathcal L=\partial_\mu\bigl(iA\partial_\nu X\cdot\bar\epsilon\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\psi\bigr).
$$

The identity is off shell. The integrated action is invariant when its boundary flux vanishes, for example on a closed worldsheet or with compatible supersymmetric endpoint conditions. Constant spinors and ordinary derivatives here refer to flat gauge; arbitrary curved worldsheets require covariant spinor derivatives and the locally supersymmetric completion.

##### Chiral proof of rigid worldsheet supersymmetry

↑ **Parent:** [Rigid worldsheet supersymmetry boundary term](#rigid-worldsheet-supersymmetry-boundary-term)

Let $\partial_\pm=\partial_\tau\pm\partial_\sigma$ and $\mathcal L=\partial_+X\cdot\partial_-X+i\psi_1\cdot\partial_+\psi_1+i\psi_2\cdot\partial_-\psi_2$. For constant Grassmann-odd parameters, set $\delta X=i(\epsilon_2\psi_1-\epsilon_1\psi_2)$, $\delta\psi_1=-\epsilon_2\partial_-X$, $\delta\psi_2=\epsilon_1\partial_+X$. In the first chiral sector the variation of the fermionic term cancels $i\epsilon_2\partial_+\psi_1\cdot\partial_-X$ from the bosonic term, leaving $i\epsilon_2\partial_-(\psi_1\cdot\partial_+X)$. The second sector gives the other [total derivative](calculus.md#total-derivative). This proves off-shell invariance when the boundary flux vanishes. Closure on [fermions](quantum-mechanics.md#fermion) uses their free [equations of motion](classical-mechanics.md#equation-of-motion). A constant parameter must also preserve the chosen boundary conditions and [spin structure](riemannian-geometry.md#spin-structure).

##### Spin-structure obstruction to constant worldsheet supersymmetry

↑ **Parent:** [Rigid worldsheet supersymmetry boundary term](#rigid-worldsheet-supersymmetry-boundary-term)

The local rigid [worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry) identity $\delta\mathcal L=\partial_\mu J^\mu$ does not by itself prove preservation of every [spin structure](riemannian-geometry.md#spin-structure). If $X$ is periodic on a spatial circle and $\psi$ is antiperiodic, a constant $\epsilon$ gives antiperiodic $\delta X=i\bar\epsilon\psi$. It does not preserve the bosonic boundary condition. Compatible periodic fields admit the constant transformation; other sectors must be handled with the local superconformal formulation and their global spin data.

#### Superconformal ghost

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry)

The commuting [beta-gamma system](#beta-gamma-system) produced by [worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry) gauge fixing has [conformal weights](#conformal-weight) $(3/2,-1/2)$ and [central charge](#central-charge) $11$ per chiral sector.

#### Worldsheet Majorana fermion

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry)

Each real chiral fermion has [conformal weight](#conformal-weight) $1/2$ and contributes $1/2$ to the [central charge](#central-charge). The [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector) and [Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector) differ by periodic versus antiperiodic boundary conditions.

##### Worldsheet chirality and propagation direction

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet Majorana fermion](#worldsheet-majorana-fermion)

In signature $(-,+)$, choose real [gamma matrices](algebra.md#gamma-matrices) $\gamma^0=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $\gamma^1=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and $\gamma_*=-\gamma^0\gamma^1$. The [Dirac equation](relativistic-quantum-field.md#dirac-equation) then gives $(\partial_\tau+\partial_\sigma)\psi_+=0$ and $(\partial_\tau-\partial_\sigma)\psi_-=0$. Positive [chirality](relativistic-quantum-field.md#chirality-physics) is right-moving and negative [chirality](relativistic-quantum-field.md#chirality-physics) is left-moving in this orientation. Reversing the definition of $\gamma_*$ exchanges the labels without changing the physics.

## String coupling

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_coupling)

The string coupling controls topology change and string splitting and joining. A constant background [dilaton](#dilaton) $\Phi_0$ sets $g_s=e^{\Phi_0}$.

### String genus expansion

↑ **Parent:** [String coupling](#string-coupling)

For a connected oriented closed-string worldsheet of genus $g$ with $n$ external closed-string vertices, the perturbative amplitude carries $g_s^{2g-2+n}$. The exponent combines the dilaton factor $g_s^{-\chi}$ for Euler characteristic $\chi=2-2g$ with one $g_s$ from each normalized vertex.

#### String-loop effective action expansion

↑ **Parent:** [String genus expansion](#string-genus-expansion)

Connected oriented [closed string](#closed-string) worldsheets of genus $g$ give the spacetime effective-action term $g_s^{2g-2}S_g$. Each $S_g$ has a separate low-energy derivative expansion in $\alpha'$. The [string coupling](#string-coupling) orders quantum string loops, while $\alpha'$ orders effects of finite string size.

#### Dilaton Euler-characteristic weighting

↑ **Parent:** [String genus expansion](#string-genus-expansion)

A constant [dilaton](#dilaton) contributes $\Phi_0\chi(\Sigma)$ to the Euclidean [worldsheet](#worldsheet) action by the [Gauss-Bonnet theorem](differential-geometry.md#gauss-bonnet-theorem). With [string coupling](#string-coupling) $g_s=e^{\Phi_0}$ and [Euler characteristic](homology.md#euler-characteristic) $\chi=2-2g$, the path-integral weight is $g_s^{2g-2}$. Canonically normalized external closed-string vertices add one power of $g_s$ each.

### String perturbation theory

↑ **Parent:** [String coupling](#string-coupling)

String perturbation theory expands amplitudes in powers of the [String coupling](#string-coupling) by summing over worldsheet topologies. It is reliable where the local effective coupling $e^{\Phi(X)}$ is small.

#### Open-string scattering amplitude

↑ **Parent:** [String perturbation theory](#string-perturbation-theory)

Tree-level scattering of four ordered [open string](#open-string) tachyons gives the [Veneziano amplitude](#veneziano-amplitude). Its poles factorize through intermediate open-string states.

##### Veneziano amplitude

↑ **Parent:** [Open-string scattering amplitude](#open-string-scattering-amplitude)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneziano_amplitude)

The ordered tree-level four-tachyon [open string](#open-string) amplitude is proportional to $\Gamma(-1-\alpha's)\Gamma(-1-\alpha't)/\Gamma(-2-\alpha'(s+t))$. Its [Mandelstam variables](special-relativity.md#mandelstam-variables) locate the mass levels, and pole residues reveal spin content.

###### Fixed-angle softness of an open-string amplitude

↑ **Parent:** [Veneziano amplitude](#veneziano-amplitude)

At high energy with $x=-t/s\in(0,1)$ fixed, reflection and Stirling formulas for the [gamma function](complex-analysis.md#gamma-function) give the displayed exponential envelope, away from real-axis resonance poles or after suitable analytic continuation. It is softer than the power-law fixed-angle behavior of typical point-particle interactions and exposes the finite string length. This limit differs from the fixed-$t$ Regge limit and does not by itself prove finiteness of every loop amplitude.

###### Veneziano amplitude pole residue

↑ **Parent:** [Veneziano amplitude](#veneziano-amplitude)

In units $\alpha'=1$, generic fixed $t$ gives [simple poles](isolated-singularity.md#simple-pole) at $s=n-1$, $n\geq0$, with [residue](analysis.md#residue) $-\prod_{j=2}^{n+1}(t+j)/n!$. The empty product is one. A [residue](analysis.md#residue) zero removes that pole, and fixed $t$ must be away from its own channel singularities.

###### Odd-level pole cancellation in the symmetric Veneziano amplitude

↑ **Parent:** [Veneziano amplitude pole residue](#veneziano-amplitude-pole-residue)

For identical open-string tachyons, $s+t+u=-4/\alpha'$. At $\alpha's=n-1$, the ordered [Veneziano amplitude](#veneziano-amplitude) residues in the $s,t$ and $s,u$ terms differ by $(-1)^n$, so their symmetric sum cancels odd-level $s$-channel poles at generic $t$. In particular the massless vector does not couple to this identical-scalar singlet amplitude. It remains a state of the open-string spectrum and is visible in suitable ordered or color-dependent amplitudes. Pole absence due to an external-state selection rule must not be interpreted as absence of the exchanged state from the theory.

###### Massless vector pole of the Veneziano amplitude

↑ **Parent:** [Veneziano amplitude pole residue](#veneziano-amplitude-pole-residue)

The pole at $s=0$ has [residue](analysis.md#residue) $-(t+2)=-(t-u)/2$ for unit-slope tachyons. It is proportional to conserved scalar-pair vector currents and linear in the scattering-angle cosine, exhibiting a massless spin-one intermediate string state.

### Dilaton

↑ **Parent:** [String coupling](#string-coupling)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilaton)

The dilaton is a scalar closed-string background field coupled to the worldsheet Ricci scalar. Its constant mode determines the [string coupling](#string-coupling), while spacetime variation modifies the background-field equations.

#### Weyl variation of a nonconstant dilaton coupling

↑ **Parent:** [Dilaton](#dilaton)

In two worldsheet dimensions, $\delta_\omega(\sqrt gR)=-2\sqrt g\Delta_g\omega$. Thus the standard [dilaton](#dilaton) curvature coupling does not have classical [Weyl invariance](#weyl-transformation) for a general spacetime-varying dilaton. A constant dilaton gives the topological [Euler characteristic](homology.md#euler-characteristic) instead. In the quantum sigma model, this classical variation combines with the loop anomaly; vanishing improved [sigma-model beta functions](#sigma-model-beta-function) is the full background conformal condition.

#### Dilaton normalization and Euler-characteristic weighting

↑ **Parent:** [Dilaton](#dilaton)

With Euclidean curvature coupling $(4\pi)^{-1}\int\sqrt h\,\varphi R$, a constant [dilaton](#dilaton) contributes $\varphi_0\chi$ by the [Gauss-Bonnet theorem](differential-geometry.md#gauss-bonnet-theorem). The genus-$g$ closed-string weight is $e^{-\varphi_0\chi}=g_s^{2g-2}$ with $g_s=e^{\varphi_0}$. If the coefficient is instead written as $\int\sqrt h\,\Phi R$, then in the same convention $\varphi=4\pi\Phi$. The field normalization must be fixed before reading off the coupling.

#### Lorentzian dilaton coupling sign convention

↑ **Parent:** [Dilaton](#dilaton)

With worldsheet signature $(-,+)$, $e^{iS_L}=e^{-S_E}$, and the curvature convention $R[e^{2\omega}h]=e^{-2\omega}(R[h]-2\Box_h\omega)$, the usual negative Lorentzian kinetic action becomes positive in Euclidean signature. A positive Lorentzian term $(4\pi)^{-1}\int\sqrt{-h}\,R\Phi$ becomes a negative Euclidean curvature coupling. Therefore the conventional Euclidean [dilaton](#dilaton) with positive curvature coupling is $\varphi=-\Phi$. The [leading metric-dilaton Weyl condition](#leading-metric-dilaton-weyl-condition) is $R_{ab}+2\nabla_a\nabla_b\varphi=0$, or $R_{ab}-2\nabla_a\nabla_b\Phi=0$ with that literal positive Lorentzian coupling. Keeping the same named field and reversing the action's curvature-coupling sign reverses the Hessian term. The continuation follows directly from $d\tau=-i\,d\tau_E$ and $S_E=-iS_L$; the geometric scalar curvature continues without an extra arbitrary sign.

## Bosonic string theory

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosonic_string_theory)

Bosonic string theory quantizes a relativistic string using only bosonic worldsheet fields. Lorentz-invariant quantization requires 26-dimensional target spacetime and contains a tachyonic ground state.

### Internal free fermions in a four-dimensional bosonic string

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory)

Keeping the ordinary reparameterization [bc ghost system](#bc-system), four embedding bosons require 44 additional real chiral fermions in each chirality to cancel the [worldsheet Weyl anomaly](#worldsheet-weyl-anomaly). These are internal matter fields, not an assumed locally supersymmetric RNS construction. Their bilinear currents generate the chiral $\mathfrak{so}(44)$ current algebra, with $\binom{44}{2}=946$ currents. A full closed fermionic model also requires consistent spin-structure and modular projections; the central-charge count alone does not determine these choices.

<h4 id="zero-and-two-fermion-vertices-of-an-internally-fermionized-bosonic-string">Zero- and two-fermion vertices of an internally fermionized bosonic string</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Internal free fermions in a four-dimensional bosonic string](#internal-free-fermions-in-a-four-dimensional-bosonic-string)

In the unprojected polynomial Neveu–Schwarz vertex sector, zero fermion insertions give the usual closed-string tachyon and the massless graviton, two-form and dilaton. Two insertions split into a left-right pair $\psi^i\bar\psi^j e^{ipX}$ of internal weights $(1/2,1/2)$ and a same-chirality current pair of weight one. The first has $M^2=-2/\alpha'$ and 44-squared scalar components. The second requires a bosonic derivative on the opposite side for level matching and gives the two sets of 946 massless gauge vectors. Four-fermion scalar-current products belong to a different insertion count. A chosen global projection may retain a subset, so these operators do not by themselves specify a projected string spectrum.

### Open string

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_string)

An open string has a worldsheet with boundary. Its endpoint conditions may be [Neumann boundary conditions](differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition), [Dirichlet boundary conditions](differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition), or mixtures selected independently in target-space directions.

#### Open-string oscillator degeneracy

↑ **Parent:** [Open string](#open-string)

For an ordinary critical [open string](#open-string) with all [Neumann boundary conditions](differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition), there are 24 independent transverse creation oscillators at each positive mode number $n$. Summing their nonnegative occupation numbers gives one factor $(1-w^n)^{-24}$. The coefficient $d_p=[w^p]F(w)$ counts oscillator polarizations at level $p$, excluding continuous center-of-mass momentum and additional endpoint multiplicities. [Cauchy coefficient formula](analysis.md#cauchy-coefficient-formula) gives $d_p=(2\pi i)^{-1}\oint F(w)w^{-p-1}dw$.

##### Large-level degeneracy of an open bosonic string

↑ **Parent:** [Open-string oscillator degeneracy](#open-string-oscillator-degeneracy)

The [Dedekind eta function](#dedekind-eta-function) modular transformation gives $F(e^{-t})\sim(t/2\pi)^{12}\exp(4\pi^2/t-t)$. The [saddle-point approximation](analysis.md#saddle-point-approximation) for coefficient extraction has $t_0=2\pi/\sqrt{p-1}$, phase $4\pi\sqrt{p-1}$ and curvature $(p-1)^{3/2}/\pi$. Its prefactor $t_0^{12}$ supplies $(p-1)^{-6}$ and the Gaussian width supplies $(p-1)^{-3/4}/\sqrt2$. This explains both the exponential growth and the power $-27/4$.

#### Open-string endpoint momentum flux

↑ **Parent:** [Open string](#open-string)

For the [Nambu-Goto phase-space action](#nambu-goto-phase-space-action), $\dot P_m=\partial_\sigma J_m$ with $J_m=eT^2X'_m+uP_m$. Thus $\dot{\mathcal P}_m=J_m(\pi)-J_m(0)$. Free endpoint variations force $J_m=0$ individually. A [Dirichlet boundary condition](differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) allows an external support or [D-brane](#d-brane) to absorb momentum in the fixed direction.

##### Variational open-string boundary conditions

↑ **Parent:** [Open-string endpoint momentum flux](#open-string-endpoint-momentum-flux)

The spatial integration-by-parts term in the [Nambu-Goto phase-space action](#nambu-goto-phase-space-action) pairs endpoint displacement with momentum flux $J_m=eT^2X'_m+uP_m$. Free endpoint displacements require zero flux. Fixed normal coordinates instead set those variations to zero, while tangential variations retain a [Neumann boundary condition](differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition). Boundary conditions must also be preserved by the equations of motion. In a boundary-preserving gauge with $u=0$, mixed [Neumann boundary conditions](differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition) and [Dirichlet boundary conditions](differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) implement a planar [D-brane](#d-brane).

##### Free-end string boundary condition

↑ **Parent:** [Open-string endpoint momentum flux](#open-string-endpoint-momentum-flux)

Free endpoint variations set the [open string](#open-string) flux $eT^2X'_m+uP_m$ to zero. In a boundary-adapted temporal gauge, freedom to vary time forces $u=0$ at the end. Nonzero lapse then gives $X'^m=0$, a [Neumann boundary condition](differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition).

###### Null motion of a free string endpoint

↑ **Parent:** [Free-end string boundary condition](#free-end-string-boundary-condition)

In a boundary-adapted [temporal gauge](relativistic-quantum-field.md#temporal-gauge), a free [open string](#open-string) endpoint has $X'^m=0$. The [Nambu–Goto phase-space constraints](#nambu-goto-phase-space-constraints) then imply $P^2=0$, while $\dot X=eP$ implies $\dot X^2=0$. For $X^0=t$, its spatial speed is one. This null boundary is compatible with a timelike interior [worldsheet](#worldsheet).

#### Open-string mode expansion

↑ **Parent:** [Open string](#open-string)

In [conformal gauge](#conformal-gauge), a free open-string coordinate with [Neumann boundary conditions](differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition) has a cosine expansion. Canonical quantization turns its nonzero Fourier coefficients into [string oscillators](#string-oscillator).

##### Integer-mode expansion with parallel D-brane endpoints

↑ **Parent:** [Open-string mode expansion](#open-string-mode-expansion)

On $0\le\sigma\le\pi$, a coordinate with [Neumann boundary conditions](differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition) at both ends has a constant position, linear time [zero mode](linear-operator-theory.md#zero-mode) and cosine oscillators. A coordinate with [Dirichlet boundary conditions](differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) at both ends has a linear spatial interpolation between endpoint positions and sine oscillators, with no [momentum](classical-mechanics.md#momentum) [zero mode](linear-operator-theory.md#zero-mode). Both have [integer](number-theory.md#integer) frequencies. This differs from an [ND boundary condition](#neumann-dirichlet-open-string-boundary-condition), which applies different conditions at the two ends of the same coordinate and gives half-integer frequencies.

##### Oscillator symplectic form of an open string

↑ **Parent:** [Open-string mode expansion](#open-string-mode-expansion)

The Fourier kinetic term $\sum_{k>0}i\alpha_{-k}\cdot\dot\alpha_k/k$ supplies conjugate pairs, with $\alpha_{-k}=\alpha_k^*$. Inverting its [symplectic form](symplectic-geometry.md#symplectic-form) yields the displayed [Poisson brackets](classical-mechanics.md#poisson-bracket). Nonzero modes are independent of the center-of-mass pair, while $\alpha_0=\sqrt{2\alpha'}p$ is the momentum zero mode. The imaginary factors are compatible with a real action up to a total derivative.

##### Neumann-Dirichlet open-string boundary condition

↑ **Parent:** [Open-string mode expansion](#open-string-mode-expansion)

A transverse [open string](#open-string) coordinate has an ND boundary condition when one endpoint obeys a [Neumann boundary condition](differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition) and the other a [Dirichlet boundary condition](differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition). On a strip of width $\pi$, its nonconstant spatial [eigenfunctions](linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction) are $\cos((n+1/2)\sigma)$ when the free endpoint is at zero. They solve the endpoint equations because their derivatives vanish at zero and their values vanish at $\pi$. Reversing the endpoints gives sine [eigenfunctions](linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction) with the same frequencies. The fixed endpoint removes the dynamical [worldsheet zero mode](#worldsheet-zero-mode).

###### Bosonic ND boundary-changing conformal weight

↑ **Parent:** [Neumann-Dirichlet open-string boundary condition](#neumann-dirichlet-open-string-boundary-condition)

For one free boson, the mixed ND open-string sector has strip vacuum energy $E_0=1/48$. The strip-to-plane transformation relates this energy to the plane [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra) zero mode by $E_0=h-c/24$, with [central charge](#central-charge) $c=1$. Hence its ground [conformal weight](#conformal-weight) is $h=1/48+1/24=1/16$. For $d$ independent ND coordinates the weights add to $d/16$. At $d=24$, the plane matter ground weight is $3/2$, while the strip oscillator vacuum energy is $1/2$. The plane physical condition $L_0^{\mathrm{plane}}-1=0$ is therefore equivalent to $\alpha'p_\parallel^2+N+1/2=0$. The apparent different constants are coordinate/constraint conventions, not different mass spectra.

### Closed string

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_string)

A closed string has a cylindrical worldsheet without boundary. Periodicity permits independent left- and right-moving oscillator sectors and, in a compact target direction, winding modes.

#### Large-level degeneracy of a closed bosonic string

↑ **Parent:** [Closed string](#closed-string)

The left and right transverse oscillator sectors each have [generating function](real-analysis.md#generating-function) $\prod_{m\ge1}(1-q^m)^{-24}$. Its coefficient satisfies $d_N\sim2^{-1/2}(N-1)^{-27/4}e^{4\pi\sqrt{N-1}}$. [Closed-string level matching](#closed-string-level-matching) requires equal levels but leaves their polarizations independent, so $g_N=d_N^2$. With $\alpha'M^2=4(N-1)$, the exponential is $e^{\beta_H M}$ with $\beta_H=4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}$. The smooth rest-mass density is proportional to $M^{-26}e^{\beta_H M}$, since $dN/dM=\alpha'M/2$. This distinguishes degeneracy per level from density per unit mass. The [polynomial](polynomial.md) prefactor matters at the boundary of canonical convergence.

#### Closed-string mode expansion

↑ **Parent:** [Closed string](#closed-string)

A free closed-string embedding coordinate is the sum of center-of-mass zero modes and independent periodic left- and right-moving oscillator expansions.

### Covariant quantization of the bosonic string

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory)

Covariant quantization keeps target-spacetime Lorentz covariance manifest, imposes canonical commutation relations on every embedding coordinate and removes unphysical states with the [Virasoro constraints](#virasoro-constraint).

#### No-ghost theorem below the critical bosonic dimension

↑ **Parent:** [Covariant quantization of the bosonic string](#covariant-quantization-of-the-bosonic-string)

For integer target dimension $2\le d\le26$, intercept $a=1$ and ordinary nonzero momentum, the [old covariant string quantization](#old-covariant-string-quantization) conditions leave a positive-semidefinite physical inner product. Quotienting its physical [null string states](#null-string-state) removes gauge degeneracy. A [DDF operator](#ddf-operator) basis displays $d-2$ positive transverse oscillators; below dimension 26 the remaining physical longitudinal sector can be organized as a unitary [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra) sector with central charge $26-d$ and highest weight zero. At $d=26$ this extra sector becomes null and the quotient reduces to the 24 transverse oscillators. The result concerns negative-norm states, not the removal of the bosonic [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon), and it does not itself cancel the [worldsheet Weyl anomaly](#worldsheet-weyl-anomaly) of a subcritical matter theory.

#### DDF operator

↑ **Parent:** [Covariant quantization of the bosonic string](#covariant-quantization-of-the-bosonic-string)

DDF operators are transverse physical [string oscillators](#string-oscillator) constructed as contour integrals of weight-one [string vertex operators](#string-vertex-operator). Choose a null reference momentum $q$ and transverse polarizations $\epsilon_i$ orthogonal to it and to a reference on-shell momentum. The integrand is proportional to $\epsilon_i\cdot\partial X\,e^{inq\cdot X}$; its normalization and $q\cdot p$ are fixed so the operators obey the oscillator algebra in the title and commute with the positive-mode [Virasoro constraints](#virasoro-constraint). For an open-string tachyon seed, one can use $2\alpha' q\cdot p=1$. Acting with negative modes shifts momentum and raises oscillator level while preserving the physical mass-shell condition. Their transverse [Fock space](quantum-field-theory.md#fock-space) has positive norm and makes the physical content of the [no-ghost theorem for the critical bosonic string](#no-ghost-theorem-for-the-critical-bosonic-string) explicit.

#### No-ghost theorem for the critical bosonic string

↑ **Parent:** [Covariant quantization of the bosonic string](#covariant-quantization-of-the-bosonic-string)

In flat 26-dimensional [bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory), at ordinary nonzero momentum, take [physical string states](#physical-string-state) satisfying $L_{n>0}|\psi\rangle=0$ and $(L_0-1)|\psi\rangle=0$. The [inner product](linear-algebra.md#inner-product) restricted to these states is positive semidefinite. Quotienting by physical [null string states](#null-string-state) removes the degeneracy; the resulting [Hilbert space](hilbert-space.md) is represented by the 24 transverse [string oscillators](#string-oscillator). This is a statement about negative-norm matter states, not about removing the auxiliary [Faddeev-Popov ghosts](relativistic-quantum-field.md#faddeev-popov-ghost). It does not remove the bosonic [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon).

#### Level-two open-string polarization decomposition

↑ **Parent:** [Covariant quantization of the bosonic string](#covariant-quantization-of-the-bosonic-string)

For $d=26$, $q^2=-2$ and the [physical string state](#physical-string-state) $[\zeta_{\mu\nu}\alpha_{-1}^\mu\alpha_{-1}^\nu+b_\mu\alpha_{-2}^\mu]|p\rangle$, the [Virasoro constraints](#virasoro-constraint) give $b_\nu=-q^\mu\zeta_{\mu\nu}$ and $t=\zeta^\mu{}_{\mu}=-2q\cdot b$. Every solution has the displayed decomposition, where $q\cdot A=0$ and $c$ is symmetric, traceless and transverse to $q$. The vector piece is $L_{-1}(2A\cdot\alpha_{-1}|p\rangle)$; the scalar piece is $t(L_{-2}+3L_{-1}^2/2)|p\rangle/10$. These are [null string states](#null-string-state), leaving the transverse traceless massive tensor as the physical polarization.

#### Negative-norm string state

↑ **Parent:** [Covariant quantization of the bosonic string](#covariant-quantization-of-the-bosonic-string)

Time-coordinate oscillators produce negative norms in the covariant indefinite [Fock space](quantum-field-theory.md#fock-space). A physical negative-norm state would violate probabilistic unitarity. Such ghosts differ from the anticommuting [worldsheet ghost fields](#worldsheet-ghost-field) introduced by [gauge fixing](relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-fixing).

#### Old covariant string quantization

↑ **Parent:** [Covariant quantization of the bosonic string](#covariant-quantization-of-the-bosonic-string)

Old covariant quantization retains all target-space [string oscillators](#string-oscillator) and imposes $(L_0-a)|\psi\rangle=0$ and $L_n|\psi\rangle=0$ for $n>0$. Negative-mode constraints are adjoints, not further annihilation conditions. Quotienting physical [null string states](#null-string-state) removes redundant polarizations.

##### Level-two scalar in covariant string quantization

↑ **Parent:** [Old covariant string quantization](#old-covariant-string-quantization)

At $a=1$, let $k=\sqrt{2\alpha'}p$, $k^2=-2$. The physical scalar $[\alpha_{-1}\cdot\alpha_{-1}+(D+4)(k\cdot\alpha_{-1})^2/10+(D-1)k\cdot\alpha_{-2}/5]|p\rangle$ has norm $2(D-1)(26-D)/25$. It is positive below 26, null at 26, and a [string ghost state](#negative-norm-string-state) above 26. Matching the ordinary transverse spectrum removes this extra scalar at 26.

##### Level-one intercept bound in covariant string quantization

↑ **Parent:** [Old covariant string quantization](#old-covariant-string-quantization)

A polarization obeys $p\cdot\epsilon=0$ and $p^2=(a-1)/\alpha'$. If $a>1$, spacelike momentum allows a negative-norm timelike polarization. Thus ghost absence requires $a\leq1$. At $a=1$ the [null string state](#null-string-state) identification $\epsilon\sim\epsilon+\lambda p$ leaves $D-2$ polarizations.

#### Physical string state

↑ **Parent:** [Covariant quantization of the bosonic string](#covariant-quantization-of-the-bosonic-string)

A physical string state obeys the Virasoro mass-shell and positive-mode constraints. States differing by a null state represent the same physical polarization.

#### Spurious string state

↑ **Parent:** [Covariant quantization of the bosonic string](#covariant-quantization-of-the-bosonic-string)

A spurious string state is a positive-level Virasoro descendant orthogonal to all physical string states.

##### Null string state

↑ **Parent:** [Spurious string state](#spurious-string-state)

A null string state is both physical and spurious. It has zero norm and is quotiented from the physical state space.

###### Null-state quotient of a string

↑ **Parent:** [Null string state](#null-string-state)

The physical state space of [old covariant quantization](#old-covariant-string-quantization) is quotiented by its physical spurious states, which are orthogonal to all physical states. In a ghost-free sector these form the radical of the semidefinite inner product. The quotient retains positive physical polarizations.

###### Level-two scalar Virasoro null state

↑ **Parent:** [Null string state](#null-string-state)

For $L_0|p\rangle=-|p\rangle$, the descendant $Z=(L_{-2}+\tfrac32L_{-1}^2)|p\rangle$ obeys $L_1Z=0$ and $L_2Z=(D-26)|p\rangle/2$. It becomes physical and null at 26 and supplies the scalar quotient needed for the massive spin-two [light-cone gauge in string theory](#light-cone-gauge-in-string-theory) spectrum.

### String tension

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_tension)

String tension is energy per unit length. The Regge-slope parameter $\alpha'$ is conventionally defined by $T=(2\pi\alpha')^{-1}$.

#### String length

↑ **Parent:** [String tension](#string-tension)

The characteristic string length is $\ell_s=\sqrt{\alpha'}$, with $T=1/(2\pi\alpha')$. Excited string states have masses of order $1/\ell_s$ and higher-derivative corrections become important at that scale.

#### Regge trajectory

↑ **Parent:** [String tension](#string-tension)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regge_trajectory)

A Regge trajectory is an approximately linear relation between angular momentum and squared mass. A classical rotating open string obeys $J=\alpha'M^2$, with quantum effects adding an intercept.

##### Regge limit

↑ **Parent:** [Regge trajectory](#regge-trajectory)

The Regge limit of a scattering amplitude takes the center-of-mass energy invariant $s$ large at fixed momentum-transfer invariant $t$. A leading trajectory produces a power $s^{\alpha(t)}$, with signature and residue factors. For the bosonic [Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude](#virasoro-shapiro-amplitude) in dimensionless string variables, $\alpha(t)=2+2t$. This differs from fixed-angle high-energy scattering.

### Polyakov action

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyakov_action)

The Polyakov action introduces an independent worldsheet metric $g_{\alpha\beta}$:

$$
S_{\rm P}=-\frac T2\int_\Sigma d^2\sigma\,\sqrt{-g}\,
g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha X\mathbin\cdot\partial_\beta X.
$$

Its metric equation makes $g_{\alpha\beta}$ conformal to the [induced worldsheet metric](#induced-worldsheet-metric), reducing it classically to the [Nambu–Goto action](#nambu-goto-action).

#### String sigma model in a spacetime metric

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action)

Replacing the flat target metric in the [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action) by a [spacetime metric](general-relativity.md#spacetime-metric) $g_{\mu\nu}(X)$ gives the displayed string sigma model. The embedding fields $X^\mu$ map the [worldsheet](#worldsheet) into target spacetime, while $h_{ab}$ is an independent [worldsheet metric](#worldsheet-metric). Classical [worldsheet diffeomorphisms](#worldsheet-diffeomorphism) and [Weyl transformations](#weyl-transformation) permit [conformal gauge](#conformal-gauge); quantum consistency additionally requires vanishing [sigma-model beta functions](#sigma-model-beta-function).

##### String sigma model in an isotropic plane wave

↑ **Parent:** [String sigma model in a spacetime metric](#string-sigma-model-in-a-spacetime-metric)

In [conformal gauge](#conformal-gauge) and [light-cone gauge in string theory](#light-cone-gauge-in-string-theory) $X^+=\kappa\tau$, the transverse part of the [string sigma model in a spacetime metric](#string-sigma-model-in-a-spacetime-metric) reduces to

$$
S_\perp=\frac T2\int d\tau d\sigma\,\left(\dot X^I\dot X^I-X'^IX'^I-m^2\kappa^2X^IX^I\right).
$$

The omitted longitudinal term is a total time derivative. Each transverse coordinate obeys a massive [wave equation](wave-equation.md). If the spatial period is $L$, the longitudinal momentum is $p^+=T\kappa L$ in this normalization. Keeping the gauge slope explicit prevents missing a factor of $p^+$ when changing conventions.

###### Plane-wave string mode Hamiltonian

↑ **Parent:** [String sigma model in an isotropic plane wave](#string-sigma-model-in-an-isotropic-plane-wave)

Expand every transverse [closed string](#closed-string) coordinate in a real orthonormal [Fourier basis](fourier-series.md#fourier-basis) of the spatial circle. The [canonical momenta](classical-mechanics.md#canonical-momentum) are $p_{Ia}=T\dot q_{Ia}$, and spatial orthogonality reduces the field [Hamiltonian](classical-mechanics.md#hamiltonian) to the displayed independent [harmonic oscillators](classical-mechanics.md#simple-harmonic-motion). Canonical quantization gives $\omega_a(a_{Ia}^\dagger a_{Ia}+1/2)$ for each nonzero-frequency mode; a zero-frequency mode retains the free particle term $p_{Ia}^2/(2T)$. The formal [zero-point energy](quantum-mechanics.md#zero-point-energy) requires a regulator; it is not automatically the massless flat-background intercept. Opposite travelling-wave oscillator combinations diagonalize the translation generator and satisfy [closed-string level matching](#closed-string-level-matching).

###### Plane-wave string mode frequency

↑ **Parent:** [String sigma model in an isotropic plane wave](#string-sigma-model-in-an-isotropic-plane-wave)

For a [closed string](#closed-string) in a [string sigma model in an isotropic plane wave](#string-sigma-model-in-an-isotropic-plane-wave), insert $e^{-i\omega_n\tau+2\pi in\sigma/L}$ into the transverse [wave equation](wave-equation.md). Periodicity requires integer $n$, giving the displayed [dispersion relation](wave-equation.md#dispersion-relation). The zero spatial mode is an oscillator of frequency $|m\kappa|$; when this mass vanishes it becomes a free particle mode and needs the position-plus-linear-time expansion.

<h4 id="classical-equivalence-of-polyakov-and-nambu-goto-actions">Classical equivalence of Polyakov and Nambu–Goto actions</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action)

Varying the independent metric in the [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action) sets the [worldsheet stress tensor](#worldsheet-stress-energy-tensor) to zero. For a nondegenerate timelike embedding in two [worldsheet](#worldsheet) dimensions, the metric is proportional to the [induced worldsheet metric](#induced-worldsheet-metric). The conformal factor cancels from the action, which becomes the [Nambu–Goto action](#nambu-goto-action). This elimination is classical; the quantum measure and [worldsheet Weyl anomaly](#worldsheet-weyl-anomaly) require separate treatment.

#### Worldsheet stress-energy tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action)

Varying the independent [worldsheet metric](#worldsheet-metric) gives $T_{ab}=T[\partial_aX\cdot\partial_bX-\tfrac12\gamma_{ab}\gamma^{cd}\partial_cX\cdot\partial_dX]$. Its classical vanishing is the metric equation and produces the [Virasoro constraints](#virasoro-constraint).

#### Phase-space identification of the Polyakov metric

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action)

For positive lapse, eliminating momentum gives $(\dot X-uX')^2/(2e)-eT^2X'^2/2$. This is the [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action) for $\gamma_{ab}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}u^2-e^2T^2&u\\u&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, up to a [Weyl transformation](#weyl-transformation). Thus $e=1/T,u=0$ is [conformal gauge](#conformal-gauge).

#### Polyakov path integral

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action)

The Polyakov path integral integrates over string embeddings and worldsheet metrics, divided by worldsheet diffeomorphisms and Weyl transformations. Gauge fixing introduces worldsheet ghosts and moduli integrals.

##### Two-dimensional quantum-gravity interpretation of strings

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov path integral](#polyakov-path-integral)

The [Polyakov path integral](#polyakov-path-integral) integrates over [string worldsheet](#worldsheet) metrics as well as embedding fields, modulo [worldsheet diffeomorphisms](#worldsheet-diffeomorphism) and [Weyl transformations](#weyl-transformation). Summing surface topologies gives perturbative [two-dimensional quantum gravity](quantum-theory.md#two-dimensional-quantum-gravity) coupled to the coordinate matter fields. Gauge fixing retains [worldsheet moduli](#worldsheet-moduli) and introduces ghosts; a conformal gauge is locally flat, not a global flat metric on every genus. Cancellation of the [worldsheet Weyl anomaly](#worldsheet-weyl-anomaly) makes the critical bosonic version consistent.

##### Worldsheet moduli

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov path integral](#polyakov-path-integral)

Worldsheet moduli parameterize inequivalent conformal structures and marked insertion points after [worldsheet diffeomorphisms](#worldsheet-diffeomorphism) and [Weyl invariance](#weyl-transformation) are fixed. At each [genus](topology.md#genus-of-a-surface), [string perturbation theory](#string-perturbation-theory) integrates over the corresponding moduli space.

###### Worldsheet conformal Killing group

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet moduli](#worldsheet-moduli)

The worldsheet conformal Killing group is the connected residual group of conformal automorphisms of the chosen compact [Riemann surface](complex-analysis.md#riemann-surfaces). Its infinitesimal vectors satisfy the [Conformal Killing equation](general-relativity.md#conformal-killing-equation), or $P_1v=0$ for the [worldsheet diffeomorphism ghost operator](#worldsheet-diffeomorphism-ghost-operator). Its complex dimensions are three on a sphere, one on a torus, and zero for [genus](topology.md#genus-of-a-surface) at least two. The sphere group consists of [Möbius transformations](group-theory.md#mobius-transformation), while the torus's connected group is translation. Additional discrete automorphisms are handled by the moduli quotient rather than counted as continuous zero modes.

###### Infinitesimal worldsheet modulus

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet moduli](#worldsheet-moduli)

After quotienting infinitesimal [worldsheet diffeomorphisms](#worldsheet-diffeomorphism) and [Weyl transformations](#weyl-transformation), choose a metric variation orthogonal to the gauge directions. Orthogonality to Weyl scaling makes its trace zero; the [formal adjoint of the worldsheet conformal Killing operator](#formal-adjoint-of-the-worldsheet-conformal-killing-operator) makes its divergence zero. These are transverse-traceless variations of the two-dimensional metric. In a local conformal complex coordinate, their nonzero components satisfy $\partial_{\bar z}t_{zz}=0$ and its conjugate, linking them to [holomorphic quadratic differentials](complex-geometry.md#holomorphic-quadratic-differential).

###### Torus modular invariance

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet moduli](#worldsheet-moduli)

The torus worldsheet path integral is invariant under the [modular group](modular-function.md#modular-group) acting on its complex parameter $\tau$. Integrating over the [standard fundamental domain of the modular group](modular-function.md#standard-fundamental-domain-of-the-modular-group) counts each inequivalent torus once and excludes the short-proper-time ultraviolet region.

##### Closed-string scattering amplitude

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov path integral](#polyakov-path-integral)

A closed-string [scattering amplitude](quantum-mechanics.md#scattering-amplitude) is a worldsheet correlation function of external [string vertex operators](#string-vertex-operator), integrated over their unfixed positions and [worldsheet moduli](#worldsheet-moduli), with the necessary [worldsheet ghost fields](#worldsheet-ghost-field).

###### Worldsheet integration count after conformal gauge fixing

↑ **Parent:** [Closed-string scattering amplitude](#closed-string-scattering-amplitude)

For a stable $n$-point [closed-string scattering amplitude](#closed-string-scattering-amplitude) on a [genus](topology.md#genus-of-a-surface)-$g$ surface, let $d_g$ count complex unmarked [worldsheet moduli](#worldsheet-moduli) and $k_g$ count complex dimensions of the [worldsheet conformal Killing group](#worldsheet-conformal-killing-group). Integrating the $n$ vertex positions and moduli, then fixing $k_g$ positions, leaves $d_g+n-k_g$ complex integrations. The [punctured Riemann surface moduli dimension](geometry-and-topology.md#punctured-riemann-surface-moduli-dimension) identifies this as $3g-3+n$. [Faddeev-Popov ghost fields](relativistic-quantum-field.md#faddeev-popov-ghost) and antighost insertions implement the removed zero modes and the surviving modulus measure; they do not add independently integrated worldsheet coordinates.

###### Sphere tachyon position integral

↑ **Parent:** [Closed-string scattering amplitude](#closed-string-scattering-amplitude)

The tree-level [Polyakov path integral](#polyakov-path-integral) for closed-string [tachyons](physics.md#tachyon) reduces in [conformal gauge](#conformal-gauge) to their vertex-position integral on the [Riemann sphere](complex-analysis.md#riemann-sphere). Three positions are fixed by the residual [Möbius group](group-theory.md#mobius-group); the other $N-3$ complex positions are integrated. The embedding-field Gaussian integral produces [momentum conservation](classical-mechanics.md#momentum-conservation) and the [Koba-Nielsen factor](#koba-nielsen-factor).

###### Sphere gauge fixing for four string vertices

↑ **Parent:** [Closed-string scattering amplitude](#closed-string-scattering-amplitude)

At genus zero the residual [Möbius transformation](group-theory.md#mobius-transformation) group fixes three marked insertion points, leaving one complex position to integrate. The corresponding [worldsheet ghost fields](#worldsheet-ghost-field) give $|z_{ij}z_{jk}z_{ki}|^2$ for the three fixed points. For on-shell weight-$(1,1)$ [string vertex operators](#string-vertex-operator), these ghost factors cancel the coordinate dependence of the fixed-point normalization, including the limit at infinity. The remaining [Koba-Nielsen factor](#koba-nielsen-factor) for four tachyons is the integrand of the [complex beta integral](complex-analysis.md#complex-beta-integral), producing the [Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude](#virasoro-shapiro-amplitude).

###### String dual resonance

↑ **Parent:** [Closed-string scattering amplitude](#closed-string-scattering-amplitude)

A single [crossing-symmetric scattering amplitude](quantum-mechanics.md#crossing-symmetry) has equivalent pole expansions in different exchange channels. Its [scattering-amplitude factorization](quantum-mechanics.md#scattering-amplitude-factorization) reproduces the string spectrum and couplings in each channel without adding the channel expansions again.

##### Worldsheet Green function

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov path integral](#polyakov-path-integral)

A worldsheet Green function inverts the scalar kinetic operator after its zero modes have been removed. In a locally flat complex coordinate it satisfies $-(\pi\alpha')^{-1}\partial\bar\partial G(z,w)=\delta^{(2)}(z-w)$ and is $G(z,w)=-(\alpha'/2)\log|z-w|^2$ up to harmonic and constant terms.

##### String vertex operator

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov path integral](#polyakov-path-integral)

A string vertex operator inserts an asymptotic string state at a point of the worldsheet. Its conformal weights encode the physical-state and mass-shell conditions.

These insertions are described by the state-field correspondence of a [vertex operator algebra](algebra.md#vertex-operator-algebra); an individual insertion is not the whole algebra.

###### Integrated string vertex operator

↑ **Parent:** [String vertex operator](#string-vertex-operator)

An integrated closed-string vertex is inserted as $\int d^2z\,V(z,\bar z)$. In conformal gauge its matter operator must have [conformal weights](#conformal-weight) $(1,1)$ so that the insertion is invariant under worldsheet coordinate changes.

###### Massless closed-string vertex operator

↑ **Parent:** [Integrated string vertex operator](#integrated-string-vertex-operator)

At the first massless closed-string level, the matter vertex has the form $\epsilon_{\mu\nu}:\!\partial X^\mu\bar\partial X^\nu e^{ik\cdot X}\!:$, with null momentum and a polarization tensor transverse in both indices. Its symmetric trace-free, antisymmetric and trace sectors describe the graviton, [Kalb–Ramond field](#kalb-ramond-field) and [dilaton](#dilaton).

###### Polarization tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Massless closed-string vertex operator](#massless-closed-string-vertex-operator)

A closed-string polarization tensor specifies the target-spacetime tensor state multiplying $\partial X^\mu\bar\partial X^\nu e^{ik\cdot X}$. Its decomposition into symmetric trace-free, antisymmetric and trace parts separates the graviton, [Kalb–Ramond field](#kalb-ramond-field) and [dilaton](#dilaton) polarizations.

###### String-state gauge redundancy

↑ **Parent:** [Massless closed-string vertex operator](#massless-closed-string-vertex-operator)

Changing a massless closed-string polarization by a momentum-longitudinal tensor changes the integrated vertex by a worldsheet total derivative, equivalently by a BRST-exact state. The corresponding target-space transformations are linearized diffeomorphisms and two-form gauge transformations.

###### String mass-shell condition

↑ **Parent:** [String vertex operator](#string-vertex-operator)

A string mass-shell condition relates the target momentum of a vertex operator to its oscillator level. Together with polarization constraints, it makes the matter vertex have the conformal weights required for an integrated insertion.

###### Tachyon vertex operator

↑ **Parent:** [String vertex operator](#string-vertex-operator)

The bosonic closed-string tachyon vertex is the normal-ordered exponential ${}:e^{ip\cdot X}:$ integrated over its worldsheet position. Pairwise contractions of such exponentials generate the [Koba-Nielsen factor](#koba-nielsen-factor).

#### Worldsheet diffeomorphism

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action)

A worldsheet diffeomorphism is a smooth reparameterization of the worldsheet coordinates under which $X^\mu$ is a scalar and $g_{\alpha\beta}$ is a rank-two tensor.

##### Temporal gauge for a string

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet diffeomorphism](#worldsheet-diffeomorphism)

On a future-directed patch, choose target time as worldsheet time, $X^0=t$. The momentum equation then gives $P^0=1/e$ and $X'^0=0$. Freedom to vary target time at an open-string endpoint sets its shift to zero, simplifying the endpoint momentum-flux condition.

##### Static gauge

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet diffeomorphism](#worldsheet-diffeomorphism)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_gauge)

Static gauge identifies selected target-space coordinates with worldvolume coordinates, such as $X^0=\tau$ for a string.

###### Monge gauge

↑ **Parent:** [Static gauge](#static-gauge)

For a string, this local [static gauge](#static-gauge) identifies two embedding coordinates with the worldsheet coordinates, $X^0=t$ and $X^1=\sigma$. The remaining coordinates describe a graph over that coordinate plane. Folded embeddings may require more than one patch.

###### Physical transverse velocity of a tilted string

↑ **Parent:** [Monge gauge](#monge-gauge)

For $X=(t,\sigma,k(\sigma+t))$, the vertical coordinate velocity is $k$, but its normal projection has magnitude $|k|/\sqrt{1+k^2}<1$. Tangential motion merely changes string labels. The [induced worldsheet metric](#induced-worldsheet-metric) has determinant $-1$ even when constant-label curves become spacelike for $|k|>1$.

###### Monge-gauge Hamiltonian of a three-dimensional string

↑ **Parent:** [Monge gauge](#monge-gauge)

With unit tension and $X^0=t$, $X^1=\sigma$, the [Virasoro constraints](#virasoro-constraint) give $P_1=-\Pi\Phi'$ and $P_0=-\sqrt{(1+\Phi'^2)(1+\Pi^2)}$. The reduced energy density is $\mathcal H=-P_0$. On the chiral branch $\Pi=\Phi'$, the [Hamiltonian field equation](classical-mechanics.md#hamiltonian-field-equation) becomes $\dot\Phi=\Phi'$.

#### Weyl transformation

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_transformation)

A [Weyl transformation](#weyl-transformation) locally rescales a [metric tensor](general-relativity.md#metric-tensor), $g\mapsto e^{2\omega}g$, while retaining the same points and coordinates. Applied to a worldsheet metric, it leaves the embedding $X^\mu$ unchanged, and the classical [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action) is invariant under these [Weyl transformations](#weyl-transformation). Applied to a spacetime metric, a [Weyl rescaling](#weyl-transformation) can relate different frame conventions, such as a string metric and an Einstein metric; the accompanying field normalizations must be transformed as well. It is not itself a coordinate transformation.

#### Conformal gauge

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_gauge)

Conformal gauge uses worldsheet diffeomorphisms and Weyl transformations to set $g_{\alpha\beta}=e^{2\phi}\eta_{\alpha\beta}$ locally.

##### Residual conformal transformation

↑ **Parent:** [Conformal gauge](#conformal-gauge)

After conformal gauge fixing, independent reparameterizations $\sigma^+\mapsto f(\sigma^+)$ and $\sigma^-\mapsto\widetilde f(\sigma^-)$ remain, accompanied by a compensating Weyl transformation.

###### Light-cone gauge in string theory

↑ **Parent:** [Residual conformal transformation](#residual-conformal-transformation)

Light-cone gauge uses residual conformal transformations to make the target-space coordinate $X^+$ linear in worldsheet time. The Virasoro constraints then determine $X^-$ from the transverse fields.

This is the string-worldsheet realization of [light cone gauge](relativistic-quantum-field.md#light-cone-gauge).

###### Light-cone super-Virasoro constraints

↑ **Parent:** [Light-cone gauge in string theory](#light-cone-gauge-in-string-theory)

On a patch with $\alpha_0^+\ne0$, use [worldsheet diffeomorphisms](#worldsheet-diffeomorphism) and [worldsheet supersymmetry](#worldsheet-supersymmetry) to fix $\alpha_{n\ne0}^+=0$ and $\psi_r^+=0$. The [Virasoro constraints](#virasoro-constraint) and [superconformal current](#superconformal-current) constraints then determine the longitudinal modes by the displayed transverse bilinears. In the [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector), reduction of the fermionic zero modes includes the zero-mode Dirac constraint; fixing only nonzero modes does not complete the reduction. Eight transverse bosons and eight transverse fermions remain in the critical ten-dimensional [RNS string](#spinning-string).

###### Residual mass-shell action in light-cone string gauge

↑ **Parent:** [Light-cone gauge in string theory](#light-cone-gauge-in-string-theory)

Fixing nonzero longitudinal oscillators leaves the center-of-mass symplectic term, transverse oscillator terms, and a zero-mode multiplier imposing the [mass-shell condition](#string-mass-shell-condition). On a patch with $p^+\ne0$, further choosing $x^+$ as time solves that [constraint](classical-mechanics.md#constraint-mechanics) for $p^-=[p_\perp^2+(N-a)/\alpha']/(2p^+)$. This distinguishes partial oscillator gauge fixing from the subsequent time choice.

##### Virasoro constraint

↑ **Parent:** [Conformal gauge](#conformal-gauge)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virasoro_constraint)

The worldsheet metric equation in conformal gauge sets the stress tensor to zero, $T_{++}=T_{--}=0$. In oscillator language these are the Virasoro constraints.

###### Closed-string physical-state Virasoro conditions

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro constraint](#virasoro-constraint)

Critical [closed bosonic strings](#closed-string) have independent left- and right-moving [Virasoro constraints](#virasoro-constraint). Positive modes in both sectors annihilate physical kets, while both zero-mode conditions impose the mass shell and [closed-string level matching](#closed-string-level-matching). Quotient by physical [null string states](#null-string-state), equivalently use the appropriate [BRST cohomology](relativistic-quantum-field.md#brst-cohomology). With $L_0=\alpha'k^2/4+N$, the result is $N=\widetilde N$ and $M^2=4(N-1)/\alpha'$. Negative matter modes are adjoints rather than additional ket-annihilation constraints.

###### Physical-state Virasoro conditions for an open string

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro constraint](#virasoro-constraint)

The covariant physical-state conditions impose positive-mode [Virasoro constraints](#virasoro-constraint) on kets and the adjoint conditions on bras, with intercept $a$ in the zero mode. They are a string version of [Gupta-Bleuler quantization](relativistic-quantum-field.md#gupta-bleuler-formalism). Imposing every positive and negative matter mode on the same state would conflict with the [Virasoro central extension](#virasoro-central-extension). In critical [bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory), $a=1$ and the matter [central charge](#central-charge) is $26$, canceled by the reparameterization ghosts.

###### Classical Virasoro constraint algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro constraint](#virasoro-constraint)

With the [oscillator symplectic form of an open string](#oscillator-symplectic-form-of-an-open-string), the quadratic generators $L_n=\sum_k\alpha_k\cdot\alpha_{n-k}/2$ obey $\{\alpha_k,L_n\}=-ik\alpha_{k+n}$. Their [Poisson brackets](classical-mechanics.md#poisson-bracket) close without a central term, so they are [first-class constraints](classical-mechanics.md#first-class-constraint). Quantization requires [normal ordering](perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#normal-ordering) and can produce the [Virasoro central extension](#virasoro-central-extension).

#### String nonlinear sigma model

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action)

The string nonlinear sigma model couples the worldsheet embedding fields to target-space backgrounds such as a metric, a [Kalb–Ramond field](#kalb-ramond-field) and a [dilaton](#dilaton). Quantum Weyl invariance requires its beta functions to vanish.

##### String-frame massless effective action

↑ **Parent:** [String nonlinear sigma model](#string-nonlinear-sigma-model)

The leading massless-sector spacetime action is proportional to $\int\sqrt{-G}e^{-2\Phi}[R+4(\nabla\Phi)^2-H^2/12]$, with $H=dB$. The backgrounds are the [string-frame metric](#string-frame-metric), [dilaton](#dilaton), and [Kalb–Ramond field](#kalb-ramond-field). Massive string exchange generates higher-derivative corrections ordered by $\alpha'$. This perturbative bosonic massless-sector truncation does not remove the [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon) instability.

##### String-frame metric

↑ **Parent:** [String nonlinear sigma model](#string-nonlinear-sigma-model)

The string-frame metric is the target metric appearing directly in the kinetic term of the [string nonlinear sigma model](#string-nonlinear-sigma-model). With conventional Euclidean [dilaton](#dilaton) $\varphi$, its leading spacetime gravitational action contains $\sqrt{-g^{(s)}}e^{-2\varphi}(R+4|\nabla\varphi|^2)$. The dilaton-dependent Ricci coefficient distinguishes it from the [Einstein-frame metric](#einstein-frame-metric). Frame changes are local field redefinitions where the conformal factor is finite and nonzero; the [Lorentzian dilaton coupling sign convention](#lorentzian-dilaton-coupling-sign-convention) fixes which named field is $\varphi$.

###### Einstein-frame metric

↑ **Parent:** [String-frame metric](#string-frame-metric)

For $D>2$, rescale the [string-frame metric](#string-frame-metric) by $g^{(E)}=e^{-4\varphi/(D-2)}g^{(s)}$. The [Weyl transformation](#weyl-transformation) of the [Ricci scalar](general-relativity.md#ricci-scalar) makes the coefficient of the Einstein-frame Ricci term independent of the [dilaton](#dilaton). Indeed $g^{(s)}=e^{4\varphi/(D-2)}g^{(E)}$ contributes factors $e^{2D\varphi/(D-2)}$ from the volume, $e^{-2\varphi}$ from the original coupling and $e^{-4\varphi/(D-2)}$ from the Ricci term; their product is one. Derivative terms supply the transformed dilaton kinetic term. The two-dimensional case is excluded from this particular rescaling.

##### Background field expansion of a string sigma model

↑ **Parent:** [String nonlinear sigma model](#string-nonlinear-sigma-model)

Expand the [string embedding map](#string-embedding-map) along target geodesics as $X=\exp_{\bar X}Y$. The quadratic Euclidean fluctuation action has a [covariant derivative](general-relativity.md#covariant-derivative) term and a curvature term $-R_{acbd}Y^aY^b\partial\bar X^c\partial\bar X^d$. The logarithmically divergent coincident contraction is proportional to $\alpha'g^{ab}$; its curvature contraction is the [Ricci tensor](general-relativity.md#ricci-tensor). A compensating metric [counterterm](perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#counterterm) gives $\beta^g_{ab}=\alpha'R_{ab}+O(\alpha'^2)$. This is why the target gravitational equation arises from quantum [worldsheet Weyl anomaly](#worldsheet-weyl-anomaly) cancellation, rather than from the classical worldsheet metric variation. Infrared regulation and removal of equation-of-motion terms by field redefinitions specify the usual one-loop scheme.

<h5 id="kalb-ramond-field">Kalb–Ramond field</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [String nonlinear sigma model](#string-nonlinear-sigma-model)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalb–Ramond_field)

The Kalb–Ramond field is an antisymmetric two-form background coupled to the oriented worldsheet area element. Its gauge-invariant field strength is the three-form $H=dB$.

###### Light-cone gauge for a two-form

↑ **Parent:** [Kalb–Ramond field](#kalb-ramond-field)

When $\partial_-$ is invertible, choose $B_{-m}=0$. Residual gauge parameters are exact one-forms and act trivially on $B$. The remaining independent modes are antisymmetric transverse components $B_{IJ}$, satisfying $\square B_{IJ}=0$; longitudinal components obey $B_{+I}=\partial_-^{-1}\partial^JB_{IJ}$. There are $(D-2)(D-3)/2$ physical polarizations.

###### Kalb-Ramond field strength

↑ **Parent:** [Kalb–Ramond field](#kalb-ramond-field)

The three-form field strength of the [Kalb–Ramond field](#kalb-ramond-field) is $H=dB$. Its components are $H_{mnp}=\partial_mB_{np}+\partial_nB_{pm}+\partial_pB_{mn}$. In flat spacetime the free equation is $\partial^mH_{mnp}=0$.

###### H-field

↑ **Parent:** [Kalb–Ramond field](#kalb-ramond-field)

The H-field is the three-form field strength of a [Kalb–Ramond field](#kalb-ramond-field). It obeys the Bianchi identity $dH=0$ and contributes quadratically to the leading string background equations.

##### Sigma-model beta function

↑ **Parent:** [String nonlinear sigma model](#string-nonlinear-sigma-model)

A sigma-model beta function measures the failure of a worldsheet background coupling to remain scale invariant. Vanishing metric, B-field and dilaton beta functions gives the spacetime field equations order by order in $\alpha'$.

###### One-loop metric beta function of a string sigma model

↑ **Parent:** [Sigma-model beta function](#sigma-model-beta-function)

For the Euclidean kinetic normalization $(4\pi\alpha')^{-1}\int g_{ab}(X)\partial X^a\partial X^b$, vanishing two-form and constant [dilaton](#dilaton), the leading beta function is $\beta^g_{ab}=\alpha'R_{ab}$. The covariant Gaussian fluctuation operator contains a curvature vertex whose one-loop trace contracts to the [Ricci tensor](general-relativity.md#ricci-tensor). Its logarithmic divergence renormalizes the metric. The scale convention is $\beta=\mu\,dg/d\mu$; Weyl consistency requires vanishing of this beta function as well as the other anomaly coefficients.

###### Leading dilaton Weyl anomaly coefficient

↑ **Parent:** [Sigma-model beta function](#sigma-model-beta-function)

For the conventional positive Euclidean [dilaton](#dilaton) curvature coupling, with no antisymmetric background field, the leading scalar Weyl coefficient is the displayed expression. The matter/ghost [central charge](#central-charge) gives $(D-26)/6$. In a geodesic [background field expansion of a string sigma model](#background-field-expansion-of-a-string-sigma-model), contracting $\tfrac12\nabla_a\nabla_b\varphi\,Y^aY^b$ in the curvature coupling produces the [counterterm](perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#counterterm) $\delta\varphi=-\alpha'\Box\varphi/(2\varepsilon)$ and hence the Laplacian term. The full embedding equation $\Box_hX^a+\Gamma^a{}_{bc}\partial X^b\partial X^c=(\alpha'/2)R^{(2)}\nabla^a\varphi$ supplies the gradient-square improvement in the dilaton Weyl variation. Setting this coefficient to zero fixes the constant in the [dilaton equation from contracted Bianchi identity](#dilaton-equation-from-contracted-bianchi-identity); the metric equation alone does not. The [Lorentzian dilaton coupling sign convention](#lorentzian-dilaton-coupling-sign-convention) determines the named-field translation.

The noncritical coefficient is also given in [Tong's string theory notes, Section 7.4.4](https://davidtong.org/pdfs/teaching/string-theory/string7.pdf).

###### Leading metric-dilaton Weyl condition

↑ **Parent:** [Sigma-model beta function](#sigma-model-beta-function)

For the conventional Euclidean [dilaton](#dilaton) coupling $+(4\pi)^{-1}\int\sqrt h\,R\varphi$ and no antisymmetric background, the metric [worldsheet Weyl anomaly](#worldsheet-weyl-anomaly) vanishes at first order in $\alpha'$ when $R_{ab}+2\nabla_a\nabla_b\varphi=0$. The one-loop Ricci term follows from the [background field expansion of a string sigma model](#background-field-expansion-of-a-string-sigma-model). Under a [Weyl transformation](#weyl-transformation), the dilaton coupling varies as $-(2\pi)^{-1}\int\sqrt h\,\omega\Box_h\varphi$; the embedding equations replace this by the target Hessian contribution. Full Weyl invariance also requires the dilaton curvature coefficient, including the central-charge deficit, to vanish. See the [Lorentzian dilaton coupling sign convention](#lorentzian-dilaton-coupling-sign-convention) when translating an action with a specified Lorentzian sign.

The conventional coefficient agrees with the background equations in [Tong's string theory notes, Section 7.2.3](https://davidtong.org/pdfs/teaching/string-theory/string7.pdf).

###### Dilaton equation from contracted Bianchi identity

↑ **Parent:** [Leading metric-dilaton Weyl condition](#leading-metric-dilaton-weyl-condition)

Diverging $R_{ab}+2\nabla_a\nabla_b\varphi=0$ and using the [contracted Bianchi identity](general-relativity.md#contracted-bianchi-identity) and [Ricci identity](general-relativity.md#curvature-commutator-on-a-covariant-tensor) gives $\nabla_b(R+4\Box_g\varphi-4|\nabla\varphi|^2)=0$. The trace equation $R+2\Box_g\varphi=0$ then gives $\Box_g\varphi-2|\nabla\varphi|^2=C$, with $C$ constant on each connected component. Zero does not follow from the tensor equation alone. The additional dilaton anomaly coefficient fixes $C=(D-26)/(3\alpha')$ at leading order for the bosonic string without an antisymmetric background. In critical dimension it is zero; noncritical backgrounds have a central-charge-deficit term.

###### Linear dilaton counterexample to zero integration constant

↑ **Parent:** [Dilaton equation from contracted Bianchi identity](#dilaton-equation-from-contracted-bianchi-identity)

In flat target space, $\varphi=q_aX^a$ has zero Hessian and therefore solves the [leading metric-dilaton Weyl condition](#leading-metric-dilaton-weyl-condition) for every constant covector $q$. But $\Box\varphi-2|\nabla\varphi|^2=-2q^2$, which need not vanish. This demonstrates the integration constant left by the [contracted Bianchi identity](general-relativity.md#contracted-bianchi-identity). Full quantum Weyl invariance fixes the allowed $q^2$ through the [central charge](#central-charge), as in a [linear dilaton conformal field theory](#linear-dilaton-conformal-field-theory); the metric equation alone does not.

###### Exponentiated dilaton wave equation

↑ **Parent:** [Dilaton equation from contracted Bianchi identity](#dilaton-equation-from-contracted-bianchi-identity)

For the [dilaton equation from contracted Bianchi identity](#dilaton-equation-from-contracted-bianchi-identity), put $F=e^{-2\varphi}$. The chain rule gives $\Box_gF=F(-2\Box_g\varphi+4|\nabla\varphi|^2)=-2CF$. Thus the exponential obeys a linear [Klein-Gordon equation](wave-equation.md#klein-gordon-equation) on the given background, although the original [dilaton](#dilaton) equation is nonlinear and the [string-frame metric](#string-frame-metric) remains coupled to the [dilaton](#dilaton). With the opposite named-field convention $\varphi=-\Phi$, this variable is $F=e^{2\Phi}$.

###### Worldsheet Weyl anomaly

↑ **Parent:** [Sigma-model beta function](#sigma-model-beta-function)

A worldsheet Weyl anomaly is the quantum failure of the gauge-fixed string path integral to remain invariant under a [Weyl transformation](#weyl-transformation). Its local coefficients are the [sigma-model beta functions](#sigma-model-beta-function), while its constant part is controlled by the total [central charge](#central-charge).

###### Bosonic sigma-model Weyl anomaly coefficients

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet Weyl anomaly](#worldsheet-weyl-anomaly)

To first order in $\alpha'$, the conventional Euclidean [dilaton](#dilaton) coupling gives $\overline\beta^G_{\mu\nu}=\alpha'[R_{\mu\nu}-H_{\mu\rho\sigma}H_\nu{}^{\rho\sigma}/4+2\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\Phi]$ and $\overline\beta^B_{\mu\nu}=\alpha'[-\nabla^\rho H_{\rho\mu\nu}/2+(\nabla^\rho\Phi)H_{\rho\mu\nu}]$. The scalar coefficient is $(d-26)/6+\alpha'[(\nabla\Phi)^2-\nabla^2\Phi/2-H^2/24]$. Here $H$ is the [Kalb-Ramond field strength](#kalb-ramond-field-strength). Vanishing of these coefficients, including higher-order corrections, is the background-field condition for quantum [Weyl transformations](#weyl-transformation) to remain a gauge symmetry. The scalar coefficient includes the [central charge](#central-charge) deficit and is not implied by the metric equation alone.

#### Worldsheet ghost field

↑ **Parent:** [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action)

Gauge fixing worldsheet diffeomorphism and Weyl symmetry introduces an anticommuting vector ghost $c^\alpha$ and a symmetric traceless antighost $b_{\alpha\beta}$. In complex coordinates they form holomorphic and antiholomorphic $bc$ systems.

##### Three-point worldsheet ghost correlator

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet ghost field](#worldsheet-ghost-field)

On the [Riemann sphere](complex-analysis.md#riemann-sphere), three holomorphic reparameterization ghost zero modes correspond to the three infinitesimal [Möbius transformations](group-theory.md#mobius-transformation). Three $c$ insertions saturate their [Grassmann integral](quantum-mechanics.md#berezin-integral). In a basis $1,z,z^2$, their integral is the Vandermonde determinant, giving $z_{12}z_{13}z_{23}$ up to an ordering-dependent sign. Including the antiholomorphic factor gives $|z_{12}z_{13}z_{23}|^2$, the Jacobian in [sphere gauge fixing for four string vertices](#sphere-gauge-fixing-for-four-string-vertices).

##### Ghost oscillator Virasoro generators

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet ghost field](#worldsheet-ghost-field)

Contracting an antighost with the cubic [FP ghost](relativistic-quantum-field.md#faddeev-popov-ghost) term of the [BRST operator](#brst-operator) produces the bilinear generators. They give $[L_m,b_n]=(m-n)b_{m+n}$ and $[L_m,c_n]=-(2m+n)c_{m+n}$. Their zero-mode additive constant depends on the vacuum and [normal ordering](perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#normal-ordering) convention; it must agree with the corresponding $c_0$ term in the charge.

##### Ghost Virasoro zero-mode convention

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet ghost field](#worldsheet-ghost-field)

With the [ghost oscillator vacuum](#ghost-oscillator-vacuum), one can define $L_0^{\mathrm{gh}}=\sum_{k>0}k(b_{-k}c_k+c_{-k}b_k)$ with vacuum eigenvalue zero. The corresponding [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra) contains a linear shift: $[L_m^{\mathrm{gh}},L_n^{\mathrm{gh}}]=(m-n)(L_{m+n}^{\mathrm{gh}}-\delta_{m+n,0})-\tfrac{26}{12}(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}$. Shifting the zero mode by minus one removes the linear term but changes the stated vacuum eigenvalue. A [BRST charge](relativistic-quantum-field.md#brst-charge) must use a consistent choice throughout.

##### Ghost oscillator vacuum

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet ghost field](#worldsheet-ghost-field)

For the anticommuting [bc system](#bc-system), $\{b_m,c_n\}=\delta_{m+n,0}$. A useful oscillator vacuum obeys $b_n|0\rangle=c_n|0\rangle=0$ for $n>0$ and $b_0|0\rangle=0$. Its partner $c_0|0\rangle$ is nonzero. This specifies a ghost zero-mode doublet and differs from imposing annihilation by both zero modes.

###### Ghost zero-mode pairing

↑ **Parent:** [Ghost oscillator vacuum](#ghost-oscillator-vacuum)

With Hermitian $b_0,c_0$ and $\{b_0,c_0\}=1$, a ket annihilated by $b_0$ has zero bare full-ghost norm. Nonzero amplitudes need [FP ghost](relativistic-quantum-field.md#faddeev-popov-ghost) zero-mode saturation. Low-level [Virasoro descendant](#virasoro-descendant) calculations can instead normalize the highest-weight coefficient and compute $L_nL_{-n}|\Omega\rangle$ algebraically. Such normalized contravariant coefficients are not positive-definite Hilbert norms. The pairing convention must be stated before using [FP ghost](relativistic-quantum-field.md#faddeev-popov-ghost) descendants in a norm argument.

##### BRST operator

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet ghost field](#worldsheet-ghost-field)

The BRST operator is the Grassmann-odd conserved charge that combines a gauge transformation with its ghost field. Quantum gauge consistency requires $Q_B^2=0$, and physical states are its cohomology classes.

###### Virasoro closure from BRST nilpotence

↑ **Parent:** [BRST operator](#brst-operator)

If $L_m=\{b_m,Q\}$, nilpotence gives $[L_m,Q]=0$. The [graded Jacobi identity](lie-algebra.md#graded-jacobi-identity) and $[b_m,L_n]=(m-n)b_{m+n}$ then imply the displayed algebra. Thus a quantum central extension in the total generators obstructs [BRST nilpotence](relativistic-quantum-field.md#brst-nilpotence).

###### BRST physical-state constraints of an open string

↑ **Parent:** [BRST operator](#brst-operator)

For a matter state tensored with the [ghost oscillator vacuum](#ghost-oscillator-vacuum), the [BRST charge](relativistic-quantum-field.md#brst-charge) produces the independent ghost states $c_0|0\rangle$ and $c_{-m}|0\rangle$ with coefficients $(L_0-1)|\Psi\rangle$ and $L_m|\Psi\rangle$. Thus BRST closure is equivalent to $(L_0-1)|\Psi\rangle=0$ and $L_{m>0}|\Psi\rangle=0$ in this sector. It reproduces [old covariant string quantization](#old-covariant-string-quantization) and the [open bosonic string mass spectrum](#open-bosonic-string-mass-spectrum).

###### BRST current

↑ **Parent:** [BRST operator](#brst-operator)

The BRST current is the conserved worldsheet current whose contour integral is the [BRST operator](#brst-operator). For the bosonic string it is built from the reparameterization ghost and the matter and ghost stress tensors; its self-OPE is nonsingular only when the total central charge vanishes.

##### Worldsheet ghost action

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet ghost field](#worldsheet-ghost-field)

For the traceless diffeomorphism operator

$$
(P_1c)_{\alpha\beta}=\nabla_\alpha c_\beta+\nabla_\beta c_\alpha-g_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\gamma c^\gamma,
$$

the ghost action is

$$
S_{\rm gh}=\frac1{2\pi}\int d^2\sigma\,\sqrt g\,
b^{\alpha\beta}(P_1c)_{\alpha\beta}.
$$

###### Chiral conformal-gauge BRST nilpotence is on shell

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet ghost action](#worldsheet-ghost-action)

In [conformal gauge](#conformal-gauge), let the odd [left-acting BRST differential](relativistic-quantum-field.md#left-acting-brst-differential) act by $sX=c^+\partial_+X+c^-\partial_-X$ and $sc^\pm=c^\pm\partial_\pm c^\pm$. The [graded Leibniz rule](commutative-algebra.md#graded-leibniz-rule) gives the displayed expression for $s^2X$. It vanishes using the ghost [equations of motion](classical-mechanics.md#equation-of-motion) $\partial_-c^+=\partial_+c^-=0$, but it is not zero for arbitrary off-shell fields. For independent [Grassmann variables](linear-algebra.md#grassmann-variable) $\xi,\rho$, take $c^+=\xi$, $c^-=\rho\sigma^+$ and $X=\sigma^-$; then $s^2X=-\xi\rho\ne0$. Each ghost itself satisfies $s^2c^\pm=0$ algebraically. With the eliminated-antighost transformation $sb_{\pm\pm}=i\theta_{\pm\pm}/\alpha'$ and $s\theta_{\pm\pm}=0$ on the solution space, the antighosts are nilpotent too. This on-shell closure is compatible with an off-shell total-derivative variation of the gauge-fixed action. A full off-shell [BRST quantization](relativistic-quantum-field.md#brst-quantization) uses the complete gauge transformation and, where required, auxiliary fields rather than silently dropping mixed ghost derivatives.

###### Trace elimination in string Faddeev-Popov gauge fixing

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet ghost action](#worldsheet-ghost-action)

In two dimensions the metric variation splits into a scalar trace and the trace-free diffeomorphism operator $(P_1v)_{ab}=\nabla_av_b+\nabla_bv_a-g_{ab}\nabla\cdot v$. Integrating the Weyl parameter in the inverse [Faddeev-Popov determinant](relativistic-quantum-field.md#faddeev-popov-determinant) removes the trace delta function. The remaining determinant is exponentiated by an anticommuting vector ghost and symmetric trace-free antighost, yielding the two chiral $b\bar\partial c$ terms. Conformal Killing vectors and moduli are zero modes treated separately from this local determinant.

###### Worldsheet diffeomorphism ghost operator

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet ghost action](#worldsheet-ghost-action)

The [trace](linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace)-free metric variation is $(P_1c)_{ab}=\nabla_ac_b+\nabla_bc_a-\gamma_{ab}\nabla_dc^d$. A symmetric [trace](linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace)-free antighost exponentiates the [Faddeev-Popov determinant](relativistic-quantum-field.md#faddeev-popov-determinant). In light-cone worldsheet coordinates its two chiral kinetic terms are $b_{++}\partial_-c^+$ and $b_{--}\partial_+c^-$.

###### Formal adjoint of the worldsheet conformal Killing operator

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet diffeomorphism ghost operator](#worldsheet-diffeomorphism-ghost-operator)

Use the half-normalized operator $(P_1v)_{ab}=(\nabla_av_b+\nabla_bv_a-h_{ab}\nabla_cv^c)/2$. On a closed [worldsheet](#worldsheet), a symmetric trace-free tensor obeys

$$
\int\sqrt h\,t^{ab}(P_1v)_{ab}=-\int\sqrt h\,(\nabla_at^{ab})v_b.
$$

This [integration by parts](calculus.md#integration-by-parts) identifies the displayed formal [adjoint operator](hilbert-space.md#adjoint-operator). A convention with twice $P_1$ has twice this adjoint, with identical kernels. The [worldsheet conformal Killing group](#worldsheet-conformal-killing-group) corresponds to the kernel of $P_1$, while [infinitesimal worldsheet moduli](#infinitesimal-worldsheet-modulus) correspond to the trace-free kernel of its adjoint.

### Worldsheet

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldsheet)

A string worldsheet $\Sigma$ is the two-dimensional surface swept out by a string in spacetime. Local coordinates are commonly denoted $\sigma^\alpha=(\tau,\sigma)$.

#### Winding-supported circular string

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet](#worldsheet)

In flat noncompact spacetime a fixed circular loop's cylindrical worldsheet is not a stationary point of the [Nambu–Goto action](#nambu-goto-action). A compact coordinate changes the possibilities. The displayed embedding in $\mathbb R^{2,1}\times S^1_R$ has unit winding, satisfies the [wave equation](wave-equation.md), and obeys $\dot X\cdot X'=0$ and $\dot X^2+X'^2=0$. Its projection is a circle with static shape, while its motion in the compact direction supplies the second chiral null tangent. The [induced worldsheet metric](#induced-worldsheet-metric) is $2R^2\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)$, so the full worldsheet is nondegenerate.

#### Proper length of a string

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet](#worldsheet)

On a spacelike constant-target-time slice, integrate the induced spatial line element to obtain the geometric string length. It depends on the embedding and is distinct from the fundamental [string length](#string-length) $\ell_s=\sqrt{\alpha'}$. For transverse local motion, energy additionally includes the local Lorentz factor; the geometric length alone does not determine the total energy.

#### Worldsheet metric

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet](#worldsheet)

The intrinsic Lorentzian metric in the [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action) is independent of the [string embedding map](#string-embedding-map). Its equation relates it conformally to the [induced worldsheet metric](#induced-worldsheet-metric). A [Weyl transformation](#weyl-transformation) changes the intrinsic metric without changing the classical action.

#### Worldsheet area

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet](#worldsheet)

The area of a [string worldsheet](#worldsheet) is computed from its [induced worldsheet metric](#induced-worldsheet-metric). The [Nambu–Goto action](#nambu-goto-action) is minus [string tension](#string-tension) times Lorentzian worldsheet area.

#### String embedding map

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet](#worldsheet)

The string embedding map $X:\Sigma\to\mathcal M$ assigns each worldsheet point its position in target spacetime.

##### Worldsheet zero mode

↑ **Parent:** [String embedding map](#string-embedding-map)

A worldsheet zero mode is a field component annihilated by the worldsheet kinetic operator. For a noncompact string embedding coordinate, its constant zero mode is the target-space center-of-mass position; integrating over it enforces target-space momentum conservation.

##### Induced worldsheet metric

↑ **Parent:** [String embedding map](#string-embedding-map)

The induced worldsheet metric is the pullback of the target-space metric:

$$
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_\alpha X^\mu\partial_\beta X^\nu\eta_{\mu\nu}.
$$

<h6 id="nambu-goto-action">Nambu–Goto action</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Induced worldsheet metric](#induced-worldsheet-metric)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nambu–Goto_action)

The Nambu–Goto action

$$
S_{\rm NG}=-T\int_\Sigma d^2\sigma\,\sqrt{-\det\gamma}
$$

is minus the string tension times the Lorentzian area of the worldsheet.

###### Pulsating circular string

↑ **Parent:** [Nambu–Goto action](#nambu-goto-action)

A circular [closed string](#closed-string) contracts and re-expands with $X^0=Rt$ and $X^1+iX^2=R\cos t\,e^{i\sigma}$. Its [proper length](special-relativity.md#proper-length) is $2\pi R|\cos t|$, while its [energy](classical-mechanics.md#energy) remains $2\pi TR$. At collapse the [induced worldsheet metric](#induced-worldsheet-metric) degenerates, but the [Nambu-Goto phase-space action](#nambu-goto-phase-space-action) supplies a regular continuation.

<h6 id="nambu-goto-equations-of-motion">Nambu–Goto equations of motion</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Nambu–Goto action](#nambu-goto-action)

For a nondegenerate [induced worldsheet metric](#induced-worldsheet-metric) $g_{ab}=\partial_aX\cdot\partial_bX$, variation gives $\partial_a(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{ab}\partial_bX^m)=0$. In [conformal gauge](#conformal-gauge) this is the two-dimensional [wave equation](wave-equation.md), supplemented by the [Virasoro constraints](#virasoro-constraint).

###### Nambu-Goto phase-space action

↑ **Parent:** [Nambu–Goto action](#nambu-goto-action)

The first-order string action is

$$
I=\int dt\,d\sigma\left[\dot X\cdot P-\frac e2(P^2+T^2X'^2)-uX'\cdot P\right].
$$

The multipliers impose the two [Virasoro constraints](#virasoro-constraint). The canonical equations give a spatial momentum flux $J_m=eT^2X'_m+uP_m$.

###### Target-space Noether charges of a string

↑ **Parent:** [Nambu-Goto phase-space action](#nambu-goto-phase-space-action)

Translations and [Lorentz transformations](special-relativity.md#lorentz-transformation) give [Noether charges](quantum-field-theory.md#noether-charge) $\mathcal P_m=\int P_m\,d\sigma$ and $\mathcal J_{mn}=\int(X_mP_n-X_nP_m)\,d\sigma$. Their time derivatives are boundary fluxes, which vanish for a [closed string](#closed-string) or a free-ended [open string](#open-string).

<h6 id="nambu-goto-phase-space-constraints">Nambu–Goto phase-space constraints</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Nambu-Goto phase-space action](#nambu-goto-phase-space-action)

The two constraints are $P^2+T^2X'^2=0$ and $P\cdot X'=0$. They are [first-class constraints](classical-mechanics.md#first-class-constraint) associated with [worldsheet diffeomorphisms](#worldsheet-diffeomorphism). Their [Lagrange multipliers](mathematical-optimization.md#lagrange-multiplier) are arbitrary gauge functions rather than propagating fields.

###### Chiral constraint algebra of a closed string

↑ **Parent:** [Nambu–Goto phase-space constraints](#nambu-goto-phase-space-constraints)

For a [closed string](#closed-string), the currents $J_\pm=P\pm TX'$ have vanishing mixed [Poisson brackets](classical-mechanics.md#poisson-bracket). The densities $\mathcal H_\pm=J_\pm^2/(4T)$ therefore generate commuting constraint algebras. With opposite Fourier orientations their modes satisfy $\{L_m,L_n\}=-i(m-n)L_{m+n}$ and the identical relation for $\widetilde L_n$. This is a [direct sum of Lie algebras](lie-algebra.md#direct-sum-of-lie-algebras) consisting of two [Witt algebras](lie-algebra.md#witt-algebra).

<h6 id="rotating-nambu-goto-string">Rotating Nambu–Goto string</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Nambu–Goto action](#nambu-goto-action)

A straight rigidly rotating classical string has [speed of light](special-relativity.md#speed-of-light) at a free endpoint. Its [energy](classical-mechanics.md#energy) and [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum) satisfy a [Regge trajectory](#regge-trajectory). A segment with one anchored and one free end has $J=E^2/(\pi T)$, twice the slope of the string with two free ends.

###### Free-ended straight-string Regge relation

↑ **Parent:** [Rotating Nambu–Goto string](#rotating-nambu-goto-string)

With [string tension](#string-tension) $T=1/(2\pi\alpha')$, the free-ended [open string](#open-string) $X^0=a\tau$, $X^1+iX^2=a\cos\sigma\,e^{i\tau}$, $0\leq\sigma\leq\pi$, is straight at every target time. Its [Nambu–Goto equations of motion](#nambu-goto-equations-of-motion) and [Virasoro constraints](#virasoro-constraint) hold, and its ends move at light speed. Integrating the [target-space Noether charges of a string](#target-space-noether-charges-of-a-string) gives $M=\pi Ta$ and $J=\pi Ta^2/2$. Thus $J=\alpha'M^2$. Extending the same map to $0\leq\sigma\leq2\pi$ describes a double-covered folded [closed string](#closed-string), with twice the mass and twice the [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum), hence $J=\alpha'M^2/2$. The different slopes record the different boundary topology.

###### Rigid circular string in four spatial dimensions

↑ **Parent:** [Rotating Nambu–Goto string](#rotating-nambu-goto-string)

If $A(t)$ and $B(t)$ are perpendicular four-dimensional spatial vectors of equal fixed magnitude, every constant-time slice is a planar circle. Opposite rotations in two orthogonal coordinate planes can produce a [Nambu–Goto action](#nambu-goto-action) solution with constant radius and a rotating plane. The [induced worldsheet metric](#induced-worldsheet-metric) can be constant even though the embedding moves. Rotating Cartesian axes clarify its shape without removing its kinetic target-space energy.

###### Straight rotating string with one fixed endpoint

↑ **Parent:** [Rotating Nambu–Goto string](#rotating-nambu-goto-string)

A [Nambu–Goto action](#nambu-goto-action) solution with $X^0=t$ is $\boldsymbol X=L\sin(\sigma/L)(\cos(t/L),\sin(t/L))$, for $0\leq\sigma\leq\pi L/2$. It solves the [wave equation](wave-equation.md), has a fixed endpoint at the origin, and obeys the [free-end string boundary condition](#free-end-string-boundary-condition) at the other end. Its [proper length](special-relativity.md#proper-length) is $L$ and its free endpoint has angular speed $1/L$ and spatial speed one.

#### Two-dimensional conformal field theory

↑ **Parent:** [Worldsheet](#worldsheet)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-dimensional_conformal_field_theory)

A two-dimensional conformal field theory is invariant under local holomorphic and antiholomorphic conformal transformations.

##### Global conformal Ward identities for chiral correlators

↑ **Parent:** [Two-dimensional conformal field theory](#two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory)

For [primary operators](#primary-field) of holomorphic weights $h_i$ and a [Möbius transformation](group-theory.md#mobius-transformation) invariant vacuum, infinitesimal translations, dilations and special conformal transformations give $\sum_i\partial_iG=0$, $\sum_i(z_i\partial_i+h_i)G=0$, and $\sum_i(z_i^2\partial_i+2h_i z_i)G=0$. Solving gives a constant one-point function only at $h=0$, a two-point function $Cz_{12}^{-2h}$ only for equal weights, and a three-point function $Cz_{12}^{h_3-h_1-h_2}z_{13}^{h_2-h_1-h_3}z_{23}^{h_1-h_2-h_3}$. Three-point covariance imposes no further equality of weights; dynamical selection rules may still force $C=0$. For more points, arbitrary functions of [conformal cross-ratios](#conformal-cross-ratio) remain.

##### Liouville field theory

↑ **Parent:** [Two-dimensional conformal field theory](#two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liouville_field_theory)

[Liouville field theory](#liouville-field-theory) is a two-dimensional scalar theory with a curvature background charge and an exponential interaction. In the usual spacelike normalization, quantum conformal invariance gives $Q=b+b^{-1}$ and [central charge](#central-charge) $c_L=1+6Q^2$. When a noncritical [string worldsheet](#worldsheet) conformal factor is retained as a dynamical field, its effective action has this form and its [central charge](#central-charge) combines with the matter and [worldsheet ghost field](#worldsheet-ghost-field) charges. It is closely related to a [background-charge scalar field](#background-charge-scalar-field), but its exponential potential supplies an additional interaction.

##### Radial quantization

↑ **Parent:** [Two-dimensional conformal field theory](#two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory)

[Radial quantization](#radial-quantization) treats circles around the origin as equal-time slices and logarithmic radius as Euclidean time. A local insertion at the origin prepares a state on a surrounding circle. Contours of the stress tensor act on that insertion, turning its singular [operator product expansion](#operator-product-expansion) into the [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra) action on the corresponding state.

##### Quasi-primary operator

↑ **Parent:** [Two-dimensional conformal field theory](#two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory)

A [quasi-primary operator](#quasi-primary-operator) transforms with weights $(h,\widetilde h)$ under global conformal, or Möbius, transformations. A [primary operator](#primary-field) has this covariance under every locally invertible holomorphic and antiholomorphic coordinate change. In [radial quantization](#radial-quantization), quasi-primary states are annihilated by $L_1,\widetilde L_1$, whereas [primary states](#primary-state) are annihilated by every positive Virasoro mode. The stress tensor at nonzero [central charge](#central-charge) is quasi-primary but not primary because its transformation under a general coordinate change includes a Schwarzian term.

##### Free chiral Majorana fermion conformal field theory

↑ **Parent:** [Two-dimensional conformal field theory](#two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory)

A real chiral [Majorana fermion](relativistic-quantum-field.md#majorana-spinor) with contraction $\psi(z)\psi(w)\sim1/(z-w)$ has stress tensor $T=-:\psi\partial\psi:/2$. Fermionic [Wick contractions](perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#wick-contraction) give $T(z)\psi(w)\sim\psi(w)/(2(z-w)^2)+\partial\psi(w)/(z-w)$. Double contractions in $TT$ give $1/(4(z-w)^4)$, so the [central charge](#central-charge) is $1/2$. Fermion bilinears of different species are weight-one currents; equal-species undifferentiated bilinears vanish by antisymmetry.

###### Ising Virasoro modules from a Majorana fermion

↑ **Parent:** [Free chiral Majorana fermion conformal field theory](#free-chiral-majorana-fermion-conformal-field-theory)

A chiral [Majorana fermion](relativistic-quantum-field.md#majorana-spinor) with modes $\{b_r,b_s\}=\delta_{r+s,0}$ and $b_r^\dagger=b_{-r}$ has $T=-:\psi\partial\psi:/2$ and [central charge](#central-charge) $1/2$. The even and odd fermion-number parts of its [Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector) give unitary modules with lowest weights $0$ and $1/2$. In its [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector), a choice of ground-state zero-mode eigenvalue gives the module of weight $1/16$; the other choice gives an isomorphic [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra) module. Their cyclic highest-weight submodules are irreducible: a positive-energy invariant proper submodule would have a lowest vector orthogonal to every descendant of the original highest vector, which is impossible in a positive inner-product cyclic module.

##### Beta-gamma system

↑ **Parent:** [Two-dimensional conformal field theory](#two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory)

A beta-gamma system is a pair of commuting chiral fields with first-order action $\int\beta\bar\partial\gamma$. If $\gamma$ has weight $h$ and $\beta$ has weight $1-h$, its central charge is $2(6h^2-6h+1)$.

###### Stress tensor of a commuting beta-gamma system

↑ **Parent:** [Beta-gamma system](#beta-gamma-system)

Using the [radial-ordering sign in commuting beta-gamma contractions](#radial-ordering-sign-in-commuting-beta-gamma-contractions), single contractions give $T(z)\beta(w)\sim\lambda\beta(w)/(z-w)^2+\partial\beta(w)/(z-w)$ and the corresponding weight $1-\lambda$ for $\gamma$. Put $A=:\partial\beta\,\gamma:$ and $B=:\beta\,\partial\gamma:$. Their double-contraction coefficients in $AA,BB,AB,BA$ are respectively $1,1,2,2$. The fourth-order pole of $TT$ is therefore $(1-\lambda)^2+\lambda^2-4\lambda(1-\lambda)=6\lambda^2-6\lambda+1$, which equals $c/2$. There is no anticommuting Wick sign.

###### Radial-ordering sign in commuting beta-gamma contractions

↑ **Parent:** [Beta-gamma system](#beta-gamma-system)

Commutation allows exchanging the factors at their existing arguments; it does not allow exchanging their arguments while retaining the same pole sign. With the displayed convention, differentiation produces $\partial\beta(z)\gamma(w)\sim(z-w)^{-2}$ and $\partial\gamma(z)\beta(w)\sim-(z-w)^{-2}$. These signs are essential for the [conformal weights](#conformal-weight) and [central charge](#central-charge) of a commuting [beta-gamma system](#beta-gamma-system).

##### bc system

↑ **Parent:** [Two-dimensional conformal field theory](#two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory)

A bc system is the anticommuting counterpart of a [beta-gamma system](#beta-gamma-system). For weights $h$ and $1-h$, its central charge is $-2(6h^2-6h+1)$.

###### Central charge of reparameterization ghosts

↑ **Parent:** [bc system](#bc-system)

The [worldsheet ghost fields](#worldsheet-ghost-field) form a [bc system](#bc-system) with [conformal weights](#conformal-weight) $2$ and $-1$. Setting $J=2$ in $c=-2(6J^2-6J+1)$ gives [central charge](#central-charge) $-26$ in each chirality. The [worldsheet Weyl anomaly](#worldsheet-weyl-anomaly) of $D$ free embedding bosons cancels at $D=26$.

###### bc ghost operator-product expansion

↑ **Parent:** [bc system](#bc-system)

For reparameterization ghosts of weights two and minus one, the canonical anticommutators imply $b(z)c(w)\sim1/(z-w)$. This operator-product expansion fixes the ghost contractions and the ghost stress tensor.

##### Primary field

↑ **Parent:** [Two-dimensional conformal field theory](#two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_field)

A primary operator of weights $(h,\widetilde h)$ transforms covariantly under local conformal maps and has a universal operator-product expansion with the stress-energy tensor.

###### Primary state

↑ **Parent:** [Primary field](#primary-field)

A [primary state](#primary-state) is a simultaneous eigenvector of $L_0,\widetilde L_0$ with eigenvalues $h,\widetilde h$ that is annihilated by all positive modes of both [Virasoro algebras](#virasoro-algebra). Negative modes generate descendants. The [state–operator correspondence](#state-operator-correspondence) identifies it with a [primary operator](#primary-field) inserted at the origin.

###### Transversality of derivative-exponential primary vertices

↑ **Parent:** [Primary field](#primary-field)

For free embedding bosons, the [operator product expansion](#operator-product-expansion) of the stress tensor with $\zeta_\mu:\partial X^\mu e^{ipX}:$ has a cubic pole $-i\alpha'(p\cdot\zeta):e^{ipX}:/2$. Its vanishing is precisely the primary condition, with weight $1+\alpha'p^2/4$. The two-derivative closed-string vertex has the two displayed transversality conditions from its holomorphic and antiholomorphic cubic poles. Being primary does not require $p^2=0$; that extra condition follows when a physical integrated vertex must have [conformal weights](#conformal-weight) $(1,1)$.

###### Conformal weight

↑ **Parent:** [Primary field](#primary-field)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_weight)

The conformal weights determine the response of a primary operator to holomorphic and antiholomorphic coordinate rescaling. Its scaling dimension is $\Delta=h+\widetilde h$ and its spin is $h-\widetilde h$.

###### Virasoro descendant

↑ **Parent:** [Primary field](#primary-field)

A Virasoro descendant is obtained by applying negative Virasoro modes $L_{-n}$ to a primary state or operator. Its level is the sum of the mode numbers.

##### Operator product expansion

↑ **Parent:** [Two-dimensional conformal field theory](#two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_product_expansion)

An operator product expansion expresses the short-distance product of local operators as a sum of local operators multiplied by singular coefficient functions.

##### Holomorphic stress-energy tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Two-dimensional conformal field theory](#two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory)

The holomorphic stress-energy tensor generates infinitesimal holomorphic conformal transformations through its operator product expansions.

###### Stress-tensor operator-product expansion

↑ **Parent:** [Holomorphic stress-energy tensor](#holomorphic-stress-energy-tensor)

In a two-dimensional conformal field theory,

$$
T(z)T(w)\sim\frac{c/2}{(z-w)^4}+\frac{2T(w)}{(z-w)^2}+\frac{\partial T(w)}{z-w}.
$$

The fourth-order pole is the central extension, while the remaining poles express that $T$ has conformal weight two.

###### Virasoro algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Holomorphic stress-energy tensor](#holomorphic-stress-energy-tensor)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virasoro_algebra)

The Virasoro algebra is the central extension of the algebra of holomorphic vector fields on a circle. Its generators are the Laurent modes of the holomorphic stress-energy tensor, and its central term is controlled by the [central charge](#central-charge) $c$.

###### Virasoro character

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra)

A [Virasoro character](#virasoro-character) records the graded dimensions of a positive-energy [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra) module. A state of weight $h+N$ contributes $q^{h+N-c/24}$, and its multiplicity is the dimension of that energy eigenspace. The shift accounts for the [Casimir energy of a two-dimensional conformal field theory](#casimir-energy-of-a-two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory). For a [Virasoro Verma module](#virasoro-verma-module), the character is $q^{h-c/24}\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{-1}$, by counting the ordered descendant basis.

###### Virasoro Verma module

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra)

The [Virasoro Verma module](#virasoro-verma-module) $M(c,h)$ is the universal [highest-weight representation](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-representation) with [central charge](#central-charge) $c$ and ground weight $h$. The [Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem](lie-algebra.md#poincare-birkhoff-witt-theorem) gives a basis $L_{-n_1}\cdots L_{-n_s}|h\rangle$ with $n_1\ge\cdots\ge n_s>0$. Its invariant Hermitian form is fixed by $L_n^\dagger=L_{-n}$ and the norm of the ground vector. Quotienting by its maximal proper submodule produces the irreducible highest-weight module; in the unitary cases this removes the null vectors.

###### Level-two Virasoro descendant Gram matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro Verma module](#virasoro-verma-module)

For a normalized [highest-weight vector](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-vector) with $L_0|h\rangle=h|h\rangle$, $L_{n>0}|h\rangle=0$ and $L_n^\dagger=L_{-n}$, the level-two descendants $L_{-2}|h\rangle,L_{-1}^2|h\rangle$ have the displayed [Gram matrix](linear-algebra.md#gram-matrix). It follows from the [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra): $\langle L_2L_{-2}\rangle=4h+c/2$, $\langle L_2L_{-1}^2\rangle=6h$ and $\langle L_1^2L_{-1}^2\rangle=4h(2h+1)$. At $h=-1,c=26$ its null direction is $(1,3/2)$, giving the [level-two scalar Virasoro null state](#level-two-scalar-virasoro-null-state). Nullity of a descendant is a metric statement; satisfying all physical constraints must be checked separately.

###### Unitary highest-weight Virasoro module

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra)

A [unitary highest-weight Virasoro module](#unitary-highest-weight-virasoro-module) is generated by a normalized vector satisfying the displayed relations, with a positive-definite [inner product](linear-algebra.md#inner-product) and $L_n^\dagger=L_{-n}$. Its [Virasoro descendants](#virasoro-descendant) have $L_0$ eigenvalues $h+N$ for nonnegative integral levels $N$. The commutator gives $\|L_{-1}|h\rangle\|^2=2h$, so unitarity requires $h\ge0$. Such a module is a quotient of a [Virasoro Verma module](#virasoro-verma-module); its [Unitary Virasoro discrete series](#unitary-virasoro-discrete-series) describes the cases with [central charge](#central-charge) below one.

###### Sugawara construction

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugawara_construction)

For a restricted representation of an affine current algebra with $[J_m^a,J_n^b]=if^{abc}J_{m+n}^c+km\delta^{ab}\delta_{m+n,0}$ and $f^{abc}f^{abd}=Q\delta^{cd}$, the displayed [normal ordering](perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#normal-ordering) construction is defined when $2k+Q\ne0$. Moving the currents past each other gives $[L_m,J_n^a]=-nJ_{m+n}^a$; the additional $Q$ in the denominator is the adjoint-Casimir reordering term. The resulting [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra) has [central charge](#central-charge) $c=k\dim\mathfrak g/(k+Q/2)$. On a compact unitary positive-energy module, $L_m^\dagger=L_{-m}$.

###### Coset construction

↑ **Parent:** [Sugawara construction](#sugawara-construction)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coset_construction)

For a current subalgebra $\mathfrak h\subset\mathfrak g$, its currents have conformal weight one under both [Sugawara construction](#sugawara-construction) stress tensors. Their difference therefore commutes with all $\mathfrak h$ currents and with the $\mathfrak h$ stress tensor. It obeys the [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra) with [central charge](#central-charge) $c_{\mathfrak g}-c_{\mathfrak h}$. For a positive-level compact unitary vacuum representation, $\|L_{-2}^{\mathfrak g/\mathfrak h}|0\rangle\|^2=(c_{\mathfrak g}-c_{\mathfrak h})/2\ge0$. A [Cartan subalgebra](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#cartan-subalgebra) has $c_{\mathfrak h}=\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak g$, giving the lower bound on $c_{\mathfrak g}$ under these hypotheses.

###### Unitary Virasoro discrete series

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra)

An irreducible unitary [highest weight](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) module of the [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra) with $c<1$ has integer $m\ge2$, $1\le r\le m-1$, $1\le s\le m$, and the displayed weights. Labels $(r,s)$ and $(m-r,m+1-s)$ represent the same module. It is obtained from the [Verma module](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#verma-module) by quotienting its zero-norm submodules; the two generating singular-vector levels are $rs$ and $(m-r)(m+1-s)$. For $m=2$ this is the trivial $c=h=0$ module. For $m=3$, the three weights are $0$, $1/2$, $1/16$.

###### Virasoro generator

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra)

[Virasoro generators](#virasoro-generator) are the contour modes of the holomorphic stress tensor in a [two-dimensional conformal field theory](#two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory). Their commutators are $[L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+c(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}/12$. A contour around an operator insertion extracts the corresponding coefficient of its stress-tensor [operator product expansion](#operator-product-expansion). $L_{-1}$ generates translations, $L_0$ measures holomorphic weight, and all positive generators annihilate a [primary state](#primary-state). An independent antiholomorphic copy acts on the other weight.

###### Virasoro zero-mode shift

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra)

Shifting the zero mode changes the algebra's central representative:

$$
[\mathcal L_m,\mathcal L_n]=(m-n)\mathcal L_{m+n}
+\left(\frac c{12}m(m^2-1)+2am\right)\delta_{m+n,0}.
$$

The linear term follows from replacing $L_{m+n}$ by $\mathcal L_{m+n}+a\delta_{m+n,0}$. It changes the zero-mode convention, not the cubic coefficient or [central charge](#central-charge). This distinction separates the intercept from the [Virasoro central extension](#virasoro-central-extension).

###### Virasoro central extension

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro algebra](#virasoro-algebra)

The Virasoro central extension is the term $c(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}/12$ in $[L_m,L_n]$. It records the conformal anomaly and commutes with every Virasoro generator.

###### Free-boson Virasoro central term

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro central extension](#virasoro-central-extension)

For $d$ free target coordinates, [normal ordering](perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#normal-ordering) of $L_m=\sum_n:\alpha_{m-n}\cdot\alpha_n:/2$ gives a matter [central charge](#central-charge) $d$. On the formal zero-momentum vacuum, the double contractions yield $\langle L_mL_{-m}\rangle=(d/2)\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}r(m-r)=d(m^3-m)/12$. Two target-metric contractions give $\eta_{ab}\eta^{ab}=d$, so timelike target coordinates do not subtract from this [central charge](#central-charge). Transverse-only and covariant generators must not be confused.

###### Central charge

↑ **Parent:** [Holomorphic stress-energy tensor](#holomorphic-stress-energy-tensor)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_charge)

The central charge is the coefficient of the fourth-order pole in the stress-tensor operator-product expansion, $T(z)T(w)\sim c/[2(z-w)^4]+\cdots$. A free real boson contributes one and a free real chiral fermion contributes one half.

###### Superconformal current

↑ **Parent:** [Central charge](#central-charge)

In a two-dimensional supersymmetric conformal field theory, the superconformal current has weight $3/2$ and its self-OPE closes on the stress tensor and the [central charge](#central-charge).

###### Canonical supercurrent bracket of free RNS matter

↑ **Parent:** [Superconformal current](#superconformal-current)

For [RNS string](#spinning-string) matter normalized by $[\dot X^\mu(\sigma),X^\nu(\sigma')]=-i\pi\eta^{\mu\nu}\delta(\sigma-\sigma')$ and $\{\psi_1^\mu(\sigma),\psi_1^\nu(\sigma')\}=\pi\eta^{\mu\nu}\delta(\sigma-\sigma')$, put $A=\partial_+X$ and $J=\psi_1\cdot A$. Then $[A^\mu(\sigma),A^\nu(\sigma')]=(i\pi/2)\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\sigma\delta(\sigma-\sigma')$. The noncentral part of $\{J(\sigma),J(\sigma')\}$ is $\pi\delta A^2+(i\pi/2)\partial_\sigma\delta\,\psi_1(\sigma)\cdot\psi_1(\sigma')$. The [distributional derivative](distribution-theory.md#distributional-derivative) identity $f(\sigma,\sigma')\delta'=f(\sigma,\sigma)\delta'+(\partial_{\sigma'}f)(\sigma,\sigma)\delta$, with a vanishing coincident Grassmann bilinear, converts this to $\pi\delta\theta_{++}$. Use the chiral [equation of motion](classical-mechanics.md#equation-of-motion) to identify $\partial_\sigma\psi_1=\partial_+\psi_1$. Quantum [normal ordering](perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#normal-ordering) adds a central contact term proportional to $\delta''$, the local form of the [N=1 super-Virasoro algebra](#n-1-super-virasoro-algebra) central extension.

<h6 id="n-1-super-virasoro-algebra">N=1 super-Virasoro algebra</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Superconformal current](#superconformal-current)

The holomorphic N=1 super-Virasoro algebra extends the Virasoro algebra by fermionic modes $G_r$ satisfying $\{G_r,G_s\}=2L_{r+s}+(c/12)(4r^2-1)\delta_{r,-s}$.

###### Ramond supercurrent zero-mode square

↑ **Parent:** [N=1 super-Virasoro algebra](#n-1-super-virasoro-algebra)

With the standard [string oscillator](#string-oscillator) algebra and $G_0=\sum_m\alpha_m\cdot\psi_{-m}$, pairing positive and negative modes gives $G_0^2=\alpha_0^2/2+\sum_{m>0}(\alpha_{-m}\cdot\alpha_m+m\psi_{-m}\cdot\psi_m)$. This is the vacuum-normal-ordered [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector) generator $L_0^R$, so $\{G_0,G_0\}=2L_0^R$. Rescaling the [superconformal current](#superconformal-current) rescales its square quadratically; its normalization cannot be ignored when comparing it with a fixed [Virasoro generator](#virasoro-generator).

##### Free boson conformal field theory

↑ **Parent:** [Two-dimensional conformal field theory](#two-dimensional-conformal-field-theory)

The free boson conformal field theory has action quadratic in a scalar $X$ and central charge one. Its oscillator partition function is expressed through the [Dedekind eta function](#dedekind-eta-function).

###### Free-boson worldsheet propagator

↑ **Parent:** [Free boson conformal field theory](#free-boson-conformal-field-theory)

For the normalization $S=(2\pi\alpha')^{-1}\int d^2z\,\partial X\bar\partial X$, the free-boson worldsheet propagator has singular part

$$
X^\mu(z,\bar z)X^\nu(w,\bar w)\sim-\frac{\alpha'}2\eta^{\mu\nu}\log|z-w|^2.
$$

Differentiation gives the singular operator-product expansions of the embedding currents.

###### Linear dilaton conformal field theory

↑ **Parent:** [Free boson conformal field theory](#free-boson-conformal-field-theory)

A linear dilaton conformal field theory improves the free-boson stress tensor by a second derivative, $T=-\alpha'^{-1}:\!\partial X\partial X\!:-q\partial^2X$. The improvement shifts the [central charge](#central-charge) to $c=1+6\alpha'q^2$ and corresponds to a dilaton linear in the target coordinate $X$.

###### Background-charge scalar field

↑ **Parent:** [Linear dilaton conformal field theory](#linear-dilaton-conformal-field-theory)

A background-charge scalar field is a free scalar coupled linearly to the worldsheet Ricci scalar. This curvature coupling produces the improved stress tensor of a [linear dilaton conformal field theory](#linear-dilaton-conformal-field-theory).

###### Torus partition function of a free boson

↑ **Parent:** [Free boson conformal field theory](#free-boson-conformal-field-theory)

For a noncompact free boson with target volume $V$, the torus partition function is $Z=V[2\pi\sqrt{\operatorname{Im}\tau}]^{-1}|\eta(\tau)|^{-2}$ in the normalization where the zero-mode Hamiltonian is $p^2/2$.

###### Dedekind eta function

↑ **Parent:** [Torus partition function of a free boson](#torus-partition-function-of-a-free-boson)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind_eta_function)

The Dedekind eta function is

$$
\eta(\tau)=q^{1/24}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^n),
\qquad q=e^{2\pi i\tau}.
$$

It packages the oscillator contribution of a chiral free boson.

###### Compact boson

↑ **Parent:** [Free boson conformal field theory](#free-boson-conformal-field-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_boson)

A compact boson takes values on a circle, $X\sim X+2\pi R$. Its states carry quantized momentum $n/R$ and integer winding $wR$.

###### Momentum and winding modes

↑ **Parent:** [Compact boson](#compact-boson)

For a compact boson of radius $R$, momentum number $n$ and winding number $w$ combine into left- and right-moving zero-mode momenta $p_L=n/R+wR$ and $p_R=n/R-wR$ in a common normalization.

###### Exceptional massless ground states of a bosonic circle

↑ **Parent:** [Momentum and winding modes](#momentum-and-winding-modes)

In the critical [closed bosonic string](#closed-string), compactification of one coordinate gives $M^2=n^2/R^2+w^2R^2/\alpha'^2+2(N+\widetilde N-2)/\alpha'$ and $\widetilde N-N+nw=0$. At oscillator level zero, level matching forces $nw=0$. Thus a pure [momentum](classical-mechanics.md#momentum) [ground state](quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) becomes a massless [scalar field](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-field) at $R=|n|\sqrt{\alpha'}/2$, while a pure winding [ground state](quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) does so at $R=2\sqrt{\alpha'}/|w|$. These are threshold states descended from the bosonic [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon); they are additional to the neutral [graviton](quantum-theory.md#graviton), two-form, vectors, [dilaton](#dilaton) and radius modulus. At the [self-dual circle](#self-dual-circle-compactification), the four charges $(\pm2,0),(0,\pm2)$ contribute to the enhanced massless [scalar field](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-field) spectrum.

###### Compact-circle closed-string level matching

↑ **Parent:** [Momentum and winding modes](#momentum-and-winding-modes)

For a compact string coordinate of radius $R$, choose $p_L=n/R+wR/\alpha'$ and $p_R=n/R-wR/\alpha'$, where the left-moving oscillators are denoted by tildes. The two classical zero-mode [Virasoro constraints](#virasoro-constraint) give $M^2=p_L^2+4\widetilde N/\alpha'=p_R^2+4N/\alpha'$. Their difference yields the displayed shifted [closed-string level matching](#closed-string-level-matching) condition. Their average is $M^2=(n/R)^2+(wR/\alpha')^2+2(N+\widetilde N)/\alpha'$. Classically the center-of-mass momentum can be continuous; the integer momentum number belongs to compact-coordinate quantization. Quantum intercepts shift the mass formula but cancel out of level matching.

###### Massless spectrum at the bosonic self-dual circle

↑ **Parent:** [Momentum and winding modes](#momentum-and-winding-modes)

The [self-dual circle](#self-dual-circle-compactification) mass equation and [closed-string level matching](#closed-string-level-matching) exhaust the possibilities at level sum zero, one or two. Besides the usual neutral $(N,\widetilde N)=(1,1)$ states, charges $(n,w)=(\pm1,\pm1)$ supply singly excited chiral states, with the sector fixed by level matching. Ground-state charges $(\pm2,0)$ and $(0,\pm2)$ also become massless. Their compact energy cancels the uncompactified [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon) mass. With $d$ physical transverse components, these families have $d^2+4d+4$ independent polarizations.

###### T-duality

↑ **Parent:** [Momentum and winding modes](#momentum-and-winding-modes)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-duality)

T-duality identifies a compact boson of radius $R$ with one of reciprocal radius by exchanging momentum and winding numbers. Factors of $\alpha'$ restore the dimensional relation $R\leftrightarrow\alpha'/R$.

###### T-duality of D-brane boundary conditions

↑ **Parent:** [T-duality](#t-duality)

For a compact [string embedding map](#string-embedding-map) coordinate, [T-duality](#t-duality) preserves its left-moving part and reverses its right-moving part. Consequently $\partial_\tau\widetilde X=\partial_\sigma X$ and $\partial_\sigma\widetilde X=\partial_\tau X$. A [Neumann boundary condition](differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition) becomes a [Dirichlet boundary condition](differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) and conversely. A [D-brane](#d-brane) wrapping the circle loses one spatial worldvolume dimension; a [D-brane](#d-brane) transverse to it gains one in the dual description.

###### Brane separation becomes a Wilson line

↑ **Parent:** [T-duality of D-brane boundary conditions](#t-duality-of-d-brane-boundary-conditions)

Under [T-duality](#t-duality), the positions $y_i$ of [D-branes](#d-brane) transverse to a circle of radius $R$ become diagonal [Wilson lines](relativistic-quantum-field.md#wilson-line) on branes wrapping the dual circle $R'=\alpha'/R$. Their holonomies are $\exp(i2\pi R'A_i)=\exp(iy_i/R)$. An [Open string stretched between D-branes](#open-string-stretched-between-d-branes) with displacement $y_j-y_i+2\pi Rw$ becomes a string with shifted compact momentum $w/R'+A_j-A_i$. The stretching contribution and the shifted-momentum contribution to its squared mass are identical.

###### Global qualifications of Abelian T-duality

↑ **Parent:** [T-duality](#t-duality)

A local [Buscher procedure](#buscher-procedure) sets a flat auxiliary connection equal to a coordinate derivative only on a simply connected patch. A full compact-circle [Polyakov path integral](#polyakov-path-integral) must also sum flat-connection holonomies, match the period of the dual multiplier, and include [momentum and winding modes](#momentum-and-winding-modes). A free circle isometry then gives the radius relation $R'=\alpha'/R$. Fixed points, noncompact orbits and global identifications require additional analysis; a local spacelike [Killing vector](general-relativity.md#killing-vector-field) does not specify these data.

###### Self-dual circle compactification

↑ **Parent:** [T-duality](#t-duality)

A circle compactification is self-dual when the radius is unchanged by the [T-duality](#t-duality) map $R\mapsto\alpha'/R$. Momentum and winding then enter the [bosonic string mass spectrum](#bosonic-string-mass-spectrum) with equal weights. Special charge sectors can become massless at this radius.

###### Self-dual circle current algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Self-dual circle compactification](#self-dual-circle-compactification)

At $R=\sqrt{\alpha'}$, a chiral [compact boson](#compact-boson) has [operator product expansion](#operator-product-expansion) $Y_L(z)Y_L(w)\sim-\alpha'\log(z-w)/2$. The currents $J^3=i\partial Y_L/\sqrt{\alpha'}$ and $J^\pm=:\!e^{\pm2iY_L/\sqrt{\alpha'}}\!:$ have [conformal weight](#conformal-weight) one. Contractions give $J^3(z)J^3(w)\sim1/[2(z-w)^2]$, $J^3(z)J^\pm(w)\sim\pm J^\pm(w)/(z-w)$ and $J^+(z)J^-(w)\sim1/(z-w)^2+2J^3(w)/(z-w)$. These are the level-one [SU(2) Lie algebra](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#su-2-lie-algebra) currents. The right-moving copy supplies a second factor. Combining a current with a noncompact coordinate derivative gives a massless [string vertex operator](#string-vertex-operator) for each of the six vectors; four are additional to the generic two Cartan vectors.

###### Radius deformation as a Higgs mechanism

↑ **Parent:** [Self-dual circle compactification](#self-dual-circle-compactification)

At the [self-dual circle](#self-dual-circle-compactification), left and right dimension-one currents generate $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$. The compact-coordinate vertex $\partial Y_L\bar\partial Y_R$ is a Cartan–Cartan component of a [scalar field](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-field) in the $(3,3)$ representation. Its [expectation value](quantum-mechanics.md#expectation-value) changes the circle radius and leaves $U(1)_L\times U(1)_R$ unbroken. The four extra charged vectors acquire the displayed mass, and four [scalar field](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-field) polarizations become their longitudinal modes. This realizes the [Higgs mechanism](standard-model.md#higgs-mechanism) geometrically. In a bosonic string the ever-present [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon) and additional [scalar field](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-field) instabilities prevent interpreting the whole family as a stable vacuum.

###### Buscher procedure

↑ **Parent:** [T-duality](#t-duality)

The Buscher procedure derives T-dual backgrounds by replacing the derivative of an isometric target coordinate with an independent worldsheet one-form and imposing its flatness with a Lagrange multiplier. Integrating out either field yields the original or dual sigma model.

###### First-order action for Abelian worldsheet duality

↑ **Parent:** [Buscher procedure](#buscher-procedure)

Replace the derivative of an isometric coordinate by an independent one-form $A$ and impose $dA=0$ with a [Lagrange multiplier](mathematical-optimization.md#lagrange-multiplier). In signature $(-,+)$ with $\epsilon^{\tau\sigma}=1$, its equation is $VA^\nu=\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\widetilde z$. Eliminating $A$ from the full action, including the multiplier term, gives $\tfrac12V^{-1}\partial_\mu\widetilde z\partial^\mu\widetilde z$. Omitting the multiplier term during substitution would give the wrong sign. This derives the block-diagonal metric inversion in the [Buscher rules](#buscher-rules).

###### Buscher dilaton shift

↑ **Parent:** [Buscher procedure](#buscher-procedure)

The quantum measure in abelian [T-duality](#t-duality) changes the conventional [dilaton](#dilaton) by $\varphi'=\varphi-\frac12\log g_{yy}$ in alpha-prime-one units for a spacelike isometry. The regulated Gaussian integration over the auxiliary gauge field produces this curvature coupling. It is consistent with invariance of the string-frame density $e^{-2\varphi}\sqrt{\det g}$ under the local Buscher rules.

###### Polar-coordinate T-dual background

↑ **Parent:** [Buscher dilaton shift](#buscher-dilaton-shift)

The flat polar metric $dr^2+r^2dz^2$ has a circle isometry away from $r=0$. Its local [Buscher rules](#buscher-rules) give the displayed dual metric and [dilaton](#dilaton). The dual [Ricci tensor](general-relativity.md#ricci-tensor) has components $R_{rr}=-2/r^2$ and $R_{\widetilde z\widetilde z}=-2/r^4$, while the dilaton Hessian has components $1/r^2$ and $1/r^4$. Thus the [leading metric-dilaton Weyl condition](#leading-metric-dilaton-weyl-condition) $R_{ab}+2\nabla_a\nabla_b\Phi=0$ holds despite nonzero curvature. Flat spectators complete a critical target; the scalar anomaly condition also holds because $|\nabla\Phi|^2=1/r^2$, $\Box\Phi=2/r^2$ and $R=-4/r^2$. The construction is local on $r>0$, excluding the degenerating circle.

###### Buscher rules

↑ **Parent:** [Buscher procedure](#buscher-procedure)

The Buscher rules invert the metric component along an Abelian isometry and transform the other metric and B-field components. The Gaussian functional determinant also shifts the dilaton; when the metric is block diagonal with $G_{zz}=V$, $\widetilde\Phi=\Phi-\tfrac12\log V$.

###### Buscher dual of a metric with vanishing two-form

↑ **Parent:** [Buscher rules](#buscher-rules)

For a nondegenerate isometry direction $y$ and initially zero two-form, the local T-dual fields in alpha-prime-one units are $g'_{yy}=1/g_{yy}$, $g'_{yi}=0$, $g'_{ij}=g_{ij}-g_{yi}g_{yj}/g_{yy}$, $B'_{yi}=g_{yi}/g_{yy}$ and $B'_{ij}=0$. Gauging the isometry and eliminating its auxiliary connection derives these rules. The spectator metric is a [Schur complement](linear-algebra.md#schur-complement). The sign of the mixed two-form follows the multiplier and dual-coordinate orientation convention.

### String oscillator

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory)

The Fourier modes $\alpha_n^i$ and $\widetilde\alpha_n^i$ of the transverse closed-string coordinates obey harmonic-oscillator commutation relations. Modes with negative index create string excitations.

#### Oscillator vacuum

↑ **Parent:** [String oscillator](#string-oscillator)

An oscillator vacuum is annihilated by all positive-frequency oscillator annihilation operators. For a [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector) string this leaves a degenerate space on which the fermionic zero modes act through the [Ramond zero-mode Clifford algebra](#ramond-zero-mode-clifford-algebra). Vacuum annihilation therefore does not select a unique spinor state.

#### Half-integer open-string oscillator

↑ **Parent:** [String oscillator](#string-oscillator)

For a [Neumann-Dirichlet open-string boundary condition](#neumann-dirichlet-open-string-boundary-condition), the transverse modes have frequencies $r\in\mathbb Z+1/2$. The [canonical commutation relations](quantum-mechanics.md#canonical-commutation-relation) give $[\alpha_r^i,\alpha_s^j]=r\delta^{ij}\delta_{r+s,0}$ and $\alpha_r^\dagger=\alpha_{-r}$. One derivation expands $X-y=\sum_{r>0}q_r\cos(r\sigma)$: spatial [orthogonality](linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-vectors) reduces the [Polyakov action](#polyakov-action) to $(8\alpha')^{-1}\int\sum_r(\dot q_r^2-r^2q_r^2)d\tau$. These are [harmonic oscillators](classical-mechanics.md#simple-harmonic-motion) of [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) $1/(4\alpha')$, whose normalized [creation operators](quantum-mechanics.md#creation-operator) satisfy $\alpha_{-r}=i\sqrt r\,a_r^\dagger$ in the conventional phased expansion.

#### String level operator

↑ **Parent:** [String oscillator](#string-oscillator)

The string level operator is the weighted oscillator number $N=\sum_{n>0}\alpha_{-n}\mathbin\cdot\alpha_n$. A closed string has independent operators $N$ and $\widetilde N$ for its two chiral sectors.

##### Integer string oscillator level

↑ **Parent:** [String level operator](#string-level-operator)

The [canonical commutation relations](quantum-mechanics.md#canonical-commutation-relation) imply $[N,\alpha_{-k}^i]=k\alpha_{-k}^i$ for $N=\sum_{k>0}\alpha_{-k}\cdot\alpha_k$. With zero level assigned to the [oscillator vacuum](#oscillator-vacuum), a [Fock space](quantum-field-theory.md#fock-space) basis state containing $r_{ki}$ [creation operators](quantum-mechanics.md#creation-operator) of each mode has level $\sum_{k,i}kr_{ki}$. The level is therefore a nonnegative integer; it counts oscillator excitation weighted by frequency, not simply the number of creation operators.

#### Closed-string level matching

↑ **Parent:** [String oscillator](#string-oscillator)

Closed-string periodicity and the residual spatial constraint require equal left- and right-moving excitation levels, $N=\widetilde N$.

#### Normal-ordering constant of a string

↑ **Parent:** [String oscillator](#string-oscillator)

The normal-ordering constant is the zero-point shift in the quantum Virasoro generator. Closure of the target-space Lorentz algebra fixes $a=1$ and the spacetime dimension $D=26$ for the bosonic string.

##### Critical-vector assumption in the string intercept argument

↑ **Parent:** [Normal-ordering constant of a string](#normal-ordering-constant-of-a-string)

In the usual critical Lorentz-invariant bosonic string, level-one transverse vector polarizations fit a massless representation, whereas a massive vector would require an extra polarization. This fixes the intercept to one. Polarization counting alone does not establish that conclusion in every dimension: in three dimensions the one transverse polarization transforms trivially under the transverse rotation group and can be interpreted as a scalar. The quantum consistency assumptions must accompany an argument that the first level must be massless.

##### Neumann-Dirichlet string zero-point energy

↑ **Parent:** [Normal-ordering constant of a string](#normal-ordering-constant-of-a-string)

Each of $d$ physical transverse [bosons](quantum-mechanics.md#boson) with a [Neumann-Dirichlet open-string boundary condition](#neumann-dirichlet-open-string-boundary-condition) contributes $\tfrac12\sum_{r>0}r=1/48$ by the [half-integer zeta-regularized mode sum](complex-analysis.md#half-integer-zeta-regularized-mode-sum). Thus $L_0^\perp=N+d/48$ and the convention $L_0=\alpha'p^2+N-a$ has $a=-d/48$. For $d=24$, the vacuum shift is $+1/2$. The ordinary integer-moded open-string shift is instead $-d/24$; these endpoint sectors have different normal-ordering constants.

###### Lowest levels of a fully transverse ND bosonic string

↑ **Parent:** [Neumann-Dirichlet string zero-point energy](#neumann-dirichlet-string-zero-point-energy)

With 24 physical [half-integer open-string oscillators](#half-integer-open-string-oscillator), the first three [string level operator](#string-level-operator) [eigenvalues](linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) are $0,1/2,1$. Their states are the vacuum, $a_{1/2}^{i\dagger}|0\rangle$, and $a_{1/2}^{i\dagger}a_{1/2}^{j\dagger}|0\rangle$. The [creation operators](quantum-mechanics.md#creation-operator) commute, so the third level is the [symmetric square](linear-algebra.md#symmetric-square) of the 24-dimensional transverse vector, of dimension $24\cdot25/2=300$. Its trace is a scalar and its trace-free part has dimension 299. Including the vacuum shift gives $\alpha'M^2=1/2,1,3/2$ when the two longitudinal directions are common directions with [Neumann boundary conditions](differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition). The representation labels refer to the surviving ND rotation group, not full unbroken target Lorentz symmetry.

#### Bosonic string mass spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [String oscillator](#string-oscillator)

For a closed bosonic string,

$$
M^2=\frac4{\alpha'}(N-a)=\frac4{\alpha'}(\widetilde N-a),
\qquad N=\widetilde N.
$$

##### Closed-bosonic-string mass shell from primary weights

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string mass spectrum](#bosonic-string-mass-spectrum)

A matter vertex with left and right oscillator levels $N,\widetilde N$ and momentum $k$ has weights $N+\alpha'k^2/4,\widetilde N+\alpha'k^2/4$. An [integrated string vertex operator](#integrated-string-vertex-operator) must have weights $(1,1)$, giving the displayed mass shell and level matching with $M^2=-k^2$. Physical states are further identified modulo [null string states](#null-string-state), equivalently through [BRST cohomology](relativistic-quantum-field.md#brst-cohomology); merely counting all weight-one primaries would include gauge redundancies.

##### Massive two-form at closed-string level two

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string mass spectrum](#bosonic-string-mass-spectrum)

Each level-two chiral sector of the critical [closed string](#closed-string) is the [symmetric traceless square](linear-algebra.md#symmetric-trace-free-square-of-the-defining-orthogonal-representation) $S^2_0V$ of the massive [little group](special-relativity.md#little-group) vector space. The [tensor product](linear-algebra.md#tensor-product) of its two sectors contains the [exterior square](linear-algebra.md#exterior-square) $\Lambda^2V$: the equivariant map $(S,T)\mapsto ST-TS$ is onto the antisymmetric matrices. Compact-group complete reducibility realizes this quotient as a subrepresentation. It describes a [massive two-form field](quantum-field-theory.md#massive-two-form-field) with $M^2=8\pi T=4/\alpha'$.

##### Massless first closed-string level

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string mass spectrum](#bosonic-string-mass-spectrum)

In the critical [bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory), the states $\alpha_{-1}^i\widetilde\alpha_{-1}^j|0;p\rangle$ have $M^2=0$. Their coefficient matrix decomposes into a symmetric trace-free tensor, an antisymmetric tensor, and a scalar trace, representing the [graviton](quantum-theory.md#graviton), [Kalb–Ramond field](#kalb-ramond-field), and [dilaton](#dilaton). Their transverse [particle polarization](special-relativity.md#particle-polarization) counts add to $(D-2)^2$. The [normal-ordering constant of a string](#normal-ordering-constant-of-a-string) is one.

##### Open bosonic string mass spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string mass spectrum](#bosonic-string-mass-spectrum)

The [string level operator](#string-level-operator) for a free-ended [open string](#open-string) is $N=\sum_{k>0}\alpha_{-k}\cdot\alpha_k$. The quantum condition is $\alpha'M^2=N-a$, where $a$ is the [normal-ordering constant of a string](#normal-ordering-constant-of-a-string). Lorentz-consistent [bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory) has $a=1$.

###### Lowest light-cone levels of an open bosonic string

↑ **Parent:** [Open bosonic string mass spectrum](#open-bosonic-string-mass-spectrum)

The first levels are the [oscillator vacuum](#oscillator-vacuum), $\alpha_{-1}^I|0\rangle$, and $\alpha_{-2}^I|0\rangle,\alpha_{-1}^I\alpha_{-1}^J|0\rangle$. At level two the transverse [vector representation](representation-theory.md#vector-representation) and [symmetric square](linear-algebra.md#symmetric-square) assemble into the massive [symmetric traceless square](linear-algebra.md#symmetric-trace-free-square-of-the-defining-orthogonal-representation) of one extra rotation dimension.

### Critical dimension of string theory

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory)

The critical dimension is the target-spacetime dimension in which quantum anomalies obstructing Lorentz or Weyl symmetry cancel. It is 26 for the bosonic string.

Here critical means cancellation of the string’s quantum anomalies; it does not mean the upper or lower critical dimension of a phase transition.

#### Bosonic-string critical dimension from ghost descendants

↑ **Parent:** [Critical dimension of string theory](#critical-dimension-of-string-theory)

For the [FP ghost](relativistic-quantum-field.md#faddeev-popov-ghost) vacuum annihilated by $b_{n\ge0}$ and $c_{n>0}$, the [FP ghost](relativistic-quantum-field.md#faddeev-popov-ghost) level-one and level-two descendant coefficients are $-2$ and $-17$. Matter contributes $\alpha_0^2$ and $2\alpha_0^2+D/2$. [Virasoro closure from BRST nilpotence](#virasoro-closure-from-brst-nilpotence) at level one fixes the total highest weight to $\alpha_0^2/2-1$; level two then requires $D=26$. Imposing ground-state BRST closure gives $\alpha_0^2=2$ and the open bosonic [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon). Interpret the coefficients with the stated [ghost zero-mode pairing](#ghost-zero-mode-pairing).

#### Critical dimension of the RNS superstring

↑ **Parent:** [Critical dimension of string theory](#critical-dimension-of-string-theory)

Each of $D$ embedding [bosons](quantum-mechanics.md#boson) contributes one to the chiral [central charge](#central-charge), and each real [worldsheet Majorana fermion](#worldsheet-majorana-fermion) contributes one half. The reparameterization [bc system](#bc-system) contributes $-26$, and the [superconformal ghosts](#superconformal-ghost) contribute $+11$. Vanishing total [worldsheet Weyl anomaly](#worldsheet-weyl-anomaly) therefore requires $3D/2-26+11=0$, or $D=10$. This differs from the critical dimension 26 of the bosonic string, which has no worldsheet [fermions](quantum-mechanics.md#fermion) or superconformal ghosts.

##### Massless chiral RNS spectrum after GSO projection

↑ **Parent:** [Critical dimension of the RNS superstring](#critical-dimension-of-the-rns-superstring)

In one critical chiral [RNS string](#spinning-string) sector, [light-cone gauge in string theory](#light-cone-gauge-in-string-theory) leaves eight transverse directions. The [Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector) massless states $b_{-1/2}^i|0;k\rangle$ give eight spacetime bosonic polarizations. The [Ramond zero-mode Clifford algebra](#ramond-zero-mode-clifford-algebra) gives sixteen ground-state polarizations, split into two eight-dimensional spinor chiralities. The supersymmetric [GSO projection](#gso-projection) keeps one chirality, giving eight spacetime fermionic polarizations. Without that projection the massless counts do not agree. Counting a chiral sector is distinct from tensoring left and right sectors into closed-string states.

### Koba-Nielsen factor

↑ **Parent:** [Bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory)

The Koba-Nielsen factor is the product of powers of pairwise worldsheet insertion-point distances produced by contractions of string vertex operators.

<h4 id="mobius-invariance-of-on-shell-closed-string-vertex-integrals">Möbius invariance of on-shell closed-string vertex integrals</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Koba-Nielsen factor](#koba-nielsen-factor)

Under $z\mapsto(az+b)/(cz+d)$ with $ad-bc=1$, the coordinate measure contributes $|cz_i+d|^{-4}$ at insertion $i$. The [Koba-Nielsen factor](#koba-nielsen-factor) contributes $|cz_i+d|^{-\alpha'\sum_{j\ne i}p_i\cdot p_j}$. [Four-momentum conservation](special-relativity.md#four-momentum-conservation) makes this exponent $\alpha'p_i^2$, so the factors cancel precisely on the displayed [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon) mass shell. The correlator alone is covariant; its product with all the coordinate measures is invariant.

<h4 id="virasoro-shapiro-amplitude">Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Koba-Nielsen factor](#koba-nielsen-factor)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virasoro–Shapiro_amplitude)

The Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude is the crossing-symmetric tree-level four-point amplitude of closed-string tachyons. Its worldsheet integral is a complex beta integral, and its poles display the closed-string spectrum.

##### Fixed-angle softness of the closed-string amplitude

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude](#virasoro-shapiro-amplitude)

For $t\simeq-as$, $u\simeq-(1-a)s$ with $0<a<1$, the smooth high-energy envelope satisfies $\log|A|\simeq-2s[-a\log a-(1-a)\log(1-a)]$. The [Gamma function](complex-analysis.md#gamma-function) asymptotics cancel the leading $s\log s$ terms, leaving exponential suppression. Exact real-axis resonance poles must be excluded or treated by analytic continuation.

<h5 id="virasoro-shapiro-amplitude-pole-residue">Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude pole residue</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude](#virasoro-shapiro-amplitude)

For $a=-1-\alpha's/4$, $b=-1-\alpha't/4$ and $a+b+c=1$, the amplitude has generic [simple poles](isolated-singularity.md#simple-pole) at $a=-n$. The [gamma function](complex-analysis.md#gamma-function) [residue](analysis.md#residue) and recurrence give

$$
\operatorname*{Res}_{s=s_n}A^{(4)}=-\frac{8\pi g_s^2}{\alpha'(n!)^2}\prod_{j=1}^n(b-j)^2.
$$

The [residue](analysis.md#residue) polynomial has degree $2n$, consistent with closed-string exchange up to spin $2n$. When $a=-n+\delta_a$ and $b=-m+\delta_b$, the reciprocal factor $1/\Gamma(a+b)$ removes a putative double pole: the leading ratio is $\binom{n+m}{n}^2(1/\delta_a+1/\delta_b)$. Thus channel singularities must be counted using the kinematic constraint, not by multiplying independent numerator poles.

#### CHY formalism

↑ **Parent:** [Koba-Nielsen factor](#koba-nielsen-factor)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CHY_formalism)

The Cachazo-He-Yuan formalism expresses tree-level scattering amplitudes as integrals localized on scattering equations.

##### Scattering equation

↑ **Parent:** [CHY formalism](#chy-formalism)

For null or hard external momenta, the scattering equations are

$$
\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{p_i\cdot p_j}{z_i-z_j}=0.
$$

They arise as stationary-point equations for the Koba-Nielsen exponent and are covariant under projective transformations of the insertion points.

## Type II string theory

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_II_string_theory)

Type II superstring theory is a ten-dimensional closed oriented superstring theory with spacetime supersymmetry and no tachyon. Its massive closed-string levels have squared masses separated by $4/\alpha'$.

<h3 id="ramond-neveu-schwarz-sector">Ramond–Neveu–Schwarz sector</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Type II string theory](#type-ii-string-theory)

A [closed string](#closed-string) sector with left-moving [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector) excitations and right-moving [Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector) excitations. Its spacetime states are [fermions](quantum-mechanics.md#fermion). The [closed-string level matching](#closed-string-level-matching) condition is $N_L=N_R-1/2$. After the supersymmetric [GSO projection](#gso-projection), the lowest states have $(N_L,N_R)=(0,1/2)$ and give the other massless [gravitino](supersymmetry.md#gravitino) and [dilatino](supersymmetry.md#dilatino) of [type II string theory](#type-ii-string-theory).

<h3 id="neveu-schwarz-ramond-sector">Neveu–Schwarz–Ramond sector</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Type II string theory](#type-ii-string-theory)

A [closed string](#closed-string) sector with left-moving [Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector) excitations and right-moving [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector) excitations. Its spacetime states are [fermions](quantum-mechanics.md#fermion). The [string intercepts](#normal-ordering-constant-of-a-string) differ, so the pair of oscillator vacua is not level matched: $N_L-1/2=N_R$. After the supersymmetric [GSO projection](#gso-projection), the lowest allowed states have $(N_L,N_R)=(1/2,0)$ and are a massless [gravitino](supersymmetry.md#gravitino) and [dilatino](supersymmetry.md#dilatino).

<h3 id="ramond-ramond-sector">Ramond–Ramond sector</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Type II string theory](#type-ii-string-theory)

The closed-string sector with [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector) states on both chiral sides. The left and right spinor tensor product describes differential-form gauge fields. Opposite chiralities in [type IIA superstring theory](#type-iia-superstring-theory) give the massless one-form and three-form, with transverse dimensions $8$ and $56$. Same chiralities instead give the type IIB form-field content.

<h4 id="ramond-ramond-potential">Ramond–Ramond potential</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ramond–Ramond sector](#ramond-ramond-sector)

A differential-form [gauge potential](relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-field) from the [Ramond–Ramond sector](#ramond-ramond-sector). A [D-brane](#d-brane) carries electric or magnetic charge under the appropriate potential. Circle reduction of the M-theory metric gives the IIA one-form potential, while its three-form gives the IIA three-form potential.

<h3 id="neveu-schwarz-neveu-schwarz-sector">Neveu–Schwarz–Neveu–Schwarz sector</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Type II string theory](#type-ii-string-theory)

The closed-string sector with [NS sector](#neveu-schwarz-sector) excitations on both chiral sides. Its massless transverse tensor product is $8_v\otimes8_v=35_v+28+1$, giving the [graviton](quantum-theory.md#graviton), [Kalb–Ramond field](#kalb-ramond-field) and [dilaton](#dilaton). These fields are common to the type II theories.

<h4 id="neveu-schwarz-two-form">Neveu–Schwarz two-form</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Neveu–Schwarz–Neveu–Schwarz sector](#neveu-schwarz-neveu-schwarz-sector)

The antisymmetric two-form [gauge potential](relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-field) couples electrically to a [fundamental string](#fundamental-string). Its field strength is locally $H_3=dB_2$; its magnetic source is an [NS5-brane](#ns5-brane).

### Type IIB superstring theory

↑ **Parent:** [Type II string theory](#type-ii-string-theory)

A closed [superstring theory](#superstring-theory) whose left and right Ramond projections have the same ten-dimensional chirality. It has 32 real supercharges and is chiral. Its low-energy fields form [type IIB supergravity](supersymmetry.md#type-iib-supergravity). Circle [T-duality](#t-duality) exchanges this theory with [type IIA superstring theory](#type-iia-superstring-theory).

#### Massless type IIB spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Type IIB superstring theory](#type-iib-superstring-theory)

The NS-NS fields are a [graviton](quantum-theory.md#graviton), [Kalb–Ramond field](#kalb-ramond-field) and [dilaton](#dilaton), with $35+28+1$ physical polarizations. The RR fields are $C_0,C_2,C_4$, with $1+28+35$ polarizations; the five-form field strength of $C_4$ is self-dual. Two same-chirality Majorana-Weyl [gravitini](supersymmetry.md#gravitino) contribute $112$ polarizations, and two Majorana-Weyl [dilatini](supersymmetry.md#dilatino) of the opposite chirality contribute $16$. Potential components, dual potentials and physical polarizations are different counts.

<h4 id="p-q-five-brane">(p,q) five-brane</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Type IIB superstring theory](#type-iib-superstring-theory)

The IIB five-brane doublet combines [NS5-brane](#ns5-brane) and Ramond–Ramond five-brane charges. Its toroidal M-description involves wrapped [M5-branes](#m5-brane) or [Kaluza-Klein monopoles](physics.md#kaluza-klein-monopole), depending on whether the IIB circle lies along or transverse to the five-brane. Electric–magnetic charge bases fix the order and signs of the integer labels.

<h4 id="p-q-string">(p,q) string</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Type IIB superstring theory](#type-iib-superstring-theory)

A [(p,q) string](#p-q-string) carries $p$ units of [fundamental string](#fundamental-string) charge and $q$ units of [D1-brane](#d1-brane) charge. Coprime integers label an elementary primitive charge vector; nonprimitive vectors describe multiple units. The displayed tension uses the string metric and vanishing axion. In the [M-theory torus duality](#m-theory-torus-duality), it comes from an [M2-brane](#m2-brane) wrapped on the corresponding primitive one-cycle.

##### String junction

↑ **Parent:** [(p,q) string](#p-q-string)

At a junction of [(p,q) strings](#p-q-string), their oriented charge vectors sum to zero. A static junction also balances the tension vectors. Supersymmetry further correlates spatial directions with charge phases so that the arms share [kappa symmetry projectors](supersymmetry.md#kappa-symmetry-projector). Additional independent conditions reduce the common [Killing spinor](supersymmetry.md#killing-spinor) space; an aligned dyonic string alone does not reduce its fraction relative to an aligned fundamental string.

#### Axion-dilaton

↑ **Parent:** [Type IIB superstring theory](#type-iib-superstring-theory)

The complex scalar combines the IIB axion and [dilaton](#dilaton). Its imaginary part is $1/g_s$ for a constant background. [S-duality](#s-duality) acts by fractional linear transformations, corresponding to changes of torus basis in the [M-theory torus duality](#m-theory-torus-duality).

### Type IIA superstring theory

↑ **Parent:** [Type II string theory](#type-ii-string-theory)

A closed [superstring theory](#superstring-theory) whose left and right [Ramond sector](#ramond-sector) projections have opposite ten-dimensional Majorana-Weyl chiralities. It has 32 real spacetime supercharges and is nonchiral. Its massless fields form [type IIA supergravity](supersymmetry.md#type-iia-supergravity); the RR gauge potentials have odd degrees, including a one-form and a three-form.

#### First massive type IIA long supermultiplet

↑ **Parent:** [Type IIA superstring theory](#type-iia-superstring-theory)

Level matching combines two chiral massive sectors, each with 128 NS and 128 R states. The NS-NS and R-R combinations give $2(128)^2=32768$ bosons, and the mixed sectors give the same fermion count. Their total $2^{16}$ matches a massive uncharged long multiplet with 32 real supercharges and sixteen fermionic creation operators.

#### Massless type IIA spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Type IIA superstring theory](#type-iia-superstring-theory)

The bosons are the metric, two-form, [dilaton](#dilaton), RR one-form and RR three-form, with physical dimensions $35+28+1+8+56=128$. Two opposite-chirality [gravitinos](supersymmetry.md#gravitino) and two [dilatinos](supersymmetry.md#dilatino) supply $2(56+8)=128$ fermionic degrees of freedom. They form the ten-dimensional [type IIA supergravity](supersymmetry.md#type-iia-supergravity) multiplet. Counts refer to on-shell polarizations, not raw field components.

### Type II superstring mass spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Type II string theory](#type-ii-string-theory)

The four-point tree amplitude exposes poles at $M_n^2=4n/\alpha'$ for $n=0,1,2,\ldots$. The $n=0$ level is massless, and positive $n$ form an infinite tower of massive string excitations.

#### Type II gamma-factor mass poles

↑ **Parent:** [Type II superstring mass spectrum](#type-ii-superstring-mass-spectrum)

At generic fixed $t$, the factor $\Gamma(-\alpha's/4)$ has simple poles at the displayed nonnegative values of $s$. Its denominator $\Gamma(1+\alpha's/4)$ is finite there. The $t,u$ factors do not generically cancel those poles under $s+t+u=0$. Factorization identifies massless and massive intermediate states at those masses. The factor has no negative-mass-squared poles; special polarization or momentum choices can suppress individual residues and cannot establish the degeneracy of an entire level.

#### First massive level of a chiral RNS sector

↑ **Parent:** [Type II superstring mass spectrum](#type-ii-superstring-mass-spectrum)

After the [GSO projection](#gso-projection), the first positive shifted level is one: $N_{\rm NS}=3/2$ and $N_R=1$. In a closed-string mass convention it corresponds to $M^2=4/\alpha'$. The transverse oscillator basis has 128 NS states and 128 R states. They form the bosonic and fermionic pieces of a massive [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md) multiplet.

##### Massive chiral superstring supersymmetry multiplet

↑ **Parent:** [First massive level of a chiral RNS sector](#first-massive-level-of-a-chiral-rns-sector)

At the first massive chiral level, 128 NS bosons and 128 R fermions furnish a massive multiplet with 16 real supercharges and no central charge. Its rest-frame [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md) algebra has eight fermionic creation operators, producing $2^8=256$ states. The massive little-group representations are $44+84$ for the bosons and a 128-dimensional vector-spinor for the fermions.

##### First massive GSO-projected Ramond states

↑ **Parent:** [First massive level of a chiral RNS sector](#first-massive-level-of-a-chiral-rns-sector)

At R level one, the families are $\alpha_{-1}^i|s\rangle$ and $d_{-1}^i|s'\rangle$, each of dimension $8\cdot8$. The zero-mode spinors have opposite chirality because the one fermionic oscillator reverses the oscillator contribution to the [GSO projection](#gso-projection). The resulting 128 states form a gamma-traceless Spin(9) vector-spinor, of dimension $9\cdot16-16$.

<h5 id="first-massive-gso-projected-neveu-schwarz-states">First massive GSO-projected Neveu–Schwarz states</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [First massive level of a chiral RNS sector](#first-massive-level-of-a-chiral-rns-sector)

At NS level $3/2$, the light-cone families are $b_{-3/2}^i|0\rangle$, $\alpha_{-1}^ib_{-1/2}^j|0\rangle$, and three distinct $b_{-1/2}$ oscillators. Their dimensions are $8+64+\binom83=128$. All have odd fermion-excitation parity. They assemble into the massive [Spin group](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#spin-group) representations $44+84$, a symmetric traceless tensor and a three-form of Spin(9).

## D-brane

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-brane)

A D-brane is a hypersurface on which open-string endpoints obey Neumann boundary conditions tangentially and Dirichlet boundary conditions transversely. Its massless open-string vector mode is a worldvolume gauge field.

### D5-brane

↑ **Parent:** [D-brane](#d-brane)

A type-IIB [D-brane](#d-brane) with five spatial dimensions. Its RR charge is magnetic with respect to the two-form potential $C_2$, or electric with respect to the dual six-form potential. It is distinct from the [NS5-brane](#ns5-brane), whose charge is magnetic under the NS-NS two-form and whose string-frame tension has a different power of the coupling.

### D-instanton

↑ **Parent:** [D-brane](#d-brane)

A Euclidean type-IIB [D-brane](#d-brane) localized in all spacetime directions, coupling electrically to the RR scalar $C_0$. It is an [instanton](quantum-field-theory.md#instanton), not a Lorentzian particle with negative spatial dimension. The magnetic partner of that scalar charge is a [D7-brane](#d7-brane).

### D9-brane

↑ **Parent:** [D-brane](#d-brane)

A space-filling type-IIB [D-brane](#d-brane). The associated ten-form potential is nonpropagating; its charge is not an additional local massless polarization. A consistent compact configuration must cancel any net space-filling RR charge, for example through appropriate other charged objects or projections.

### D7-brane

↑ **Parent:** [D-brane](#d-brane)

A type-IIB [D-brane](#d-brane) with seven spatial dimensions. Its RR charge is magnetic with respect to $C_0$, or electric with respect to the dual eight-form potential. Its transverse geometry is codimension two, so the simple asymptotically flat $r^{p-7}$ harmonic formula for lower-dimensional D-branes does not apply unchanged.

### D-brane supergravity solution

↑ **Parent:** [D-brane](#d-brane)

For the asymptotically flat extremal D$p$ solution with $p<7$, $H=1+Q/r^{7-p}$ is harmonic away from the source and $e^\Phi=g_sH^{(3-p)/4}$. The electric potential can be chosen as $C_{0\cdots p}=g_s^{-1}(H^{-1}-1)$; magnetic descriptions use the dual flux, and a [D3-brane](#d3-brane) has self-dual five-form flux. An equally oriented probe has DBI density $-\mu_p g_s^{-1}H^{-1}$ and Wess-Zumino density $+\mu_p g_s^{-1}(H^{-1}-1)$, whose sum is position independent. This proves the corresponding [parallel D-brane no-force identity](#parallel-d-brane-no-force-identity) in the classical background. The supergravity approximation requires controlled curvature and coupling; it does not resolve every brane core.

### Parallel D-brane no-force identity

↑ **Parent:** [D-brane](#d-brane)

Identical, parallel, equally oriented type-II [D-branes](#d-brane) preserve the same sixteen spacetime [supercharges](supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator). Their annulus oscillator sum has the displayed numerator, which vanishes by the [Jacobi abstruse identity](modular-function.md#jacobi-abstruse-identity). In the closed-string channel this is cancellation of attractive NS-NS exchange and repulsive RR exchange. The force vanishes in the flat supersymmetric configuration; anti-oriented branes, relative angles or supersymmetry-breaking backgrounds need not have this cancellation.

The low-energy fields of an isolated flat [D3-brane](#d3-brane) form an abelian theory with sixteen linearly realized [supersymmetry generators](supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator). A [D1-brane](#d1-brane) has one spatial dimension and couples to the two-form [Ramond–Ramond potential](#ramond-ramond-potential). Its tension differs from the [fundamental string](#fundamental-string) tension by a factor $g_s^{-1}$ at zero axion.

### Dirac-Born-Infeld action

↑ **Parent:** [D-brane](#d-brane)

The [Dirac-Born-Infeld action](#dirac-born-infeld-action) describes the leading derivative dynamics of one [D-brane](#d-brane), including nonlinear dependence on slowly varying [worldvolume](#worldvolume) field strengths. Its induced tensors are [D-brane worldvolume pullbacks](#d-brane-worldvolume-pullbacks). The combination $P[B]+2\pi\alpha'F$ is invariant under the combined two-form and boundary gauge transformation. The [dilaton](#dilaton) factor reflects a disk's [Euler characteristic](homology.md#euler-characteristic). Expanding about flat space with small field strengths and embedding gradients gives the [worldvolume](#worldvolume) gauge and scalar kinetic terms. A coincident stack has a non-Abelian low-energy Yang-Mills action; this does not imply that a naive traced determinant is exact at every order.

#### Dimensionless electric displacement in DBI theory

↑ **Parent:** [Dirac-Born-Infeld action](#dirac-born-infeld-action)

With $\mathbf E_i=2\pi\alpha'F_{0i}$, this normalization separates the nonlinear constitutive relation from the [D3-brane](#d3-brane) tension. It is the normalized canonical electric momentum. The [Gauss law constraint in gauge theory](relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauss-law-constraint-in-gauge-theory) requires its divergence to vanish away from string endpoints. On the electric [BIon](#born-infeld-soliton) locus it equals the scaled electric field, but this equality is not true for arbitrary configurations.

<h4 id="born-infeld-soliton">Born–Infeld soliton</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Dirac-Born-Infeld action](#dirac-born-infeld-action)

A supersymmetric spike of a [D-brane](#d-brane) combines a worldvolume gauge field with a transverse scalar profile. Its geometry and charge represent strings ending on the brane. Electric spikes represent [fundamental strings](#fundamental-string); magnetic spikes on a [D3-brane](#d3-brane) represent [D1-branes](#d1-brane). The original construction is [Brane Dynamics From the Born-Infeld Action](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9708147).

##### Validity of the abelian BIon approximation

↑ **Parent:** [Born–Infeld soliton](#born-infeld-soliton)

The [DBI action](#dirac-born-infeld-action) resums field strengths but truncates their [derivative expansion](quantum-field-theory.md#derivative-expansion). A slowly varying large-charge throat may therefore be controlled even when its field strengths are not small. Large derivatives, large embedding curvature, string loops and gravitational backreaction restrict this description, and its ideal singular core is not uniformly controlled. The electric throat window $|N|g_s\gg1$, $g_s^2|N|\ll1$ and magnetic window $|N|\gg1$, $g_s|N|\ll1$ separate derivative and backreaction requirements.

##### String-tension interpretation of BIon energy

↑ **Parent:** [Born–Infeld soliton](#born-infeld-soliton)

For $X-X_\infty=c/r$, subtracting the planar [D3-brane](#d3-brane) energy leaves $T_3\int|\nabla X|^2d^3x=4\pi T_3c^2/r_0$ outside a cutoff. Since $X_{\rm cut}-X_\infty=c/r_0$, its coefficient is the carried string tension. Electric charge quantization gives $c=\pi g_s|N|\alpha'$ and coefficient $|N|T_{F1}$; magnetic charge gives $c=\pi|N|\alpha'$ and coefficient $|N|T_{D1}$.

##### BPS equations for a one-scalar D3-brane

↑ **Parent:** [Born–Infeld soliton](#born-infeld-soliton)

For one constant charge angle, the [DBI Hamiltonian bound for an electric spike](#dbi-hamiltonian-bound-for-an-electric-spike) and its dyonic extension give the displayed equations away from sources. Each aligned branch preserves eight of the sixteen linearly realized worldvolume supersymmetries. The corresponding spacetime string–D3 configuration preserves eight of thirty-two. The bound and charge interpretation are derived in [BPS Bounds for Worldvolume Branes](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9711205).

##### DBI Hamiltonian bound for an electric spike

↑ **Parent:** [Born–Infeld soliton](#born-infeld-soliton)

For a static [D3-brane](#d3-brane) with one scalar and no magnetic field, taking the Legendre transform of the [DBI action](#dirac-born-infeld-action) gives the displayed identity. It bounds energy by the electric scalar charge and is saturated at $\mathbf d=\nabla X$. The opposite orientation uses the analogous identity with reversed signs. On saturation, the constitutive relation gives $\mathbf E=\mathbf d$ and the [Gauss law constraint in gauge theory](relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauss-law-constraint-in-gauge-theory) reduces to the [Laplace equation](partial-differential-equation.md#laplace-equation) for $X$.

#### Hermitian commutator potential in D-brane Yang-Mills theory

↑ **Parent:** [Dirac-Born-Infeld action](#dirac-born-infeld-action)

For Hermitian scalar matrices, their commutator is anti-Hermitian, so $-\operatorname{Tr}[\phi^I,\phi^J]^2=\operatorname{Tr}[\phi^I,\phi^J]^\dagger[\phi^I,\phi^J]\geq0$. Dimensional reduction gives $F_{IJ}=i[\phi^I,\phi^J]$, fixing the minus sign before the literal commutator square inside the conventional overall-negative Yang-Mills action. Commuting scalar expectation values minimize this potential and describe ordinary separated branes. A plus sign instead requires interpreting the squared symbol as the positive Hermitian norm, not literal matrix multiplication.

#### D-brane worldvolume pullbacks

↑ **Parent:** [Dirac-Born-Infeld action](#dirac-born-infeld-action)

The brane embedding pulls the spacetime metric and two-form back to the [worldvolume](#worldvolume). The [dilaton](#dilaton) is evaluated at that embedding. If the quoted tension includes the constant-background factor $e^{-\Phi_0}$, use $\widetilde\Phi=\Phi(X)-\Phi_0$; otherwise use $\widetilde\Phi=\Phi(X)$ and a tension coefficient independent of that factor. These are normalization conventions for the same [Dirac-Born-Infeld action](#dirac-born-infeld-action).

### Worldvolume fields from open-string massless states

↑ **Parent:** [D-brane](#d-brane)

A massless open-string vector polarization tangential to a planar [D-brane](#d-brane) becomes a worldvolume gauge field. Transverse polarizations become [scalar fields](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-field) describing fluctuations of its position. This supports the interpretation of a Dirichlet plane as a dynamical extended object. The unprojected bosonic open-string [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon) additionally signals that its brane ground state is unstable; the massless field interpretation does not remove that instability.

### Chan-Paton factor

↑ **Parent:** [D-brane](#d-brane)

An endpoint label attached to an open string. With several coincident branes, two endpoint labels organize the massless open-string fields as matrices and give nonabelian gauge symmetry. Strings between different stacks carry representations associated with the two endpoint groups. These labels are physical internal degrees of freedom, not extra spacetime coordinates.

### Open string stretched between D-branes

↑ **Parent:** [D-brane](#d-brane)

An open string stretched a distance $L$ between parallel D-branes has squared mass $M^2=T^2L^2+(N-1)/\alpha'$. The stretching energy makes strings between separated branes massive.

#### Bosonic stretched-string tachyon threshold

↑ **Parent:** [Open string stretched between D-branes](#open-string-stretched-between-d-branes)

For two static parallel [D-branes](#d-brane) separated transversely by $d$, the [open bosonic string mass spectrum](#open-bosonic-string-mass-spectrum) becomes $M^2=d^2/(4\pi^2\alpha'^2)+(N-1)/\alpha'$. The classical stretching contribution is the square of the [string tension](#string-tension) times the separation, while the oscillator vacuum contributes the intercept term. Thus the ground state is a [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon) for $d<2\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}$, massless at equality, and massive above it. Its real rest energy, when stable, is $\sqrt{d^2/(4\pi^2\alpha'^2)-1/\alpha'}$; the vacuum correction belongs to energy squared and is not an additive fixed subtraction from the stretching energy. Angled branes change oscillator moding and require a different formula.

#### D-brane scalar-position normalization

↑ **Parent:** [Open string stretched between D-branes](#open-string-stretched-between-d-branes)

A commuting diagonal expectation value has one transverse eigenvalue vector for each brane. In the covariant derivative normalization $D_a\phi=\partial_a\phi+i[A_a,\phi]$, the off-diagonal gauge field has mass $m^2=\sum_I(\phi_1^I-\phi_2^I)^2$. The corresponding stretched string has mass $|X_1-X_2|/(2\pi\alpha')$ in its massless oscillator sector. Equality fixes the displayed scalar-position normalization, up to a common coordinate origin. The trace describes center-of-mass motion and the eigenvalue difference describes separation.

#### Coincident-D-brane gauge enhancement

↑ **Parent:** [Open string stretched between D-branes](#open-string-stretched-between-d-branes)

When $n$ parallel D-branes coincide, oriented strings joining distinct branes become massless alongside strings whose ends lie on one brane. Their Chan-Paton labels fill the adjoint representation of $U(n)$, enhancing $U(1)^n$ to $U(n)$.

##### Bosonic D3-brane low-energy field content

↑ **Parent:** [Coincident-D-brane gauge enhancement](#coincident-d-brane-gauge-enhancement)

For coincident [D3-branes](#d3-brane) in the critical 26-dimensional [bosonic string theory](#bosonic-string-theory), the massless open-string oscillator has a four-dimensional gauge vector and 22 transverse [scalar fields](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-field). [Chan-Paton factors](#chan-paton-factor) put all these fields in the adjoint of $U(k)$ for $k$ coincident branes. Ignoring the [tachyon](physics.md#tachyon), their leading interactions are the [dimensional reduction](physics.md#dimensional-reduction) of 26-dimensional [Yang-Mills theory](relativistic-quantum-field.md#yang-mills-theory). This contains a Yang-Mills sector but is not pure four-dimensional [Yang-Mills theory](relativistic-quantum-field.md#yang-mills-theory) and is not the supersymmetric six-scalar D3-brane theory of the ten-dimensional superstring.

### Open-string boundary condition in a B-field

↑ **Parent:** [D-brane](#d-brane)

For a D-brane in metric $G$, pulled-back B-field $B$ and worldvolume field strength $F$, tangential open-string coordinates obey the mixed condition $G_{ab}\partial_\sigma X^b+(B+2\pi\alpha'F)_{ab}\partial_\tau X^b=0$. Only the gauge-invariant combination $B+2\pi\alpha'F$ is physical.

## Gauge-gravity duality

↑ **Parent:** [String theory](string-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge-gravity_duality)

Gauge/gravity duality relates a quantum field theory without dynamical gravity to a gravitational or string theory in one additional emergent dimension.

### Conformal field theory

↑ **Parent:** [Gauge-gravity duality](#gauge-gravity-duality)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_field_theory)

A conformal field theory is a quantum field theory invariant under conformal transformations. Local operators are organized into conformal families labelled by scaling dimension and spin.

#### Conformal cross-ratio

↑ **Parent:** [Conformal field theory](#conformal-field-theory)

A conformal cross-ratio is a dimensionless combination of point separations invariant under conformal transformations. Four generic points in dimension greater than two have two independent cross-ratios,

$$
u=\frac{x_{12}^2x_{34}^2}{x_{13}^2x_{24}^2},
\qquad
v=\frac{x_{14}^2x_{23}^2}{x_{13}^2x_{24}^2}.
$$

#### Scaling dimension

↑ **Parent:** [Conformal field theory](#conformal-field-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scaling_dimension)

The scaling dimension of a local operator is its eigenvalue under dilations. Under $x\mapsto\lambda x$, a scalar operator of dimension $\Delta$ transforms by $\mathcal O(x)\mapsto\lambda^{-\Delta}\mathcal O(\lambda x)$.

##### Exactly marginal operator

↑ **Parent:** [Scaling dimension](#scaling-dimension)

An exactly marginal operator has scaling dimension equal to the spacetime dimension and a deformation coupling whose beta function vanishes to all orders. Varying that coupling moves along a family of conformal field theories without introducing a scale.

#### Conformal algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Conformal field theory](#conformal-field-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_algebra)

The conformal algebra in $d$ Euclidean dimensions is generated by translations $P_a$, rotations $M_{ab}$, dilations $D$, and special conformal transformations $K_a$.

#### Conformal primary operator

↑ **Parent:** [Conformal field theory](#conformal-field-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_primary_operator)

A conformal primary operator is annihilated at the origin by every special conformal generator. Acting with translation generators produces its conformal descendants.

##### Scalar primary operator

↑ **Parent:** [Conformal primary operator](#conformal-primary-operator)

A scalar primary operator transforms without a spin rotation and is characterized by its [scaling dimension](#scaling-dimension). Conformal symmetry fixes its two- and three-point functions up to constants, while a four-point function can depend on conformal cross-ratios.

##### Conformal descendant

↑ **Parent:** [Conformal primary operator](#conformal-primary-operator)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_descendant)

A conformal descendant is obtained by applying one or more translation generators to a [conformal primary operator](#conformal-primary-operator). Its scaling dimension exceeds that of the primary by a nonnegative integer.

##### Conformal unitarity bound

↑ **Parent:** [Conformal primary operator](#conformal-primary-operator)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_unitarity_bound)

Positivity of descendant norms constrains the scaling dimension of a primary operator. For a nonidentity scalar primary in $d\geq2$, unitarity requires $\Delta\geq(d-2)/2$.

<h4 id="state-operator-correspondence">State–operator correspondence</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Conformal field theory](#conformal-field-theory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State–operator_correspondence)

The state–operator correspondence maps a local operator of scaling dimension $\Delta$ to a state on $S^{d-1}\times\mathbb R$ whose cylinder energy is $\Delta/r$ for sphere radius $r$.

##### State-operator correspondence for Virasoro primaries

↑ **Parent:** [State–operator correspondence](#state-operator-correspondence)

For a [primary operator](#primary-field), $T(z)\mathcal O(0)\sim h\mathcal O(0)/z^2+\partial\mathcal O(0)/z$. Taking the residue against $z^{n+1}$ gives the weight for $n=0$, the derivative descendant for $n=-1$, and zero for every $n>0$. Conversely the absence of positive-mode actions excludes higher poles in this operator product and gives the primary transformation law. The antiholomorphic argument is identical. Thus [primary states](#primary-state) and [primary operators](#primary-field) correspond under [radial quantization](#radial-quantization).

##### Low-energy pure-gravity spectrum in AdS4

↑ **Parent:** [State–operator correspondence](#state-operator-correspondence)

If the only light field in AdS4 is the graviton, the dual CFT3 spectrum begins with the vacuum at dimension zero, the spin-two stress tensor at dimension three, and its non-null level-one descendants of spins two and three at dimension four.

<h3 id="ads-cft-correspondence">AdS/CFT correspondence</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Gauge-gravity duality](#gauge-gravity-duality)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AdS/CFT_correspondence)

The AdS/CFT correspondence identifies a conformal field theory with quantum gravity on an asymptotically anti-de Sitter spacetime. Boundary sources couple to CFT operators and equal boundary values of bulk fields.

#### Euclidean conformal transformation

↑ **Parent:** [AdS/CFT correspondence](#ads-cft-correspondence)

A Euclidean conformal transformation pulls the flat metric back to a position-dependent positive multiple of itself. Its infinitesimal generator obeys the conformal Killing equation.

#### Scalar-primary two-point function

↑ **Parent:** [AdS/CFT correspondence](#ads-cft-correspondence)

Conformal invariance fixes the two-point function of identical scalar primaries of dimension $\Delta$ to $C_2/|x_{12}|^{2\Delta}$.

#### Scalar-primary three-point function

↑ **Parent:** [AdS/CFT correspondence](#ads-cft-correspondence)

Three scalar primaries have a conformally fixed position dependence, leaving one theory-dependent coefficient $C_{123}$.

#### Witten diagram

↑ **Parent:** [AdS/CFT correspondence](#ads-cft-correspondence)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witten_diagram)

A Witten diagram is a position-space perturbative diagram in anti-de Sitter spacetime whose external bulk-to-boundary propagators end at boundary operator insertions.

#### Single-trace operator

↑ **Parent:** [AdS/CFT correspondence](#ads-cft-correspondence)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-trace_operator)

In a large-rank gauge theory, a single-trace operator is dual at leading order to a single bulk field, while multi-trace operators correspond to multiparticle bulk states.

#### Holographic dictionary

↑ **Parent:** [AdS/CFT correspondence](#ads-cft-correspondence)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holographic_dictionary)

The holographic dictionary identifies leading near-boundary bulk-field coefficients with CFT sources and subleading normalizable coefficients with operator expectation values.

<h5 id="fefferman-graham-coordinates">Fefferman--Graham coordinates</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Holographic dictionary](#holographic-dictionary)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fefferman--Graham_coordinates)

Fefferman--Graham coordinates put an asymptotically anti-de Sitter metric in the form $ds^2=L^2[dZ^2/Z^2+g_{ij}(Z,x)dx^idx^j/Z^2]$.

##### AdS scalar-field boundary asymptotics

↑ **Parent:** [Holographic dictionary](#holographic-dictionary)

A scalar field of mass $m$ in $\operatorname{AdS}_{d+1}$ behaves near the conformal boundary as $\phi=z^{d-\Delta}J+z^\Delta A+\cdots$, where $m^2=\Delta(\Delta-d)$. The leading coefficient is the source and the normalizable coefficient determines the operator expectation value.

<h6 id="ads-scalar-mass-dimension-relation">AdS scalar mass--dimension relation</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [AdS scalar-field boundary asymptotics](#ads-scalar-field-boundary-asymptotics)

For a scalar field of mass $m$ on $\operatorname{AdS}_{d+1}$ of radius $L$, the scaling dimension of the dual scalar primary obeys $m^2L^2=\Delta(\Delta-d)$. The two roots correspond to the two independent near-boundary falloffs.

###### Alternative quantization of an AdS scalar field

↑ **Parent:** [AdS scalar-field boundary asymptotics](#ads-scalar-field-boundary-asymptotics)

When both scalar falloffs in anti-de Sitter spacetime are normalizable, alternative quantization exchanges which near-boundary coefficient is treated as the source and which as the expectation value. In AdS4, a field with $m^2=-2$ may be dual to a scalar operator of dimension one.

##### Holographic renormalization

↑ **Parent:** [Holographic dictionary](#holographic-dictionary)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holographic_renormalization)

Holographic renormalization subtracts local boundary counterterms from the regulated on-shell bulk action. It gives finite correlation functions and identifies the renormalized normalizable coefficient with an operator expectation value.

##### Holographic entanglement entropy

↑ **Parent:** [Holographic dictionary](#holographic-dictionary)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holographic_entanglement_entropy)

At leading semiclassical order, holographic entanglement entropy is the area of a bulk extremal surface anchored on the boundary of the chosen region, divided by $4G_N$.

<h6 id="ryu-takayanagi-formula">Ryu–Takayanagi formula</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Holographic entanglement entropy](#holographic-entanglement-entropy)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryu–Takayanagi_formula)

For a static holographic state and boundary region $A$,

$$
S(A)=\frac{\operatorname{Area}(\gamma_A)}{4G_N},
$$

where $\gamma_A$ is the least-area bulk surface homologous to $A$ and anchored on $\partial A$.

<h6 id="ryu-takayanagi-surface-for-a-disk">Ryu–Takayanagi surface for a disk</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ryu–Takayanagi formula](#ryu-takayanagi-formula)

For a round disk of radius $R$ in the vacuum of a holographic conformal field theory on flat space, the static Poincare-AdS Ryu--Takayanagi surface is the hemisphere $r^2+z^2=R^2$.

<h6 id="brown-henneaux-central-charge">Brown--Henneaux central charge</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Holographic entanglement entropy](#holographic-entanglement-entropy)

The Brown--Henneaux central charge is the central charge of the two-dimensional conformal field theory dual to Einstein gravity on $\operatorname{AdS}_3$:

$$
c=\frac{3R_{\rm AdS}}{2G_3}.
$$

###### Casimir energy of a two-dimensional conformal field theory

↑ **Parent:** [Brown--Henneaux central charge](#brown-henneaux-central-charge)

For a unit spatial circle, the vacuum energy of a two-dimensional conformal field theory is $E_0=-c/12$. It arises from the anomalous Schwarzian shift when the plane vacuum is mapped to the cylinder and is dual to the renormalized energy of global $\operatorname{AdS}_3$.

<h6 id="hubeny-rangamani-takayanagi-surface">Hubeny–Rangamani–Takayanagi surface</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Holographic entanglement entropy](#holographic-entanglement-entropy)

A Hubeny–Rangamani–Takayanagi surface is the covariant extremal-area surface homologous to a boundary region. Its area divided by $4G_N$ gives leading semiclassical entanglement entropy in a time-dependent holographic spacetime.

###### One-sided unitary excitation of a thermofield double state

↑ **Parent:** [Hubeny–Rangamani–Takayanagi surface](#hubeny-rangamani-takayanagi-surface)

A unitary acting on only one factor of a thermofield double state preserves the spectrum of either reduced density matrix and hence preserves left--right entanglement. A bulk shell created on one boundary can enlarge the late event horizon while the HRT surface remains at the earlier bifurcation surface.

###### Thermofield double state

↑ **Parent:** [Holographic entanglement entropy](#holographic-entanglement-entropy)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermofield_double_state)

For two copies of a quantum system,

$$
|\Psi_\beta\rangle=Z(\beta)^{-1/2}
\sum_ne^{-\beta E_n/2}|n\rangle_L^*|n\rangle_R.
$$

Tracing out either copy produces the thermal density operator $e^{-\beta H}/Z$.

###### Modular Hamiltonian

↑ **Parent:** [Thermofield double state](#thermofield-double-state)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_Hamiltonian)

The modular Hamiltonian of a density operator is $K=-\log\rho$. For a Gibbs state, $K=\beta H+\log Z$.

###### First law of entanglement entropy

↑ **Parent:** [Modular Hamiltonian](#modular-hamiltonian)

Linearizing the von Neumann entropy around a reference state gives $\delta S=\operatorname{Tr}(\delta\rho K)$. Around a thermal state this becomes $\delta S=\beta\,\delta\langle H\rangle$.

###### Holographic mutual information

↑ **Parent:** [Holographic entanglement entropy](#holographic-entanglement-entropy)

Holographic mutual information is nonnegative because the disconnected union $\gamma_A\cup\gamma_B$ is an admissible surface for $A\cup B$, whose minimizing surface can have no larger area.

###### Entanglement-wedge phase transition

↑ **Parent:** [Holographic mutual information](#holographic-mutual-information)

As two boundary regions separate, the minimizing surface for their union can switch from connected to disconnected. At leading order this produces a continuous entropy with a kink and makes holographic mutual information vanish beyond a critical separation.

##### Holographic chemical potential

↑ **Parent:** [Holographic dictionary](#holographic-dictionary)

For a bulk gauge field, the leading boundary value of its time component is the dual chemical potential, while the normalizable radial coefficient determines the charge density.

<h5 id="holographic-d-c-conductivity">Holographic D.C. conductivity</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Holographic dictionary](#holographic-dictionary)

At nonzero charge density, a translationally invariant holographic state has infinite D.C. conductivity because an electric field continuously injects conserved momentum. Momentum relaxation broadens the zero-frequency delta function and makes the D.C. value finite.

#### ABJM theory

↑ **Parent:** [AdS/CFT correspondence](#ads-cft-correspondence)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABJM_theory)

ABJM theory is a three-dimensional supersymmetric Chern–Simons matter theory with a large-$N$ holographic dual. In its M-theory regime the bulk Newton constant scales as $G_N\sim N^{-3/2}$ in AdS units.
