# Thermodynamic internal pressure

↑ **Parent:** [Internal energy](internal-energy-split.md)

For a [simple compressible system](simple-compressible-system.md) at fixed composition and particle number, the internal pressure is the [partial derivative](partial-derivative.md) of [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md) with respect to [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) at fixed [temperature](temperature-split.md). From $dU=T\,dS-p\,dV$ and the [Maxwell relation](maxwell-relations.md) $(\partial S/\partial V)_T=(\partial p/\partial T)_V$, it has the measurable expression in the title. It is distinct from the mechanical [pressure](pressure-split.md) $p$.

For an [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md), $p=nRT/V$ gives $\pi_T=0$, so its [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md) depends only on [temperature](temperature-split.md) at fixed amount of gas. For the [Dieterici equation](dieterici-equation.md) $p=nRT\exp[-an/(VRT)]/(V-nb)$, direct [differentiation](differentiation.md) gives

$$
\pi_T=\frac{an^2}{V(V-nb)}\exp\left(-\frac{an}{VRT}\right).
$$

Thus an [equation of state](equation-of-state.md) determines this volume sensitivity even without determining the full caloric [function](function-split.md) $U(T,V)$.
