# Missing four-momentum in electron-positron collisions

↑ **Parent:** [SPEAR Mark I tau discovery experiment](spear-mark-i-tau-discovery-experiment.md)

Use [natural units](natural-units.md) $c=1$ and the [Minkowski metric](minkowski-metric.md) with signs $(+,-,-,-)$. In the ideal [centre-of-momentum frame](center-of-momentum-frame.md), assume a known initial [four-momentum](four-momentum.md) $P=(\sqrt{s},\mathbf0)$, no unaccounted radiation and exact visible reconstruction. Define

$$
P_{\rm miss}=P-p_e-p_\mu=(E_{\rm miss},\mathbf p_{\rm miss}),\qquad
M_{\rm miss}^2=P_{\rm miss}^2=E_{\rm miss}^2-|\mathbf p_{\rm miss}|^2.
$$

The [missing four-momentum in electron-positron collisions](missing-four-momentum-in-electron-positron-collisions.md) is a subtraction of measured quantities. It is not itself a [particle detector](particle-detector.md) hit. With $M_{e\mu}^2=(p_e+p_\mu)^2$, expansion gives the useful identity

$$
M_{\rm miss}^2=s+M_{e\mu}^2-2\sqrt{s}(E_e+E_\mu).
$$

For any collection of future-directed massless invisible [four-momenta](four-momentum.md) $q_i=E_i(1,\widehat{\mathbf n}_i)$,

$$
M_{\rm miss}^2=2\sum_{i<j}E_iE_j(1-\widehat{\mathbf n}_i\cdot\widehat{\mathbf n}_j)\geq0.
$$

Thus a positive [missing invariant mass](missing-invariant-mass.md) excludes exactly one massless invisible [particle](particle-physics.md) under the stated assumptions. It does not exclude one massive invisible [particle](particle-physics.md), identify a [neutrino flavor](neutrino-flavor.md), or determine the number of massless invisible [particles](particle-physics.md) beyond that lower bound. Several collinear massless [particles](particle-physics.md) can even have zero combined [invariant mass](invariant-mass.md). Detector resolution and unaccounted radiation invalidate a literal event-by-event interpretation of these ideal inequalities.

For an original exact example in [natural units](natural-units.md), choose total [energy](energy-split.md) $6\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and visible massless [four-momenta](four-momentum.md), in $\mathrm{GeV}$,

$$
p_e=(1;1,0,0),\quad p_\mu=(1;0,1,0),\quad
P_{\rm miss}=(4;-1,-1,0),\quad M_{\rm miss}^2=14\,\mathrm{GeV}^2.
$$

Two possible invisible massless [four-momenta](four-momentum.md) are $(2;-1/2,-1/2,\sqrt{14}/2)$ and $(2;-1/2,-1/2,-\sqrt{14}/2)$. Splitting either into two positive collinear parts gives a three-particle interpretation with identical recoil. Alternatively, one invisible [particle](particle-physics.md) of [mass](mass.md) $\sqrt{14}\,\mathrm{GeV}$ has that recoil. This example tests the ambiguity of the subtraction, without asserting any of these alternatives is a [tau lepton](tau-particle.md) decay.
