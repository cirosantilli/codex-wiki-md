# Curvature of a two-dimensional warped metric

↑ **Parent:** [Gaussian curvature](gaussian-curvature.md)

For positive twice [differentiable](differentiable-function.md) $f$, the only nonzero [Christoffel symbols](christoffel-symbol.md) are $\Gamma^x_{yy}=-ff'$ and $\Gamma^y_{xy}=\Gamma^y_{yx}=f'/f$. In the convention $R^d{}_{abc}=\partial_b\Gamma^d_{ac}-\partial_c\Gamma^d_{ab}+\Gamma^e_{ac}\Gamma^d_{eb}-\Gamma^e_{ab}\Gamma^d_{ec}$, substitution gives $R^x{}_{yxy}=-ff''$. Dividing $R_{xyxy}$ by $g_{xx}g_{yy}-g_{xy}^2=f^2$ gives the [Gaussian curvature](gaussian-curvature.md) $K=-f''/f$. Thus $f=x$ gives the flat metric in [polar coordinates](polar-coordinates.md) away from its axis, $f=\sin x$ gives unit positive curvature, and $f=\sinh x$ gives unit negative curvature.
