# Astronomy

↑ **Parent:** [Codex Wiki](split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy)

Astronomy studies celestial objects and phenomena, including [stars](star.md), [planets](planet.md) and [galaxies](galaxy-split.md). [Astrometry](astrometry.md) measures their [positions](position.md) and motion; [astrophysics](astrophysics-split.md) uses [physics](physics-split.md) to explain their properties and behaviour.

Much of [astronomy](astronomy-split.md) begins with incoming [light](light.md). An image tells us which direction the [light](light.md) arrived from, rather than directly providing the source's three-dimensional [position](position.md). Two [stars](star.md) close together on the sky may be very far apart in space. The [celestial sphere](celestial-sphere.md) organizes these viewing directions; locating an object on it is a different task from measuring how far away that object is. Repeated observations turn this limitation into an opportunity: the changing direction of [light](light.md) contains evidence about both [Euclidean distance](euclidean-distance.md) and motion.

The particularly satisfying combination is [stellar parallax](stellar-parallax.md) with [proper motion](proper-motion.md). The first supplies a geometric [Euclidean distance](euclidean-distance.md), while the second measures how rapidly the viewing direction changes after accounting for the observer's annual motion. Together they give a transverse [speed](speed.md), connecting tiny [angles](angle.md) on an image to kilometres travelled each second. This bridge from a flat sky map to moving [stars](star.md) is one reason [astrometry](astrometry.md) matters to the study of the [Milky Way](milky-way.md).
