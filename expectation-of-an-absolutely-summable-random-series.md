# Expectation of an absolutely summable random series

↑ **Parent:** [Linearity of expectation](linearity-of-expectation.md)

If [random variables](random-variable-split.md) $X_i$ satisfy $\sum_iE|X_i|<\infty$, then $\sum_iX_i$ converges absolutely [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md), is integrable, and its [expected value](expected-value-split.md) equals $\sum_iE[X_i]$. Indeed, the [monotone convergence theorem](monotone-convergence-theorem.md) gives $E[\sum_i|X_i|]=\sum_iE|X_i|<\infty$, so that nonnegative sum is finite [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md). It dominates every finite partial sum in absolute value; the [dominated convergence theorem](dominated-convergence-theorem.md) then justifies passing the [limit](limit-of-a-function.md) through the [expected value](expected-value-split.md). No [independence](independent-random-variables.md) is required.

For example, if $|X_i|\le Mq^i$ for a fixed $M$ and $0<q<1$, the [geometric series](geometric-series.md) bound proves the hypothesis directly. This is useful when repeated observations are multiplied by geometrically shrinking distances or weights.
