# Climate model

↑ **Parent:** [Climate change](climate-change.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_model)

A [climate model](climate-model.md) is a mathematical description of the mechanisms and [statistics](statistic.md) of a [planet](planet.md)'s [climate change](climate-change.md) and variability. A useful hierarchy starts with [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) in a [zero-dimensional planetary energy balance](zero-dimensional-planetary-energy-balance.md), adds vertical [radiative transfer](radiative-transfer.md) and [convection](convection-split.md) in a [radiative-convective climate column](radiative-convective-climate-column.md), and can resolve three-dimensional [fluid mechanics](fluid-mechanics-split.md) and [ocean circulation](ocean-circulation.md). Each level answers different questions. A column can explain why changing an absorber changes surface [temperature](temperature-split.md); it cannot locate tomorrow's storm.

Three questions should be kept distinct: what sets an average [temperature](temperature-split.md), how fast does it respond to [radiative forcing](radiative-forcing.md), and how large are its unforced fluctuations? [Climate sensitivity](climate-sensitivity.md) concerns the first, [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) controls response times, and the [Hasselmann stochastic climate model](hasselmann-stochastic-climate-model.md) illustrates the third. An observational test must compare a predicted observable with an instrument's actual readout, as in the [satellite test of greenhouse spectral change](satellite-test-of-greenhouse-spectral-change.md).
