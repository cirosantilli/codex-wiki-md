# Strict convexity separates uniqueness from existence in best approximation

↑ **Parent:** [Uniqueness of best approximation in a strictly convex space](uniqueness-of-best-approximation-in-a-strictly-convex-space.md)

A strictly convex [norm](norm.md) permits at most one [best approximation](best-approximation-in-a-normed-space.md) from a [linear subspace](vector-subspace.md), but does not force the distance [infimum](infimum.md) to be attained. In the [Hilbert space](hilbert-space-split.md) $\ell^2$, the finite-support [vector subspace](vector-subspace.md) $c_{00}$ is dense and omits $u=(2^{-j})_{j\geq1}$. Its distance from $u$ is zero and no member attains that distance. Finite-dimensionality restores [existence](existence.md) by [compactness](compact-space.md) of bounded sets in the [vector subspace](vector-subspace.md). If $Y$ is a bounded [projection](projection-linear-algebra.md) onto a [vector subspace](vector-subspace.md), the estimate $\|u-Yu\|\leq(1+\|Y\|)\inf_q\|u-q\|$ follows directly by writing $u-Yu=(I-Y)(u-q)$ for each candidate $q$, even when the [infimum](infimum.md) is not attained.
