# FRET dipole orientation factor

↑ **Parent:** [Förster distance](forster-distance.md)

The [FRET dipole orientation factor](fret-dipole-orientation-factor.md) describes the geometry of the two transition [electric dipoles](electric-dipole.md) relative to their separation. Let $\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_D$, $\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_A$ and $\widehat{\mathbf r}$ be their [unit vectors](unit-vector.md). Then

$$
\kappa^2=\left[\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_D\cdot\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_A-3(\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_D\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})(\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_A\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})\right]^2,\qquad 0\leq\kappa^2\leq4.
$$

For a separation along $z$, parallel [electric dipoles](electric-dipole.md) along $z$ give $\kappa^2=4$, parallel ones along $x$ give $1$, and one along $x$ with the other along $y$ gives $0$. Thus rotating a pair can change [FRET efficiency](fret-efficiency.md) without changing its separation.

The familiar value $\kappa^2=2/3$ requires suitable rapid, independent orientation averaging during the [FRET donor lifetime](fret-donor-lifetime.md). Random orientations across a population of slowly rotating fluorescent [proteins](protein-split.md) do not establish that dynamic condition.
