# Fermi-liquid effective mass

↑ **Parent:** [Landau quasiparticle](landau-quasiparticle.md)

In a three-dimensional uniform isotropic continuum obeying the [Principle of Galilean relativity](principle-of-galilean-relativity.md), with one bare particle [mass](mass.md) $m$, the [Fermi-liquid effective mass](fermi-liquid-effective-mass.md) satisfies

$$
\frac{m^*}{m}=1+\frac{F_1^s}{3}.
$$

A [band effective mass](band-effective-mass.md) need not satisfy this relation.[Section 2, equations (8)–(11), metallic-quasimomentum footnote](the-theory-of-a-fermi-liquid-by-lev-davidovich-landau.md)

For the stipulated $m^*=2m$ in the occupation figure, this relation requires $F_1^s=3$ if those assumptions apply. At fixed $p_F$, $v_F$ is half its free-particle value, so moving by $0.04p_F$ from the [Fermi surface](fermi-surface.md) costs approximately $0.02p_F^2/m$ instead of $0.04p_F^2/m$. The factor of three belongs to the three-dimensional angular average, not to a universal definition of [effective mass](band-effective-mass.md).
