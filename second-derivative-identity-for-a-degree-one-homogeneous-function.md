# Second derivative identity for a degree-one homogeneous function

↑ **Parent:** [Euler theorem for homogeneous functions](euler-theorem-for-homogeneous-functions.md)

For a twice [continuously differentiable](continuously-differentiable-function.md) [homogeneous function](homogeneous-function.md) $h(x,y)$ of degree one, [Euler theorem for homogeneous functions](euler-theorem-for-homogeneous-functions.md) gives $xh_x+yh_y=h$. Differentiation first with respect to $x$ and then, separately, with respect to $y$ gives

$$
xh_{xx}+yh_{xy}=0,\qquad xh_{xy}+yh_{yy}=0.
$$

Multiplying by $x$ and $y$, respectively, shows that $x^2h_{xx}=y^2h_{yy}=-xyh_{xy}$. This identity can verify a second-order [partial differential equation](partial-differential-equation-split.md) without expanding a complicated homogeneous expression twice.
