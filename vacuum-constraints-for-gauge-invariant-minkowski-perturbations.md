# Vacuum constraints for gauge-invariant Minkowski perturbations

↑ **Parent:** [Gauge-invariant scalar-vector-tensor perturbations of Minkowski spacetime](gauge-invariant-scalar-vector-tensor-perturbations-of-minkowski-spacetime.md)

With the reversed convention in [curvature-sign convention in Killing derivative identities](curvature-sign-convention-in-killing-derivative-identities.md), choose the representative $h_{00}=2\Phi$, $h_{0i}=V_i$, $h_{ij}=2\Psi\delta_{ij}+h_{ij}^{\rm TT}$. The linearized Einstein components are $G_{00}^{(1)}=-2\Delta\Psi$, $G_{0i}^{(1)}=-2\partial_i\dot\Psi-\Delta V_i/2$ and $G_{ij}^{(1)}=(\partial_i\partial_j-\delta_{ij}\Delta)(\Phi-\Psi)-2\delta_{ij}\ddot\Psi-(\partial_i\dot V_j+\partial_j\dot V_i)/2+\Box h_{ij}^{\rm TT}/2$. On all space with regular decaying fields, or for nonzero spatial Fourier modes, the vacuum constraints remove the scalar and vector parts, leaving two radiative [gravitational wave polarizations](gravitational-wave-polarization.md). Singular Coulomb fields, spatial boundaries and homogeneous modes are not covered by that removal argument.
