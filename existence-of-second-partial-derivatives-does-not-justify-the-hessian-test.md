# Existence of second partial derivatives does not justify the Hessian test

↑ **Parent:** [Second-derivative test](second-derivative-test.md)

Define $f(0,0)=0$ and, elsewhere,

$$
f(x,y)=x^2+y^2-\frac{6x^2y^2}{x^2+y^2}.
$$

All first and second [partial derivatives](partial-derivative.md) exist at the origin: $f_x=f_y=f_{xy}=f_{yx}=0$ and $f_{xx}=f_{yy}=2$. Thus its [Hessian matrix](hessian-matrix.md) there is the [positive-definite matrix](positive-definite-matrix.md) $2I$. Nevertheless $f(t,0)=t^2>0$ and $f(t,t)=-t^2<0$ for $t\ne0$, so the origin is a [saddle point of a scalar function](saddle-point-of-a-scalar-function.md). A valid [second-derivative test](second-derivative-test.md) needs a second-order [Taylor expansion](taylor-expansion.md) with a remainder $o(x^2+y^2)$; continuous second [partial derivatives](partial-derivative.md) in a neighborhood are a sufficient hypothesis. Existence of those derivatives at the point alone does not supply this hypothesis.
