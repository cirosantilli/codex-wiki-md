# Integer inverse criterion for a unimodular matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Unimodular matrix](unimodular-matrix.md)

For a square [integer matrix](integer-matrix.md) $U$ with nonzero [determinant](determinant.md), **the [inverse matrix](matrix-inverse-split.md) has integer entries exactly when $\det U=\pm1$**. The [adjugate matrix](adjugate-matrix.md) formula proves one direction:

$$
U^{-1}=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(U)}{\det U}.
$$

Every entry of $\operatorname{adj}(U)$ is an integer [cofactor](cofactor.md), so division by $1$ or $-1$ leaves integer entries. Conversely, if both $U$ and $U^{-1}$ are [integer matrices](integer-matrix.md), their [determinants](determinant.md) are [integers](integer-split.md) with product one. The only possibilities are that both are one or both are minus one. This explains why a general [integer matrix](integer-matrix.md) with nonzero [determinant](determinant.md) need not have an integer [inverse matrix](matrix-inverse-split.md).
