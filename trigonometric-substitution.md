# Trigonometric substitution

↑ **Parent:** [Integration by substitution](integration-by-substitution.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_substitution)

A [trigonometric substitution](trigonometric-substitution.md) is [integration by substitution](integration-by-substitution.md) using a [trigonometric function](trigonometric-function.md) to simplify an integrand, especially a square root. For example, $x=a\sin u$, with $a>0$ and $-\pi/2\le u\le\pi/2$, changes $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$ to $a\cos u$ and $dx$ to $a\cos u\,du$. The interval restriction fixes the sign of the square root. Similarly $x=a\tan u$ simplifies $\sqrt{a^2+x^2}$. The transformed [integral](integral-split.md) must include the differential and, for a [definite integral](definite-integral.md), the new limits.
