# Fresnel half-period zone

↑ **Parent:** [Fresnel diffraction](fresnel-diffraction.md)

A [Fresnel half-period zone](fresnel-half-period-zone.md) is a region of an aperture whose optical path to an observation point differs from the axial path by an interval of half a [wavelength](wavelength.md). For an axial [optical point source](optical-point-source.md) a distance $s$ before the aperture and an axial observer a distance $r$ beyond it, the paraxial path excess at aperture radius $u$ is $u^2(s^{-1}+r^{-1})/2$. The outer radius of zone $n$ is therefore

$$
u_n^2=\frac{n\lambda sr}{s+r}.
$$

Successive zones contribute approximately opposite [phases](phase-waves.md) to the [Huygens-Fresnel principle](huygens-fresnel-principle.md) integral. The finite source distance matters: using the plane-wave formula $u_n^2=n\lambda r$ requires $s\to\infty$.
