# Bilinear form

↑ **Parent:** [Linear algebra](linear-algebra-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilinear_form)

A [bilinear form](bilinear-form-split.md) takes two [vectors](vector.md) in a [vector space](vector-space-split.md) $V$ over a [field](field.md) $F$ and returns a [scalar](scalar.md) in $F$. Its defining property is separate [linearity](linearity.md): holding either input fixed gives a [linear functional](linear-functional.md) of the other. For [scalars](scalar.md) $a,b$ and [vectors](vector.md) $u,v,w$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
B(au+bv,w)&=aB(u,w)+bB(v,w),\\
B(u,av+bw)&=aB(u,v)+bB(u,w).
\end{aligned}
$$

This is a scalar-valued [bilinear map](bilinear-map.md); the chosen [field](field.md) is part of the definition.[MIT Algebra I, Lecture 24, §24.2: bilinear forms](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-18-011-algebra-i-student-notes-fall-2021/mit18_701f21_lect24.pdf)

Separate [linearity](linearity.md) does not mean that $B$ is a [linear map](linear-map.md) on the [vector space](vector-space-split.md) $V\times V$ with componentwise [vector addition](vector-addition.md) and [scalar multiplication](scalar-multiplication.md). Over the [real numbers](real-number.md), $B(2v,2w)=4B(v,w)$, whereas a [linear map](linear-map.md) on the pair would give $2B(v,w)$. The simplest example is $B(x,y)=xy$ on the one-dimensional [vector space](vector-space-split.md) $\mathbb R$: fixing $y=3$ gives $x\mapsto3x$, but doubling both $x$ and $y$ quadruples their product.

A [bilinear form](bilinear-form-split.md) measures how two inputs interact. The [dot product](dot-product.md) measures alignment; a [symmetric bilinear form](symmetric-bilinear-form.md) can describe a [quadratic form](quadratic-form-split.md); an [alternating bilinear form](alternating-bilinear-form.md) can measure signed [area](surface-area.md). A [nondegenerate bilinear form](nondegenerate-bilinear-form.md) turns each [vector](vector.md) into a unique [linear functional](linear-functional.md) and so connects geometry with the [dual space](dual-space-split.md). The same short definition therefore supports lengths, [bilinear orthogonal complements](orthogonal-complement-for-a-bilinear-form.md), and [symplectic vector spaces](symplectic-vector-space.md), depending on the additional properties imposed.

Over the [complex numbers](complex-number.md), a [bilinear form](bilinear-form-split.md) is [linear](linearity.md) in both inputs without conjugating either [scalar](scalar.md). A [Hermitian form](hermitian-form.md) instead is a [sesquilinear form](sesquilinear-form.md) and conjugates when its inputs are exchanged. For complex [matrices](matrix.md), the adjoint $A^*$ used with [Hermitian forms](hermitian-form.md) and matrix [C-star algebras](c-star-algebra.md) is the [conjugate transpose](conjugate-transpose.md), whereas a [bilinear form](bilinear-form-split.md) uses the ordinary [transpose](transpose.md) $A^T$. For example, $B(z,w)=zw$ has $B(i,i)=-1$, while the [Hermitian form](hermitian-form.md) $H(z,w)=z\overline w$ has $H(i,i)=1$.[§1: examples and the Hermitian distinction](bilinear-forms-by-keith-conrad.md)
