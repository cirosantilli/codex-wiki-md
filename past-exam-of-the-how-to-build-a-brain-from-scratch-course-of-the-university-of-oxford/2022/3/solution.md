**Supervised deep [convolutional neural networks](../../../convolutional-neural-network.md) were strong quantitative models of aspects of rapid primate object vision, but the claim is too broad if it identifies them with the whole visual system or uniquely endorses [supervised learning](../../../supervised-learning.md).** Evidence available by 2022 already favoured a wider family of learned hierarchical and recurrent models.

A [convolutional neural network](../../../convolutional-neural-network.md) combines spatially local filters, [weight sharing](../../../weight-sharing.md), nonlinear [activation functions](../../../activation-function.md) and repeated processing stages. Later units integrate over larger effective [receptive fields](../../../receptive-field.md). These properties give a useful analogy to the hierarchy of the [ventral visual stream](../../../ventral-visual-stream.md). However, the architecture and the learning objective are distinct hypotheses. Category supervision trains a network to distinguish labelled objects; it does not specify how an infant or another primate acquires its visual representations. Nor is [convolution](../../../convolution.md) itself equivalent to viewpoint or translation invariance: shared local filters give [translation equivariance](../../../translation-equivariance.md) under appropriate boundary and stride conditions, while pooling and later readouts change tolerance.

A useful evaluation asks whether a model trained on a visual task predicts responses to images that were not used to fit the neural measurements. Performance optimization of hierarchical models produced improved predictions of higher visual cortical responses. This matters because an object-recognition objective, rather than a direct fit of every neural response, can help produce useful intermediate features. Yet success with a fitted neural readout does not establish that each artificial unit corresponds to one biological [neuron](../../../neuron-split.md) or that the biological learning rule is [backpropagation](../../../backpropagation.md).[Abstract and Results; PNAS 111, pp. 8619–8624, object-recognition optimization and held-out neural prediction](../../../performance-optimized-hierarchical-models-predict-neural-responses-in-higher-visual-cortex-by-daniel-l-k-yamins-and-others.md)

[Representational similarity analysis](../../../representational-similarity-analysis.md) provides a complementary test. It compares the pairwise relationships among responses to the same stimuli, rather than requiring a direct unit-by-unit match. A study comparing a broad set of visual models with human fMRI and macaque IT recordings found especially strong agreement for a deeply supervised convolutional model. Additional fitted readouts and reweighting improved the match to the measured [representational dissimilarity matrix](../../../representational-dissimilarity-matrix.md). The qualification is essential: reaching a dataset's [noise ceiling](../../../noise-ceiling.md) after fitting does not show that all aspects of the brain representation have been explained. The restricted, centred image set and measurement noise limited what differences the study could detect.[Abstract; Results, remixing and reweighting; Discussion, stimulus-set and noise-ceiling limitations](../../../deep-supervised-but-not-unsupervised-models-may-explain-it-cortical-representation-by-seyed-mahdi-khaligh-razavi-and-others.md)

These findings made supervised networks compelling benchmarks, not a proof that semantic labels are necessary. By 2021, [self-supervised learning](../../../self-supervised-learning.md) with contrastive objectives produced models whose prediction accuracy across several ventral visual areas equalled or exceeded supervised comparators. Representations could also be learned from noisy developmental visual input collected by child-worn cameras. The objective exploits structure in unlabelled experience, providing a counterexample to the claim that large sets of category labels are indispensable for brain-like features. Nevertheless, successful [contrastive representation learning](../../../contrastive-representation-learning.md) does not establish its exact implementation at biological [synapses](../../../synapse.md).[Abstract and Results; PNAS 118, e2014196118: contrastive learning, multi-area prediction and child-view [training data](../../../training-data.md)](../../../unsupervised-neural-network-models-of-the-ventral-visual-stream-by-chengxu-zhuang-and-others.md)

Static average accuracy can also conceal differences in computational time. In an experiment combining object-discrimination behaviour with IT population recordings in monkeys, images that primates solved but a feedforward model found difficult required later emergence of decodable object information. Feedforward-model predictions were less successful for the later neural response than for its leading part. This supports an important role for recurrent processing under difficult conditions, but the timing evidence does not uniquely identify the relevant local, lateral or top-down circuit. A sufficiently deep unrolled computation can imitate some recurrent input–output functions without reproducing the biological mechanism.[Introduction and Results; Nature Neuroscience 22, pp. 974–983: challenge images and IT population response timing](../../../evidence-that-recurrent-circuits-are-critical-to-the-ventral-stream-s-execution-of-core-object-recognition-behavior-by-kohitij-kar-and-others.md)

Behavioural error structure supplies another constraint. In texture–shape conflict images, standard networks trained on [ImageNet](../../../imagenet.md) often followed texture while human observers followed shape. Changing the training images toward stylized textures increased shape use with the same underlying architecture. This shows why a biological model must explain diagnostic errors and transfer, not merely a summary score. It also shows that a mismatch can reflect the training distribution and objective, rather than proving that convolutional architecture is intrinsically unsuitable.[Abstract and cue-conflict experiments; ICLR 2019, texture–shape conflict and Stylized-ImageNet training](../../../imagenet-trained-cnns-are-biased-towards-texture-increasing-shape-bias-improves-accuracy-and-robustness-by-robert-geirhos-and-others.md)

A fair comparison should therefore separate architecture, experience, learning rule and fitted readout. Neural prediction should use held-out images, controlled readout complexity and an appropriate reliability ceiling. Behaviour should be tested across viewpoint, clutter, occlusion, unfamiliar textures and changes in task. Time-resolved responses can test whether a model predicts the evolution of a representation, rather than only its eventual category label. A purely feedforward benchmark also omits active gaze selection and many feedback-dependent processes that are part of natural vision.

Biological plausibility is consequently graded. Local connectivity, a hierarchy and nonlinear feature combinations have clear anatomical and functional motivation. Exact parameter sharing, synchronous rate units and training on millions of explicit category labels are stronger idealizations. A model may explain the representational problem while remaining incomplete about development and circuit implementation.

**I would retain supervised deep [convolutional neural networks](../../../convolutional-neural-network.md) as informative baselines for mature ventral-stream representations, while rejecting their exclusive status as the most plausible model of primate vision as a whole.** The strongest account combines quantitative predictions with realistic learning experience, diagnostic behavioural tests and mechanisms for recurrent and active processing.
