<h1 id="a/2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The difference of two solutions would satisfy $Ly=0$ with $y(0)=0$, $y'(1)=0$. This would be a zero [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of the homogeneous problem, but part (b) shows there is none. Hence **at most one solution exists**. For continuous forcing the standard second-order existence construction also works: the free initial slope is fixed by an equation with coefficient $\cos1\ne0$. Thus for the stated regular boundary-value problem the solution is unique even with nonzero endpoint data.
