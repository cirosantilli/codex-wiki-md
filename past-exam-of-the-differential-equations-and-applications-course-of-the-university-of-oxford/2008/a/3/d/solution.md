<h1 id="a/3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The nonzero [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) of $L$ rules out a unique solution for every value of $c$. By the [Fredholm alternative](../../../../../fredholm-alternative.md), solvability requires [orthogonality](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) of the forcing to $\ker L^*=\operatorname{span}\{3a-5b\}$. Its pairing with $6a+cb$ is

$$
\langle3a-5b,6a+cb\rangle=9+c-10-\frac{5c}4=-1-\frac c4.
$$

Thus only $c=-4$ is compatible. In that case $6a-4b=2y_{-4}$ and a particular solution is $-y_{-4}/2$. All solutions are

$$
\boxed{y(x)=-\frac32e^{-x}+e^{-2x}+C(5e^{-x}-6e^{-2x}),\qquad c=-4.}
$$

Therefore **infinitely many solutions exist for $c=-4$, no solution exists for $c\ne-4$, and no parameter value gives a unique solution**.
