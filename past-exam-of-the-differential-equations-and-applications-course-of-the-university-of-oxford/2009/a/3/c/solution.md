<h1 id="a/3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Before intersection, the characteristic map is $x=\xi+\rho_0(\xi)^3t$, with derivative $1+3\rho_0^2\rho_0't$. Because the initial profile decreases, higher-[density](../../../../../density.md) characteristics behind move faster than lower-[density](../../../../../density.md) characteristics ahead. Compression therefore forms [shocks](../../../../../shock-wave.md), which absorb more characteristics. The far states remain one and zero, so a [shock](../../../../../shock-wave.md) joining their limiting traces has speed tending to $[q]/[\rho]=1/4$.

The precise large-scale interpretation uses $\zeta=x/t$. The rescaled initial transition tends to a step, and entropy comparison for the convex [flux](../../../../../flux.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\rho(\zeta t,t)\longrightarrow1\text{ for }\zeta<1/4,\qquad \rho(\zeta t,t)\longrightarrow0\text{ for }\zeta>1/4.}
$$

One way to see the comparison is to choose distant initial points beyond which the profile is within $\delta$ of each far state. Step profiles with those nearby states bracket it; their [shock](../../../../../shock-wave.md) speeds tend to $1/4$ as $\delta\downarrow0$, while their fixed initial locations disappear after division by $t$.

A fixed translation of the [shock](../../../../../shock-wave.md) can persist. If the deviation from a reference step is integrable, its signed [mass](../../../../../mass.md) fixes a phase shift. Without such a tail assumption one should assert convergence on the $x/t$ scale and $S(t)/t\to1/4$, rather than claim that the exact line $x=t/4$ is the unshifted asymptote for every smooth initial transition.
