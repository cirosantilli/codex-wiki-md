Antisymmetry $k(x,t)=-k(t,x)$ instead gives $L^*=-L$, so $L$ is [skew-adjoint](../../../../../../../skew-adjoint-generator.md). Its quadratic form satisfies $\langle y,Ly\rangle=-\overline{\langle y,Ly\rangle}$. Hence an eigenpair satisfies

$$
\lambda\|y\|^2=-\overline\lambda\|y\|^2,\qquad\boxed{\operatorname{Re}\lambda=0.}
$$

Nonzero [eigenvalues](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are purely imaginary in the complexified function space; zero is allowed as well. On a strictly real vector space this means there are no nonzero real [eigenvalues](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md).
