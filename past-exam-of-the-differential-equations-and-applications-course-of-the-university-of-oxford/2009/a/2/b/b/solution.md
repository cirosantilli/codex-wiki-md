<h1 id="a/2/b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $c(x)=\cos2x$. The separated [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) gives

$$
Ly=c(x)\int_0^\pi y(t)\,dt+\int_0^\pi c(t)y(t)\,dt.
$$

Thus its [image](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) is $E=\operatorname{span}\{1,c\}$. Since $L1=\pi c$ and $Lc=\pi/2$, its matrix on this basis is $\begin{pmatrix}0&\pi/2\\\pi&0\end{pmatrix}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\lambda_\pm=\pm\frac\pi{\sqrt2},\qquad y_\pm(x)=1\pm\sqrt2\cos2x.}
$$

Both [eigenspaces](../../../../../../eigenspace.md) have dimension one, and there are no further nonzero [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) because every such [eigenfunction](../../../../../../eigenfunction.md) belongs to the [image](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md). The zero [eigenspace](../../../../../../eigenspace.md) is $E^\perp$, which is infinite-dimensional. One explicit nonzero example is $\boxed{y(x)=\sin2x}$, [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) to both $1$ and $\cos2x$.
