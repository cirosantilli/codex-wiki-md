<h1 id="b/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume a classical solution continuous up to the relevant boundary. At an interior positive maximum, $u_t=0$ and $u_{xx}\leq0$, hence $Lu=u_t-u_{xx}+u>0$, a contradiction. At a positive maximum on the top edge $t=\tau$, away from the spatial endpoints, the left time derivative satisfies $u_t\geq0$, while again $u_{xx}\leq0$; the same contradiction applies. Thus a positive maximum can occur only on the initial or lateral [parabolic boundary](../../../../../parabolic-boundary.md).

For two solutions with the same data, their difference $w$ satisfies $Lw=0$ and vanishes on that [parabolic boundary](../../../../../parabolic-boundary.md). The [weak parabolic maximum principle](../../../../../weak-parabolic-maximum-principle.md) prohibits a positive maximum of $w$ and also of $-w$. Therefore $w=0$, proving **at most one solution**. The positive reaction term is what gives the immediate strict contradiction at a positive maximum.
