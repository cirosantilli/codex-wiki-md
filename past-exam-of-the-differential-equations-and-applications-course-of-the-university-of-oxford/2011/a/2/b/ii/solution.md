<h1 id="a/2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Since $\lambda_1=-4/5$, the [kernel of a linear map](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) $L+\tfrac45I$ is the span of $\psi(x)=\cos(3x/2)$. The self-adjoint [Fredholm alternative](../../../../../../fredholm-alternative.md) requires $\langle f,\psi\rangle=0$ for solvability. If it holds, an arbitrary multiple of $\psi$ can be added, so a solution is not unique.

For $f=\cos x$,

$$
\int_0^\pi\cos x\cos(3x/2)\,dx=\frac65\ne0,
$$

so **case (A) has no solution**. For $f=\sin x\sin(3x/2)$,

$$
\int_0^\pi f(x)\psi(x)\,dx=\frac12\int_0^\pi\sin x\sin3x\,dx=0
$$

by sine-function [orthogonality](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md). Thus **case (B) has solutions, and they are nonunique**.
