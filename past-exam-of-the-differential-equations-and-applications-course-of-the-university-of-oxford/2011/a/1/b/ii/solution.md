<h1 id="a/1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In the [distribution](../../../../../../distribution-mathematical-analysis.md) definition, the [Green's function](../../../../../../green-s-function-split.md) is characterized, for fixed $0<\xi<1$, by

$$
\boxed{L_xg(x,\xi)=\delta(x-\xi),\quad g_x(0,\xi)+g(0,\xi)=0,\quad g(1,\xi)=0.}
$$

In the classical definition, solve $L_xg=0$ separately for $0<x<\xi$ and $\xi<x<1$, with those endpoint conditions, and impose

$$
\boxed{g(\xi^+,\xi)=g(\xi^-,\xi),\qquad g_x(\xi^+,\xi)-g_x(\xi^-,\xi)=4.}
$$

A jump in $g$ would introduce a [distributional derivative](../../../../../../distributional-derivative.md) of the [Dirac delta](../../../../../../dirac-delta-function.md), absent from the right side. Continuity eliminates it; integrating the equation across $\xi$ leaves one quarter of the derivative jump equal to one. These [Green function jump conditions](../../../../../../green-function-jump-conditions.md) make the definitions equivalent. The forced solution is $y(x)=\int_0^1g(x,\xi)f(\xi)\,d\xi$.
