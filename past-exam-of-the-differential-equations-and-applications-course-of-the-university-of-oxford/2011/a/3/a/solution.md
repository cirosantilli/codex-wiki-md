<h1 id="a/3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

After division by $x$, the [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) has coefficients $P(x)=(m+1)/x-1$ and $Q(x)=n/x$. At zero, $xP\to m+1$ and $x^2Q\to0$, making zero a [regular singular point](../../../../../regular-singular-point.md). The [indicial equation](../../../../../indicial-equation.md) is

$$
\rho(\rho-1)+(m+1)\rho=\rho(\rho+m)=0,
$$

so the [indicial exponents](../../../../../indicial-exponent.md) are $\boxed{0,-m}$, coincident when $m=0$.

At infinity put $t=1/x$ and $Y(t)=y(1/t)$. The transformed equation is

$$
Y''+\left(\frac{1-m}{t}+\frac1{t^2}\right)Y'+\frac n{t^3}Y=0.
$$

The coefficient of $Y'$ already fails the regular-singularity test at $t=0$. Thus $\boxed{x=\infty\text{ is an irregular singular point}}$, including when $n=0$.
