<h1 id="a/2/ii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the cosine difference identity to separate the [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md):

$$
k(x,t)=\cos2x\cos2t+\tfrac12\sin2x\sin2t.
$$

Thus $L$ has [image](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) $E=\operatorname{span}\{c,s\}$, where $c(x)=\cos2x$, $s(x)=\sin2x$. Their squared norms in $L^2(0,\pi)$ are $\pi/2$ and they are [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md). Consequently

$$
Lc=(\pi/2)c,\qquad Ls=(\pi/4)s.
$$

Every nonzero [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) must belong to this two-dimensional [image](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md), so the complete list of [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of finite multiplicity is

$$
\boxed{\lambda=\pi/2\text{ with eigenfunctions }Ac,\qquad \lambda=\pi/4\text{ with eigenfunctions }Bs,\quad A,B\ne0.}
$$

Each has multiplicity one. On $E^\perp$ the operator vanishes, giving the zero [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) infinite multiplicity. For example $\boxed{y(x)=1}$ is a nonzero element of the [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) because both relevant integrals over the interval vanish.
