<h1 id="a/3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Normalize the [Laguerre differential equation](../../../../../laguerre-differential-equation.md) as $y''+P(x)y'+Q(x)y=0$, where $P(x)=1/x-1$ and $Q(x)=n/x$. At $x=0$, the products $xP=1-x$ and $x^2Q=nx$ are [analytic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md). Thus zero is a [regular singular point](../../../../../regular-singular-point.md), with [indicial equation](../../../../../indicial-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{r(r-1)+r=r^2=0,\qquad r_1=r_2=0.}
$$

At $x=1$ the coefficients $P,Q$ are [analytic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md), so it is an **[ordinary point](../../../../../ordinary-point-criterion-for-a-second-order-equation.md)**. No singular-point [indicial equation](../../../../../indicial-equation.md) is needed there.

For infinity put $z=1/x$ and $Y(z)=y(1/z)$. Then the equation becomes

$$
Y''+(z^{-1}+z^{-2})Y'+nz^{-3}Y=0.
$$

At $z=0$, the coefficient $z(z^{-1}+z^{-2})=1+1/z$ is not [analytic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md). Therefore infinity is an **[irregular singular point](../../../../../irregular-singular-point.md)**, including when $n=0$. A terminating [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) solution does not change the classification of the differential equation.
