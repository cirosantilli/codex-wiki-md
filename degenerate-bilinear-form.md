# Degenerate bilinear form

↑ **Parent:** [Bilinear form](bilinear-form.md)

A [bilinear form](bilinear-form.md) is degenerate if some nonzero [vector](vector.md) pairs to zero with every [vector](vector.md) on at least one side. For a [symmetric bilinear form](symmetric-bilinear-form.md) or an [alternating bilinear form](alternating-bilinear-form.md), this says its [radical of a bilinear form](radical-of-a-bilinear-form.md) contains a nonzero [vector](vector.md). On a [finite-dimensional vector space](finite-dimensional-vector-space.md), it is equivalent to a singular [matrix of a bilinear form](matrix-of-a-bilinear-form.md), or $\det A=0$.

For $A=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$, $B((x,y),(s,t))=xs$. The nonzero [vector](vector.md) $(0,1)$ is invisible to every pairing, and changing $y$ never changes $B((x,y),w)$. The [quadratic form](quadratic-form.md) $q=x^2$ therefore has two parallel lines $x=\pm1$ as its unit [level set](level-set.md). This is a lost direction, a stronger condition than being an [isotropic vector](isotropic-vector.md): with $A=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, $B((1,1),(1,1))=0$ but $B((1,1),(1,0))=1$.
