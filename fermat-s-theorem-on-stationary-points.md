<h1 id="fermat-s-theorem-on-stationary-points">Fermat's theorem on stationary points</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Derivative](derivative.md)

If a [function](function-split.md) is [differentiable](differentiable-function.md) at an interior [local minimum](local-minimum.md) or [local maximum](local-maximum.md), its [derivative](derivative.md) there is zero. At a [local minimum](local-minimum.md) $c$, for small $h>0$ the [difference quotient](difference-quotient.md) $(f(c+h)-f(c))/h$ is nonnegative, while for small $h<0$ it is nonpositive. The one-sided [limit of a function](limit-of-a-function.md) from each side equals $f'(c)$, forcing $f'(c)\geq0$ and $f'(c)\leq0$. Apply the same argument to $-f$ at a [local maximum](local-maximum.md).

The converse fails: $f(x)=x^3$ has $f'(0)=0$ but no [local minimum](local-minimum.md) or [local maximum](local-maximum.md) at zero. The interior hypothesis is essential; a [minimum](minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) attained only at an endpoint need not have [derivative](derivative.md) zero.
