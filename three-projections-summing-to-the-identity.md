# Three projections summing to the identity

↑ **Parent:** [Sum of two linear projections](sum-of-two-linear-projections.md)

Over a [field](field.md) of [characteristic](characteristic-of-a-field.md) different from two, three [linear projections](projection-linear-algebra.md) summing to the identity annihilate one another in both orders. Each pair sum is the complementary projection to the third. The [sum of two linear projections](sum-of-two-linear-projections.md) criterion therefore proves [zero](zero-of-a-function.md) products. Every [vector](vector.md) is the sum of its three images; applying each projection to a [zero](zero-of-a-function.md) sum proves that these images form a [direct sum](direct-sum.md). Choosing a [basis](basis.md) in each image gives a simultaneous [eigenbasis](eigenbasis.md), with triples of [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) $(1,0,0)$, $(0,1,0)$ or $(0,0,1)$. This is algebraic [orthogonality](orthogonal-vectors.md), and does not assume that the projections are [orthogonal projections](orthogonal-projection.md) for a specified [inner product](inner-product-split.md).
