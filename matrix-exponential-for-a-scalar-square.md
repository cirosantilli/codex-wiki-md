# Matrix exponential for a scalar square

↑ **Parent:** [Matrix exponential](matrix-exponential.md)

When a [square matrix](square-matrix.md) satisfies $M^2=\kappa I$, its powers reduce to $M^{2n}=\kappa^nI$ and $M^{2n+1}=\kappa^nM$. Absolute convergence of the [matrix exponential](matrix-exponential.md) allows even and odd subseries to be separated. Thus $C(\kappa)=\sum_{n\geq0}\kappa^n/(2n)!$ and $S(\kappa)=\sum_{n\geq0}\kappa^n/(2n+1)!$. For real $\kappa>0$, these are $\cosh\sqrt\kappa$ and $\sinh\sqrt\kappa/\sqrt\kappa$. For $\kappa<0$, they are $\cos\sqrt{-\kappa}$ and $\sin\sqrt{-\kappa}/\sqrt{-\kappa}$. At $\kappa=0$, $e^M=I+M$, including nonzero [nilpotent matrices](nilpotent-matrix.md) of square zero. This also explains the limiting sine-over-argument coefficients without dividing by zero.
