# Rayleigh scattering

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_scattering)

[Rayleigh scattering](rayleigh-scattering.md) is [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md) of [electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation.md) by particles much smaller than the incident [wavelength](wavelength.md). In the stationary particle approximation, the scattered [electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation.md) keeps the incident [frequency](frequency.md): its direction changes, rather than its color. The incident [electric field](electric-field.md) induces an oscillating [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md), which produces [electric dipole radiation](electric-dipole-radiation.md). This is a useful example of how a microscopic response can explain a familiar large-scale sight, [why the sky is blue](why-the-sky-is-blue.md). [Richard Fitzpatrick, Rayleigh Scattering: bound-charge response and wavelength dependence](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/jk1/lectures/node108.html).

The smallness condition for [Rayleigh scattering by a small sphere](rayleigh-scattering-by-a-small-sphere.md) of [radius](radius.md) $a$ is $ka\ll1$, where the [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) is $k=2\pi/\lambda$ and $\lambda$ is the surrounding medium's [wavelength](wavelength.md). For ordinary materials with modest [refractive index](refractive-index.md), the incident [electric field](electric-field.md) then varies little across the particle, making the [electric-dipole approximation for radiation](electric-dipole-approximation-for-radiation.md) useful. For very large [refractive index](refractive-index.md), also check the internal size $|m|ka\ll1$ before applying the simple [electric dipole](electric-dipole.md) model. Once these conditions fail, use [Mie scattering](mie-scattering.md) for a [sphere](sphere.md) instead of extrapolating the [Rayleigh scattering wavelength dependence](rayleigh-scattering-wavelength-dependence.md).

Historically, the 1871 paper [Rayleigh, On the Light from the Sky, Its Polarization and Colour: original-paper transcription](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Scientific_Papers/Volume_1/On_the_Light_from_the_Sky,_Its_Polarization_and_Colour) connected the color and [polarization of an electromagnetic wave](polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave.md) of scattered skylight with particles smaller than the [wavelength](wavelength.md). Its [dimensional analysis](dimensional-analysis.md) anticipates scattered [light intensity](light-intensity.md) proportional to particle [volume](volume.md) squared and to $\lambda^{-4}$. The original mechanical picture is historical; the modern explanation uses induced [electric dipole radiation](electric-dipole-radiation.md).
