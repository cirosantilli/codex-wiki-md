# Cumulative distribution function

↑ **Parent:** [Probability distribution](probability-distribution.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulative_distribution_function)

The [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md) of a real [random variable](random-variable-split.md) $X$ is

$$
F_X(x)=\mathbb P(X\leq x).
$$

It records the probability accumulated up to a threshold. It is a [nondecreasing function](nondecreasing-function.md) and is [right-continuous](right-continuous-function.md); its limits are zero at $-\infty$ and one at $+\infty$. These properties follow from ordered threshold events, [continuity from above of a measure](continuity-from-above-of-a-measure.md), and [continuity from below of a measure](continuity-from-below-of-a-measure.md). A [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md) determines the entire [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md) on the [real line](real-line.md). [MIT Lecture 4: properties of cumulative distribution functions](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-436j-fundamentals-of-probability-fall-2018/9797310bed4c7f5b5d40d007783eec8d_MIT6_436JF18_lec04.pdf).

For $a<b$,

$$
\mathbb P(a<X\leq b)=F_X(b)-F_X(a),\qquad
\mathbb P(X=x)=F_X(x)-F_X(x^-),
$$

where $F_X(x^-)=\lim_{t\uparrow x}F_X(t)$ is its left-hand [limit](limit-of-a-function.md). The second formula measures a jump. Thus $\mathbb P(a\leq X\leq b)=F_X(b)-F_X(a^-)$; changing an endpoint is harmless only when its point probability is zero.

For the finite example under [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md), $F_X(x)$ is $0$ below zero, $0.2$ on $[0,1)$, $0.7$ on $[1,3)$, and $1$ on $[3,\infty)$. In particular $F_X(1)=0.7$, but $F_X(1^-)=0.2$, so the jump gives $\mathbb P(X=1)=0.5$. For the density example, $F_Y(y)=y^2$ on $[0,1]$, and its increase from $1/4$ to $3/4$ is $9/16-1/16=1/2$.

When an [absolutely continuous probability distribution](absolutely-continuous-probability-distribution.md) has [probability density function](probability-density-function.md) $f$, its [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md) is $F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}f(t)\,dt$ and $F'=f$ [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md). A continuous [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md) by itself does not guarantee such a density: the [Cantor distribution](cantor-distribution.md) is an important exception.
