# Recursive area bounds and the word problem

↑ **Parent:** [Isoperimetric function of a group presentation](isoperimetric-function-of-a-group-presentation.md)

A [finite group presentation](finite-group-presentation.md) has a recursive upper bound on its [Dehn function](dehn-function.md) exactly when its [word problem for a group](word-problem-for-groups.md) is decidable. If the maximal [relator](relator.md) length is $\ell$ and a word has length $n$, a diagram of area at most $N$ can be chosen with at most $\ell N+n$ edges. Enumerating all finite labelled planar diagrams within that bound gives a terminating test. Conversely, a word decider identifies the finitely many null words of length at most $n$; exhaustive finite diagram searches find their minimal areas, whose maximum computes the [Dehn function](dehn-function.md).
