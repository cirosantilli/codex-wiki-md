# Arithmetic

↑ **Parent:** [Area of mathematics](area-of-mathematics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic)

[Arithmetic](arithmetic-split.md) studies operations on [numbers](number.md): combining quantities by [addition](addition.md), finding differences by [subtraction](subtraction.md), scaling through [multiplication](multiplication.md), and reversing a nonzero scale through [division](division-mathematics.md). Its usefulness goes beyond producing an answer. A calculation can express how resources are shared, how a measurement changes with scale, or why two different descriptions of a quantity agree.

The allowed [numbers](number.md) matter. Start with the [natural numbers](natural-number.md) $0,1,2,\ldots$. Counting collections gives $2+3=5$, but removing three from two calls for the [integer](integer-split.md) $-1$. Sharing three units equally between two recipients gives the [rational number](rational-number.md) $3/2$. These operations motivate larger number systems: [integers](integer-split.md) supply [additive inverses](additive-inverse.md), and [rational numbers](rational-number.md) supply [division](division-mathematics.md) by nonzero [integers](integer-split.md). A [real number](real-number.md) can also express [lengths](length.md) such as a diagonal of [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md) $\sqrt2$, which is not a [rational number](rational-number.md).

The beauty is that bookkeeping, geometry, and algebra can check one another. The same product $3(2+4)=18$ counts three groups of six, measures the [area](surface-area.md) of a [rectangle](rectangle.md), and splits as $3\cdot2+3\cdot4$ by [distributivity](distributive-property.md). A [number line](number-line.md) makes negative [integers](integer-split.md) visible as positions; a [fraction](fraction.md) records an exact share even when its [decimal representation](decimal-representation.md) does not terminate. An [arithmetic](arithmetic-split.md) rule becomes easier to remember when these interpretations fit together.
