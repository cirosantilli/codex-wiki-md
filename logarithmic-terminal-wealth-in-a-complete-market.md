# Logarithmic terminal wealth in a complete market

↑ **Parent:** [Utility duality with martingale deflators](utility-duality-with-martingale-deflators.md)

In a [complete market](complete-market.md) with a unit cash asset and normalized strictly positive [state-price density](state-price-density.md) $Z$, the optimal positive terminal [portfolio wealth](portfolio-wealth.md) is $x/Z$ whenever it is replicable in the admissible class. The [state-price budget constraint](state-price-budget-constraint.md) is an equality: $\mathbb E[Z(x/Z)]=x$. The [utility conjugate](utility-conjugate.md) inequality gives $\mathbb E\log H\leq\log x-\mathbb E\log Z$ for every affordable positive claim. This value can be infinite, because $\mathbb E(\log Z)^+<\infty$ while $\mathbb E(\log Z)^-$ need not be finite. Even completeness for bounded claims suffices to approximate the value: set $b_n=\mathbb E[Z/(Z\vee n^{-1})]$ and $H_n=x/[b_n(Z\vee n^{-1})]$. Each claim is bounded and costs $x$, while its expected [logarithmic utility](logarithmic-utility.md) tends to the displayed value by [monotone convergence](monotone-convergence-theorem.md) after an integrable lower bound is subtracted.
