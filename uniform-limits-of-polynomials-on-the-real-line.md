# Uniform limits of polynomials on the real line

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform convergence](uniform-convergence.md)

If real [polynomials](polynomial-split.md) $p_n$ converge by [uniform convergence](uniform-convergence.md) to $f$ on all of the [real line](real-line.md), then for some $N$ and constants $c_n$, $p_n=p_N+c_n$ for every $n\geq N$, with $c_n$ convergent. Indeed, a common uniform error below one gives $|p_n-p_N|<2$ everywhere. A bounded [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) on the [real line](real-line.md) is constant, since every nonconstant [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) grows in [absolute value](absolute-value.md) at infinity. Evaluating at zero identifies the [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) of constants, so $f=p_N+c$ is a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md). This conclusion depends on the entire [real line](real-line.md) being the domain.
