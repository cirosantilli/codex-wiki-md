# Finite supremum of positive measures

↑ **Parent:** [Positive measure](positive-measure.md)

For finite [positive measures](positive-measure.md) on the same [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md), their least common upper bound in the order on [measures](measure-split.md) is

$$
\left(\bigvee_i\mu_i\right)(A)=\sup_{A=\bigsqcup_iA_i}\sum_i\mu_i(A_i).
$$

Empty cells are allowed. This is a [measure](measure-split.md), not merely a set function: for a disjoint [countable union](countable-union.md), intersect any candidate partition with its components for the upper bound, and combine partitions whose errors sum to less than a prescribed $\varepsilon$ for the lower bound. [Countable additivity](countable-additivity.md) of each component gives both inequalities. Any [measure](measure-split.md) dominating every $\mu_i$ dominates each partition sum and hence this supremum. The result is bounded above by $\sum_i\mu_i$ and so remains finite.

If all components have densities $h_i$ with respect to a common [measure](measure-split.md) $\kappa$, then the supremum has density $\max_i h_i$. For the upper bound integrate the pointwise maximum. For equality, partition by the smallest index attaining that maximum; these are [measurable sets](measurable-set.md). For example two [measures](measure-split.md) supported on disjoint sets have their sum as supremum, rather than the numerical maximum of their masses on the whole space.
