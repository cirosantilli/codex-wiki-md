# Radial effective potential on a surface of revolution

↑ **Parent:** [Effective potential](effective-potential.md)

A particle constrained to a stationary smooth [surface of revolution](surface-of-revolution.md) $z=f(r)$ under uniform [gravity](gravity.md) conserves $h=r^2\dot\theta$ and [mechanical energy](mechanical-energy.md) per unit [mass](mass.md). Substitute $\dot z=f'(r)\dot r$ and $\dot\theta=h/r^2$ into that [energy](energy-split.md) to obtain

$$
\frac12[1+f'(r)^2]\dot r^2+U_h(r)=E.
$$

This proves the radial reduction. Simple roots of $E-U_h$ are [turning points](turning-point.md); a stationary point $U_h'(r_0)=0$ supports an exact [circular orbit](circular-orbit.md). At a local maximum of $U_h$, another trajectory at the same [energy](energy-split.md) can approach the orbit asymptotically as a [separatrix](separatrix.md). Existence of roots elsewhere is insufficient for confinement: their dynamically accessible ordering relative to the launch radius matters.
