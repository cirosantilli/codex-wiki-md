# Exterior power

↑ **Parent:** [Exterior algebra](exterior-algebra.md)

The $k$th [exterior power](exterior-power.md) $\Lambda^kV$ is the [quotient vector space](quotient-vector-space.md) of the [tensor power](tensor-power.md) $V^{\otimes k}$ that makes every elementary tensor with two equal factors zero. More precisely, take the [quotient vector space](quotient-vector-space.md) by the [linear span](linear-span.md) of all such tensors. Its elementary products $v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_k$ turn alternating [multilinear maps](multilinear-map.md) into [linear maps](linear-map.md): every alternating $k$-linear map on $V$ factors uniquely through $\Lambda^kV$.[Exterior Powers, §3, Definition 3.1 and Theorem 3.3: quotient construction and universal property](exterior-powers-by-keith-conrad.md)

If $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is an [ordered basis](ordered-basis.md), the increasing products

$$
e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_k},\qquad
1\leq i_1<\cdots<i_k\leq n
$$

form a [basis](basis.md) of $\Lambda^kV$. There is one for each $k$-element choice of basis directions, so

$$
\boxed{\dim\Lambda^kV=\binom nk}.
$$

Repeated directions vanish; products with a different order can be sorted using their sign. Coordinate [determinants](determinant.md) distinguish the remaining increasing products, establishing [linear independence](linear-independence.md).[Exterior Powers, §4, Theorem 4.2: basis and rank of an exterior power](exterior-powers-by-keith-conrad.md)

For $V=\mathbb R^3$, a [basis](basis.md) of $\Lambda^2V$ is $e_1\wedge e_2,e_1\wedge e_3,e_2\wedge e_3$. This explains the three [bivector](bivector.md) components in the worked example. The top [exterior power](exterior-power.md) $\Lambda^3V$ has one basis element, $e_1\wedge e_2\wedge e_3$, while $\Lambda^4V=0$: four independent directions cannot fit into that [vector space](vector-space-split.md). By convention $\Lambda^0V$ is the scalar [field](field.md), even when $V$ has [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) zero.
