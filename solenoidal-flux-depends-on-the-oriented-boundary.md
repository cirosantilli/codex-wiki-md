# Solenoidal flux depends on the oriented boundary

↑ **Parent:** [Solenoidal vector field](solenoidal-vector-field.md)

Suppose two oriented surfaces have the same oriented boundary and $S_1\cup(-S_2)$ bounds a volume inside a domain where the [vector field](vector-field.md) $\mathbf F$ is smooth and [solenoidal](solenoidal-vector-field.md). The [divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md) gives zero flux through their closed union, proving the displayed equality. If a global [vector potential](vector-potential.md) $\mathbf F=\nabla\times\mathbf G$ exists on the relevant surfaces, the [Stokes theorem](stokes-theorem.md) proves the equality directly from their common boundary. The domain condition matters: a singularity inside the joining volume can obstruct the divergence argument.
