# Ffowcs Williams-Hawkings equation

↑ **Parent:** [Distributional acoustic analogy across a moving interface](distributional-acoustic-analogy-across-a-moving-interface.md)

The [Lighthill acoustic analogy](lighthill-acoustic-analogy.md) for a moving body has a volume [acoustic quadrupole](acoustic-quadrupole.md), a surface [acoustic monopole](acoustic-monopole.md) from [acoustic thickness noise](acoustic-thickness-noise.md), and a surface [acoustic dipole](acoustic-dipole.md) from [acoustic loading noise](acoustic-loading-noise.md). For an impermeable moving surface, with outward normal $n$, normal surface speed $v_n$, and fluid and body [normal velocities](normal-velocity.md) equal, the surface [mass](mass.md) coefficient is $\rho_0v_n$ and the surface loading is the [force](force.md) exerted on the fluid. Schematically, the density-source equation is

$$
(\partial_t^2-c_0^2\nabla^2)\rho'=\partial_i\partial_j(T_{ij}H(f))+\partial_t(Q\delta_s)-\partial_i(L_i\delta_s),
\quad \delta_s=\delta(f)|\nabla f|.
$$

The density perturbation is understood with the chosen interior extension. Permeable-surface versions have additional [mass](mass.md) and momentum flux terms. Source approximations must distinguish local thickness radiation from cancellation of its compact net-volume contribution.
