<h1 id="galton-s-stature-regression">Galton's stature regression</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Regression toward the mean](regression-toward-the-mean.md)

[Francis Galton](francis-galton.md)'s paper [Regression towards mediocrity in hereditary stature by Francis Galton](regression-towards-mediocrity-in-hereditary-stature-by-francis-galton.md) analyzes adult offspring heights against the average of their parents' adjusted heights. He multiplied female heights by $1.08$ before making that parental average. Table I contains 928 adult offspring from 205 parental pairs; the opening prose instead states 930. Its grouped readout is a [median](median.md), and the explanation of Plate IX describes a smoothed offspring deviation about two-thirds of the parental deviation.[Printed pp. 247-248, Table I; pp. 261-262, explanation of Plate IX(a): adjusted heights, counts, grouped medians and 2:3 deviation ratio](regression-towards-mediocrity-in-hereditary-stature-by-francis-galton.md)

These were parent-offspring records, not repeat readings of an unchanged signal. The paper's group tendency does not say that every child must be closer to the population [mean](expected-value-split.md). Galton also reports uncertainty over whether some recorded heights included shoes, a concrete [measurement error](measurement-error.md) limitation.[Printed p. 252: shoes-on versus shoes-off uncertainty and the average two-thirds height-deviation claim](regression-towards-mediocrity-in-hereditary-stature-by-francis-galton.md) The $0.6$ [Pearson correlation coefficient](pearson-correlation-coefficient.md) and all generated values above are our chosen model, not a reconstruction of his data.
