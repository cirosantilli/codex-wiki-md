# Three-body decay energy endpoints

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic three-body decay](relativistic-three-body-decay.md)

Use [natural units](natural-units.md) $c=1$ and assume open [phase space](phase-space.md), $M>m_1+m_2+m_3$. In the parent's [rest frame](rest-frame.md), define $s_{23}=(p_2+p_3)^2$. [Four-momentum conservation](four-momentum-conservation.md) gives

$$
E_1=\frac{M^2+m_1^2-s_{23}}{2M},\qquad (m_2+m_3)^2\leq s_{23}\leq(M-m_1)^2.
$$

The upper recoil-mass limit follows from $E_1\geq m_1$. For positive $m_2,m_3$, the lower recoil-mass limit follows in their pair [rest frame](rest-frame.md) and is attained when their relative momentum vanishes. If both recoil particles are massless, $s_{23}=2E_2E_3(1-\cos\theta_{23})$ reaches zero with nonzero parallel momenta. If exactly one is massless, $s_{23}$ approaches the nonzero daughter's squared mass only when the massless particle becomes soft. Thus that upper $E_1$ bound is a supremum for a genuine three-body event with all massless particles of positive energy. If $m_1=0$, the lower endpoint is likewise a soft limit. Multiplying the mass expressions for $E_1$ by $c^2$ restores energy units.
