<h1 id="section-a-i/13/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [carbon nanotube chiral vector](../../../../../../carbon-nanotube-chiral-vector.md) identifies equivalent sites of [graphene](../../../../../../graphene.md) that become one point on rolling:

$$
\mathbf C_h=n\mathbf a_1+m\mathbf a_2,\quad
|\mathbf a_1|=|\mathbf a_2|=a=\sqrt3b,\quad
d=\frac a\pi\sqrt{n^2+nm+m^2}.
$$

The primitive vectors meet at $60^\circ$, $b$ is the C–C bond length, and one can choose $0\le m\le n$. The [carbon nanotube chiral angle](../../../../../../carbon-nanotube-chiral-angle.md) satisfies $\tan\theta=\sqrt3m/(2n+m)$. **Armchair tubes have $(n,n)$ and $\theta=30^\circ$; zigzag tubes have $(n,0)$ and $\theta=0$**. Other pairs are chiral, with mirror-related left- and right-handed forms. Armchair and zigzag tubes themselves have mirror symmetry.

The circumferential [boundary condition](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) requires $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf C_h=2\pi\ell$. In [nanotube zone folding](../../../../../../nanotube-zone-folding.md), an allowed [wavevector](../../../../../../wavevector.md) line intersects one of the [Dirac valleys in graphene](../../../../../../dirac-valleys-in-graphene.md) precisely when **$n-m$ is divisible by three**. Thus all armchair tubes are metallic in this approximation. Zigzag tubes are metallic for $n$ divisible by three and otherwise semiconducting; general chiral tubes follow the same modular rule. Curvature can open small gaps in nominally metallic non-armchair tubes. Strain, interactions and broken symmetries can modify the ideal result; metallic band structure does not imply zero device resistance.
