<h1 id="section-a-i/14/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The competition between crystallization, exposed surfaces, adhesion and elastic strain can be summarized by

$$
\Delta G=-V\Delta g+\sum_j\gamma_jA_j+
(\gamma_{\rm int}-\gamma_{\rm support})A_{\rm contact}+E_{\rm strain}.
$$

Here $\Delta g>0$ measures the bulk driving force from [supersaturation](../../../../../../supersaturation.md), and the $\gamma$ values are interfacial [free energies](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md). For a free spherical nucleus, $\Delta G=4\pi r^2\gamma-4\pi r^3\Delta g/3$ gives $r_*=2\gamma/\Delta g$. A support changes the [heterogeneous nucleation](../../../../../../heterogeneous-nucleation.md) barrier through adhesion and geometry.

Precursor activities and supply rate, [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), time, solvent and ligands control nucleation, attachment, detachment and [surface diffusion](../../../../../../surface-diffusion.md). Many nuclei divide a fixed solute inventory into smaller particles; continued growth or [Ostwald ripening](../../../../../../ostwald-ripening.md) can enlarge them. Substrate defects determine preferred nucleation sites; [lattice mismatch](../../../../../../lattice-mismatch.md) and interface bonding constrain orientation and elastic relaxation. Facet-selective adsorption can change both surface energies and growth rates.

Near equilibrium, a free [nanocrystal](../../../../../../nanocrystal.md) follows a [Wulff construction](../../../../../../wulff-construction.md); a supported particle can follow a [Winterbottom construction](../../../../../../winterbottom-construction.md). Under kinetic control, slowly advancing facets remain visible while rapidly growing ones disappear. **Equilibrium facet energy and kinetic growth rate are different controls**, so a crystal structure does not uniquely determine particle shape.
