<h1 id="section-a-i/14/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Two applications are **ultraviolet photodetection and ultraviolet/near-band-edge light emission**. The bulk edge corresponds approximately to

$$
\lambda_g=\frac{hc}{E_g}\simeq\frac{1240\ {\rm eV\,nm}}{3.2\ {\rm eV}}
\simeq388\ {\rm nm}.
$$

Above-gap absorption creates carriers that can be collected in a [photodetector](../../../../../../photodetector.md). Measure dark current and wavelength-dependent response to separate photocarriers from heating or leakage. An optically or electrically excited emitter instead uses [electron-hole recombination](../../../../../../electron-hole-recombination.md). [quantum confinement](../../../../../../quantum-confinement.md) in sufficiently small dots can shift absorption and emission above the bulk [band gap](../../../../../../band-gap.md). Surface states can produce broad defect luminescence or nonradiative loss, so a direct bulk gap does not guarantee an efficient, narrow-band emitter.
