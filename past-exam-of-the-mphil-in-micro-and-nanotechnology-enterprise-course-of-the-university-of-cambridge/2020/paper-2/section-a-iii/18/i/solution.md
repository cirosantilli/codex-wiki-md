<h1 id="section-a-iii/18/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

At a straight [contact line](../../../../../../contact-line.md), take forces per unit transverse length and measure the [contact angle](../../../../../../contact-angle.md) $\theta$ through the liquid. Solid–vapor [surface tension](../../../../../../surface-tension-split.md) pulls outward along the solid, solid–liquid [surface tension](../../../../../../surface-tension-split.md) pulls inward, and the liquid–vapor force has horizontal component $\gamma_{LV}\cos\theta$. Tangential equilibrium gives

$$
\gamma_{SV}-\gamma_{SL}-\gamma_{LV}\cos\theta=0,\qquad
\boxed{\cos\theta=\frac{\gamma_{SV}-\gamma_{SL}}{\gamma_{LV}}}.
$$

This is [Young's equation](../../../../../../young-s-equation.md). The substrate supplies the normal reaction balancing the vertical capillary component. The original force sketch labels directions at a right-hand contact line. The result assumes smooth, chemically uniform, rigid surfaces in partial-wetting equilibrium, with negligible pinning and line tension.
