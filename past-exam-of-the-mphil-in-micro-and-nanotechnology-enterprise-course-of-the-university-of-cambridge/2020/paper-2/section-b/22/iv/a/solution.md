<h1 id="section-b/22/iv/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose an asymmetric [diblock copolymer](../../../../../../../diblock-copolymer.md) in a cylinder-forming regime: minority cylinders lie in a continuous majority matrix. Form and anneal a film to develop [microphase separation](../../../../../../../microphase-separation.md), control substrate and top-surface preferences or apply a suitable field to orient cylinders normal to the film, then selectively remove the minority phase. The retained majority matrix becomes the porous support. Infiltrate the channels with [liquid crystal](../../../../../../../liquid-crystal.md). **The majority phase forms the channel walls; removing that continuous matrix would not generally leave a continuous nanoporous membrane.**

With [homeotropic anchoring](../../../../../../../homeotropic-anchoring.md), the director is radial at a cylindrical side wall. A strictly transverse radial texture has a line core, but an **escaped radial texture** can avoid it:

$$
\mathbf n(r)=\sin\beta(r)\hat{\mathbf r}+\cos\beta(r)\hat{\mathbf z},
\quad \beta(0)=0,\quad\beta(a)=\pi/2.
$$

The director turns axially at the center and is normal to the wall at radius $a$. The original axial-section sketch shows this three-dimensional escape. End conditions, channel dimensions and anchoring strength determine whether escape or a defect-containing profile is favored.
