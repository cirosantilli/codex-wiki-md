<h1 id="section-b/20/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

An isolated nanometre-sized island has small total [capacitance](../../../../../../capacitance.md) $C_\Sigma=C_S+C_D+C_G+\cdots$, so discrete charge carries appreciable [charging energy](../../../../../../charging-energy.md). A simple [constant interaction model](../../../../../../constant-interaction-model.md) is

$$
U_N=E_C(N-n_g)^2,\quad E_C=\frac{e^2}{2C_\Sigma},\quad
n_g=\frac{C_GV_G+Q_0+\text{other induced charges}}e.
$$

Away from charge degeneracy, adding or removing an electron costs more energy than a sufficiently small thermal/bias window supplies. **[Coulomb blockade](../../../../../../coulomb-blockade.md) suppresses sequential tunnelling between resolved charge states.**

A [single-electron transistor](../../../../../../single-electron-transistor.md) has source and drain reservoirs connected to an island through two [tunnel junctions](../../../../../../tunnel-junction.md), plus an insulated capacitive gate. Each junction has resistance and [capacitance](../../../../../../capacitance.md); the gate shifts electrostatic energies without carrying the transport current. The original device schematic labels all components.

Require $k_BT\ll E_C$, a source–drain window below the relevant transition, and junction resistances well above $h/e^2\simeq25.8\ {\rm k\Omega}$ to suppress strong quantum charge fluctuations. Electromagnetic noise and lifetime broadening must also be small. Gate-induced charge degeneracy permits conduction even at small bias; there is no gate-independent blockade threshold. Resolving orbital levels additionally requires [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) and broadening below their spacing between [energy levels](../../../../../../energy-level.md).
