<h1 id="section-b/19/iii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Separated [graphene](../../../../../../../graphene.md) sheets offer high accessible [surface area](../../../../../../../surface-area.md), short lithium-transport distances and a conducting network. Edges and exposed sheet faces can supply additional storage sites; mechanical flexibility can help preserve contact during cycling. These advantages can improve rate capability when electrolyte access and electronic continuity are retained.

The [graphite](../../../../../../../graphite.md) reference composition $\mathrm{LiC_6}$ gives

$$
Q=\frac{F}{3.6(6M_C)}\simeq372\ {\rm mAh\,g^{-1}},
$$

where the host-carbon mass is used. An idealized $\mathrm{LiC_3}$ inventory doubles this figure, but **two accessible faces do not prove a reversible $\mathrm{LiC_3}$ electrode**. Binding energies, aggregation, defects and electrolyte stability determine actual storage.

Large area increases electrolyte decomposition and [solid electrolyte interphase](../../../../../../../solid-electrolyte-interphase.md) formation, consuming lithium in early cycles. Restacking reduces accessible area, low packing density reduces volumetric capacity, and residual oxygen, defects or binder can lower conductivity and active mass fraction. A broad storage-voltage profile may lower full-cell energy efficiency. Compare electrodes at matched areal loading and practical [current density](../../../../../../../current-density.md), reporting first-cycle efficiency, retention, voltage profile and volumetric as well as gravimetric capacity.
