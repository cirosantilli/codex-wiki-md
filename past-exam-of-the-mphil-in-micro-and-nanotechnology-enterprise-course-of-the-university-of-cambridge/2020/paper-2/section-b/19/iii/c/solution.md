<h1 id="section-b/19/iii/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**Yes: [carbon nanotubes](../../../../../../../carbon-nanotube.md) can store lithium and form a porous, conducting anode network.** Their external surfaces, defects and intertube spaces provide possible storage sites. Open ends and suitable defects may make internal surfaces accessible; a closed pristine tube should not be credited with its full internal area.

Axial conduction and an entangled mechanical scaffold can support either active storage or a composite electrode. Limitations include tube–tube [contact resistance](../../../../../../../contact-resistance.md), low packing density, catalyst residues and large-area [solid electrolyte interphase](../../../../../../../solid-electrolyte-interphase.md) formation. Opening or functionalizing tubes may improve ion access while increasing defects and irreversible reactions. Compare reversible capacity, first-cycle efficiency, retention and full-cell lithium inventory under matched loading and voltage-window conditions. A high first-cycle capacity alone is insufficient evidence of a superior anode.
