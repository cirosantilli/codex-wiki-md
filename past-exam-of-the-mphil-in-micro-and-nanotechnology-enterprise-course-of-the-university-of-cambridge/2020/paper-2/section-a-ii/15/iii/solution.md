<h1 id="section-a-ii/15/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Spin-polarized injection produces different spin-resolved [electrochemical potentials](../../../../../../electrochemical-potential.md) in Cu, $\mu_s=\mu_\uparrow-\mu_\downarrow$. Their imbalance diffuses beyond the charge-current path. Oppositely directed spin-resolved currents can cancel in net charge while transporting spin.

The second Py contact is a spin-selective voltage probe. In a weakly invasive model,

$$
V_{\rm NL}-V_0\simeq\frac{P_{\rm det}}{2e}\mu_s(L),\qquad
\mu_s(L)\propto P_{\rm inj}I\,e^{-L/\lambda_s}.
$$

Its open-circuit potential adjusts until the total detector charge current vanishes. That potential changes when the detector [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) reverses relative to the injected spins. **Zero detector charge current does not imply zero spin imbalance or zero measured voltage.**

Reverse injector current and each electrode's alignment to test the predicted signal symmetries. Remove spin-independent thermoelectric offsets and stray-field Hall contributions. A nonzero voltage alone would not prove [spin accumulation](../../../../../../spin-accumulation.md).[2003, nonlocal detection and separation from contact-related magnetoresistance](../../../../../../spin-injection-and-spin-accumulation-in-all-metal-mesoscopic-spin-valves-by-f-j-jedema-and-others.md)
