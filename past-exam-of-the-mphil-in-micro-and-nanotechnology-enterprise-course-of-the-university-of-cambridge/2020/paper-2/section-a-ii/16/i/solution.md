<h1 id="section-a-ii/16/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For standard deviations of [position](../../../../../../position.md) and [momentum](../../../../../../momentum-split.md),

$$
\boxed{\Delta x\,\Delta p_x\ge\hbar/2}.
$$

Confinement to a region of size $R$ requires a momentum spread of order $\hbar/R$, hence kinetic energy of order $\hbar^2/(2m^*R^2)$. A sufficiently small semiconducting [nanocrystal](../../../../../../nanocrystal.md) therefore has discrete [energy levels](../../../../../../energy-level.md) and an optical edge shifted above the bulk [band gap](../../../../../../band-gap.md).

An illustrative strong-confinement model is

$$
E_{\rm opt}(R)\simeq E_g+
\frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2R^2}\left(\frac1{m_e^*}+\frac1{m_h^*}\right)
-\frac{\alpha e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\epsilon_rR},
$$

where $\alpha$ is a geometry-dependent number of order unity. The positive confinement contribution grows as $R^{-2}$; [electron-hole Coulomb interaction](../../../../../../electron-hole-coulomb-interaction.md) contributes an attractive term of order $-R^{-1}$. The uncertainty principle motivates the scale, not the exact coefficient or oscillator strength. Strong size dependence occurs near the [exciton Bohr radius](../../../../../../exciton-bohr-radius.md). Finite barriers, dielectric mismatch and surface states modify this model, while size distributions broaden ensemble spectra.
