<h1 id="section-a-i/1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [atomic force microscope](../../../../../../atomic-force-microscope.md) measures the [cantilever](../../../../../../cantilever.md) deflection $d$, converted to normal [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) by $F=k_cd$ after [calibration](../../../../../../calibration-differential-geometry.md). On approach, the [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) is initially almost zero, then attractive; a sufficiently steep attractive [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) gradient causes [jump-to-contact](../../../../../../jump-to-contact.md). After contact, further approach bends the [cantilever](../../../../../../cantilever.md) and indents the sample, giving a rising repulsive branch. With tip-surface separation increasing to the right, approach proceeds from right to left. The instability occurs when the magnitude of the attractive [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) gradient exceeds the [cantilever](../../../../../../cantilever.md) stiffness. The contact-branch slope depends on both [cantilever](../../../../../../cantilever.md) and sample compliance.
