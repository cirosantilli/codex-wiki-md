<h1 id="section-a-ii/4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use **thermal evaporation** or electron-beam evaporation: evaporated Au travels approximately ballistically to the Si wafer. For [line-of-sight deposition](../../../../../../line-of-sight-deposition.md), the [gas](../../../../../../gas.md) [mean free path](../../../../../../mean-free-path.md)

$$
\ell=\frac{k_BT}{\sqrt2\pi d_g^2p}
$$

should greatly exceed the source-wafer distance $L$. Increasing the chamber's useful transport length therefore requires a proportionately lower pressure to retain the same $\ell/L$, for fixed [gas](../../../../../../gas.md) and temperature. There is no universal pressure requirement determined by chamber volume alone.

A distant compact source gives a more uniform angular flux across the wafer, but the deposition rate falls roughly as $L^{-2}$. For an ideal cosine-emitting point source and a parallel wafer, the relative thickness at lateral radius $r$ is approximately $[1+(r/L)^2]^{-2}$. Mitigate the trade-off with wafer rotation and planetary motion, multiple or extended sources, shaped masks and suitable source/wafer geometry. Rotation averages azimuthal variation; ordinary rotation alone does not remove a purely radial thickness gradient. A thin Ti or Cr [adhesion](../../../../../../adhesion.md) layer may be needed for robust Au [adhesion](../../../../../../adhesion.md) to oxidized Si.
