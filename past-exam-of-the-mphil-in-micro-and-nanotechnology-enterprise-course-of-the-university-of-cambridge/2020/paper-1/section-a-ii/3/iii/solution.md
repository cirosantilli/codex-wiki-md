[Projection lithography](../../../../../../projection-lithography.md) images a mask through [reduction](../../../../../../reduction-chemistry.md) optics; its resolution scales approximately as $k_1\lambda/\mathrm{NA}$. It offers parallel exposure, high throughput and accurate repeat patterning, but small features demand short wavelengths, large [numerical aperture](../../../../../../numerical-aperture.md), costly optics and high-quality masks, with a shrinking depth of focus.

[Electron beam lithography](../../../../../../electron-beam-lithography.md) directly writes a programmable pattern using a focused [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) beam. Its much shorter wavelength and small probe permit nanometre-scale features without an optical mask, making it attractive for research prototypes, low-volume devices and mask fabrication. Its limitations are serial writing time, charging of insulating substrates, beam drift, field stitching and forward/backscattering [proximity effects in electron-beam lithography](../../../../../../proximity-effects-in-electron-beam-lithography.md). [Electron](../../../../../../electron.md) wavelength alone does not set the resist resolution. **E-beam is often preferable for a few very small custom structures; projection remains preferable for high-volume repeated manufacture.**
