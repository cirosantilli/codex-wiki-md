A [MOSFET](../../../../../../mosfet.md) is a voltage-controlled three-terminal switch: the insulated gate controls a conductive channel, giving high input impedance and useful current gain at the circuit level. A [CMOS inverter](../../../../../../cmos-inverter.md) actively restores either logic rail, has [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) gain near its switching threshold, and ideally draws no static supply current in either settled logic state. Real devices have leakage and consume energy while switching.

A single [p-n junction](../../../../../../p-n-junction.md) is a two-terminal diode; its current is controlled by its own terminal [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md), so it does not by itself provide an independently controlled switch, gain and restoring inversion. Cross-coupled [CMOS inverters](../../../../../../cmos-inverter.md) form a bistable memory element. **A lone inverter is not itself a complete memory cell**, and a [p-n junction](../../../../../../p-n-junction.md) can still serve useful rectification or capacitance functions.
