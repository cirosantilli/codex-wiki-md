<h1 id="section-b/9/iii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Clean the exposed [silicon](../../../../../../../silicon.md) and grow a thin, high-quality **SiO2 gate dielectric by controlled dry thermal [oxidation](../../../../../../../oxidation.md)**. Deposit polycrystalline [silicon](../../../../../../../silicon.md), conventionally by low-pressure [chemical vapour deposition](../../../../../../../chemical-vapor-deposition.md) from silane, dope it in situ or by subsequent implantation and activation, and pattern/etch it to define the two gates. [Phosphorus](../../../../../../../phosphorus.md) or [arsenic](../../../../../../../arsenic.md) gives n+ polysilicon; [boron](../../../../../../../boron.md) gives p+ polysilicon where the selected gate-work-function process requires it. A traditional single-poly process may use a common gate doping instead; gate and source/drain doping need not be identical.

The patterned gates subsequently serve as self-alignment masks for donor-doped nMOS source/drain and acceptor-doped pMOS source/drain regions. Annealing activates these dopants. Keep the oxide thin and uniform enough for strong electrostatic control but free from pinholes and excessive leakage. A high-permittivity dielectric deposited by [atomic layer deposition](../../../../../../../atomic-layer-deposition.md) with a metal gate is a modern alternative, rather than the conventional polysilicon/SiO2 stack drawn.
