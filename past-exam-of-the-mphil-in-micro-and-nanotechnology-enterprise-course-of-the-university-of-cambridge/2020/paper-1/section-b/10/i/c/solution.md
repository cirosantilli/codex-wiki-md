<h1 id="section-b/10/i/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Treat the two film surfaces as an effective interfacial tension $\Gamma=2\gamma$. The relevant [Bond number](../../../../../../../bond-number.md) for the gas-density difference is $\mathrm{Bo}=\Delta\rho gr^2/\Gamma$. Substituting the [Young–Laplace equation](../../../../../../../young-laplace-equation.md) and the preceding density relation gives

$$
\mathrm{Bo}=\frac{(4\rho_0\gamma/(rp_0))gr^2}{2\gamma}=\frac{2r}{h_0},\qquad h_0=\frac{p_0}{\rho_0g}.
$$

Thus the formal crossover $\mathrm{Bo}>1$ gives **$r>h_0/2\simeq4.25\,\mathrm{km}$**. This compares gravity on the excess [gas](../../../../../../../gas.md) with capillary restoration and neglects film weight. It does not say gravity-driven drainage of a real water film is negligible below kilometre scales. At this enormous radius, treating ambient pressure and density as uniform is itself no longer quantitatively reliable.
