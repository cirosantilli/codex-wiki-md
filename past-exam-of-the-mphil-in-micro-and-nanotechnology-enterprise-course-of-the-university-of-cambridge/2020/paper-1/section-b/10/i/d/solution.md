<h1 id="section-b/10/i/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Approximating the skin as a uniform thin water shell with density $1000\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$,

$$
\boxed{m_{\rm skin}\simeq4\pi r^2\tau\rho_w=1.26\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.126\,\mathrm g.}
$$

This is about **2500 times** the $5.03\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{kg}$ buoyant mass of the enclosed [gas](../../../../../../../gas.md). The film weight therefore dominates the bubble's downward gravitational [force](../../../../../../../force-split.md) under the stated assumptions. Equal-temperature air inside does not provide a net lift to balance it: an apparently floating bubble can be transiently supported by air currents or have different internal temperature/composition. Film thickness is generally nonuniform and changes with drainage and evaporation; the uniform $1\,\mu\mathrm m$ thickness is an estimate, not a stable equilibrium condition.
