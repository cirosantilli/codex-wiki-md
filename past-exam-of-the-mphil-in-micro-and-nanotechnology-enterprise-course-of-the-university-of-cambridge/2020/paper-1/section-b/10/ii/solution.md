<h1 id="section-b/10/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a spherical liquid droplet, the [Kelvin equation](../../../../../../kelvin-equation.md) is

$$
\ln\frac{p(r)}{p(\infty)}=\frac{2\gamma V_m}{rRT}.
$$

Here $V_m=0.018/957=1.881\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3\,mol^{-1}}$, so

$$
\boxed{r=\frac{2(0.0585)(1.881\times10^{-5})}{8.314(375)\ln2}=1.02\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm m.}
$$

The formal answer is **about $1.0\,\mathrm{nm}$**. At molecular dimensions, bulk [surface tension](../../../../../../surface-tension-split.md), incompressible-liquid and continuum curvature assumptions are only approximate. There is one liquid-vapour interface here, unlike a soap bubble's two interfaces.
