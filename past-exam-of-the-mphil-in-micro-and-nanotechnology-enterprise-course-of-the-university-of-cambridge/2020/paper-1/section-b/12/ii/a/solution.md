The **[Shockley-Queisser limit](../../../../../../../shockley-queisser-limit.md)** is the detailed-balance efficiency bound for an ideal single-junction [solar cell](../../../../../../../solar-cell.md) with [radiative recombination](../../../../../../../radiative-recombination.md) under specified illumination and optical conditions. Below-gap photons are not absorbed and excess energy of above-gap photons is thermalized; radiative emission further limits the output [voltage](../../../../../../../voltage.md). The often quoted unconcentrated terrestrial maximum is roughly one third, not an illumination-independent universal percentage.

A [multi-junction solar cell](../../../../../../../multi-junction-solar-cell.md) places a high-gap junction before lower-gap junctions, dividing the spectrum so photons generate carriers in a better-matched absorber. This reduces transmission and thermalization losses and can exceed the single-junction bound without violating its assumptions: there is now more than one junction. In a two-terminal series stack, all junctions carry the same current, so the lowest photocurrent limits the whole device. **Current matching** uses bandgap choice, absorber thickness, optical management and sometimes lateral area to make the generated currents comparable for the intended spectrum. Independently contacted junctions need not share the same current.[Single-junction radiative detailed-balance assumptions](../../../../../../../detailed-balance-limit-of-efficiency-of-p-n-junction-solar-cells-by-william-shockley-and-hans-j-queisser.md)
