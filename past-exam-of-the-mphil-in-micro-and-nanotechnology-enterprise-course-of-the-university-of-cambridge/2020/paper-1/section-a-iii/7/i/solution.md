<h1 id="section-a-iii/7/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [Seebeck effect](../../../../../../seebeck-effect.md) produces an [open-circuit voltage](../../../../../../open-circuit-voltage.md) when a material has a temperature gradient: $S=-dV/dT$ for the stated sign convention. A circuit containing dissimilar thermoelectric legs converts this into useful [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) and current. The intrinsic [thermoelectric figure of merit](../../../../../../thermoelectric-figure-of-merit.md) is

$$
\boxed{Z=\frac{S^2\sigma}{\kappa},\qquad ZT=\frac{S^2\sigma T}{\kappa}.}
$$

Here $\sigma$ is [electrical conductivity](../../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md) and $\kappa=\kappa_e+\kappa_L$ total [thermal conductivity](../../../../../../thermal-conductivity.md). $Z$ has units $\mathrm{K^{-1}}$; $ZT$ is dimensionless. A large [Seebeck coefficient](../../../../../../seebeck-coefficient.md) and [electrical conductivity](../../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md), together with low [thermal conductivity](../../../../../../thermal-conductivity.md), favour efficient conversion.
