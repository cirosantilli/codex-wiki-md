<h1 id="section-a-iii/5/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

[DLVO theory](../../../../../../dlvo-theory.md) adds short-range attractive [Van der Waals forces](../../../../../../van-der-waals-force.md) to repulsive overlap of the [electric double layer](../../../../../../electric-double-layer.md). Increasing salt concentration raises the [ionic strength](../../../../../../ionic-strength.md) and shortens the [Debye length](../../../../../../debye-length.md), screening the repulsion. The repulsive barrier falls, so thermal collisions can carry particles into the primary attractive minimum and cause [colloidal coagulation](../../../../../../colloidal-coagulation.md). An intermediate shallow secondary minimum can instead produce weak, potentially reversible association.

The figure's assignments are **a: low salt; b: intermediate salt; c: high salt**. Curve a has the largest protective barrier, b a lower barrier and secondary minimum, and c no effective repulsive barrier. This explanation assumes the surface charging chemistry does not independently reverse or radically change the surface potential.
