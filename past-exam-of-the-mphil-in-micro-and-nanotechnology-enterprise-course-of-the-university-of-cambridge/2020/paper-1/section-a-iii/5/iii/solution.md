<h1 id="section-a-iii/5/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In the usual high-surface-potential [Schulze-Hardy rule](../../../../../../schulze-hardy-rule.md) approximation, $c_{\rm ccc}\propto z^{-6}$, where $z$ is the magnitude of the counterion valence. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{c_{2:2}=\frac{100}{2^6}=1.56\,\mathrm{mM},\qquad c_{3:3}=\frac{100}{3^6}=0.137\,\mathrm{mM}.}
$$

Multivalent counterions screen surface charge much more effectively and strongly lower the electrostatic barrier. The sixth-power estimate includes the way the critical [ionic strength](../../../../../../ionic-strength.md) and screening condition change with valence; merely scaling the ordinary [Debye length](../../../../../../debye-length.md) at fixed salt concentration would not derive it. Specific adsorption, charge regulation or [ion](../../../../../../ion.md) correlations can produce substantial deviations.[Counterion-valence sensitivity of the [critical coagulation concentration](../../../../../../critical-coagulation-concentration.md)](../../../../../../schulze-hardy-rule-in-the-iupac-gold-book.md)
