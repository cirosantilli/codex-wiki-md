<h1 id="section-a-i/2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In [quantitative nanomechanical mapping](../../../../../../quantitative-nanomechanical-mapping.md), the operational [adhesion](../../../../../../adhesion.md) is the magnitude of the most attractive [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) immediately before detachment on a calibrated retraction curve:

$$
F_{\rm adh}=-\min F_{\rm retract},\qquad F=k_c\delta_c.
$$

Here $k_c$ is the [cantilever spring constant](../../../../../../cantilever-spring-constant.md) and $\delta_c$ its deflection after baseline subtraction. The area between approach and retraction relates to dissipated work, whereas the minimum [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) is a [force](../../../../../../force-split.md), not an [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md). Chemical attraction, [Van der Waals forces](../../../../../../van-der-waals-force.md) and ambient-water capillary bridges can all contribute. Match loading rate and peak load, calibrate deflection sensitivity and $k_c$, compare clean reference surfaces, and control humidity; otherwise different maps may reflect different tips or capillary conditions rather than different specimens.
