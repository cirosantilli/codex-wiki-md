<h1 id="section-b/11/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The oxygen-pressure exponents can be explained by a change in the dominant charge state of metal vacancies, with holes carrying the p-type current. For a divalent metal oxide, a doubly negative metal vacancy is $V_{\rm M}^{\prime\prime}$ in [Kröger–Vink notation](../../../../../../kroger-vink-notation.md). A conventional oxygen-incorporation reaction is

$$
\frac12\mathrm O_2(g)\rightleftharpoons
O_{\rm O}^{\times}+V_{\rm M}^{\prime\prime}+2h^\bullet.
$$

Treating regular oxygen-site activity as constant, the [law of mass action](../../../../../../law-of-mass-action.md) gives $K_2=[V_{\rm M}^{\prime\prime}]p_h^2/p_{\rm O_2}^{1/2}$. If these are the compensating defects, [charge neutrality](../../../../../../charge-neutrality.md) gives $p_h=2[V_{\rm M}^{\prime\prime}]$, hence $p_h^3\propto p_{\rm O_2}^{1/2}$ and **$\sigma\propto p_{\rm O_2}^{1/6}$**, assuming nearly constant hole mobility.

For singly negative vacancies, use

$$
\frac12\mathrm O_2(g)\rightleftharpoons
O_{\rm O}^{\times}+V_{\rm M}^{\prime}+h^\bullet.
$$

Now $p_h=[V_{\rm M}^{\prime}]$ and $p_h^2\propto p_{\rm O_2}^{1/2}$, so **$\sigma\propto p_{\rm O_2}^{1/4}$**. The charge-state equilibrium $V_{\rm M}^{\prime}\rightleftharpoons V_{\rm M}^{\prime\prime}+h^\bullet$ gives $[V_{\rm M}^{\prime\prime}]/[V_{\rm M}^{\prime}]=K_{\rm ion}/p_h$: the singly charged fraction can therefore dominate as oxygen pressure and hole [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) increase. This is a consistent dilute-defect model, not a unique experimental identification of every defect. Changes in mobility, defect association or other compensating defects require independent tests.
