<h1 id="section-b/11/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The reversible voltage follows from the two-electron hydrogen reaction:

$$
E_{\rm rev}=-\frac{\Delta_rG^\circ}{2F}=\frac{237000}{193000}
=1.22798\ \mathrm V.
$$

At the specified geometric [current density](../../../../../../current-density.md), the magnitude of each activation loss follows its [Tafel equation](../../../../../../tafel-equation.md), $\eta=b\log_{10}(j/j_0)$:

$$
\eta_{\rm H}=0.0295\log_{10}(10/10^{-4})=0.1475\ \mathrm V,
\qquad
\eta_{\rm O}=0.09\log_{10}(10/10^{-7})=0.7200\ \mathrm V.
$$

Both losses reduce the delivered [fuel cell](../../../../../../fuel-cell.md) voltage. Therefore **$V=0.36048\ \mathrm V$ and $P/A=jV=3.60\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$**. The very small oxygen exchange current is responsible for most of the activation loss. The calculation uses [current densities](../../../../../../current-density.md) on a common area basis, unit water activity and the stated gas conditions, and neglects the losses the problem excludes.
