A [thermoelectric generator](../../../../../../thermoelectric-generator.md) uses the [Seebeck effect](../../../../../../seebeck-effect.md) and needs a spatial [temperature gradient](../../../../../../temperature-gradient.md): a couple develops $V\simeq(S_p-S_n)\Delta T$ between hot and cold junctions. It can deliver continuous power under a steady thermal gradient and has no moving parts, but requires sustained heat flow and adequate hot- and cold-side thermal contacts. Thermal conduction, internal [electrical resistance](../../../../../../electrical-resistance.md) and the material's [thermoelectric figure of merit](../../../../../../thermoelectric-figure-of-merit.md) limit conversion.

A [pyroelectric generator](../../../../../../pyroelectric-generator.md) responds to a time-dependent spontaneous [electric polarization](../../../../../../polarization-density.md). Under approximately uniform heating or cooling, $I=pA\,dT/dt$ when the electrical boundary condition allows charge flow. It can harvest temporal thermal fluctuations without a persistent spatial gradient. However, at a constant uniform [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) its ordinary pyroelectric current stops; practical extraction needs cycling, low-leakage electronics and a suitable load. A large open-circuit voltage is not evidence of a large available average power. Temperature-driven nanowire measurements provide a primary experimental example of this distinction.[Nano Letters 12 (2012), abstract and temperature-variation mechanism](../../../../../../pyroelectric-nanogenerators-for-harvesting-thermoelectric-energy-by-ya-yang-and-others.md)
