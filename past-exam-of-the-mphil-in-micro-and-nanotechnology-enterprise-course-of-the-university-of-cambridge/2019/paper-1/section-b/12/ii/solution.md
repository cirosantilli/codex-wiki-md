<h1 id="section-b/12/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For [electrode](../../../../../../electrode.md) area $A$, the change in spontaneous [polarization density](../../../../../../polarization-density.md) supplies a charge scale $\Delta Q=pA\Delta T$, while the film's [capacitance](../../../../../../capacitance.md) is $C=\varepsilon A/d$. The voltage magnitude is therefore $|\Delta V|=|p|d|\Delta T|/\varepsilon$. More explicitly, for an initially compensated film with no change in free [electrode](../../../../../../electrode.md) charge, $\Delta D=\varepsilon\Delta E+p\Delta T=0$. Taking $z$ from the lower [electrode](../../../../../../electrode.md) to the upper one gives $\Delta V_{\rm upper-lower}=-d\Delta E=pd\Delta T/\varepsilon$.

$$
\Delta V_{\rm upper-lower}
=\frac{(-30\times10^{-6})(10^{-6})(1)}{10\varepsilon_0}
=\boxed{-0.339\ \mathrm V}.
$$

The measurable magnitude is **$0.339\ \mathrm V$**, with polarity reversed by reversing the poling direction or the [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) change. This is an ideal open-circuit estimate: leakage, the measuring instrument's input [capacitance](../../../../../../capacitance.md) and finite charge-relaxation time can reduce or reshape the observed voltage.
