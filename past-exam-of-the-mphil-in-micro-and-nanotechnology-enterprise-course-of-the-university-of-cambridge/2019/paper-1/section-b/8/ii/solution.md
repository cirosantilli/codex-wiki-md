<h1 id="section-b/8/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

This is a conventional transmitted-beam [bright-field transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../../bright-field-transmission-electron-microscopy.md) image containing high-resolution [electron phase contrast](../../../../../../electron-phase-contrast.md) and visible lattice fringes, rather than an element-specific chemical map. Relative to the scale bar, the smaller particle has an outer projected diameter of roughly **$40\ \mathrm{nm}$**. It is approximately rounded and contains a denser outer rim with several lighter internal regions separated by material. A porous or internally hollow particle is a plausible morphological interpretation; the image is not a simple featureless solid sphere.

Greater projected thickness generally removes more intensity from the transmitted beam, producing darker [mass–thickness contrast](../../../../../../mass-thickness-contrast.md). Different crystallite orientations also scatter intensity differently and give [diffraction contrast](../../../../../../diffraction-contrast.md). Local lattice fringes reveal crystalline material and changes of fringe orientation indicate more than one coherent domain. Bright/dark edge fringes also contain defocus-dependent [Fresnel contrast](../../../../../../fresnel-contrast.md); their width is not a direct shell-thickness measurement. **Neither an exact alumina polymorph nor three-dimensional closed porosity can be uniquely established from this projection alone.** Use calibrated fringe spacings and [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) [diffraction](../../../../../../diffraction.md) for phase identification, tilt-series imaging for void geometry, and a through-focus or dose series to test contrast artefacts.
