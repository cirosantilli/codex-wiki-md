<h1 id="section-b/8/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For a weak phase object, the [contrast transfer function](../../../../../../contrast-transfer-function.md) gives the signed transfer of an object's spatial Fourier components into intensity contrast. In a common convention,

$$
H(u)=E(u)\sin\chi(u),\qquad
\chi(u)=\pi\left(\frac12 C_S\lambda^3u^4+\Delta f\lambda u^2\right),
$$

where $u$ is a cyclic [spatial frequency](../../../../../../spatial-frequency.md), $\Delta f$ the defocus, $C_S$ the [spherical aberration](../../../../../../spherical-aberration.md) coefficient, and $E(u)$ a coherence envelope. A zero suppresses that [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md); crossing a zero reverses contrast. The overall sign depends on the image convention, but the zeros do not. Single-image division by $H$ cannot recover information exactly at its zeros. The microscope manufacturer's discussion explicitly connects the first zero with interpretable resolution and the higher-frequency sign changes.[glossary, first-zero and envelope-function discussion](../../../../../../phase-contrast-transfer-function-pctf-by-jeol.md)

The plotted first nontrivial zero is at approximately $u_0=4.0\ \mathrm{nm^{-1}}$. Hence **the smallest spacing in the first uninterrupted contrast band is about $d_0=1/u_0=0.25\ \mathrm{nm}$**. This axis is cycles per [length](../../../../../../length.md), not an angular wave number requiring a factor of $2\pi$. Smaller spacings can contribute in later bands, but their contrast has reversed and requires appropriate interpretation or multiple-defocus reconstruction.
