<h1 id="section-b/8/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Disperse a very small amount of the dry powder in a clean volatile solvent compatible with [aluminium oxide](../../../../../../aluminium-oxide.md), use brief gentle sonication to separate loose agglomerates, and place a dilute drop on a holey carbon-coated [transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../../transmission-electron-microscopy.md) grid. Wick away excess liquid and dry thoroughly. Look first at low magnification and select isolated particles or thin edges over holes, rather than an electron-opaque pile. Record a solvent-only grid to identify contamination and compare several independently prepared areas.

This is quick and preserves individual particles without mechanical thinning. However, [spray pyrolysis](../../../../../../spray-pyrolysis.md) can make hollow or porous particles that may fracture during sonication; sedimentation and drying can select sizes and aggregate particles. [aluminium oxide](../../../../../../aluminium-oxide.md) is insulating, so charging or beam-induced changes can complicate images, and the carbon support can overlap weak contrast. Vary dose and dwell time to test stability. A projected image of one selected thin particle is not an unbiased three-dimensional size or porosity distribution of the whole powder.
