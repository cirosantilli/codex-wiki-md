<h1 id="section-b/10/i/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [Pickering emulsion](../../../../../../../pickering-emulsion.md) is stabilized by particles adsorbed at the liquid–liquid interface. A partially wetting sphere removes a patch of that interface. The smaller detachment barrier into either bulk phase is approximately

$$
\Delta E=\pi r_p^2\gamma_{ow}(1-|\cos\theta|)^2,
$$

where $\theta$ is the three-phase [contact angle](../../../../../../../contact-angle.md). At approximately neutral wetting, $\theta=90^\circ$, the stated particle radius gives

$$
\Delta E=\pi(20\times10^{-9})^2(0.050)
=6.28\times10^{-17}\ \mathrm J
\simeq\boxed{1.53\times10^4\,k_BT}
$$

at $25^\circ\mathrm C$. Such a large attachment [energy](../../../../../../../energy-split.md) makes thermal detachment unlikely. A sufficiently covered interface resists drop coalescence, and a water-wet particle preference supports an oil-in-water dispersion. However, **radius alone does not guarantee stabilization**: completely water-wet or oil-wet particles have a vanishing attachment barrier in this model, and sufficient particle coverage is necessary. Sedimentation and droplet ripening are separate failure modes. The classical particle-stabilized-emulsion experiments provide the historical basis of the terminology.[Journal of the Chemical Society, Transactions 91 (1907), pp. 2001–2021](../../../../../../../cxcvi-emulsions-by-spencer-umfreville-pickering.md)
