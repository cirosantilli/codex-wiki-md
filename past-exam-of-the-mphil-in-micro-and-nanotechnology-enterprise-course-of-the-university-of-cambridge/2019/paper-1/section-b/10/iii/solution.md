<h1 id="section-b/10/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For two equal spheres, the [Derjaguin approximation](../../../../../../derjaguin-approximation.md) uses $R_{\rm eff}=R/2$. A planar nonretarded [Van der Waals force](../../../../../../van-der-waals-force.md) interaction has [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) per area $W(h)=-A_H/(12\pi h^2)$, so

$$
F=2\pi R_{\rm eff}W(h)=-\frac{A_HR}{12h^2}
=-\frac{(15.6\times10^{-20})(10^{-3})}{12(10^{-9})^2}
=\boxed{-1.30\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm N}.
$$

The [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) is **attractive, with magnitude $13.0\ \mathrm{\mu N}$**, taking separation increase as positive. Using the sphere–plane value of $R_{\rm eff}$ would overestimate it by a factor of two. The gap is much smaller than either radius, as required. Treat the supplied [Hamaker constant](../../../../../../hamaker-constant.md) as the effective one for the intervening medium; roughness, solvation and molecular-scale contact can modify a literal $1\ \mathrm{nm}$ experiment.
