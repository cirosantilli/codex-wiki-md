<h1 id="section-a-iii/5/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

[charge stabilization of colloids](../../../../../../charge-stabilization-of-colloids.md) supplies repulsion when like-charged surfaces and their [electric double layers](../../../../../../electric-double-layer.md) approach. [steric stabilization](../../../../../../steric-stabilization.md) uses attached or adsorbed molecular layers whose overlap costs conformational and osmotic [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md). Combining both gives [electrosteric stabilization](../../../../../../electrosteric-stabilization.md); a solvated coating must remain attached and well solvated. A viscous medium slows encounters but does not by itself create an equilibrium repulsive barrier.

[DLVO theory](../../../../../../dlvo-theory.md) adds screened electrostatic repulsion to [Van der Waals force](../../../../../../van-der-waals-force.md). A representative close-gap form is

$$
U(h)\simeq B e^{-\kappa h}-\frac{A_Ha}{12h},
$$

for equal spheres of radius $a$, with [Hamaker constant](../../../../../../hamaker-constant.md) $A_H$ and inverse [Debye–Hückel screening length](../../../../../../debye-huckel-screening-length.md) $\kappa$. The formula is schematic near molecular contact, where hydration, surface roughness and finite [ion](../../../../../../ion.md) size matter. A repulsive barrier many $k_BT$ high produces kinetic stability even if the primary attractive minimum is deeper than the dispersed state. Entry into that primary minimum generally gives strongly bound aggregation; a shallow secondary minimum can give reversible flocculation.

Adding [electrolyte](../../../../../../electrolyte.md) increases $\kappa$, shortens the repulsion and can remove the barrier, enabling fast diffusion-limited aggregation. The [critical coagulation concentration](../../../../../../critical-coagulation-concentration.md) is the approximate salt [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) at which this rapid-coagulation regime begins under specified conditions. It is not a universal property of the salt alone. Test the prediction using time-resolved [dynamic light scattering](../../../../../../dynamic-light-scattering.md) or turbidity at controlled particle [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md), pH and [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md); check that added [ions](../../../../../../ion.md) do not specifically adsorb or change the surface chemistry.
