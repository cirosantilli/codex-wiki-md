<h1 id="section-a-iii/5/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In the high-surface-potential [Schulze-Hardy rule](../../../../../../schulze-hardy-rule.md), the [critical coagulation concentration](../../../../../../critical-coagulation-concentration.md) scales approximately as $z^{-6}$ with the [counterion](../../../../../../counterion.md) valence. Hence

$$
c_{\rm Al}=c_{\rm Ca}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^6
=0.01\frac{64}{729}
=\boxed{8.78\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}}.
$$

The more highly charged [counterion](../../../../../../counterion.md) therefore requires about eleven times less [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) in this idealized estimate. This assumes the cations are the [counterions](../../../../../../counterion.md) of a negatively charged surface, the surface-potential regime is appropriate, and the only changing mechanism is electrostatic stabilization. Specific [adsorption](../../../../../../adsorption.md) and hydrolysis of aluminium [ions](../../../../../../ion.md) can invalidate this scaling; matching [ionic strengths](../../../../../../ionic-strength.md) instead is not the same prediction.
