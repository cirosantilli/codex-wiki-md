<h1 id="section-a-iii/6/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

With $n=2$, the standard reaction [Gibbs free energy](../../../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md) and dimensionless [equilibrium constant](../../../../../../equilibrium-constant.md) are

$$
\Delta_rG^\circ=-nFE^\circ=-2(96500)(0.304)
=\boxed{-58.672\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}},
\qquad
K=\exp\!\left(\frac{nFE^\circ}{RT}\right)
=\boxed{1.93\times10^{10}}.
$$

For the reaction as written, $Q=a_{\rm Fe^{2+}}/a_{\rm Sn^{2+}}$, and the [Nernst equation](../../../../../../nernst-equation.md) gives $E=E^\circ-(RT/2F)\ln Q$. Consequently **zero cell potential requires $[\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}]/[\mathrm{Sn^{2+}}]=K\simeq1.93\times10^{10}$** under the specified unit-activity-coefficient assumption. Reversing that ratio reverses the conclusion. This mathematical ratio does not by itself guarantee experimentally attainable [concentrations](../../../../../../concentration.md) without precipitation or other chemistry.
