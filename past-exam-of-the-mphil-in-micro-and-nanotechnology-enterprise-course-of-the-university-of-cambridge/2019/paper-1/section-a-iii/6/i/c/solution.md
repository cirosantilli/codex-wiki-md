↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**Iron is the negative [anode](../../../../../../../anode.md) and tin is the positive [cathode](../../../../../../../cathode.md)** of this spontaneous [galvanic cell](../../../../../../../galvanic-cell.md). [electrons](../../../../../../../electron.md) travel through the external circuit from iron to tin. An intact tin coating protects iron mainly by physically excluding the corrosive medium. If a defect exposes iron while the metals remain electrically connected, tin is not a sacrificial coating: the exposed iron can become the anodic corrosion site. A small exposed iron area coupled to a large cathodic tin area can be especially damaging; this distinction follows from the electrode-potential order rather than the appearance of the coating.
