<h1 id="section-a-ii/3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

On a graph of normalized remaining thickness against $\log_{10}D$, both [positive photoresist](../../../../../../positive-photoresist.md) curves remain near unity below $D_0$ and fall to zero by $D_c$. Their transition widths are $\log_{10}(D_c/D_0)=1/\gamma$: $0.752$ decades for the lower-contrast material and $0.177$ decades for the higher-contrast material. The latter therefore has a much steeper transition. The curves can be aligned at a common illustrative clearing dose; their absolute horizontal placement cannot be inferred from [photoresist contrast](../../../../../../photoresist-contrast.md) alone.

The higher-contrast material distinguishes bright and dark regions even when [diffraction](../../../../../../diffraction.md) has reduced their intensity modulation. The lower-contrast material requires a much stronger aerial-image modulation and loses small features earlier. Finite resist thickness, standing waves, acid [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion.md), development conditions and photon statistics can limit actual performance beyond this threshold model.
