This is a **[multi-walled carbon nanotube](../../../../../../multi-walled-carbon-nanotube.md)** with a hollow interior and approximately concentric graphitic shells running along its axis. The walls show many resolved (002) fringes: a relatively straight section supports an approximate count of **16 shells**, with some uncertainty from contrast and overlapping/terminated fringes. The tube is not a uniform straight cylinder: the inner channel narrows and changes direction, walls kink and curve, and some inner fringes terminate or join a partly closed internal region. The rough outer boundary contains disordered carbon or incomplete outer layers. The high-contrast material below the tube should not simply be counted as additional concentric shells.

Bending, incomplete walls and changes of atomic bonding scatter [charge carriers](../../../../../../charge-carrier.md) and can mix shells, reducing the [mean free path](../../../../../../mean-free-path.md) and causing extra resistance or localization. Covalent intershell links can also increase transfer between shells. Nonhexagonal rings or vacancies are plausible atomic causes, but this image does not resolve a unique ring inventory or prove a specific [Stone-Wales defect](../../../../../../stone-wales-defect.md). Different shell chiralities can intrinsically give different metallic or semiconducting behaviour; a (002) image alone does not assign those chiralities.
