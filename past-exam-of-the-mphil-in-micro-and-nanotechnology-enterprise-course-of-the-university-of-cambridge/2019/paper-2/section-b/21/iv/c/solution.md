<h1 id="section-b/21/iv/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use the one-way density per unit length $g_+/L=2/(h|v|)$. The electron-flux magnitude in an interval is charge times velocity times line density:

$$
dI^+=e|v|\frac{2}{h|v|}f(\epsilon,\mu-eV)\,d\epsilon=\boxed{\frac{2e}{h}f(\epsilon,\mu-eV)\,d\epsilon}.
$$

If $g$ is instead defined for a finite length $L$, the traversal frequency is $|v|/L$; the same cancellation follows. The printed $I=Nev$ therefore requires $N$ to be a line density, not an unscaled total particle number.

Subtract the opposite reservoir's incoming flux. With the stated [electron](../../../../../../../electron.md) [electrochemical potentials](../../../../../../../electrochemical-potential.md), conventional positive current is opposite to electron-number flux, so

$$
I=\frac{2e}{h}\sum_i\int_{E_i}^\infty\left[f(\epsilon,\mu)-f(\epsilon,\mu-eV)\right]d\epsilon.
$$

At low [temperature](../../../../../../../temperature-split.md) and small bias, each fully transmitted occupied spin-degenerate subband contributes $2e^2/h$. Hence **$\boxed{G=N_b\,2e^2/h}$**. More generally, the [Landauer formula](../../../../../../../landauer-formula.md) weights each mode by its [transmission probability](../../../../../../../transmission-probability.md); imperfect transmission removes exact integer plateaux.
