↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**n-type doping** adds donor impurities with weakly bound [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) near the conduction-band edge; ionization supplies conduction [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) and leaves positive donor ions. **p-type doping** introduces acceptors near the valence-band edge, producing [holes](../../../../../../electron-hole.md) and negatively charged ionized acceptors. Silicon and germanium commonly use group-V donors and group-III acceptors; dopant behaviour in a compound [semiconductor](../../../../../../semiconductor-split.md) depends on its lattice site.

Doping sets carrier concentration, [Fermi level](../../../../../../fermi-level.md) and resistivity. Spatially patterned donor and acceptor regions form [p-n junctions](../../../../../../p-n-junction.md), transistor source/drain regions and electrical contacts; remote doping can supply carriers to a cleaner conducting layer. Compensation and incomplete ionization mean [carrier density](../../../../../../carrier-density.md) need not equal the total impurity concentration.
