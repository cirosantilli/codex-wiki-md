<h1 id="section-b/23/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For ideal spherical [micelles](../../../../../../micelle.md), $v/a_0=r/3$ and $r\leq\ell_c$, giving $P\leq1/3$. For a long cylindrical [micelle](../../../../../../micelle.md), $v/a_0=r/2$, so cylinders can accommodate larger packing parameters while keeping $r\leq\ell_c$: **roughly $1/3<P\lesssim1/2$ favours cylinders**. Relatively small head area or greater tail volume reduces positive curvature.

For a planar bilayer leaflet of hydrophobic thickness $\ell$, $v/a_0\simeq\ell$, with $\ell\leq\ell_c$. Thus **$1/2\lesssim P\lesssim1$ favours bilayer/vesicle structures**, with nearly planar bilayers toward $P\simeq1$. The two leaflets put hydrophobic tails together and hydrophilic heads toward water. For $P>1$, inverse structures become possible. These bounds are approximate packing guides; chain stretching, head interactions and aggregate [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) determine actual phase boundaries.
