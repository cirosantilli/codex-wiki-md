<h1 id="section-a-ii/15/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A physical bound-state [wave function](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) must be single-valued, finite, square-integrable and normalizable. Across a finite step in a regular potential, both $\psi$ and its first derivative are continuous when the particle [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) is the same on either side; integrate the [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) across an infinitesimal interval to obtain the derivative condition. The [second derivative](../../../../../../second-derivative.md) need not be continuous.

**An infinite hard wall is an exception to derivative matching**: the interior [wave function](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) vanishes at the boundary, but its derivative need not equal the exterior zero derivative. A delta-function potential also permits a prescribed derivative jump, and an effective-mass interface requires the appropriate mass-weighted [matching condition](../../../../../../asymptotic-matching-condition.md). Thus ordinary finite-step conditions should not be asserted without qualification for every singular potential.
