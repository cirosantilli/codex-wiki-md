<h1 id="section-a-ii/16/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

[Spin-valve read-head noise](../../../../../../spin-valve-read-head-noise.md) limits the field-to-electrical response: sensitivity is a response relative to noise, not merely a large total resistance. A small free-layer volume gives larger thermally driven angular fluctuations and can lose magnetic stability when its [anisotropy](../../../../../../anisotropy.md) barrier is insufficient compared with $k_BT$. Edge damage, stray coupling to the reference layer, nonuniform domains and Barkhausen jumps cause [hysteresis](../../../../../../hysteresis.md) and noise. [Johnson noise](../../../../../../thermal-electrical-noise.md) and low-frequency resistance noise compete with a limited GMR signal; raising sense current is restricted by heating and current-induced disturbance.

One useful design combines a **single-domain, gently biased free layer with a flux-compensated synthetic-antiferromagnetic reference**. This reduces reference stray fields, suppresses unstable domain switching and holds the free layer near a linear [operating point](../../../../../../operating-point.md). The active sensing width can remain small while magnetic and electrical design preserve adequate stability and signal. An alternative is a [magnetic tunnel junction](../../../../../../magnetic-tunnel-junction.md) with an optimized barrier, giving a larger magnetoresistance signal; its resistance-area product, [shot noise](../../../../../../shot-noise.md) and barrier reliability still require optimization. Shrinking every magnetic dimension indefinitely is not a sensitivity improvement.
