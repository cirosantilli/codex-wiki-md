<h1 id="section-b/9/vii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vii](../vii.md)

**Taking the printed reference to part(iii) literally**, measure an equilibrium [water](../../../../../../water.md) [contact angle](../../../../../../contact-angle.md) with a calibrated sessile-drop goniometer on a smooth homogeneous surface. With independently constrained interfacial tensions, test $\gamma_{SV}-\gamma_{SL}=\gamma_{LV}\cos\theta_C$. Advancing and receding measurements reveal pinning and hysteresis; [contact angle](../../../../../../contact-angle.md) alone cannot independently determine both unknown solid-interface energies.

If the intended reference was instead the polymer-size prediction in the preceding part, use [static light scattering](../../../../../../static-light-scattering.md) or [small-angle neutron scattering](../../../../../../small-angle-neutron-scattering.md) at both temperatures on appropriately dilute solutions. At small scattering wave number $q$, the [Guinier approximation](../../../../../../guinier-approximation.md)

$$
I(q)\simeq I(0)\exp(-q^2R_g^2/3)
$$

gives $R_g$ from the slope of $\ln I$ against $q^2$. Control concentration, aggregation and scattering contrast, and compare the extracted radii. Dynamic light scattering normally measures a hydrodynamic size rather than directly the same [radius of gyration](../../../../../../radius-of-gyration.md). Answering both readings avoids silently correcting the printed cross-reference.
