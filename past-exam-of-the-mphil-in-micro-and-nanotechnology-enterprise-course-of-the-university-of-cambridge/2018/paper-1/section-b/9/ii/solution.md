↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**The [contact angle](../../../../../../contact-angle.md) is measured through the liquid between the solid surface and the tangent to the liquid–vapor interface at the [contact line](../../../../../../contact-line.md).** The right panel of the wetting diagram labels that angle and the three interfacial tensions.

At $0^\circ$, the ideal macroscopic limit is [complete wetting](../../../../../../complete-wetting.md): a film spreads rather than remaining a finite-angle cap. Angles between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ are conventionally called hydrophilic for [water](../../../../../../water.md); the drop wets the surface relatively well. At $90^\circ$ the tangent is vertical. Between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$ the [water](../../../../../../water.md) drop is conventionally hydrophobic, with increasingly weak wetting. The exact $180^\circ$ limit represents vanishing macroscopic contact in an ideal model, not a routine finite-area sessile drop on a real solid.

The categories describe [water](../../../../../../water.md) under specified conditions. Roughness, contamination and contact-line pinning can make advancing and receding angles differ from each other and from the ideal equilibrium angle.
