<h1 id="section-b/9/vi-second-occurrence/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi (second occurrence)](../vi-second-occurrence.md)

This is the second printed “(vi)”, concerning solvent [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). **The coil is expected to expand, so its [radius of gyration](../../../../../../radius-of-gyration.md) increases.** For the usual upper-critical-solution-temperature behavior of polystyrene in cyclohexane, heating above the stated theta condition improves [solvent quality](../../../../../../solvent-quality.md). Effective repulsion between segments favors more expanded conformations than the ideal coil.

In a [Flory–Huggins solution theory](../../../../../../flory-huggins-solution-theory.md) description, the theta condition corresponds approximately to interaction parameter $\chi=1/2$, whereas a [good solvent](../../../../../../good-solvent.md) has a positive effective [excluded volume](../../../../../../excluded-volume.md). The asymptotic size dependence crosses toward $R_g\propto N^\nu$ with $\nu$ near $0.59$ for a flexible chain in three dimensions. These scaling ideas establish the expected direction, not a numerical expansion factor for a ten-degree [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) change. Its value would require measured solvent-interaction data or a calibrated crossover model.
