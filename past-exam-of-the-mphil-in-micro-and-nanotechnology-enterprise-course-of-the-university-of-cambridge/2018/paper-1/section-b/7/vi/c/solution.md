<h1 id="section-b/7/vi/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**The measured potential is a spatially weighted electrostatic signal, not an infinitesimal-point [voltage](../../../../../../../voltage.md).** The apex, cone and [cantilever](../../../../../../../cantilever.md) all contribute capacitance; increasing lift height weakens the signal and broadens its lateral averaging. Small pits can consequently show reduced or distorted contrast.

Imperfect topographic tracking changes the true separation on the lift pass, producing crosstalk correlated with pit geometry. Tip contamination or work-function drift changes the reference. Adsorbed [water](../../../../../../../water.md), mobile charges, illumination, atmosphere and surface states can change the specimen during measurement; [semiconductor](../../../../../../../semiconductor-split.md) band bending can also depend on the applied electrical excitation.

There is a tradeoff between small lift height and suppressing short-range/topographic interference. Repeat at several heights, check trace/retrace and an electrical reference, and compare with independently measured electrical response. A stable potential contrast is evidence of a local electrostatic difference, not by itself proof of a particular [dislocation](../../../../../../../dislocation.md)'s conductivity.
