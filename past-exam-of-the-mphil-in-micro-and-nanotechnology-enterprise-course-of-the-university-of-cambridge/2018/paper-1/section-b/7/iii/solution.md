<h1 id="section-b/7/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**[Conductive atomic force microscopy](../../../../../../conductive-atomic-force-microscopy.md) can map current near the pits while recording topography.** Use a conducting tip, a suitable counter-contact to the film and an imposed bias, then compare current maps and local current–[voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) curves near and away from dislocations. [Spreading resistance microscopy](../../../../../../spreading-resistance-microscopy.md) is another useful electrical mode when its contact conditions and calibration are suitable.

Current depends on tip contact area, contact resistance, surface oxide, bias and local carrier barriers as well as the material's conductivity. Compare regions under controlled [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) and bias, check repeated scans for damage and distinguish a geometrical contact artifact from an electrical change. A surface-potential image can complement these measurements, but it does not by itself measure conductivity.
