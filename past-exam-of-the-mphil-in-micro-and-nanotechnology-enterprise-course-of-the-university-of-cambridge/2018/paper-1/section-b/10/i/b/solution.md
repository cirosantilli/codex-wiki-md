↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**The useful bandgap depends on the illumination spectrum, not only on whether the device is indoors or outdoors.** The stipulated $1.45\,\mathrm{eV}$ gap gives a long-wavelength absorption threshold near $855\,\mathrm{nm}$; $1.7\,\mathrm{eV}$ gives about $729\,\mathrm{nm}$ using $\lambda_g\simeq1240/E_g$ in nanometers.

Outdoor sunlight contains substantial visible and near-infrared [power](../../../../../../../power.md), so cadmium telluride can use a broader portion of that spectrum while retaining a useful output [voltage](../../../../../../../voltage.md). Its direct-gap absorption permits thin absorbers and large-area thin-film manufacture. Challenges include contacts and interfaces, collection and [recombination](../../../../../../../recombination-cosmology.md), material supply and managing cadmium-containing material through fabrication and end-of-life handling.

Many indoor lamps concentrate their emission in the visible and provide little near-infrared [power](../../../../../../../power.md). A wider-gap absorber can then trade fewer lost useful photons for a higher attainable [voltage](../../../../../../../voltage.md) and a smaller thermal dark-current penalty. Hydrogenated amorphous [silicon](../../../../../../../silicon.md) has strong visible absorption, permits low-temperature large-area deposition and can suit low-power indoor devices. Disorder and defects restrict transport, and light-induced degradation and [passivation](../../../../../../../passivation.md) stability must be controlled. Its larger gap forfeits more of the outdoor red/near-infrared spectrum.

These are suitability arguments, not guaranteed efficiencies. Indoor lamp spectra differ, and shunt leakage and [recombination](../../../../../../../recombination-cosmology.md) become particularly important at low light. Bandgap alone does not establish which finished device produces more [power](../../../../../../../power.md) in every room.
