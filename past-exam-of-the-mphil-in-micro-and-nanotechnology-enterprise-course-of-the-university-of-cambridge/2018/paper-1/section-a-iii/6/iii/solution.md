<h1 id="section-a-iii/6/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At fixed [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) difference and constant internal resistance,

$$
P_L=I^2R_L=\frac{V_{\rm oc}^2R_L}{(R+R_L)^2}.
$$

Differentiation gives

$$
\frac{dP_L}{dR_L}=\frac{V_{\rm oc}^2(R-R_L)}{(R+R_L)^3}.
$$

It is positive below $R_L=R$ and negative above it, while [power](../../../../../../power.md) vanishes for an open or shorted load. Therefore the [maximum power transfer theorem](../../../../../../maximum-power-transfer-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{R_L=R,\qquad P_{L,\max}=\frac{V_{\rm oc}^2}{4R}
=\frac{S_{\rm eff}^2\Delta T^2}{4R}.}
$$

At this point $I=V_{\rm oc}/(2R)$ and half the electromotive [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) drops across the load. Maximum electrical [power](../../../../../../power.md) is not generally maximum thermal-conversion efficiency. If changing current also changes the [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) difference because the thermal source has finite impedance, the fixed-$\Delta T$ optimization must be reconsidered.
