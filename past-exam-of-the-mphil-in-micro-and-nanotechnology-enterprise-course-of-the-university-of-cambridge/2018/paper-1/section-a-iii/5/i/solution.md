<h1 id="section-a-iii/5/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In [contact-mode atomic force microscopy](../../../../../../contact-mode-atomic-force-microscopy.md), **a sharp tip on a compliant [cantilever](../../../../../../cantilever.md) remains in contact with the surface while a feedback loop controls its normal deflection**. A laser reflected from the [cantilever](../../../../../../cantilever.md) onto a position-sensitive detector converts bending into an electrical error signal. With calibrated deflection $d$ and stiffness $k_c$, the normal [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) is approximately $F=k_cd$ under the stated contact convention.

A piezoelectric scanner raster-scans the sample or tip. The controller adjusts vertical position to maintain the chosen deflection setpoint; that vertical displacement is recorded as topography. The right panel of the microscopy diagram identifies tip, [cantilever](../../../../../../cantilever.md), optical deflection readout, controller and vertical actuator.

The result is a probe-accessible surface, affected by finite tip shape, sample deformation, adhesion and feedback bandwidth. Lateral friction can distort or damage a soft surface, so constant deflection is not a guarantee of unchanged specimen structure.
