<h1 id="section-a-ii/4/i/a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

**A compatible, reaction-limited [chemical vapor deposition](../../../../../../../../chemical-vapor-deposition.md) process can give nearly conformal coverage of tops, sidewalls and floor.** Gas-phase precursors reach recessed surfaces and react there; the coating is not restricted to the line of sight of a solid source. The result is a continuous film over the resist pattern, which is unfavorable for ordinary [lift-off process](../../../../../../../../lift-off-process.md).

If each wall receives the same $0.20\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ coating as a horizontal surface, the nominal $0.50\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ gap narrows to

$$
\boxed{0.50-2(0.20)=0.10\,\mathrm{\mu m}.}
$$

That numerical result is the conformal idealization. Precursor depletion or rapid surface reaction can instead give poorer bottom coverage or premature closure near the mouth. Actual silver chemistry and deposition [temperature](../../../../../../../../temperature-split.md) must also be compatible with the resist; a process that decomposes the resist does not preserve the illustrated mask.
