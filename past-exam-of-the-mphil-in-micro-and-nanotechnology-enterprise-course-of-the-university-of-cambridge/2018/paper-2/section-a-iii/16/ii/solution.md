<h1 id="section-a-iii/16/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The dimensionally meaningful small-potential condition is **$|e\Phi|\ll k_BT$**, not a comparison of volts directly with [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md). Expanding $\sinh u=u+O(u^3)$ gives the physical linearized equation

$$
\Phi''=\kappa_D^2\Phi,\qquad
\boxed{\lambda_D=\kappa_D^{-1}=
\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_0\epsilon_r k_BT}{2e^2n_0}}}.
$$

For a single plane with bulk [boundary condition](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) $\Phi(\infty)=0$, $\Phi(x)=\Phi_0e^{-x/\lambda_D}$. At fixed [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) and [permittivity](../../../../../../permittivity-split.md), **$\lambda_D\propto n_0^{-1/2}$**: the curve decreases with increasing density, diverging in the ideal zero-salt limit.

Literal expansion of the printed minus-sign equation instead gives $\Phi''=-\kappa_D^2\Phi$, with sine/cosine solutions. A nonzero such solution cannot decay to zero at infinity. That literal equation does not describe a [Debye length](../../../../../../debye-length.md). The figure and following ion profiles use the physically derived plus-sign equation, with this correction explicit.
