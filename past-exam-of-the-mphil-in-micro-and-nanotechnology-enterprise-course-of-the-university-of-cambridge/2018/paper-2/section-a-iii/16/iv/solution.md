<h1 id="section-a-iii/16/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For large positive dimensionless surface potential $u_0=e\Phi_0/(k_BT)\gtrsim1$, use the nonlinear [Boltzmann distribution](../../../../../../boltzmann-distribution.md) rather than their linear expansion:

$$
n_{\rm co}=n_0e^{-u(x)},\qquad n_{\rm counter}=n_0e^{u(x)}.
$$

The [counterions](../../../../../../counterion.md) become strongly enriched near the wall and [co-ions](../../../../../../co-ion.md) strongly depleted. A planar mean-field solution is

$$
u(x)=4\operatorname{artanh}\!\left[\tanh(u_0/4)e^{-x/\lambda_D}\right].
$$

It approaches the linear screening form far from the wall. **Nonlinearity preserves positive densities and produces a much stronger near-wall asymmetry**; an extrapolated linear [co-ion](../../../../../../co-ion.md) profile could incorrectly become negative. The total local ion density is $2n_0\cosh u$, no longer constant.

The original figure compares weak- and strong-potential profiles. At very large local concentrations, finite ion size, specific adsorption and ion correlations limit this ideal point-ion mean-field model; it does not predict unlimited physically realizable [counterion](../../../../../../counterion.md) packing.
