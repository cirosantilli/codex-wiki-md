<h1 id="section-a-iii/16/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $\Phi(x)$ be the [electrostatic potential](../../../../../../electric-potential.md) in volts, relative to the bulk value, and $x$ the distance normal to the surface. The positive quantity $e$ is the elementary charge; the monovalent ions carry $\pm e$. The bulk number density $n_0$ refers to each ionic species in a symmetric electrolyte, so the total bulk ion density is $2n_0$. $\epsilon_0$ is vacuum [permittivity](../../../../../../permittivity-split.md), $\epsilon_r$ relative [permittivity](../../../../../../permittivity-split.md), $k_B$ the Boltzmann constant and $T$ absolute [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). The hyperbolic sine acts on the dimensionless potential $u=e\Phi/(k_BT)$.

**The printed sign is inconsistent with physical Poisson–Boltzmann screening under these usual definitions.** Indeed,

$$
n_\pm=n_0e^{\mp u},\qquad
\rho=e(n_+-n_-)=-2en_0\sinh u,
$$

and [Poisson's equation](../../../../../../poisson-s-equation.md) gives $\Phi''=-\rho/(\epsilon_0\epsilon_r)=+2en_0\sinh u/(\epsilon_0\epsilon_r)$, with a plus sign. The literal printed equation and the physical screened solution must therefore be distinguished.
