<h1 id="section-a-ii/13/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At greater concentration, the mean separation falls and the independent-moment approximation can fail. The [magnetic dipole interaction](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-interaction.md) has characteristic scale

$$
E_{\rm dd}\sim\frac{\mu_0\mu^2}{4\pi r^3},
$$

with sign and angular dependence set by moment orientation. Correlated clusters, chains or frustrated arrangements change local fields and relaxation barriers.

A plausible example is a progressively less reversible loop with larger [remanence](../../../../../../remanent-magnetization.md)/[magnetic coercivity](../../../../../../magnetic-coercivity.md) when interactions slow collective relaxation; the figure illustrates that case. **The trend is not universal:** some arrangements reduce susceptibility or suppress [remanence](../../../../../../remanent-magnetization.md), and aggregation can introduce a different response from a uniform concentrated dispersion. Neither loop area nor [magnetic coercivity](../../../../../../magnetic-coercivity.md) must vary monotonically with concentration.

At the actual drive [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md), the dissipated [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) per cycle per unit [volume](../../../../../../volume.md) is proportional to $\mu_0\oint H\,dM$. Measure this dynamic loop rather than inferring dissipation from an unrelated slow sweep. Compare equal magnetic-material amounts, inspect dispersion state, and vary [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) to separate concentration-driven interactions from simple signal scaling. No biological efficacy is inferred.
