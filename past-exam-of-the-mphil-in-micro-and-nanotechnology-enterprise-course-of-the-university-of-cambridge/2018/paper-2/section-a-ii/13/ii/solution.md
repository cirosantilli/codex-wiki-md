<h1 id="section-a-ii/13/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Thermally activated moment reversal, or [Néel relaxation](../../../../../../neel-relaxation.md), often has an activated timescale

$$
\tau_N\simeq\tau_0e^{E_a/(k_BT)}.
$$

Lowering [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) increases $\tau_N$. When it becomes comparable to the observation time $t_m$, the ensemble crosses an operational [magnetic blocking temperature](../../../../../../magnetic-blocking-temperature.md),

$$
T_B\simeq\frac{E_a}{k_B\ln(t_m/\tau_0)}.
$$

Above this blocking crossover, an equilibrated ensemble remains reversible; its low-field susceptibility can increase as [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) falls. Below it, moments cannot fully relax during the field sweep, so **[magnetic coercivity](../../../../../../magnetic-coercivity.md) and [remanence](../../../../../../remanent-magnetization.md) can emerge and the loop opens**. A distribution of particle [volumes](../../../../../../volume.md)/anisotropies broadens the crossover.

This is a timescale-dependent blocking effect, not necessarily a thermodynamic phase transition. Rotation of whole particles in a fluid can supply an additional relaxation channel, and the particle orientation distribution affects the observed loop. Compare sweep rates and [temperatures](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) before interpreting one curve as an intrinsic static material law.
