<h1 id="section-a-ii/13/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For the nonbiology fabrication objective, **reduce unwanted dipolar coupling while preserving a useful single-particle dynamic response**. Lithographically reduce magnetic [volume](../../../../../../volume.md) or adjust aspect ratio to control $\mu\simeq M_sV$ and [magnetic anisotropy](../../../../../../magnetic-anisotropy.md). Reducing $V$ reduces the characteristic pair interaction approximately as $V^2/r^3$, but also changes $E_a$ and relaxation time.

A nonmagnetic spacer coating or stable surface layer can maintain minimum center-to-center separation after release and reduce aggregation. At a fixed magnetic [volume](../../../../../../volume.md) fraction this geometric constraint has a real packing cost; separation cannot be increased without limit while claiming unchanged loading.

Tune particle [volume](../../../../../../volume.md), [magnetic anisotropy](../../../../../../magnetic-anisotropy.md) and spacing together, then measure dynamic susceptibility/loop area at the specified [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) and [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md). In a simple single-relaxation model, useful phase lag occurs near $\omega\tau\sim1$; making particles so small that reversal is essentially instantaneous can reduce dissipative response. **Clinical treatment optimization remains user-deferred**; these are materials/fabrication controls, not a treatment recommendation.
