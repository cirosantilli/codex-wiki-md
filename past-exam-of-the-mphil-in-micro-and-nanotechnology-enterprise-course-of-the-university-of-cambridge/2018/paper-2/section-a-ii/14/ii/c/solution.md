<h1 id="section-a-ii/14/ii/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Each [energy](../../../../../../../energy-split.md) component acquires its own phase:

$$
\psi(x,t)=\frac1{\sqrt S}\left[
0.2e^{-i\epsilon t/\hbar}\psi_1(x)+
0.3e^{-4i\epsilon t/\hbar}\psi_2(x)+
0.4e^{-9i\epsilon t/\hbar}\psi_3(x)+
0.843e^{-16i\epsilon t/\hbar}\psi_4(x)\right].
$$

The $1/\sqrt S$ factor normalizes the printed rounded input; it can be omitted if the coefficients are regarded as an already normalized rounded representation.

**The [energy](../../../../../../../energy-split.md) probabilities and $\langle E\rangle$ remain constant** under this time-independent [hamiltonian](../../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) because phase factors have unit modulus. [position](../../../../../../../position.md) probabilities can nevertheless vary: interference between different [energy](../../../../../../../energy-split.md) components generally changes with their relative phases. A stationary [energy](../../../../../../../energy-split.md) distribution is not the same thing as a stationary spatial [probability distribution](../../../../../../../probability-distribution-split.md).
