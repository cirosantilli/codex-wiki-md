<h1 id="section-a-ii/14/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a nonrelativistic [electron](../../../../../../electron.md),

$$
\lambda=\frac hp=\frac h{\sqrt{2m_eE}},\qquad
E=20(1.6\times10^{-19})=3.2\times10^{-18}\ {\rm J}.
$$

Using the supplied constants gives

$$
\boxed{\lambda\simeq2.75\times10^{-10}\ {\rm m}=0.275\ {\rm nm}.}
$$

This is comparable with atomic lattice spacings. A suitable crystalline surface or sufficiently thin crystal can therefore diffract the [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) beam; the reciprocal-lattice geometry and scattering conditions determine the actual angles. A grating or slit can also diffract matter waves if its scale and coherence conditions are suitable. The [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is tiny compared with the [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) rest [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md), so relativistic corrections are negligible at the stated precision.
