<h1 id="section-b/21a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write $C_4=\alpha Q^2/[2(4\pi\epsilon_0\epsilon_r)^2]$. Differentiating the stipulated potential gives

$$
\boxed{F_r=-\frac{du}{dr}=-\frac{4C_4}{r^5}
=-\frac{2\alpha Q^2}{(4\pi\epsilon_0\epsilon_r)^2r^5}}.
$$

For positive $\alpha$, the [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) points toward smaller separation for either sign of $Q$.

Its physical origin is **[charge-induced dipole attraction](../../../../../../charge-induced-dipole-attraction.md)**, rather than [London dispersion forces](../../../../../../london-dispersion-force.md). A point-charge field induces $\mathbf p=\alpha\mathbf E$ and the [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) cost of inducing that moment gives $u=-\alpha E^2/2$. The charge changes the [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md); it does not, within this linear model, change the intrinsic coefficient $\alpha$. For a conducting sphere in a uniform medium, $\alpha=4\pi\epsilon_0\epsilon_r a^3$; other dielectric contrasts give another [electric polarizability](../../../../../../electric-polarizability.md).

There is a distance inconsistency in the stated model: the point-charge field uses center-to-center separation $R$, whereas the question calls $r$ a surface gap. For a point ion and sphere, **$R=a+r$**. A controlled far-field dipole model therefore uses $u\simeq-C_4/(a+r)^4$, with $R\gg a$. The boxed derivative is the literal result for the given mathematical $u(r)$; it is not a justification for extending that dipole formula to contact.
