↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $\alpha>0$, the given model predicts **attraction proportional to $r^{-5}$ in [force](../../../../../../force-split.md)**, independent of the ion's charge sign; the [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) is proportional to $-r^{-4}$ and vanishes at large separation. A larger [electric polarizability](../../../../../../electric-polarizability.md) gives a stronger interaction.

This is induction by a charged object. Ordinary nonretarded London interaction between two small neutral polarizable objects instead has an $R^{-6}$ [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) dependence; geometry-integrated colloidal dispersion laws can have other powers. Calling the specified $r^{-4}$ law a generic dispersion [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) would conceal its charge-dependent origin.

Attraction can favor association or adsorption. A stable suspension normally requires an opposing free-energy barrier, such as double-layer or [steric repulsion](../../../../../../steric-repulsion.md), relative to [Brownian motion](../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md). Salt, surface coatings and solvent quality alter that balance. However, one ion–particle pair potential alone does not establish the phase behavior or aggregation rate of a many-particle suspension; concentrations, diffusion, surface chemistry and the remaining interactions also matter.
