<h1 id="section-b/21a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The literal $u(r)=-C_4/r^4$ curve is monotone increasing from $-\infty$ at $r\to0^+$ toward zero from below as $r\to\infty$:

$$
u'(r)=4C_4/r^5>0.
$$

**Taken literally at a zero gap, it predicts an unbounded collapse [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) and [force](../../../../../../force-split.md).** That extrapolation is outside the far-field dipole approximation and cannot describe actual touching matter.

Finite atomic/ionic dimensions, electronic overlap and [Pauli repulsion](../../../../../../exchange-repulsion.md) supply short-distance constraints. Solvent and hydration layers, steric coatings and surface chemistry can add further contributions. An illustrative regularized model is $u_{\rm toy}=A/r^{12}-C_4/r^4$ with $A>0$; it has a finite minimum at $r=(3A/C_4)^{1/8}$ and becomes repulsive at shorter distances. This is a schematic regularization, not a derived complete ion–sphere contact law.

Separately, correcting the center-distance convention to $R=a+r$ already removes this particular $r=0$ divergence from the far-field expression, but does not make a dipole approximation accurate at contact. Near-field multipoles and microscopic repulsion still require a better model. Calibrated [colloidal probe technique](../../../../../../colloidal-probe-technique.md) measurements can test where a long-range [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) model ceases to agree with actual approach curves.[1991, pp.239–241, sphere–plane force measurements and short-range deviations](../../../../../../direct-measurement-of-colloidal-forces-using-an-atomic-force-microscope-by-william-a-ducker-and-others.md)
