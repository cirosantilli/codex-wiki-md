<h1 id="section-b/21b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In a **[material creep](../../../../../../creep-deformation.md) experiment**, apply a small step [shear stress](../../../../../../shear-stress.md) $\sigma_0H(t)$ and measure the resulting [strain](../../../../../../strain.md). The [creep compliance](../../../../../../creep-compliance.md) is $J(t)=\gamma(t)/\sigma_0$. An elastic solid reaches a bounded [strain](../../../../../../strain.md); a fluid can continue deforming.

In a **stress-relaxation experiment**, impose a small [strain](../../../../../../strain.md) step $\gamma_0H(t)$ and measure [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) while holding the deformation. The [relaxation modulus](../../../../../../relaxation-modulus.md) is $G_{\rm rel}(t)=\sigma(t)/\gamma_0$ after the loading transient. A fluid's stored [shear stress](../../../../../../shear-stress.md) can decay to zero, while an elastic solid retains a nonzero long-time contribution.

The original curves distinguish the controlled input from measured output. The [strain](../../../../../../strain.md)/[stress](../../../../../../stress.md) must be small enough for [linear response](../../../../../../linear-response-theory.md), and real finite loading rise time, instrument compliance, wall slip and [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) must be characterized. Otherwise an instrumental transient can be mistaken for a material relaxation mode.
