<h1 id="section-b/21b/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

For $\omega\tau\ll1$,

$$
G'(\omega)\simeq\frac{\eta_M^2}{G}\omega^2,\qquad
G''(\omega)\simeq(\eta_M+\eta_s)\omega,
$$

and

$$
\eta^*(\omega)=\frac{G^*}{i\omega}
=\eta_s+\frac{\eta_M}{1+i\omega\tau}
\longrightarrow\boxed{\eta_0=\eta_M+\eta_s}.
$$

Thus **the material flows at sufficiently low [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md)**: its static [shear modulus](../../../../../../shear-modulus.md) vanishes and its terminal response is viscous. The [dashpot](../../../../../../dashpot.md) in the Maxwell branch allows its [spring](../../../../../../spring.md) [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) to relax, so there is no permanent elastic path through the parallel network.

The fact that both $G'$ and $G''$ tend to zero is not evidence of a force-free material: [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) at fixed [shear rate](../../../../../../shear-rate.md) tends to $\eta_0\dot\gamma$. At higher [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) the [spring](../../../../../../spring.md) stores [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md), but a finite-frequency elastic response does not make the long-time material a solid.
