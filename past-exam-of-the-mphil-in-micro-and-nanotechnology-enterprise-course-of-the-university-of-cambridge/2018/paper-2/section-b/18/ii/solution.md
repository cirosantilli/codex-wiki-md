<h1 id="section-b/18/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Two suitable epitaxial techniques are **[metalorganic vapour-phase epitaxy](../../../../../../metalorganic-vapour-phase-epitaxy.md) and [molecular beam epitaxy](../../../../../../molecular-beam-epitaxy.md)**.

[MOVPE](../../../../../../metalorganic-vapour-phase-epitaxy.md) supplies metalorganic group-III precursors and a reactive nitrogen source in a flowing gas. Surface reactions, mass transport and selective nucleation control growth; it is well suited to conformal radial growth and relatively high throughput. [molecular beam epitaxy](../../../../../../molecular-beam-epitaxy.md) supplies molecular/atomic beams in high vacuum, with beam fluxes and shutters controlling delivery. It offers precise flux switching and in-situ surface monitoring, but flux, geometry and nitrogen activation can limit growth rate and conformality. Neither method automatically gives identical growth on all wire [crystal facets](../../../../../../crystal-facet.md).

For an overall target near **$40\ {\rm nm\,min^{-1}}=2.4\ {\rm \mu m\,h^{-1}}$**, [MOVPE](../../../../../../metalorganic-vapour-phase-epitaxy.md) is a natural choice. This is a practical selection, not a theorem that [molecular beam epitaxy](../../../../../../molecular-beam-epitaxy.md) cannot attain that rate.

Prepare the substrate/buffer and patterned SiN growth mask; grow donor-doped [gallium nitride](../../../../../../gallium-nitride.md) cores with controlled axial morphology; change conditions to favor radial deposition and incorporate indium for the thin [indium gallium nitride](../../../../../../indium-gallium-nitride.md) active shell; then overgrow acceptor-doped p-GaN without degrading the active layer. Finally expose/contact the core and shell appropriately. Monitor facet-dependent thickness and indium content, and characterize the interfaces. If the active-layer normal rate itself were $40\ {\rm nm\,min^{-1}}$, 5 nm would take 7.5 s; the stated overall rate does not establish that the alloy shell actually grows at that same rate.
