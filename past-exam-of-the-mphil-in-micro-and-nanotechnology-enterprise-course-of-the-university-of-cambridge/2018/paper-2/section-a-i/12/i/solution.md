<h1 id="section-a-i/12/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Read the displayed axes as horizontal $[001]$ and vertical $[010]$; the viewing direction is therefore consistent with $[100]$. [crystal facets](../../../../../../crystal-facet.md) edge-on in this projection have normals in the $yz$ plane. The flat upper/lower boundaries are approximately horizontal, the end face vertical, and the bevels approximately $45^\circ$ to both.

A probable low-index assignment is **upper face $(010)$, end face $(001)$ and lower-right bevel $(0\bar11)$**, with the upper-right bevel $(011)$ if it is also indexed. Thus the flat faces belong to $\{100\}$ and the diagonal faces to $\{110\}$.

For example,

$$
\cos\phi_{(010),(001)}=0,\qquad
\cos\phi_{(001),(011)}=\frac1{\sqrt2},
$$

giving $90^\circ$ and $45^\circ$ between normals. The upper $(010)$ normal and lower bevel $(0\bar11)$ normal instead make $135^\circ$; their acute unoriented plane angle is $45^\circ$. Adjacent polygon corners between a flat edge and bevel have internal angles near $135^\circ$.

**The distinction between a normal angle, an acute plane angle and an internal polygon angle avoids a supplementary-angle error.** This is a probable projected indexation, not a unique full three-dimensional [crystal facet](../../../../../../crystal-facet.md) reconstruction. The associated work uses tomography precisely to recover information absent from one projection.[2013, study scope: three-dimensional structure and composition from electron-tomography data](../../../../../../three-dimensional-elemental-mapping-at-the-atomic-scale-in-bimetallic-nanocrystals-by-bart-goris-and-others.md) The original facet-normal diagram in the growth-and-LED figure shows the $45^\circ$ relation independently of the source micrograph.
