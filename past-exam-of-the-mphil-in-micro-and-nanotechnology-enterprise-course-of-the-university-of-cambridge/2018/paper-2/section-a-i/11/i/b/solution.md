<h1 id="section-a-i/11/i/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use [floating-catalyst chemical vapor deposition](../../../../../../../floating-catalyst-chemical-vapor-deposition.md) to form a continuous suspended network. Introduce a carbon feed together with a volatile catalyst precursor into a hot gas-flow reactor. Catalyst [nanoparticles](../../../../../../../nanoparticle.md) nucleate in the gas stream; tubes grow while moving through the reaction zone and assemble into an interconnected aerogel.

Withdraw that network continuously, collect it on a rotating spindle, and consolidate or twist it into a fiber. Long tubes, suitable number density and adequate overlap allow the network to transmit the drawing [force](../../../../../../../force-split.md) without breaking into isolated clumps. The take-up rate must be matched to network production; collection is not simply a geometrically scaled version of a substrate carpet.

**The catalyst and tubes form in the flowing gas, and the product is drawn directly from a growing aerogel.** This differs both from a supported carpet and from later spinning of a separately prepared nanotube dispersion. The original demonstration combined CVD synthesis with continuous product withdrawal from the furnace.[Science304, pp.276–278, synthesis-zone spinning and rotating collection](../../../../../../../direct-spinning-of-carbon-nanotube-fibers-from-chemical-vapor-deposition-synthesis-by-ya-li-li-and-others.md)
