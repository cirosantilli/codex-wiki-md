<h1 id="section-a-i/11/i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use substrate-supported [chemical vapor deposition](../../../../../../../chemical-vapor-deposition.md). Prepare a clean [quartz](../../../../../../../quartz.md) support with a suitable supported transition-metal catalyst, often nanoscale Fe-containing particles. Catalyst loading, support chemistry and pretreatment control the active particle size and density. A heated carbon-containing gas supplies carbon to those particles, from which [carbon nanotubes](../../../../../../../carbon-nanotube.md) grow.

At sufficiently high nucleation density, neighboring tubes constrain one another's lateral motion, producing a roughly aligned [carbon nanotube forest](../../../../../../../carbon-nanotube-forest.md) normal to the support. This is collective crowding, not a requirement that [quartz](../../../../../../../quartz.md) epitaxially orient every tube. Gas delivery, catalyst lifetime and growth time set length and uniformity. Depending on catalyst–support adhesion, catalyst particles can remain near the support or move with growing tips.

**The catalyst is supported and the carpet remains attached to a substrate.** Check particle distribution and film uniformity separately from tube diameter and alignment; a dense black coating alone does not establish a nanotube forest. The original growth sketch contrasts this supported route with a floating-catalyst reactor.
