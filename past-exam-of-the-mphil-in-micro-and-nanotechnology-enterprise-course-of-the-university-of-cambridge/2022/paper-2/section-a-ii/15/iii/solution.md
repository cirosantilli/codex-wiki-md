<h1 id="section-a-ii/15/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In [current-perpendicular-to-plane magnetoresistance](../../../../../../current-perpendicular-to-plane-magnetoresistance.md), the resistance difference between parallel and antiparallel ferromagnetic layers relies on spin-dependent transmission and retention of spin information. Here, an electron traversing the stack must pass a [platinum](../../../../../../platinum.md) layer between the two Co films. Strong [spin-orbit coupling](../../../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md) and associated [spin relaxation](../../../../../../spin-relaxation.md) in Pt, together with [spin-memory loss](../../../../../../spin-memory-loss.md) at interfaces, can reduce the polarization reaching the second Co layer and weaken [giant magnetoresistance](../../../../../../giant-magnetoresistance.md). This is more relevant to the stated perpendicular current than an in-plane shunting explanation.

**First, redesign the spacer path so a low-spin-relaxation conductor such as Cu directly separates the two ferromagnets**, while retaining perpendicular anisotropy through other interfaces or a different layer sequence. Minimize unnecessary Pt traversal and improve interface sharpness. Merely thinning the Cu layer is not sufficient if it reintroduces magnetic coupling or leaves the Pt spin-relaxation bottleneck unchanged.

**Second, replace the metallic spin-transport section with a suitable thin insulating tunnel barrier**, for example an engineered MgO-based [magnetic tunnel junction](../../../../../../magnetic-tunnel-junction.md), and choose compatible magnetic electrodes. Spin-selective tunnelling can give a much larger resistance contrast, but pinholes, disorder and a poorly matched barrier defeat it. Retaining distinct switching fields and a stable antiparallel configuration remains essential in either design.
