<h1 id="section-a-ii/16/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For an in-plane parabolic band $E(k)=E_0+\hbar^2k^2/(2m^*)$, one state occupies area $(2\pi)^2/A$ in two-dimensional wave-vector space. Including spin degeneracy two and the single GaAs conduction-band valley,

$$
\frac{N(E)}{A}=2\frac{\pi k^2}{(2\pi)^2}=\frac{k^2}{2\pi}
=\frac{m^*}{\pi\hbar^2}(E-E_0).
$$

Differentiation gives the constant two-dimensional [density of states](../../../../../../density-of-states.md)

$$
\boxed{D_{2D}(E)=\frac{m^*}{\pi\hbar^2}\,\Theta(E-E_0)=\frac{4\pi m^*}{h^2}\,\Theta(E-E_0).}
$$

Using the supplied $h$ and effective mass gives

$$
\boxed{D_{2D}=1.75\times10^{36}\,\mathrm{J^{-1}m^{-2}}
=2.80\times10^{13}\,\mathrm{eV^{-1}cm^{-2}}.}
$$

This counts available one-electron states, not the number actually occupied at arbitrary temperature. Occupation requires the [Fermi-Dirac distribution](../../../../../../fermi-dirac-distribution.md); each additional subband would add another step above its own threshold.
