<h1 id="section-a-i/14/i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In [atomic layer deposition](../../../../../../../atomic-layer-deposition.md), expose the surface to a molybdenum precursor, purge unreacted precursor and volatile products, expose it to a sulfur reactant, and purge again. Each half-reaction should saturate the available reactive sites rather than continue indefinitely with dose. Repetition builds a [molybdenum disulfide](../../../../../../../molybdenum-disulfide.md) film, with nucleation, incubation and layer closure checked independently.

**Self-limited surface chemistry is not the same as depositing exactly one complete crystal monolayer per cycle.** A cycle may add only a fraction of a layer, and initially isolated islands can thicken before all bare substrate is covered. Reactions on hydroxylated [silicon dioxide](../../../../../../../silicon-dioxide.md) can also differ from those on the comparatively inert MoS$_2$ basal surface. A precursor pair must actually show saturation under the chosen conditions; simply alternating gases is insufficient evidence of ALD.

In [metalorganic chemical vapor deposition](../../../../../../../metalorganic-vapour-phase-epitaxy.md), metalorganic and sulfur-containing precursors are typically supplied simultaneously or with overlapping residence times, and film growth depends on decomposition, transport and surface reaction rates. Both methods involve chemistry, but ALD separates saturating half-reactions and purge steps. An ALD process need not use a metalorganic precursor, and pulsed CVD is not automatically ALD.
