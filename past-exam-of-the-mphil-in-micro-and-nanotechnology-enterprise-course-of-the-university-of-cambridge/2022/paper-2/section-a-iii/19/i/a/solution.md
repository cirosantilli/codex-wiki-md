<h1 id="section-a-iii/19/i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**Positive [dielectrophoresis](../../../../../../../dielectrophoresis.md) acts up the gradient of the squared RMS field, towards stronger-field regions; negative [dielectrophoresis](../../../../../../../dielectrophoresis.md) acts down that gradient, towards weaker-field regions.** The sign is the sign of $\Re\widetilde K(\omega)$.

The force is not generally tangent to an [electric field](../../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) line. Field lines show the direction of the field itself, whereas $\nabla |\mathbf E_{\rm RMS}|^2$ describes how its strength changes in space. A spatially uniform field gives zero dipole-order dielectrophoretic force even when it is large. Reversing the field each half-cycle does not reverse this time-averaged force, because the relevant intensity-like quantity is quadratic.
