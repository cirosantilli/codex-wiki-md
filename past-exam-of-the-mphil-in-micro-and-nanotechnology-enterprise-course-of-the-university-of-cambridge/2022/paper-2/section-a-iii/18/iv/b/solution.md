<h1 id="section-a-iii/18/iv/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**Neutralizing the top boundary removes its preference for A, but the substrate still prefers A.** The original integer-period constraint from two A-selective interfaces is therefore relaxed. The film can adjust its top termination without paying the same surface penalty, and thickness frustration can be reduced.

A possible morphology is an A-rich wetting layer near the substrate with lamellae turning towards a more perpendicular arrangement in the upper film, as schematically illustrated. A predominantly parallel stack is also possible if the substrate anchoring and thickness commensurability dominate. Neutrality of the top alone does not uniquely determine the orientation.

If **both** boundaries were neutral, perpendicular lamellae could expose comparable A and B areas without a selective-surface penalty and would avoid the same parallel-stack thickness constraint. That is a different boundary condition from the one given here. A correct sketch must retain the A preference at the bottom rather than silently replacing it with a second neutral interface.
