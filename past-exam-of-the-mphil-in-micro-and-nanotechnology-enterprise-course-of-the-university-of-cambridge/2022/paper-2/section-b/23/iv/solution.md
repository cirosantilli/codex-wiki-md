<h1 id="section-b/23/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For an ideal electrolyte with species number densities $n_i$, valences $z_i$, solvent permittivity $\epsilon$ and common temperature,

$$
\boxed{\lambda_D=\left(\frac{\epsilon k_BT}{e^2\sum_i n_iz_i^2}\right)^{1/2}.}
$$

Increasing salt concentration supplies more mobile charge to screen a surface perturbation, so a shorter distance is needed for the potential to decay. At fixed ion composition, permittivity and temperature, $\lambda_D\propto c^{-1/2}$.

A fivefold concentration increase therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\lambda_D'=\lambda_D/\sqrt5=0.447\lambda_D.}
$$

Equivalently, the inverse screening length grows by $\sqrt5$. Changing ion valence changes the ionic-strength weighting as well, and highly concentrated electrolytes can depart from this dilute-solution screening model.
