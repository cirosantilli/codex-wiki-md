↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**Cooling changes the catalyst state that is being measured.** Crossing Au–Ga phase boundaries can cause solidification, segregation and intermetallic formation. Solubility changes can expel Ga or precipitate additional GaAs after the intended growth interval. The droplet contact angle and interfacial shape also change as temperature and composition change.

A cooled catalyst–wire interface can consequently have a different composition, phase assemblage, geometry and even last-grown crystal segment from the active growth interface. Continued precursor supply during cooling can add another uncontrolled history. An ex situ observation of a solid catalyst does not prove that growth occurred by a solid-catalyst mechanism, and its final geometry cannot simply be substituted into a nucleation model for the liquid growth stage.

Use in situ microscopy, diffraction or spectroscopy under representative growth conditions, or compare controlled interruption and quench protocols. Quenching reduces some rearrangement but does not guarantee a perfectly frozen interface. The Au-catalysed GaAs composition experiment provides a direct example of measuring catalyst state during growth instead of inferring it from the cooled residue.[2019, motivation for and implementation of in situ catalyst analysis](../../../../../../in-situ-analysis-of-catalyst-composition-during-gold-catalyzed-gaas-nanowire-growth-by-carina-b-maliakkal-and-others.md)
