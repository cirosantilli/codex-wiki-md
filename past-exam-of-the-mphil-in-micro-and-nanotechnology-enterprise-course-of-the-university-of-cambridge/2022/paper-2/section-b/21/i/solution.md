↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

One consistent five-postulate formulation is the following; different textbooks group the same content differently.

- **State.** A pure state is a normalized vector $|\psi\rangle$ in a complex [Hilbert space](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md), with $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=1$; vectors differing only by an overall phase represent the same state. For a spinless particle in position representation, $\psi(\mathbf r)=\langle\mathbf r|\psi\rangle$ is its wavefunction.
- **Observables.** A physical observable A is represented by a [self-adjoint operator](../../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md) $\hat A$. In a Cartesian position representation, $\hat x=x$ and $\hat p_x=-i\hbar\partial_x$, with $\hbar=h/(2\pi)$.
- **Outcomes and probabilities.** Allowed ideal measurement outcomes are spectral values $a$ of $\hat A$. The [Born rule](../../../../../../born-rule.md) gives $P(a)=\langle\psi|P_a|\psi\rangle$, where $P_a$ projects onto the corresponding eigenspace; continuous spectra require the analogous spectral probability measure. The expectation is $\langle\hat A\rangle=\langle\psi|\hat A|\psi\rangle$.
- **Ideal measurement update.** Conditional on a nonzero-probability outcome $a$, an ideal projective measurement leaves $P_a|\psi\rangle/\sqrt{\langle\psi|P_a|\psi\rangle}$. For a nondegenerate result this is its eigenstate. This is the ideal projection rule, not a description of every general measurement apparatus.
- **Time evolution.** Between measurements a closed system evolves by the [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md), $i\hbar\partial_t|\psi(t)\rangle=\hat H|\psi(t)\rangle$, with [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) $\hat H$. For time-independent $\hat H$, this gives unitary evolution $e^{-i\hat Ht/\hbar}$.

The position-dependent probability density is $|\psi|^2$, and its flux is the [probability current](../../../../../../probability-current.md). These distinguish the wave amplitude from the measurable particle flow in the following scattering problem.
