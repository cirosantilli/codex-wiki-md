**[Scanning tunneling microscopy](../../../../../../../scanning-tunneling-microscopy.md)** uses electron tunnelling between a conducting tip and a sample across a narrow vacuum gap. At fixed bias, the strong distance dependence of the current enables sensitive height or electronic-structure measurements; the result depends on local electronic states as well as topography.

**A [tunnel diode](../../../../../../../tunnel-diode.md)** uses tunnelling between occupied and available states across a very thin, heavily doped semiconductor junction. The alignment of those states changes with bias and can produce a region of negative differential resistance, useful in fast switching or oscillation circuits. Both examples require a finite barrier with states available on the other side; an infinitely extended forbidden region is not an equivalent transport geometry.
