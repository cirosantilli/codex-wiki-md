<h1 id="section-a-i/1/ii/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**Use [high-resolution transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../../../high-resolution-transmission-electron-microscopy.md) with a coherent, well-aligned beam and an objective aperture admitting the direct beam and the spatial frequencies required at the interface.** Orient a crystalline region near a suitable [zone axis](../../../../../../../zone-axis.md) so that useful lattice fringes are excited; minimize drift, vibration and astigmatism. Choose defocus and aberration correction to transfer the target frequencies, rather than treating the image as a direct map of atoms at arbitrary defocus.

An aberration-corrected [scanning transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../../../scanning-transmission-electron-microscopy.md) probe with [high-angle annular dark-field imaging](../../../../../../../high-angle-annular-dark-field-imaging.md) can instead resolve an interface with more directly interpretable atomic-number contrast. A nominal $150\,\mathrm{nm}$ thickness may be too great for clean atomic-resolution interpretation: thin the interface further if necessary, finish [focused ion beam milling](../../../../../../../focused-ion-beam-milling.md) at lower ion energy to reduce damaged surface layers, and check for curtaining and implantation. The best mode depends on crystallinity and damage sensitivity; an amorphous interface calls for resolving its boundary and composition rather than inventing lattice fringes.
