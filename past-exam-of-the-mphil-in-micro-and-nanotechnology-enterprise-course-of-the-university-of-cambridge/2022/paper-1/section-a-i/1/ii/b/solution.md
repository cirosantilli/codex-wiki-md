<h1 id="section-a-i/1/ii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**Use a low-dose, dose-efficient acquisition rather than assuming that a lower accelerating voltage is always gentler.** Focus and align on a sacrificial neighbouring region, blank the beam between exposures, illuminate only when recording and use an efficient detector. A broad, low-current-density [transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../../../transmission-electron-microscopy.md) exposure can avoid a high instantaneous local dose; scanning can also work if probe current, dwell time and repeated visits are controlled.

[Radiolysis](../../../../../../../radiolysis.md) starts with [inelastic scattering](../../../../../../../inelastic-scattering.md) and ionization. For an appropriate thin specimen, increasing the electron energy can reduce the inelastic interaction probability and energy deposition per incident electron, so a relatively high voltage can help. However, increasing voltage can also activate [knock-on displacement](../../../../../../../knock-on-displacement.md). The optimum therefore depends on the material and the amount of useful information obtained per damaging event; $150\,\mathrm{nm}$ alone does not determine it. Cooling may reduce diffusion-driven chemical changes but does not eliminate primary ionization. An experimental study of nonbiological layered polymer films explicitly found that the voltage choice must balance damage with image signal.[2022, accelerating-voltage comparison and discussion of the inelastic scattering cross-section](../../../../../../../optimal-acceleration-voltage-for-near-atomic-resolution-imaging-of-layer-stacked-2d-polymer-thin-films-by-baokun-liang-and-others.md)
