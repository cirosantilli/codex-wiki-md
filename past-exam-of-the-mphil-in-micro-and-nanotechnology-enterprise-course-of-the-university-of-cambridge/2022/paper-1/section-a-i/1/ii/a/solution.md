<h1 id="section-a-i/1/ii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**Use conventional [bright-field transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../../../bright-field-transmission-electron-microscopy.md), with nearly parallel illumination, a suitably small [objective aperture](../../../../../../../objective-aperture.md) and low magnification covering the whole cross-section.** More strongly scattering material sends more intensity outside the selected central beam and therefore looks darker. Tilt crystalline layers away from strong [Bragg diffraction](../../../../../../../bragg-diffraction.md) conditions to reduce [diffraction contrast](../../../../../../../diffraction-contrast.md), and use a near-focus image so that [electron phase contrast](../../../../../../../electron-phase-contrast.md) does not dominate.

The nominally uniform $150\,\mathrm{nm}$ thickness helps separate composition from thickness variations, but does not make the contrast purely compositional: density, atomic number and multiple scattering still matter. Choose an accelerating voltage sufficient to transmit this thickness, commonly $200$–$300\,\mathrm{kV}$ for a robust inorganic film, subject to its damage limit. [High-angle annular dark-field imaging](../../../../../../../high-angle-annular-dark-field-imaging.md) in [scanning transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../../../scanning-transmission-electron-microscopy.md) is a useful alternative for approximately monotonic mass-thickness contrast, but is a different acquisition mode and still requires thickness and detector-angle control.
