<h1 id="section-a-iii/6/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**A useful aqueous [anode](../../../../../../anode.md) must deliver the desired oxidation current while limiting parasitic corrosion and hydrogen evolution.** [Thermodynamics](../../../../../../thermodynamics-split.md) sets the driving force, while [activation overpotential](../../../../../../activation-overpotential.md), surface films and mass transport control the rate.

For [zinc](../../../../../../zinc.md), the acid calculation favours dissolution with hydrogen evolution, but a substantial kinetic barrier to hydrogen evolution on a clean zinc surface can slow this loss. Impurities and catalytic inclusions can increase it. In alkaline electrolyte, zinc can be useful in batteries, but the unit-activity $\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}$ calculation is incomplete: soluble zincate and zinc oxide or hydroxide alter the relevant equilibrium. A surface film can protect against corrosion or obstruct useful discharge, depending on its conductivity and transport properties.

For [lead](../../../../../../lead.md), acid corrosion has a much smaller driving force in the stipulated model, and slow hydrogen kinetics can permit practical use. The electrolyte anion matters: a lead sulfate layer in a lead–acid system has a different role from arbitrary soluble lead salts. In alkaline media, hydroxide formation, soluble complexes and [passivation](../../../../../../passivation.md) again need consideration before drawing a practical conclusion from the free-ion potential.

**Metallic [lithium](../../../../../../lithium.md) is unsuitable as an exposed anode in an ordinary aqueous electrolyte.** Its strongly favourable reaction with water overwhelms the argument for simply relying on a hydrogen overpotential. A lithium-ion host electrode is a different material and must be judged by its own operating potential and interfacial chemistry.
