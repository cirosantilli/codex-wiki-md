<h1 id="section-a-iii/7/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For constant current and nearly constant capacitance,

$$
I=C\frac{dV}{dt},\qquad V(t)=V(0)\pm\frac{|I|}{C}t.
$$

An ideal [supercapacitor](../../../../../../supercapacitor.md) therefore has **approximately linear charge and discharge ramps**, making a triangular voltage–time trace. Series resistance adds an immediate voltage step when current starts and a step of magnitude $2|I|R_s$ when it reverses directly from $+I$ to $-I$. Leakage and voltage-dependent capacitance introduce curvature.

A secondary [battery](../../../../../../electric-battery.md) instead follows electrode chemical potentials as its state of charge changes; many chemistries have broad plateaus or sloping plateaus, with polarization separating the charge and discharge curves. This distinction is typical rather than universal: some batteries have steeply sloping profiles, while strongly potential-dependent pseudocapacitive materials can depart substantially from a triangle.
