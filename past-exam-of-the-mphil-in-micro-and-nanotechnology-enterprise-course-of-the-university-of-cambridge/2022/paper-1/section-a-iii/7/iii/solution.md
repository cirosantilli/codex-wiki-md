Define mass-specific differential [capacitance](../../../../../../capacitance.md), or [specific capacitance](../../../../../../specific-capacitance.md), by

$$
\boxed{C_{\rm sp}(V)=\frac1m\frac{dQ}{dV}},\qquad [C_{\rm sp}]=\mathrm{F\,g^{-1}}.
$$

State whether $m$ is one electrode's active mass, both electrodes' active mass or the entire device mass; these conventions are not interchangeable. An average capacitance inferred from a finite voltage interval is $\Delta Q/(m\Delta V)$ and need not equal the local derivative.

**A nearly ideal double-layer [supercapacitor](../../../../../../supercapacitor.md) has an approximately flat capacitance over its stable operating window**, so stored charge rises nearly linearly with potential. Real electrodes need not be flat: dilute-electrolyte double-layer models can give a minimum near the potential of zero charge, while concentrated electrolytes, finite ion size and pore confinement can produce bell-shaped or double-peaked curves. [Pseudocapacitance](../../../../../../pseudocapacitance.md) can add broad potential-dependent contributions near a redox transition. The sketch above therefore labels several possible behaviours instead of asserting a universal monotonic trend. Constant-potential simulations have directly found transitions between double-peaked and bell-shaped differential-capacitance curves as interfacial interactions change.[2022, constant-potential molecular-dynamics results](../../../../../../insight-into-the-camel-to-bell-transition-of-differential-capacitance-in-ionic-liquid-based-supercapacitors-by-zhongdong-gan-and-yanlei-wang.md)
