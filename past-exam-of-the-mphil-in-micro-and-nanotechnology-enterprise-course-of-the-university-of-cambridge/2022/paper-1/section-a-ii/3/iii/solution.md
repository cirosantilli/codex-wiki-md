<h1 id="section-a-ii/3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A fully clear opening requires the higher clearing threshold, not merely the onset of thinning. At $\tau=20\,\mathrm s$,

$$
I_{\rm clear}=\frac{D_{100}}{\tau}=\frac{50}{20}=2.5\,\mathrm{mW\,cm^{-2}}.
$$

The intensity curve crosses this level at about $x=0.25$ and $3.75\,\mu\mathrm m$. Therefore the clear opening is approximately

$$
\boxed{w\simeq3.75-0.25=3.5\,\mu\mathrm m.}
$$

The longer exposure widens the opening because even the relatively dim image tails now accumulate enough [exposure dose](../../../../../../exposure-dose.md) to clear the positive [photoresist](../../../../../../photoresist.md). The edge readings are approximate; interpolating a plotted curve does not support nanometre precision.
