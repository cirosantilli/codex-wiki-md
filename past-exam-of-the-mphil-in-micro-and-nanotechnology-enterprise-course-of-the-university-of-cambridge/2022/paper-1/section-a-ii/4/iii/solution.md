<h1 id="section-a-ii/4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Increasing the angular acceptance can collect higher spatial frequencies, but rays at large angles and away from the optical axis are harder to bring to the same focus. [Spherical aberration](../../../../../../spherical-aberration.md), coma, astigmatism and field curvature can degrade contrast across a large exposure field. The [depth of focus](../../../../../../depth-of-focus.md) also decreases approximately as $\lambda/\mathrm{NA}^2$, making wafer height errors more important. A larger geometrical aperture is therefore not automatically better over the whole wafer field.

**[Immersion lithography](../../../../../../immersion-lithography.md) raises $\mathrm{NA}=n\sin\theta$ by increasing the refractive index of the medium between the final lens and wafer.** A suitable UV-transparent liquid permits a larger numerical aperture without increasing the ray angle by the same factor, easing the purely geometrical demand on the lens. Lens design and focus control remain necessary; immersion does not abolish aberrations. The liquid must also be compatible with the resist and free of bubbles and contamination, and absorption at $193\,\mathrm{nm}$ must be low.
