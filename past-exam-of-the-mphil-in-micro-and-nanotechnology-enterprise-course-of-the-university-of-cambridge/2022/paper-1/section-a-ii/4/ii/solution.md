<h1 id="section-a-ii/4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The projection lens maps the mask's angular spectrum into its back focal plane: within scalar Fourier optics this is the **[Fraunhofer diffraction](../../../../../../fraunhofer-diffraction.md) or spatial-Fourier plane**. A periodic mask produces discrete diffraction orders, separated by $1/P$ in object-side spatial frequency after accounting for projection magnification. The finite [numerical aperture](../../../../../../numerical-aperture.md) bounds the transverse wave vectors admitted by the lens, so the pupil is a spatial-frequency filter.

For an ideal on-axis coherent system,

$$
\widetilde U_{\rm image}(\nu_x,\nu_y)=P(\nu_x,\nu_y)\widetilde U_{\rm mask}(\nu_x,\nu_y),
\qquad |\boldsymbol\nu|<\frac{\mathrm{NA}}{\lambda}.
$$

A second Fourier transformation forms the image, and the recorded intensity is $|U_{\rm image}|^2$. If only the zero order passes, the field is uniform and the line pattern disappears. Passing zero and first orders restores a periodic modulation; passing more orders sharpens the edges. Thus the loss of fine structure is explained by rejected diffraction orders, not by a geometrical inability to draw narrow lines. The field-transfer cutoff differs from the twice-as-large incoherent intensity-transfer cutoff.[coherent and incoherent transfer: pupil filtering and intensity formation](../../../../../../advanced-optical-imaging-incoherent-imaging-by-delft-university-of-technology.md)
