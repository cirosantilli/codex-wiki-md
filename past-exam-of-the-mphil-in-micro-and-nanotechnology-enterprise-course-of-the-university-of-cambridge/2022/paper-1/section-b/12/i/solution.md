<h1 id="section-b/12/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For one homogeneous thermoelectric material, the dimensional [thermoelectric figure of merit](../../../../../../thermoelectric-figure-of-merit.md) is

$$
\boxed{Z=\frac{S^2\sigma}{\kappa}=\frac{S^2}{\rho\kappa}},\qquad[Z]=\mathrm{K^{-1}}.
$$

The dimensionless quantity is $ZT$. The [Seebeck coefficient](../../../../../../seebeck-coefficient.md) $S$ measures the open-circuit voltage response to a temperature difference and reflects the energy asymmetry of charge-carrier transport. The [electrical conductivity](../../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md) $\sigma$ depends on carrier density and mobility and sets electrical loss; $\rho=1/\sigma$. The [thermal conductivity](../../../../../../thermal-conductivity.md) is $\kappa=\kappa_e+\kappa_l$, with contributions from carriers and [phonons](../../../../../../phonon.md), and represents unwanted heat leakage.

Increasing carrier concentration usually raises $\sigma$ while reducing $|S|$ once transport becomes strongly degenerate. The electronic part of $\kappa$ also tends to rise, approximately through the [Wiedemann-Franz law](../../../../../../wiedemann-franz-law.md) where applicable. This competition produces an intermediate optimum, commonly in **heavily doped [semiconductors](../../../../../../semiconductor-split.md)**, rather than in a very resistive insulator or an ordinary high-carrier-density metal. The optimum depends on band structure and scattering; the schematic is not a universal measured concentration curve.
