↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**Nanowires can increase $Z$ by reducing lattice heat conduction more strongly than they degrade useful electronic transport.** A wire diameter comparable to important phonon mean free paths introduces boundary scattering and reduces the lattice part of [thermal conductivity](../../../../../../thermal-conductivity.md). Interfaces, surface roughness and suitable nanostructure can suppress long-mean-free-path phonons while preserving shorter-mean-free-path carrier transport.

Electronic confinement or energy-dependent scattering can also change the [Seebeck coefficient](../../../../../../seebeck-coefficient.md) and [thermoelectric power factor](../../../../../../thermoelectric-power-factor.md), but neither improvement is automatic. Surface depletion, disorder and contact resistance may instead reduce [electrical conductivity](../../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md); a smaller wire is not necessarily better. The necessary comparison is the complete ratio $S^2\sigma/(\kappa_e+\kappa_l)$ over the operating temperature range. The experimentally established benefit of nanostructuring is especially clear when it suppresses phonon heat conduction while retaining a useful power factor.[2008, nanostructured-alloy thermal-conductivity and figure-of-merit measurements](../../../../../../high-thermoelectric-performance-of-nanostructured-bismuth-antimony-telluride-bulk-alloys-by-bed-poudel-and-others.md)
