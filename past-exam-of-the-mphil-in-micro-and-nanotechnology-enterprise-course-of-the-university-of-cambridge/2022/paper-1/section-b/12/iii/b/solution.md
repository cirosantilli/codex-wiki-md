<h1 id="section-b/12/iii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use absolute temperatures $T_H=523.15\,\mathrm K$, $T_C=323.15\,\mathrm K$ and $\overline T=423.15\,\mathrm K$. With the explicit identical-per-leg interpretation from the preceding part,

$$
\sqrt{1+Z\overline T}=\sqrt{1+0.1622}=1.0781,
$$

and the stated ideal efficiency expression gives

$$
\boxed{\eta_{\max}=\frac{200}{523.15}\frac{1.0781-1}{1.0781+323.15/523.15}=0.0176\simeq1.76\%.}
$$

With the alternative already-combined pair convention, $Z\overline T=0.6488$ instead gives **$\eta_{\max}\simeq5.71\%$**. Both lie below the [Carnot efficiency](../../../../../../../carnot-efficiency.md) $1-T_C/T_H=38.2\%$. These are constant-property ideal conversion efficiencies, neglecting contact resistance, parasitic heat paths and temperature dependence. They are not the efficiency specifically at the maximum-power load.
