<h1 id="section-a-i/14/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [gas impingement flux](../../../../../../gas-impingement-flux.md) onto a surface follows from the normal component of the [Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution](../../../../../../maxwell-boltzmann-distribution.md):

$$
J=\frac{P}{\sqrt{2\pi m k_BT}}.
$$

Here $P=0.1\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $m=18(1.66\times10^{-27})=2.99\times10^{-26}\,\mathrm{kg}$, giving

$$
J\simeq3.60\times10^{21}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}s^{-1}}.
$$

Assume a conventional [monolayer](../../../../../../monolayer.md) density $N_s=10^{19}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$ and unit [sticking coefficient](../../../../../../sticking-coefficient.md). The incident-monolayer time is

$$
t_{\mathrm{ML}}=\frac{N_s}{J}\simeq2.8\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm s.
$$

Thus **a few milliseconds** is the usual contamination-time estimate at this pressure.

The assumed molecular footprint matters: neither $N_s$ nor the [sticking coefficient](../../../../../../sticking-coefficient.md) is specified. More generally $t_{\mathrm{ML}}=N_s/(sJ)$ if the sticking probability is $s$ and [desorption](../../../../../../desorption.md) is negligible. Water can desorb rapidly from clean [graphene](../../../../../../graphene.md) at room temperature, so the incident-monolayer estimate does not establish that a stable, complete water layer actually forms in that time. Residues and defects can increase local adsorption.
