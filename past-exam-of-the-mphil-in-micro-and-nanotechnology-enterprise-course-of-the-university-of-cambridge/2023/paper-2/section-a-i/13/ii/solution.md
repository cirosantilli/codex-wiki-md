<h1 id="section-a-i/13/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $\mathbf a_1,\mathbf a_2$ be equal-length primitive translation vectors of the [graphene](../../../../../../graphene.md) lattice, separated by $60^\circ$. The [carbon nanotube chiral vector](../../../../../../carbon-nanotube-chiral-vector.md)

$$
\mathbf C_h=n\mathbf a_1+m\mathbf a_2
$$

identifies the two points joined when the sheet is rolled: $|\mathbf C_h|$ is the circumference, not the tube axis. The right-hand panel of the preceding original diagram shows $\mathbf C_h=6\mathbf a_1+6\mathbf a_2$ and $\mathbf C_h=7\mathbf a_1$.

The periodic boundary condition is $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf C_h=2\pi\ell$, with $\ell$ an integer. Applying it at either [Dirac valley](../../../../../../dirac-valley.md) gives the [nanotube zone folding](../../../../../../nanotube-zone-folding.md) criterion

$$
n-m\equiv0\pmod3
$$

for a metallic tube. Thus $(6,6)$ is an armchair metallic example, whereas $(7,0)$ is a zigzag semiconducting example. Other choices with $n-m\not\equiv0\pmod3$ also give a [semiconducting carbon nanotube](../../../../../../semiconducting-carbon-nanotube.md).

This criterion is the ideal flat-sheet approximation. Curvature can open a small gap in nominally metallic non-armchair tubes; ideal armchair tubes remain gapless in that approximation. The property is determined by the wrapping vector, not simply by calling a tube “chiral” or by its diameter alone.
