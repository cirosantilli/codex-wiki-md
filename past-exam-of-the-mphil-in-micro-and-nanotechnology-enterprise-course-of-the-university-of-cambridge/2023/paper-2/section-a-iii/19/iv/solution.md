<h1 id="section-a-iii/19/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Absorption converts part of the incident optical power into heat. Increasing laser power can therefore raise the local [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), potentially driving a thermotropic [nematic liquid crystal](../../../../../../nematic-liquid-crystal.md) through its nematic-isotropic transition. The associated loss or change of orientational order, [refractive index](../../../../../../refractive-index.md) and anchoring can alter the trapping force or destabilize the trap.

Even below that transition, thermal gradients can induce convection, thermophoretic drift and changes in viscosity or Brownian fluctuations; sufficiently strong heating can damage the particle or host. Thus increasing laser power need not simply strengthen an otherwise unchanged [optical trap](../../../../../../optical-tweezers.md).
