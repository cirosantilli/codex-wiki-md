↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [Flory interaction parameter](../../../../../../flory-interaction-parameter.md) $\chi$ measures the free-energy penalty of unlike block contacts relative to like contacts, in units of $k_BT$. The [degree of polymerization](../../../../../../degree-of-polymerization.md) $N$ counts the reference segments per chain, using the segment-volume convention associated with $\chi$. Their product, the [block copolymer segregation strength](../../../../../../block-copolymer-segregation-strength.md) $\chi N$, compares the tendency to avoid unlike contacts with the entropy cost of organizing and stretching connected chains.

At small $\chi N$, the [diblock copolymer](../../../../../../diblock-copolymer.md) is disordered. Increasing $\chi N$ can cause [microphase separation](../../../../../../microphase-separation.md): the blocks form distinct domains, but their covalent connection prevents complete macroscopic separation. Domain interfaces cost energy while chain stretching and lost configurations cost [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md); their competition selects a finite domain scale.

A common temperature law is $\chi=A/T+B$, so increasing temperature often decreases segregation when $A>0$. This is not a universal sign law for every polymer pair. For an intact, fixed chain, $N$ does not change just because temperature or solvent concentration changes.

Adding a nonselective good solvent usually dilutes unlike-block contacts and reduces an effective segregation strength; it does not chemically shorten the chain. A selective solvent additionally changes domain swelling and solvent-block interactions. One needs a ternary-mixture description, or a clearly defined effective $\chi$, rather than treating the melt's $\chi$ as a universal solvent-concentration function.[Definition of the segregation and composition variables and experimental phase behaviour](../../../../../../polyisoprene-polystyrene-diblock-copolymer-phase-diagram-near-the-order-disorder-transition-by-ashish-k-khandpur-and-others.md)
