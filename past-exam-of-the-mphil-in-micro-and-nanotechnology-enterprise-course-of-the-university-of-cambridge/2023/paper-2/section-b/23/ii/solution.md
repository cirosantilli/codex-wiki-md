<h1 id="section-b/23/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [sedimentation scale height](../../../../../../sedimentation-scale-height.md) simplifies because the same drag coefficient controls both drift and diffusion:

$$
z_0=\frac D{v_s}
=\frac{k_BT}{m_bg}
=\frac{3k_BT}{4\pi a^3(\rho_p-\rho_f)g}.
$$

Applying the given criterion $a\leq z_0$ gives

$$
a^4\leq\frac{3k_BT}{4\pi(\rho_p-\rho_f)g},
\qquad
a_{\max}=
\left[\frac{3k_BT}{4\pi(\rho_p-\rho_f)g}\right]^{1/4}.
$$

Equivalently, the gravitational [Péclet number](../../../../../../peclet-number.md) on the particle-radius scale is $a/z_0=m_bga/(k_BT)$ and must be at most one.

At $300\,\mathrm K$ the limiting **radius** is $a_{\max}\simeq8.42\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm m=0.84\,\mu\mathrm m$, corresponding to diameter approximately $1.68\,\mu\mathrm m$. Viscosity cancels from this equilibrium criterion, although it affects the time to approach equilibrium.

For the $100\,\mathrm{nm}$ radius particle in part (i), $D\simeq2.20\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$ and $z_0\simeq0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$, about $5.0\times10^3$ particle radii. It is therefore firmly diffusion-dominated on its own size scale. This does not mean uniform concentration over an arbitrarily tall vessel: the equilibrium density still decays on the finite scale $z_0$. The supplied size criterion is an approximate gravity-versus-diffusion criterion, not a universal definition of every stable [colloid](../../../../../../colloid.md).
