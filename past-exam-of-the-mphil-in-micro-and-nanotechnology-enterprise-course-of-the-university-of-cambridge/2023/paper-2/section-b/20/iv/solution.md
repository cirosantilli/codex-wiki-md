<h1 id="section-b/20/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

In catalytic [chemical vapor deposition](../../../../../../chemical-vapor-deposition.md), a metal nanoparticle activates a carbon-containing feedstock and supplies mobile carbon to the growing rim. Carbon can diffuse over the particle surface and, depending on the catalyst state, through its bulk. Once a curved carbon cap nucleates, continued incorporation extends the tube. Strong catalyst-support adhesion favors base growth; weaker adhesion can allow the particle to lift with the tube tip. A liquid catalyst is not universally required.

Particle size constrains tube diameter, but semiconducting character requires a particular wrapping family: $n-m\not\equiv0\pmod3$. Merely making particles smaller does not select that congruence class. Routes toward predominantly [semiconducting carbon nanotubes](../../../../../../semiconducting-carbon-nanotube.md) include controlling catalyst composition, exposed facets and size distribution to bias cap/rim nucleation and growth, or adjusting carbon chemical potential and growth/etching conditions so that metallic tubes are less likely to survive.

These are mechanistic strategies rather than a universal recipe. Experiments with catalyst conditioning found that high semiconducting selectivity depended sensitively on oxidizing/reducing gas conditions and came with a strong yield penalty, motivating a catalyst-deactivation model.[Catalyst conditioning, gas composition and selectivity-yield tradeoff](../../../../../../a-phenomenological-model-for-selective-growth-of-semiconducting-single-walled-carbon-nanotubes-based-on-catalyst-deactivation-by-shunsuke-sakurai-and-others.md)

The useful distinction is between selecting a structure at nucleation, selecting its growth rate, and selectively terminating or removing unwanted tubes. Each can enrich the semiconducting fraction, but they do not establish the same growth mechanism.
