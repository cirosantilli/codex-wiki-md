<h1 id="section-a-ii/15/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Using the magnitude of the classical slope,

$$
n_s=\frac{1}{e(4000\,\mathrm{m^2/C})}
=1.56\times10^{15}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}.
$$

The [Landau filling factor](../../../../../../landau-filling-factor.md) at $1\,\mathrm T$ is

$$
\nu_{\mathrm{fill}}=\frac{n_sh}{eB}
=6.47.
$$

Under the convention that the “last Landau level” means the last resolved spin branch, its degeneracy per area is $eB/h$. Setting $\nu_{\mathrm{fill}}=1$ gives

$$
B_1=\frac{n_sh}{e}=6.47\,\mathrm T.
$$

Above this threshold all carriers fit into that lowest resolved branch, which is partially filled when $\nu_{\mathrm{fill}}<1$.

There is a spin convention to state explicitly: if “Landau level” instead means one orbital level with unresolved twofold spin degeneracy, its capacity is $2eB/h$, and all carriers first fit into orbital index $n=0$ at **$B=3.24\,\mathrm T$**, where $\nu_{\mathrm{fill}}=2$. The slope alone determines $n_s$ and $\nu_{\mathrm{fill}}$, but does not specify the spin splitting.
