<h1 id="section-a-ii/15/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take a two-dimensional conducting sheet in the $xy$ plane with [magnetic flux density](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) $\mathbf B=B\hat{\mathbf z}$ and [electric current density](../../../../../../current-density.md) along $x$. Moving carriers feel the transverse [Lorentz force](../../../../../../lorentz-force.md) $q\mathbf v_d\times\mathbf B$. They accumulate at opposite edges, producing a transverse [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) until its force balances the magnetic deflection:

$$
q(\mathbf E_H+\mathbf v_d\times\mathbf B)=0.
$$

For the transverse-voltage convention $E_y/j_x$, this gives $E_y=v_xB$ and

$$
\frac{E_y}{j_x}=\frac{B}{nq}.
$$

The [Hall effect](../../../../../../hall-effect.md) therefore measures carrier sign as well as density in the single-carrier approximation: electrons give the opposite transverse-voltage sign from positive carriers. Some tensor conventions call $E_y/j_x$ $\rho_{yx}$ rather than $\rho_{xy}$; the physical sign is fixed by the stated lead and field directions.

The [Hall voltage](../../../../../../hall-voltage.md) is the integral of this transverse [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) across the sample width. In a three-dimensional slab of thickness $t$, its magnitude is $|V_H|=IB/(n|q|t)$; for a sheet, use the sheet number density $n_s$ and omit $t$.
