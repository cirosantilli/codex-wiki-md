<h1 id="section-a-ii/16/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Specify the [magnetoresistance ratio](../../../../../../magnetoresistance-ratio.md) as $(R_{AP}-R_P)/R_P$. Increasing a continuous Cu spacer adds spin-independent series resistance. Even when $d$ remains below the [spin diffusion length](../../../../../../spin-diffusion-length.md), this dilutes the ratio; spin information need not be destroyed to reduce the percentage signal. In the illustrative [two-current resistor model](../../../../../../two-current-resistor-model.md),

$$
\frac{R_{AP}-R_P}{R_P}
=\frac{(a-b)^2}{(2a+r)(2b+r)},\qquad r\propto d,
$$

which decreases as $d$ increases. The absolute difference also decreases in this model. At a fixed bias current, the measured voltage contrast is $\Delta V=I(R_{AP}-R_P)$.

For $d$ comparable to or greater than the [spin diffusion length](../../../../../../spin-diffusion-length.md), additional [spin relaxation](../../../../../../spin-relaxation.md) further suppresses the contrast. At the other extreme an ultrathin or discontinuous Cu layer can introduce direct magnetic coupling or pinholes, preventing a fully antiparallel state. Thickness-dependent interlayer coupling can therefore make the very-thin-spacer trend nonmonotonic; a universal exponential law does not follow from the supplied assumptions.
