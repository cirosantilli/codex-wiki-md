<h1 id="section-a-ii/16/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Two practical approaches retaining Co and NiFe are:

- Improve the spin-selective interfaces: control deposition and intermixing, avoid oxidation or magnetic dead layers, and reduce pinholes and spin-flip defects. The aim is large majority/minority transport asymmetry with low spin-memory loss, rather than merely the smallest total resistance.
- Optimize the Cu thickness and electrical stack: use the thinnest continuous spacer that still allows independent layer reversal and remains well below its [spin diffusion length](../../../../../../spin-diffusion-length.md); reduce extraneous contact or lead resistance. This preserves a full antiparallel state and limits spin-independent dilution of the measured [magnetoresistance ratio](../../../../../../magnetoresistance-ratio.md).

Increasing magnetic-layer thickness toward the useful spin-dependent-scattering scale can be another optimization, but growth beyond the relevant spin diffusion scale eventually gives diminishing returns. Merely increasing the bias current increases the readout voltage and possible heating; it does not, by itself, increase the intrinsic [giant magnetoresistance](../../../../../../giant-magnetoresistance.md) ratio.
