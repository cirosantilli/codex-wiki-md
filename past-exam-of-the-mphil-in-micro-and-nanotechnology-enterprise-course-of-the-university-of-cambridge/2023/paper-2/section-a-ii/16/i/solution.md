<h1 id="section-a-ii/16/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [giant magnetoresistance](../../../../../../giant-magnetoresistance.md) mechanism is spin-dependent conduction through the two ferromagnets. Exchange splitting gives majority and minority $d$ bands different [density of states](../../../../../../density-of-states.md) values at the [Fermi energy](../../../../../../fermi-energy.md). Scattering into those bands, together with interface scattering, produces different resistances for the two spin channels. The relevant quantity is the spin-dependent transport/scattering rate, not simply a count of all available states.

For parallel layer [magnetizations](../../../../../../magnetization.md), one global spin channel encounters relatively weak scattering in both Co and NiFe, providing a low-resistance path. For antiparallel [magnetizations](../../../../../../magnetization.md), each global spin channel encounters one more strongly scattering ferromagnet, so both paths are comparatively resistive. The channels conduct in parallel while each crosses the layers in series. This is [current-perpendicular-to-plane magnetoresistance](../../../../../../current-perpendicular-to-plane-magnetoresistance.md), because the current crosses the interfaces.[Spin-dependent conduction and the distinction between CPP and CIP transport](../../../../../../nobel-lecture-origin-development-and-future-of-spintronics-by-albert-fert.md)

In the simplest [two-current resistor model](../../../../../../two-current-resistor-model.md), with equal magnetic layers having channel resistances $a,b$ and spacer resistance $r$ per channel,

$$
R_P=\frac{(2a+r)(2b+r)}{2(a+b+r)},\qquad
R_{AP}=\frac{a+b+r}{2},\qquad
R_{AP}-R_P=\frac{(a-b)^2}{2(a+b+r)}.
$$

Real Co/NiFe layers need not have equal parameters, but the same-sign spin asymmetries normally give $R_{AP}>R_P$. Their different [magnetic coercivities](../../../../../../magnetic-coercivity.md) allow the two configurations to be selected separately.
