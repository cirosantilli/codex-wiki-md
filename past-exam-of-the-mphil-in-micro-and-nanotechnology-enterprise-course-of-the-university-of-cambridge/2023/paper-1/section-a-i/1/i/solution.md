[Moiré fringes](../../../../../../moire-fringe.md) arise when two overlapping periodic structures contribute closely spaced [reciprocal lattice vectors](../../../../../../reciprocal-lattice-vector.md) to the [transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../../transmission-electron-microscopy.md) image. Their relative [phase](../../../../../../phase-waves.md) varies slowly across the image, producing a coarse modulation with a period much larger than either atomic spacing. The structures may have different lattice spacings, a small relative [rotation](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md), or both. The modulation is an [interference](../../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) or beat phenomenon; its spacing is not itself a new atomic-plane spacing. Its orientation is perpendicular to the difference vector of the two contributing projected reciprocal-lattice vectors.
