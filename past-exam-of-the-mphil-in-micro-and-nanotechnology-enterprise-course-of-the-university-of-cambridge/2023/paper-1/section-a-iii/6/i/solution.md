<h1 id="section-a-iii/6/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [reference electrode](../../../../../../reference-electrode.md) should provide a stable, reproducible potential from a well-defined reversible equilibrium, with fixed relevant activities. It should pass negligible measuring current, resist contamination and drift, and avoid changing the sulfate sample through incompatible ions or reactions.

**Silver is suitable** when coupled to a sparingly soluble silver salt and a controlled filling electrolyte; a [silver sulfate reference electrode](../../../../../../silver-sulfate-reference-electrode.md) avoids introducing chloride into a sulfate system. **Mercury is also electrochemically suitable** in a mercury/mercurous-sulfate reference system, although its toxicity and handling requirements make silver attractive where practical.

**Sodium is unsuitable** because it reacts with water rather than maintaining a quiet metal/ion reference equilibrium. **Calcium and strontium are likewise too reactive** in water. **Magnesium is not a dependable reference simply because it sometimes reacts slowly:** corrosion, oxide/hydroxide films and mixed potentials make its open-circuit potential dependent on surface history and solution conditions. A relatively inert-looking surface is not equivalent to a reproducible reversible electrode.

Even a suitable reference is not just an arbitrary piece of metal immersed in the sample. The metal, equilibrium salt, controlled electrolyte and liquid junction together define the reference system.
