↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The material figure of merit is

$$
\boxed{ZT=\frac{S^2\sigma T}{\kappa_e+\kappa_l}}.
$$

Large [thermoelectric power factor](../../../../../../thermoelectric-power-factor.md) supplies electrical output, while low thermal conductivity limits heat leaking without useful conversion. For a constant-property ideal generator, a common approximation is

$$
\eta_{\max}=\frac{T_h-T_c}{T_h}
\frac{\sqrt{1+\overline{ZT}}-1}{\sqrt{1+\overline{ZT}}+T_c/T_h}.
$$

Increasing the appropriately averaged figure of merit raises efficiency toward, but not beyond, the Carnot limit. Actual devices also incur contact, geometric and heat-transfer losses, and a single peak material ZT does not specify their efficiency.

The supplied trends show the trade-off with carrier density: conductivity rises, the magnitude of the [Seebeck coefficient](../../../../../../seebeck-coefficient.md) tends to fall, and electronic thermal conductivity rises approximately with conductivity through the [Wiedemann-Franz law](../../../../../../wiedemann-franz-law.md). Thus neither arbitrarily high doping nor maximization of conductivity alone gives the best ZT; the power factor and thermal terms produce an intermediate optimum.

Two complementary strategies are:

- **Reduce lattice thermal conductivity while preserving useful charge transport.** Alloy disorder, grain boundaries and suitably designed nanoscale interfaces scatter phonons. Nanostructured bismuth–antimony telluride provides an experimental example in which microstructure and transport measurements supported reduced lattice heat conduction.[Science 320 (2008), pp. 634–638; grain-boundary/defect phonon scattering and enhanced ZT](../../../../../../high-thermoelectric-performance-of-nanostructured-bismuth-antimony-telluride-bulk-alloys-by-bed-poudel-and-others.md)
- **Improve the power factor through band engineering and carrier-density optimization.** [Band convergence](../../../../../../band-convergence.md) increases useful valley degeneracy and can support a larger Seebeck coefficient without sacrificing conductivity as severely as a simple low-density strategy. Composition and doping tuning in lead telluride–selenide alloys demonstrated this route.[Nature 473 (2011), pp. 66–69; experimentally engineered valley convergence](../../../../../../convergence-of-electronic-bands-for-high-performance-bulk-thermoelectrics-by-yanzhong-pei-and-others.md)

Both strategies must be checked for mobility penalties and electronic heat transport. Simply suppressing every kind of transport would also suppress electrical output.
