↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The surface carboxylic acid is less reactive toward direct [nucleophilic acyl substitution](../../../../../../../nucleophilic-acyl-substitution.md): hydroxide is a poor leaving group, and an amine initially tends to form an ammonium carboxylate salt. An alcohol also does not normally give rapid, quantitative ester formation merely by mixing with the acid.

Conversion to an [acyl chloride](../../../../../../../acyl-chloride.md) activates the carbonyl and provides chloride as a substantially better leaving group. Amine or alcohol attack can then form a tetrahedral intermediate followed by elimination and proton transfer, giving the grafted amide or ester. Other coupling or esterification methods are possible; acid-chloride activation supplies the efficient route used in this scheme. Surface functionalization with the corresponding alkylamine and diol was demonstrated in the underlying carbon-material study.[Carbon 47 (2009), pp. 622–628; covalent alkylamine and diol surface grafting](../../../../../../../controlled-dispersion-of-carbon-nanospheres-through-surface-functionalization-by-margaret-j-sobkowicz-and-others.md)
