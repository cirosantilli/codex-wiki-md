<h1 id="section-a-ii/4/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**Zero [step coverage](../../../../../../step-coverage.md)** means that a sidewall or hidden surface receives essentially no film even though a horizontal reference surface is coated. **[Deposition shadowing](../../../../../../deposition-shadowing.md)** occurs when an upper edge intercepts trajectories that would otherwise reach a lower region. A directional high-vacuum evaporant flux can therefore coat a feature top while leaving vertical or reentrant walls uncoated.

[Sputtering](../../../../../../sputtering.md) generally releases material from an extended target with a broad angular distribution; gas collisions can further redistribute arrival directions. More oblique trajectories can reach sidewalls, so the extreme zero-coverage behavior of an ideal collimated evaporant beam is reduced.

The absolute claim that these effects never occur in sputtering is too strong. Low-pressure directional sputtering and high-aspect-ratio or reentrant features can still exhibit shadowing and poor coverage. Broader incidence improves access; it does not make a physical deposition process universally conformal.
