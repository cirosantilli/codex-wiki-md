<h1 id="7/iii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Treat the film as a [parallel-plate capacitor](../../../../../../parallel-plate-capacitor.md), initially compensated and then isolated electrically. Its [capacitance](../../../../../../capacitance.md) and temperature-induced [electric polarization](../../../../../../polarization-density.md) charge are

$$
C=\frac{\epsilon_0\epsilon A}{d},\qquad\Delta Q=A p\Delta T.
$$

The corresponding ideal open-circuit signal, with the [electrode](../../../../../../electrode.md) polarity defined consistently with this charge convention, is

$$
\boxed{V_{\rm oc}=\frac{\Delta Q}{C}=\frac{p\,d\,\Delta T}{\epsilon_0\epsilon}}.
$$

Area cancels because both charge and [capacitance](../../../../../../capacitance.md) are proportional to it. Substitution gives

$$
V_{\rm oc}=\frac{(-35\times10^{-6})(2\times10^{-6})(1)}{(8.854\times10^{-12})(8)}
=\boxed{-0.988\,\mathrm V}.
$$

The [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) magnitude is approximately **$0.99\,\mathrm V$**; interchanging the voltmeter leads reverses its sign. This is an ideal capacitive result, before leakage, mobile-charge screening or loading by the measurement circuit reduces the signal.
