<h1 id="7/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [thermocouple](../../../../../thermocouple.md) uses **two materials with different [Seebeck coefficients](../../../../../seebeck-coefficient.md)**. With their junctions at different [temperatures](../../../../../temperature-split.md), the net [open-circuit voltage](../../../../../open-circuit-voltage.md) is

$$
V_{\rm oc}=\int_{T_c}^{T_h}(\alpha_p-\alpha_n)\,dT
\simeq(\alpha_p-\alpha_n)\Delta T.
$$

Using p-type and n-type legs makes the two contributions add. Many pairs can be connected electrically in series to add [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) while remaining thermally in parallel between the same hot and cold plates. A single homogeneous wire does not provide this same complete-circuit [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) when its return lead has the same [thermopower](../../../../../seebeck-coefficient.md).

The reciprocal [Peltier effect](../../../../../peltier-effect.md) transports heat when [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) passes through a junction. The Kelvin relation $\Pi=\alpha T$ connects the heat carried per unit charge to the [Seebeck coefficient](../../../../../seebeck-coefficient.md). The same combination of large [thermopower](../../../../../seebeck-coefficient.md), high [electrical conductivity](../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md) and low [thermal conductivity](../../../../../thermal-conductivity.md) gives a favorable dimensionless figure of merit

$$
ZT=\frac{\alpha^2\sigma T}{\kappa}.
$$

Thus materials useful for a generator are also useful for a cooler when [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) is driven in the appropriate direction. [Joule heating](../../../../../joule-heating.md) and thermal leakage oppose cooling; device geometry, contact resistance and operating [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) must still be appropriate. Reversibility of the thermoelectric coupling does not make either device lossless.
