<h1 id="10/ii/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The rigid [naphthalene](../../../../../../naphthalene.md) core fixes the separation and directions of the two arm attachment points. In A they point from more separated regions of the core, so closure requires the flexible arms to span the core and adopt less favorable torsional arrangements. In B the closer, more convergent attachment geometry permits the UPy ends to meet with **less conformational [strain](../../../../../../strain.md) and better preorganization**.

Both rings gain similar local UPy hydrogen-bonding interactions, but A pays a larger deformation cost in its linkers and overall geometry. That can rationalize its higher calculated total [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) and its greater tendency to open into intermolecular chains.

The quoted $50\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ is large compared with $RT\simeq2.48\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Nevertheless a total-energy difference is not automatically a solution Gibbs free-energy difference: [solvent](../../../../../../solvent.md) and [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) contributions would be needed before converting it into an equilibrium population ratio.
