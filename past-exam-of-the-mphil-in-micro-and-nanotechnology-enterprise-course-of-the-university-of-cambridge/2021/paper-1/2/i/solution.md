<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**Deposit a dilute dispersion onto a clean, electron-transparent [TEM](../../../../../transmission-electron-microscopy.md) support and let the [ethanol](../../../../../ethanol.md) evaporate.** A practical sequence is to redisperse gently, dilute enough to reduce overlap, place a small drop on a holey or lacey support grid, wick away excess liquid and dry thoroughly. Choose support chemistry compatible with wetting by [ethanol](../../../../../ethanol.md).

Look for flakes spanning support holes, where no support film obscures the light-element lattice. Adjacent supported regions help locate and stabilize the flakes. Dilution reduces accidental multilayer stacks, while a clean grid and clean [solvent](../../../../../solvent.md) reduce hydrocarbon background. Gentle agitation may be useful; prolonged aggressive sonication can itself change the defects one intends to measure.

Drying prevents a volatile liquid from entering the microscope [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md). Additional cleaning must be chosen cautiously because heating or energetic [plasma](../../../../../plasma-physics.md) treatment can alter [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) groups and defects in [reduced graphene oxide](../../../../../reduced-graphene-oxide.md). Record a low-dose survey before atomic-resolution imaging, and distinguish pre-existing defects from changes induced during acquisition.
