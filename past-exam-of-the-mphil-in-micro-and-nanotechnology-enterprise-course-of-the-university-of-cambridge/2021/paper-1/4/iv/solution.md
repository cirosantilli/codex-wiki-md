<h1 id="4/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Decreasing the source diameter increases [spatial optical coherence](../../../../../spatial-optical-coherence.md) and moves the illumination towards the coherent on-axis limit. Here the grating frequency is only

$$
f=1/(800\ \mathrm{nm}),\qquad
\frac f{f_c}=\frac{193}{1.35(800)}\simeq0.179.
$$

The zero and first orders are comfortably within the pupil for on-axis illumination. Reducing an extended source can prevent some off-axis source points from losing [diffraction](../../../../../diffraction.md) orders at the pupil edge, and can therefore **increase the grating modulation, or leave it approximately unchanged if the relevant orders were already fully passed**. It need not give a universal numerical increase: higher orders, source shape, [defocus](../../../../../defocus-aberration.md) and [optical aberrations](../../../../../optical-aberration.md) matter for a binary grating.

The resist's $\gamma$ and critical modulation $M_c$ in part (i) are material/process properties and **do not change merely because the source becomes smaller**. What may change is part (ii)'s comparison between the actual image modulation and $M_c$. If the improved image exceeds about 0.667, an exposure window opens; the source information alone does not establish a new numerical value.
