<h1 id="8/ii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The thin [scanning transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../../scanning-transmission-electron-microscopy.md) foil restricts scattering to a much smaller projected specimen volume than bulk [SEM](../../../../../../scanning-electron-microscope.md) at $30\,\mathrm{kV}$. It reveals **the nanoscale shape and continuity of the [gold](../../../../../../gold.md)/NiP interface, the narrow low-density opening and the fine heterogeneous region underneath the [gold](../../../../../../gold.md)**, which the larger [SEM](../../../../../../scanning-electron-microscope.md) interaction volume and surface-sensitive collection obscure.

The enlarged view resolves structure on the tens-of-nanometres scale: local interface roughness, small bright and dark domains and the relationship of the [gold](../../../../../../gold.md) layer to the damaged or porous-looking NiP$_y$ region. This helps distinguish an interface-associated defect from merely a change between nominal alloy layers. It still does not identify a crystalline phase or quantify composition without [diffraction](../../../../../../diffraction.md) or spectroscopic analysis. Since the [SEM](../../../../../../scanning-electron-microscope.md) surface and [focused ion beam milling](../../../../../../focused-ion-beam-milling.md) lamella need not cut precisely the same volume, correlations should allow for section position.
