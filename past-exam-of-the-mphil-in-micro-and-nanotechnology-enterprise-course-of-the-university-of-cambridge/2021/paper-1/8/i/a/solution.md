<h1 id="8/i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The main characteristic peaks identify **[nickel](../../../../../../nickel.md) and [phosphorus](../../../../../../phosphorus.md)**: the spectrum has a strong Ni $K\alpha$ peak near $7.48\,\mathrm{keV}$, the Ni $L$ region near $0.85\,\mathrm{keV}$ and P $K\alpha$ near $2.01\,\mathrm{keV}$. Weaker associated lines, including Ni $K\beta$ near $8.26\,\mathrm{keV}$, can accompany them. The continuous background is [bremsstrahlung](../../../../../../bremsstrahlung.md) from decelerated [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md).

Small [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) and [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) signals at low [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) can arise from contamination, surface [oxide](../../../../../../oxide.md) or preparation materials; a [copper](../../../../../../copper.md) contribution can arise from the adjacent substrate within the finite interaction volume. The Cu $L$ region lies close to Ni $L$, so overlap is particularly troublesome at low [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md). A weak Cu $K\alpha$ contribution near $8.04\,\mathrm{keV}$ is better separated, though still close to Ni $K\beta$. At $30\,\mathrm{kV}$ the beam can excite these K lines.

Peak height is not an [atomic fraction](../../../../../../atomic-fraction.md): [ionization](../../../../../../ionization.md) cross-sections, [fluorescence](../../../../../../fluorescence-split.md) yields, absorption, detector response, background and line overlap require correction. The large [nickel](../../../../../../nickel.md) peak therefore supports the nickel-rich assignment but does not directly give the nickel-to-phosphorus atomic ratio.
