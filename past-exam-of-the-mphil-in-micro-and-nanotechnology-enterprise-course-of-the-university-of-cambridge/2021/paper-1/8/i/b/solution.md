<h1 id="8/i/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the supplied polynomial as an effective-atomic-number approximation for the alloys. Interpreting each quoted [phosphorus](../../../../../../phosphorus.md) number as an [atomic fraction](../../../../../../atomic-fraction.md) $x_P=N_P/(N_P+N_{\rm Ni})$,

$$
\bar Z=(1-x_P)28+x_P15=28-13x_P,
\qquad C_Z=\frac{|\eta(79)-\eta(\bar Z)|}{\eta(79)}.
$$

The [gold](../../../../../../gold.md) reference yield is $\eta(79)=0.486996$. Substitution gives:

| Layer | Effective [atomic number](../../../../../../atomic-number.md) | Backscatter yield | Contrast relative to [gold](../../../../../../gold.md) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Au | $79$ | $0.486996$ | **$0$** |
| Cu | $29$ | $0.302417$ | **$0.3790$** |
| NiP$_x$ | $25.79$ | $0.277764$ | **$0.4296$** |
| NiP$_y$ | $24.75$ | $0.269247$ | **$0.4471$** |
| NiP$_z$ | $26.70$ | $0.285001$ | **$0.4148$** |

The ordering is [gold](../../../../../../gold.md) brightest, then [copper](../../../../../../copper.md), NiP$_z$, NiP$_x$ and NiP$_y$. The phosphorus-rich layer has the lowest effective [atomic number](../../../../../../atomic-number.md).

There is a wording ambiguity in “[atomic fraction](../../../../../../atomic-fraction.md) of P relative to Ni.” If those numbers instead mean $r=N_P/N_{\rm Ni}$, first convert $x_P=r/(1+r)$, giving $\bar Z=(28+15r)/(1+r)$. The three alloy contrasts then become **$0.4243$, $0.4361$, $0.4129$**, respectively. The qualitative ordering is unchanged. Both calculations explicitly use $\eta(\bar Z)$; this simple alloy approximation is not a full electron-transport simulation.
