<h1 id="11/ii/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**This is a primary cell and should not be treated as rechargeable.** Discharge converts the fluorinated-carbon [electrode](../../../../../../electrode.md) into [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) and [lithium](../../../../../../lithium.md) fluoride. It does not simply insert [lithium](../../../../../../lithium.md) into a host that can be reversibly deintercalated. Reversing [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md) does not cleanly reconstruct the original distributed C–F bonding and [electrode](../../../../../../electrode.md) microstructure.

Insulating LiF, loss of the original reaction interfaces and unwanted [electrolyte](../../../../../../electrolyte.md) reactions obstruct reversibility. Replating [lithium](../../../../../../lithium.md) can also produce an irregular metallic deposit and internal shorting. A thermodynamically possible formal reverse reaction is therefore not evidence of a practical rechargeable cell.
