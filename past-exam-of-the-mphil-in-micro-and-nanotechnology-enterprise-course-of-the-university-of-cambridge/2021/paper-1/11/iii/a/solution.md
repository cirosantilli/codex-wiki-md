<h1 id="11/iii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

If the [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) were held at $4.57\,\mathrm V$ for the entire ideal discharge, then

$$
\boxed{E=VQ=(4.57)(175.2)=800.664\,\mathrm{Wh}=0.801\,\mathrm{kWh}},
$$

and the electrical [power](../../../../../../power.md) would be

$$
\boxed{P=VI=(4.57)(0.010)=0.0457\,\mathrm W=45.7\,\mathrm{mW}}.
$$

These are **constant-voltage estimates**, not a determination of the actual [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) from the fresh-cell [open-circuit voltage](../../../../../../open-circuit-voltage.md). Under load the [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) depends on state of discharge, [electric polarization](../../../../../../polarization-density.md) and internal resistance. The physical result is $E=I\int_0^{t_f}V_{\rm load}(t)\,dt$, and $P(t)=IV_{\rm load}(t)$. The supplied single open-circuit value does not specify that [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) curve; practical delivered [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) will generally be lower than the simple estimate.
