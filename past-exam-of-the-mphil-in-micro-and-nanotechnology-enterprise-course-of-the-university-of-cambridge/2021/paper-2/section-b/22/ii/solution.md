<h1 id="section-b/22/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a no-slip spherical particle in a [Newtonian fluid](../../../../../../newtonian-fluid.md) at low [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md), [Stokes law](../../../../../../stokes-s-law.md) gives $\zeta=6\pi\eta a$. The [fluctuation-dissipation theorem](../../../../../../fluctuation-dissipation-theorem.md) then gives the [Stokes–Einstein relation](../../../../../../stokes-einstein-relation.md) $D=k_BT/\zeta$. Using the conventional [radial Brownian displacement](../../../../../../radial-brownian-displacement.md) in three dimensions,

$$
\boxed{d_{\rm rms}=\sqrt{\langle|\Delta\mathbf r|^2\rangle}
=\sqrt{6Dt}=\sqrt{\frac{k_BTt}{\pi\eta a}}.}
$$

Here $k_B$ is the [Boltzmann constant](../../../../../../boltzmann-constant.md), $T$ absolute [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), $\eta$ [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md), $a$ particle radius and $t$ elapsed time. Larger thermal forcing raises [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion.md); larger viscous drag reduces it. The formula requires times long relative to momentum relaxation and neglects walls, interactions and external drift.

The word “average” needs a statistic: the vector mean is zero, while the mean radial displacement for Gaussian three-dimensional [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion.md) is $\langle|\Delta\mathbf r|\rangle=4\sqrt{Dt/\pi}$, not $\sqrt{6Dt}$. Both scale as $t^{1/2}$. Neither is the total length of a microscopic random path.
