One explanation is **[block-copolymer kinetic trapping](../../../../../../block-copolymer-kinetic-trapping.md)**. A solvent-swollen transient sheet structure can be immobilized when a block vitrifies during drying; accurately measured block fractions do not guarantee [thermodynamic equilibrium](../../../../../../thermodynamic-equilibrium.md). Thermal [annealing](../../../../../../annealing-materials-science.md) above the relevant [glass transition](../../../../../../glass-transition.md), or a controlled nonselective solvent-vapour anneal followed by structural characterization, can test whether the sheets relax toward the bulk composition-appropriate morphology.

A second is **surface or confinement selection of [lamellar microphase](../../../../../../lamellar-microphase.md)**. The diagram describes a bulk reference model. A thin film, preferential [wetting](../../../../../../wetting.md) at the substrate/free surface or a commensurate confining thickness adds interfacial and geometrical [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) terms that can favour layered arrangements even at an asymmetric global fraction. Unequal layer thicknesses can accommodate $f_A=0.15$; equal block fractions are not required for every layered metastable or confined structure. Varying film thickness and surface chemistry, and comparing bulk-like and confined samples after adequate [annealing](../../../../../../annealing-materials-science.md), distinguishes this explanation from kinetic arrest. Strong conformational asymmetry is another possible departure from the reference model, but an incorrect measured composition need not be invoked.
