<h1 id="section-b/22/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In the imposed [free-draining polymer friction](../../../../../../free-draining-polymer-friction.md) model, the drag coefficients of the $N$ [monomers](../../../../../../monomer.md) add, giving $\zeta_1=N(6\pi\eta a)$ and $D_1=k_BT/(6\pi\eta aN)$. Taking the same three-dimensional centre-of-mass RMS convention as part (ii),

$$
\boxed{d_1=\sqrt{6D_1t}=\sqrt{\frac{k_BTt}{\pi\eta aN}}.}
$$

The [polymer](../../../../../../polymer-split.md) is a one-dimensional connected contour, but that alone does not restrict its [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) to one spatial axis. If the intended coordinate is instead literal one-dimensional curvilinear motion along an entanglement tube, its RMS contour displacement is $\sqrt{2D_1t}=\sqrt{k_BTt/(3\pi\eta aN)}$. That is a different coordinate and should not be compared with a three-dimensional displacement without stating the conversion/model.

Additive local drag is the free-draining [Rouse model](../../../../../../rouse-model.md) approximation. It neglects long-range solvent-mediated [hydrodynamic interactions](../../../../../../hydrodynamic-interaction.md) and is not a complete theory of an entangled chain. In a tube/[reptation](../../../../../../reptation.md) description, curvilinear [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion.md) can scale as $N^{-1}$ while long-time spatial centre-of-mass [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion.md) scales as $N^{-2}$ after the chain renews its tube. Conversely a dilute unentangled coil can exhibit [Zimm model](../../../../../../zimm-model.md) hydrodynamic coupling. [Monomer](../../../../../../monomer.md) motion, centre-of-mass motion and contour sliding therefore cannot all be identified with the same displacement law.
