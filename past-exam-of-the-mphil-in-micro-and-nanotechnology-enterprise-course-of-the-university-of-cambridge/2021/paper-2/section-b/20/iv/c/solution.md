<h1 id="section-b/20/iv/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The transport [mean free path](../../../../../../../mean-free-path.md) is $\ell=v_F\tau_{\rm tr}$, while $\mu=e\tau_{\rm tr}/m^*$. Eliminating the scattering time gives $\ell=\mu\hbar k_F/e$. The usual [ballistic focusing mobility criterion](../../../../../../../ballistic-focusing-mobility-criterion.md) $\ell\gtrsim L$ therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\mu_{\rm min}\sim\frac{eL}{\hbar k_F}=11.1\ \mathrm{m^2\,V^{-1}s^{-1}}
=1.11\times10^5\ \mathrm{cm^2\,V^{-1}s^{-1}}.}
$$

A more geometrically faithful first-peak survival criterion uses the semicircular path length $s=\pi L/2$, giving $\mu\gtrsim17.4\ \mathrm{m^2\,V^{-1}s^{-1}}$. There is no sharp universal visibility threshold: clearly resolved peaks favour $\ell\gg s$, small aperture broadening and [specular reflection](../../../../../../../specular-reflection.md). The mobility inferred in part (ii) corresponds to $\ell\simeq314\ \mathrm{\mu m}$, comfortably above both estimates. Higher-order peaks can still be reduced by boundary roughness even when bulk mobility is high.
