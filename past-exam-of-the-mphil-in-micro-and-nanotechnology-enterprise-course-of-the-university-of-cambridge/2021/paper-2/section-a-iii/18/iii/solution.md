<h1 id="section-a-iii/18/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Increase [particle volume fraction](../../../../../../particle-volume-fraction.md) slowly by controlled evaporation or gentle [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) until ordering becomes favourable, while preserving sufficient colloidal repulsion to prevent irreversible sticking. Hard-sphere-like monodisperse suspensions can crystallize by packing [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md); weak, reversible attractions can also favour ordered assembly if they allow defect correction. A controlled drying front or patterned surface may guide the orientation, and gentle [sedimentation](../../../../../../sedimentation.md) can create a [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) gradient that crosses the ordering range.

The practical requirement is **slow structural change relative to [Brownian motion](../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md)**, with uniform [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), well-characterized [ionic strength](../../../../../../ionic-strength.md) and a narrow size distribution. An abrupt salt addition or rapid drying can remove the repulsive barrier and trap aggregates instead of growing a [crystal](../../../../../../crystal.md). Scattering or microscopy should distinguish crystalline peaks/order from merely increased turbidity.
