<h1 id="section-a-iii/18/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A useful microscopic screening estimate compares [thermal energy](../../../../../../thermal-energy.md) with the buoyant gravitational [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) over one particle radius. Write $\Delta\rho=50\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and $m_b=(4\pi/3)a^3\Delta\rho$. The [sedimentation length](../../../../../../sedimentation-length.md) is $\ell_g=k_BT/(m_bg)$, and the [gravitational Peclet number](../../../../../../gravitational-peclet-number.md) is

$$
\mathrm{Pe}_g=\frac{m_bga}{k_BT}
=\frac{4\pi\Delta\rho ga^4}{3k_BT}.
$$

Using $T=298\ \mathrm K$ and requiring $\mathrm{Pe}_g\lesssim1$ gives

$$
\boxed{a\lesssim\left(\frac{3k_BT}{4\pi\Delta\rho g}\right)^{1/4}
=1.19\ \mathrm{\mu m},\qquad d\lesssim2.4\ \mathrm{\mu m}.}
$$

Equivalently, combining [Stokes law](../../../../../../stokes-s-law.md) speed $v_s=2\Delta\rho ga^2/(9\eta)$ and [diffusion coefficient](../../../../../../diffusion-coefficient.md) $D=k_BT/(6\pi\eta a)$ gives $v_sa/D=\mathrm{Pe}_g$. [Buoyancy](../../../../../../buoyancy.md), not the full [polystyrene](../../../../../../polystyrene.md) density, is essential to the estimate. Taking a diameter rather than radius as the comparison length changes the numerical cutoff by an order-one factor.

There is **no universal largest particle that stays uniformly suspended indefinitely**. If uniformity over a vessel height $H$ is required, use $\ell_g\gtrsim H$, not merely $\ell_g\gtrsim a$. For example $H=1\ \mathrm{cm}$ gives a much smaller radius, about 58 nm. Observation time, density matching, convection, particle [polydispersity](../../../../../../polydispersity.md) and shape also matter. Independent of [sedimentation](../../../../../../sedimentation.md), electrostatic/steric barriers must prevent aggregation; low [ionic strength](../../../../../../ionic-strength.md), suitable surface chemistry and clean [solvent](../../../../../../solvent.md) can be as important as size.
