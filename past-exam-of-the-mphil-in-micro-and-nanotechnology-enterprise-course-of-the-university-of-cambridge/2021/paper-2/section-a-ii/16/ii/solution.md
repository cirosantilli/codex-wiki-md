<h1 id="section-a-ii/16/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Heat the bilayer into a window **$T_N<T<T_c$**, apply an in-plane [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) sufficient to align the [ferromagnet](../../../../../../ferromagnet.md), and cool through the [antiferromagnet](../../../../../../antiferromagnetism.md)'s ordering/blocking range while retaining the field. The [ferromagnet](../../../../../../ferromagnet.md) supplies a preferred interfacial spin direction as the [antiferromagnet](../../../../../../antiferromagnetism.md) orders. Sufficient antiferromagnetic anisotropy then pins part of the interfacial spin configuration, producing [unidirectional magnetic anisotropy](../../../../../../unidirectional-magnetic-anisotropy.md) rather than merely [uniaxial magnetic anisotropy](../../../../../../uniaxial-magnetic-anisotropy.md) in the [ferromagnet](../../../../../../ferromagnet.md).

Measure a full major [hysteresis](../../../../../../hysteresis.md) loop after removing the cooling field, and compare with the isolated [ferromagnet](../../../../../../ferromagnet.md), a zero-field-cooled bilayer and the result of reversing the cooling-field direction. Those controls separate a true loop-centre shift from a changed coercive width, minor-loop artefacts or trapped applied field. The effect requires appropriate interfacial exchange and stable pinning; an ideally compensated or freely rotating interface need not produce a large bias. Exchange anisotropy in coupled ferromagnetic/antiferromagnetic material was demonstrated in the classic experiment below.[Physical Review 102 (1956), pp. 1413–1414](../../../../../../new-magnetic-anisotropy-by-w-h-meiklejohn-and-c-p-bean.md)
