<h1 id="section-a-ii/15/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The map shows **[Coulomb blockade](../../../../../../coulomb-blockade.md) with discrete single-electron charging**: low-current diamonds separate charge-stable regions from finite-bias transport windows. A small conducting island or [quantum dot](../../../../../../quantum-dot.md) must be tunnel-coupled to source and drain, with a gate shifting its [electrochemical potential](../../../../../../electrochemical-potential.md). Charging prevents sequential transport until an island addition/removal energy enters the [bias voltage](../../../../../../bias-voltage.md) window.

The tunnel resistances should substantially exceed the [resistance quantum](../../../../../../resistance-quantum.md) $h/e^2\simeq25.8\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, preventing charge fluctuations from washing out integer island occupancy, while still permitting a measurable current. [Temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) and tunnel broadening must be small compared with the addition-energy scale. Here the smaller diamonds imply $e^2/C_\Sigma\simeq2.5\ \mathrm{meV}$, so $k_BT\ll2.5\ \mathrm{meV}$, or $T\ll29\ \mathrm K$; resolving the inferred 1 meV orbital structure requires the stricter scale $T\ll12\ \mathrm K$. Stray [capacitance](../../../../../../capacitance.md) and unintended multiple islands can complicate this simple interpretation.
