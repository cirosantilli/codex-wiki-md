<h1 id="section-a-i/14/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Prepare size-characterized gold-particle ensembles, heat them on a calibrated in-situ [electron diffraction](../../../../../../electron-diffraction.md) stage, and follow the loss of sharp fcc [diffraction](../../../../../../diffraction.md) features and appearance of liquid-like diffuse scattering. Measure the [particle size distribution](../../../../../../particle-size-distribution.md) rather than treating a polydisperse ensemble as one diameter. Temperature-dependent weakening of a [diffraction](../../../../../../diffraction.md) peak alone is insufficient: thermal vibrations, particle motion, coalescence, evaporation and beam damage can also reduce intensity. Low-dose repeats, heating/cooling runs, size checks before and after heating, a bulk-like reference and support-dependent controls help distinguish [melting](../../../../../../melting.md) from those effects. [Electron diffraction](../../../../../../electron-diffraction.md) measurements on [gold](../../../../../../gold.md) particles down to about 2 nm are a classic demonstration.[Physical Review A 13 (1976), pp. 2287–2298, abstract: scanning diffraction and size-distribution analysis](../../../../../../size-effect-on-the-melting-temperature-of-gold-particles-by-ph-buffat-and-j-p-borel.md)

The physical origin is the large interface contribution to [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) at small radius. A curved solid–liquid interface raises the solid's [thermodynamic equilibrium](../../../../../../thermodynamic-equilibrium.md) [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) relative to the flat-interface case, so coexistence occurs below the bulk [melting](../../../../../../melting.md) [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). Reduced coordination and surface disorder provide a microscopic interpretation, but the quantitative continuum relation is a [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) balance, not simply a claim that every surface atom has a universal lower [melting](../../../../../../melting.md) point.
