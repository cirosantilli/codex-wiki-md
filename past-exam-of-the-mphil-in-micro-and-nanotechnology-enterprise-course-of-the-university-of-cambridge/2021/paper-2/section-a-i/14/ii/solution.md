<h1 id="section-a-i/14/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $g_l-g_s$ be the liquid-minus-solid [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) density at the same [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). Near the bulk coexistence [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) $T_m$, linearization gives

$$
g_l-g_s\simeq\Delta s_V(T_m-T)
=\frac{\Delta H_V}{T_m}\Delta T.
$$

For a sphere, an interface displacement changes area and volume with $dA/dV=2/r$. Equality of the bulk driving force and the capillary cost at a curved solid–liquid interface therefore gives $g_l-g_s=2\gamma/r^*$. Equivalently, differentiating $4\pi r^2\gamma-(4\pi/3)r^3(g_l-g_s)$ gives the same stationary radius. Combining the expressions yields

$$
\boxed{\Delta T=\frac{2\gamma T_m}{r^*\Delta H_V}.}
$$

This form of the [Gibbs-Thomson effect](../../../../../../gibbs-thomson-relation.md) assumes isotropic interfacial energy, a spherical interface, bulk-like [latent heat](../../../../../../latent-heat.md) per volume and a near-coexistence [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) expansion. More detailed free-particle models must distinguish solid–vapor, liquid–vapor and solid–liquid energies.
