<h3 id="1/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

**The recorded profile has approximately 30 nm deep, flat-bottomed depressions with broadened, sloping edges.** The full-height regions are the inorganic surface; the lower plateaux are the infiltrated [perovskite structure](../../physical-chemistry.md#perovskite-structure). With adequate [feedback control](../../control-theory.md#closed-loop-control), [imaging trace and retrace](../../engineering.md#imaging-trace-and-retrace) should agree when plotted against the same laboratory position.

The tip has a finite opening angle. Its flank contacts a pore rim before its apex can follow an ideal vertical wall, so [tip convolution](../../engineering.md#tip-convolution) makes a narrow depression appear narrower and its sidewall less steep. For a flank angle $\alpha$ measured from the vertical, the horizontal distance needed to descend a height $h$ is $h\tan\alpha$. Along the [cantilever](../../elasticity-physics.md#cantilever), the nominal front and back values give

$$
\boxed{30\tan25^\circ\simeq14.0\ \mathrm{nm},\qquad
30\tan15^\circ\simeq8.0\ \mathrm{nm}.}
$$

Thus the two edges can have different apparent slopes. Across the [cantilever](../../elasticity-physics.md#cantilever), the nominal side angle instead gives about $12.4$ nm on each side. The scan orientation relative to the [cantilever](../../elasticity-physics.md#cantilever) must therefore be specified before choosing the relevant pair of slopes.

Even a 100 nm wide pore leaves ample room for the tip apex to reach a flat bottom: in the along-cantilever idealization its bottom plateau is about $100-14-8=78$ nm wide. A 200 nm pore gives about 178 nm. The tip height is far greater than the 30 nm step, so it does not prevent access. An unreported finite apex radius adds edge rounding; its size cannot be inferred from the listed flank angles.

<h3 id="1/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/ii)

Reducing the [feedback gain](../../control-theory.md#feedback-gain) increases the distance traveled before the vertical actuator responds to a change in height. A simple illustrative response is

$$
\tau\frac{dz_m}{dt}+z_m=z_g[x(t)],
$$

where $z_g$ is the accessible, tip-convolved profile and $z_m$ is the recorded actuator height. At scan speed $v$, the characteristic spatial lag is **$v\tau$**.

On a left-to-right trace, descent into a pore is late: the tip initially remains too high and may lose contact. At the opposite rim it also rises late, increasing the contact force before the actuator catches up. The lower plateau may be shortened or not reached, making the depression appear too shallow. The whole response is displaced towards larger $x$.

On the right-to-left retrace the same temporal lag produces the opposite displacement in laboratory coordinates. Descent is now delayed at the right-hand edge and ascent at the left-hand edge. **[imaging trace and retrace](../../engineering.md#imaging-trace-and-retrace) separate most strongly near height changes, with opposite spatial shifts and rounded transitions.** The central low-gain curves in the figure show this behavior on common $x$ axes. Reduced gain alone does not require oscillatory ringing; the first-order lag already explains the principal difference.

<h3 id="1/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/iii)

[friction force microscopy](../../engineering.md#friction-force-microscopy) records [cantilever](../../elasticity-physics.md#cantilever) torsion caused by lateral tip–sample force. Choose the detector sign so that [friction](../../newtonian-mechanics.md#friction) on a flat region contributes positively during a left-to-right trace. On a common laboratory-position axis, write the two signals schematically as

$$
S_+(x)=B+G(x)+F_f(x),\qquad
S_-(x)=B+G(x)-F_f(x).
$$

Here $B$ is an offset and $G$ represents a slope-related lateral-force or detector cross-talk contribution. A reversed electronic convention reverses all plotted signs without changing the physical conclusions.

At the same controlled [normal contact force](../../continuum-mechanics.md#normal-contact-force) $N$, the flat-region contribution is approximately $F_f=\mu N$. **The lower [perovskite structure](../../physical-chemistry.md#perovskite-structure) plateaux give a larger trace–retrace separation than the upper inorganic surface**, because their [coefficient of kinetic friction](../../newtonian-mechanics.md#coefficient-of-kinetic-friction) is larger. Genuine [friction](../../newtonian-mechanics.md#friction) reverses sign with scan direction, so a larger [friction](../../newtonian-mechanics.md#friction) magnitude means a higher positive trace and a more negative retrace in this convention.

Sloped edges produce additional peaks or shoulders. The normal reaction to a slope has a lateral component, and the finite tip flank can contact the rim, so edge contrast is not a direct map of the local [friction](../../newtonian-mechanics.md#friction) coefficient. With the two scans registered and load control maintained,

$$
\frac{S_+-S_-}{2}\simeq F_f,\qquad
\frac{S_++S_-}{2}\simeq B+G.
$$

The half-difference therefore emphasizes [friction](../../newtonian-mechanics.md#friction) while the half-sum diagnoses common geometrical contributions. Real [contact mechanics](../../continuum-mechanics.md#contact-mechanics) and imperfect registration can prevent exact cancellation. The bottom panel of the figure shows these two kinds of contrast separately.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 1](paper-1.md)

<h3 id="2/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

**Deposit a dilute dispersion onto a clean, electron-transparent [TEM](../../optics.md#transmission-electron-microscopy) support and let the [ethanol](../../biology.md#ethanol) evaporate.** A practical sequence is to redisperse gently, dilute enough to reduce overlap, place a small drop on a holey or lacey support grid, wick away excess liquid and dry thoroughly. Choose support chemistry compatible with wetting by [ethanol](../../biology.md#ethanol).

Look for flakes spanning support holes, where no support film obscures the light-element lattice. Adjacent supported regions help locate and stabilize the flakes. Dilution reduces accidental multilayer stacks, while a clean grid and clean [solvent](../../mixture.md#solvent) reduce hydrocarbon background. Gentle agitation may be useful; prolonged aggressive sonication can itself change the defects one intends to measure.

Drying prevents a volatile liquid from entering the microscope [vacuum](../../physics.md#vacuum). Additional cleaning must be chosen cautiously because heating or energetic [plasma](../../physics.md#plasma-physics) treatment can alter [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) groups and defects in [reduced graphene oxide](../../chemical-substance.md#reduced-graphene-oxide). Record a low-dose survey before atomic-resolution imaging, and distinguish pre-existing defects from changes induced during acquisition.

<h3 id="2/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/ii)

**Use low-voltage, preferably aberration-corrected [HRTEM](../../optics.md#high-resolution-transmission-electron-microscopy), with about 60–80 kV as a defensible operating range.** An approximately 80 kV starting point balances useful lattice resolution against [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) displacement damage; use the lower end if the instrument still resolves the lattice and defect stability is the priority.

A higher accelerating [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) reduces the [electron](../../physics.md#electron) [wavelength](../../wave-equation.md#wavelength), but can remove [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) atoms by [knock-on displacement](../../biology.md#knock-on-displacement). Measurements on pristine single-layer [graphene](../../chemical-substance.md#graphene) show a strong increase in displacement probability above the vicinity of 80 kV, so simply selecting a conventional 200–300 kV setting is a poor way to preserve the original lattice.[Beam-energy dependence of displacement cross sections, Physical Review Letters 108, 196102](../../physics-bibliography.md#accurate-measurement-of-electron-beam-induced-displacement-cross-sections-for-single-layer-graphene-by-jannik-c-meyer-and-others)

[reduced graphene oxide](../../chemical-substance.md#reduced-graphene-oxide) is already defective and chemically modified. Its vulnerable sites need not share the pristine-graphene threshold, and [ionization](../../atomic-physics.md#ionization), contamination and chemical etching remain possible at low [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage). Minimize dose, spread the beam while aligning and compare successive short exposures. **The [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) choice suppresses one damage mechanism; it does not guarantee damage-free imaging.**

<h3 id="2/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/iii)

**Atomic-resolution contrast is primarily [electron phase contrast](../../optics.md#electron-phase-contrast).** [elastic scattering](../../quantum-scattering.md#elastic-scattering) by the specimen potential changes the phase of the transmitted [electron](../../physics.md#electron) wave. [interference](../../physical-optics.md#interference-wave-propagation) between transmitted and diffracted components, modified by the objective lens, converts that phase change into intensity variations. Appropriate [defocus](../../point-spread-function.md#defocus-aberration), sufficiently small [optical aberrations](../../geometrical-optics.md#optical-aberration), good [spatial optical coherence](../../physical-optics.md#spatial-optical-coherence) and transfer of the [graphene](../../chemical-substance.md#graphene) [spatial frequencies](../../fourier-analysis.md#spatial-frequency) are needed. At exact focus an ideal weak phase object need not give a strong visible lattice.

Mass–thickness or amplitude contrast can also distinguish thicker overlap regions, folds, contamination and support film, especially when scattered [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) are excluded by an aperture. It is comparatively weak for a clean monolayer of low-$Z$ [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon). [diffraction contrast](../../optics.md#diffraction-contrast) becomes important in crystalline, multilayer or differently oriented regions when Bragg-scattered beams are selected or excluded. Relative rotation of layers can additionally produce a [moiré pattern](../../physical-optics.md#moire-pattern).

A dark region alone is not a reliable layer count: [electron phase contrast](../../optics.md#electron-phase-contrast) can change sign with [defocus](../../point-spread-function.md#defocus-aberration), and contamination also changes intensity. Use fold-edge fringes, [diffraction](../../quantum-scattering.md#diffraction) information and calibrated image simulations or thickness-sensitive measurements together. Defect identification should likewise compare a structural model with the actual imaging conditions, rather than reading every dark lattice site as a missing atom.

<h3 id="2/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/iv)

**Aberration-corrected [scanning transmission electron microscopy](../../optics.md#scanning-transmission-electron-microscopy) with annular dark-field imaging and spatially resolved [EELS](../../physics.md#electron-energy-loss-spectroscopy)** can locate the Si atoms and test their chemical environment. [silicon](../../chemical-substance.md#silicon) scatters much more strongly to high angles than [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon), so an isolated Si impurity can stand out in a thin [graphene](../../chemical-substance.md#graphene) lattice. Neighboring [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) positions reveal whether a site is, for example, three- or four-coordinated in projection.

Acquire a spectrum image at the same time. A [silicon](../../chemical-substance.md#silicon) core-loss edge identifies the element, while its [energy-loss near-edge structure](../../physics.md#energy-loss-near-edge-structure) contains information about the unoccupied states and bonding. This combination has experimentally distinguished individual Si coordination environments in [graphene](../../chemical-substance.md#graphene).[Figures 1–2, atomic-resolution imaging and Si-edge spectra](../../physics-bibliography.md#direct-determination-of-the-chemical-bonding-of-individual-impurities-in-graphene-by-wu-zhou-and-others)

Advantages are atom-scale spatial localization, simultaneous structural and chemical evidence, and stronger chemical identification than brightness alone. Limitations include low counts at one atom, background subtraction, finite [energy](../../energy.md) resolution, drift and the high dose needed for a useful spectrum. The beam can move or damage an impurity, and a two-dimensional projection does not by itself determine its out-of-plane geometry. Compare spectra and images with plausible structural calculations rather than assigning coordination solely from one bright pixel.

<h4 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h4>

**The resist is positive tone**, because the written regions are the regions removed during development. A suitable example is [poly(methyl methacrylate)](../../polymer.md#poly-methyl-methacrylate), conventionally [PMMA](../../polymer.md#poly-methyl-methacrylate), used in its ordinary positive-tone electron-beam exposure regime. Electron-induced [chain scission](../../polymer.md#chain-scission) reduces its [molar mass](../../chemistry.md#molar-mass) and increases its solubility in the developer.

The fact that this writing step fabricates an [extreme ultraviolet](../../optics.md#extreme-ultraviolet) mask does not make the resist an EUV-exposed resist: the exposure process being classified here is the electron-beam mask-writing step.

<h3 id="3/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/ii)

The deposited [energy](../../energy.md) is a blurred, nonlocal version of the commanded write pattern. Forward scattering spreads [energy](../../energy.md) within the resist; [backscattered electrons](../../physics.md#backscattered-electron) from the substrate contribute a wider background; [secondary electrons](../../physics.md#secondary-electron) and the finite incident probe add further spreading. Development then removes regions whose accumulated dose exceeds the effective clearing requirement.

Line ends and isolated short rectangles receive less supporting exposure from neighboring written regions. Their ends can recede and round, and isolated features may shrink to narrow openings. Dense features receive more overlapping scattered dose. Thus **one global dose cannot generally reproduce both isolated and dense shapes faithfully**.

Use [electron beam proximity correction](../../engineering.md#electron-beam-proximity-correction): calibrate a scattering point-spread model for the actual resist, thickness, substrate and beam [energy](../../energy.md), then adjust local dwell time or beam [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current). Increase or reshape the write dose at underexposed isolated features and line ends, while compensating for the excess background in dense regions. Geometric prebias, such as modest line-end extension, can complement dose correction. A thinner resist and an appropriate higher beam [energy](../../energy.md) may reduce forward-scattering blur, but the backscatter distribution must still be included. Correction must be based on the developed result, rather than assuming the incident beam diameter is the only resolution limit.

<h3 id="3/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iii/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/iii)

<h5 id="3/iii/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/iii/a)

**A transmissive mask is placed in contact with the resist, or a small distance above it for proximity printing.** Chromium-patterned glass or quartz is a typical mask. Illumination is normally collimated [ultraviolet](../../optics.md#ultraviolet) light, with refractive illumination optics used to prepare the beam.

There is no projection objective imaging a reduced mask pattern onto the resist: the mask shadows the resist approximately one-to-one. The finite gap in proximity printing introduces [diffraction](../../quantum-scattering.md#diffraction) and limits edge sharpness; contact printing reduces that gap at the cost of mask wear and contamination risk.

<h4 id="3/iii/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/iii)

<h5 id="3/iii/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/iii/b)

**Use multilayer reflective optics and a reflective patterned mask.** Near the usual [extreme ultraviolet](../../optics.md#extreme-ultraviolet) [wavelength](../../wave-equation.md#wavelength) of 13.5 nm, ordinary lenses and thick transmissive masks absorb too strongly. Multilayer mirrors focus and project the image, while an absorber pattern on a multilayer-coated mask defines the exposed regions.

The optical path operates in [vacuum](../../physics.md#vacuum) to avoid absorption by air. The key distinction is reflective mask projection, rather than a transparent chromium-on-glass mask or a scanned [electron](../../physics.md#electron) probe.

<h4 id="3/iii/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/iii)

<h5 id="3/iii/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/iii/c)

**Use [electron](../../physics.md#electron) lenses and a digitally scanned, blanked [electron](../../physics.md#electron) beam; no physical mask is required for direct writing.** Electromagnetic lenses focus the charged-particle beam, deflectors place it, and the pattern generator controls position and dose. Electrostatic elements can also form part of [electron](../../physics.md#electron) optics, but these are not refractive glass lenses.

The [extreme ultraviolet](../../optics.md#extreme-ultraviolet) mask mentioned in the problem is the object being manufactured by this maskless write process, rather than a mask placed in the [electron](../../physics.md#electron) path.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 1](paper-1.md)

<h3 id="4/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#4/i)

Reading the straight-line extrapolation in the printed [photoresist contrast](../../engineering.md#photoresist-contrast) curve gives approximately $D_0=20\ \mathrm{mJ\,cm^{-2}}$ at unit remaining thickness and $D_c=100\ \mathrm{mJ\,cm^{-2}}$ at clearing. On the logarithmic dose axis,

$$
\boxed{\gamma=\frac1{\log_{10}(D_c/D_0)}
=\frac1{\log_{10}5}\simeq1.43.}
$$

The critical image modulation follows by requiring one exposure time to give $D_{\max}\geq D_c$ in a bright region and $D_{\min}\leq D_0$ in a dark region:

$$
\frac{I_{\max}}{I_{\min}}\geq\frac{D_c}{D_0}.
$$

For unit object modulation, this means

$$
\boxed{M_c=\frac{D_c-D_0}{D_c+D_0}=\frac{100-20}{100+20}\simeq0.667.}
$$

The doses are graphical estimates, so extra significant figures would not represent greater measurement accuracy.

<h3 id="4/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#4/ii)

The printed aerial image has approximately $I_{\max}=2.0$ and $I_{\min}=0.5\ \mathrm{mW\,cm^{-2}}$. Thus its modulation, and its modulation transfer for a fully modulated object, is

$$
\boxed{M=\frac{I_{\max}-I_{\min}}{I_{\max}+I_{\min}}
=\frac{2.0-0.5}{2.0+0.5}=0.60.}
$$

**The image does not meet the full-thickness printing criterion**, because $0.60<0.667$. Equivalently, clearing the bright regions requires $t\geq100/2=50$ s, while preserving the full thickness in the dark regions requires $t\leq20/0.5=40$ s. There is no common exposure time.

Partial thickness modulation may remain visible, but that is different from a clean grating with fully cleared spaces and retained full-thickness lines. Small ripple peaks in the source drawing do not change this conclusion.

<h3 id="4/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#4/iii)

For a spatially coherent, on-axis source, the coherent spatial-frequency cutoff is

$$
f_c=\frac{\mathrm{NA}}\lambda.
$$

A line-space grating must place at least its first diffracted orders within the pupil. Its limiting pitch is therefore $\lambda/\mathrm{NA}$, and its equal line/space half-pitch is

$$
\boxed{a_{\min}=\frac{\lambda}{2\,\mathrm{NA}}
=\frac{193}{2(1.35)}\ \mathrm{nm}\simeq71.5\ \mathrm{nm}.}
$$

The corresponding full pitch is **143 nm**. Explicitly distinguishing pitch from half-pitch avoids a factor-of-two ambiguity in the phrase “feature size.” This is an ideal optical cutoff estimate; finite resist contrast and process requirements can make the usable feature size larger.

<h3 id="4/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#4/iv)

Decreasing the source diameter increases [spatial optical coherence](../../physical-optics.md#spatial-optical-coherence) and moves the illumination towards the coherent on-axis limit. Here the grating frequency is only

$$
f=1/(800\ \mathrm{nm}),\qquad
\frac f{f_c}=\frac{193}{1.35(800)}\simeq0.179.
$$

The zero and first orders are comfortably within the pupil for on-axis illumination. Reducing an extended source can prevent some off-axis source points from losing [diffraction](../../quantum-scattering.md#diffraction) orders at the pupil edge, and can therefore **increase the grating modulation, or leave it approximately unchanged if the relevant orders were already fully passed**. It need not give a universal numerical increase: higher orders, source shape, [defocus](../../point-spread-function.md#defocus-aberration) and [optical aberrations](../../geometrical-optics.md#optical-aberration) matter for a binary grating.

The resist's $\gamma$ and critical modulation $M_c$ in part (i) are material/process properties and **do not change merely because the source becomes smaller**. What may change is part (ii)'s comparison between the actual image modulation and $M_c$. The important change is **preorganization of the chloride-binding [amide](../../chemical-substance.md#amide) groups**. In the hydroxylated host, intramolecular [hydrogen bonds](../../chemical-bond.md#hydrogen-bond) from the phenolic OH groups to the [amide](../../chemical-substance.md#amide) [carbonyl](../../chemical-bond.md#carbonyl-group) [oxygens](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) restrict rotation and favor a geometry in which the [amide](../../chemical-substance.md#amide) NH donors converge on the anion-binding cavity. [chloride](../../chemistry.md#chloride) can therefore accept two favorable [hydrogen bonds](../../chemical-bond.md#hydrogen-bond) with a smaller conformational penalty than in the unsubstituted host. The hydroxyl groups need not be counted as two additional freely available [chloride](../../chemistry.md#chloride) donors: their role includes organizing the existing donors.

The [association constant](../../chemical-potential.md#association-constant) increases by $5230/195=26.8$. At $298\,\mathrm K$, the corresponding difference in standard [Gibbs free energy](../../thermodynamic-potential.md#gibbs-free-energy) is

$$
\Delta\Delta G^\circ=-RT\ln(5230/195)\simeq\boxed{-8.15\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}}.
$$

This is a modest molecular free-energy advantage amplified into a substantial change in the [equilibrium constant](../../chemical-potential.md#equilibrium-constant). [solvent](../../mixture.md#solvent) competition and changes in [solvation](../../ionic-bonding.md#solvation) also contribute to measured binding; the structures chiefly suggest the preorganization explanation.

The [molecule](../../molecule.md) combines three chemically different binding regions.

- **[sodium](../../chemical-substance.md#sodium): the oxygen-rich [calixarene](../../chemical-substance.md#calixarene)/[polyether](../../polymer.md#polyether) region**, including the [ether](../../chemical-substance.md#ether) [oxygens](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) and suitably oriented [ester](../../chemical-substance.md#ester) [carbonyl](../../chemical-bond.md#carbonyl-group) [oxygens](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). The scaffold holds several donors near one another and can reduce the conformational cost of binding.
- **[nitrate](../../chemistry.md#nitrate): the two [urea](../../chemical-substance.md#urea) groups.** Their NH groups donate [hydrogen bonds](../../chemical-bond.md#hydrogen-bond) to [nitrate](../../chemistry.md#nitrate) [oxygens](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). Several NH donors can cooperate around an [anion](../../chemistry.md#anion); the [carbonyl](../../chemical-bond.md#carbonyl-group) [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) of a [urea](../../chemical-substance.md#urea) is an acceptor and is not the proposed nitrate-binding donor.
- **[silver](../../chemical-substance.md#silver): the terminal bipyridyl nitrogen donors.** The [pyridine](../../chemical-substance.md#pyridine) lone pairs coordinate the softer [silver](../../chemical-substance.md#silver) [ion](../../chemistry.md#ion). The actual coordination may involve donor sites on more than one arm or more than one receptor, depending on geometry and [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration).

These assignments identify the preferred local sites, rather than guaranteeing three independent binding constants: occupancy can alter [conformation](../../stereochemistry.md#conformation), electrostatics and the affinity of neighboring sites.

Each [imidazole](../../chemical-substance.md#imidazole) already has one nitrogen substituted. Its remaining pyridine-like nitrogen attacks a benzylic [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) bearing chlorine in an $S_\mathrm N2$ reaction, expelling [chloride](../../chemistry.md#chloride) and producing an [imidazolium](../../molecule.md#imidazolium) center. The expected product is therefore **a dicationic bis-imidazolium [macrocycle](../../chemical-substance.md#macrocycle)**, in which two meta-xylylene bridges connect the two [imidazolium](../../molecule.md#imidazolium) rings. Both nitrogens of each [imidazolium](../../molecule.md#imidazolium) are alkylated; the aryl rings remain intact.

A compact way to specify its connectivity is to traverse the ring:

$$
\mathrm{m\!\!-!C_6H_4-CH_2-N(imidazolium)-CH_2-
 m\!\!-!C_6H_4-CH_2-N(imidazolium)-CH_2-}\quad\text{back to the first aryl group}.

Here each indicated [imidazolium](../../molecule.md#imidazolium) connects its two substituents through its two different ring nitrogen atoms, rather than through a single nitrogen.

The species that oxidizes [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel) is **[gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold)(III) [ion](../../chemistry.md#ion)**, rather than metallic [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold). The [half-reactions](../../electrochemistry.md#half-reaction) are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Ni}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}+2e^-,\\
\mathrm{Au^{3+}}+3e^-&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Au}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Multiplying the first by three and the second by two gives

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{3Ni+2Au^{3+}\longrightarrow3Ni^{2+}+2Au}}.
$$

Standard [electrode potentials](../../electrochemistry.md#electrode-potential) are not multiplied by the balancing factors. Thus $E^\circ_{\rm cell}=1.50-(-0.25)=1.75\,\mathrm V$ and six [moles](../../physics.md#mole-unit) of [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) are transferred per [mole](../../physics.md#mole-unit) of this balanced reaction:

$$
\boxed{\Delta G^\circ=-6FE^\circ_{\rm cell}\simeq-1.01\times10^6\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}}.
$$

The negative sign predicts a thermodynamically favorable [galvanic replacement](../../electrochemistry.md#galvanic-replacement) under standard conditions.

<h3 id="6/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#6/ii)

Clean and degrease the [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel) surface, remove its inhibiting [oxide](../../chemical-substance.md#oxide) by an appropriate brief surface treatment, rinse, and immerse it in an aqueous bath containing reducible [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold)(III) species. Control [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration), [temperature](../../temperature.md) and immersion time, then rinse after the desired deposition. A solution of a [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold) complex is practical, but its actual [reduction](../../chemical-reaction.md#reduction-chemistry) potential must be evaluated for that complex and bath: it need not equal the supplied free-ion standard potential.

At anodic sites [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel) dissolves as $\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}$, releasing [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) into the conducting substrate. Those [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) reduce [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold) [ions](../../chemistry.md#ion) at cathodic sites, nucleating and growing metallic [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold). The [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel) itself is the reducing agent, so **no external electrical supply or separate reducing reagent is required**. The coupled [electron](../../physics.md#electron) balance is three [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel) atoms dissolved for two [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold) atoms deposited for the stated [oxidation states](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state).

The process can slow or stop when a sufficiently continuous [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold) coating prevents the [electrolyte](../../electrochemistry.md#electrolyte) from reaching [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel). This is not an absolute thickness rule: pores, cracks or exposed edges preserve [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel) dissolution and allow continued deposition, sometimes with undercutting. [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel) [passivation](../../electrochemistry.md#passivation), depletion of [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold) [ions](../../chemistry.md#ion) near the surface, [diffusion](../../thermodynamics.md#diffusion) through a porous layer, bath speciation and competing reactions also affect the rate. Dense [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold) coverage and porous [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold) coverage therefore have very different limiting behavior.

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

Use a shaped [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel) particle or [nanowire](../../engineering.md#nanowire) as both **sacrificial template and reducing agent**. [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold) grows where [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel) is consumed, allowing gold-coated particles, porous shells and, with suitable removal of the remaining [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel), hollow [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold) [nanostructures](../../engineering.md#nanostructure). Controlled composition and transport can give a progression from core–shell to porous or hollow structures. This is useful for high-surface-area [electrodes](../../electrochemistry.md#electrode), catalytic surfaces and plasmonic structures, while avoiding an electrical lead to every individual [nanoparticle](../../engineering.md#nanoparticle).

<h3 id="7/i">i</h3>

<h4 id="7/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#7/i)

Three standard approaches are **piezoelectric, electromagnetic and [electrostatic energy harvesting](../../energy.md#electrostatic-energy-harvesting)**.

- A piezoelectric microcantilever or [nanowire](../../engineering.md#nanowire) develops [electric polarization](../../electromagnetism.md#polarization-density) when strained. An inertial mass, bending substrate or vibration bends the element; [electrodes](../../electrochemistry.md#electrode) and a rectifier collect charge. [PZT](../../chemical-substance.md#lead-zirconate-titanate), ZnO and [PVDF](../../polymer.md#polyvinylidene-fluoride) illustrate different material choices. This approach needs no external bias for transduction and can give appreciable [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) in a compact element. Its [capacitance](../../electromagnetism.md#capacitance) gives high source impedance and relatively small [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current); brittle materials, fatigue, depolarization and a narrow mechanical [resonance](../../dynamical-systems.md#resonance) can limit performance.
- Electromagnetic harvesting uses relative motion of a magnet and coil to change [magnetic flux](../../magnetic-field.md#magnetic-flux), producing $\mathcal E=-N\,d\Phi/dt$. A micromachined coil and movable magnet can implement it at the microscale. It can offer low source impedance and useful [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current), but the small flux and coil area make [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) and [power](../../energy.md#power) increasingly difficult to maintain on miniaturization; coil resistance and fabrication of magnets also matter. A practical microscale device does not imply efficient scaling all the way to individual [nanowires](../../engineering.md#nanowire).
- Electrostatic harvesting uses a variable [capacitor](../../electromagnetism.md#capacitor), for example [MEMS](../../engineering.md#microelectromechanical-system) comb fingers or moving plates. Mechanical motion changes [capacitance](../../electromagnetism.md#capacitance); an initial charge, electrical bias or [electret](../../permittivity.md#electret) allows this change to do electrical work. Small gaps favor strong coupling and compatibility with microfabrication. Limitations include the need for charge or bias, parasitic [capacitance](../../electromagnetism.md#capacitance), leakage, pull-in, stiction and [dielectric breakdown](../../permittivity.md#dielectric-breakdown).

In all three cases the output is constrained by the available mechanical input and damping. Nanoscale dimensions alone do not supply an [energy](../../energy.md) advantage.

<h3 id="7/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

<h4 id="7/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#7/ii)

A [thermocouple](../../thermoelectric-effect.md#thermocouple) uses **two materials with different [Seebeck coefficients](../../thermoelectric-effect.md#seebeck-coefficient)**. With their junctions at different [temperatures](../../temperature.md), the net [open-circuit voltage](../../electrical-engineering.md#open-circuit-voltage) is

$$
V_{\rm oc}=\int_{T_c}^{T_h}(\alpha_p-\alpha_n)\,dT
\simeq(\alpha_p-\alpha_n)\Delta T.
$$

Using p-type and n-type legs makes the two contributions add. Many pairs can be connected electrically in series to add [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) while remaining thermally in parallel between the same hot and cold plates. A single homogeneous wire does not provide this same complete-circuit [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) when its return lead has the same [thermopower](../../thermoelectric-effect.md#seebeck-coefficient).

The reciprocal [Peltier effect](../../thermoelectric-effect.md#peltier-effect) transports heat when [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) passes through a junction. The Kelvin relation $\Pi=\alpha T$ connects the heat carried per unit charge to the [Seebeck coefficient](../../thermoelectric-effect.md#seebeck-coefficient). The same combination of large [thermopower](../../thermoelectric-effect.md#seebeck-coefficient), high [electrical conductivity](../../electric-charge.md#electrical-conductivity) and low [thermal conductivity](../../heat.md#thermal-conductivity) gives a favorable dimensionless figure of merit

$$
ZT=\frac{\alpha^2\sigma T}{\kappa}.
$$

Thus materials useful for a generator are also useful for a cooler when [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) is driven in the appropriate direction. [Joule heating](../../electric-charge.md#joule-heating) and thermal leakage oppose cooling; device geometry, contact resistance and operating [temperature](../../temperature.md) must still be appropriate. Reversibility of the thermoelectric coupling does not make either device lossless.

<h3 id="7/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

<h4 id="7/iii/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#7/iii)

<h5 id="7/iii/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#7/iii/a)

A pyroelectric material has a spontaneous [electric polarization](../../electromagnetism.md#polarization-density) that changes with [temperature](../../temperature.md). [electrodes](../../electrochemistry.md#electrode) collect the resulting change in compensating charge. For a uniform element,

$$
\Delta Q=A p\Delta T,\qquad i=A p\frac{dT}{dt}.
$$

Consequently **[temperature](../../temperature.md) variation in time**, rather than a steady [temperature](../../temperature.md) difference alone, drives a [pyroelectric generator](../../electromagnetism.md#pyroelectric-generator). A cyclic thermal input and a suitable electrical circuit are needed for repeated [energy](../../energy.md) extraction.

The [primary pyroelectric effect](../../electromagnetism.md#primary-pyroelectric-effect) is the change of [electric polarization](../../electromagnetism.md#polarization-density) at fixed [strain](../../continuum-mechanics.md#strain). The [secondary pyroelectric effect](../../electromagnetism.md#secondary-pyroelectric-effect) arises because [thermal expansion](../../thermodynamics.md#thermal-expansion) [strains](../../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) a piezoelectric crystal and thereby changes its [electric polarization](../../electromagnetism.md#polarization-density). In component notation its contribution is

$$
p_i^{\rm secondary}=\sum_j e_{ij}\alpha_j,\qquad
p_i^{\rm free}=p_i^{\rm clamped}+p_i^{\rm secondary},
$$

with piezoelectric coefficients $e_{ij}$ and thermal-expansion coefficients $\alpha_j$. Choosing orientation, mechanical constraint and substrate expansion so that the secondary contribution reinforces the primary one can increase the response; the signs can instead oppose one another.

An array of slender [nanowires](../../engineering.md#nanowire) can expand more freely than a film clamped to a substrate and can exchange heat rapidly because of its high [surface-to-volume ratio](../../geometry-and-topology.md#surface-to-volume-ratio). Reduced clamping can recover a secondary response suppressed in the film. Faster thermal cycling can also increase [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) through $dT/dt$. This is a geometrical opportunity rather than a universal increase of the intrinsic coefficient: screening, leakage, orientation and the actual thermal excursion must be compared. ZnO [nanowire](../../engineering.md#nanowire) generators provide an experimental realization of pyroelectric [energy](../../energy.md) conversion.[Abstract: vertically aligned ZnO nanowire arrays used for pyroelectric generation](../../physics-bibliography.md#pyroelectric-nanogenerators-for-harvesting-thermoelectric-energy-by-ya-yang-and-others)

<h4 id="7/iii/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#7/iii)

<h5 id="7/iii/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#7/iii/b)

Treat the film as a [parallel-plate capacitor](../../electromagnetism.md#parallel-plate-capacitor), initially compensated and then isolated electrically. Its [capacitance](../../electromagnetism.md#capacitance) and temperature-induced [electric polarization](../../electromagnetism.md#polarization-density) charge are

$$
C=\frac{\epsilon_0\epsilon A}{d},\qquad\Delta Q=A p\Delta T.
$$

The corresponding ideal open-circuit signal, with the [electrode](../../electrochemistry.md#electrode) polarity defined consistently with this charge convention, is

$$
\boxed{V_{\rm oc}=\frac{\Delta Q}{C}=\frac{p\,d\,\Delta T}{\epsilon_0\epsilon}}.
$$

Area cancels because both charge and [capacitance](../../electromagnetism.md#capacitance) are proportional to it. Substitution gives

$$
V_{\rm oc}=\frac{(-35\times10^{-6})(2\times10^{-6})(1)}{(8.854\times10^{-12})(8)}
=\boxed{-0.988\,\mathrm V}.
$$

The [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) magnitude is approximately **$0.99\,\mathrm V$**; interchanging the voltmeter leads reverses its sign. This is an ideal capacitive result, before leakage, mobile-charge screening or loading by the measurement circuit reduces the signal.

Atomic-number contrast can explain why the gold-rich regions are bright and why the phosphorus-rich NiP$_y$ layer should be somewhat darker than the other [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel)–[phosphorus](../../chemical-substance.md#phosphorus) layers. However, the calculated alloy yields differ only modestly: the NiP$_y$ and NiP$_z$ yields differ by about $5.5\%$ relative to NiP$_z$. **Composition-only backscattering does not explain all of the strong local edge and defect contrast.**

An [Everhart–Thornley detector](../../optics.md#everhart-thornley-detector) normally collects [secondary electrons](../../physics.md#secondary-electron), not a pure backscattered-electron signal. Secondary-electron escape and collection depend strongly on local slope and edge geometry. Differential polishing, relief at interfaces, a crack or cavity, surface [oxidation](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation) and local charging can therefore alter brightness without the corresponding change in bulk [atomic number](../../chemical-substance.md#atomic-number). [backscattered electrons](../../physics.md#backscattered-electron) can also generate [secondary electrons](../../physics.md#secondary-electron) elsewhere. Crystallographic channeling and orientation can contribute additional contrast. The observed dark and bright areas should therefore be correlated with topography and composition rather than assigned a phase from intensity alone.

The contact dimensions are readily accessible to **UV [photolithography](../../engineering.md#photolithography) followed by directional evaporation and lift-off**. A possible sequence is:

- Clean the [semiconductor](../../semiconductor.md) contact surface and coat it with a positive-tone lift-off resist stack. A bilayer giving a small undercut is useful. Soft-bake and align the mask to the intended mesa.
- Use a transparent annular opening in an otherwise opaque mask to expose the contact ring, leaving the central optical aperture covered by resist. Develop away the exposed positive resist. Contact or proximity exposure is adequate for the micrometre-scale ring; projection exposure also works.
- Evaporate the [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold) contact layer directionally onto the wafer. Keep the resist appreciably thicker than the metal and preserve the undercut so that metal on top of the resist does not bridge continuously to the desired ring. Evaporation is more convenient for lift-off than a strongly conformal coating.
- Dissolve the remaining resist and lift away its overlying metal. The annulus on the exposed [semiconductor](../../semiconductor.md) remains, while the central emitting aperture is free of metal.

A real device must also have appropriate **[ohmic contact](../../semiconductor.md#ohmic-contact) metallurgy**. [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold) alone on arbitrary GaAs is not automatically an [ohmic contact](../../semiconductor.md#ohmic-contact): surface preparation, heavy contact-layer doping and, where required, a suitable alloyed contact stack are needed. The schematic [gold](../../chemical-substance.md#gold) label specifies the visible [electrode](../../electrochemistry.md#electrode), not all such process details. With conventional [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) defined into the positive terminal, an ideal junction has

$$
I=I_0\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{qV}{n k_BT}\right)-1\right].
$$

The reverse-bias branch tends to **$-I_0$** before breakdown. In [forward bias](../../electrical-circuit.md#forward-bias) the [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) rises exponentially; a visibly sharp “turn-on” is a convenient operating description rather than a discontinuity in the equation. A powered [LED](../../electrical-circuit.md#light-emitting-diode) emits in **quadrant I**, where $V>0$, $I>0$ and the device absorbs electrical [power](../../energy.md#power) $VI$. Strong reverse breakdown lies outside the ideal curve below.

<h4 id="9/ii/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#9/ii)

<h5 id="9/ii/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#9/ii/b)

[forward bias](../../electrical-circuit.md#forward-bias) opposes the built-in [electric field](../../electric-field.md) and lowers the junction barrier from approximately $V_{\rm bi}$ to $V_{\rm bi}-V$. In the abrupt-junction [depletion approximation](../../semiconductor.md#depletion-approximation),

$$
W=\sqrt{\frac{2\epsilon_s}{q}\left(\frac1{N_A}+\frac1{N_D}\right)(V_{\rm bi}-V)}.
$$

Thus **the [depletion region](../../semiconductor.md#depletion-region) narrows** and carriers cross the reduced barrier more readily. [electron](../../physics.md#electron) injection into the p side and hole injection into the n side increase strongly, allowing [radiative recombination](../../atomic-physics.md#radiative-recombination) in an [LED](../../electrical-circuit.md#light-emitting-diode).

The injected minority-carrier [concentrations](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration), and therefore the ideal [diffusion](../../thermodynamics.md#diffusion) [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current), vary approximately as $\exp(qV/k_BT)$. This produces the steep forward branch of the [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current)–[voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) curve. One should not extrapolate the depletion-width formula to claim that the junction must lose its entire [depletion region](../../semiconductor.md#depletion-region) before conducting; appreciable [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) flows while a barrier remains, and high injection and series resistance eventually invalidate the simple formulas.

$$

The $-1$ term is negligible here. A different precise interpretation of room [temperature](../../temperature.md) changes the result noticeably because of the exponential; the calculation above states its [temperature](../../temperature.md) explicitly.

For a lower-bandgap material, the [intrinsic carrier concentration](../../semiconductor.md#intrinsic-carrier-concentration) generally rises, increasing the reverse saturation [current density](../../electric-charge.md#current-density), approximately through $J_0\propto n_i^2$ with $n_i^2\propto\exp(-E_g/k_BT)$ when other parameters are held comparable. The end groups associate reversibly through a complementary hydrogen-bond array. At low [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration), bringing the two ends of the same [molecule](../../molecule.md) together avoids the concentration-dependent cost of finding another [molecule](../../molecule.md), so an **intramolecular ring** is favored.

For A, increasing [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) permits end-to-end intermolecular association to compete successfully with ring closure. It produces long, reversible supramolecular chains. Increasing chain length and entanglement account for the highly viscous solution without requiring covalent [polymerization](../../polymer.md#polymerization).

B has more favorably positioned attachment points on its rigid aromatic core. Its arms can close a less strained ring, so intramolecular association remains strongly competitive over the stated range. Predominantly discrete rings do not form the same extended chain network, explaining the retained fluidity. Both compounds can make the same local [hydrogen bonds](../../chemical-bond.md#hydrogen-bond); their different balance of ring [strain](../../continuum-mechanics.md#strain), preorganization and intermolecular association explains the macroscopic difference.

$$

This is the stoichiometric minimum for one cell, or one cell-equivalent parallel assembly, with complete utilization and no self-discharge. Practical loading must be larger. If a pack uses several cells in series, each series cell must carry the full delivered charge and the total [lithium](../../chemical-substance.md#lithium) mass increases accordingly; no required pack [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) or series count is supplied.

<h4 id="11/ii/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#11/ii)

<h5 id="11/ii/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#11/ii/b)

The reaction consumes one [mole](../../physics.md#mole-unit) of CF for each [mole](../../physics.md#mole-unit) of [electrons](../../physics.md#electron). Using $M_{\rm CF}=12.01+19.00=31.01\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$,

$$
\boxed{m_{\rm CF}=\frac{630720}{96500}(31.01)=202.7\,\mathrm g}.
$$

This is active CF alone for the same single-cell-equivalent assumption, not the full positive-electrode or pack mass. Conductive additive, binder, [electrolyte](../../electrochemistry.md#electrolyte), [battery separator](../../electrical-circuit.md#battery-separator), collectors and casing add mass, and incomplete utilization increases the required CF.

<h4 id="11/ii/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#11/ii)

<h5 id="11/ii/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#11/ii/c)

For the $x=1$ reaction there is one [mole](../../physics.md#mole-unit) of transferred [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) per [mole](../../physics.md#mole-unit) of reaction. **Treating the supplied fresh-cell [open-circuit voltage](../../electrical-engineering.md#open-circuit-voltage) as the standard equilibrium cell potential** gives

$$
\boxed{\Delta G^\circ=-FE^\circ=-(96500)(4.57)=-441005\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}
\simeq-441\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}}.
$$

A fresh-cell [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) alone would not normally establish standard conditions or equilibrium. The result above is the formal standard-free-energy estimate under the interpretation required to use the given datum; a metastable [open-circuit voltage](../../electrical-engineering.md#open-circuit-voltage) should not be mistaken for an independently measured standard thermodynamic potential.

<h4 id="11/ii/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#11/ii)

<h5 id="11/ii/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#11/ii/d)

**This is a primary cell and should not be treated as rechargeable.** Discharge converts the fluorinated-carbon [electrode](../../electrochemistry.md#electrode) into [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) and [lithium](../../chemical-substance.md#lithium) fluoride. It does not simply insert [lithium](../../chemical-substance.md#lithium) into a host that can be reversibly deintercalated. Reversing [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) does not cleanly reconstruct the original distributed C–F bonding and [electrode](../../electrochemistry.md#electrode) microstructure.

Insulating LiF, loss of the original reaction interfaces and unwanted [electrolyte](../../electrochemistry.md#electrolyte) reactions obstruct reversibility. Replating [lithium](../../chemical-substance.md#lithium) can also produce an irregular metallic deposit and internal shorting. A thermodynamically possible formal reverse reaction is therefore not evidence of a practical rechargeable cell.

<h3 id="11/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [11](#11)

<h4 id="11/iii/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#11/iii)

<h5 id="11/iii/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#11/iii/a)

If the [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) were held at $4.57\,\mathrm V$ for the entire ideal discharge, then

$$
\boxed{E=VQ=(4.57)(175.2)=800.664\,\mathrm{Wh}=0.801\,\mathrm{kWh}},
$$

and the electrical [power](../../energy.md#power) would be

$$
\boxed{P=VI=(4.57)(0.010)=0.0457\,\mathrm W=45.7\,\mathrm{mW}}.
$$

These are **constant-voltage estimates**, not a determination of the actual [energy](../../energy.md) from the fresh-cell [open-circuit voltage](../../electrical-engineering.md#open-circuit-voltage). Under load the [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) depends on state of discharge, [electric polarization](../../electromagnetism.md#polarization-density) and internal resistance. The physical result is $E=I\int_0^{t_f}V_{\rm load}(t)\,dt$, and $P(t)=IV_{\rm load}(t)$. The supplied single open-circuit value does not specify that [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) curve; practical delivered [energy](../../energy.md) will generally be lower than the simple estimate.

<h4 id="11/iii/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#11/iii)

<h5 id="11/iii/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#11/iii/b)

Increase the **electrochemically accessible area and reduce transport resistance** rather than merely forcing more [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) through the same thick [electrode](../../electrochemistry.md#electrode). A thinner, porous composite distributed over a larger area, a conductive-carbon network, good [electrolyte](../../electrochemistry.md#electrolyte) wetting and short ionic paths reduce local [current density](../../electric-charge.md#current-density) and [electric polarization](../../electromagnetism.md#polarization-density). Adequate [battery separator](../../electrical-circuit.md#battery-separator) conductivity and low-resistance collectors also help. Parallel cells can distribute total [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) while retaining the total active-material inventory.

The objective is to preserve usable capacity at a higher rate by avoiding premature [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage) cutoff and inaccessible active material. It does not create more charge: a fixed inventory with $175.2\,\mathrm{Ah}$ delivers a greater [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) for a proportionally shorter time. Maintaining the original two-year duration at greater [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) requires proportionally more capacity.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 1](paper-1.md)

<h3 id="12/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [12](#12)

<h4 id="12/i/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#12/i)

<h5 id="12/i/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#12/i/a)

Absorbed [photons](../../quantum-mechanics.md#photon) with [energy](../../energy.md) at least the [band gap](../../bloch-s-theorem.md#band-gap) generate [electron](../../physics.md#electron)–hole pairs. The built-in field of the [p-n junction](../../semiconductor.md#p-n-junction) separates carriers generated in or reaching the [depletion region](../../semiconductor.md#depletion-region); [minority carriers](../../semiconductor.md#minority-carrier) generated nearby can diffuse into that collection region. Selective contacts carry [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) and holes into an external circuit before they recombine.

Define positive [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) into the positive-voltage terminal. In the ideal illuminated-diode model,

$$
I(V)=I_0\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{qV}{n k_BT}\right)-1\right]-I_{\rm ph}.
$$

The dark curve has $I_{\rm ph}=0$; illumination shifts it downward. At short circuit $I\simeq-I_{\rm ph}$; at open circuit $I=0$ and $V=V_{\rm oc}$. Between them the illuminated cell lies in **quadrant IV** and supplies [power](../../energy.md#power) $P_{\rm out}=-VI>0$. The [maximum power point](../../quantum-mechanics.md#maximum-power-point) maximizes the area of the rectangle with sides $V$ and $-I$:

$$
\boxed{\frac{d(-VI)}{dV}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad I+V\frac{dI}{dV}=0}.
$$

It is neither the open-circuit point nor the short-circuit point, both of which supply zero [power](../../energy.md#power).

<h4 id="12/i/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#12/i)

<h5 id="12/i/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#12/i/b)

At fixed spectrum and [temperature](../../temperature.md), photocurrent is approximately proportional to [irradiance](../../radiometry.md#irradiance), so the magnitude of [short-circuit current](../../quantum-mechanics.md#short-circuit-current) increases nearly linearly. In the ideal model,

$$
V_{\rm oc}=n\frac{k_BT}{q}\ln\!\left(1+\frac{I_{\rm ph}}{I_0}\right),
$$

so [open-circuit voltage](../../electrical-engineering.md#open-circuit-voltage) rises only logarithmically with [irradiance](../../radiometry.md#irradiance). More incident optical [power](../../energy.md#power) generally raises the maximum electrical [power](../../energy.md#power), within the device's thermal and resistive limits.

Increasing [temperature](../../temperature.md) strongly increases the dark saturation [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current). In [silicon](../../chemical-substance.md#silicon) this normally reduces [open-circuit voltage](../../electrical-engineering.md#open-circuit-voltage) and maximum [power](../../energy.md#power), despite a small increase in [short-circuit current](../../quantum-mechanics.md#short-circuit-current) associated with the [temperature](../../temperature.md) dependence of absorption and [band gap](../../bloch-s-theorem.md#band-gap). The thermal-voltage factor alone is insufficient to predict the sign; the [temperature](../../temperature.md) dependence of $I_0$ matters.

**[maximum power point tracking](../../quantum-mechanics.md#maximum-power-point-tracking) adjusts the load seen by the cell** as [irradiance](../../radiometry.md#irradiance) and [temperature](../../temperature.md) change. A controlled DC–DC converter can transform the external load so that operation stays near the changing maximum of $-VI$, instead of at the generally suboptimal intersection with a fixed load. It matches operating conditions and cannot generate additional [energy](../../energy.md) beyond the photovoltaic output.

<h3 id="12/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [12](#12)

<h4 id="12/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#12/ii)

The illuminated area and incident [power](../../energy.md#power) are

$$
A=(0.04)^2=1.6\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2},\qquad
P_{\rm in}=87A=0.1392\,\mathrm W.
$$

A $412\,\mathrm{nm}$ [photon](../../quantum-mechanics.md#photon) has [energy](../../energy.md) $hc/\lambda=4.821\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm J=3.01\,\mathrm{eV}$, above the [silicon](../../chemical-substance.md#silicon) [band gap](../../bloch-s-theorem.md#band-gap). With one collected [electron](../../physics.md#electron) per [photon](../../quantum-mechanics.md#photon),

$$
I_{\rm ph}=q\frac{P_{\rm in}\lambda}{hc}=0.0462562\,\mathrm A.
$$

Use the ideal-diode approximation with **ideality factor $n=1$**, negligible shunt leakage and the same dark [diode](../../electrical-circuit.md#diode) parameters under illumination. At $300\,\mathrm K$, $V_T=k_BT/q=0.025852\,\mathrm V$. The measured dark point then fixes

$$
I_0=\frac{10^{-4}}{\exp(0.466/V_T)-1}=1.4844\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm A.
$$

At open circuit the forward [diode](../../electrical-circuit.md#diode) [electric current](../../electric-charge.md#electric-current) balances the photocurrent:

$$
\boxed{V_{\rm oc}=V_T\ln\!\left(1+\frac{I_{\rm ph}}{I_0}\right)=0.62465\,\mathrm V\simeq0.625\,\mathrm V}.
$$

This is below the $1.1\,\mathrm{eV}$ [band gap](../../bloch-s-theorem.md#band-gap) expressed per elementary charge. Excess [photon](../../quantum-mechanics.md#photon) [energy](../../energy.md) does not translate directly into terminal [voltage](../../random-walk.md#voltage); much of it thermalizes.

The paper does not explicitly specify an ideality factor. If it is not taken as unity, one dark operating point cannot determine both $I_0$ and $n$. The corresponding family is

$$
V_{\rm oc}(n)=nV_T\ln\!\left[1+\frac{I_{\rm ph}}{10^{-4}}\left(\exp\!\left(\frac{0.466}{nV_T}\right)-1\right)\right].
$$

Thus $0.625\,\mathrm V$ is a stated-model estimate, not a parameter-free inference from that one measurement.
