↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In a **[vibrating-sample magnetometer](../../../../../../vibrating-sample-magnetometer.md)**, a magnetized specimen vibrates sinusoidally in a nominally uniform applied field. Its motion changes the magnetic flux through stationary pickup coils, producing an induced [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) by [Faraday's law of induction](../../../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md). The component at the vibration frequency is proportional to the sample [magnetic moment](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) times vibration amplitude and frequency, for a calibrated coil geometry.

A lock-in detector referenced to the vibration extracts this small signal. Calibrate with a known moment, stabilize the vibration amplitude and sweep the applied field to measure a [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) loop. Dividing moment by film volume gives $M$ in $\mathrm{A\,m^{-1}}$; the silicon substrate and holder background must be subtracted for such a thin film.[Section 4.1: pickup signal, vibration amplitude/frequency and synchronous detection](../../../../../../7300-series-vsm-system-user-s-manual-by-lake-shore-cryotronics.md)
