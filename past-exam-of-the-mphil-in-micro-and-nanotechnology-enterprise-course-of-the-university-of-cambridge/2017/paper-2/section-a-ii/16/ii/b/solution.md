<h1 id="section-a-ii/16/ii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Along the **in-plane hard axis**, the ideal uniform-magnetization model gives reversible rotation from the easy direction toward the applied field. With uniaxial anisotropy [energy](../../../../../../../energy-split.md) [density](../../../../../../../density.md) $K_u\sin^2\theta$, minimization gives

$$
\boxed{M_{\parallel H}=M_s\frac{H}{H_k}\quad(|H|\leq H_k),\qquad H_k=\frac{2K_u}{\mu_0M_s},}
$$

followed by saturation at $\pm M_s$. The right panel of the preceding figure therefore has a nearly linear central section, essentially zero remanence and ideally no [hysteresis](../../../../../../../hysteresis.md). Actual domains, misalignment and defects can produce rounding or a small loop. This is an in-plane hard axis; the much larger out-of-plane [demagnetizing field](../../../../../../../demagnetizing-field.md) is a different effect.
