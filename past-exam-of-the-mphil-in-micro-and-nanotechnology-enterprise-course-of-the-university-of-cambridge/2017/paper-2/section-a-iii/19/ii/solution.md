An **[exciton](../../../../../../exciton.md)** is a Coulomb-correlated electron-hole pair, electrically neutral overall. Three useful properties are:

- **[Exciton binding energy](../../../../../../exciton-binding-energy.md):** the [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) required to separate the correlated pair into unbound carriers. A larger value makes thermal dissociation less effective; [dielectric screening](../../../../../../dielectric-screening.md) and electronic delocalization influence it.
- **Spatial extent:** the characteristic electron-hole separation or [exciton](../../../../../../exciton.md) radius. A localized Frenkel-like [exciton](../../../../../../exciton.md) is small compared with a [Wannier-Mott exciton](../../../../../../wannier-mott-exciton.md) spread over many [unit cells](../../../../../../unit-cell.md).
- **Lifetime:** the characteristic time before radiative or nonradiative decay. Together with its [diffusion coefficient](../../../../../../diffusion-coefficient.md) it determines a transport scale, often expressed as $L_D=\sqrt{D_X\tau_X}$ in a specified diffusion-length convention.

In many molecular [organic semiconductors](../../../../../../organic-semiconductor.md), limited screening and localization make [exciton](../../../../../../exciton.md) binding important, so absorption need not immediately produce collectable free charges.
