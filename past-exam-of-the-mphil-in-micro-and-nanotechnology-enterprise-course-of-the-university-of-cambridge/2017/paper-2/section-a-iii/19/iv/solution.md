<h1 id="section-a-iii/19/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Relative to conventional crystalline-silicon technology, many organic absorbers offer low-temperature thin-film processing and lightweight flexible devices, but have more strongly bound [excitons](../../../../../../exciton.md) and more disorder-limited charge transport. These are common material trends, not universal statements about every organic or inorganic device.

Relevant [semiconductor](../../../../../../semiconductor-split.md) properties include the absorption spectrum and optical gap, donor/acceptor [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) levels and offsets, [dielectric screening](../../../../../../dielectric-screening.md) and [exciton](../../../../../../exciton.md) dissociation, electron/hole mobilities, [recombination](../../../../../../recombination-cosmology.md) lifetimes, traps and morphological stability. Contacts must extract the appropriate carrier selectively. These properties determine the device quantities in

$$
\boxed{\eta=\frac{J_{\rm sc}V_{\rm oc}\,\mathrm{FF}}{P_{\rm in}}.}
$$

A favourable gap alone does not fix efficiency: current generation, [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) loss and fill factor must all be controlled.
