<h1 id="section-b/22/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Ignoring access effects and wall charge, the cylindrical conducting liquid has

$$
\boxed{R=\frac{\ell}{\sigma\pi r^2}=\frac{100\times10^{-9}}{1.25\pi(5\times10^{-9})^2}=1.02\times10^9\,\Omega.}
$$

The corresponding conductance is approximately $0.982\,\mathrm{nS}$. A charged surface can contribute mobile [counterions](../../../../../../counterion.md) and change this estimate. Surface conduction is important when its conductance is comparable with the bulk contribution: for surface conductance $K_s$, their ratio in a cylinder is $2K_s/(\sigma r)$, twice the [Dukhin number](../../../../../../dukhin-number.md) convention $K_s/(\sigma r)$. Double-layer overlap, when the [Debye length](../../../../../../debye-length.md) is comparable with the pore radius, is another warning that uniform bulk conductivity is inadequate. No surface-charge or ionic-strength data are supplied to quantify those corrections.
