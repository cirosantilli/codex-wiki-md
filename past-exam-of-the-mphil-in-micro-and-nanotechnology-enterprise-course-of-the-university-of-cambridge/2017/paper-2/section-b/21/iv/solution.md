<h1 id="section-b/21/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The intended **double-barrier resonant-tunnelling diode** has a bias-dependent well level. Initially increasing bias can bring a quasi-bound level into alignment with available occupied emitter states, sharply increasing current. At larger bias the resonant level moves out of the effective injection window, for example below the emitter conduction-band edge, so resonant transmission and current decrease despite increasing [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md). This produces a peak-to-valley interval with **$dI/dV<0$**. Further bias can increase nonresonant or higher-level current again.

The right panel of the preceding figure is a qualitative example. The device can supply small-signal negative differential conductance to an appropriate oscillator circuit while its current and [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) remain positive. A bias-independent transmission function alone does not guarantee NDR: the voltage-dependent potential profile, contacts and carrier supply must be included. The source's reference to part (ii) appears to point to the earlier barrier matching; the resonant device mechanism uses the added barrier of part (iii).
