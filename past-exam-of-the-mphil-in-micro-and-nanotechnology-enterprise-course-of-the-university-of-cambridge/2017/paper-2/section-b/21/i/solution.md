<h1 id="section-b/21/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take $0<E<V_0$ and equal electron [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) in all regions. Define $k=\sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$ and $\kappa=\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm I:&\quad-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''=E\psi,\qquad\psi_I=Ae^{ikx}+Be^{-ikx},\\
\mathrm {II}:&\quad-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''+V_0\psi=E\psi,\qquad\psi_{II}=Ce^{\kappa x}+De^{-\kappa x},\\
\mathrm {III}:&\quad-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''=E\psi,\qquad\psi_{III}=Fe^{ikx}.
\end{aligned}
$$

There is no wave incident from the far right. Region I contains oscillatory incident and reflected waves; region II is **evanescent**, with real exponentials rather than propagating [plane waves](../../../../../../plane-wave.md). Both exponential terms are allowed in a finite barrier; deleting the growing term before boundary matching is generally incorrect. A complex superposition in the barrier can carry a nonzero conserved tunnelling current.
