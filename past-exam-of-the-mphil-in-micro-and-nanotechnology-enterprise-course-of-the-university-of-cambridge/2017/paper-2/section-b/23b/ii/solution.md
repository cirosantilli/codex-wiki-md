<h1 id="section-b/23b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For radius $a=10\,\mathrm{nm}$ and gold [density](../../../../../../density.md) $2.0\times10^4\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, $m=8.38\times10^{-20}\,\mathrm{kg}$. The supplied Stokes model gives $\zeta=3.96\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$ and $D=1.05\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$ at $300\,\mathrm K$. Therefore the standard one-second diffusive rms distances are **$45.7\,\mu\mathrm m$ in one coordinate** and **$79.2\,\mu\mathrm m$ in three dimensions**.

Using the source's supplied shorthand literally with $m\langle v^2\rangle=3k_BT$ instead gives $\sqrt{3k_BTt/\zeta}=56.0\,\mu\mathrm m$. That shorthand lacks the factor two in the standard long-time mean-square displacement; it should not be identified with either of the preceding standard rms conventions. None of these rms values is a cumulative path length.

An ideal drag-free fall from rest gives **$gt^2/2=4.91\,\mathrm m$**. Actual gravitational settling in the assumed viscous model is much smaller: $\tau_p=m/\zeta=2.12\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm s$, $u_t=mg/\zeta=0.208\,\mu\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, and displacement in one second is approximately **$0.208\,\mu\mathrm m$**. Thus [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion.md) overwhelms settling, even though ideal vacuum fall exceeds the rms [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion.md) distance. For [rarefied-gas nanoparticle drag](../../../../../../rarefied-gas-nanoparticle-drag.md), at a 20-nm size in argon, continuum no-slip drag may require a rarefaction/slip correction; pressure and gas mean-free-path data are absent, so these numerical transport estimates are conditional on the supplied Stokes model.
