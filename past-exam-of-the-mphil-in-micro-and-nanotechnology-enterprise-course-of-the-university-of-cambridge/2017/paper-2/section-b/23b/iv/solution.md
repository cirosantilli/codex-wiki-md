<h1 id="section-b/23b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Interpreting the nominal size as diameter $d=10\,\mathrm{nm}$, fluid viscous dominance requires

$$
\boxed{U\ll\frac{\eta}{\rho_f d}=\frac{6.0\times10^4}{\rho_f}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},}
$$

with $\rho_f$ in $\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. **The liquid [density](../../../../../../density.md) is not supplied**, so the conventional fluid-Reynolds crossover is not numerically fixed. As an explicitly additional illustrative assumption, $\rho_f=870\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ gives $\mathrm{Re}=1$ near $69\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; ordinary much slower [nanoparticle](../../../../../../nanoparticle.md) motion is well within the viscous regime.

If instead the supplied gold [density](../../../../../../density.md) is inserted into the dimensionless ratio $\rho_pUd/\eta$, that ratio reaches one at **$3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$**. It is not the fluid [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md). Including the spherical Stokes prefactor, the particle relaxation criterion $\tau_pU/d=1$ occurs at $54\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. These quantify different inertia comparisons. The wording's speed direction is reversed: viscous dominance is obtained by making speed sufficiently **small**. If 10 nm denotes radius, the length-based thresholds halve.
