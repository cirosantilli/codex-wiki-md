**[Brownian motion](../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md)** is the fluctuating motion of a suspended particle produced by thermal molecular collisions. Using the standard [Brownian displacement conventions](../../../../../../brownian-displacement-conventions.md), in a uniform fluid without external drift, mean displacement is zero but mean-square displacement grows with time. For overdamped [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion.md), $\langle\Delta x^2\rangle=2Dt$ in one coordinate and $\langle|\Delta\mathbf r|^2\rangle=6Dt$ in three dimensions, with $D=k_BT/\zeta$ and Stokes friction $\zeta=6\pi\eta a$ for a no-slip sphere.

Brownian transport brings [nanoparticles](../../../../../../nanoparticle.md) into encounters and allows them to explore configurations during [self-assembly](../../../../../../self-assembly.md). Reversible interactions permit rearrangement toward low-free-energy structures; interactions that are too strong can instead trap disordered aggregates. [Thermal fluctuations](../../../../../../thermal-fluctuation.md) also oppose localization in shallow traps. The symbol $\zeta$ avoids confusing friction with an unrelated chemical potential.
