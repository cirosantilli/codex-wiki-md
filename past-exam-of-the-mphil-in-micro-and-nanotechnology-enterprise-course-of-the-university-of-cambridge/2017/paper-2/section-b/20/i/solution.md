<h1 id="section-b/20/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In floating-catalyst CNT growth, a controlled [sulfur](../../../../../../sulfur.md) additive acts as a **growth promoter** for the [catalyst](../../../../../../catalyst.md), commonly iron. [Sulfur](../../../../../../sulfur.md) modifies [catalyst](../../../../../../catalyst.md) surface composition, carbon uptake and the strength of the catalyst-carbon interaction; it can facilitate cap formation/lift-off and sustained tube growth instead of complete [catalyst](../../../../../../catalyst.md) encapsulation. It also changes catalyst-particle evolution and hence tube yield, diameter and wall-count distributions.

There is an optimum range: excessive [sulfur](../../../../../../sulfur.md) can occupy active sites or form an insufficiently active sulfide-rich [catalyst](../../../../../../catalyst.md) and reduce growth. The additive is not simply a constituent that must be incorporated into every carbon wall. Its detailed action depends on [catalyst](../../../../../../catalyst.md), carbon source and process conditions; a single universal melting-point explanation is insufficient.[First-principles evidence for [sulfur](../../../../../../sulfur.md) weakening iron-carbon binding; later mechanistic support, not an official answer](../../../../../../the-promoter-role-of-sulfur-in-carbon-nanotube-growth-by-balazs-orban-and-tibor-holtzl.md)
