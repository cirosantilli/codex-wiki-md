<h1 id="section-a-i/13/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In [cluster-beam deposition](../../../../../../cluster-beam-deposition.md), evaporated or sputtered cobalt atoms cool in an aggregation gas, nucleate clusters and are carried to a substrate. Source [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), gas pressure, aggregation length and residence time tune the mean size and distribution. A [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) selector can narrow the delivered size range, at the cost of transmitted flux; an unfiltered beam does not automatically produce monodisperse particles.

Shape control is less direct. Growth kinetics and cluster [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) can favour particular structures, while multiple morphologies can coexist at one size. Landing [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md), substrate interactions and post-deposition annealing can change the delivered shape or cause coalescence. Soft landing helps preserve gas-phase clusters. In solution, [ligands](../../../../../../ligand-biochemistry.md), selective facet binding, seed-mediated growth and precursor feeding can give stronger chemical control over shape and size, but introduce [ligand](../../../../../../ligand-biochemistry.md)/solvent residues and require purification. **Neither method guarantees a unique shape merely by selecting particle size.**
