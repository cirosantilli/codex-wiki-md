A **[decahedral nanoparticle](../../../../../../decahedral-nanoparticle.md)** is a [multiply twinned nanoparticle](../../../../../../multiply-twinned-nanoparticle.md): five fcc-like tetrahedral domains share a common fivefold axis and meet on coherent twin planes. The angle of an ideal fcc tetrahedral wedge is $\arccos(1/3)=70.53^\circ$, so five wedges leave about **$7.36^\circ$ of angular deficit**. Elastic distortion and defects accommodate closure. The external shape can be a pentagonal bipyramid or a truncated variant; it is not one unstrained, periodically repeated fcc crystal.

An **[icosahedral nanoparticle](../../../../../../icosahedral-nanoparticle.md)** comprises twenty tetrahedral domains arranged around a common centre, with twenty approximately triangular outer facets, twelve vertices and thirty edges. Five domains meet around each radial fivefold direction. Coherent twinning exposes many low-energy close-packed facets, but fivefold symmetry cannot tile ordinary three-dimensional space periodically without strain. The competition between favourable surface/twin energies and volume-dependent elastic strain explains why such morphologies are especially plausible for small particles.
