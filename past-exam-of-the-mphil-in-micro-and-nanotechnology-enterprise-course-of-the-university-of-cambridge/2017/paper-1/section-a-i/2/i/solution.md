<h1 id="section-a-i/2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Model the [AFM cantilever](../../../../../../afm-cantilever.md) as a driven [damped harmonic oscillator](../../../../../../damped-harmonic-oscillator.md). If the drive stays at the free-space [resonance](../../../../../../resonance.md) and the [spring constant](../../../../../../spring-constant.md) $k$ and effective [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) $m$ are unchanged, its steady amplitude is

$$
A=\frac{F_d}{\sqrt{(k-m\omega^2)^2+(\gamma\omega)^2}},\qquad A(\omega_0)=\frac{F_d}{(\gamma_0+\gamma_{\rm ts})\omega_0}.
$$

Here $\gamma_0$ is intrinsic damping and $\gamma_{\rm ts}$ the added dissipative interaction with the surface. At large average separation there is no tapping, so $\gamma_{\rm ts}\simeq0$ and $A=A_{\rm free}$. As the lower turning point reaches the surface, intermittent contacts dissipate [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) and the amplitude decreases. With further approach the oscillation becomes strongly constrained, eventually approaching a continuously contacting, nearly quenched response. **The damping-only sketch has a free-amplitude plateau followed by a descending amplitude on approach.** The rounded transition in the sketch is illustrative; [adhesion](../../../../../../adhesion.md) and actual contact mechanics determine its shape. The deliberately omitted force-gradient-induced [resonance](../../../../../../resonance.md) shift can make real approach curves more complicated.
