<h1 id="section-a-ii/4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Approximate each source as localized, keep the angular factors unchanged and use inverse-square flux dilution. The increased distance gives

$$
R_{120}\simeq R_{80}\left(\frac{80}{120}\right)^2=1.54\frac49=\boxed{0.684\,\mathrm{nm\,s^{-1}}}.
$$

This carries the composition/thickness convention of the previous answer. A greater source distance reduces fractional distance and angle variation across a given substrate, improving composition uniformity at the cost of rate. A real [magnetron sputtering](../../../../../../magnetron-sputtering.md) target is extended, with an angular emission distribution, and gas scattering can change the distance law; this is an estimate rather than a calibrated prediction.
