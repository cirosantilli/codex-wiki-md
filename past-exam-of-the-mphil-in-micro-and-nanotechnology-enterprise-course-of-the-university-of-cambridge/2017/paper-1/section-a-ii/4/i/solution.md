<h1 id="section-a-ii/4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Assume that each target's incorporated flux is proportional to its applied [power](../../../../../../power.md) over this operating range, and that the same composition convention is used throughout. The calibration gives $F_A/F_B=0.35/0.65$ at equal [powers](../../../../../../power.md). Holding the A target unchanged while making the two incorporated fluxes equal therefore requires

$$
\boxed{P_B=30\frac{0.35}{0.65}=16.15\,\mathrm W}.
$$

The B [power](../../../../../../power.md) decreases because B originally supplies more material. This linear [magnetron sputtering](../../../../../../magnetron-sputtering.md) approximation neglects power-dependent yield, target condition, resputtering and changes in sticking probability. A second composition measurement is the experimental check.
