<h1 id="section-a-ii/4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In the intended additive-deposition model, A originally contributes $0.35\times2.2=0.77\,\mathrm{nm\,s^{-1}}$. At equal incorporated contributions, B also supplies $0.77\,\mathrm{nm\,s^{-1}}$, giving **$1.54\,\mathrm{nm\,s^{-1}}$**. Equivalently, the new total incorporated flux is $0.35+0.35=0.70$ times the calibrated total.

There is a composition-convention qualification. If the percentages represent [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) flux and the [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) stays fixed, the preceding thickness calculation follows directly. If they instead represent atomic fractions, a constant atomic number density would justify the same result, whereas constant [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) alone does not. With atomic [masses](../../../../../../mass.md) $M_A,M_B$ and constant [mass density](../../../../../../density.md), the ratio would be

$$
\frac{R_{\rm new}}{R_{\rm old}}=\frac{0.35(M_A+M_B)}{0.35M_A+0.65M_B}.
$$

The PDF does not specify atomic versus [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) percentages or the two elements. Thus **1.54 nm/s is the intended common flux-to-thickness approximation**, and the formula above states the additional dependence if atomic fraction and constant [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) are meant literally.
