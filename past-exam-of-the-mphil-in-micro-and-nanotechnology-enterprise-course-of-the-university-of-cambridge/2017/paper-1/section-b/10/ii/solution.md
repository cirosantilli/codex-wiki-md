<h1 id="section-b/10/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [La Mer diagram](../../../../../../la-mer-diagram.md) plots the [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) of growth species against time. Label the equilibrium [solubility](../../../../../../solubility.md) $C_s$ and a higher effective [nucleation](../../../../../../nucleation.md) threshold $C_n$. Generate growth species until the [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) briefly exceeds $C_n$; a short [burst nucleation](../../../../../../burst-nucleation.md) interval then creates seeds. Consumption brings the [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) below $C_n$ while leaving it above $C_s$, allowing existing particles to grow without substantial continuing [nucleation](../../../../../../nucleation.md). Rapid mixing, a short seed-formation interval and a common growth period help produce a narrow [particle size distribution](../../../../../../particle-size-distribution.md). Suppress secondary [nucleation](../../../../../../nucleation.md) and aggregation, then quench or consume the remaining growth species when the desired size is reached. The threshold is an operational kinetic scale, not a sharp new equilibrium phase boundary.

Reducing the solid-liquid [interfacial tension](../../../../../../interfacial-tension.md) lowers the classical barrier, which scales as $\gamma^3$ at fixed [supersaturation](../../../../../../supersaturation.md). More seeds can then form at a smaller [supersaturation](../../../../../../supersaturation.md); at fixed total precipitated [mass](../../../../../../mass.md), more seeds generally mean a **smaller mean final particle size**. A **narrower distribution is not guaranteed**: if [nucleation](../../../../../../nucleation.md) continues throughout growth, particles have different ages and the distribution can broaden. A brief well-separated burst and uniform subsequent growth are the controlling requirements, not low [interfacial tension](../../../../../../interfacial-tension.md) alone. The historical model concerns separated [nucleation](../../../../../../nucleation.md)/growth in [sulfur](../../../../../../sulfur.md) hydrosols, rather than a universal demonstrated mechanism for every nanoparticle synthesis.[JACS 72 (1950), pp.4847–4854, model and monodisperse-hydrosol preparation](../../../../../../theory-production-and-mechanism-of-formation-of-monodispersed-hydrosols-by-victor-k-la-mer-and-robert-h-dinegar.md)
