<h1 id="section-b/10/iii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[wetting](../../../../../../../wetting.md) [liquid](../../../../../../../liquid.md) in narrow pores and gaps between fibers can have a concave meniscus. Its signed curvature is negative, so the [Kelvin equation](../../../../../../../kelvin-equation.md) permits equilibrium at a [vapor pressure](../../../../../../../vapor-pressure.md) below that above a flat [liquid](../../../../../../../liquid.md) surface. **[capillary condensation](../../../../../../../capillary-condensation.md) in sufficiently small, wettable gaps explains moisture accumulation at high relative humidity.** Increased humidity brings the [vapor](../../../../../../../vapor.md) closer to flat-surface saturation and permits condensation in larger gaps. Surface [adsorption](../../../../../../../adsorption.md) can precede a bulk-like meniscus; large open spaces do not automatically fill at every humidity. The interpretation assumes a [wetting](../../../../../../../wetting.md) contact geometry and does not require the entire cloth to be below the ordinary planar dew point.
