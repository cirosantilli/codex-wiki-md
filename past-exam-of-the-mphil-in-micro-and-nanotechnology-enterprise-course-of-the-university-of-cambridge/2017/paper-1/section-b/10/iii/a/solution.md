<h1 id="section-b/10/iii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a convex droplet, the [Kelvin equation](../../../../../../../kelvin-equation.md) gives $\ln(p_{\rm eq}(r)/p_{\rm sat})=2\gamma\overline V_l/(rRT)$. Smaller droplets therefore require a higher equilibrium [vapor pressure](../../../../../../../vapor-pressure.md). At a common ambient [vapor pressure](../../../../../../../vapor-pressure.md), sufficiently small droplets evaporate while larger ones can grow, transferring material through the [vapor](../../../../../../../vapor.md). **[Ostwald ripening](../../../../../../../ostwald-ripening.md) decreases the droplet count and increases a characteristic surviving radius.** Direct collision and coalescence can also reduce the count but are a distinct mechanism. In a closed container the final state depends on total [liquid](../../../../../../../liquid.md)/[vapor](../../../../../../../vapor.md) inventory and volume; an initially undersaturated, liquid-poor system can lose all droplets rather than indefinitely grow a large one.
