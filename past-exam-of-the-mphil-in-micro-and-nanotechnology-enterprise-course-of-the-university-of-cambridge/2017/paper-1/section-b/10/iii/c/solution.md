<h1 id="section-b/10/iii/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The coarse texture comes from large ice crystals. Form many small crystals by rapid cooling with agitation and avoid extended storage near partial melting or repeated warming/cooling cycles. Curvature raises the [chemical potential](../../../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) of smaller ice crystals, so material can transfer from smaller to larger crystals during recrystallization; partial melting followed by slow refreezing accelerates loss of a fine texture. A more viscous unfrozen phase and suitable stabilizing ingredients slow molecular transport and coarsening. **Create small crystals quickly and suppress their subsequent growth with stable cold storage and slow transport.** This applies interfacial and phase-change physics; it is not a claim that reducing droplet [vapor pressure](../../../../../../../vapor-pressure.md) alone completely models a multicomponent frozen mixture.
