<h1 id="section-b/8/iv/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For cubic [lattice planes](../../../../../../../lattice-plane.md), $d_{hkl}=a/\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}$. The [face-centred cubic selection rule](../../../../../../../face-centred-cubic-selection-rule.md) permits indices that are all odd or all even. Thus

$$
d_{111}=0.2336\,\mathrm{nm},\qquad d_{200}=0.2023\,\mathrm{nm},\qquad d_{220}=0.1430\,\mathrm{nm}.
$$

The first two spacings exceed the quoted 0.19 nm real-space resolution, whereas $d_{220}$ does not. This restriction is for the resolved image and its transform; a [diffraction](../../../../../../../diffraction.md) instrument need not have the same cutoff.

A natural candidate is a beam near a **local $\langle110\rangle$ [zone axis](../../../../../../../zone-axis.md)**. For example, the $[110]$ [zone law](../../../../../../../zone-law.md) $h+k=0$ includes four $\{111\}$ reflections and two $\{200\}$ reflections, all within that real-space cutoff. Their reciprocal-space [length](../../../../../../../length.md) ratio is $g_{200}/g_{111}=2/\sqrt3=1.1547$. The angle between $(1\bar11)$ and $(1\bar1\bar1)$ reciprocal vectors is $\cos^{-1}(1/3)=70.53^\circ$. In contrast, a $[001]$ view gives a fourfold arrangement of the resolvable $\{200\}$ family, while the lowest ordinary $[111]$ zone reflections are $\{220\}$ and would not be resolved at the stated cutoff.

In a five-domain decahedral interpretation, neighboring tetrahedral fcc regions meet on $\{111\}$ twin planes around a common near-$\langle110\rangle$ axis. Five unstrained wedges supply $5\times70.53^\circ=352.64^\circ$; elastic distortion accommodates the remaining $7.36^\circ$. Their rotated [diffraction](../../../../../../../diffraction.md) contributions need not resemble a single six-spot $[110]$ pattern. **Near-$\langle110\rangle$ fcc domains in a [multiply twinned particle](../../../../../../../multiply-twinned-particle.md) are a defensible orientation proposal, not a unique full indexing.** The printed transform has no reciprocal-space scale or indexed spot list, so it cannot establish every domain rotation or exclude an overlapping-particle contribution. Calibrated spacings and transforms from individual regions would settle those distinctions. The five-domain geometry is independently observed in atomic-resolution gold-particle measurements.[printed p.2, Fig.1 and accompanying discussion of decahedral gold and angular deficit](../../../../../../../study-of-nanoparticles-at-utsa-one-year-of-using-the-first-jem-arm200f-installed-in-usa-by-alvaro-mayoral-and-others.md)
