In [transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../../transmission-electron-microscopy.md), the [electron objective lens](../../../../../../electron-objective-lens.md) forms an intermediate real-space image and intermediate/projector lenses enlarge it onto a screen or camera. The total optical [magnification](../../../../../../magnification.md) is the product of the relevant stage magnifications. The user changes imaging-lens excitations to select [magnification](../../../../../../magnification.md), while separately controlling illumination and focus. Display resizing introduces an additional enlargement, so the recorded pixel calibration matters.

In [scanning transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../../scanning-transmission-electron-microscopy.md), a focused probe is rastered across the specimen and a detector signal is assigned to each probe position. The displayed [magnification](../../../../../../magnification.md) is display width divided by scanned specimen width. The user increases [magnification](../../../../../../magnification.md) by reducing raster-deflection amplitude, independently of choosing a smaller incident probe with the probe-forming lenses. **TEM changes image-forming lens magnification; STEM changes the raster field of view.** A smaller field or more pixels does not create finer physical resolution than the probe, specimen scattering and detection permit. A calibrated real-space scale is more transferable than the apparent size on a monitor.[printed p.7, raster-width/display-width magnification; this geometric relation also applies to scanned transmitted-electron imaging](../../../../../../scanning-electron-microscope-a-to-z-by-jeol.md)
