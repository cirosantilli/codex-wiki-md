<h1 id="section-b/12/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A material exhibiting [piezoelectricity](../../../../../../piezoelectricity.md) converts cyclic mechanical stress into [electric charge](../../../../../../electric-charge-split.md); its mechanical and electrical boundary conditions jointly set the extractable [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md). [piezoelectric ceramics](../../../../../../piezoelectric-ceramic.md) such as [lead zirconate titanate](../../../../../../lead-zirconate-titanate.md) are stiff and often have large charge coefficients and electromechanical coupling, but are brittle and can be difficult to match to large-strain flexible motion. [piezoelectric polymers](../../../../../../piezoelectric-polymer.md) such as [polyvinylidene fluoride](../../../../../../polyvinylidene-fluoride.md) are compliant, light and compatible with flexible large-area films. Their lower charge coefficient is partly offset in voltage-sensitive applications by lower [permittivity](../../../../../../permittivity-split.md): the [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) coefficient is $g=d/\epsilon$.

**Ceramics often favor strong charge generation and coupling; polymers favor flexibility and strain accommodation.** The larger open-circuit [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) of a low-capacitance film does not itself imply more useful [power](../../../../../../power.md). For a simple fixed-stress comparison the electrical [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) scale involves $d^2/\epsilon$, while coupling also depends on mechanical compliance. At fixed imposed strain, different stiffnesses change the available mechanical [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md). [resonance](../../../../../../resonance.md) tuning, fatigue, mechanical mounting, rectifier losses and load matching can reverse a simplistic material ranking; compare complete devices under the same actual vibration source.
