<h1 id="section-b/12/iii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Each [lithium](../../../../../../../lithium.md) atom supplies one [electron](../../../../../../../electron.md). If all of the stated [lithium](../../../../../../../lithium.md) is electrochemically accessible and there is enough [iron disulfide](../../../../../../../iron-disulfide.md), the ideal [electric charge](../../../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) is

$$
Q=\frac{m_{\rm Li}}{M_{\rm Li}}F=\frac{0.453}{6.94}(96540)=\boxed{6.30\times10^3\,\mathrm C=1.75\,\mathrm{Ah}}.
$$

The [voltage](../../../../../../../voltage.md) is not needed to calculate charge. At an assumed constant 1.5 V the corresponding ideal [energy](../../../../../../../energy-split.md) would be $VQ\simeq9.45\,\mathrm{kJ}=2.63\,\mathrm{Wh}$, but an actual discharge [voltage](../../../../../../../voltage.md) varies with load and state of charge. Side reactions, incomplete utilization and the practical cutoff reduce accessible capacity. The preceding optimal ratio would require approximately 1.96 g of [iron disulfide](../../../../../../../iron-disulfide.md) for this amount of [lithium](../../../../../../../lithium.md).
