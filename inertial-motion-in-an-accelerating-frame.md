# Inertial motion in an accelerating frame

↑ **Parent:** [Newton's first law](newton-s-first-law.md)

Constant [velocity](velocity.md) depends on the [reference frame](reference-frame.md). For nonrotating axes and a moving origin of laboratory [position](position.md) $\mathbf R(t)$, subtracting that origin gives

$$
\mathbf r'=\mathbf r-\mathbf R(t),\qquad
\mathbf v'=\mathbf v-\dot{\mathbf R}(t),\qquad
\mathbf a'=\mathbf a-\ddot{\mathbf R}(t).
$$

If $\mathbf R(t)=\mathbf R_0+\mathbf U t$ with constant [velocity](velocity.md) $\mathbf U$, the [Galilean transformation](galilean-transformation.md) leaves [acceleration](acceleration.md) unchanged. The cart moving at $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the laboratory is at rest in a frame moving right at $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Both descriptions obey [Newton's first law](newton-s-first-law.md).

Instead choose an origin with $R_x(t)=\tfrac12At^2$, where the stipulated constant $A=0.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ points right. The same cart has

$$
x'(t)=(1.5\,\mathrm{m/s})t-\tfrac12(0.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2})t^2,\qquad
v_x'(t)=1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}-(0.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2})t,\qquad
a_x'=-0.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$

At $t=3\,\mathrm s$, the cart is instantaneously at rest relative to this accelerating origin, but its relative [acceleration](acceleration.md) is nonzero. In this [translating accelerated reference frame](translating-accelerated-reference-frame.md), [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) must include the effective inertial [force](force-split.md) $-mA=-1.0\,\mathrm N$. This term accounts for the accelerating coordinates; the actual [external forces](external-force.md) on the cart still sum to zero. No rotation-dependent terms are present because the axes remain parallel to the laboratory axes.
