# Steepest descent on a graph surface

↑ **Parent:** [Surface gradient](surface-gradient.md)

On the [graph of a function](graph-of-a-function.md) $z=f(x,y)$, assume $f$ is [differentiable](differentiable-function.md), its planar [gradient](gradient.md) $p$ is nonzero, and speed $U>0$ is measured by the three-dimensional [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md). The upward [unit normal vector](unit-normal.md) is $n=(-p,1)/\sqrt{1+|p|^2}$. Projecting the downward vertical vector $-e_z$ onto the tangent plane gives $-(p,|p|^2)/(1+|p|^2)$. By the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md), normalizing this projected vector produces the tangent direction with greatest height decrease at the prescribed speed, giving the displayed [velocity](velocity.md). Its vertical component is $-U|p|/\sqrt{1+|p|^2}$, and its horizontal direction is parallel to $-\nabla f$. Consequently its planar paths agree with negative [gradient flow](gradient-flow.md) up to a change of time, although the speeds differ. Where $p=0$, every tangent direction has zero first-order height change and this normalized formula is undefined.
