# Donsker theorem for empirical distribution functions

↑ **Parent:** [Empirical distribution process](empirical-distribution-process.md)

For an independent sample with [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md) $F$, the [empirical distribution process](empirical-distribution-process.md) converges in the [supremum norm](supremum-norm.md) on $\ell^\infty(\mathbb R)$ to the [F-Brownian bridge](f-brownian-bridge.md). The convergence uses [outer expectation](outer-expectation.md) and inner expectation when finite-sample Borel measurability fails. It is a full function-space theorem, stronger than the [Finite-dimensional Brownian bridge limit of an empirical distribution process](finite-dimensional-brownian-bridge-limit-of-an-empirical-distribution-process.md). No continuity of $F$ is required. The [continuous mapping theorem](continuous-mapping-theorem.md) gives convergence of the supremum statistic; for continuous $F$, its range contains $(0,1)$ and the bridge vanishes at zero and one, giving the [Kolmogorov-Smirnov theorem](kolmogorov-smirnov-theorem.md). The function-space statement is stated here as a theorem; its tightness assertion is an additional ingredient beyond the finite-dimensional covariance calculation.
