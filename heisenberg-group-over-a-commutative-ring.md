# Heisenberg group over a commutative ring

↑ **Parent:** [Heisenberg group](heisenberg-group.md)

For a [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md) $R$ with an [identity element](identity-element.md), use coordinates

$$
A(x,y,z)=\begin{pmatrix}1&x&y\\0&1&z\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

These matrices form a [group](group-split.md) under multiplication, with

$$
A(x,y,z)A(x',y',z')=A(x+x',y+y'+xz',z+z'),\qquad A(x,y,z)^{-1}=A(-x,xz-y,-z).
$$

These formulas require no division, so they remain valid over [rings](ring.md) with [zero divisors](zero-divisor.md). The [center of a group](center-of-a-group.md) is $\{A(0,y,0):y\in R\}$: the [commutator](commutator.md) has only central coordinate $xz'-x'z$, and testing $x'=0,z'=1$ and $x'=1,z'=0$ forces $x=z=0$. For finite $R$, its [group order](order-of-a-finite-group.md) is $|R|^3$. This includes real, integer and modular versions of the [Heisenberg group](heisenberg-group.md); the [ring](ring.md) $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ must not be confused with the [finite field](finite-field.md) of four elements.
