# First isomorphism theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Group theory](group-theory-split.md)

For a [group homomorphism](group-homomorphism.md) $\varphi:G\to H$, the [group isomorphism](group-isomorphism.md) $g\ker\varphi\mapsto\varphi(g)$ gives

$$
G/\ker\varphi\cong\operatorname{im}\varphi.
$$

The [group kernel](kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) records which inputs become indistinguishable, and the image records which outputs actually occur. The [first isomorphism theorem](first-isomorphism-theorem.md) says that identifying precisely those indistinguishable inputs gives a [group](group-split.md) isomorphic to the image. It is one of the general [isomorphism theorems](isomorphism-theorems.md). [McKernan, MIT 18.703, Theorem 10.1: the first isomorphism theorem](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-703-modern-algebra-spring-2013/b40d60898322bbd1d63410b0a925946a_MIT18_703S13_pra_l_10.pdf).

For example, reduction modulo three gives a [surjective group homomorphism](surjective-group-homomorphism.md) from the additive [cyclic group](cyclic-group.md) $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$ to $\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$. Its [group kernel](kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) is $\{0,3\}$. The three [cosets](coset.md) $\{0,3\}$, $\{1,4\}$, and $\{2,5\}$ become the three outputs zero, one, and two, respectively. Thus

$$
(\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z)/\{0,3\}\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
$$

The [quotient group](quotient-group.md) diagram shows both the identification and the resulting operation. More generally, [fibres of a group homomorphism are cosets of its kernel](fibres-of-a-group-homomorphism-are-cosets-of-its-kernel.md) explains why this map is well-defined and [injective](injective-function.md); it is [surjective](surjective-function.md) onto the image by definition.
