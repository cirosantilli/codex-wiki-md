# Reaction rate

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical kinetics](chemical-kinetics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_rate)

For one [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md) in a [closed system](closed-system.md) of fixed [volume](volume.md) $V$, with uniform [concentrations](concentration.md) and no competing reactions or appreciable accumulation of unlisted species, choose the [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md)

$$
a\mathrm A+b\mathrm B\longrightarrow c\mathrm C,\qquad a,b,c>0.
$$

Brackets denote [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) per [volume](volume.md), for example $[\mathrm A]=n_{\mathrm A}/V$. The [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md) gives one instantaneous [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md)

$$
v=-\frac1a\frac{d[\mathrm A]}{dt}
=-\frac1b\frac{d[\mathrm B]}{dt}
=\frac1c\frac{d[\mathrm C]}{dt}
=\frac1V\frac{d\xi}{dt},
$$

where $\xi$ is the existing [extent of reaction](extent-of-reaction.md). The minus signs make forward consumption positive; dividing by the coefficients makes measurements of different [chemical species](chemical-species.md) agree. Units can be $\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$. See [IUPAC Gold Book: rate of reaction](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/R05156) and [Flowers et al., Chemistry 2e, §12.1: chemical reaction rates](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/12-1-chemical-reaction-rates).

For the illustrative [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) $2\mathrm A+\mathrm B\longrightarrow3\mathrm C$, a measured $d[\mathrm C]/dt=0.060\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$ implies $v=0.020\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$, $d[\mathrm A]/dt=-0.040\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$, and $d[\mathrm B]/dt=-0.020\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$. These numbers express [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md), without supplying a [rate law](rate-law.md).

Each [derivative](derivative.md) is the slope of a [concentration](concentration.md) curve at one instant. Over a finite interval, $-\Delta[\mathrm A]/(a\Delta t)$ instead gives an average [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md). For A converting reversibly to B, the net [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) is $v=d[\mathrm B]/dt=J_f-J_r$; both directional [reaction rates](reaction-rate.md) can remain positive when $v=0$ at [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md).
