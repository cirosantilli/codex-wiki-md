<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The quotient $q(w)=\phi(w)/w$ extends to a [holomorphic function](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) at zero with value one. Since $\phi$ is [injective](../../../../../injective-function.md) and vanishes at zero, $q$ has no zero in the [unit disc](../../../../../unit-disc.md). A [holomorphic logarithm](../../../../../holomorphic-logarithm.md) $\ell$ exists there, with $\ell(0)=0$. Define the [odd square-root transform of a normalized univalent function](../../../../../odd-square-root-transform-of-a-normalized-univalent-function.md) by

$$
h(z)=z\exp[\ell(z^2)/2].
$$

It satisfies $h(z)^2=\phi(z^2)$, $h(0)=0$, $h'(0)=1$ and $h(-z)=-h(z)$. To check [injectivity](../../../../../injective-function.md), $h(z)=h(w)$ gives $\phi(z^2)=\phi(w^2)$, hence $z^2=w^2$. If $w=-z$, oddness gives $h(z)=0$, so $z=w=0$. Thus the two arguments always coincide, and $h\in\mathcal S$. Choosing the square root by its derivative at zero removes its sign ambiguity.
