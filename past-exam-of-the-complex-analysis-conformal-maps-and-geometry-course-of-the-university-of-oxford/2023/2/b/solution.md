<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [symmetry rule for extremal length](../../../../symmetry-rule-for-extremal-length.md) says that if a domain and its curve family are invariant under reflection, the supremum defining [extremal length](../../../../extremal-length.md) may be taken over reflection-invariant [conformal metrics](../../../../conformal-metric.md). More generally, for a conformal or anticonformal involution $T$, use metrics satisfying $T^*\rho=\rho$.

To see this, replace $\rho$ by $\rho_s=(\rho+T^*\rho)/2$. Every curve in the invariant family and its image have length at least $L_\rho$, so $L_{\rho_s}\geq L_\rho$. Convexity of the square and change of variables give $A_{\rho_s}\leq A_\rho$. Therefore symmetrizing cannot reduce the length-squared to area ratio.
