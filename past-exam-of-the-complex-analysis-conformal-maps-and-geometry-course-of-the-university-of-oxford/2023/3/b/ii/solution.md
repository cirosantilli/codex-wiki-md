<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $|z|<r$, the [Schwarz integral on the unit disk](../../../../../schwarz-integral-on-the-unit-disk.md), rescaled to radius $r$, is

$$
g(z)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}u(re^{i\theta})\frac{re^{i\theta}+z}{re^{i\theta}-z}\,d\theta.
$$

On each smaller closed disk $|z|\leq s<r$, its kernel and all its derivatives in $z$ are uniformly bounded. Since the boundary values of the real [harmonic function](../../../../../harmonic-function.md) $u$ are continuous on this circle, [differentiation under the integral sign](../../../../../differentiation-under-the-integral-sign.md) proves that $g$ is holomorphic. Taking the real part gives the [Poisson integral](../../../../../poisson-integral.md) of those boundary values. It agrees with $u$ by uniqueness of the [Dirichlet problem](../../../../../dirichlet-problem.md) on the radius-$r$ disk. Hence **$g$ is holomorphic and $\operatorname{Re}g=u$ on $|z|<r$**. Its imaginary part is the [harmonic conjugate](../../../../../harmonic-conjugate.md) normalized by $\operatorname{Im}g(0)=0$.
