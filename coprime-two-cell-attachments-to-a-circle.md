# Coprime two-cell attachments to a circle

↑ **Parent:** [Cellular cohomology](cellular-cohomology.md)

The cellular boundary $\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z$ is $(m,n)$, with kernel $\mathbb Z$ and zero cokernel when the degrees are coprime. The [van Kampen theorem](seifert-van-kampen-theorem.md) gives trivial fundamental group. The [Hurewicz theorem](hurewicz-theorem.md) supplies a map from the sphere representing a homology generator, and the [homological Whitehead theorem](homological-whitehead-theorem.md) makes it a homotopy equivalence. For degrees $2,3$ the space cannot be homeomorphic to a sphere: an embedded circle in a sphere bounds its two complementary regions, so both characteristic boundary maps would have degree of absolute value one.
