# Linear reversibility and yield in tensile tests

↑ **Parent:** [Tensile modulus experiment](tensile-modulus-experiment.md)

[Linearity](linearity.md) and reversibility answer different questions. The [proportional limit](proportional-limit.md) ends the interval in which axial [stress](stress.md) is proportional to axial [strain](strain.md). [Elastic deformation](elastic-deformation.md) means that deformation is recovered after unloading; a nonlinear response can still be elastic. Conversely, a short straight portion of a loading curve does not by itself demonstrate recovery.

To check recovery, unload and examine the local [extensometer](extensometer.md) [strain](strain.md) relative to its initial value, allowing for instrument resolution, drift and any time dependence. A residual extension can indicate [plasticity in continuum mechanics](plasticity-physics.md), but grip slip can produce a residual in [crosshead displacement](crosshead-displacement.md) without the same local residual. Compare the channels before assigning the effect to the specimen. [Yield point](yield-point.md) concerns the onset of irreversible deformation; a specified offset [proof stress](proof-stress.md) is an operational convention rather than [Young's modulus](young-s-modulus.md). For example, a $0.2\%$ offset [proof stress](proof-stress.md) is the intersection of the loading curve with $\sigma=E(\varepsilon-0.002)$, using the correctly referenced [strain](strain.md). Report the fitted [stress](stress.md)/[strain](strain.md) window and the unloading evidence alongside the modulus, so that apparatus seating, departure from [linearity](linearity.md) and onset of [plasticity in continuum mechanics](plasticity-physics.md) remain distinguishable.
