# Nuclear quadrupole vibration

↑ **Parent:** [Collective nuclear model](collective-nuclear-model.md)

A collective oscillation of the quadrupole shape coordinates of an [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md). Near spherical equilibrium, the ideal harmonic description has five components, corresponding to the five magnetic substates of [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) two. Each quantum carries $I^\pi=2^+$; it is an excitation of the many-[nucleon](nucleon.md) system rather than an extra constituent. Its frequency is $\omega$, and its [energy](energy-split.md) spacing $\hbar\omega$ follows the [quantum harmonic oscillator](quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md).

One quantum produces a $2^+$ level at $\hbar\omega$. Two identical quadrupole quanta can couple to $I=0,2,4$ in a symmetric state, giving a degenerate $0^+,2^+,4^+$ multiplet at $2\hbar\omega$ in the ideal model. Odd total $I$ would be antisymmetric under their exchange. Thus the first $4^+$ to first $2^+$ excitation ratio is $2$, rather than the [nuclear rotational band](nuclear-rotational-band.md) value $10/3$. Real [nuclear quadrupole vibrations](nuclear-quadrupole-vibration.md) can be anharmonic and their multiplets split: the ratio is a diagnostic of a limiting model, not a universal classification rule.

There is a useful state-counting check. Two identical quanta distributed among five substates span $5(5+1)/2=15$ symmetric states. The proposed multiplet contains $(2\cdot0+1)+(2\cdot2+1)+(2\cdot4+1)=1+5+9=15$. This verifies the angular-state bookkeeping independently of the chosen [energy](energy-split.md) scale.
