# Intracellular recording of membrane potential

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane potential](membrane-potential-split.md)

In 1939, [Alan Hodgkin](alan-hodgkin.md) and [Andrew Huxley](andrew-huxley.md) reported intracellular [action potential](action-potential.md) recordings from squid giant [axons](axon.md) at Plymouth.[Action Potentials Recorded from Inside a Nerve Fibre, Nature 144 (1939), pp. 710–711: inspected publisher abstract, preparation and intracellular recording](action-potentials-recorded-from-inside-a-nerve-fibre-by-alan-hodgkin-and-andrew-huxley.md)

[Alan Hodgkin](alan-hodgkin.md)'s later account describes advancing a capillary [electrode](electrode.md) from a cut end into intact [axon](axon.md), avoiding scraping the [cell membrane](cell-membrane-split.md). Its Figure 3 uses a seawater-filled capillary internally, with external seawater as zero: the readout is internal relative to external [electric potential](electric-potential.md). This supports a voltage across the surface [cell membrane](cell-membrane-split.md). As a separate ionic control, the account reports that raising external [potassium](potassium.md) toward its internal [concentration](concentration.md) reversibly abolished resting voltage; this was later work, not a control asserted for the 1939 recording. Injury and electrode-solution junction potentials limit interpretation. Voltage recording alone does not identify every conducting [ion](ion.md). A [voltage clamp](voltage-clamp.md) additionally uses feedback to impose voltage and measures the required [electric current](electric-current.md).[Alan Lloyd Hodgkin, The ionic basis of nervous conduction, Nobel Lecture (11 December 1963), pp. 34–36, Figures 2–3: placement, seawater reference and later potassium control; p. 42: voltage clamp](the-ionic-basis-of-nervous-conduction-by-alan-hodgkin.md)

In the schematic, connect the intracellular [electrode](electrode.md) to the positive input and the external [reference electrode](reference-electrode.md) to the negative input of a voltage recorder. After electrode-offset correction, a negative reading means $\phi_{\mathrm{inside}}<\phi_{\mathrm{outside}}$. A useful baseline check is to compare both [electrodes](electrode.md) in the same external [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) before insertion; this is a measurement check, not an additional historical observation claimed here. Swapping inputs reverses the readout sign without changing the [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md)'s [membrane potential](membrane-potential-split.md).
