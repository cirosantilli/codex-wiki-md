# Wave equation

↑ **Parent:** [Partial differential equation](partial-differential-equation-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_equation)

The wave equation is a second-order [linear partial differential equation](linear-partial-differential-equation.md) describing [waves](wave-split.md) with [finite propagation speed](finite-propagation-speed.md). In one spatial dimension its homogeneous, constant-coefficient form is

$$
u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx},\qquad c>0.
$$

Here $x$ is position, $t$ is time, $u(x,t)$ is the disturbance, and subscripts denote [partial derivatives](partial-derivative.md). For a [wave equation on a string](wave-equation-on-a-string.md), $u$ is transverse displacement; in [linear acoustics](linear-acoustics-split.md), it can be a pressure perturbation. In several spatial dimensions, $u_{xx}$ becomes the [Laplacian](laplacian.md) $\Delta u$. The same mathematical structure connects vibrating strings and [acoustic waves](acoustic-wave.md), although their physical assumptions and [wave speeds](wave-speed.md) differ. [MIT, Wave Propagation, Chapter 1](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-062j-wave-propagation-spring-2017/2f9f71f7f386b36c9c42f48c03aecfe6_MIT2_062J_S17_Chap1.pdf)

For the whole-line [Cauchy problem](cauchy-problem.md), prescribe both displacement $u(x,0)=f(x)$ and [velocity](velocity.md) $u_t(x,0)=g(x)$. A [classical solution](classical-solution.md) has continuous [partial derivatives](partial-derivative.md) through order two; $f\in C^2(\mathbb R)$ and $g\in C^1(\mathbb R)$ suffice for [D'Alembert's formula](d-alembert-s-formula.md) to produce one. A finite string additionally needs [boundary conditions](boundary-condition.md). These statements concern the unforced model with constant $c$; [damping](damping.md), spatially varying coefficients and nonlinear terms change the solution formula.

A [travelling wave](travelling-wave.md) $F(x-ct)$ carries its entire profile to the right at [wave speed](wave-speed.md) $c$; $G(x+ct)$ travels left. Their sum is again a solution by the [superposition principle](superposition-principle.md). A [standing wave](standing-wave.md) instead has a fixed [mode shape](mode-shape.md) whose size and sign oscillate: in $\sin(kx)\cos(ckt)$, each [node of a standing wave](node-physics.md) stays stationary. The distinction is especially clear in the pulse and string diagrams below. For this ideal model, the [dispersion relation](dispersion-relation-split.md) is $\omega^2=c^2k^2$: each propagation branch is [nondispersive](nondispersive-wave.md), so a localized profile translates without spreading. This makes the equation a useful reference against which to understand [wave dispersion](wave-dispersion.md) and [damping](damping.md).

The appealing combination is local interaction with global predictability: neighbouring displacements determine [acceleration](acceleration.md) through $u_{xx}$, yet [D'Alembert's formula](d-alembert-s-formula.md) explicitly identifies exactly which initial locations can influence a later observation. [Finite propagation speed](finite-propagation-speed.md) turns that observation into a [domain of dependence](domain-of-dependence.md), rather than requiring knowledge of the entire initial string.
