# Induced charge as harmonic measure

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatic reciprocity identity](electrostatic-reciprocity-identity.md)

In a grounded conducting enclosure, let the [harmonic function](harmonic-function.md) $w_j$ equal one on boundary component $j$ and zero on the others. A [point charge](point-charge.md) $q$ at $\mathbf r_0$ induces total [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $Q_j=-q w_j(\mathbf r_0)$ on that component. The weighting [harmonic function](harmonic-function.md) is its [harmonic measure](harmonic-measure.md).

To prove the identity, let $\phi$ be the zero-boundary [electric potential](electric-potential.md), so $\nabla^2\phi=-q\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0)/\epsilon_0$. [Green's second identity](green-second-identity.md) gives $-qw_j(\mathbf r_0)/\epsilon_0=\int_{\partial D}w_j\partial_n\phi\,dS$, where $\mathbf n$ points out of the vacuum domain into the metal. The [electrostatic boundary conditions at a conductor](electrostatic-boundary-conditions-at-a-conductor.md) give $\sigma=\epsilon_0\partial_n\phi$, proving the result. In an unbounded domain require the additional boundary term at infinity to vanish. Between concentric spheres, $w_a(r)=a(b-r)/(r(b-a))$; between parallel planes $z=0,d$, $w_0=1-z/d$ and $w_d=z/d$.
