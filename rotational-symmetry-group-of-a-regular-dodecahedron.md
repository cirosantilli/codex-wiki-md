# Rotational symmetry group of a regular dodecahedron

↑ **Parent:** [Rotational symmetry](rotational-symmetry.md)

The [rotations in three dimensions](rotation-in-three-dimensions.md) preserving a [dodecahedron](dodecahedron.md) form a group of order $60$. There are $24$ nonidentity rotations about the six axes through opposite face centres, $20$ about the ten axes through opposite vertices, and $15$ half-turns about the fifteen axes through opposite edge midpoints. Its nonidentity [conjugacy classes](conjugacy-class.md) have sizes $12,12,20,15$: the [centralizer](centralizer.md) of a rotation of order five or three consists of rotations about its oriented axis and has order five or three, respectively, while all edge half-turns are conjugate. A [normal subgroup](normal-subgroup.md) is a union of whole [conjugacy classes](conjugacy-class.md) containing the identity. No proper nontrivial sum of these class sizes plus one divides $60$, proving that it is a [simple group](simple-group.md). The [centralizers](centralizer.md) of the fifteen [involutions](involution.md) are five distinct [Klein four-groups](klein-four-group.md), each containing three [involutions](involution.md). Conjugation permutes these five subgroups nontrivially. The kernel is a [normal subgroup](normal-subgroup.md), so simplicity makes this nontrivial action injective. Its sign map to a group of order two must be trivial, since simplicity would otherwise force an impossible injection of sixty elements into two. Thus its image lies in the [alternating group](alternating-group.md) $A_5$, and equality of orders makes the image all of $A_5$.
