# Calorimetry

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calorimetry)

Calorimetry measures [heat](heat.md) transfers. A [calorimeter](calorimeter.md), a calibrated [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) and an accounting of unwanted [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) connect an observed [temperature](temperature.md) change to an [energy](energy.md) balance, as in [heat-engine calorimetry](heat-engine-calorimetry.md) and [heat-pump calorimetry](heat-pump-calorimetry.md).

The central idea is to measure a response that is easier to observe than the transfer itself: a known [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) turns a [temperature](temperature.md) change into an [energy](energy.md) estimate. [Mixing calorimetry](mixing-calorimetry.md) can determine an unknown [specific heat capacity](specific-heat-capacity.md), while a [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md) can be studied by measuring the response of its surroundings. The useful observable is a change in [temperature](temperature.md), together with a stated [thermodynamic system](thermodynamic-system.md) boundary; a hot object does not contain a separately conserved store of [heat](heat.md).

For a [closed system](closed-system.md) with unchanged bulk [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and [potential energy](potential-energy.md), let $Q_{\rm in}$ be net inward [heat](heat.md) and $W_{\rm on}$ net inward [work](work.md). The [first law of thermodynamics](first-law-of-thermodynamics.md) gives

$$
\Delta U=Q_{\rm in}+W_{\rm on}.
$$

If the boundary includes a hot sample, receiving [water](water.md) and the participating vessel, their mutual [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) is internal. With no external input, the total storage change is minus the signed outward [heat](heat.md) leakage, not the positive amount transferred from sample to [water](water.md). A [calorimeter calibration](calorimeter-calibration.md) with an immersed [resistor](resistor.md) instead supplies electrical [work](work.md) across this boundary; [Joule heating](joule-heating.md) converts it into [internal energy](internal-energy.md). Choosing only the receiving [water](water.md) as the boundary changes which transfers count as external.

The mechanical constraint decides which storage quantity the readout represents. For a [closed system](closed-system.md) at fixed [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md), with no other [work](work.md), $Q_V=\Delta U$. At constant external [pressure](pressure.md), with only expansion [work](work.md) $p\Delta V$, $Q_p=\Delta H$, where $H=U+pV$ is [enthalpy](enthalpy.md). Thus the corresponding warming balances use [heat capacity at constant volume](heat-capacity-at-constant-volume.md) or [heat capacity at constant pressure](heat-capacity-at-constant-pressure.md). Electrical or shaft [work](work.md) must be included separately when present. These endpoint balances do not require the actual exchange to be a [reversible thermodynamic process](reversible-thermodynamic-process.md).

A covered cup open to atmospheric [pressure](pressure.md) approximately realizes the second constraint when escaping material is negligible. A sealed rigid reaction vessel realizes the first; its surrounding [water](water.md), vessel and immersed parts form the calibrated receiver. The reaction's outward [heat](heat.md) equals the receiver's gain only after losses and ignition input have been accounted for. The distinction concerns the reaction boundary, even if its receiver is open to atmospheric [pressure](pressure.md).[§5.2, paragraph beginning “If the amount of heat absorbed by a calorimeter”, and Figure 5.17: vessel storage, constant-pressure versus constant-volume apparatus and calibration](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/5-2-calorimetry.md)

A historical quantitative result appears in [James Prescott Joule](james-prescott-joule.md)'s [On the Mechanical Equivalent of Heat](on-the-mechanical-equivalent-of-heat-by-james-prescott-joule.md) (1850): Table IX gives $772.692$ foot-pounds of mechanical [work](work.md) per warming of one pound of [water](water.md) by one degree Fahrenheit, with weighing reduced to vacuum. His final rounded statement specifies [water](water.md) between $55$ and $60$ degrees Fahrenheit. He acknowledged residual vibration and sound as possible upward bias. This is an original reported conversion result, not the definition of the modern [joule](joule.md); the apparatus and resting controls are described in [Joule's paddle-wheel experiment](joule-s-paddle-wheel-experiment.md).[p. 82, Table IX and concluding paragraphs: water-series equivalent and residual losses](on-the-mechanical-equivalent-of-heat-by-james-prescott-joule.md)
