# Anaerobic respiration

↑ **Parent:** [Cellular respiration](cellular-respiration.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaerobic_respiration)

[Anaerobic respiration](anaerobic-respiration.md) uses a respiratory [electron transport chain](electron-transport-chain.md) with a final [electron acceptor](electron-acceptor.md) other than [molecular oxygen](molecular-oxygen.md). External acceptors can include [sulfate](sulfate.md), depending on the [organism](organism-split.md). “External” distinguishes a supplied terminal acceptor from the fuel-derived organic intermediates used in [fermentation](fermentation.md); it does not mean that the [reduction](reduction-chemistry.md) must take place outside the [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md). In [fermentation](fermentation.md), regeneration of carriers such as [NADH](nadh.md) to their oxidized form permits [glycolysis](glycolysis.md) to continue without a respiratory [electron transport chain](electron-transport-chain.md). Both routes can operate without [molecular oxygen](molecular-oxygen.md), but they are different mechanisms.[Biology 2e, §7.5, opening distinction, Anaerobic Cellular Respiration and Figure 7.16](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others.md)
