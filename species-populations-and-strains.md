# Species, populations and strains

↑ **Parent:** [Species](species.md)

A [species](species.md) can encompass many [biological populations](population-biology.md). A [biological population](population-biology.md) specifies a set of [organisms](organism.md) in a place and time; a [biological strain](strain-biology.md) identifies a maintained variant or lineage, often in a laboratory. A [species](species.md) name, a local census and a [biological strain](strain-biology.md) designation therefore describe different things. Multiple independently maintained cultures can also share the same [biological strain](strain-biology.md) designation.

For example, a culture identified as [Escherichia coli](escherichia-coli.md) K-12 MG1655 specifies more than the [species](species.md) alone. Two flasks inoculated from that culture are separate experimental [biological populations](population-biology.md); the separate containers do not establish two [species](species.md) or two different [biological strains](strain-biology.md). This is an original experimental bookkeeping example, not a claim that every [biological strain](strain-biology.md) name encodes a complete [genotype](genotype.md).
