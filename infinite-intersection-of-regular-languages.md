# Infinite intersection of regular languages

↑ **Parent:** [Regular language](regular-language.md)

[Regular languages](regular-language.md) are closed under finite [intersection](set-intersection.md), but not countably infinite [intersection](set-intersection.md). Indeed, for any [formal language](formal-language.md) $K\subseteq\Sigma^*$ over a finite [alphabet](alphabet.md), let $L_i$ contain every [word](string.md) of length at least $i$, together with the members of $K$ of length less than $i$. Each $L_i$ is a [regular language](regular-language.md), since its exceptional short part is a [finite language](finite-language.md), and $\bigcap_{i\geq1}L_i=K$. Thus every [formal language](formal-language.md), including one that is not a [regular language](regular-language.md), can arise in this way.
