# Vector normalization

↑ **Parent:** [Unit vector](unit-vector.md)

[Vector normalization](vector-normalization.md) converts a nonzero [vector](vector.md) in a [normed vector space](normed-vector-space-split.md) into a [unit vector](unit-vector.md). [Absolute homogeneity of a norm](absolute-homogeneity-of-a-norm.md) gives

$$
\left\|\frac{v}{\|v\|}\right\|=\frac{\|v\|}{\|v\|}=1.
$$

The direction is unchanged by multiplying by a positive real [scalar](scalar.md). For $c>0$, $\|cv\|=c\|v\|$ and $\widehat{cv}=\widehat v$; for a negative real [scalar](scalar.md), $\widehat{cv}=-\widehat v$. Thus $(3,4)$ and $(6,8)$ normalize to the same [unit vector](unit-vector.md), while $(-3,-4)$ normalizes to its negative. This decomposition is useful for specifying a direction independently of a [force](force-split.md), [velocity](velocity.md) or [displacement](displacement.md) magnitude.

In a complex [normed vector space](normed-vector-space-split.md), a nonzero complex [scalar](scalar.md) $c$ instead gives $\widehat{cv}=(c/|c|)\widehat v$. The remaining [complex argument](argument-complex-analysis.md) is not removed by normalization. For a pure [quantum state](quantum-state.md), vectors differing only by this overall [global phase](global-phase.md) represent the same physical state; [vector normalization](vector-normalization.md) alone does not identify them.

The [zero vector](zero-vector.md) cannot be normalized this way. The construction also applies in a [function space](function-space.md): an [eigenfunction](eigenfunction.md) of a [Sturm-Liouville problem](sturm-liouville-problem.md) can be scaled to unit [weighted norm](weighted-norm.md) by

$$
y_{\rm normalized}(x)=\frac{y(x)}{\sqrt{\int_a^b w(s)|y(s)|^2\,ds}}.
$$

The positive weight $w$ specifies the [weighted inner product](weighted-inner-product.md). Multiplication by this nonzero constant preserves the [eigenvalue equation](eigenvalue-equation.md) and its homogeneous [boundary conditions](boundary-condition.md).
