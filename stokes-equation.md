# Stokes equation

↑ **Parent:** [Stokes flow](stokes-flow-split.md)

For a steady [incompressible flow](incompressible-flow.md) of a [Newtonian fluid](newtonian-fluid.md) with constant [density](density.md) $\rho$ and constant [dynamic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) $\mu>0$, neglecting [inertia](inertia.md) in the [Navier-Stokes equation](navier-stokes-equation.md) gives

$$
-\nabla p+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf u+\mathbf f=\mathbf 0,
\qquad
\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=0.
$$

The first equation is local [force](force.md) balance; the second is [mass conservation](mass-conservation.md). The term $\mu\nabla^2\mathbf u$ is the [divergence](divergence.md) of the viscous part of the [Newtonian fluid stress tensor](newtonian-fluid-stress-tensor.md). The [body force](body-force.md) $\mathbf f$ has units of [force](force.md) per unit [volume](volume.md), $\mathrm{N\,m^{-3}}$, and equals $\rho\mathbf g$ for uniform [gravity](gravity.md). Appropriate boundary data for [velocity](velocity.md) or [traction](traction.md), together with suitable distant conditions for an exterior flow, complete the problem; a rigid surface normally imposes the [no-slip boundary condition](no-slip-boundary-condition.md).

With uniform [gravity](gravity.md), write $p=p_h+p'$ where the [hydrostatic pressure](hydrostatic-pressure.md) satisfies $\nabla p_h=\rho\mathbf g$. Then the disturbance equation is $-\nabla p'+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf u=\mathbf 0$. This removes [gravity](gravity.md) from the fluid disturbance equation, while the [hydrostatic pressure](hydrostatic-pressure.md) still supplies [buoyancy](buoyancy.md) on an immersed body. Omitting it from the total [force](force.md) balance would give the wrong [Stokes settling velocity](stokes-settling-velocity.md).
