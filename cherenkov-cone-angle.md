# Cherenkov cone angle

↑ **Parent:** [Cherenkov radiation](cherenkov-radiation.md)

Let a [charged particle](charged-particle.md) travel along $+z$ at constant [speed](speed.md) $v=\beta c$, with $0<\beta<1$. For a [plane electromagnetic wave](plane-electromagnetic-wave.md) with [wave phase](phase-waves.md) $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t$, two points along the track separated by time $\Delta t$ contribute with phase difference

$$
\Delta\phi=(kv\cos\theta-\omega)\Delta t,
\qquad k=\frac{n(\omega)\omega}{c}.
$$

Here $\theta$ is the [angle](angle.md) between the wave's [wave vector](wavevector.md) and the forward particle track. Constructive addition along a long uniform track requires $kv\cos\theta=\omega$. Thus

$$
\cos\theta=\frac{1}{\beta n(\omega)},\qquad
\beta n(\omega)>1.
$$

A real forward cone with nonzero opening exists only above this threshold. At $\beta n=1$ the cone closes and the ideal [Frank-Tamm formula](frank-tamm-formula.md) gives zero yield. For $\beta n<1$ there is no allowed [angle](angle.md), rather than an imaginary observable cone. The requirement is [phase matching](phase-matching.md); substituting the [group refractive index](group-refractive-index.md) would describe a different condition.

There is a second geometric derivation when $n$ is constant. In time $\Delta t$, the charge advances $OP=v\Delta t$, while an earlier spherical phase front has radius $OQ=c\Delta t/n$. Its tangent envelope passes through the current position $P$. The radius $OQ$ is normal to the envelope, so the right triangle gives $\cos\theta=OQ/OP$. Rotating about the track makes a cone. Its surface half-angle $\eta$ is complementary to the emission [angle](angle.md) $\theta$, not equal to it.

![](cherenkov-wavefront-cone-geometry.png)

**[Figure 10](#image-original-cherenkov-radiation-geometry-phase-fronts-their-tangent-cone-and-the-emission-normal). Original Cherenkov radiation geometry: phase fronts, their tangent cone, and the emission normal**. Chosen constant [refractive index](refractive-index.md) $n=1.50$ and particle [speed](speed.md) $v=0.90c$. The circles are phase fronts at one time from successive earlier track positions. The orange normal makes $\theta\simeq42.21^\circ$ with the track; the tangent cone makes $\eta\simeq47.79^\circ$. This is a nondispersive geometric construction, not a measured particle track or a broadband pulse envelope.

In a dispersive medium, apply the [phase matching](phase-matching.md) derivation separately at each [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md). The circle construction uses one fixed phase speed; it does not determine the envelope of a broadband [wave packet](wave-packet.md). In particular, [group velocity](group-velocity.md) controls packet propagation under its usual narrow-band assumptions, and the simple complementary-angle construction need not describe a dispersive pulse front. A finite track also broadens the ideal directional selection: adding contributions over duration $T$ gives a factor proportional to $\sin(\Delta T/2)/\Delta$, with $\Delta=kv\cos\theta-\omega$, rather than an infinitely sharp direction.
