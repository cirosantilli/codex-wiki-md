# Condensation, matter-wave coherence and superfluidity

↑ **Parent:** [Dilute-gas Bose-Einstein condensation](dilute-gas-bose-einstein-condensation.md)

[Bose-Einstein condensation](bose-einstein-condensation.md) concerns occupation of a [one-particle state](one-particle-state.md). [Matter-wave coherence](matter-wave-coherence.md) concerns field correlations between positions or times. [Superfluidity](superfluidity.md) concerns a flow response, including suppression of dissipation under appropriate conditions. A density peak diagnoses neither a relative [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md) between two separately prepared clouds nor a critical flow speed. These observables require different experiments.

For example, in a single-mode fixed-number [Fock state](fock-state.md), $\langle\hat a\rangle=0$ but $\langle\hat a^\dagger\hat a\rangle=N$. Writing $\hat\psi(\mathbf r)=\phi(\mathbf r)\hat a$ for that occupied mode gives

$$
\langle\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf r)\hat\psi(\mathbf r')\rangle
=N\phi^*(\mathbf r)\phi(\mathbf r').
$$

Thus vanishing mean field does not imply vanishing first-order [matter-wave coherence](matter-wave-coherence.md). For two independent fixed-number modes, $\langle\hat a^\dagger\hat b\rangle=0$: there is no ensemble mean interference term establishing a fixed relative [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md). Interpreting fringes in an individual detection record requires considering correlations and the measurement; assigning a pre-existing definite relative [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md) is unnecessary.

[Wolfgang Ketterle](wolfgang-ketterle.md)'s lecture describes a separate coherence test: two condensates expanded into one another and produced fringes. Selectively removing one condensate made the fringes disappear on alternating preparation cycles, testing against diffraction from the confinement. This compared two-cloud and one-cloud signals; it did not measure dissipationless flow.[pp. 1143–1145, Fig. 15, condensate-elimination control](nobel-lecture-when-atoms-behave-as-waves-bose-einstein-condensation-and-the-atom-laser-by-wolfgang-ketterle.md)
