# Finite-system analyticity

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamic limit](thermodynamic-limit.md)

For finitely many [microstates](microstate.md) with finite [energies](energy-split.md) $E_\alpha(h)$ that are [real analytic functions](real-analytic-function.md) of a real control $h$, the [canonical partition function](canonical-partition-function.md) at $T>0$ is a finite positive sum:

$$
Z_N(T,h)=\sum_\alpha e^{-E_\alpha(h)/(k_BT)}>0.
$$

It and the resulting [Helmholtz free energy](helmholtz-free-energy.md) are locally [real analytic functions](real-analytic-function.md) of $T$ and $h$. Hence such a finite [Ising model](ising-model.md) has no literal nonanalytic equilibrium [phase transition](phase-transition.md). A limit of these [real analytic functions](real-analytic-function.md) can nevertheless fail to be a [real analytic function](real-analytic-function.md); the narrow but smooth crossover can become a sharp [phase transition](phase-transition.md).[§1.2.1, discussion following equation (1.14): finite sums, analyticity, and macroscopic sharpness](statistical-field-theory-by-david-tong/1-from-spins-to-fields.md)

Finite particle number alone is not the hypothesis of a finite sum. Classical [phase space](phase-space.md) is continuous, and a system of [quantum states](quantum-state.md) can have infinitely many [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue-split.md): its [partition function](canonical-partition-function.md) requires convergence and local bounds that permit differentiation or an analytic extension of the integral or [thermal trace](thermal-trace.md). A divergent [partition function](canonical-partition-function.md), a singularly changed [statistical Hamiltonian](statistical-hamiltonian.md), or the zero-[temperature](temperature-split.md) limit falls outside the finite-positive-sum argument. Observing a steep change in a finite sample therefore motivates a limiting model; it does not measure an actual infinity.
