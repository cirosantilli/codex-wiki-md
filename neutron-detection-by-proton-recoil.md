# Neutron detection by proton recoil

↑ **Parent:** [Neutron](neutron-split.md)

In [neutron detection by proton recoil](neutron-detection-by-proton-recoil.md), a neutron strikes a [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md), namely a [proton](proton.md), and the moving [proton](proton.md) produces detectable [ionization](ionization.md). [Paraffin wax](paraffin-wax.md) provides many [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) targets. There are two distinct questions: how much [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) can a collision transfer, and how often does it occur? [Elastic collision](elastic-collision.md) kinematics answers the first. It does not determine a [collision cross-section](collision-cross-section.md) or a detector's efficiency.

For the following derivations the target is initially at rest in the laboratory but is free to recoil. Its [mass](mass.md) is finite. In particular a [proton](proton.md) must not be treated as an immovable target. All massive particles are [nonrelativistic particles](nonrelativistic-particle.md), their translational [kinetic energies](kinetic-energy.md) are $mv^2/2$, and the collision changes no internal state. Target binding and thermal motion are neglected relative to the incident [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md). These assumptions suit an introductory description of fast-neutron [elastic collisions](elastic-collision.md); they do not cover every [neutron capture](neutron-capture.md) or other [nuclear reaction](nuclear-reaction.md).
