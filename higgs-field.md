# Higgs field

↑ **Parent:** [Electroweak interaction](electroweak-interaction.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_field)

The Standard Model Higgs field is a complex $SU(2)_L$ doublet of hypercharge $1/2$. Its nonzero vacuum expectation value triggers the [Higgs mechanism](higgs-mechanism.md).

The [Higgs field](higgs-field.md) is a [scalar field](scalar-field.md): its components carry internal [weak isospin](weak-isospin.md) and [hypercharge](hypercharge.md), but no spacetime [spin](spin.md). A complex [Higgs doublet](higgs-field.md) contains four real field components. The [vacuum expectation value](vacuum-expectation-value.md) is a property of the lowest-[energy](energy-split.md) state, not a gas of [Higgs bosons](higgs-boson.md). A [Higgs boson](higgs-boson.md) is an excitation about that state.

Why introduce this field? A bare mass term for a [gauge field](gauge-field.md) generally fails to respect the local [gauge symmetry](gauge-invariance.md). A bare [Dirac mass term](dirac-mass-term.md) for a charged [Standard Model fermion](standard-model-fermion.md) also joins left- and right-handed fields with different [Standard Model representations](standard-model-representation.md). A [Higgs doublet](higgs-field.md) permits [gauge-invariant](gauge-invariance.md) interactions whose expansion about a nonzero [vacuum expectation value](vacuum-expectation-value.md) supplies both masses and definite [Higgs boson](higgs-boson.md) interactions. The field therefore links the mass problem to experimentally testable coupling relations, as illustrated by the [Higgs boson coupling to Z bosons](higgs-boson-coupling-to-z-bosons.md).
