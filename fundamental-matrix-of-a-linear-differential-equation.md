# Fundamental matrix of a linear differential equation

↑ **Parent:** [Linear differential equation](linear-differential-equation.md)

For a system of [linear differential equations](linear-differential-equation.md) $u'=A(t)u$, a fundamental matrix has columns forming a [basis](basis.md) of its solution space. It solves $X'=A(t)X$ and is invertible at every time in its interval. Every solution is $u(t)=X(t)c$ for a constant vector $c$: differentiating $X^{-1}u$ gives zero. Invertibility at one time implies invertibility throughout the interval by uniqueness of the [initial value problem](initial-value-problem.md). This notion is distinct from a Markov-chain fundamental matrix or the projective-geometric matrix used in stereo vision.
