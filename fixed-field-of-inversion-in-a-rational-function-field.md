# Fixed field of inversion in a rational function field

↑ **Parent:** [Rational function field](rational-function-field.md)

Let $t$ be transcendental over a field $k$ and let $\tau$ be the [field automorphism](field-automorphism.md) sending $t$ to $1/t$. The rational function $u=t+1/t$ is fixed, and $t$ satisfies $X^2-uX+1=0$. Thus the extension over $k(u)$ has degree at most two. It is not one because the nontrivial automorphism $\tau$ fixes $k(u)$. The [fixed field](fixed-field.md) of $\tau$ also has index two by the fixed-field theorem, so it equals $k(u)$.

No nonconstant [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) in $k[t]$ is fixed by inversion. For a polynomial of positive degree, substitution of $1/t$ creates negative powers, whereas the original has positive powers. Uniqueness of the coefficients of a [Laurent polynomial](laurent-polynomial.md) rules out equality. Consequently this fixed field cannot have a generator that is a polynomial in the original variable, even though it has the simple rational-function generator above. The statement is valid in characteristic two as well: $t$ and $1/t$ remain distinct transcendental rational functions.
