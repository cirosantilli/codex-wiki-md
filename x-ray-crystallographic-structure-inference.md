# X-ray crystallographic structure inference

↑ **Parent:** [Crystallography](crystallography.md)

[X-ray crystallographic structure inference](x-ray-crystallographic-structure-inference.md) is an [inverse problem](inverse-problem-split.md): infer a [crystal structure](crystal-structure.md) from measured [X-ray](x-ray.md) [diffraction](diffraction.md). A proposed [unit cell](unit-cell.md) and [atomic basis of a crystal](atomic-basis-of-a-crystal.md) predict a pattern; structure determination asks which proposals survive comparison with that pattern. The useful separation is between directions and strengths. Peak positions constrain the [reciprocal lattice](reciprocal-lattice.md) and hence [interplanar spacings](interplanar-spacing.md); relative [wave intensities](intensity-physics.md) constrain the motif through the [crystal basis structure factor](crystal-basis-structure-factor.md). Knowing a repeat distance does not locate all the [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) within that repeat.

The [lattice constant from two indexed reflections](lattice-constant-from-two-indexed-reflections.md) example isolates the geometric inference. [Basis intensities and phase ambiguity](basis-intensities-and-phase-ambiguity.md) then holds the translations fixed while changing the motif. Even complete, noiseless [wave intensity](intensity-physics.md) data need not determine all [wave phases](phase-waves.md): this is the [phase problem](phase-problem.md). Chemical composition, [symmetry](symmetry-physics.md), and additional measurements can supply constraints beyond the original pattern.[Online Dictionary of Crystallography by International Union of Crystallography; Definition and Methods of solution: intensities determine structure-factor magnitudes but not phases; additional structural information enters phase estimation](online-dictionary-of-crystallography-by-international-union-of-crystallography/phase-problem.md)
