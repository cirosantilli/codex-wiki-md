# Exponential ground state of a linearly weighted Sturm-Liouville problem

↑ **Parent:** [Sturm-Liouville problem](sturm-liouville-problem.md)

For $\omega>0$ on $x>0$, use the regular finite branch at zero, decay at infinity, and a finite quadratic-form energy. For trial functions with $[xy^2]_0^\infty=0$, [integration by parts](integration-by-parts.md) gives

$$
\int_0^\infty x(y'^2+\omega^2y^2)dx=\omega\int_0^\infty y^2dx+\int_0^\infty x(y'+\omega y)^2dx.
$$

The [Rayleigh quotient](rayleigh-quotient.md) is at least $\omega$, with equality exactly for a nonzero multiple of $e^{-\omega x}$. Direct substitution verifies that this is an [eigenfunction](eigenfunction.md) with [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) $\omega$, so the lower bound is attained. The zero coefficient $p(0)=0$ makes this a singular-endpoint [Sturm-Liouville problem](sturm-liouville-problem.md); regularity replaces a prescribed zero endpoint value. Continuous piecewise differentiable trials of finite form energy are allowed in the [Rayleigh-Ritz method](rayleigh-ritz-method.md), even if their derivative has a jump away from the endpoint.
