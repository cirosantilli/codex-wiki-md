# Two-population molar mass example

↑ **Parent:** [Molar mass](molar-mass.md)

Suppose a synthetic [polymer](polymer-split.md) population contains three chains of [molar mass](molar-mass.md) $10000\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$ for every one chain of [molar mass](molar-mass.md) $40000\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$. Multiplying both chain counts by any common factor leaves the averages unchanged. Substitution into the existing [number-average molar mass](number-average-molar-mass.md) and [weight-average molar mass](weight-average-molar-mass.md) definitions gives

$$
M_n=\frac{3(10000)+40000}{4}\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}=17500\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}},\qquad M_w=\frac{3(10000)^2+(40000)^2}{3(10000)+40000}\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\approx27143\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

The larger chains are only one quarter of the chain count but supply $40000/70000=4/7$ of the [mass](mass.md). Thus a [weighted mean](weighted-arithmetic-mean.md) that emphasizes [mass](mass.md) gives a larger result than one that weights each [molecule](molecule-split.md) equally. Total [mass](mass.md) divided by total chain [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) gives $M_n$, while $M_w/N_{\mathrm A}$ is not the mean [mass](mass.md) of a randomly chosen chain. Inferring chain counts from [size-exclusion chromatography](size-exclusion-chromatography.md) still requires the existing [SEC detector response](sec-detector-response.md) and [SEC molar mass calibration](sec-molar-mass-calibration.md) assumptions.
