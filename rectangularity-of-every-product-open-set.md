# Rectangularity of every product-open set

↑ **Parent:** [Product topology](product-topology.md)

Every open subset of a product of two [topological spaces](topological-space.md) is a rectangle exactly when at least one factor has the [indiscrete topology](indiscrete-topology.md). For necessity, if both factors have nonempty proper [open sets](open-set.md) $U\subsetneq X$, $V\subsetneq Y$, then $(U\times Y)\cup(X\times V)$ is open, projects onto both full factors, and omits every point of $(X\setminus U)\times(Y\setminus V)$. A rectangle with both full projections would be the whole product, a contradiction. For sufficiency, if $X$ is indiscrete, every basic open rectangle is $X\times V$ or empty; an arbitrary union is $X\times\bigcup V$. The same argument works when $Y$ is indiscrete. Empty factors cause no exception because their topology is indiscrete.
