<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the [commutator](../../../../commutator.md) convention $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$, and let $[A,B]$ denote the [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) generated by all such commutators with $x\in A,y\in B$. The [lower central series](../../../../lower-central-series.md) is

$$
\gamma_1(G)=G,\qquad\gamma_{i+1}(G)=[\gamma_i(G),G].
$$

A [central series](../../../../central-series.md) has $[G_j,G]\le G_{j-1}$ for each $j\ge1$. We prove the requested inclusion by induction. At $i=0$, $\gamma_1(G)=G_n$. If $i<n$ and $\gamma_{i+1}(G)\le G_{n-i}$, then

$$
\gamma_{i+2}(G)=[\gamma_{i+1}(G),G]
\le[G_{n-i},G]\le G_{n-i-1}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\gamma_{i+1}(G)\le G_{n-i}\quad(0\le i\le n).}
$$

In particular $\gamma_{n+1}(G)=1$. The proof uses the central-series condition directly; no equality of the intermediate terms is claimed.
