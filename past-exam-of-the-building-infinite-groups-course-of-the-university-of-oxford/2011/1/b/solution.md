<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Suppose inductively that $G=H\gamma_i(G)$. In the [quotient](../../../../quotient.md) $G/\gamma_{i+1}(G)$, the image of $\gamma_i(G)$ is central. Hence this [quotient](../../../../quotient.md) is generated by the image of $H$ together with a central [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md), so its [commutator subgroup](../../../../commutator-subgroup.md) lies in the image of $H$. Pulling this back gives

$$
\gamma_2(G)\le H\gamma_{i+1}(G).
$$

The original assumption now gives $G=H\gamma_2(G)\le H\gamma_{i+1}(G)\le G$. Starting with $i=2$, induction proves $G=H\gamma_i(G)$ for every $i\ge2$. These products are subgroups because each $\gamma_i(G)$ is normal; no [normality](../../../../normality-statistics.md) of $H$ is needed.

If $G$ is finitely generated, every [quotient](../../../../quotient.md), including its [abelianization](../../../../abelianization.md) $G/\gamma_2(G)$, is finitely generated. Conversely, lift a finite [generating set](../../../../generating-set.md) of that [quotient](../../../../quotient.md) to $g_1,\ldots,g_t\in G$, and set $H=\langle g_1,\ldots,g_t\rangle$. Then $G=H\gamma_2(G)$. For a [nilpotent group](../../../../nilpotent-group.md), $\gamma_{c+1}(G)=1$ for some $c$, and the established identity gives $G=H$. Thus

$$
\boxed{G\text{ finitely generated}\iff G/\gamma_2(G)\text{ finitely generated}.}
$$
