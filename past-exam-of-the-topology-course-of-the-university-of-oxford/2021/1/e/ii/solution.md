<h1 id="1/e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

**$X$ does not have [sequential compactness](../../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md).** Take the [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md)

$$
x_n=1-\frac1n\qquad(n\geq1).
$$

Every [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md) has usual [limit of a sequence](../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md) $1$. If a [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md) converged in the [subspace topology](../../../../../subspace-topology.md) of the [Sorgenfrey line](../../../../../lower-limit-topology.md), it would also converge in the usual [subspace topology](../../../../../subspace-topology.md), since part (c) makes the [identity function](../../../../../identity-function.md) from the former to the latter [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md). Uniqueness of a [limit of a sequence](../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md) in the usual [Hausdorff space](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) therefore forces that [limit of a sequence](../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md) to be $1$.

But $\{1\}$ is an [open](../../../../../open-set.md) [neighbourhood](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of $1$ in $X$, and no term $x_n$ belongs to it. Thus no [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md) converges to $1$ in $X$, proving the assertion.
