**Yes, $[0,1]$ is [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md).** Its [complement](../../../../../complement-of-a-set.md) is $(-\infty,0)\cup(1,\infty)$. Every $x<0$ has the basic [open](../../../../../open-set.md) [neighbourhood](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) $[x,0)$ contained in this [complement](../../../../../complement-of-a-set.md), and every $x>1$ has $[x,x+1)$ contained in it. Hence the [complement](../../../../../complement-of-a-set.md) is [open](../../../../../open-set.md) in $\mathbb R_{\mathcal T}$.
