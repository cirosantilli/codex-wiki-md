<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [c](../c.md)

Let $U$ be [open](../../../../open-set.md) in the [Euclidean topology](../../../../euclidean-topology.md) on the [real line](../../../../real-line.md), and let $x\in U$. There is an $\varepsilon>0$ with $(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)\subseteq U$. The basic [open set](../../../../open-set.md) $[x,x+\varepsilon)$ contains $x$ and is contained in $U$. Taking the [union](../../../../set-union.md) of these basic [neighbourhoods](../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) over $x\in U$ shows that $U\in\mathcal T$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathcal T_{\mathrm{usual}}\subseteq\mathcal T.}
$$

In fact this is a strictly [finer topology](../../../../finer-topology.md): $[0,1)$ is [open](../../../../open-set.md) in $\mathcal T$ but not in the [Euclidean topology](../../../../euclidean-topology.md), since no usual [open ball](../../../../open-ball.md) about $0$ is contained in it.
