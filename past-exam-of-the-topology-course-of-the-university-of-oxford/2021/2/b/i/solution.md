<h1 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [i](../i.md)

The [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) $S^2$ is [path-connected](../../../../../path-connected-space.md). For $u,v\in S^2$ which are not an [antipodal pair](../../../../../antipodal-pair.md), a [continuous path](../../../../../continuous-path.md) is

$$
\gamma(t)=\frac{(1-t)u+tv}{\|(1-t)u+tv\|},\qquad 0\leq t\leq1.
$$

The denominator never vanishes: a zero would make $u$ and $v$ antipodal. If $v=-u$, choose a [unit vector](../../../../../unit-vector.md) $w$ perpendicular to $u$ and concatenate the paths from $u$ to $w$ and from $w$ to $v$ of the same form. Thus $S^2$ is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md). The [quotient map](../../../../../quotient-map.md) $q_S$ is [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) and [surjective](../../../../../surjective-function.md), so the [continuous image of a connected space](../../../../../continuous-image-of-a-connected-space.md) theorem proves that **$\mathbb{RP}^2$ is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md)**. In fact composing the paths above with $q_S$ also proves [path connectedness](../../../../../path-connected-space.md).
