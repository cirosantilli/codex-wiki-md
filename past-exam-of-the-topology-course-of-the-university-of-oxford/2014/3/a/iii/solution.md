<h1 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) stars form an [open](../../../../../open-set.md) cover of $|K|$: every point has a nonempty positive-coordinate support, and therefore belongs to the star of at least one [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md). At a [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) $w$ itself, only its own barycentric coordinate is positive. Thus $w$ belongs to its own star and to no other [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) star.

Any finite selection of stars omits every [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) not among their finitely many labels. Infinitely many vertices therefore prevent a finite subcover, proving

$$
\boxed{|V|=\infty\Longrightarrow |K|\text{ is not compact}.}
$$

No local-finiteness hypothesis is needed for this [vertex-star cover obstruction to compactness](../../../../../vertex-star-cover-obstruction-to-compactness.md).
