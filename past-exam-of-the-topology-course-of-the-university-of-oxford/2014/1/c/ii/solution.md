<h1 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a nonempty [open subset](../../../../../open-set.md) $U$ of the quotient, $p^{-1}(U)$ is nonempty and [open](../../../../../open-set.md) in the cofinite space. Therefore

$$
p^{-1}\bigl((X/\mathcal R)\setminus U\bigr)=X\setminus p^{-1}(U)
$$

is finite. [Surjectivity](../../../../../surjective-function.md) implies that $(X/\mathcal R)\setminus U$ is finite as well: it is the [image](../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) of that finite [preimage](../../../../../preimage.md). Hence every quotient-open set is cofinite or empty, proving the desired inclusion.

**Equality is not necessary.** Give $X=\mathbb Z$ its [cofinite topology](../../../../../cofinite-topology.md) and use two equivalence classes, the even and odd integers. Neither singleton of the quotient is [open](../../../../../open-set.md), since its [preimage](../../../../../preimage.md) and its [complement](../../../../../complement-of-a-set.md) are both infinite. The quotient is the two-point [indiscrete topology](../../../../../indiscrete-topology.md), whereas the [cofinite topology](../../../../../cofinite-topology.md) on a finite two-point set is discrete.

$$
\boxed{\mathcal T_{\mathrm{quotient}}\subsetneq\mathcal T_{\mathrm{cofinite}}\text{ can occur}.}
$$

This is the [quotient topology of a cofinite space](../../../../../quotient-topology-of-a-cofinite-space.md) phenomenon, not a failure of [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) of the projection.
