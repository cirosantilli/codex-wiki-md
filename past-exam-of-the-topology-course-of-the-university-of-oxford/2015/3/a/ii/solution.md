<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

List the finite vertex set as $V=\{v_1,\ldots,v_N\}$. Map a point to its [barycentric coordinates](../../../../../barycentric-coordinate-system.md):

$$
\boxed{f:|K|\longrightarrow\mathbb R^N,\qquad f\left(\sum_jt_jv_j\right)=(t_1,\ldots,t_N).}
$$

On each simplex this is an [affine map](../../../../../affine-map.md) and therefore [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md). The definitions agree on every common face, so the [quotient topology](../../../../../quotient-topology.md) defining the realization makes $f$ continuous on all of $|K|$. Barycentric coordinates are unique after zero coordinates are added, so $f$ is [injective](../../../../../injective-function.md).

Since [Euclidean space](../../../../../euclidean-norm.md) is [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md), distinct images have disjoint open neighbourhoods. Their inverse images separate the original points. Hence **$|K|$ is [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md)**. A continuous injection into a Hausdorff space is enough for this conclusion; no prior Hausdorff assumption on the domain is being used.
