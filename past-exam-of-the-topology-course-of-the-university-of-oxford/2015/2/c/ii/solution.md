<h1 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use $F:[0,1]\to S^1$, $F(t)=e^{2\pi it}$. This is [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md), identifies the two endpoints, and is otherwise injective. Its fibres are exactly the [equivalence classes](../../../../../equivalence-class.md) for [collapsing a subset to a point](../../../../../collapsing-a-subset-to-a-point.md) with $A=\{0,1\}$. Hence it factors uniquely through a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md)

$$
\overline F:X_A\longrightarrow S^1,\qquad\overline F([t])=e^{2\pi it}.
$$

By the [universal property of the quotient topology](../../../../../universal-property-of-the-quotient-topology.md), $\overline F$ is [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md). The interval is [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md), so its quotient $X_A$ is compact. The [unit circle](../../../../../complex-unit-circle.md) is [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md). The [compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem](../../../../../compact-to-hausdorff-continuous-bijection-theorem.md) now shows that $\overline F$ is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../homeomorphism.md):

$$
\boxed{[0,1]/\{0\sim1\}\cong S^1.}
$$

Indeed the theorem makes the inverse continuous because images of closed subsets of a compact space are compact and hence closed in a Hausdorff target.
