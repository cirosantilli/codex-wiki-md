<h1 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Since $K$ misses $A$, the open set $U=X\setminus K$ contains the whole collapsed [equivalence class](../../../../../equivalence-class.md) $A$. Every other [equivalence class](../../../../../equivalence-class.md) is a [singleton](../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md), so $U$ is saturated under the [saturation of a subset](../../../../../saturation-of-a-subset.md) operation:

$$
p^{-1}(p(U))=U.
$$

Its preimage is open, so the [quotient topology](../../../../../quotient-topology.md) gives

$$
\boxed{p(X\setminus K)\text{ is open in }X_A.}
$$

The hypothesis $K\cap A=\varnothing$ is exactly what prevents the [saturation of a subset](../../../../../saturation-of-a-subset.md) from adding missing points of the collapsed class.
