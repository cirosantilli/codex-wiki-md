<h1 id="2/c/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

**No countable subset works.** For any [countable set](../../../../../countable-set.md) $A\subseteq\mathbb R$, choose $b_n\in(n,n+1/2)\setminus A$: each nonempty interval is [uncountable](../../../../../uncountable-set.md). The sequence tends to infinity, so every tail

$$
K_n=\{b_k:k\geq n\}
$$

is [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md) in $\mathbb R$ and disjoint from $A$. By part (i), $p(\mathbb R\setminus K_n)$ is open in $X_A$. These sets increase and cover $X_A$, since no point belongs to every tail. But any finite selection is contained in its largest member, which still misses every quotient point $p(b_k)$ in that member's remaining tail. The $b_k$ represent distinct singleton classes.

This is an [open cover](../../../../../open-cover.md) without a [finite subcover](../../../../../finite-subcover.md), proving

$$
\boxed{A\text{ countable}\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathbb R_A\text{ is not compact}.}
$$

The argument does not assume that $A$ is closed, bounded, or discrete. It is the [noncompactness after collapsing a countable real subset](../../../../../noncompactness-after-collapsing-a-countable-real-subset.md) obstruction.
