<h1 id="2/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Every quotient class has a representative in $[0,1]$: the collapsed class has representatives $0,1$, and the others are the points of $(0,1)$. Thus $p|_{[0,1]}$ is a continuous surjection from a [compact interval](../../../../../compact-interval.md) onto $X_A$. It follows that **$X_A$ is [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md)**, despite the noncompact original space.

Define the continuous map

$$
F(x)=\begin{cases}e^{2\pi ix},&0\leq x\leq1,\\1,&x\leq0\text{ or }x\geq1.\end{cases}
$$

The formulas agree at both junctions, and its fibres are exactly the required [equivalence classes](../../../../../equivalence-class.md). It induces a continuous bijection $X_A\to S^1$, which is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) by compactness and the [Hausdorff condition](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) on the [circle](../../../../../circle.md).

$$
\boxed{\mathbb R/\bigl(({-\infty},0]\cup[1,\infty)\bigr)\cong S^1.}
$$

The quotient notation means that the displayed entire subset becomes one point.
