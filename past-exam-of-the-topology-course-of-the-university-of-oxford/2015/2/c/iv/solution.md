<h1 id="2/c/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**Collapsing the integers gives a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md).** For two distinct noninteger points, choose disjoint small open intervals around them that avoid $\mathbb Z$. Each interval is saturated under the [saturation of a subset](../../../../../saturation-of-a-subset.md) operation, so its image is an open neighbourhood of its class. For a noninteger $x$ and the collapsed class, choose $0<r<\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb Z)$. The disjoint open sets

$$
U=(x-r,x+r),\qquad V=\mathbb R\setminus[x-r,x+r]
$$

are saturated; $U$ contains $x$ and $V$ contains all integers. Their images separate the two quotient points. These exhaust all pairs of distinct classes.

**Collapsing the rationals does not give a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md).** The preimage of the singleton collapsed point is $\mathbb Q$, which is not [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md) in $\mathbb R$. Therefore that quotient singleton is not closed, whereas every singleton of a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) is closed.

$$
\boxed{X_{\mathbb Z}\text{ is Hausdorff},\qquad X_{\mathbb Q}\text{ is not Hausdorff}.}
$$

Equivalently, every nonempty saturated open subset of $\mathbb R$ meets the [dense set](../../../../../dense-set.md) $\mathbb Q$ and therefore contains it all. Every nonempty quotient open set contains the collapsed point, making separation from any other quotient point impossible.
