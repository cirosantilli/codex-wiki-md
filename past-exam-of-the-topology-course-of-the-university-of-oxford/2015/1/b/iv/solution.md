<h1 id="1/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Every [topological subspace](../../../../../topological-subspace.md) $S\subseteq X$ again has the [cofinite topology](../../../../../cofinite-topology.md): its nonempty [open subsets](../../../../../open-set.md) have finite complements in $S$, and every finite exclusion is obtained by intersecting with a cofinite open subset of $X$. As usual, the [empty set](../../../../../empty-set.md) is also open; this exception is implicit in the paper's shorthand definition.

If $S$ is infinite, any two nonempty relatively [open sets](../../../../../open-set.md) intersect, since their complements are finite. Hence $S$ is [hyperconnected space](../../../../../hyperconnected-space.md) and therefore [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md). If $S$ is finite, its [cofinite topology](../../../../../cofinite-topology.md) is [discrete topology](../../../../../discrete-space.md). It is then [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) exactly when it has at most one point. Thus

$$
\boxed{S\text{ connected}\iff |S|\leq1\text{ or }S\text{ is infinite}.}
$$

There are also **infinite disconnected subsets of the product**. Choose distinct $a,b\in X$ and put $D=\{a,b\}\times X$. This is infinite. The traces on $D$ of the ambient [open sets](../../../../../open-set.md)

$$
(X\setminus\{b\})\times X,\qquad (X\setminus\{a\})\times X
$$

are respectively $\{a\}\times X$ and $\{b\}\times X$. They are disjoint, nonempty and cover $D$, giving a [separation](../../../../../axiom-schema-of-specification.md). This answers the infinite-subset question, rather than only producing two isolated product points.
