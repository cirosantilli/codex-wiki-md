<h1 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Choose $x_0\in X\setminus A$ and $y_0\in Y\setminus B$, possible because the two subsets are proper. The cross

$$
H=(X\times\{y_0\})\cup(\{x_0\}\times Y)
$$

lies in the indicated complement. Its two arms are [homeomorphic](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) to the [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) factors and meet at $(x_0,y_0)$, so $H$ is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) by part (a)(ii).

For any remaining point $(x,y)$, either $x\notin A$ or $y\notin B$. In the first case the whole [connected subset](../../../../../connected-subset.md) $\{x\}\times Y$ lies in the complement and meets $H$ at $(x,y_0)$. In the second case use $X\times\{y\}$, which meets $H$ at $(x_0,y)$. These connected fibres together with $H$ cover the complement. The [connected union with a connected hub](../../../../../connected-union-with-a-connected-hub.md) argument therefore gives

$$
\boxed{(X\times Y)\setminus(A\times B)\text{ is connected}.}
$$

Neither proper subset needs to be [open](../../../../../open-set.md), [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md), or [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md).
