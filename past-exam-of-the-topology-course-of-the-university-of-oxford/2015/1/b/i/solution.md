<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the [basis of a topology](../../../../../basis-of-a-topology.md) consisting of open rectangles:

$$
\boxed{\mathcal B=\{U\times V:U\in\mathcal T_X,\ V\in\mathcal T_Y\}.}
$$

It covers $X\times Y$, since $X\times Y\in\mathcal B$. Whenever a point lies in two basis rectangles, their [intersection](../../../../../set-intersection.md) is another basis rectangle containing it:

$$
(U_1\times V_1)\cap(U_2\times V_2)=(U_1\cap U_2)\times(V_1\cap V_2).
$$

These are precisely the two [basis of a topology](../../../../../basis-of-a-topology.md) axioms. Arbitrary [unions](../../../../../set-union.md) of the rectangles therefore form a [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md), the [product topology](../../../../../product-topology.md). To check the finite-intersection axiom directly, distribute the intersection of two unions over all pairs of rectangles and use the displayed formula. The empty union gives the [empty set](../../../../../empty-set.md).
