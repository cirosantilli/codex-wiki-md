<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If both factors are [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md), two distinct product points differ in at least one coordinate. Disjoint [open neighbourhoods](../../../../../open-neighbourhood.md) in that coordinate, multiplied by the other whole factor, give disjoint [open neighbourhoods](../../../../../open-neighbourhood.md) of the product points. Thus the product is [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md).

Conversely, assume the product is [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md). Fix $y_0\in Y$, using nonemptiness. For $x\ne x'$, choose disjoint [open neighbourhoods](../../../../../open-neighbourhood.md) of $(x,y_0)$ and $(x',y_0)$ and refine them to basis rectangles $U\times V$ and $U'\times V'$. Since $y_0\in V\cap V'$, their disjointness forces $U\cap U'=\varnothing$. These separate $x,x'$, proving that $X$ is [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md). Fixing a point of $X$ proves the same for $Y$.

$$
\boxed{X\times Y\text{ Hausdorff}\iff X,Y\text{ Hausdorff},\quad X,Y\ne\varnothing.}
$$

The nonempty hypothesis is essential: a product with the [empty set](../../../../../empty-set.md) is empty regardless of the other factor. This is the [Hausdorff property of a nonempty product](../../../../../hausdorff-property-of-a-nonempty-product.md).
