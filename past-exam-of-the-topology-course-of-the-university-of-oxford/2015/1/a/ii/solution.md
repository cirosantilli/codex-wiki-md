<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose $A\cup B=U\cup V$ were a [separation](../../../../../axiom-schema-of-specification.md). By [connectedness](../../../../../connected-space.md), all of $A$ lies on one side, say $U$: otherwise $A\cap U,A\cap V$ would separate $A$. A point of $A\cap B$ then lies in $B\cap U$, so [connectedness](../../../../../connected-space.md) of $B$ forces all of $B$ into $U$ as well. This makes $V$ empty, a contradiction.

Therefore **the union of two intersecting [connected subsets](../../../../../connected-subset.md) is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md)**. The argument also proves the [connected union with a connected hub](../../../../../connected-union-with-a-connected-hub.md) rule for arbitrarily many connected pieces meeting one connected subset.
