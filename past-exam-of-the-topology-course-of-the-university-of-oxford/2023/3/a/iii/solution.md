A [topological surface](../../../../../topological-surface-split.md) is a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) with a countable [basis of a topology](../../../../../basis-of-a-topology.md) in which every point has an [open](../../../../../open-set.md) [neighbourhood](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) [homeomorphic](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) to an [open subset](../../../../../open-set.md) of the [Euclidean plane](../../../../../euclidean-plane.md). We use the convention that a [surface](../../../../../topological-surface-split.md) has no boundary unless a [surface with boundary](../../../../../surface-with-boundary.md) is explicitly specified. A closed [surface](../../../../../topological-surface-split.md) is [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) and has no boundary. Some conventions also include [connectedness](../../../../../connected-space.md) in the definition; we impose it on the [closed combinatorial surface](../../../../../closed-combinatorial-surface.md) below.

A [closed combinatorial surface](../../../../../closed-combinatorial-surface.md) is a finite [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) [simplicial complex](../../../../../simplicial-complex.md) $K$ with the following properties: every [simplex](../../../../../simplex.md) is a [face of a simplex](../../../../../face-of-a-simplex.md) of dimension two, and the [link of a simplex](../../../../../link-of-a-simplex.md) at every vertex is a [cycle graph](../../../../../cycle-graph.md). Explicitly, for a vertex $v$ its link is the [simplicial subcomplex](../../../../../simplicial-subcomplex.md)

$$
\operatorname{lk}_K(v)=\{\rho\in K:v\notin\rho,\ \rho\cup\{v\}\in K\}.
$$

To say this is a [cycle graph](../../../../../cycle-graph.md) means that there are distinct vertices $w_1,\ldots,w_m$, with $m\geq3$, and exactly the link [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $\{w_i,w_{i+1}\}$, with indices modulo $m$. In particular, every edge of $K$ belongs to exactly two triangles. The vertex-link condition also requires that the triangles around each vertex form one cyclic fan; edge incidence alone would allow singular examples.

These conditions make $|K|$ a closed [surface](../../../../../topological-surface-split.md). A point inside a triangle has a disc [neighbourhood](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md), the two triangles at an edge give a disc [neighbourhood](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md), and the cyclic fan at a vertex gives a disc [neighbourhood](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md). Finiteness gives [compactness](../../../../../compact-space.md), the [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) property, and a countable [basis of a topology](../../../../../basis-of-a-topology.md) from a realization in a finite-dimensional [Euclidean space](../../../../../euclidean-norm.md). If disconnected [closed combinatorial surfaces](../../../../../closed-combinatorial-surface.md) are allowed, each [connected component](../../../../../connected-component.md) satisfies this definition separately.
