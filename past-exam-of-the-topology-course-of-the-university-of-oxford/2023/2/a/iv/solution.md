<h1 id="2/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Any two nonempty [open sets](../../../../../open-set.md) of the infinite [cofinite topology](../../../../../cofinite-topology.md) intersect: their complements are [finite sets](../../../../../finite-set.md), so the complement of their intersection is finite and cannot be all of $\mathbb R$.

Suppose the [quotient space](../../../../../quotient-topological-space.md) had distinct points with disjoint [open](../../../../../open-set.md) [neighbourhoods](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) $U,V$. Since the [quotient map](../../../../../quotient-map.md) $p$ is [surjective](../../../../../surjective-function.md), both [preimages](../../../../../preimage.md) $p^{-1}(U),p^{-1}(V)$ would be nonempty. Since $p$ is [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md), these would be disjoint [open subsets](../../../../../open-set.md) of $\mathbb R$ with its [cofinite topology](../../../../../cofinite-topology.md), contradicting the observation above. Thus any such [quotient space](../../../../../quotient-topological-space.md) with at least two points is not [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md).
