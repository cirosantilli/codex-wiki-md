<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**False.** Give $X=\mathbb R$ its usual [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md), identify all points of $(0,1)$ with one another, and leave every other point in a [singleton](../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md) [equivalence class](../../../../../equivalence-class.md). Take the [closed set](../../../../../closed-set.md) $K=\{1/2\}$.

Its image $p(K)$ is a [singleton](../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md) in the [quotient space](../../../../../quotient-topological-space.md), but its full [preimage](../../../../../preimage.md), also called the [saturation of a subset](../../../../../saturation-of-a-subset.md), is

$$
p^{-1}(p(K))=(0,1).
$$

This is not [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md) in $\mathbb R$, since $0$ belongs to its [closure](../../../../../closure-topology.md). In the [quotient topology](../../../../../quotient-topology.md), a [subset](../../../../../subset.md) is [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md) exactly when its [preimage](../../../../../preimage.md) is [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md), by applying the defining open-set rule to complements. Thus $p(K)$ is not [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md), and this [quotient map](../../../../../quotient-map.md) is not a [closed map](../../../../../closed-map.md).
