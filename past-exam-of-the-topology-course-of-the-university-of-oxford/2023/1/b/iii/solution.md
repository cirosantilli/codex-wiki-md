<h1 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The answer is **no**, because the number of [non-isolated points](../../../../../non-isolated-point.md) differs. In $X$, every $1/n$ is an [isolated point](../../../../../isolated-point.md): a sufficiently small [open interval](../../../../../open-interval.md) around it contains no other member of $X$. The only [non-isolated point](../../../../../non-isolated-point.md) is $0$, since every [neighbourhood](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of $0$ contains some $1/n$.

In the [product topology](../../../../../product-topology.md), $(x,y)\in X\times X$ is an [isolated point](../../../../../isolated-point.md) exactly when both coordinates are [isolated points](../../../../../isolated-point.md) of $X$. One direction follows because the product of two [open](../../../../../open-set.md) [singletons](../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md) is [open](../../../../../open-set.md). For the other, any basic [open](../../../../../open-set.md) [rectangle](../../../../../rectangle.md) containing $(x,y)$ contains another point if either coordinate has no [open](../../../../../open-set.md) [singleton](../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md). Hence the set of [non-isolated points](../../../../../non-isolated-point.md) of $X\times X$ is

$$
(\{0\}\times X)\cup(X\times\{0\}),
$$

which is infinite. A [homeomorphism](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) takes [open](../../../../../open-set.md) [singletons](../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md) to [open](../../../../../open-set.md) [singletons](../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md), so it induces a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) between the sets of [non-isolated points](../../../../../non-isolated-point.md). A space with just one such point cannot be [homeomorphic](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) to a space with infinitely many.
