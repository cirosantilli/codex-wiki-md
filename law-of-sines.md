# Law of sines

↑ **Parent:** [Triangle](triangle.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_sines)

In a nondegenerate [triangle](triangle.md), let $a,b,c$ be the side lengths opposite the interior [angles](angle.md) $A,B,C$. Its [area](surface-area.md) $K$ satisfies $2K=bc\sin A=ca\sin B=ab\sin C$. Division by $abc>0$ proves the law of sines. The area formula itself follows by taking the magnitude of the [cross product](cross-product.md) of two edge vectors. Unlike the [law of cosines](law-of-cosines.md), this relation alone may leave an ambiguity between two supplementary angles when two sides and a nonincluded angle are given.
