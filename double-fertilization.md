# Double fertilization

↑ **Parent:** [Fertilization](fertilization.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_fertilization)

In the common [angiosperm](angiosperm.md) arrangement, one [sperm](sperm.md) fuses with the [ovum](ovum.md) to form a [diploid](diploid.md) [zygote](zygote.md); another fuses with the central [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md)'s two polar [cell nuclei](cell-nucleus.md), initiating [endosperm](endosperm.md) with three [chromosome](chromosome.md) sets. The [zygote](zygote.md) develops into a [plant embryo](plant-embryo.md), and the [ovule](ovule.md) develops into a [seed](seed.md).[Biology 2e, §32.2 Double Fertilization, Figure 32.18](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/32-2-pollination-and-fertilization.md)

For an original counting example, stipulate a [plant](plant.md) with $2n=14$ [chromosomes](chromosome.md) and the common two-polar-nucleus arrangement. Each [haploid](haploid.md) [sperm](sperm.md) and [ovum](ovum.md) contributes $n=7$. The [zygote](zygote.md) therefore has $7+7=14$ [chromosomes](chromosome.md), while the initiating [endosperm](endosperm.md) [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md) has $7+7+7=21$. The maternal [seed coat](seed-coat.md) remains derived from $2n=14$ [plant tissue](plant-tissue.md); the whole [seed](seed.md) has no single uniform [chromosome](chromosome.md) count. This is bookkeeping for a stipulated example, not identification of a particular [species](species.md). It follows by counting the [chromosome](chromosome.md) sets entering each fusion, rather than assigning one count to everything enclosed by a [seed coat](seed-coat.md).
