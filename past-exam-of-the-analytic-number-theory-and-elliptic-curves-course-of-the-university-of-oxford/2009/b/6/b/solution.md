<h1 id="b/6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $E:y^2=x^3+px$ with $p>0$, the factor $x^2+p$ is strictly positive on the real line. Thus every nonzero rational $x$-coordinate is positive. In the [two-isogeny descent](../../../../../two-isogeny-descent.md) map, [prime](../../../../../prime-number-split.md) support restricts its [square class](../../../../../square-class.md) to $1$ or $p$. Both are realized by the identity and $(0,0)$, although only the upper bound is needed:

$$
|\alpha_E(E(\mathbb Q))|\leq2.
$$

The two-isogenous curve is $E':Y^2=X^3-4pX$. Its [two-torsion square-class homomorphism](../../../../../two-torsion-square-class-homomorphism.md) has signed squarefree representatives supported on the [primes](../../../../../prime-number-split.md) dividing $4p$. For odd $p$ these are among

$$
1,-1,2,-2,p,-p,2p,-2p.
$$

Hence the second image has at most eight elements. At $p=2$ the list reduces to $1,-1,2,-2$, so the bound by eight remains valid. The [two-isogeny rank formula](../../../../../square-class-index-formula-for-two-isogeny-descent.md) therefore gives

$$
2^r\leq\frac{2\cdot8}{4}=4,\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)\leq2.}
$$

In fact the same simple count yields $r\leq1$ when $p=2$. The positivity restriction on the first descent image is essential for the stated bound of two for all [primes](../../../../../prime-number-split.md).
