<h1 id="b/6/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a nonsingular curve $y^2=x^3+Ax+B$ with [integers](../../../../../integer-split.md) $A,B$, the [Nagell–Lutz theorem](../../../../../nagell-lutz-theorem.md) states that a finite [rational torsion](../../../../../torsion-subgroup.md) point has [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) coordinates and satisfies either $y=0$ or

$$
\boxed{y^2\mid4A^3+27B^2.}
$$

The point at infinity is the identity and is treated separately. Integrality is the result allowed in the source. To prove the remaining divisibility, suppose $y\ne0$. Then the doubled point is finite and also torsion, so its coordinates are integral. Its [tangent](../../../../../tangent.md) slope is $m=(3x^2+A)/(2y)$ and its abscissa is $m^2-2x$. Thus $m^2$ is an [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md). A [rational number](../../../../../rational-number.md) whose square is an [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) is itself an [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md), so $2y$ divides $3x^2+A$. In particular $y^2$ divides $\psi_1(x)=(3x^2+A)^2$. It also divides $\psi_2(x)=x^3+Ax+B=y^2$. Evaluating the permitted [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) identity at the [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) $x$ therefore proves the divisibility of $4A^3+27B^2$ by $y^2$.

For $y=0$, the point equals its negative and has order two. The theorem gives a finite set of torsion candidates, but the divisibility condition by itself does not assert that every candidate is torsion.
