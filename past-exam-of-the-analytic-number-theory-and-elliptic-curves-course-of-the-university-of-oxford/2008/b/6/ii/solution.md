<h1 id="b/6/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Here $4A^3+27B^2=31$. A nonzero ordinate of a [torsion point](../../../../../torsion-point-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) must have its square dividing 31, so $y=\pm1$. The curve equation then becomes $x(x^2+1)=0$, whose only [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) solution is $x=0$. No point with $y=0$ exists over the rationals: the monic cubic $x^3+x+1$ has no [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) [root](../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md), since its only possible [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) [roots](../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) are $\pm1$ and neither works.

It remains to test $(0,\pm1)$. Doubling either gives abscissa $(1/(\pm2))^2=1/4$, which is not an [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md). If the original point were torsion its double would satisfy the integrality part of the [Nagell–Lutz theorem](../../../../../nagell-lutz-theorem.md), a contradiction. Therefore

$$
\boxed{E(\mathbb Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}=\{\mathbf o\}.}
$$

The [rational point](../../../../../rational-point.md) $(0,1)$ consequently has infinite order, so its multiples give infinitely many distinct [rational points](../../../../../rational-point.md).
