<h1 id="b/5/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The reduction has equation $F=x^3+y^3=0$. At the origin both first [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) vanish, so the point is singular, also when $p=3$. A lift reducing to the origin would require $x,y\in p\mathbb Z_p$. Its two cubes would then lie in $p^3\mathbb Z_p$, whereas the right side of the original equation has [valuation](../../../../../valuation.md) one. This is impossible.

For an example which does lift, choose

$$
\boxed{\mathcal D:x^3+y^3=p^3.}
$$

It has the same reduction and the [rational point](../../../../../rational-point.md) $(p,0)$ reduces to the origin. Over $\mathbb Q_p$, which has [characteristic](../../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) zero, a singular affine point would require $x=y=0$, which does not lie on this curve. Its [projective closure](../../../../../projective-completion.md) is also smooth: the simultaneous [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) of $X^3+Y^3-p^3Z^3$ cannot vanish at a projective point. Thus this is a nonsingular curve for every [prime](../../../../../prime-number-split.md) $p$, including three. A [singular point](../../../../../singular-point-of-an-algebraic-variety.md) in the reduction can therefore fail to lift or can lift, depending on the original equation.
