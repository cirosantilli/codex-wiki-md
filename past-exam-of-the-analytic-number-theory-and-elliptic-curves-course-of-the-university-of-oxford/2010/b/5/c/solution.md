<h1 id="b/5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Modulo five the curve is $y^2=x^3+x$, with [good reduction](../../../../../good-reduction-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) because $4a^3+27b^2\equiv4\pmod5$. It has one point above each of $x=0,2,3$ and no others, plus infinity, so its group has order four. Rational torsion therefore has order dividing four.

A nonzero [rational point](../../../../../rational-point.md) of order two would have $y=0$ and integral $x$, so would give an [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) root of $x^3+ax+b$. But modulo two this [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is $x^3+x+1$, which is one at both residues. Thus there is no rational [2-torsion](../../../../../2-torsion.md). Every nontrivial finite group of order dividing four contains an element of order two, so

$$
\boxed{E(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}=\{O\}.}
$$
