<h1 id="b/5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $y^2=x^3+x+2$, the point $P=(1,2)$ doubles to $(-1,0)$: its [tangent](../../../../../tangent.md) slope is $(3+1)/(2\cdot2)=1$, giving $x(2P)=-1$ and $y(2P)=0$. Thus $P$ has order four.

The curve has [good reduction](../../../../../good-reduction-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) at $3$, since $4+27\cdot4=112$ is nonzero modulo three. Its reduced points are the point at infinity, two points above $x=1$, and one above $x=2$, so there are four in total. Injectivity of torsion reduction bounds the rational torsion order by four. The known four points exhaust it:

$$
\boxed{E(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}=\{O,(-1,0),(1,2),(1,-2)\}\cong C_4.}
$$
