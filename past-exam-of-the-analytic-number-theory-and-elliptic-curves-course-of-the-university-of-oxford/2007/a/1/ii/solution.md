<h1 id="a/1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a finite set of primes $p\le y$, multiply the absolutely convergent local [series](../../../../../series-mathematics.md). Unique [prime factorization](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md) and multiplicativity, with $g(1)=1$, give

$$
\prod_{p\le y}\sum_{e\ge0}g(p^e)p^{-es}
=\sum_{\substack{n\ge1\\\text{every prime divisor of }n\le y}}g(n)n^{-s}.
$$

The omitted [integers](../../../../../integer-split.md) all exceed $y$, so the absolute difference from $G(s)$ is at most $\sum_{n>y}|g(n)|n^{-\sigma}$, which tends to zero. Hence the finite products converge to $G(s)$:

$$
\boxed{G(s)=\prod_pG_p(s)\qquad(\operatorname{Re}s>1).}
$$

This proves the [Euler product](../../../../../euler-product.md) directly from [absolute convergence](../../../../../absolute-convergence.md), rather than treating an infinite formal multiplication as automatically valid.
