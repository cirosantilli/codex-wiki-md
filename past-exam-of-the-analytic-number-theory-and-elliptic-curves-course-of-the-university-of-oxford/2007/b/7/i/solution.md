<h1 id="b/7/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In the typical case, the line through $P,Q$, or their tangent when they coincide, is $v=mu+c$. Its intersections with $D$ have first coordinates given by

$$
u^3+(-2a-m^2)u^2+(b_1-2mc)u-c^2=0.
$$

Their product is $c^2$. The third intersection is $-(P+Q)$, which has the same first coordinate as $P+Q$. Consequently

$$
u(P)u(Q)u(P+Q)=c^2.
$$

Passing to the quotient by rational squares gives $q(P)q(Q)q(P+Q)=1$. Every [square class](../../../../../square-class.md) is its own inverse, so

$$
\boxed{q(P+Q)=q(P)q(Q).}
$$

The excluded cases have their specified special values; the source asks only for this chord-and-tangent argument away from them.
