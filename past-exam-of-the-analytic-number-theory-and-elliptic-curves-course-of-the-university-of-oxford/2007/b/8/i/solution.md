<h1 id="b/8/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The doubling slope at $P=(2,1)$ is $(3\cdot2^2-1)/(2\cdot1)=11/2$, giving

$$
2P=\left(\frac{105}{4},-\frac{1075}{8}\right).
$$

These denominators are invertible modulo $N$. To add $P$ again, the difference of first coordinates is $105/4-2=97/4$, so the new slope would be $-1083/194$. This requires inversion of a number divisible by 97. The [elliptic curve method](../../../../../elliptic-curve-method.md) instead computes

$$
\boxed{\gcd(97,10573)=97,\qquad10573=97\cdot109.}
$$

It is a proper factor. The failure of the group-law division modulo the composite [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) reveals different behavior modulo its prime factors.
