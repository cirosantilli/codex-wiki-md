# Electrode

↑ **Parent:** [Electrochemistry](electrochemistry.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrode)

An [electrode](electrode.md) is an electronic [electrical conductor](electrical-conductor.md) in contact with an [electrolyte](electrolyte.md). In a cell carrying sustained [redox](redox.md) [electric current](electric-current.md), [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction.md) at this interface exchange [Electrons](electron.md) with [chemical species](chemical-species.md). An [electrode](electrode.md) can participate in the [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md), as the dissolving [zinc](zinc.md) [anode](anode.md) of a [Daniell cell](daniell-cell.md) does, or provide a conducting surface without appearing in the net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md). This distinction is illustrated by active and inert [electrodes](electrode.md) in [Flowers, Theopold, Langley and Robinson, Chemistry 2e, §17.2](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/17-2-galvanic-cells).

The interface connects two kinds of [electric charge](electric-charge.md) transport: [Electrons](electron.md) in the external [electrical circuit](electrical-circuit.md) and [ions](ion.md) in the [electrolyte](electrolyte.md). The same [Electron](electron.md) does not travel through the wire, swim through the [salt bridge](salt-bridge.md), and complete a circuit! The [half-reactions](half-reaction.md) account for how electronic and ionic transport meet. This is the useful idea behind both obtaining electrical [work](work.md) in a [galvanic cell](galvanic-cell.md) and supplying electrical [work](work.md) in an [electrolytic cell](electrolytic-cell.md).

Name an [electrode](electrode.md) from its net [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md): [oxidation](oxidation.md) means [anode](anode.md), and [reduction](reduction-chemistry.md) means [cathode](cathode.md). State the operating mode before assigning terminal signs. For a [galvanic cell](galvanic-cell.md) supplying a passive load, the [anode](anode.md) is negative and the [cathode](cathode.md) positive; for an ordinary two-terminal [electrolytic cell](electrolytic-cell.md) driven by a source, the [anode](anode.md) is positive and the [cathode](cathode.md) negative. Here positive means higher terminal [electric potential](electric-potential.md) than the other [electrode](electrode.md), not a claim that the bulk material has a large positive net [electric charge](electric-charge.md). See [Pingarrón et al., Terminology of electrochemical methods of analysis (IUPAC Recommendations 2019), §§3.1 and 3.4](https://doi.org/10.1515/pac-2018-0109).
