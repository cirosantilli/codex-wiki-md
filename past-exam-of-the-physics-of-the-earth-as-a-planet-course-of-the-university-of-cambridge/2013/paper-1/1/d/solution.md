<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[Ridge push](../../../../../ridge-push.md) is a horizontal [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) [force](../../../../../force-split.md) integrated with depth, equivalently a difference in gravitational [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) per horizontal [area](../../../../../surface-area.md). It is not the weight of a slab multiplied by the surface slope at one chosen point.

For an explicit compensated-column derivation, let $y$ measure depth below the ridge seafloor; the old seafloor is at $y=h$. Set $b=\rho_m\alpha\Delta T$. The hot column has greater [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). Its [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) excess over the old column is

$$
\Delta p(y)=\begin{cases}
g(\rho_m-\rho_w)y,&0<y<h,\\
\dfrac{gb}{2a}(a-s)^2,&y=h+s,\quad0<s<a,\\
0,&y>h+a.
\end{cases}
$$

The second line follows by integrating $\delta\rho(s)=b(1-s/a)$ upwards from the compensation level. The two expressions agree at $y=h$ because $(\rho_m-\rho_w)h=ba/2$. Integrating this positive [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) excess gives the [ridge-push force in a plate model](../../../../../ridge-push-force-in-a-plate-model.md), directed away from the ridge:

$$
F=\int\Delta p\,dy
=\frac{\rho_mg\alpha\Delta T a^2}{6}
+\frac{g(\rho_m-\rho_w)h^2}{2}.
$$

Consistently retaining only first order in [thermal expansion](../../../../../thermal-expansion.md) gives

$$
\boxed{F\simeq2.60\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}.}
$$

If the topographic term is retained within the same linear-density column model, it adds $1.52\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$, giving **$F\simeq2.75\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$**. This is a useful small correction, not a claim that all other second-order material effects have been included. Both values use the intended hot initial plate, not the inconsistent printed zero-age profile.
