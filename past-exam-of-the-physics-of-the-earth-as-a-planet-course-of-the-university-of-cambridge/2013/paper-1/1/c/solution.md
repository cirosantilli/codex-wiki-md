<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use the intended hot-ridge state and a linear law of [thermal expansion](../../../../../thermal-expansion.md) about [mantle](../../../../../planetary-mantle.md) [density](../../../../../density.md) $\rho_m$ at $T_1$. Let $s$ be depth below the old seafloor. Its excess [density](../../../../../density.md) is

$$
\delta\rho(s)=\rho_m\alpha\Delta T(1-s/a),\qquad 0<s<a,
$$

and its excess column [mass](../../../../../mass.md) is $\int_0^a\delta\rho\,ds=\rho_m\alpha\Delta T a/2$. [Isostasy](../../../../../isostasy.md) requires equal [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) at a compensation depth below both columns. Lowering the old seafloor by $h$ replaces rock by water, reducing column [mass](../../../../../mass.md) by $(\rho_m-\rho_w)h$. Therefore the [isostatic ridge elevation in a plate model](../../../../../isostatic-ridge-elevation-in-a-plate-model.md) is

$$
\boxed{h=\frac{\rho_m\alpha\Delta T a}{2(\rho_m-\rho_w)}
=3.90\times10^3\,\mathrm m=3.9\,\mathrm{km}.}
$$

Here $\alpha\Delta T=0.052$; the result is a first-order estimate and neglects sediments, crustal-composition differences and flexural support. If one instead insists on the literal printed zero-age profile, its column is denser than the old plate and the formal relative elevation reverses sign, $-3.9\,\mathrm{km}$ to this order. **That profile cannot describe the elevated hot ridge posited by the question.**
