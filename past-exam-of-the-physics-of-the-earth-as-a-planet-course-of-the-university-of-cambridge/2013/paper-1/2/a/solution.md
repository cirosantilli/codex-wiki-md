**Oceanic [magnetic anomalies](../../../../../magnetic-anomaly.md).** Newly formed oceanic [crust](../../../../../planetary-crust.md) contains magnetic minerals. As it cools through their [magnetic blocking temperatures](../../../../../magnetic-blocking-temperature.md), it acquires [thermoremanent magnetization](../../../../../thermoremanent-magnetization.md) recording the ambient field polarity. Continuing [seafloor spreading](../../../../../seafloor-spreading.md) carries that record away from the ridge. [Geomagnetic reversals](../../../../../geomagnetic-reversal.md) therefore generate approximately paired [marine magnetic stripes](../../../../../marine-magnetic-stripe.md) parallel to the ridge, with older [crust](../../../../../planetary-crust.md) farther from its axis. A [magnetometer](../../../../../magnetometer.md) measures the superposed field above this magnetized material; subtracting a reference field reveals [magnetic anomalies](../../../../../magnetic-anomaly.md). The anomaly sign and shape depend on [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) direction, latitude, source depth and geometry, so a positive measured anomaly is not universally identical to normal rock polarity.[Sections Magnetic striping and polar reversals, and Seafloor spreading and recycling of oceanic crust](../../../../../developing-the-theory-by-united-states-geological-survey.md)

Matching a sequence of stripes to an independently dated reversal chronology gives plate ages and spreading histories. A stripe of width $w$ formed over interval $\Delta t$ implies [half-spreading rate](../../../../../half-spreading-rate.md) $v=w/\Delta t$ under steady symmetric spreading; full separation [speed](../../../../../speed.md) is $2v$. Unequal widths on opposite flanks can reflect asymmetric spreading or later deformation. Alteration, remagnetization and overlapping magnetic sources complicate inversion. **The sequence correlation is stronger evidence than an isolated stripe or a field-strength peak.**

**[Rayleigh-Bénard convection](../../../../../rayleigh-benard-convection.md).** Consider a horizontal fluid layer of thickness $d$, hotter below by $\Delta T>0$. The conductive state is motionless with a linear [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md). A rising warm parcel is buoyant, but [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) resists its motion and thermal [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) erases its [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) anomaly. In the [Boussinesq approximation](../../../../../boussinesq-approximation.md), [density](../../../../../density.md) variations are retained in [buoyancy](../../../../../buoyancy.md) while the flow is otherwise incompressible. The competing effects are measured by the [Rayleigh number](../../../../../rayleigh-number.md) and [Prandtl number](../../../../../prandtl-number.md),

$$
\operatorname{Ra}=\frac{g\alpha\Delta T d^3}{\nu\kappa},\qquad
\operatorname{Pr}=\frac\nu\kappa.
$$

For stress-free impermeable, fixed-temperature boundaries, linear stationary disturbances with horizontal [wavenumber](../../../../../wavenumber.md) $q/d$ give

$$
\operatorname{Ra}(q)=\frac{(q^2+\pi^2)^3}{q^2}.
$$

Minimizing gives $q_c=\pi/\sqrt2$ and the [critical Rayleigh number for an isothermal layer](../../../../../critical-rayleigh-number-for-an-isothermal-layer.md), $\operatorname{Ra}_c=27\pi^4/4\simeq657.5$. Two rigid no-slip, fixed-temperature boundaries instead give approximately $1708$; the critical value is not independent of [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md).[Chapter 20, §20.4, critical value for two rigid isothermal plates](../../../../../boundary-layer-turbulence-by-massachusetts-institute-of-technology.md)

Above onset, [buoyancy](../../../../../buoyancy.md) can sustain overturning rolls and [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) transfer exceeds pure conduction, with [Nusselt number](../../../../../nusselt-number.md) $\operatorname{Nu}>1$. Nonlinear advection eventually limits the disturbance; larger driving can produce time-dependent or turbulent states. The ideal Boussinesq problem does not require hexagonal cells: planform selection depends on boundaries and non-Boussinesq effects. [Convection](../../../../../convection-split.md) driven by [surface tension](../../../../../surface-tension-split.md) at a [free surface](../../../../../free-surface.md) is a distinct mechanism. **An adverse [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) supplies driving, but overcoming [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) and [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) requires a finite threshold.**
