<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [phase velocity](../../../../../phase-velocity.md) in Figure 1 is measured along the [Earth](../../../../../earth.md)'s surface. Spherical geometry makes the local horizontal phase [speed](../../../../../speed.md) at radius $r$ equal to $cr/R_E$. In the ray approximation, squared radial shear [wavenumber](../../../../../wavenumber.md) is

$$
q_r^2=\omega^2\left[\frac1{\beta(r)^2}-\frac{R_E^2}{c^2r^2}\right].
$$

The [spherical surface-wave evanescence threshold](../../../../../spherical-surface-wave-evanescence-threshold.md) immediately below the discontinuity is therefore

$$
\boxed{c<5.08\frac{6371}{6371-410}
\simeq5.43\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

Equality is the grazing limit. Using $5.08\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ directly would omit the requested spherical correction. Immediately above the discontinuity the corresponding boundary is $4.87(6371/5961)\simeq5.20\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. Hence the interval $5.20<c<5.43\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ specifically describes propagation just above the jump and evanescence just below it. Slower modes can already be evanescent above $410\,\mathrm{km}$.

Intersecting the plotted branches with $5.43\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ gives the following approximate upper [periods](../../../../../period-of-a-function.md). Below each upper [period](../../../../../period-of-a-function.md), that branch is evanescent immediately beneath the discontinuity:

- [fundamental mode](../../../../../fundamental-mode.md): throughout the displayed $10$–$100\,\mathrm s$ range;
- first and second [overtones](../../../../../overtone.md): approximately $67$ and $38\,\mathrm s$;
- successive higher curves: approximately $27$, $21$, $18$, $15$, $13$ and $11\,\mathrm s$.

For these [overtones](../../../../../overtone.md) the displayed ranges thus start near $10\,\mathrm s$ and end at their respective crossings. Any still higher curve already above the threshold at the lower plotted [period](../../../../../period-of-a-function.md) has no evanescent interval within the displayed range. Readings of the closely spaced higher curves are only accurate to about a second, and the plot does not establish their continuation to unshown [periods](../../../../../period-of-a-function.md). Nor does the local inequality prove that a mode remains evanescent at every greater depth in an unspecified [Earth](../../../../../earth.md) model.
