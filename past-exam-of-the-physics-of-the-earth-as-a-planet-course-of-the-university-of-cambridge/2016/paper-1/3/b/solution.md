<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A real symmetric $3\times3$ [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) has three real [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) and a complete [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md). For a stable elastic material, [strong ellipticity](../../../../../strong-ellipticity.md) makes $\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}B\mathbf a>0$ for every nonzero [polarization](../../../../../polarization-waves.md), so its [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are positive squared wave [speeds](../../../../../speed.md). There are therefore three orthogonal plane-wave [polarizations](../../../../../polarization-waves.md) in a given direction, with three potentially distinct [speeds](../../../../../speed.md) in an anisotropic medium. Degenerate [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) allow arbitrary orthonormal combinations within their eigenspace; they do not remove [polarization](../../../../../polarization-waves.md) degrees of freedom. General anisotropic modes need not be exactly longitudinal or transverse.

For an [isotropic](../../../../../isotropy.md) medium let $\lambda=\kappa-2\mu/3$. Contract the [isotropic](../../../../../isotropy.md) stiffness with $\hat p_k\hat p_l$:

$$
B_{ij}=\frac1\rho\big[(\lambda+\mu)\hat p_i\hat p_j+\mu\delta_{ij}\big].
$$

Using

$$
\alpha^2=\frac{\kappa+4\mu/3}{\rho}=\frac{\lambda+2\mu}{\rho},\qquad\beta^2=\frac\mu\rho,
$$

gives

$$
\boxed{B_{ij}=(\alpha^2-\beta^2)\hat p_i\hat p_j+\beta^2\delta_{ij}.}
$$

For $\mathbf a\parallel\hat{\mathbf p}$ the longitudinal projector has [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) one, so $B\mathbf a=\alpha^2\mathbf a$: this is the [P wave](../../../../../p-wave.md). For either of two independent $\mathbf a\perp\hat{\mathbf p}$ the projector vanishes, so $B\mathbf a=\beta^2\mathbf a$: these are two degenerate transverse [S wave](../../../../../s-wave.md) [polarizations](../../../../../polarization-waves.md). **An [isotropic](../../../../../isotropy.md) solid has one P [speed](../../../../../speed.md) and one S [speed](../../../../../speed.md), but three [polarization](../../../../../polarization-waves.md) modes: one longitudinal and two transverse.** A fluid limit with zero [shear modulus](../../../../../shear-modulus.md) has no propagating [S wave](../../../../../s-wave.md), so the positive-speed conclusion requires a solid with positive shear stiffness.
