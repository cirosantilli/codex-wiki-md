**[gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) and [surface topography](../../../../../surface-topography.md).** [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) responds to all [density](../../../../../density.md) structure, while [surface topography](../../../../../surface-topography.md) records both load and support. First specify the reference field and measurement height. [latitude](../../../../../geographical-latitude.md) and planetary [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) affect normal [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md). At elevation $h$, the reference field decreases approximately by $2gh/R$; a [free-air gravity anomaly](../../../../../free-air-gravity-anomaly.md) restores this elevation effect but retains topographic [mass](../../../../../mass.md). A [Bouguer gravity anomaly](../../../../../bouguer-gravity-anomaly.md) additionally subtracts an approximate topographic slab contribution $2\pi G\rho_ch$. [terrain correction](../../../../../terrain-correction.md) replaces the unrealistic slab near valleys and ridges, and spherical geometry matters over large areas. A [density](../../../../../density.md) choice is required for these reductions; a numerical crustal [density](../../../../../density.md) is not provided here.

In [Airy isostasy](../../../../../airy-isostasy.md), [surface topography](../../../../../surface-topography.md) of height $h$ and crustal [density](../../../../../density.md) $\rho_c$ is compensated by a root of thickness $b$ replacing mantle of [density](../../../../../density.md) $\rho_m>\rho_c$. Equal column [masses](../../../../../mass.md) give $\rho_ch=(\rho_m-\rho_c)b$. The positive [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) of the relief is partly cancelled by the negative [density](../../../../../density.md) contrast of the root. At long wavelengths a compensated mountain can therefore have a modest free-air anomaly and a negative Bouguer anomaly. [Pratt isostasy](../../../../../pratt-isostasy.md) instead varies column [density](../../../../../density.md) at a roughly common compensation depth. Neither model says that [surface topography](../../../../../surface-topography.md) itself exerts no [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md).

Potential-field depth filtering makes wavelength crucial. In a planar Fourier approximation, a [density](../../../../../density.md) interface at depth $z$ is attenuated by $e^{-kz}$ at horizontal [wavenumber](../../../../../wavenumber.md) $k$. For a small Airy-compensated height perturbation and a compensation interface at depth $D$, the [gravity-topography admittance](../../../../../gravity-topography-admittance.md) is approximately

$$
Z_{\rm FA}(k)=2\pi G\rho_c(1-e^{-kD}),\qquad Z_{\rm B}(k)=-2\pi G\rho_ce^{-kD}.
$$

Long wavelengths allow strong cancellation between load and root. At short wavelengths the root's [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) is filtered out, so the observed near-surface [mass](../../../../../mass.md) can dominate even if the column is compensated. In a [flexural isostasy](../../../../../flexural-isostasy.md) model, an elastic [lithosphere](../../../../../lithosphere.md) supports and spreads the load; for downward deflection $w$ and imposed load $q$, $D_f\nabla^4w+\Delta\rho g w=q$, with $D_f=ET_e^3/[12(1-\nu^2)]$. Thus $w_k=q_k/(D_fk^4+\Delta\rho g)$, and the effective elastic thickness controls the support scale.

Estimate admittance from cross-spectra of [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) and [surface topography](../../../../../surface-topography.md), and compare its wavelength dependence and coherence with local-compensation, elastic-flexure and subsurface-load models. A primary Atlantic study sampled ship-track free-air [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) and bathymetry and compared their Fourier-domain relationship with compensation models, illustrating this observational route rather than inferring a root from the sign of one [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) value.[pp. 1903–1915, ship-track sampling, spectral transfer estimates and wavelength-dependent compensation](../../../../../the-relationship-between-bathymetry-and-gravity-in-the-atlantic-ocean-by-dan-mckenzie-and-carl-bowin.md) Check navigation, instrument drift, terrain/[density](../../../../../density.md) reductions, sediment and water loads, and independent seismic crustal constraints. Density-depth trade-offs and mantle dynamic support make the [inverse problem](../../../../../inverse-problem-split.md) nonunique; [surface topography](../../../../../surface-topography.md) and [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) together constrain support, but do not uniquely map every [density](../../../../../density.md) anomaly.

**[mantle viscosity](../../../../../mantle-viscosity.md).** A second, independent route concerns deformation rates rather than static compensation. [glacial isostatic adjustment](../../../../../glacial-isostatic-adjustment.md) follows loading and unloading of a deformable [Earth](../../../../../earth.md): an ice sheet depresses the surface, and subsequent removal leaves time-dependent uplift, horizontal [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md), sea-level and [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) changes. Fit dated relative sea-level indicators and raised shorelines together with geodetic [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) rates and a reconstructed ice/water load history to a viscoelastic [Earth](../../../../../earth.md) model. Relative sea level includes solid-surface motion, changing [geoid](../../../../../geoid.md) and redistribution of ocean water; treating it only as local vertical rock motion biases the inference.

For scale, a sinusoidal surface perturbation of [wavenumber](../../../../../wavenumber.md) $k$ on a uniform incompressible viscous half-space relaxes under [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) with $\tau_k\sim2\eta k/(\rho g)$. A Maxwell material separately has a stress-relaxation scale $\tau_M=\eta/\mu$. These are different experiments and generally different times: rebound modes also involve [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md), spherical geometry, a layered mantle and elastic [lithosphere](../../../../../lithosphere.md). Fit a family of relaxation modes or a full load-history convolution, rather than equating every observed rebound time to $\eta/\mu$.

Use independent ice-margin, exposure-age and sea-level constraints to reduce the trade-off between ice history and [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md); compare horizontal as well as vertical [GNSS](../../../../../gnss.md) motions, and separate present ice loss, groundwater changes and tectonic signals from the older loading response. A primary global analysis jointly tested ice-history and mantle-response predictions against space-geodetic observations, rather than treating a local uplift rate as a unique [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) measurement.[abstract and pp. 450–487, joint ice-history/GIA model and space-geodetic constraints](../../../../../space-geodesy-constrains-ice-age-terminal-deglaciation-the-global-ice-6g-c-vm5a-model-by-w-r-peltier-and-others.md)

[postseismic relaxation](../../../../../postseismic-relaxation.md) probes shallower or faster relaxation after a [stress](../../../../../stress.md) perturbation, but must be distinguished from afterslip and poroelastic deformation. Long-wavelength [geoid](../../../../../geoid.md) and mantle-flow models provide complementary constraints when [density](../../../../../density.md) structure is estimated independently from seismic structure and mineral physics; unknown density-to-velocity conversion and boundary [tractions](../../../../../traction.md) remain trade-offs. Laboratory creep experiments constrain constitutive laws as functions of [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), [stress](../../../../../stress.md) and grain size, but require a large extrapolation to geological conditions. **Combine spatially and temporally distinct observations; there is no single directly measured [mantle viscosity](../../../../../mantle-viscosity.md) independent of depth, [rheology](../../../../../rheology-split.md) and loading assumptions.**
