<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At the north pole $\mathbf r=+R\hat z$, so the [velocities](../../../../../velocity.md) reverse:

$$
\mathbf v_C=(0,0),\qquad\mathbf v_A=(48,0),\qquad\mathbf v_B=(48,-20)\quad\mathrm{mm\,yr^{-1}}.
$$

The triangle still has side lengths 20, 48 and 52 mm/year. Across AB, A moves toward positive $y$ relative to B; across AC, A moves toward positive $x$ relative to C. Both boundaries therefore open and are [spreading ridges](../../../../../spreading-ridge.md), with full opening [speeds](../../../../../speed.md) 20 and 48 mm/year. Together with BC they form a [ridge-ridge-transform triple junction](../../../../../ridge-ridge-transform-triple-junction.md).

In the symmetric normal-spreading model, a ridge's normal [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) is the mean normal [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) of its two plates. AB gives $U_y=(0-20)/2=-10$, and AC gives $U_x=(48+0)/2=24$. Their intersection lies on the BC transform [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) line:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf U_N=(24,-10)\,\mathrm{mm\,yr^{-1}}=\frac{\mathbf v_B+\mathbf v_C}{2}.}
$$

**This northern junction is also instantaneously kinematically stable.** The diagrams use the same fixed geocentric tangent axes at both poles; reversing [velocities](../../../../../velocity.md) is not the same operation as reflecting a map drawn from an exterior observer's viewpoint.

Not every ridge-ridge-transform geometry works. For pure normal spreading, each ridge [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) line is the [perpendicular bisector](../../../../../perpendicular-bisector.md) of the corresponding side of the [plate velocity triangle](../../../../../plate-velocity-triangle.md). Their intersection is the triangle's [circumcenter](../../../../../circumcenter.md). A transform junction must also lie on the third side's line. A nondegenerate triangle's [circumcenter](../../../../../circumcenter.md) lies there only at that side's midpoint, so the opposite angle is a right angle. Equivalently, $\big(\mathbf v_B-\mathbf v_A\big)\cdot\big(\mathbf v_C-\mathbf v_A\big)=0$. The two ridge directions are then perpendicular, as they are here. **RRF junctions are conditionally stable, with the right-angle ridge geometry in this symmetric pure-normal model; they are not all stable.** Oblique or asymmetric accretion changes the velocity-line assumptions and must be specified separately.
