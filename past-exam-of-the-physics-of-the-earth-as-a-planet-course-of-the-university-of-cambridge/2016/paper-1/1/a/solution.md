<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $\hat x$ point to the equatorial meridian at zero [longitude](../../../../../longitude.md), $\hat y$ to the equator at $90^\circ$ E, and $\hat z$ to the north pole. Use a right-hand positive [Euler angular velocity vector](../../../../../euler-angular-velocity-vector.md). Relative [rotations](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) add as [vectors](../../../../../vector.md), not as geographic coordinates:

$$
\boldsymbol\omega_{B/C}=\boldsymbol\omega_{B/A}+\boldsymbol\omega_{A/C}=(5\hat x+12\hat y)\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm{rad\,yr^{-1}}.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{|\boldsymbol\omega_{B/C}|=13\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm{rad\,yr^{-1}},\qquad (\phi,\lambda)=(0^\circ,67.380^\circ\mathrm E).}
$$

Here $\lambda=\tan^{-1}(12/5)$ is the [longitude](../../../../../longitude.md) of the positive [Euler pole](../../../../../euler-pole.md), and its [latitude](../../../../../geographical-latitude.md) $\phi$ is zero. The antipodal pole with the opposite angular-velocity sign is an equivalent convention; mixing a pole reversal with an unchanged sign would give the wrong motion.
