<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use the supplied surface [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), $420^\circ\mathrm C\simeq693\,\mathrm K$, as the premise. With otherwise comparable material and [geothermal gradient](../../../../../geothermal-gradient.md), this hotter boundary increases thermally activated [material creep](../../../../../creep-deformation.md) at shallow depth and places a chosen mechanical cutoff [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) closer to the surface. That effect alone would favour a thinner elastic layer than in a cool terrestrial oceanic plate.

[Water weakening of rocks](../../../../../water-weakening-of-rocks.md) makes dryness act in the opposite direction. Water can enhance [material creep](../../../../../creep-deformation.md); sufficiently dry material can remain strong at temperatures at which its wet equivalent relaxes. Experiments on dry diabase provide a concrete basis for a strong dry [crust](../../../../../planetary-crust.md) and possible mechanical coupling between [crust](../../../../../planetary-crust.md) and [mantle](../../../../../planetary-mantle.md), rather than requiring a weak crustal layer solely because the surface is hot.[1996, printed pages 793–794, dry strength and implications for crustal coupling](../../../../../high-temperature-deformation-of-dry-diabase-with-application-to-crustal-deformation-on-venus-by-s-j-mackwell-and-others.md)

For an illustrative steady conductive geotherm with [thermal conductivity](../../../../../thermal-conductivity.md) $k_T$ and [heat flux](../../../../../heat-flux-density.md) $q$, a fixed strength cutoff gives

$$
T_e\sim\frac{k_T(T_*-T_s)}q.
$$

Therefore the comparison would scale as

$$
\frac{T_{e,V}}{T_{e,E}}\sim
\frac{k_{T,V}}{k_{T,E}}\frac{q_E}{q_V}
\frac{T_{*,V}-T_{s,V}}{T_{*,E}-T_{s,E}},
$$

where all temperatures are compared consistently and a dry rheology may have a higher effective cutoff $T_{*,V}$. This is a conditional strength-layer estimate, not a universal definition of $T_e$.

**[Venus](../../../../../venus.md) can retain appreciable [elastic thickness](../../../../../effective-elastic-thickness.md) despite its hot surface; [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) favours thinning, while dryness favours strengthening. The supplied facts alone determine neither a numerical $T_e$ nor a definite ordering relative to [Earth](../../../../../earth.md)'s oceanic plates.** The comparison requires heat flow, subsurface thermal history, material rheology, interior water content and loading timescale. Little atmospheric water is evidence relevant to the hypothesis of dry rocks, not a direct measurement of deep water activity.
