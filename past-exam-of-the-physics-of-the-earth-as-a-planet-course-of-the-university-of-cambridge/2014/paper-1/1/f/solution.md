<h1 id="1/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Let the [seismic moment tensor](../../../../../seismic-moment-tensor.md) of a [double-couple seismic source](../../../../../double-couple-seismic-source.md) be $M=M_0(\mathbf s\otimes\mathbf n+\mathbf n\otimes\mathbf s)$ for perpendicular unit slip and normal [vectors](../../../../../vector.md). Its T axis is $\mathbf T=(\mathbf s+\mathbf n)/\sqrt2$, with [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $+M_0$, and its P axis is $\mathbf P=(\mathbf s-\mathbf n)/\sqrt2$, with [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $-M_0$. They bisect the fault-plane and auxiliary-plane quadrants.

These [earthquake pressure and tension axes](../../../../../earthquake-pressure-and-tension-axes.md) describe the source deformation. **They are not generally the [principal axes](../../../../../principal-axis.md) of the ambient [stress tensor](../../../../../cauchy-stress-tensor.md).** [Friction](../../../../../friction.md), inherited fault orientation and local [stress](../../../../../stress.md) changes can rotate their relationship to that [tensor](../../../../../tensor.md); a [stress](../../../../../stress.md) inversion requires further assumptions and multiple mechanisms.
