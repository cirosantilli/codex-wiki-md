<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

In the three-dimensional [Earth](../../../../../earth.md), a network identifies P- and S-wave arrivals and fits source coordinates $(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ and origin time $t_0$ using a travel-time model. Time differences constrain path lengths without requiring a common absolute clock zero; calibrated absolute times then fix the origin time. Real inversions trace refracted rays through a layered or heterogeneous velocity model and use many stations, azimuths, and phases, rather than literal [circles](../../../../../circle.md) at fixed [wave speed](../../../../../wave-speed.md). Residuals and the sensitivity [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) quantify uncertainty and expose poor station geometry or a poor velocity model.

**Depth and origin time are often the weakest pair**, especially for a shallow event observed by distant surface stations. In the homogeneous example,

$$
t_i=t_0+\frac{\sqrt{r_i^2+z_0^2}}{v},\qquad
\delta t_i=\delta t_0+\frac{z_0}{v\sqrt{r_i^2+z_0^2}}\delta z_0.
$$

If the depth sensitivities are small or nearly alike across the network, a deeper source's longer [travel time](../../../../../travel-time.md) can be offset by an earlier origin time. In contrast, horizontal displacement often gives opposite-sign residuals on opposite sides of an event and is better constrained by good azimuthal coverage. Nearby stations and depth-sensitive reflected phases can break the [earthquake depth–origin-time trade-off](../../../../../earthquake-depth-origin-time-trade-off.md); the ranking is not universal for every network or event depth.
