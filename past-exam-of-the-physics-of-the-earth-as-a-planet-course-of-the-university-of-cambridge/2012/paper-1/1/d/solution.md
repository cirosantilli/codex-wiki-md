<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**A persistent barrier to whole-mantle circulation adds an internal thermal bottleneck and permits the deep [mantle](../../../../../planetary-mantle.md) to retain a larger [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) excess.** In a single well-mixed layer there is one interior [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and one surface heat-loss law. The [coupled thermal histories of layered mantle convection](../../../../../coupled-thermal-histories-of-layered-mantle-convection.md) arise because separately mixed layers can have different [temperatures](../../../../../temperature-split.md), exchanging [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) through an interfacial [boundary layer](../../../../../boundary-layer.md) as well as losing [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) at the surface. Their thermal histories then have coupled relaxation modes rather than the single mode in (c).

For example, let $\theta_l,\theta_u$ be lower- and upper-layer [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) excesses, $H_l,H_u$ their [heat capacities](../../../../../heat-capacity.md) per area, and

$$
J=C_i(\theta_l-\theta_u)^{4/3},\qquad E=C_s\theta_u^{4/3}.
$$

With no internal heating the budgets are $H_l\dot\theta_l=F-J$ and $H_u\dot\theta_u=J-E$. At [steady state](../../../../../steady-state.md),

$$
\theta_u=(F/C_s)^{3/4},\qquad
\theta_l=\theta_u+(F/C_i)^{3/4}.
$$

Thus a fixed basal flux still leaves the surface eventually with $E=F$, but the lower layer is hotter than it would be in the single-temperature model. For a given deep [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) instead, the extra interfacial drop reduces outward [heat transfer](../../../../../heat-transfer.md). This favours delayed loss of deep primordial [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) and distinct upper- and lower-mantle cooling histories. A numerical change in cooling time cannot be obtained from layer count alone: interface transport, layer thicknesses, temperature-dependent [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md), internal heating, and whether material can cross the interface matter. Merely replacing $d$ by $d/2$ in (c) misses the added interface and is not a model of two-layer cooling.
