<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Vigorous [convection](../../../../../convection-split.md) keeps the interior nearly uniform, so the upper [thermal boundary layer](../../../../../thermal-boundary-layer.md) carries a [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) drop of order $\theta$. A [boundary layer](../../../../../boundary-layer.md) becomes unstable when its local [Rayleigh number](../../../../../rayleigh-number.md) reaches an order-fixed critical value $R_c$:

$$
\operatorname{Ra}_\delta=\frac{\rho_0g\alpha\theta\delta^3}{\eta\kappa}\sim R_c.
$$

This is [marginal-stability thermal boundary-layer scaling](../../../../../marginal-stability-thermal-boundary-layer-scaling.md) using the [Local Rayleigh-number closure](../../../../../local-rayleigh-number-closure.md): boundary-layer [buoyancy](../../../../../buoyancy.md) overcomes viscous and thermal-diffusive stabilization. Equivalently, a buoyancy-driven velocity scale $u\sim\rho_0g\alpha\theta\delta^2/\eta$ makes $u\delta/\kappa$ order $R_c$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\delta\sim\left(\frac{R_c\eta\kappa}{\rho_0g\alpha\theta}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
E(t)\sim\frac{k\theta}{\delta}=C\theta^{4/3},\quad
C=k\left(\frac{\rho_0g\alpha}{R_c\eta\kappa}\right)^{1/3}.}
$$

There is no $d$ in this closure, as required for a thin [boundary layer](../../../../../boundary-layer.md) controlled by its own stability. The exponent is fixed by scaling; its absolute coefficient is not. Here $R_c$ represents the geometry and [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md), together with any order-one boundary-layer coefficient. The question supplies no numerical value for it. The assumption requires $\delta\ll d$, positive $\theta$, and properties sufficiently constant over the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) contrast.
