**Hawaii: short-wavelength volcanic loading and long-wavelength [mantle](../../../../../planetary-mantle.md) support must be distinguished.** A dense volcanic edifice replacing seawater adds mass and gives a positive [free-air gravity anomaly](../../../../../free-air-gravity-anomaly.md). Its load bends the [lithosphere](../../../../../lithosphere.md) down, producing a surrounding moat and an outer flexural arch. The associated displaced [density](../../../../../density.md) interfaces contribute negative and positive [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) signals at neighbouring distances. Observed [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) near the Hawaiian ridge includes strong positive anomalies over the ridge, negative flanks, and a broader positive belt. At much longer wavelengths, the [Hawaiian swell](../../../../../hawaiian-swell.md) also correlates with a positive [gravity anomaly](../../../../../gravity-anomaly.md), with a smaller gravity-to-topography ratio that requires compensation; the precise source of support is not determined by that [correlation](../../../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md) alone.[Abstract, ridge, flanking, outer-belt and long-wavelength swell anomalies](../../../../../gravity-and-bathymetry-in-the-central-pacific-ocean-by-a-b-watts.md)

The mechanical distinction can be expressed with a simple elastic-plate model. For load height $h$, downward deflection $w$, plate rigidity $D$, loading [density](../../../../../density.md) contrast $\Delta\rho_l$ relative to water, and restoring [density](../../../../../density.md) contrast $\Delta\rho_b$ at an interface,

$$
D\nabla^4w+\Delta\rho_b g w=\Delta\rho_l g h.
$$

For a Fourier component of horizontal [wavenumber](../../../../../wavenumber.md) $q$, this gives $w_q=\Delta\rho_l g h_q/(Dq^4+\Delta\rho_b g)$. The load's direct [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) contribution competes with the negative contribution of a depressed interface at reference depth $t$. In a linear thin-interface approximation,

$$
Z(q)\equiv\frac{\delta g_q}{h_q}=2\pi G\Delta\rho_l\left[1-\frac{e^{-qt}}{1+Dq^4/(\Delta\rho_b g)}\right].
$$

At short wavelengths rigidity suppresses compensation, leaving relatively high positive [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) admittance. At long wavelengths the plate can deflect and compensate more completely, reducing admittance. Fitting the wavelength dependence constrains [effective elastic thickness](../../../../../effective-elastic-thickness.md) through $D=E_Y T_e^3/[12(1-\nu^2)]$, subject to [density](../../../../../density.md) structure, load distribution and subsurface loading assumptions. This explains why local Airy compensation alone misses the spatially extended flexural response.

The broad swell needs a deeper explanation than the narrow volcanic pile: hot, buoyant [mantle](../../../../../planetary-mantle.md) and [mantle](../../../../../planetary-mantle.md) flow can support regional topography, while deep [density](../../../../../density.md) anomalies and surface elevation jointly determine its [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md). A free-air anomaly keeps the attracting mass of the topography; a Bouguer correction removes an assumed direct topographic attraction and can reveal its compensating mass deficit. **[Gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) plus topography constrain support as a function of wavelength; neither alone uniquely fixes the [mantle](../../../../../planetary-mantle.md) [density](../../../../../density.md) distribution.**

For the **OR alternative, [triple junctions](../../../../../triple-junction.md)** are points where three plate boundaries meet. Each boundary can be a [spreading ridge](../../../../../spreading-ridge.md), [subduction trench](../../../../../subduction-trench.md), or [transform fault](../../../../../transform-fault.md). The three plate velocities satisfy

$$
\mathbf v_{AB}+\mathbf v_{BC}+\mathbf v_{CA}=0.
$$

This velocity triangle is necessary but not enough: a single junction velocity $\mathbf u_J$ must also keep the meeting point on all three moving boundaries. For symmetric spreading between A and B, the ridge constraint is $[\mathbf u_J-(\mathbf v_A+\mathbf v_B)/2]\cdot\mathbf n=0$. For a transform, $\mathbf v_A\cdot\mathbf n=\mathbf v_B\cdot\mathbf n$, and the junction must have that same normal component. For a trench, its normal migration must match the adopted subduction/rollback model; a trench fixed relative to the [overriding plate](../../../../../overriding-plate.md) shares that plate's normal velocity.

In velocity space each boundary supplies a line of possible junction velocities. A common intersection means the junction can migrate while preserving its local configuration; incompatible constraints force boundary reorganization. This is kinematic stability, not the claim that the junction is stationary. A symmetric ridge–ridge–ridge example has three arms meeting at $120^\circ$ and a common junction velocity; unequal spreading rates do not require those equal angles. Transform and trench junctions depend on the allowed relative motions and boundary geometry. Stable and unstable evolution were formulated through plate-velocity geometry in the original triple-junction analysis.[Abstract, stable versus unstable junction configurations](../../../../../evolution-of-triple-junctions-by-dan-mckenzie-and-w-j-morgan.md)
