# Group of order six

↑ **Parent:** [Finite group theory](finite-group-theory-split.md)

Every [group](group-split.md) of order six is isomorphic to the [cyclic group](cyclic-group.md) $C_6$ or the [symmetric group on three points](symmetric-group-on-three-points.md) $S_3$. [Lagrange's theorem](lagrange-s-theorem.md) restricts element orders to $1,2,3,6$. If there is an element of order six, it generates the group. If every nonidentity element had order two, the [group of exponent two is abelian](group-of-exponent-two-is-abelian.md) result would produce a subgroup $\{e,a,b,ab\}$ of order four, contradicting Lagrange's theorem. Thus the noncyclic case has an element $x$ of order three.

There cannot be two distinct subgroups of order three: their intersection would be trivial and their nine pairwise products would be distinct elements of a six-element group. Hence exactly two elements have order three, and the other three nonidentity elements have order two. The subgroup $\langle x\rangle$ has index two and is normal. Choose an involution $h$ outside it. Conjugation sends $x$ to $x$ or $x^{-1}$. The first case would make $xh$ have order six, so $hxh^{-1}=x^{-1}$. The generators therefore obey $x^3=h^2=e$, $hxh^{-1}=x^{-1}$, the relations realized by $(123)$ and $(12)$ in $S_3$.
