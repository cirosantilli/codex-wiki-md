# Enantiomeric excess

↑ **Parent:** [Specific rotation](specific-rotation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomeric_excess)

The [enantiomeric excess](enantiomeric-excess.md) of an [enantiomer](enantiomer.md) pair is the magnitude of the difference between their relative [mole fractions](mole-fraction.md), $\mathrm{ee}=|x_+-x_-|$, often reported as a percentage. Here $x_++x_-=1$ counts the pair only, excluding [solvent](solvent.md); the signs label [optical rotation](optical-rotation.md) under the specified conditions, not [R and S configuration](r-and-s-configuration.md).

In the model above, $x_+=0.75$ and $x_-=0.25$ give $\mathrm{ee}=0.50=50\%$. Keep the same total pair [concentration](concentration.md) $c=0.200\,\mathrm{g\,mL^{-1}}$, path length, [solvent](solvent.md), [temperature](temperature-split.md) and [wavelength](wavelength.md). The two contributions are

$$
c_+=0.150,\quad c_-=0.050\quad(\mathrm{g\,mL^{-1}}),\qquad
\alpha=40(1)(0.150-0.050)^\circ=+4^\circ.
$$

The $+8^\circ$ pure-member reference therefore gives $\alpha/\alpha_{\mathrm{pure},+}=+0.50$. This ratio measures signed population imbalance only for a pure pair with additive linear responses under matching conditions. Adding unrelated [solutes](solute.md), changing total [concentration](concentration.md), or changing [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md) can invalidate that inference. For $x_+=x_-=0.50$, the [racemate](racemate.md) gives zero net [optical rotation](optical-rotation.md) by cancellation; each [molecule](molecule-split.md) remains [chiral](chirality-chemistry.md). A zero reading alone proves neither a [racemate](racemate.md) nor molecular [achirality](achirality.md).
