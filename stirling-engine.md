# Stirling engine

↑ **Parent:** [Heat engine](heat-engine.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stirling_engine)

A Stirling engine repeatedly heats and cools a confined [gas](gas.md), whose expansion and compression drive a moving mechanical load. This makes it a particularly accessible example of a [heat engine](heat-engine.md): both the repeating motion and the two regions exchanging [heat](heat.md) can be observed.

In the published [Demonstration experiments with a Stirling engine by Christopher G Deacon and others](demonstration-experiments-with-a-stirling-engine-by-christopher-g-deacon-and-others.md), an electrical heater warms one end of a cylinder and flowing [water](water.md) cools the other at $12\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. A moving displacer shuttles [gas](gas.md) through [copper](copper.md) wool that temporarily stores and returns [internal energy](internal-energy.md). A [laser](laser.md) spot traces a closed [pressure](pressure.md)-[volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) path on a screen: mechanical motion controls one coordinate, [pressure](pressure.md) the other. Calibrating both scales converts the enclosed [area](surface-area.md) to [work](work.md) per [thermodynamic cycle](thermodynamic-cycle.md); multiplying by rotation [frequency](frequency.md) gives gas-cycle [power](power.md). At $97\,\mathrm W$ heater input, the paper reports $4.4\,\mathrm W$ gas-cycle [power](power.md), while a separate [Prony brake](prony-brake.md) measures $3.4\,\mathrm W$ shaft output. The cold-side [temperature](temperature.md) is controlled and two higher heater settings are compared. The gas has a nonuniform [temperature](temperature.md), and heater [energy](energy.md) also escapes to the room: neither output/input ratio measures $W/Q_h$ for an ideal two-[thermal reservoir](heat-bath.md) engine. These observations demonstrate [work](work.md) production and losses, not a direct measurement of the bound in [Carnot's theorem](carnot-s-theorem.md). The inspected apparatus, readouts and results are on pp. 180–182, Figures 1–3 and Table 1.
