<h1 id="tutte-s-theorem-on-perfect-matchings">Tutte's theorem on perfect matchings</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Perfect matching](perfect-matching.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutte's_theorem_on_perfect_matchings)

A finite [graph](graph-split.md) has a [perfect matching](perfect-matching.md) if and only if the displayed inequalities hold, where $o(H)$ counts the [connected components of a graph](component-graph-theory.md) $H$ with an [odd integer](odd-integer.md) number of [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md). The theorem is associated with [William Thomas Tutte](william-thomas-tutte.md).[final paragraph, the criterion for a perfect matching](non-bipartite-matching-lecture-notes-by-mit.md)

Necessity has a direct counting proof: each such component of $G-S$ must send at least one [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) of a [perfect matching](perfect-matching.md) to $S$, and different components must use different [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md) of $S$. The sufficiency direction is the substantive content of the theorem. The criterion is useful because [vertex degree](degree-graph-theory.md) and [parity](parity.md) estimates can prove existence without constructing the [perfect matching](perfect-matching.md), as in the [perfect matching theorem for a cubic graph with at most two bridges](perfect-matching-theorem-for-a-cubic-graph-with-at-most-two-bridges.md).
