<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [deuteron](../../../../../deuteron.md) is the bound [proton](../../../../../proton.md)–[neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) system. Its ground state has $J^P=1^+$ and [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md) $T=0$, with binding [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) approximately $2.225\ \mathrm{MeV}$ and no excited bound state. The small binding compared with characteristic nuclear [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) scales produces an extended tail. With [reduced mass](../../../../../reduced-mass.md) $\mu\simeq m_N/2$, the asymptotic radial wavefunction decays as $e^{-\kappa r}$, where

$$
\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{2\mu B}}\hbar,
\qquad \kappa^{-1}\simeq4.3\ \mathrm{fm}.
$$

The decay length is not a sharp deuteron [radius](../../../../../radius.md) or the force range. It illustrates weak binding outside the region of strong attraction.

The observed total [spin](../../../../../spin.md) is consistent with aligned nucleon spins, $S=1$, and a predominantly $L=0$ spatial state, $\,{}^3S_1$. A central attraction identical in both spin–isospin channels would not distinguish this channel from a spin-singlet [proton](../../../../../proton.md)–[neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) channel. In fact the triplet channel binds while the corresponding singlet channel does not. A realistic [nuclear force](../../../../../nuclear-force-split.md) must therefore distinguish these spin–isospin channels, for example through a term $V_\sigma(r)\boldsymbol\sigma_p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma_n$, whose eigenvalues are $+1$ and $-3$ in triplet and singlet states. The absence of a bound dineutron and diproton reinforces the need to distinguish channels; [proton](../../../../../proton.md)–[proton](../../../../../proton.md) Coulomb repulsion is an additional effect, not a complete explanation of the singlet nuclear interaction.

The clearest evidence against a purely central force is the nonzero [electric quadrupole moment](../../../../../electric-quadrupole-moment.md). A pure isotropic S-wave charge distribution would give zero. The [deuteron quadrupole evidence for a tensor force](../../../../../deuteron-quadrupole-evidence-for-a-tensor-force.md) instead points to a [nuclear tensor force](../../../../../nuclear-tensor-force.md),

$$
V_T(r)S_{12},\qquad
S_{12}=3(\boldsymbol\sigma_p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})
(\boldsymbol\sigma_n\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})
-\boldsymbol\sigma_p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma_n.
$$

It depends on how the spins point relative to the line joining the particles. It preserves total $J$ but couples orbital angular momenta differing by two, producing

$$
|d\rangle=\frac{u(r)}r|{}^3S_1\rangle+\frac{w(r)}r|{}^3D_1\rangle.
$$

Both terms have $J^P=1^+$, and their interference permits a quadrupole moment. The 1939 theoretical analysis explicitly connected the measured quadrupole moment with such directional dependence.[Accessible abstract, Nature 144, 476–477](../../../../../the-electric-quadrupole-moment-of-the-deuteron-and-the-field-theory-of-nuclear-forces-by-christian-moller-and-leon-rosenfeld.md)

The [nuclear magnetic moment](../../../../../nuclear-magnetic-moment.md) provides a second check. A pure aligned-spin S wave would give approximately $\mu_p+\mu_n\simeq0.880\mu_N$, close to but larger than the observed deuteron value approximately $0.857\mu_N$. The discrepancy is consistent with non-S-wave structure and interaction-current corrections; it is not by itself a model-independent measurement of a D-state probability. The [deuteron D-state admixture](../../../../../deuteron-d-state-admixture.md) depends on the adopted Hamiltonian and electromagnetic current. The magnetic moments and [mass](../../../../../mass.md) differences used for these scales are tabulated physical constants.[2022 CODATA adjustment, [proton](../../../../../proton.md), [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) and deuteron magnetic moments and rest-mass energies](../../../../../fundamental-physical-constants-complete-listing-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology-and-others.md)

A useful schematic interaction therefore contains central, spin-dependent and tensor terms, and possibly [spin](../../../../../spin.md)–orbit terms in other channels. Long-range [one-pion exchange](../../../../../one-pion-exchange.md) naturally supplies a tensor component, with its finite range set by the pion [mass](../../../../../mass.md). **The deuteron establishes an attractive triplet channel and a noncentral tensor interaction; a spin-independent central potential is insufficient.**
