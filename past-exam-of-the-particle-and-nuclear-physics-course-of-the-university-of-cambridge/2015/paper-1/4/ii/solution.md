<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The mirror nuclei have interchanged [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) and [proton](../../../../../proton.md) numbers. Neglect their strong-interaction binding difference and describe them with the same uniformly charged sharp sphere of [radius](../../../../../radius.md) $R$. The [nuclear Coulomb energy](../../../../../nuclear-coulomb-energy.md) is

$$
E_C(Z)=\frac35\frac{Z^2e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0R}
=\frac35\frac{Z^2\alpha\hbar c}{R}.
$$

Changing one [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) into a [proton](../../../../../proton.md) decreases the nucleon rest-mass sum by $m_n-m_p=1.294\ \mathrm{MeV}/c^2$, but raises the Coulomb [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). The diagram gives $(M_F-M_O)c^2=2.25\ \mathrm{MeV}$ for nuclear masses, so

$$
2.25=-1.294+\frac35(9^2-8^2)\frac{\alpha\hbar c}{R}.
$$

With the standard electromagnetic conversion $\alpha\hbar c\simeq1.44\ \mathrm{MeV\,fm}$, the [uniform-sphere Coulomb displacement energy](../../../../../uniform-sphere-coulomb-displacement-energy.md) estimate is

$$
\boxed{R=\frac{(3/5)(17)(1.44)}{3.544}\ \mathrm{fm}\simeq4.14\ \mathrm{fm}.}
$$

This $R$ is the uniform sphere's outer [radius](../../../../../radius.md). If “charge [radius](../../../../../radius.md)” means the rms [nuclear charge radius](../../../../../nuclear-charge-radius.md), the same distribution gives

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{\langle r^2\rangle}=\sqrt{3/5}\,R\simeq3.21\ \mathrm{fm}.}
$$

The numerical result is an estimate within the charge-density model, not a model-independent [radius](../../../../../radius.md) measurement. A discrete-proton correction replaces $Z^2$ by $Z(Z-1)$, giving $\Delta[Z(Z-1)]=16$ and $R\simeq3.90\ \mathrm{fm}$. The main answer above follows the literal continuum charged-sphere model. Strong isospin-breaking, nonuniform density and changes in the valence-nucleon distribution also limit the extraction.
