<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The entrance [Yukawa coupling](../../../../../yukawa-interaction.md) is proportional to the incident lepton [mass](../../../../../mass.md), so the partial Higgs width into that lepton pair is proportional to its [mass](../../../../../mass.md) squared. With the question's masses,

$$
\frac{\Gamma_{\mu\mu}}{\Gamma_{ee}}\simeq
\left(\frac{106\ \mathrm{MeV}}{0.5\ \mathrm{MeV}}\right)^2
=\boxed{4.49\times10^4.}
$$

The same factor enhances direct s-channel [Higgs boson](../../../../../higgs-boson.md) formation when all other factors are held fixed. **The much larger [muon](../../../../../muon.md) Yukawa coupling motivates a [muon-collider Higgs resonance](../../../../../muon-collider-higgs-resonance.md).** Heavier [muons](../../../../../muon.md) also radiate less strongly in a curved orbit, but their decay lifetime makes a collider technically demanding. The resonance remains narrow, so a sufficiently small beam-energy spread is essential to retain a large fraction of the ideal peak.
