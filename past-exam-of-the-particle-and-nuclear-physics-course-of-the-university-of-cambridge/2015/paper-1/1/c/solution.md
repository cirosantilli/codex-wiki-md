<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use [natural units](../../../../../natural-units.md) and invariant squared center-of-mass [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $s$. For a massless incident [neutrino](../../../../../neutrino.md) and an initially stationary [electron](../../../../../electron.md),

$$
s=(p_\nu+p_e)^2=m_e^2+2m_eE_\nu.
$$

At the production threshold the final massless [neutrino](../../../../../neutrino.md) can have arbitrarily small center-of-mass [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), so the lower endpoint is $s=m_\ell^2$. The [charged-current neutrino-electron threshold](../../../../../charged-current-neutrino-electron-threshold.md) is consequently

$$
\boxed{E_{\nu,\min}=\frac{m_\ell^2-m_e^2}{2m_e}.}
$$

For the three flavours, using precisely the masses given in this part, the answers are **$0$ for $\nu_e$**, **$1.0994\times10^4\ \mathrm{MeV}\simeq11.0\ \mathrm{GeV}$ for $\nu_\mu$**, and **$3.0898\times10^6\ \mathrm{MeV}\simeq3.09\ \mathrm{TeV}$ for $\nu_\tau$**. The zero threshold for the [electron](../../../../../electron.md) flavour is an infimum for elastic scattering; it does not imply a nonzero interaction rate at zero incident [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). The large heavy-lepton thresholds arise because most laboratory [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) becomes center-of-mass motion against a very light target.
