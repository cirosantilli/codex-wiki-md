<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A physical [baryon](../../../../../baryon.md) is a [colour singlet](../../../../../colour-singlet.md). Its three-quark colour factor is antisymmetric, proportional to $\epsilon_{abc}|abc\rangle$. In the hypothetical scalar-quark theory, the [quarks](../../../../../quark.md) obey [Bose-Einstein statistics](../../../../../bose-einstein-statistics.md), so the complete three-particle state must be symmetric under exchange. At $L=0$ the spatial factor is symmetric, and three zero spins supply no nontrivial [spin](../../../../../spin.md) factor. The flavour factor must therefore be antisymmetric to cancel the antisymmetry of the colour factor.

There is exactly one completely antisymmetric state made from three flavour basis states: the [flavour singlet of three scalar quarks](../../../../../flavour-singlet-of-three-scalar-quarks.md),

$$
|F\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt6}\bigl(|uds\rangle+|dsu\rangle+|sud\rangle
-|usd\rangle-|dus\rangle-|sdu\rangle\bigr).
$$

Equivalently, $\dim\bigwedge^3\mathbb C^3=1$. A repeated flavour gives zero under complete antisymmetrization. **Only one ground-state flavour singlet, containing $uds$ and having $J^P=0^+$, remains.** Keeping fermionic exchange antisymmetry while changing the spins to zero would contradict the assumed [Spin-statistics theorem](../../../../../spin-statistics-theorem.md). The standard antisymmetric colour factor and the ordinary-quark flavour decomposition are documented in the quark-model review.[[Quark](../../../../../quark.md) Model, §15.5, equations (15.22)–(15.24)](../../../../../review-of-particle-physics-by-particle-data-group-2022.md)
