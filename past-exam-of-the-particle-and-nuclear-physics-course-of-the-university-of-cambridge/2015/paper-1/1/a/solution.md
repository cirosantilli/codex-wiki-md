<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A positive-parity $K=0$ [nuclear rotational band](../../../../../nuclear-rotational-band.md) in an [even-even nucleus](../../../../../even-even-nucleus.md) has $J=0,2,4,\ldots$ and excitation [energies](../../../../../energy-split.md)

$$
E_J=\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mathcal I}J(J+1).
$$

The four nonzero levels give $E_J/[J(J+1)]=15.17,14.85,14.48,14.01\ \mathrm{keV}$. These approximately constant coefficients, together with the even-spin positive-[parity](../../../../../parity.md) sequence, support a rotational interpretation. The roughly eight-percent decline means a constant-inertia rigid rotor is only an approximation: the effective [moment of inertia](../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) increases along the sequence.

Using the lowest excited level gives

$$
\boxed{\mathcal I=\frac{3\hbar^2}{91\ \mathrm{keV}}
=2.29\times10^{-54}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2}.}
$$

Using the highest level instead gives $2.48\times10^{-54}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, showing the scale of the departure from a constant inertia. This is a comparison of [nuclear rotational inertia relative to a rigid sphere](../../../../../nuclear-rotational-inertia-relative-to-a-rigid-sphere.md), not evidence for an exactly spherical rotating intrinsic state.

For a conventional size estimate, explicitly take a uniform sharp [radius](../../../../../radius.md) $R=r_0A^{1/3}$ with $r_0\simeq1.2\ \mathrm{fm}$ and [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $M\simeq A m_u$. These are standard scale approximations, not additional measured properties of this isotope supplied by the paper. They give $R\simeq6.70\ \mathrm{fm}$ and

$$
\mathcal I_{\rm rigid}=\frac25MR^2\simeq5.19\times10^{-54}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2},\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\mathcal I}{\mathcal I_{\rm rigid}}\simeq0.44
\left(\frac{1.2\ \mathrm{fm}}{r_0}\right)^2.}
$$

Thus the fitted rotational inertia is substantially smaller than the rigid-body scale. [Nuclear pairing](../../../../../nuclear-pairing.md) provides a physical reason collective nuclear inertia need not equal the classical rigid-body value. The comparison's numerical [radius](../../../../../radius.md) dependence is explicit; it is not an independently determined [radius](../../../../../radius.md).
