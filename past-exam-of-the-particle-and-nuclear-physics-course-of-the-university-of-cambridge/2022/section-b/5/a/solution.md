<h1 id="section-b/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A ground-state [baryon](../../../../../baryon.md) contains three [quarks](../../../../../quark.md) in a spatially symmetric $L=0$ [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md), giving positive [parity](../../../../../parity.md). Each [quark](../../../../../quark.md) is a [spin one-half](../../../../../spin-one-half.md) [fermion](../../../../../fermion.md), so the complete [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) must be antisymmetric under interchange of its complete quark labels. A physical [baryon](../../../../../baryon.md) is a [colour singlet](../../../../../colour-singlet.md), with the antisymmetric colour factor

$$
\psi_{\mathrm{colour}}\propto\epsilon_{abc}|a\,b\,c\rangle.
$$

Consequently the combined spin-and-flavour factor must be symmetric. The product notation in the question organizes the degrees of freedom; it need not mean that spin and flavour are separately unentangled. In particular the octet requires a correlated sum of products.

For the three light flavours, the [flavour symmetry](../../../../../flavor-symmetry.md) decomposition is

$$
\mathbf3\otimes\mathbf3\otimes\mathbf3
=\mathbf{10}_{S}\oplus\mathbf8_{M}\oplus\mathbf8_{M}\oplus\mathbf1_{A}.
$$

The subscripts describe permutation symmetry, with $M$ denoting mixed symmetry. The [addition of angular momentum](../../../../../addition-of-angular-momentum.md) of three [spin one-half](../../../../../spin-one-half.md) particles gives

$$
\mathbf2\otimes\mathbf2\otimes\mathbf2
=\mathbf4_S\oplus\mathbf2_M\oplus\mathbf2_M.
$$

The symmetric spin quartet has total $J=3/2$; the two spin doublets have $J=1/2$.

For $J=3/2$, the spin factor is symmetric, so the flavour factor must also be symmetric: this gives the **spin-$3/2$ baryon decuplet**. Its flavour content is four $\Delta$ states, three $\Sigma^*$ states, two $\Xi^*$ states, and one $\Omega$ state.

For $J=1/2$, the mixed-symmetry spin functions combine with the mixed-symmetry flavour functions to make one fully symmetric spin-flavour factor for each octet weight: this gives the **spin-$1/2$ baryon octet**, containing two [nucleons](../../../../../nucleon.md), three [Sigma baryons](../../../../../sigma-baryon.md), one [Lambda baryon](../../../../../lambda-baryon.md), and two [Xi baryons](../../../../../xi-baryon.md). The two mixed-symmetry octets in the raw flavour product are not two distinct ground-state octets; their permutation partners are correlated with the spin factors. An antisymmetric flavour singlet would require a fully antisymmetric spin factor to make a symmetric product, but three spin states drawn from a two-dimensional single-quark spin space have no such factor.

Equivalently, the symmetric three-particle representation of [spin-flavour symmetry](../../../../../spin-flavour-symmetry.md) has dimension $\binom{8}{3}=56$ and decomposes under flavour and spin into

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{56}_{S}=(\mathbf{10},\mathbf4)\oplus(\mathbf8,\mathbf2),
\qquad56=10\times4+8\times2.}
$$

These are approximate multiplets: the larger [strange quark](../../../../../strange-quark.md) [mass](../../../../../mass.md) breaks exact [flavour symmetry](../../../../../flavor-symmetry.md) and splits their [energies](../../../../../energy-split.md).
