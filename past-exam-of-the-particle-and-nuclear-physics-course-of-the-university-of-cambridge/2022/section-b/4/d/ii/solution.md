<h1 id="section-b/4/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $K$ be the common normalization converting the quoted $N_i$ to photon numbers per parent decay. Apply [nuclear level population balance](../../../../../../nuclear-level-population-balance.md) to scheme A, starting with the top level:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_3&=K(N_2+N_5+N_6),\\
\Gamma_2+KN_2&=K(N_3+N_4),\\
\Gamma_1+K(N_3+N_5)&=KN_1.
\end{aligned}
$$

For example, the $3\to2$ transition contributes $KN_2$ to the population of level 2; it must be subtracted from the total leaving level 2 to obtain its direct beta feeding. Similarly, a [photon](../../../../../../photon.md) in the strong $1\to0$ line need not imply a direct beta transition to level 1.

The three equations give

$$
\Gamma_1=K(N_1-N_3-N_5),\quad
\Gamma_2=K(N_3+N_4-N_2),\quad
\Gamma_3=K(N_2+N_5+N_6).
$$

Adding them cancels the internal cascade counts:

$$
1-\Gamma_0=K(N_1+N_4+N_6),\qquad
K=\frac{1-\Gamma_0}{N_1+N_4+N_6}.
$$

This also has a simple physical meaning: each decay that initially feeds an excited daughter eventually contributes exactly one of the three transitions into the [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md). The number of all emitted [photons](../../../../../../photon.md) is not the correct normalization, because a single cascade can emit several.

Consequently the required formulae are

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_1&=(1-\Gamma_0)\frac{N_1-N_3-N_5}{N_1+N_4+N_6},\\
\Gamma_2&=(1-\Gamma_0)\frac{N_3+N_4-N_2}{N_1+N_4+N_6},\\
\Gamma_3&=(1-\Gamma_0)\frac{N_2+N_5+N_6}{N_1+N_4+N_6}.
\end{aligned}}
$$
