<h1 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

All three processes are [weak charged current](../../../../charged-current.md) transformations between neighboring [nuclear isobars](../../../../isobar-nuclide.md). Neglecting [neutrino masses](../../../../neutrino-mass.md), and writing $M_a(A,Z)$ for a neutral atomic [mass](../../../../mass.md), their [Q values](../../../../q-value.md) are:


- [Beta-minus decay](../../../../beta-minus-decay.md): a [neutron](../../../../neutron-split.md) converts to a [proton](../../../../proton.md), emitting an [electron](../../../../electron.md) and an [electron antineutrino](../../../../electron-antineutrino.md),


$$
(A,Z)\longrightarrow(A,Z+1)+e^-+\bar\nu_e,\qquad
Q_{\beta^-}=M_a(A,Z)-M_a(A,Z+1)>0.
$$


- [Beta-plus decay](../../../../beta-plus-decay.md): a [proton](../../../../proton.md) converts to a [neutron](../../../../neutron-split.md), emitting a [Positron](../../../../positron.md) and an [electron neutrino](../../../../electron-neutrino.md),


$$
(A,Z)\longrightarrow(A,Z-1)+e^++\nu_e,\qquad
Q_{\beta^+}=M_a(A,Z)-M_a(A,Z-1)-2m_e>0.
$$


- [Electron capture](../../../../electron-capture.md): a bound atomic [electron](../../../../electron.md) is absorbed in the conversion of a [proton](../../../../proton.md) to a [neutron](../../../../neutron-split.md),


$$
e^-+(A,Z)\longrightarrow(A,Z-1)+\nu_e,\qquad
Q_{\mathrm{EC}}=M_a(A,Z)-M_a(A,Z-1)>0.
$$

These formulae use the paper's [natural units](../../../../natural-units.md). With energies in MeV, [beta-plus decay](../../../../beta-plus-decay.md) requires an atomic mass-energy difference exceeding $2m_ec^2=1.022\,\mathrm{MeV}$. The extra electron-mass subtraction arises from using neutral atomic rather than bare nuclear [masses](../../../../mass.md). [Electron capture](../../../../electron-capture.md) can occur when the atomic mass-energy difference is positive but below this threshold. For a particular capture shell or excited daughter, the available [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) must also cover the electron-hole and nuclear [excitation energies](../../../../excitation-energy.md), and the small [nuclear recoil](../../../../nuclear-recoil.md). Small atomic binding corrections are suppressed in the displayed threshold formulae.

The question's fixed-$A$ family consists of [nuclear isobars](../../../../isobar-nuclide.md), rather than [isotopes](../../../../isotope.md): [isotopes](../../../../isotope.md) have fixed $Z$. The [semi-empirical mass formula](../../../../semi-empirical-mass-formula.md) gives a convex [nuclear mass parabola](../../../../nuclear-mass-parabola.md) as a function of $Z$ at fixed $A$. For odd $A$, [nuclear pairing](../../../../nuclear-pairing.md) does not split the family into two branches, so there is **typically one beta-stable isobar**, at the discrete minimum. Neighboring members can lower their [mass](../../../../mass.md) by changing $Z$ toward it.

For even $A$, [nuclear pairing](../../../../nuclear-pairing.md) lowers even-even [nuclei](../../../../atomic-nucleus-split.md) and raises odd-odd [nuclei](../../../../atomic-nucleus-split.md), producing two offset [nuclear mass parabolas](../../../../nuclear-mass-parabola.md). There are **often two beta-stable even-even isobars**, separated by an odd-odd member to which neither can energetically decay. The number is not an exact universal rule: some even-$A$ chains have one or more than two such minima against single [beta decay](../../../../beta-decay.md), and a few light odd-odd [nuclei](../../../../atomic-nucleus-split.md) are stable. Stability against single [beta decay](../../../../beta-decay.md) also does not guarantee stability against [double beta decay](../../../../double-beta-decay.md).
