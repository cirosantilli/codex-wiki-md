<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a single-photon transition, [angular momentum addition](../../../../../angular-momentum-addition.md) requires $|J_i-J_f|\le L\le J_i+J_f$, $L\ge1$, together with the electric/magnetic [parity](../../../../../parity.md) rules above. Thus a known final state and a measured multipole restrict both the [spin](../../../../../spin.md) difference and [parity](../../../../../parity.md) of the emitting level. For example, an E1 line reverses [parity](../../../../../parity.md) and allows $\Delta J=0,1$ except $0\to0$; a mixed M1+E2 line preserves [parity](../../../../../parity.md) and must satisfy both angular triangles. An E2 line preserves [parity](../../../../../parity.md) and allows [spin](../../../../../spin.md) differences up to two.

Line energies identify connected levels, while calibrated [intensities](../../../../../intensity-physics.md) and coincidences test cascade flow and branching. If a proposed assignment permits a fast low-order transition to a lower state, an experimentally stringent absence of that line argues against the assignment. Nevertheless, **an allowed transition can have a small or vanishing nuclear [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) element**. Pure E2 radiation alone does not prove that M1 is forbidden by [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md). Detection sensitivity, efficiency and overlap must be considered, and in real nuclei [internal conversion](../../../../../internal-conversion.md) can remove [photons](../../../../../photon.md); this question explicitly excludes that competing channel. Lifetimes or reduced-strength estimates make a missing-line argument substantially stronger than a bare selection-rule statement.
