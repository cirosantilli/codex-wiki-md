<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For comparable transition energies and ordinary single-particle nuclear strengths in the long-wavelength regime, the usual qualitative ordering is **$E1$, $M1$, $E2$, $M2$, $E3$, $M3$ from faster to slower**. Each increase of multipole order is strongly suppressed, and magnetic radiation is usually weaker than electric radiation of the same order. More precisely,

$$
\lambda(\sigma L)\propto E_\gamma^{2L+1}B(\sigma L),\qquad \sigma=E\text{ or }M.
$$

The nuclear reduced transition strength $B$ is essential: collective enhancement, cancellation and [selection rules](../../../../../../selection-rule.md) can overturn this benchmark. It is not a universal ranking between arbitrary transitions of different energies. The weak measured M3 branch is consistent with high-multipole suppression, but its absolute lifetime cannot be obtained from its arbitrary-unit [intensity](../../../../../../intensity-physics.md) alone.[Appendix C, printed pp.60–67, transition rates and Weisskopf single-particle estimates](../../../../../../guidelines-for-nuclear-structure-evaluators-by-murray-j-martin.md)
