<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A useful convention for the [semi-empirical mass formula](../../../../../semi-empirical-mass-formula.md) is

$$
B(A,Z)=a_vA-a_sA^{2/3}-a_c\frac{Z(Z-1)}{A^{1/3}}
-a_a\frac{(A-2Z)^2}{A}+\delta(A,Z),
$$

where $B>0$ is the binding [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). The nuclear [mass](../../../../../mass.md) is $Zm_p+(A-Z)m_n-B/c^2$. Atomic-mass comparisons instead use hydrogen-atom [masses](../../../../../mass.md) and account for [electron](../../../../../electron.md) binding at the desired accuracy. No numerical fit coefficients are specified here.

The volume term represents short-range nuclear attraction with saturation: each [nucleon](../../../../../nucleon.md) has a limited number of close neighbours. The surface term subtracts the binding lost by [nucleons](../../../../../nucleon.md) at the boundary. Coulomb repulsion penalizes a large [proton](../../../../../proton.md) fraction. The asymmetry term penalizes unequal [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) and [proton](../../../../../proton.md) populations, reflecting the cost of filling different [Fermi seas](../../../../../fermi-sea.md) and nuclear interactions. Pairing favours even-even nuclei and penalizes odd-odd nuclei; odd-$A$ nuclei have zero pairing term in this approximation. For example, one may choose $\delta=\pm a_pA^{-1/2}$; an alternative fitted power requires a correspondingly different coefficient.

At fixed $A$, the smooth [mass](../../../../../mass.md) dependence on $Z$ is approximately parabolic. Neglecting the neutron-proton [mass](../../../../../mass.md) difference, pairing and small linear-in-$Z$ Coulomb corrections, minimization gives

$$
Z_{\rm stable}\simeq\frac{A}{2+(a_c/2a_a)A^{2/3}}.
$$

Thus the preferred [neutron excess](../../../../../neutron-excess.md) grows with [mass number](../../../../../mass-number.md). The surface penalty disfavors very light systems, while the growing Coulomb penalty reduces the binding per [nucleon](../../../../../nucleon.md) of very heavy systems; this explains broad fusion and fission [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) trends. The formula is a bulk approximation: shell closures, deformation and small separation-energy differences can give important exceptions. A fitted average binding trend is not a reliable substitute for a measured decay threshold near a boundary of stability.
