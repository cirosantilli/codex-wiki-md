<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The radiative $X^0$ decay conserves [strangeness](../../../../../strangeness.md) and proceeds electromagnetically. A strong $\Lambda\pi$ decay is closed because even the lighter neutral-pion threshold exceeds the supplied $X^0$ [mass](../../../../../mass.md). The $Y^-$ decay has about $65\,\mathrm{MeV}$ of available [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), but changes [strangeness](../../../../../strangeness.md) from $-2$ to $-1$, so it requires the [weak interaction](../../../../../weak-interaction.md). Its lifetime is therefore ordinarily much longer than the electromagnetic lifetime of $X^0$; no numerical lifetime follows from these [masses](../../../../../mass.md) alone.

Both particles are [baryons](../../../../../baryon.md) with half-integer [spin](../../../../../spin.md). A radiative transition to [spin](../../../../../spin.md) $1/2$ can couple through [photon](../../../../../photon.md) multipoles $L\ge1$, giving $J_X=L\pm1/2$. Electromagnetic [parity](../../../../../parity.md) is conserved, but without the electric/magnetic multipolarity neither $J_X$ nor its [parity](../../../../../parity.md) is fixed. In the weak $Y^-$ decay, coupling orbital $L$ to the final $\Lambda$ [spin](../../../../../spin.md) gives $J_Y=L\pm1/2$; [parity conservation](../../../../../parity-conservation.md) cannot be imposed on that weak [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md). The supplied modes therefore do not determine unique spin-parity assignments.

The [masses](../../../../../mass.md) and flavours identify the familiar candidates as $\Sigma^0$ and $\Xi^-$. Their established ground-state assignments are both $1/2^+$, consistent with the lowest-orbital [quark model](../../../../../quark-model.md), but this is additional empirical/model information rather than a consequence of the two [decay channels](../../../../../decay-channel.md) alone.[Sigma-zero and Xi-minus listings, each p.1, established quantum numbers and decay information](../../../../../review-of-particle-physics-by-particle-data-group-2022.md)
