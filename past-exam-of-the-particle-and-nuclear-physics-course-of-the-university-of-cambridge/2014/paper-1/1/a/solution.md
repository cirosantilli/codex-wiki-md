<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In [time](../../../../../time-in-physics.md) $\Delta t$ the particle advances $\beta c\Delta t$, while a light wavelet travels $c\Delta t/n$. Coherent addition along the direction of emitted [photons](../../../../../photon.md) gives the [Cherenkov cone angle](../../../../../cherenkov-cone-angle.md)

$$
\boxed{\cos\alpha=\frac1{n\beta},\qquad n\beta>1.}
$$

The angle is measured between [photon](../../../../../photon.md) propagation and the particle velocity. It is complementary to the angle between the conical [wavefront](../../../../../wavefront.md) surface and the trajectory; confusing those two angles exchanges sine and cosine. At threshold the [photon](../../../../../photon.md) cone closes, $\alpha\to0$.
