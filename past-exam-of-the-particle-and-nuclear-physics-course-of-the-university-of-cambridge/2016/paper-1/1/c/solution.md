<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For [neutrino-electron elastic scattering](../../../../../neutrino-electron-elastic-scattering.md), work in the laboratory frame with a stationary [electron](../../../../../electron.md) and a massless incoming [neutrino](../../../../../neutrino.md) of [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $E$. If the [electron](../../../../../electron.md) receives [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) $T$ and [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) $\mathbf p$, its [relativistic energy-momentum relation](../../../../../energy-momentum-relation.md) gives

$$
p^2=(m+T)^2-m^2=T^2+2mT.
$$

[conservation of energy](../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) fixes the outgoing [neutrino](../../../../../neutrino.md) [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) to $E-T$, and [conservation of momentum](../../../../../momentum-conservation.md) fixes its [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) to $\mathbf k-\mathbf p$. Its zero [mass](../../../../../mass.md) therefore requires

$$
(E-T)^2=E^2+p^2-2Ep\cos\theta.
$$

Substitution and cancellation give

$$
(E+m)T=E\sqrt{T(T+2m)}\cos\theta.
$$

For $T>0$, this requires $\cos\theta>0$; squaring must not introduce a backward-electron branch. Squaring, cancelling $T$ and solving gives

$$
\boxed{T=\frac{2mE^2\cos^2\theta}{(E+m)^2-E^2\cos^2\theta}.}
$$

The expression increases with $\cos^2\theta$ on the physical interval $0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2$. Maximum transfer occurs for a forward [electron](../../../../../electron.md) and a backward outgoing [neutrino](../../../../../neutrino.md):

$$
T_{\max}=\frac{2E^2}{m+2E}
=\frac{2(0.86)^2}{0.511+2(0.86)}\,\mathrm{MeV}
=\boxed{0.663\,\mathrm{MeV}.}
$$

The remaining $0.197\,\mathrm{MeV}$ is carried by the [neutrino](../../../../../neutrino.md). An elastic collision cannot transfer the entire incident [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) to a free massive [electron](../../../../../electron.md).
