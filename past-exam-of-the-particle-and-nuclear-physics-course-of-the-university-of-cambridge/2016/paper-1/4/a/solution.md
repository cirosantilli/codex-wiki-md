<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [S wave](../../../../../s-wave-quantum-scattering.md) has $L=0$ and positive [parity](../../../../../parity.md). The [deuteron](../../../../../deuteron.md)'s $J=1$ therefore requires a [spin-one-half triplet state](../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md), $S=1$. The two-nucleon [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md) description makes this the antisymmetric $T=0$ channel. Its existence proves sufficient attraction to bind in the $\,{}^3S_1$-dominated channel.

A [spin one-half](../../../../../spin-one-half.md) pair could also form $S=0$. The absence of a bound $\,{}^1S_0$ [deuteron](../../../../../deuteron.md) indicates that the singlet interaction does not bind, not that it is everywhere repulsive. Together with triplet binding, this shows that a spin-independent central attraction is insufficient. No radial excited bound level also constrains the strength and range: the attraction supports only a shallow low-energy [bound state](../../../../../bound-state.md).

For identical [neutrons](../../../../../neutron-split.md) or [protons](../../../../../proton.md), two-particle [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md) has $T=1$ and is symmetric. At even $L$, spatial exchange is symmetric, so the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) requires an antisymmetric [spin](../../../../../spin.md) state, $S=0$. A low-energy $nn$ or $pp$ pair cannot occupy the [deuteron](../../../../../deuteron.md)'s $S=1,T=0$ channel. With approximate [charge independence of nuclear forces](../../../../../charge-independence-of-nuclear-forces.md), their singlet [strong interaction](../../../../../strong-interaction.md) corresponds to the unbound neutron-proton singlet; proton-proton electrostatic repulsion further disfavors binding. **There is no bound [dineutron](../../../../../dineutron.md) or [diproton](../../../../../diproton.md) in these physical low-energy channels.** The inference uses charge independence and singlet-channel behavior; the [deuteron](../../../../../deuteron.md)'s quantum numbers alone do not constrain every imaginable potential.
