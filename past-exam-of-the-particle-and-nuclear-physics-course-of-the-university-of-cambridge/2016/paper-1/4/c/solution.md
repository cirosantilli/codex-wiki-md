<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In a pure $L=0,S=1$ stretched state both [spins](../../../../../spin.md) point along the total [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md), with no orbital contribution. The [nuclear magnetic moment](../../../../../nuclear-magnetic-moment.md) would be

$$
\mu_d^{(S)}=\mu_p+\mu_n=(2.793-1.913)\mu_N=0.880\mu_N.
$$

The measured $0.857\mu_N$ differs. More decisively, a pure [S wave](../../../../../s-wave-quantum-scattering.md) spatial state is isotropic and has zero [electric quadrupole moment](../../../../../electric-quadrupole-moment.md). Its nonzero value requires a nonspherical component of the quantum charge distribution.

Positive [parity](../../../../../parity.md) allows even $L$; coupling $S=1$ to $J=1$ permits $L=0$ and $L=2$. The state contains a [deuteron D-state admixture](../../../../../deuteron-d-state-admixture.md):

$$
|\psi_d\rangle=\sqrt{1-P_D}\,|{}^3S_1\rangle+\sqrt{P_D}\,|{}^3D_1\rangle,
$$

with radial [wavefunctions](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) implicit. A [nuclear tensor force](../../../../../nuclear-tensor-force.md) couples the orbital components. The quadrupole moment involves their interference as well as a D-state contribution, making it sensitive to a small admixture. This is a quantum angular correlation, not two particles permanently fixed in space.

In the nonrelativistic one-body magnetic-moment approximation,

$$
\frac{\mu_d}{\mu_N}=(1-P_D)\mu_s+P_D\left(\frac34-\frac12\mu_s\right),
\qquad \mu_s=\frac{\mu_p+\mu_n}{\mu_N}=0.880.
$$

The D-state coupling has $\langle S_z\rangle=-1/2$ and $\langle L_z\rangle=3/2$ at $J_z=1$; the [proton](../../../../../proton.md) supplies approximately half the relative orbital magnetic moment. Thus

$$
P_D\simeq\frac{0.880-0.857}{\frac32(0.880-\frac12)}
=\boxed{0.040\ \text{in this approximation}.}
$$

The robust conclusion is **a predominantly [S wave](../../../../../s-wave-quantum-scattering.md) state with a small tensor-induced [D wave](../../../../../d-wave.md) component**. The 4% estimate is model dependent: exchange currents and other corrections also affect the magnetic moment, and a [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) component probability is not itself a uniquely measured observable.[§I.F, Observables and not-so observable quantities; §II.B, spin-dependent nuclear interactions](../../../../../modern-theory-of-nuclear-forces-by-evgeny-epelbaum-and-others.md)
