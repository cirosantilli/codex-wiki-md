A [nuclear excited state](../../../../../nuclear-excited-state.md) has internal [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) above its [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md), with levels labelled by [spin](../../../../../spin.md) and [parity](../../../../../parity.md) $J^\pi$ and, where useful, approximate [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md). In the shell model, nucleons occupy orbitals split by [spin-orbit coupling](../../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md); [nuclear particle-hole excitation](../../../../../nuclear-particle-hole-excitation.md) and broken pairs produce excited configurations. An orbital has [parity](../../../../../parity.md) $(-1)^l$, and an even-even paired [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) is commonly $0^+$. Residual interactions mix configurations, so shell spacings are not a complete spectrum.

Collective motion gives distinct patterns. A deformed [nucleus](../../../../../atomic-nucleus-split.md) can have a rotational band with $E_J-E_0=\hbar^2J(J+1)/(2\mathcal I)$, so a simple even-even $0^+,2^+,4^+$ band has $E_4/E_2=10/3$. Near spherical shape, a quadrupole vibration gives a one-phonon $2^+$ excitation; two such [phonons](../../../../../phonon.md) can couple to $0^+,2^+,4^+$ near twice that [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) in the harmonic limit. Anharmonicity and coupling to single-particle motion modify these ideal patterns.

De-excitation occurs by gamma emission, [internal conversion](../../../../../internal-conversion.md), or particle emission if energetically open. [photon](../../../../../photon.md) multipolarity $\lambda\ge1$ must satisfy the angular-momentum triangle rule. Electric multipoles change [parity](../../../../../parity.md) by $(-1)^\lambda$ and magnetic multipoles by $(-1)^{\lambda+1}$; a single [photon](../../../../../photon.md) cannot connect $0\to0$. Large multipolarity or a small transition matrix element can make a long-lived [nuclear isomer](../../../../../nuclear-isomer.md). Gamma energies, coincidence cascades, angular correlations and lifetimes constrain level schemes; inelastic scattering and transfer reactions supply complementary [spin](../../../../../spin.md)/configuration information. One observed line alone need not identify a unique level assignment.
