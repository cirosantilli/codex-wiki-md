<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [diphoton invariant mass](../../../../../diphoton-invariant-mass.md) follows by adding the two [photon](../../../../../photon.md) four-momenta: $M^2c^4=2E_1E_2(1-\cos\theta)$. Thus

$$
\boxed{M=126.65\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^2}.
$$

For [correlated diphoton mass resolution](../../../../../correlated-diphoton-mass-resolution.md), at fixed angle, $dM/M=(dE_1/E_1+dE_2/E_2)/2$. If the two quoted fractional errors are independent one-standard-deviation uncertainties,

$$
\frac{\sigma_M}{M}=\frac12\sqrt{0.1^2+0.1^2},\qquad
\boxed{M=(127\pm9)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^2}.
$$

An unknown common energy-scale error would be correlated: with [correlation coefficient](../../../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md) $\rho$, the fractional variance is $(0.1^2+0.1^2+2\rho\,0.1^2)/4$. Fully correlated scale errors give 10%, rather than 7.1%. A [mass](../../../../../mass.md) consistent with a candidate is not by itself evidence establishing its identity.
