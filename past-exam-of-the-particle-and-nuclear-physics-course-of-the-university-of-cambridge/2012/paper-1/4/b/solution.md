<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

An unstable isolated level has survival amplitude $a(t)=\theta(t)e^{-iE_0t/\hbar}e^{-\Gamma t/(2\hbar)}$, because its [survival probability](../../../../../survival-probability.md) falls as $e^{-\Gamma t/\hbar}$. Its energy-domain amplitude is proportional to

$$
\int_0^\infty e^{i(E-E_0)t/\hbar}e^{-\Gamma t/(2\hbar)}dt
=\frac{\hbar}{\Gamma/2-i(E-E_0)}.
$$

Taking the modulus squared gives the denominator of the [Breit-Wigner resonance cross section](../../../../../breit-wigner-resonance-cross-section.md)

$$
\boxed{(E-E_0)^2+(\Gamma/2)^2}.
$$

At $E-E_0=\pm\Gamma/2$ the resonant factor is half its peak, so $\Gamma$ is the [full width at half maximum](../../../../../full-width-at-half-maximum.md). [exponential decay](../../../../../exponential-decay.md) is the isolated-level approximation; energy-dependent thresholds or overlapping levels need a more general lineshape.
