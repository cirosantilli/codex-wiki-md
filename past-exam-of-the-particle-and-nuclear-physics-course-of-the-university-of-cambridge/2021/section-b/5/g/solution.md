<h1 id="section-b/5/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

The [weak charged current](../../../../../charged-current.md) creates a negative charged [lepton](../../../../../lepton.md) in a left-chiral state. For a massless particle, that would have negative [helicity](../../../../../helicity.md); part (e) instead requires positive [helicity](../../../../../helicity.md). A nonzero [mass](../../../../../mass.md) supplies the necessary wrong-helicity component. This [helicity suppression](../../../../../helicity-suppression.md) is extremely strong for the fast [electron](../../../../../electron.md), but much weaker for the slow [muon](../../../../../muon.md).

For a positive-helicity charged lepton with spinors normalized to unit probability, the left-chiral weight is $(1-\beta_\ell)/2$. With the box-state normalization of part (c), this gives the useful consistent rate ratio

$$
R=\frac{\rho_\mu}{\rho_e}\frac{1-\beta_\mu}{1-\beta_e}.
$$

Indeed $1-\beta_\ell=2m_\ell^2/(m_\pi^2+m_\ell^2)$ cancels the extra energy-dependent factor in that density ratio, leaving the standard [pion electron-to-muon decay ratio](../../../../../pion-electron-to-muon-decay-ratio.md) in inverted form:

$$
\boxed{R=\frac{m_\mu^2}{m_e^2}
\left[\frac{1-m_\mu^2/m_\pi^2}{1-m_e^2/m_\pi^2}\right]^2.}
$$

Using the rounded masses in this paper gives $R\simeq8185$, close to the stated order of $8000$. Precision predictions require more accurate [masses](../../../../../mass.md) and electromagnetic radiative corrections. The essential mechanism is the charged-lepton-mass dependence of the [decay amplitude](../../../../../decay-amplitude.md); simply counting available final [momenta](../../../../../momentum-split.md) misses it.
