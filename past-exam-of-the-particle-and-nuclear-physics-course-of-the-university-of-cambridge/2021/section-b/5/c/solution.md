<h1 id="section-b/5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In the simple box-state counting used here, the final [three-momentum](../../../../../three-momentum.md) lies in a spherical shell: $dn=Cp^2dp$, where the common constant $C$ contains the normalization volume and angular factor. [Momentum conservation](../../../../../momentum-conservation.md) fixes the other final particle's [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md). Hence the [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md) is

$$
\rho_\ell(E_0)=Cp_\ell^2\frac{dp_\ell}{dE_0}.
$$

Combining parts (a) and (b) gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{\rho_\mu}{\rho_e}
=\left[\frac{m_\pi^2-m_\mu^2}{m_\pi^2-m_e^2}\right]^2
\frac{m_\pi^2+m_\mu^2}{m_\pi^2+m_e^2}.}
$$

With the paper's rounded [masses](../../../../../mass.md),

$$
R_{\mathrm{naive}}=\left(\frac{19600-11236}{19600-0.25}\right)^2
\frac{19600+11236}{19600+0.25}
=0.28650.
$$

By [Fermi's golden rule](../../../../../fermi-s-golden-rule.md), equal squared matrix elements make the rate ratio equal to this density ratio. Thus **the naive constant-matrix-element estimate is $R\simeq0.287$**, which favours the electronic decay by about $3.49$ to one.

This is the particular noncovariant state-counting convention in the question. A calculation with the [Lorentz-invariant phase-space measure](../../../../../lorentz-invariant-phase-space-measure.md) places factors $1/(2E)$ in the measure and correspondingly normalizes its [decay amplitude](../../../../../decay-amplitude.md) differently. Only the complete product of phase space and squared matrix element is convention-independent; mixing the two conventions would give a wrong rate.
