<h1 id="section-b/4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In the [Wu experiment](../../../../../wu-experiment.md), [Chien-Shiung Wu](../../../../../chien-shiung-wu.md) and collaborators studied [beta-minus decay](../../../../../beta-minus-decay.md) of polarized [cobalt-60](../../../../../cobalt-60.md),

$$
{}^{60}_{27}\mathrm{Co}\longrightarrow{}^{60}_{28}\mathrm{Ni}^{*}+e^-+\bar\nu_e.
$$

A thin [cobalt-60](../../../../../cobalt-60.md) layer on a [cerium magnesium nitrate](../../../../../cerium-magnesium-nitrate.md) crystal was cooled by [adiabatic demagnetization](../../../../../adiabatic-demagnetization.md) to a few millikelvin. A polarizing [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) produced [nuclear polarization](../../../../../nuclear-polarization.md). An [anthracene](../../../../../anthracene.md) scintillation counter and [photomultiplier](../../../../../photomultiplier.md) detected the emitted [electrons](../../../../../electron.md); angular [gamma ray](../../../../../gamma-ray.md) observations from the daughter de-excitation monitored whether the nuclei remained polarized.[Apparatus and polarization controls, pp. 1413–1414](../../../../../experimental-test-of-parity-conservation-in-beta-decay-by-chien-shiung-wu-and-others.md)

The [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) were emitted preferentially opposite the parent [nuclear spin](../../../../../nuclear-spin.md). Reversing the [nuclear polarization](../../../../../nuclear-polarization.md) reversed the asymmetry, while warming the sample removed both polarization and the effect. Those controls distinguished a spin-correlated asymmetry from a fixed detector bias.

The relevant angular correlation is a [polarized beta-decay angular distribution](../../../../../polarized-beta-decay-angular-distribution.md) of the form

$$
W(\theta)\propto1+A_\beta P\frac vc\cos\theta,
\qquad \cos\theta=\widehat{\mathbf J}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf p}_e.
$$

Under [parity](../../../../../parity.md), $\mathbf p_e\to-\mathbf p_e$ but $\mathbf J\to\mathbf J$, so the spin-momentum correlation changes sign. [Parity conservation](../../../../../parity-conservation.md) would require equal rates for these two directions relative to the same spin. The observed nonzero coefficient therefore demonstrated **parity violation in beta decay**. An unpolarized source would average this correlation to zero and could not provide the same test.
