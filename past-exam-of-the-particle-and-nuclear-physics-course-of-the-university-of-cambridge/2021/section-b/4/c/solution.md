<h1 id="section-b/4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For both modes, a leading weak quark transition in $K^+=u\bar s$ is $\bar s\to\bar u+W^+$, followed by $W^+\to u\bar d$, with the original $u$ acting as a [spectator quark](../../../../../spectator-quark.md). This produces the four quarks needed for $\pi^+\pi^0$, using the $u\bar u$ component of $\pi^0$. Producing three pions additionally requires a $d\bar d$ pair from strong [hadronization](../../../../../hadronization.md). A representative quark-line diagram draws it as gluon emission and splitting.

<a id="section-b/4/c/image-representative-weak-quark-diagrams-for-the-two-pion-and-three-pion-kaon-decays"></a>
![](../../../../../past-exam-of-the-particle-and-nuclear-physics-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2021-kaon-decay-diagrams.png)

**[Figure 3](#section-b/4/c/image-representative-weak-quark-diagrams-for-the-two-pion-and-three-pion-kaon-decays). Representative weak quark diagrams for the two-pion and three-pion kaon decays**.

The three-pion assignment is $u\bar d$, $u\bar d$, and $d\bar u$, matching $\pi^+\pi^+\pi^-$. The extra soft pair creation is a schematic representation of [hadronization](../../../../../hadronization.md), not a controlled perturbative prediction of the relative decay rates. Both processes are first order in the effective weak interaction.

There is an explicit error or hypothetical assumption to address: the original PDF assigns **$0^+$ to all these mesons**. Taken literally, the two-pion final state has $L=0$, because all particles have zero [spin](../../../../../spin.md) and the initial $J$ is zero. Its [parity](../../../../../parity.md) is $(+1)^2(-1)^0=+1$. For three spinless particles, use two Jacobi [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) quantum numbers $l,L$. Coupling to total zero requires $l=L$, so the orbital parity $(-1)^{l+L}$ is $+1$ and the three-particle parity is also $+1$. Thus **under the printed $0^+$ assignment, both channels conserve parity**.

The named physical [pions](../../../../../pion.md) and [kaons](../../../../../kaon.md) are instead [pseudoscalar mesons](../../../../../pseudoscalar-meson.md) with $J^P=0^-$. With those physical assignments,

$$
P_{2\pi}=(-1)^2(-1)^0=+1,\qquad
P_{3\pi}=(-1)^3(-1)^{l+L}=-1,
\qquad P_K=-1.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{Physical mesons: }K^+\to2\pi\text{ violates parity; }K^+\to3\pi\text{ preserves parity}.}
$$

These two opposite-parity final states from the same physical kaon are a classic illustration of [parity violation](../../../../../parity-violation.md) in a weak decay. The physical conclusion requires correcting the printed parity assignment explicitly.
