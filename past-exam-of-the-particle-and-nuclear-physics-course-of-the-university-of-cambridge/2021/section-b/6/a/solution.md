<h1 id="section-b/6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

There are three free-particle rest-energy terms and five nuclear corrections. In the paper's [natural units](../../../../../natural-units.md), their meanings are:

- $Zm_p$: the rest [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) of $Z$ free [protons](../../../../../proton.md).
- $Zm_e$: the rest [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) of the atomic [electrons](../../../../../electron.md); including this term makes the result an atomic, rather than bare nuclear, [mass](../../../../../mass.md). Small atomic binding energies are neglected.
- $(A-Z)m_n$: the rest [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) of the free [neutrons](../../../../../neutron-split.md).
- $a_1A$, with $a_1<0$: the attractive volume term. The short-range [nuclear force](../../../../../nuclear-force-split.md) mainly binds each [nucleon](../../../../../nucleon.md) to nearby neighbours, so the leading attraction is extensive.
- $a_2A^{2/3}>0$: the surface correction. Surface [nucleons](../../../../../nucleon.md) have fewer neighbours and are less bound; surface area grows as $A^{2/3}$ at approximately constant nuclear density.
- $a_3Z^2/A^{1/3}>0$: the [nuclear Coulomb energy](../../../../../nuclear-coulomb-energy.md) cost of repelling [protons](../../../../../proton.md), scaling as $Z^2/R$ with nuclear radius $R\propto A^{1/3}$.
- $a_4(A-2Z)^2/A>0$: the [nuclear symmetry energy](../../../../../symmetry-energy.md), penalizing unequal neutron and proton populations. The [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) forces an excess species into higher single-particle states; strong-interaction effects also contribute.
- $\delta(A)/A^{1/2}$: [nuclear pairing](../../../../../nuclear-pairing.md). It lowers the mass for even-even [nuclei](../../../../../atomic-nucleus-split.md), raises it for odd-odd [nuclei](../../../../../atomic-nucleus-split.md), and vanishes for odd $A$ in the stated model.

The sign convention is significant: the quantity labeled $E_{\mathrm{bind}}$ in the supplied mass formula is added to the free-particle masses and is normally negative for a bound nucleus. It is the negative of the conventional positive [nuclear binding energy](../../../../../nuclear-binding-energy.md) $B$:

$$
\boxed{M=Zm_p+Zm_e+(A-Z)m_n+E_{\mathrm{bind}},\qquad B=-E_{\mathrm{bind}}>0.}
$$

The coefficients must therefore retain their printed signs; using positive $B$ while still adding it to the masses would be inconsistent.
