<h1 id="section-b/6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The qualitative explanation is [nuclear pairing](../../../../../nuclear-pairing.md): $^{40}\mathrm K$ is odd-odd ($Z=19,N=21$), while both $^{40}\mathrm{Ar}$ and $^{40}\mathrm{Ca}$ are even-even. At $A=40$, moving from the odd-odd to either even-even branch lowers the pairing contribution to the atomic [mass](../../../../../mass.md) by

$$
\frac{(+11.2)-(-11.2)}{\sqrt{40}}=3.542\,\mathrm{MeV}.
$$

Two neighboring even-even [nuclear isobars](../../../../../isobar-nuclide.md) can therefore both be lower in mass than the intervening odd-odd member. The two possible directions are

$$
{}^{40}_{19}\mathrm K\to{}^{40}_{18}\mathrm{Ar}
\quad\text{by electron capture or positron emission},\qquad
{}^{40}_{19}\mathrm K\to{}^{40}_{20}\mathrm{Ca}
\quad\text{by beta-minus decay}.
$$

However, the particular coefficients printed in this paper do **not** quantitatively reproduce both allowed directions. Let $D=m_p+m_e-m_n\simeq-0.7823\,\mathrm{MeV}$. Using every supplied term, rather than pairing alone, gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(40,19)-M(40,18)
&=D+\frac{0.71(19^2-18^2)}{40^{1/3}}
+\frac{23.3(2^2-4^2)}{40}+\frac{22.4}{\sqrt{40}}
\simeq+3.451\,\mathrm{MeV},\\
M(40,19)-M(40,20)
&=-D+\frac{0.71(19^2-20^2)}{40^{1/3}}
+\frac{23.3(2^2-0^2)}{40}+\frac{22.4}{\sqrt{40}}
\simeq-1.443\,\mathrm{MeV}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus this parametrization would forbid the calcium beta-minus branch. The physical atomic mass differences are instead approximately $+1.505\,\mathrm{MeV}$ to argon and $+1.311\,\mathrm{MeV}$ to calcium, so both physical directions are allowed.[Decay scheme and atomic Q values](../../../../../the-kdk-experiment-by-kdk-collaboration-2021.md)

The valid interpretation is therefore **pairing explains how both directions can be possible, but the supplied smooth mass formula is insufficient to establish both for this isotope**. The [semi-empirical mass formula](../../../../../semi-empirical-mass-formula.md) has MeV-scale residual errors and omits [nuclear shell closure](../../../../../nuclear-shell-closure.md) corrections; $^{40}\mathrm{Ca}$ has the especially stable $Z=N=20$ shell closures. One must use measured [masses](../../../../../mass.md) or a model with those corrections for a quantitative proof, rather than changing the printed numbers silently.
