↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The two processes are [electron capture](../../../../../electron-capture.md) and [beta-plus decay](../../../../../beta-plus-decay.md):

$$
\boxed{{}^{40}_{19}\mathrm K+e^-\to{}^{40}_{18}\mathrm{Ar}+\nu_e,\qquad
{}^{40}_{19}\mathrm K\to{}^{40}_{18}\mathrm{Ar}+e^++\nu_e.}
$$

For neutral atomic [masses](../../../../../mass.md), their available total [energies](../../../../../energy-split.md) obey

$$
Q_{\mathrm{EC}}=M_a(\mathrm K)-M_a(\mathrm{Ar}),\qquad
Q_{\beta^+}=M_a(\mathrm K)-M_a(\mathrm{Ar})-2m_e.
$$

Hence **electron capture has approximately $2m_ec^2=1.022\,\mathrm{MeV}$ more available energy**. Electron-hole energies and [nuclear recoil](../../../../../nuclear-recoil.md) give smaller corrections for capture from a particular shell. With the physical mass difference, $Q_{\mathrm{EC}}\simeq1.505\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and the positron kinetic-energy budget is only about $0.483\,\mathrm{MeV}$.

[Electron capture](../../../../../electron-capture.md) dominates the argon-producing decays. Energetic advantage alone is not a universal theorem about branching fractions: electron density at the nucleus and nuclear transition matrix elements and selection rules also matter. In this case capture can populate the approximately $1.461\,\mathrm{MeV}$ excited argon level, whereas positron emission cannot; this is an additional reason the physical capture branch is much larger.[Dominant capture branch to the 1460.8-keV argon level and rare positron branch](../../../../../the-kdk-experiment-by-kdk-collaboration-2021.md)
