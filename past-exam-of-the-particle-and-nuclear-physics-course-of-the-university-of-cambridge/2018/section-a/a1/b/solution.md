<h1 id="section-a/a1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For [gamma decay](../../../../../gamma-decay.md), the [nuclear gamma-ray multipole selection rules](../../../../../nuclear-gamma-ray-multipole-selection-rules.md) require the radiation's [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) $L$ to satisfy the triangle inequality

$$
|J_i-J_f|\leq L\leq J_i+J_f.
$$

Here $J_i=J_f=5/2$, so $0\leq L\leq5$. A real emitted [photon](../../../../../photon.md) has no monopole radiation mode, leaving $L=1,2,3,4,5$.

The two nuclear [quantum states](../../../../../quantum-state.md) have the same [parity](../../../../../parity.md). An electric multipole has parity factor $(-1)^L$, while a magnetic multipole has factor $(-1)^{L+1}$. Thus electric radiation requires even $L$ and magnetic radiation odd $L$:

$$
\boxed{M1,\quad E2,\quad M3,\quad E4,\quad M5.}
$$

In particular, an $E0$ nuclear transition cannot emit the single real [photon](../../../../../photon.md) requested here; processes such as [internal conversion](../../../../../internal-conversion.md) are different decay channels.

For usual nuclear [gamma ray](../../../../../gamma-ray.md) energies, $kR\ll1$, where $k=E_\gamma/(\hbar c)$ and $R$ is the nuclear size. Higher [multipole radiation](../../../../../multipole-radiation.md) orders bring extra powers of this small parameter, so **$M1$ and $E2$ are the likely important contributions**, with $M3$, $E4$ and $M5$ generally much smaller. Often $M1$ is dominant, but an allowed $M1$ [matrix element](../../../../../matrix-element.md) can be hindered and an $E2$ transition can be collectively enhanced. The [quantum numbers](../../../../../quantum-number.md) alone do not fix a numerical relative contribution or an unconditional ordering of the $M1$ and $E2$ rates.
