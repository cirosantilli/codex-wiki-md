# Transverse magnification

↑ **Parent:** [Magnification](magnification.md)

[Transverse magnification](transverse-magnification.md) is the ratio of corresponding signed lengths perpendicular to the [optical axis](optical-axis.md). For an ideal image of a small arrow of nonzero height,

$$
m=\frac{h_i}{h_o}.
$$

Use the same upward-positive height convention in both planes. Thus an upright $0.50\,\mathrm{cm}$ arrow imaged as $-0.25\,\mathrm{cm}$ has $m=-1/2$: inverted and half as tall. The sign describes orientation; [real image](real-image.md) versus [virtual image](virtual-image.md) describes ray intersections. They are separate properties, even when a particular [thin lens](thin-lens.md) setup correlates them.[§H: Magnification](mit-2-71-and-2-710-optics-spring-2014-by-nicholas-x-fang/lecture-notes-on-geometrical-optics.md)

For a [thin lens](thin-lens.md) with object coordinate $-u$ and image coordinate $v$, the undeviated centre [light ray](light-ray.md) is the [straight line](straight-line.md) through $(-u,h_o)$ and $(0,0)$, so its height at $x=v$ is $h_i=-h_ov/u$. This is the signed [similar triangles](similar-triangle.md) argument. Combining it with the [thin lens](thin-lens.md) equation gives

$$
m=-\frac vu=-\frac{f}{u-f}\qquad (u\ne f).
$$

For $f>0$, $u>2f$ gives $-1<m<0$; $u=2f$ gives $m=-1$; $f<u<2f$ gives $m<-1$; and $0<u<f$ gives $m>1$. At $u=f$, the image is conventionally “at infinity”: the divergent expressions for $v$ and $m$ describe the limiting behaviour, not a finite image of infinite height. Different object points produce parallel bundles in different directions, which is why [angular magnification](angular-magnification.md) remains meaningful.

The [real image](real-image.md) experiment tests $m$ in two independent ways: signed ratios of measured corresponding intervals, and $-v/u$ from measured longitudinal distances. As a further prediction, moving from $u=30\,\mathrm{cm}$ to $u=15\,\mathrm{cm}$ for $f=10\,\mathrm{cm}$ swaps $u,v$, changing $m=-1/2$ to $m=-2$. The two image sizes differ by a factor of four, while both images remain inverted. This reversal follows from the symmetric [thin lens](thin-lens.md) equation and connects to the [Bessel lens displacement method](bessel-lens-displacement-method.md).
