<h1 id="fermi-s-golden-rule">Fermi's golden rule</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Time-dependent perturbation theory](time-dependent-perturbation-theory.md)

For a weak time-independent perturbation switched on at zero time, first-order [time-dependent perturbation theory](time-dependent-perturbation-theory.md) gives a transition [probability](probability.md)

$$
P_{i\to f}(t)=\frac{|V_{fi}|^2}{\hbar^2}
\frac{4\sin^2[(\omega_f-\omega_i)t/2]}{(\omega_f-\omega_i)^2}.
$$

The frequency kernel has area $2\pi t$ and width of order $1/t$. Against a smoothly varying continuum of final states, it approaches $2\pi t\delta(\omega_f-\omega_i)$, giving the rate $2\pi|V_{fi}|^2\delta(\omega_f-\omega_i)/\hbar^2$. Using $E=\hbar\omega$ gives the energy form above. One integrates the [Dirac delta function](dirac-delta-function.md) against the appropriate [density of states](density-of-states.md); it is not an ordinary finite number for one isolated resonant level.

The time must be long enough to resolve the smooth continuum and short enough that the total transition [probability](probability.md) remains small. For continuum variation scale $\Delta\omega$ and total rate $\Gamma$, the usual window is $(\Delta\omega)^{-1}\ll t\ll\Gamma^{-1}$. A discrete finite spectrum also limits this approximation through its recurrences.
