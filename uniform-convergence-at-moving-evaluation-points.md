# Uniform convergence at moving evaluation points

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform convergence](uniform-convergence.md)

Suppose [continuous functions](continuous-function.md) $f_n$ converge by [uniform convergence](uniform-convergence.md) to $f$ on a [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) $X$, and $x_n\to x$ with all these inputs in $X$. The [uniform limit theorem](uniform-limit-theorem.md) makes $f$ a [continuous function](continuous-function.md), and the [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md) gives

$$
|f_n(x_n)-f(x)|\leq\|f_n-f\|_\infty+|f(x_n)-f(x)|\longrightarrow0.
$$

The first term handles the changing approximation and the second handles the changing input. For $f_n(x)=x^n$ on $[0,1]$, the inputs $x_n=2^{-1/n}$ instead give $x_n\to1$, $f_n(x_n)=1/2$, and $f(1)=1$. [Pointwise convergence](pointwise-convergence.md) cannot supply the first uniform bound, and this [pointwise limit](pointwise-limit.md) also lacks [continuity](continuous-function.md) at one.
