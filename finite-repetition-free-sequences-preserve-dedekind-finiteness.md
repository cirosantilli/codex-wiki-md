# Finite repetition-free sequences preserve Dedekind-finiteness

↑ **Parent:** [Dedekind-finite set](dedekind-finite-set.md)

If $X$ is a [Dedekind-finite set](dedekind-finite-set.md), its [set](set-split.md) of [finite repetition-free sequences](finite-repetition-free-sequence.md) is also Dedekind-finite. An injective [sequence](sequence.md) of distinct finite lists would, by [countable union of explicitly ordered finite lists without choice](countable-union-of-explicitly-ordered-finite-lists-without-choice.md), either produce an injection $\mathbb N\to X$ or use only finitely many entries. The latter possibility is impossible because a fixed finite pool supports only finitely many repetition-free lists. If $X$ is infinite, the one-entry lists also show that the resulting [set](set-split.md) is infinite.
