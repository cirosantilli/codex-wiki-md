# Riemannian divergence theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md)

On an oriented [Riemannian manifold](riemannian-manifold.md) with boundary, the [divergence of a Riemannian vector field](divergence-of-a-riemannian-vector-field.md) obeys the displayed integral identity for smooth [compactly supported](compact-support.md) [vector fields](vector-field.md) $X$, or for arbitrary smooth $X$ if the manifold is compact. Here $N$ is the outward unit [normal vector](normal-vector.md) and the boundary has the [outward-normal-first boundary orientation](outward-normal-first-boundary-orientation.md). Its induced [Riemannian volume form](riemannian-volume-form.md) is $j^*\iota_N\omega_g$. Splitting $X$ into its normal and tangential components gives $j^*\iota_X\omega_g=g(X,N)\omega_{\widetilde g}$, since a top form vanishes on $n$ boundary-tangent arguments. Apply the [Stokes theorem](stokes-theorem.md) to $d\iota_X\omega_g=(\operatorname{div}X)\omega_g$.

Without a support or decay condition, flux at infinity can invalidate the result even if the integrals converge. On $[0,\infty)$ with [Riemannian metric](riemannian-metric.md) $dx^2$, $X=(\arctan x)\partial_x$ has boundary flux zero but integral of divergence $\pi/2$.
