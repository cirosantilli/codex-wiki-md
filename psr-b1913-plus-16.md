<h1 id="psr-b1913-plus-16">PSR B1913+16</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Binary pulsar](binary-pulsar.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulse–Taylor_pulsar)

[Russell Alan Hulse](russell-alan-hulse.md) and [Joseph Hooton Taylor Jr.](joseph-hooton-taylor-jr.md) discovered this [binary pulsar](binary-pulsar.md) at Arecibo in 1974; their January 1975 paper reports a roughly $59\,\mathrm{ms}$ pulse [period](period-of-a-function.md) and $7.75\,\mathrm{h}$ [orbital period](orbital-period.md). Its unseen companion was not detected as a second [radio pulsar](pulsar.md). Discovery of the [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) preceded the later [binary-pulsar orbital decay](binary-pulsar-orbital-decay.md) test.[Discovery of a Pulsar in a Binary System, 15 January 1975, L51 opening and section II; L53 section IV](discovery-of-a-pulsar-in-a-binary-system-by-russell-alan-hulse-and-joseph-hooton-taylor-jr.md)

The 1993 [Nobel Prize in Physics](nobel-prize-in-physics-split.md) recognized the discovery.[1993 entry, joint award and discovery motivation](all-nobel-prizes-in-physics-by-nobel-prize-outreach.md)

By 1993, the system was interpreted as a pair of [neutron stars](neutron-star.md).[Section IV, p. 83, Figure 7](binary-pulsars-and-relativistic-gravity-by-joseph-hooton-taylor-jr.md)
