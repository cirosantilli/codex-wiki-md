# Inverse of a scalar plus a cross-product operator

↑ **Parent:** [Cross product](cross-product.md)

For a [unit vector](unit-vector.md) $\mathbf n$, real $\alpha\ne0$, and real $\beta$, the displayed [linear equation](linear-equation.md) has the unique [vector](vector.md) solution

$$
\mathbf x=\frac{\alpha\mathbf b-\beta\mathbf n\times\mathbf b+(\beta^2/\alpha)\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf b)}{\alpha^2+\beta^2}.
$$

To prove it, split [vectors](vector.md) into components parallel and [orthogonal](orthogonal-vectors.md) to $\mathbf n$. On the parallel line, the [linear map](linear-map.md) is multiplication by $\alpha$. On the perpendicular plane, the operator $N\mathbf v=\mathbf n\times\mathbf v$ obeys $N^2=-I$, by the [vector triple product](vector-triple-product.md). Therefore $(\alpha I-\beta N)(\alpha I+\beta N)=(\alpha^2+\beta^2)I$ on that plane. Combining its inverse with the parallel solution gives the formula. This avoids component calculations in equations with an axial [cross product](cross-product.md) term.
