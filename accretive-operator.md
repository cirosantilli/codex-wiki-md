# Accretive operator

↑ **Parent:** [Linear operator](linear-operator.md)

An operator on a complex [Hilbert space](hilbert-space-split.md) is accretive when the displayed inequality holds throughout its domain. Its negative is a [dissipative operator](dissipative-operator.md). For a bounded everywhere-defined operator, expansion gives $\|(I+T)x\|^2\geq\|x\|^2$. Its [adjoint operator](adjoint-operator.md) satisfies the same accretivity condition. Consequently $I+T$ has closed range and trivial kernel, and its range is dense because its adjoint has trivial kernel. Thus $I+T$ is onto and $\|(I+T)^{-1}\|\leq1$. Boundedness and the full domain are material here; for unbounded operators accretivity alone need not imply surjectivity.
