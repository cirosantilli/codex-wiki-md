<h1 id="1/solution-hardy-contradiction">Solution (Hardy contradiction)</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

In a [deterministic local hidden-variable model](../../../../deterministic-local-hidden-variable-model.md), write $C^+(R,\lambda)$ or $D^+(I,\lambda)$ without an argument for the remote splitter setting. That omission is the [parameter independence](../../../../parameter-independence.md) assumption: the local response for a fixed complete hidden state does not change when the other wing changes its apparatus. [Measurement independence](../../../../measurement-independence.md) also requires the hidden-state ensemble to be independent of the freely selected settings, allowing the same $\lambda$ assignments to be compared across configurations.

The zero probabilities derived above then constrain those assignments, apart from sets of hidden states of zero measure:

- Case $RR$ requires $C^+(R,\lambda)C^-(R,\lambda)=0$.
- Case $IR$ requires $D^+(I,\lambda)=1\Rightarrow C^-(R,\lambda)=1$.
- Case $RI$ requires $D^-(I,\lambda)=1\Rightarrow C^+(R,\lambda)=1$.

For a trial with $D^+(I,\lambda)D^-(I,\lambda)=1$, the last two implications [force](../../../../force-split.md) $C^+(R,\lambda)=C^-(R,\lambda)=1$, contradicting the first condition. Thus these deterministic local assignments predict zero [probability](../../../../probability.md) for the double-$D$ event, whereas quantum theory predicts $1/16$. This is [Hardy's paradox](../../../../hardy-s-paradox.md), an inequality-free ideal contradiction. With imperfect apparatus the zero-event conditions become small nonzero rates and require a statistical inequality rather than declaring a contradiction from one imperfect detection.[1992 abstract: a two-particle proof of Bell's theorem without inequalities](../../../../quantum-mechanics-local-realistic-theories-and-lorentz-invariant-realistic-theories-by-lucien-hardy.md)

The logical contradiction combines counterfactual **hidden-variable assignments**; it does not assign simultaneously observed path properties to a [quantum state](../../../../quantum-state.md). Nor does it allow a message to be sent by changing a distant splitter: the observable local marginal obeys [quantum no-signalling](../../../../quantum-no-signalling.md). For example, fixing the plus-side splitter to $I$ gives $P(D^+)=1/8$ for either minus-side setting in the results above.
