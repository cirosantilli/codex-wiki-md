# Emission-limited thermionic current

↑ **Parent:** [Thermionic emission](thermionic-emission-split.md)

An [emission-limited thermionic current](emission-limited-thermionic-current.md) is set by the rate at which the heated surface supplies [electrons](electron.md), with nearly complete collection. At fixed [temperature](temperature-split.md), increasing the attracting [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) then produces an approximate collection plateau. That plateau is the useful regime for fitting the [Richardson-Dushman law](richardson-dushman-law.md), provided the [electric field](electric-field-split.md) has not significantly lowered the surface barrier.

The emitting surface still matters in this regime. [Adsorption](adsorption.md) changes the [work function](work-function.md); different crystal faces can have different barriers. Heating may desorb contaminants or change a coating, so repeated measurements at the same [temperature](temperature-split.md) need not agree unless the surface preparation is controlled. For fixed $A,T$, a barrier change $\Delta W$ multiplies $J$ by $e^{-\Delta W/(k_BT)}$. In the chosen model at $1000\,\mathrm K$, raising the barrier by $0.10\,\mathrm{eV}$ reduces $J$ to about 0.313 of its previous value.
