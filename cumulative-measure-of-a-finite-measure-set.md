# Cumulative measure of a finite-measure set

↑ **Parent:** [Divisibility of Lebesgue measure](divisibility-of-lebesgue-measure.md)

For a [Lebesgue measurable set](lebesgue-measurable-set.md) $E\subseteq\mathbb R$ of finite [measure](measure-split.md), the displayed [function](function-split.md) satisfies $|H_E(t)-H_E(s)|\leq|t-s|$. Indeed, for $s<t$, [finite additivity](finite-additivity-of-a-set-function.md) and [monotonicity of a measure](monotonicity-of-a-measure.md) give $H_E(t)-H_E(s)=m(E\cap(s,t])\leq t-s$. Thus $H_E$ is a nondecreasing [Lipschitz function](lipschitz-continuity.md) and is [uniformly continuous](uniform-continuity.md) even if $E$ is unbounded.

[Continuity from above of a measure](continuity-from-above-of-a-measure.md) gives $H_E(t)\to0$ as $t\to-\infty$, while [continuity from below of a measure](continuity-from-below-of-a-measure.md) gives $H_E(t)\to m(E)$ as $t\to\infty$. Every strictly intermediate value is attained by the [intermediate value theorem](intermediate-value-theorem.md). The corresponding half-line cut of $E$ realizes any prescribed [measure](measure-split.md) between zero and $m(E)$; the endpoints are realized by the [empty set](empty-set.md) and $E$ itself.
