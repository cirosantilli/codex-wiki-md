# Distance extrema on a real quadratic surface

↑ **Parent:** [Quadric surface](quadric-surface.md)

For a real [symmetric matrix](symmetric-matrix.md) $A$, every stationary squared distance $x^Tx$ on $x^TAx=1$ occurs in a positive-[eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) [eigenspace](eigenspace.md). The [Lagrange multiplier](lagrange-multiplier.md) equation is $x=\Lambda Ax$; multiplying by $x^T$ gives $\Lambda=|x|^2>0$, hence $Ax=|x|^{-2}x$. If $A$ is a [positive-definite matrix](positive-definite-matrix.md), the nearest and farthest squared distances are $1/\lambda_{\max}$ and $1/\lambda_{\min}$. With a repeated [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md), the stationary set may contain an entire sphere within its [eigenspace](eigenspace.md). For an indefinite or singular form the surface may be unbounded, and a stationary point need not be a global extremum.
