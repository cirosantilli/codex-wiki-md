# Local diffeomorphism

↑ **Parent:** [Inverse function theorem](inverse-function-theorem.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_diffeomorphism)

A local diffeomorphism is a [differentiable map](differentiable-map.md) that restricts near every point to a [diffeomorphism](diffeomorphism.md) onto an [open subset](open-set.md) of its target. The local [inverse functions](inverse-function.md) need not combine into a single global [inverse function](inverse-function.md): different parts of the [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) can map onto the same output region.

For [continuously differentiable](continuously-differentiable-function.md) maps between [open subsets](open-set.md) of $\mathbb R^n$, being a [local diffeomorphism](local-diffeomorphism.md) is equivalent to having an invertible [derivative](derivative.md) at every point. The [inverse function theorem](inverse-function-theorem.md) proves one direction. For the other, the [chain rule](chain-rule.md) for each local [inverse function](inverse-function.md) gives two inverse [linear maps](linear-map.md). The periodic angle example under the [inverse function theorem](inverse-function-theorem.md) has this property everywhere, even though it has infinitely many inputs for every point of the punctured plane. The [noninjective map with constant Jacobian determinant](noninjective-map-with-constant-jacobian-determinant.md) gives another useful distinction: even a constant nonzero [Jacobian determinant](jacobian-determinant.md) need not give global [injectivity](injective-function.md).

Every [local diffeomorphism](local-diffeomorphism.md) is an [open map](open-map.md). To see this, take an [open subset](open-set.md) of the source and intersect it with the neighbourhoods where the map is a [diffeomorphism](diffeomorphism.md). Each local image is [open](open-set.md), and their [union](set-union.md) is the full image. Thus nearby outputs remain available locally, despite the possible global ambiguity.
