# Stellar obliquity

↑ **Parent:** [Planetary migration](planetary-migration.md)

The stellar obliquity is the angle between a star's spin and a planet's orbital angular momentum. The [Rossiter-McLaughlin effect](rossiter-mclaughlin-effect.md) constrains the angle $\lambda$ between the sky projections of those two axes, rather than a component of $\psi$. If the orbital and stellar-spin inclinations to the line of sight are $i_{\rm orb}$ and $i_*$, then $\cos\psi=\cos i_*\cos i_{\rm orb}+\sin i_*\sin i_{\rm orb}\cos\lambda$; $\lambda$ alone generally does not determine $\psi$. Large misalignments constrain formation and [planetary migration](planetary-migration.md), but can also reflect a tilted birth disc.
