<h3 id="a/1">1</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/1/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/1/a)

Start with the [propositional variables](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-variable) as atomic symbols and close under the two formation operations

$$
p_i\in\operatorname{Form}(L_0),\qquad
\psi\in\operatorname{Form}(L_0)\Longrightarrow\neg\psi\in\operatorname{Form}(L_0),\qquad
\psi,\chi\in\operatorname{Form}(L_0)\Longrightarrow(\psi\to\chi)\in\operatorname{Form}(L_0).
$$

The [set](../set.md) of [propositional formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-formula) is the **smallest** [set](../set.md) satisfying these conditions: every [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) has a finite construction from [logical variables](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) by [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) and [logical implication](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-implication), and no other strings qualify. In particular, parentheses belong to the binary [logical implication](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-implication) [formation rule](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#formation-rule); they cannot be added or removed arbitrarily. Each symbol $p_i$ is one token, regardless of how its index is printed.

<h4 id="a/1/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/1/b)

Use [structural induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#structural-induction) on the formation of a [propositional formula](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-formula). A [propositional variable](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-variable) has no parentheses. Applying [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) to a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) adds no parentheses, so preserves [equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics) of the two counts. If $\psi$ and $\chi$ each have equally many opening and closing parentheses, the formation of $(\psi\to\chi)$ adds one of each to their combined counts. Thus every [propositional formula](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-formula) satisfies

$$
\boxed{\#\text{ opening parentheses}=\#\text{ closing parentheses}.}
$$

This is a necessary condition for being a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula); it is not sufficient, since the [logical connectives](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-connective) and the placement of symbols must also follow the [formation rules](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#formation-rule).

<h5 id="a/1/c/c">c</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/1/c)

<h6 id="a/1/c/c/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/1/c/c)

**A [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula).** Both $p_1$ and $p_2$ are [propositional variables](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-variable), so $(p_1\to p_2)$ and $(p_2\to p_1)$ are [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula). Applying the binary [logical implication](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-implication) [formation rule](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#formation-rule) to this [ordered pair](../set.md#ordered-pair) of [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) gives the displayed whole expression. This supplies a construction of its [formula syntax tree](../programming-language.md#syntax-tree-of-a-formula) rather than merely checking that its parentheses balance.

<h4 id="a/1/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#a/1/d)

The [unique readability theorem](../programming-language.md#unique-readability-theorem) says that every [propositional formula](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-formula) has **exactly one** of the following forms: a unique [propositional variable](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-variable); $\neg\psi$ for a unique [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\psi$; or $(\psi\to\chi)$ for a unique [ordered pair](../set.md#ordered-pair) of [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $(\psi,\chi)$. These three alternatives are disjoint. Consequently each [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) has a unique [formula syntax tree](../programming-language.md#syntax-tree-of-a-formula) and a uniquely determined principal [logical connective](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-connective) whenever it is not a [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable).

<h4 id="a/1/e">e</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/e/i">i</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#a/1/e)

<h6 id="a/1/e/i/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a/1/e/i)

A [propositional variable](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-variable) $p_i$ is a single symbol of the alphabet. Its only nonempty substring is that symbol itself. Every [propositional formula](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-formula) is nonempty, so the only substring that is a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) is $p_i$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Sub}(p_i)=\{p_i\}.}
$$

The subscript is part of the name of an atomic token; it does not introduce additional symbol positions inside the [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable).

<h5 id="a/1/e/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#a/1/e)

<h6 id="a/1/e/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a/1/e/ii)

A useful consequence of the [formation rules](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#formation-rule) is that [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) form a prefix-free [set](../set.md) of strings. To see this, describe a deterministic [parser](../programming-language.md#parser) that reads one [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) from its starting position. On a [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) it consumes that one symbol. On a [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) symbol it recursively reads one following [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula). On an opening parenthesis it recursively reads a left [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula), requires a [logical implication](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-implication) symbol, recursively reads a right [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula), and requires the closing parenthesis. Induction on formation proves that, if a valid [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) begins at the current position, this procedure consumes exactly that [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) and stops at its end.

Suppose now that both $\alpha$ and $\phi$ are [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) and $\phi=\alpha\epsilon$. The same [parser](../programming-language.md#parser), started at their common first symbol, must stop both at the end of $\alpha$ and at the end of $\phi$. Its deterministic stopping position cannot differ, so $\epsilon$ is empty. Hence

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\phi.}
$$

This argument applies to symbol strings; it does not use [truth values](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value) or [logical equivalence](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equivalence).

<h5 id="a/1/e/iii">iii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#a/1/e)

<h6 id="a/1/e/iii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#a/1/e/iii)

First note that a substring which is itself a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) must coincide with a subtree of the [formula syntax tree](../programming-language.md#syntax-tree-of-a-formula) of the surrounding [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula). Indeed, a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) can begin only at a [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable), a [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) symbol or an opening parenthesis. Every such position in a valid [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) is the start of one of its parsed subtrees. If $\beta$ is the [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) at that subtree and $\alpha$ is another [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) starting at the same position, one is an initial segment of the other; [prefix-freeness of fully parenthesized formulas](../programming-language.md#prefix-freeness-of-fully-parenthesized-formulas) from the preceding part forces $\alpha=\beta$.

Now consider a final [subformula](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula). For a [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable), it can only be the whole [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula). For a binary [logical implication](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-implication) $(\psi\to\chi)$, every proper subtree is contained in $\psi$ or $\chi$ and finishes before the final closing parenthesis of the whole expression. It therefore cannot be a final substring of the whole. The only remaining possible outer formation is [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation). Thus

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\phi\quad\text{or}\quad\phi=\neg\psi\text{ for some formula }\psi.}
$$

The second alternative is a necessary possibility, not a claim that every negated [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) has every one of its [subformulas](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula) as a suffix.

<h5 id="a/1/e/iv">iv</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#a/1/e)

<h6 id="a/1/e/iv/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#a/1/e/iv)

The whole [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\neg\psi$ is a [subformula](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula) of itself, and any [subformula](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula) of $\psi$ remains a contiguous substring after placing a [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) symbol in front of $\psi$.

Conversely, a [subformula occurrence](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula-occurrence) starting at the first symbol of $\neg\psi$ is an initial [subformula](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula), so equals the whole [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) by [prefix-freeness of fully parenthesized formulas](../programming-language.md#prefix-freeness-of-fully-parenthesized-formulas). An occurrence starting later lies wholly inside the following string $\psi$, and is therefore a [subformula](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula) of $\psi$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Sub}(\neg\psi)=\{\neg\psi\}\cup\operatorname{Sub}(\psi).}
$$

Here $\operatorname{Sub}$ records distinct [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula); multiple occurrences of the same [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) do not create different elements of this [set](../set.md).

<h5 id="a/1/e/v">v</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#a/1/e)

<h6 id="a/1/e/v/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#a/1/e/v)

A [subformula](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula) beginning at the first opening parenthesis equals the whole [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) by [prefix-freeness of fully parenthesized formulas](../programming-language.md#prefix-freeness-of-fully-parenthesized-formulas). Every other [subformula](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula) is the [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) of a proper syntax subtree, by the [parsing](../programming-language.md#parsing) argument in part (iii). The two children of the root are precisely $\psi$ and $\chi$, so such a subtree lies entirely inside one of those children. In particular no [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) substring can begin in $\psi$ and cross the principal [logical implication](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-implication) symbol into $\chi$.

Conversely, each [subformula](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula) of either child is visibly a substring of the whole expression, as is the whole expression itself. Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Sub}((\psi\to\chi))=\{(\psi\to\chi)\}\cup\operatorname{Sub}(\psi)\cup\operatorname{Sub}(\chi).}
$$

Together these cases identify the substring definition of a [subformula](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula) with its usual syntax-tree definition.

<h4 id="a/1/f">f</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/f/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#a/1/f)

Fix the particular occurrence of $\alpha$ that is being replaced. The [subformula](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula) analysis above makes it a node of the [formula syntax tree](../programming-language.md#syntax-tree-of-a-formula) of $\phi$. Proceed by [structural induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#structural-induction) on the surrounding [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula).

If this node is the root, the [subformula replacement](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula-replacement) gives $\beta$, already a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula). Otherwise, if $\phi=\neg\psi$, the chosen occurrence lies inside $\psi$. The induction hypothesis gives a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\psi'$ after the [subformula replacement](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula-replacement) there, and the whole result is the [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\neg\psi'$. If $\phi=(\psi\to\chi)$, a proper chosen occurrence lies in exactly one of the two children. Replacing inside that child gives either $(\psi'\to\chi)$ or $(\psi\to\chi')$, each a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) by the [formation rule](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#formation-rule). A [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) has no proper [subformula](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula), so there is no additional base case.

Therefore **replacing the designated [subformula occurrence](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula-occurrence) by any [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) preserves well-formedness**. The argument concerns one chosen occurrence; it does not require simultaneous [subformula replacement](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula-replacement) of every occurrence of the same string.

<h4 id="a/1/g">g</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/g/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [G](#a/1/g)

Fix any [Boolean valuation](../mathematical-logic.md#boolean-valuation) $v$. [Logical equivalence](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equivalence) of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ means $v(\alpha)=v(\beta)$ for this [Boolean valuation](../mathematical-logic.md#boolean-valuation) and every other one. Follow the unique path from the replaced node to the root of the [formula syntax tree](../programming-language.md#syntax-tree-of-a-formula). At a [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) node, equal input [truth values](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value) have equal [negations](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation). At a [logical implication](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-implication) node, the unaffected child has the same [truth value](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value) before and after [subformula replacement](../mathematical-logic.md#subformula-replacement), while the affected child has the same [truth value](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value) by induction; the resulting [logical implications](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-implication) therefore agree as well.

Induction along that finite path yields $v(\phi)=v(\phi')$. Since $v$ was arbitrary,

$$
\boxed{\phi\equiv\phi'.}
$$

This is the [replacement theorem for logical equivalence](../mathematical-logic.md#replacement-theorem-for-logical-equivalence): the semantics of each [logical connective](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-connective) depends only on the [truth values](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value) of its immediate inputs.

<h3 id="a/2">2</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/2/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/2/a)

A collection of [logical connectives](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-connective) is functionally complete, or adequate, if every [Boolean function](../analysis-of-boolean-functions.md#boolean-function) of finitely many [propositional variables](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-variable) is represented by a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) using only those [logical connectives](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-connective). More explicitly, for every $n\geq1$ and every $F:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$ there is a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\theta(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ whose [truth value](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value) under each [Boolean valuation](../mathematical-logic.md#boolean-valuation) is $F(v(p_1),\ldots,v(p_n))$. [Constant functions](../function.md#constant-function) with Boolean values may use an otherwise irrelevant [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable), for example $p\to p$ for truth and $\neg(p\to p)$ for falsity.

<h4 id="a/2/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/2/b)

Translate [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) recursively, leaving [propositional variables](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-variable) unchanged and translating [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) by applying [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) to the translation. For the other two [logical connectives](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-connective) [set](../set.md)

$$
T(\psi\lor\chi)=(\neg T(\psi)\to T(\chi)),\qquad
T(\psi\land\chi)=\neg(T(\psi)\to\neg T(\chi)).
$$

The first [logical implication](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-implication) is false exactly when both original inputs are false; the second displayed [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) is true exactly when both inputs are true. Hence each clause preserves [truth values](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value). [Structural induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#structural-induction) gives $v(T(\theta))=v(\theta)$ for every [Boolean valuation](../mathematical-logic.md#boolean-valuation) and every [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\theta$ in the larger language.

By the assumed [functional completeness](../analysis-of-boolean-functions.md#functional-completeness) of [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation), [logical conjunction](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-conjunction) and [logical disjunction](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-disjunction), any finite [Boolean function](../analysis-of-boolean-functions.md#boolean-function) has such a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\theta$. Its translation $T(\theta)$ uses only [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) and [logical implication](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-implication) and represents the same [function](../function.md). **Thus $\{\neg,\to\}$ is adequate.**

<h4 id="a/2/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/2/c)

Under any [Boolean valuation](../mathematical-logic.md#boolean-valuation) there are two possibilities. If $\neg(\psi\to\chi)$ is false, the outer [logical implication](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-implication) is true by [vacuous truth](../mathematical-logic.md#vacuous-truth). If it is true, then $\psi\to\chi$ is false, which forces $\psi$ true and $\chi$ false. In that case $\neg\chi$ is true, so the outer [logical implication](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-implication) is again true. These possibilities exhaust the [truth values](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value) of its antecedent; therefore

$$
\boxed{\models\neg(\psi\to\chi)\to\neg\chi.}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{rll}
1.&\chi\to(\psi\to\chi)&\text{A1},\\
2.&(\chi\to(\psi\to\chi))\to(\neg(\psi\to\chi)\to\neg\chi)&\text{A3},\\
3.&\neg(\psi\to\chi)\to\neg\chi&\text{modus ponens on 1,2}.
\end{array}
$$

This is a direct [logical derivation](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-derivation) in the displayed [Hilbert proof system](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-proof-system). An $L$-structure consists of a nonempty domain $A$, an element $c^{\mathcal A}\in A$ for each [constant symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol), a [function](../function.md) $f^{\mathcal A}:A^n\to A$ for each $n$-ary [function symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#function-symbol), and a relation $R^{\mathcal A}\subseteq A^n$ for each $n$-ary [predicate symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#predicate-symbol). When [equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics) is part of the logic it is interpreted as actual identity on $A$.

An [assignment in a first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#assignment-in-a-first-order-structure) is a [function](../function.md) $s$ from [logical variables](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) to $A$. It induces a [term valuation](../mathematical-logic.md#term-valuation) of [first-order terms](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term) recursively:

$$
x^{\mathcal A}[s]=s(x),\qquad c^{\mathcal A}[s]=c^{\mathcal A},\qquad
f(t_1,\ldots,t_n)^{\mathcal A}[s]=f^{\mathcal A}(t_1^{\mathcal A}[s],\ldots,t_n^{\mathcal A}[s]).
$$

For an [atomic formula](../mathematical-logic.md#atomic-formula), substitute these [first-order term](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term) values into the interpreted relation, or compare them for [equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics). Extend [satisfaction for a set structure](../mathematical-logic.md#satisfaction-for-a-set-structure) through the [truth tables](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-table) of the [logical connectives](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-connective), and through the [quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) clauses

$$
\mathcal A,s\models\forall x\theta\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (\forall a\in A)\ \mathcal A,s[x\mapsto a]\models\theta,
$$



$$
\mathcal A,s\models\exists x\theta\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (\exists a\in A)\ \mathcal A,s[x\mapsto a]\models\theta.
$$

Thus the [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) and [assignment in a first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#assignment-in-a-first-order-structure) together determine [truth values](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value) for all [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula). A [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) has no [free variables](../mathematical-logic.md#free-variable), so its truth does not depend on the [assignment in a first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#assignment-in-a-first-order-structure).

**No [finite axiomatization](../mathematical-logic.md#finite-axiomatization) exists.** Write $\Gamma$ for the [axioms](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) just constructed and suppose a [finite set](../set.md#finite-set) $\Delta$ of [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) had exactly the same [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory). By the [compactness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#compactness-theorem) there is a finite [subset](../set.md#subset) $\Gamma_0\subseteq\Gamma$ with $\Gamma_0\models\delta$: otherwise $\Gamma\cup\{\neg\delta\}$ would be finitely satisfiable and hence satisfiable, a contradiction.

Only finitely many of the integers $n$ in the torsion-free scheme occur in $\Gamma_0$. Choose a [prime number](../prime-number.md) $p$ larger than all of them. The [cyclic group](../group.md#cyclic-group) $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$ satisfies all the [group axioms](../group.md#group-axioms) and each of these finitely many conditions from the [torsion-free abelian group axiom scheme](../group.md#torsion-free-abelian-group-axiom-scheme): [multiplication](../arithmetic.md#multiplication) by an [integer](../integer.md) $1\le n<p$ is invertible modulo $p$, so $nx=0$ implies $x=0$. It consequently satisfies $\Gamma_0$, then $\delta$, and therefore $\Delta$.

But its nonzero elements have [finite order](../group-theory.md#finite-order) $p$, so it is not a [torsion-free group](../group.md#torsion-free-group), contradicting the assumed description of the [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $\Delta$. This establishes the [nonfinite axiomatizability of torsion-free abelian groups](../group.md#nonfinite-axiomatizability-of-torsion-free-abelian-groups), ruling out every [finite set](../set.md#finite-set) of defining [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence), not just finite [subsets](../set.md#subset) of the particular scheme in part (c).

For a standard classical propositional calculus, strong completeness states

$$
\boxed{\Gamma\models\phi\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Gamma\vdash\phi.}
$$

Together with [soundness](../mathematical-logic.md#soundness), this identifies semantic consequence with [formal derivability](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-derivability). One proves the contrapositive by the [maximal consistent set in propositional logic](../mathematical-logic.md#maximal-consistent-set-in-propositional-logic) construction. If $\Gamma\nvdash\phi$, the [set](../set.md) $\Gamma\cup\{\neg\phi\}$ is [propositionally consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-consistency), by the [deduction theorem for propositional logic](../mathematical-logic.md#deduction-theorem-for-propositional-logic) and classical propositional reasoning. Extend it to a [maximal consistent set in propositional logic](../mathematical-logic.md#maximal-consistent-set-in-propositional-logic) $M$, deciding each [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) in turn. At each stage at least one selection of $\theta$ or $\neg\theta$ preserves [propositional consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-consistency); a contradiction in the [union](../set.md#set-union) would use only finitely many assumptions and hence already occur at a stage.

Define a [Boolean valuation](../mathematical-logic.md#boolean-valuation) by $v(p_i)=1$ exactly when $p_i\in M$. [Maximal consistency in propositional logic](../mathematical-logic.md#maximal-consistency-in-propositional-logic) gives $\neg\theta\in M$ exactly when $\theta\notin M$, and $(\theta\to\eta)\in M$ exactly when either $\theta\notin M$ or $\eta\in M$. [Structural induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#structural-induction) on [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) therefore proves the [maximal consistent set truth lemma](../mathematical-logic.md#maximal-consistent-set-truth-lemma) $v(\theta)=1\Longleftrightarrow\theta\in M$. Thus $v$ satisfies $\Gamma$ but falsifies $\phi$, contradicting [semantic entailment](../mathematical-logic.md#semantic-entailment). The countable language here permits an enumeration of [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula); the corresponding general-language argument uses a [well-ordering](../set.md#well-order) or a principle of [maximality in a partially ordered set](../set.md#maximality-in-a-partially-ordered-set).

↑ **Parent:** [4](#a/4)

<h5 id="a/4/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/4/b)

[Gödel's completeness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-s-completeness-theorem) for [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) says that, in a standard sound first-order [Hilbert calculus](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-proof-system),

$$
\boxed{T\models\sigma\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T\vdash\sigma.}
$$

Equivalently every [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) [set](../set.md) of [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) has a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory): apply this existence statement to $T\cup\{\neg\sigma\}$ for the consequence formulation.

For the [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) existence [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) use a [Henkin construction](../mathematical-logic.md#henkin-construction). Enlarge the language by fresh [constant symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol) and adjoin [Henkin witness axioms](../mathematical-logic.md#henkin-witness-axiom)

$$
\exists x\theta(x)\to\theta(c_\theta).
$$

Freshness of $c_\theta$ preserves [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency). In detail, if a contradiction followed after adjoining one witness [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom), the [deduction theorem for first-order logic](../mathematical-logic.md#deduction-theorem-for-first-order-logic) would yield its [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation), from which one obtains both $\exists x\theta(x)$ and $\neg\theta(c_\theta)$ over the old theory. Since the new [constant symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol) does not occur in that theory or in $\theta$, replace it by a fresh [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) and generalize to derive $\forall x\neg\theta(x)$, a contradiction. Finitely many witness additions preserve [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) successively, and [unions](../set.md#set-union) preserve [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) because [formal proofs](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) are finite. Iterate the enlargement so that [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) involving newly introduced [constant symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol) also receive witnesses. Include at least one [constant symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol) to guarantee [closed terms](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term).

Extend the resulting [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) [witnessed first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#henkin-theory) to a [maximal consistent set of first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#maximal-consistent-set-of-first-order-sentences) $H$. In the equality-free case make the domain the [set](../set.md) of [closed terms](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term). Interpret [constant symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol) and [function symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#function-symbol) by their formal [first-order term](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term) constructions; interpret an [atomic formula](../mathematical-logic.md#atomic-formula) by

$$
R(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\text{ holds in the term model}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\in H.
$$

The [truth lemma for a Henkin term model](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-lemma-for-a-henkin-term-model) follows by induction on [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) with [closed terms](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term) substituted for their [free variables](../mathematical-logic.md#free-variable). The [logical connective](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-connective) steps use [maximal consistency of a first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#maximal-consistency-of-a-first-order-theory). In the existential step, membership of $\exists x\theta$ supplies a [witness constant](../mathematical-logic.md#witness-constant) and hence a [first-order term](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term) satisfying $\theta$; conversely a satisfying [closed term](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term) gives the [existential sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-sentence) by an inference of the calculus. The [term model](../mathematical-logic.md#term-model) consequently satisfies $H$ and the original theory. [Equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics) is absent here, so distinct [closed terms](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term) may remain distinct domain elements without further identifications.

In the presence of [equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics), two syntactically different [closed terms](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term) can be asserted equal by the [Henkin theory](../mathematical-logic.md#henkin-theory), so the raw [first-order term](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term) domain from part (b) must be replaced by a [quotient set](../set-theory.md#quotient-set). Define

$$
t\sim u\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (t=u)\in H.
$$

The [equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics) [axioms](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) give reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity, so this is an [equivalence relation](../set-theory.md#equivalence-relation). Their substitution consequences make it a [congruence relation on a first-order structure](../set-theory.md#congruence-relation-on-a-first-order-structure): replacing an input by an equivalent [first-order term](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term) preserves equivalence of [function](../function.md) values and preserves membership of [atomic formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#atomic-formula) in $H$.

Take domain $\{[t]:t\text{ is a closed term}\}$. Define a [function symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#function-symbol) by $f([t_1],\ldots,[t_n])=[f(t_1,\ldots,t_n)]$, and relations by the same membership test as before using representatives. The [congruence relation on a first-order structure](../set-theory.md#congruence-relation-on-a-first-order-structure) makes these definitions independent of representatives. Actual identity of [equivalence classes](../set-theory.md#equivalence-class) now agrees with the formal [equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics) [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) in $H$:

$$
\boxed{[t]=[u]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(t=u)\in H.}
$$

This supplies the [equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics) case of the [truth lemma for a Henkin term model](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-lemma-for-a-henkin-term-model); the [logical connective](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-connective) and [quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) cases then proceed exactly as in the [Henkin construction](../mathematical-logic.md#henkin-construction). Thus the [term model](../mathematical-logic.md#term-model) built from the [quotient set](../set-theory.md#quotient-set) is a genuine [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) with [equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics) interpreted as identity.

For a countable first-order language, the [Henkin construction](../mathematical-logic.md#henkin-construction) introduces only countably many [constant symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol): each round needs at most one for each [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula), there are countably many finite strings in a countable alphabet, and countably many rounds suffice. There are therefore at most countably many [closed terms](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term). The [term model](../mathematical-logic.md#term-model), and also its [quotient set](../set-theory.md#quotient-set) by [equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics), has at most countably many elements.

It follows that **every satisfiable theory in a countable first-order language has an at most countable [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory)**. If the theory has no finite [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory), the constructed [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) is countably infinite. More generally the same counting argument gives a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) at most $\max(|L|,\aleph_0)$ for any satisfiable theory in language $L$.

For the elementary-substructure version of the downward [Löwenheim-Skolem theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#lowenheim-skolem-theorem), begin with a [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) $M$ and a [set](../set.md) $S$ of designated elements. Close $S$, the named [constant symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol) and one initial element under the language's [functions](../function.md) and under chosen witnesses in $M$ for [existential formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-formula) with parameters already chosen. Iterate countably often. The resulting [set](../set.md) has [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) at most $\max(|L|,|S|,\aleph_0)$, and its induced substructure $N$ is elementary: induction on [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) proves the truth equivalence, with the existential case supplied by the chosen witnesses. This is the same witness mechanism as the Henkin [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof), now keeping witnesses inside a given [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory).

Let $T=\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb C)$ in the finite language of rings: it is the [set](../set.md) of all [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) true in the [field](../algebra.md#field) of [complex numbers](../complex-analysis.md#complex-number). It is satisfiable, with [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) $\mathbb C$, so the countable-language [Löwenheim-Skolem theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#lowenheim-skolem-theorem) gives an at most countable [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) $K$ of $T$.

All [field axioms](../algebra.md#field-axioms) belong to $T$, hence $K$ is a [field](../algebra.md#field). For every positive [integer](../integer.md) $n$, the [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) saying that there are at least $n$ distinct elements is also true in $\mathbb C$ and belongs to $T$. Thus $K$ cannot be finite, and is countably infinite.

Finally, for each [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) $\sigma$, either $\sigma\in T$ or $\neg\sigma\in T$. Since $K$ satisfies all of $T$, it satisfies $\sigma$ exactly when $\mathbb C$ does. Therefore

$$
\boxed{|K|=\aleph_0,\qquad K\equiv\mathbb C.}
$$

[Elementary equivalence](../model-theory.md#elementary-equivalence) concerns first-order [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) and does not imply an [isomorphism](../algebra.md#isomorphism); the two [fields](../algebra.md#field) have different [cardinalities](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality). The stronger downward form may even choose $K$ as a countable elementary subfield of $\mathbb C$.

First give the recursive definitions, keeping the right argument as the recursion [variable](../mathematics.md#variable-mathematics). For [ordinals](../ordinal.md) $\alpha,\beta$ and nonzero [limit ordinals](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal) $\lambda$,

$$
\alpha+0=\alpha,\qquad \alpha+(\beta+1)=(\alpha+\beta)+1,\qquad
\alpha+\lambda=\sup_{\xi<\lambda}(\alpha+\xi),
$$



$$
\alpha\cdot0=0,\qquad \alpha\cdot(\beta+1)=\alpha\beta+\alpha,\qquad
\alpha\lambda=\sup_{\xi<\lambda}\alpha\xi.
$$

Here $\xi+1$ is the [successor ordinal](../ordinal.md#successor-ordinal), and the [supremum](../real-analysis.md#supremum) of a [set](../set.md) of [ordinals](../ordinal.md) is its [union](../set.md#set-union). These are [ordinal arithmetic](../ordinal.md#ordinal-arithmetic), defined by [transfinite recursion](../ordinal.md#transfinite-recursion), and differ from [cardinal sum](../cardinal-number.md#cardinal-sum) and [ordinal multiplication](../ordinal.md#ordinal-multiplication).

**[Associativity](../algebraic-operation.md#associative-property) of [ordinal addition](../ordinal.md#ordinal-addition) is true.** Fix $\alpha,\beta$ and use [transfinite induction](../ordinal.md#transfinite-induction) on $\gamma$. The case $\gamma=0$ is immediate. In the successor step the recursive definitions and the induction hypothesis give

$$
(\alpha+\beta)+(\gamma+1)=((\alpha+\beta)+\gamma)+1
=(\alpha+(\beta+\gamma))+1=\alpha+(\beta+(\gamma+1)).
$$

For a nonzero [limit ordinal](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal) $\lambda$, the [ordinals](../ordinal.md) $\beta+\xi$ for $\xi<\lambda$ form an increasing [cofinal subset](../cardinal-number.md#cofinal-subset) of the [limit ordinal](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal) $\beta+\lambda$. The limit clause for [ordinal addition](../ordinal.md#ordinal-addition), restricted to this [cofinal subset](../cardinal-number.md#cofinal-subset), gives

$$
(\alpha+\beta)+\lambda
=\sup_{\xi<\lambda}((\alpha+\beta)+\xi)
=\sup_{\xi<\lambda}(\alpha+(\beta+\xi))
=\alpha+(\beta+\lambda).
$$

[Cofinal](../cardinal-number.md#cofinal-subset) restriction is valid because [ordinal addition](../ordinal.md#ordinal-addition) by a fixed [ordinal](../ordinal.md) on the left is increasing and continuous in its right argument, directly from its recursive definition. This completes the induction.

The [axiom of pairing](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-pairing) first produces $\{a\}$ by pairing $a$ with itself, then $\{a,b\}$, and then the pair of these two [sets](../set.md). To recover the coordinates from $P=\langle a,b\rangle$, observe that

$$
\bigcap P=\{a\},\qquad \bigcup P=\{a,b\}.
$$

The first [equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics) recovers $a$. Once $a$ is known, the second recovers $b$: if its [union](../set.md#set-union) is the [singleton](../set.md#singleton-mathematics) $\{a\}$ then $b=a$, and otherwise $b$ is its unique element different from $a$. Hence [equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics) of two encoded pairs forces [equality](../set-theory.md#equality-mathematics) of both coordinates, including the degenerate case $a=b$.

**The first [ordered triple](../set-theory.md#ordered-triple) encoding works.** If

$$
\langle a,\langle b,c\rangle\rangle=\langle a',\langle b',c'\rangle\rangle,
$$

[injectivity](../algebra.md#injective-function) of the [ordered pair](../set.md#ordered-pair) construction first gives $a=a'$ and $\langle b,c\rangle=\langle b',c'\rangle$, and a second application gives $b=b'$ and $c=c'$. The construction uses only two successive [ordered pair](../set.md#ordered-pair) constructions, so is a [set](../set.md) as well.

<h5 id="b/7/1/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#b/7/1)

The [Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein theorem](../cardinal-number.md#cantor-schroder-bernstein-theorem) states that [injections](../algebra.md#injective-function) $f:A\to B$ and $g:B\to A$ imply the existence of a [bijection](../function.md#bijection) $A\to B$. Here is the injection-chain [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof), so no fixed-point theorem is needed. Define

$$
A_0=A\setminus g(B),\qquad A_{n+1}=g(f(A_n)),\qquad C=\bigcup_{n<\omega}A_n,
$$

and [set](../set.md)

$$
h(a)=\begin{cases}f(a),&a\in C,\\ g^{-1}(a),&a\notin C.\end{cases}
$$

The second branch is defined because $a\notin C$ implies $a\notin A_0$, hence $a\in g(B)$, and the inverse there is unique by [injectivity](../algebra.md#injective-function) of $g$. Each branch is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function). A collision between branches would have $f(c)=g^{-1}(a)$ with $c\in C$ and $a\notin C$, implying $a=g(f(c))\in C$, a contradiction. Thus $h$ is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function).

For [surjectivity](../algebra.md#surjective-function) let $b\in B$. If $g(b)\notin C$, then $h(g(b))=b$. If $g(b)\in C$, it is not in $A_0$, so it belongs to some $A_{n+1}=g(f(A_n))$. There is $a\in A_n$ with $g(b)=g(f(a))$; [injectivity](../algebra.md#injective-function) of $g$ gives $b=f(a)=h(a)$. This proves that $h$ is a [bijection](../function.md#bijection) and completes the theorem without the [axiom of choice](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-choice).

For the comparison of [cardinalities](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) take the [power set](../set.md#power-set) of $\omega$ as the reference [set](../set.md). Its [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) is by definition

$$
\boxed{|\mathcal P(\omega)|=2^{\aleph_0}.}
$$

The remaining parts give explicit [injections](../algebra.md#injective-function) or [bijections](../function.md#bijection) comparing each requested [set](../set.md) with this one.

Fixing a [bijection](../function.md#bijection) between $\omega$ and the [countable set](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) $\mathbb Q$ turns this into an [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) $\mathbb R\to\mathcal P(\omega)$. \boxed{|\mathbb R|=2^{\aleph_0}.}

Write $C(\mathbb R,\mathbb R)$ for the [set](../set.md) of [continuous real-valued functions](../calculus.md#continuous-real-valued-function) on the [real line](../real-analysis.md#real-line). [Constant functions](../function.md#constant-function) give an [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) of $\mathbb R$ into this [set](../set.md). In the other direction, restriction to the [rational numbers](../number-theory.md#rational-number) is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function). Indeed, if [continuous functions](../calculus.md#continuous-function) $f,g$ agree at every rational and $x$ is real, choose [rational numbers](../number-theory.md#rational-number) $q_n\to x$. [Continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) gives

$$
f(x)=\lim_n f(q_n)=\lim_n g(q_n)=g(x).
$$

Thus restriction embeds $C(\mathbb R,\mathbb R)$ into $\mathbb R^{\mathbb Q}$, which has the same [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) as the real-sequence space because $\mathbb Q$ is countable. The previous part gives the [upper bound](../set.md#upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) $2^{\aleph_0}$, while [constant functions](../function.md#constant-function) give the matching lower bound. Hence

$$
\boxed{|C(\mathbb R,\mathbb R)|=2^{\aleph_0}.}
$$

This computes the [cardinality of continuous real functions](../calculus.md#cardinality-of-continuous-real-functions). All five [sets](../set.md) consequently have the same [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality). The [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) assumption is decisive for restriction to a [dense subset](../topology.md#dense-set) to determine a [function](../function.md).

<h3 id="b/8">8</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/8/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#b/8)

[Hartogs theorem](../set-theory.md#hartogs-theorem) says that for every [set](../set.md) $X$ there is a least [ordinal](../ordinal.md) $h(X)$ for which no [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) $h(X)\to X$ exists. This does not require the [axiom of choice](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-choice). Form the [set](../set.md) $W$ of [well-orderings](../set.md#well-order) of [subsets](../set.md#subset) of $X$. It can be defined by the [axiom schema of specification](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-specification) inside

$$
\mathcal P(X)\times\mathcal P(X\times X),
$$

recording a [subset](../set.md#subset) and its [well-order](../set.md#well-order). Each member of $W$ has a unique [ordinal](../ordinal.md) [order type](../ordinal.md#order-type). **[Axiom schema of replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) is used to collect those order types into a [set](../set.md)**

$$
H=\{\operatorname{otp}(Y,R):(Y,R)\in W\}.
$$

Every member of an [ordinal](../ordinal.md) in $H$ is the [order type](../ordinal.md#order-type) of an initial segment of the corresponding [well-ordering](../set.md#well-order), so also belongs to $H$. Thus $H$ is a [transitive set](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) of [ordinals](../ordinal.md) and is itself an [ordinal](../ordinal.md). An [ordinal](../ordinal.md) $\alpha$ belongs to $H$ exactly when it injects into $X$: use a [bijection](../function.md#bijection) to a [well-ordered](../set.md#well-order) [subset](../set.md#subset) in one direction, and transport the [ordinal](../ordinal.md) order along the [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) in the other. If $H$ injected into $X$, this characterization would give $H\in H$, impossible for an [ordinal](../ordinal.md). All smaller [ordinals](../ordinal.md) lie in $H$ and do inject into $X$. Therefore $H=h(X)$. Apart from the standard order-type theorem, the explicit collection of all order types is the [Axiom schema of replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) step; no [axiom of choice](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-choice) of a representative ordering for each type is needed.

[Zorn's lemma](../set-theory.md#zorn-s-lemma) states that a nonempty [partially ordered set](../set.md#partially-ordered-set) in which every [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) has an [upper bound](../set.md#upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) has a [maximal element](../set.md#maximal-element-of-a-partially-ordered-set). Use the [Hartogs bound on partial ordinal injections](../set-theory.md#hartogs-bound-on-partial-ordinal-injections) to obtain a set-sized [partially ordered set](../set.md#partially-ordered-set). Consider the partial [injections](../algebra.md#injective-function) into $X$ whose domains are [ordinals](../ordinal.md). [Hartogs theorem](../set-theory.md#hartogs-theorem) bounds every such domain strictly below $h=h(X)$. Their [graphs of a function](../function.md#graph-of-a-function) therefore form a [set](../set.md)

$$
P=\{f\subseteq h\times X:f\text{ is an injective function and }\operatorname{dom}f\text{ is an ordinal}\},
$$

obtained by the [axiom schema of specification](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-specification) from $\mathcal P(h\times X)$. Order $P$ by extension. It is nonempty because it contains the empty [function](../function.md). A [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) has as an [upper bound](../set.md#upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) the [union](../set.md#set-union) of its [graphs of a function](../function.md#graph-of-a-function): extension makes this a [function](../function.md), [injectivity](../algebra.md#injective-function) is preserved, and its domain is an [union](../set.md#set-union) of [ordinals](../ordinal.md) and hence an [ordinal](../ordinal.md). Its range is contained in $X$, so [Hartogs theorem](../set-theory.md#hartogs-theorem) forces its domain to be strictly below $h$; thus it belongs to $P$. The empty [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) also has the empty [function](../function.md) as an [upper bound](../set.md#upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set).

By [Zorn's lemma](../set-theory.md#zorn-s-lemma) choose a maximal $f:\alpha\to X$. If an element $x\in X$ lay outside its range, appending $f(\alpha)=x$ would give a larger [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) on $\alpha+1$. Such an [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) itself guarantees $\alpha+1<h$ by [Hartogs theorem](../set-theory.md#hartogs-theorem), so it is a legitimate member of $P$, contradicting [maximality in a partially ordered set](../set.md#maximality-in-a-partially-ordered-set). Therefore $f$ is [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function), and

$$
\boxed{X\text{ is equinumerous with an ordinal}.}
$$

For this single extension one only selects an element of one specified nonempty [set](../set.md); no additional simultaneous choices are hidden in the argument. [Zorn's lemma](../set-theory.md#zorn-s-lemma) is the form of the [axiom of choice](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-choice) used to obtain [maximality in a partially ordered set](../set.md#maximality-in-a-partially-ordered-set).

Finally work with [cardinals](../cardinal-number.md), represented by initial [ordinals](../ordinal.md), and put $\mu=\max(\kappa,\lambda)$. It is infinite by hypothesis. Nonzeroness of each factor gives an [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) of the larger [cardinal](../cardinal-number.md) into the [Cartesian product](../set-theory.md#cartesian-product) by holding a coordinate of the other fixed. The supplied infinite-ordinal square result gives $|\mu\times\mu|=\mu$. Hence

$$
\mu\leq\kappa\lambda\leq|\mu\times\mu|=\mu.
$$

For the sum, use [disjoint union](../set-theory.md#disjoint-union):

$$
\mu\leq\kappa+\lambda\leq|\mu\times\{0,1\}|\leq|\mu\times\mu|=\mu.
$$

The last [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) exists because $\mu$ is infinite and contains two distinct elements. [Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein theorem](../cardinal-number.md#cantor-schroder-bernstein-theorem) turns both pairs of bounds into equalities, proving

$$
\boxed{\kappa+\lambda=\kappa\lambda=\max(\kappa,\lambda).}
$$

These final operations are [cardinal sum](../cardinal-number.md#cardinal-sum) and [multiplication](../arithmetic.md#multiplication), measuring disjoint [unions](../set.md#set-union) and Cartesian products. They are not the noncommutative [ordinal arithmetic](../ordinal.md#ordinal-arithmetic) considered in question 5.
