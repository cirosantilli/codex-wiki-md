<h1 id="b/7/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Every strictly increasing [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) is in $\mathbb N^{\mathbb N}$, whose [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md) is $\aleph_0^{\aleph_0}=\mathfrak c$. This gives the [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md).

For each binary [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $\epsilon=(\epsilon_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$, define

$$
a_n=2n+\epsilon_n.
$$

Then $a_{n+1}-a_n=2+\epsilon_{n+1}-\epsilon_n\ge1$, so the [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) is strictly increasing. Distinct binary [sequences](../../../../../sequence.md) give distinct increasing [sequences](../../../../../sequence.md). This injects a [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of size $2^{\aleph_0}$ into the desired family, giving the matching [lower bound](../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{cardinality}=\mathfrak c.}
$$

One may also identify an increasing [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) with its infinite range [subset](../../../../../subset.md) of $\mathbb N$; the explicit binary coding establishes the [lower bound](../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) without discarding finite [subsets](../../../../../subset.md) by cardinal subtraction.
