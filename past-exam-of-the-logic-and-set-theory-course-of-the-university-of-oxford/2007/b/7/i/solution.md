<h1 id="b/7/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein theorem](../../../../../cantor-schroder-bernstein-theorem.md) says that [injections](../../../../../injective-function.md) $X\to Y$ and $Y\to X$ imply a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) $X\to Y$. Thus mutual cardinal inequalities imply equal cardinalities, without a [choice](../../../../../axiom-of-choice.md) assumption.

Write $\mathfrak c=2^{\aleph_0}=|\mathbb R|$. Standard [cardinal arithmetic](../../../../../cardinal-arithmetic.md) gives $\mathfrak c\cdot\mathfrak c=\mathfrak c$ and $\mathfrak c^{\aleph_0}=\mathfrak c$. The correspondence $x+iy\leftrightarrow(x,y)$ is a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) $\mathbb C\leftrightarrow\mathbb R^2$. Hence

$$
\boxed{|\mathbb C|=\mathfrak c.}
$$

Equivalently, the real axis supplies the lower inequality and a coding of two real coordinates supplies the upper one; Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein then gives the equality.
