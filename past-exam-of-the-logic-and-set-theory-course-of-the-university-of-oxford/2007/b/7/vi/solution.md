<h1 id="b/7/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

Inclusion gives $|\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q|\le\mathfrak c$. If $\kappa=|\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q|$, the disjoint decomposition of $\mathbb R$ gives

$$
\mathfrak c=\aleph_0+\kappa.
$$

The [irrational numbers](../../../../../irrational-number.md) are infinite, for instance $n+\sqrt2$ are pairwise distinct irrationals. Standard infinite cardinal [addition](../../../../../addition.md) gives $\aleph_0+\kappa=\max(\aleph_0,\kappa)$. Since [Cantor's theorem](../../../../../cantor-s-theorem.md) gives $\mathfrak c>\aleph_0$, the equality forces $\kappa=\mathfrak c$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{|\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q|=\mathfrak c.}
$$

This is the permitted cardinal-arithmetic [formal proof](../../../../../formal-proof.md); it does not subtract infinite cardinals as though they were finite numbers.
