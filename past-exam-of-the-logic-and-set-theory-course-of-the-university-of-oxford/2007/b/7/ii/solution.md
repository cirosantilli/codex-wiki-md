<h1 id="b/7/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of all [functions](../../../../../function-split.md) from a [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of size $\mathfrak c$ to another [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of size $\mathfrak c$ has size $\mathfrak c^{\mathfrak c}$. [Cardinal exponentiation](../../../../../cardinal-exponentiation.md) and the law $(\kappa^\lambda)^\mu=\kappa^{\lambda\mu}$ give

$$
\mathfrak c^{\mathfrak c}
=(2^{\aleph_0})^{\mathfrak c}
=2^{\aleph_0\cdot\mathfrak c}
=2^{\mathfrak c}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{|\mathbb R^{\mathbb C}|=2^{\mathfrak c}.}
$$

The [lower bound](../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) can also be seen by taking characteristic [functions](../../../../../function-split.md) of all [subsets](../../../../../subset.md) of $\mathbb C$. [Cantor's theorem](../../../../../cantor-s-theorem.md) makes this strictly larger than $\mathfrak c$, unlike the continuous-function space in the next part.
