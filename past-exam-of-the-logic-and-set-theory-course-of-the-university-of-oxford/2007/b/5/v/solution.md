<h1 id="b/5/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Fix $m$ and induct on $n$. The initial equality $m+0=0+m$ follows from the defining [zero](../../../../../zero-of-a-function.md) clause and (ii). Assuming $m+n=n+m$, the successor clause and (iii) give

$$
m+n^+=(m+n)^+=(n+m)^+=n+m^+=n^++m.
$$

This proves

$$
\boxed{m+n=n+m\quad(m,n\in\omega).}
$$

The extension to arbitrary [ordinals](../../../../../ordinal-split.md) is false. By the [limit](../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) clause for [ordinal addition](../../../../../ordinal-addition.md),

$$
1+\omega=\sup_{n<\omega}(1+n)=\omega,
$$

whereas $\omega+1$ is the [successor ordinal](../../../../../successor-ordinal.md) of $\omega$ and is strictly greater than it. Therefore

$$
\boxed{1+\omega\ne\omega+1.}
$$

Finite [addition](../../../../../addition.md) is commutative; [ordinal addition](../../../../../ordinal-addition.md) records the order of concatenated well-orders and need not be commutative.
