<h1 id="a/4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [compactness theorem](../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) says that a [set](../../../../../set-split.md) $T$ of [first-order sentences](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) has a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) if and only if every finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md) of $T$ has a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md). One direction follows by using the same [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) for each [subset](../../../../../subset.md).

For the nontrivial direction suppose every finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md) has a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md), but $T$ has none. Semantically $T\models\bot$, so completeness gives a formal [logical derivation](../../../../../logical-derivation.md) $T\vdash\bot$. A formal [logical derivation](../../../../../logical-derivation.md) is finite and uses only finitely many assumptions, say $T_0\subseteq T$. Thus $T_0\vdash\bot$. [Soundness](../../../../../soundness.md) would make $T_0$ unsatisfiable, contradicting the hypothesis. Here $\bot$ can be any fixed contradictory [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md), such as $\exists x\,(x\ne x)$, if it is not a primitive logical symbol. Therefore **finite satisfiability implies satisfiability**.
