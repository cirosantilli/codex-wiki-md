<h1 id="a/4/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Suppose such a [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) $S$ existed. Add two new constants $c,d$ and consider

$$
T=S\cup\{\neg E(c,d)\}\cup
\{\theta_n(c),\theta_n(d):n\ge1\},
$$

where $\theta_n$ is the finite lower-bound [logical formula](../../../../../logical-formula.md) from (iii).

Every finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md) of $T$ has a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md). Choose a [first-order structure](../../../../../first-order-structure.md) with exactly two equivalence classes: one countably infinite, and one finite of size at least the largest bound appearing in that [subset](../../../../../subset.md). Interpret $c$ in the infinite class and $d$ in the finite class. The [first-order structure](../../../../../first-order-structure.md) satisfies all of $S$, by its assumed characterization, and meets the finite demands on both named classes.

[Compactness theorem](../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) in the language with the two new constants gives a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of all of $T$. Its classes of $c$ and $d$ are distinct and both infinite. Its reduct still satisfies $S$, which is impossible if $S$ characterizes exactly one infinite class. Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{Exactly one infinite equivalence class is not first-order axiomatizable}.}
$$

The finite approximations work because any finite list of [lower bounds](../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) permits the second class to remain finite; the entire list prevents that escape.
