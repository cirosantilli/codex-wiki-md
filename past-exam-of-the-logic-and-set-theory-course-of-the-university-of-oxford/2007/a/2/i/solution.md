↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [logical derivation](../../../../../logical-derivation.md) from $\Gamma$ is a finite [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) of [propositional formulas](../../../../../propositional-formula.md), each either a member of $\Gamma$, an instance of one of the three [axioms](../../../../../axiom.md), or obtained from earlier lines by one of the two displayed [inference rules](../../../../../inference-rule.md). The notation $\Gamma\vdash\alpha$ means that some such [logical derivation](../../../../../logical-derivation.md) ends in $\alpha$. Writing $\vdash\alpha$ means derivability with no assumptions.

[Propositional consistency](../../../../../propositional-consistency.md) means that there is no [propositional formula](../../../../../propositional-formula.md) $\theta$ for which both $\Gamma\vdash\theta$ and $\Gamma\vdash\neg\theta$. Equivalently not every [propositional formula](../../../../../propositional-formula.md) is derivable. To check the equivalence directly for this calculus, use the identity theorem proved in (ii): from $\theta\to\theta$ and L3 obtain $\theta\to(\neg\theta\to\eta)$; two uses of [modus ponens rule](../../../../../implication-elimination-rule.md) give arbitrary $\eta$. Thus a contradictory theory proves everything. Conversely a theory proving everything proves a [propositional formula](../../../../../propositional-formula.md) and its [negation](../../../../../negation.md).
