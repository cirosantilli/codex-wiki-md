↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

**False.** Take $\alpha=1$ and $\beta=\omega$. By the limit clause for [ordinal addition](../../../../../ordinal-addition.md),

$$
1+\omega=\sup_{n<\omega}(1+n)=\omega,
\qquad \omega+1>\omega.
$$

The first [order type](../../../../../order-type.md) is one point followed by a countably infinite [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md), which is still of type $\omega$. The second has a greatest element after that infinite [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md), so its [order type](../../../../../order-type.md) is the [successor ordinal](../../../../../successor-ordinal.md) $\omega+1$. Thus [ordinal addition](../../../../../ordinal-addition.md) is not commutative, even though the two resulting [sets](../../../../../set-split.md) have equal [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md).
