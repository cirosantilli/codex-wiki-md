<h1 id="b/7/5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Write $C(\mathbb R,\mathbb R)$ for the [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of [continuous real-valued functions](../../../../../continuous-real-valued-function.md) on the [real line](../../../../../real-line.md). [Constant functions](../../../../../constant-function.md) give an [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) of $\mathbb R$ into this [set](../../../../../set-split.md). In the other direction, restriction to the [rational numbers](../../../../../rational-number.md) is [injective](../../../../../injective-function.md). Indeed, if [continuous functions](../../../../../continuous-function.md) $f,g$ agree at every rational and $x$ is real, choose [rational numbers](../../../../../rational-number.md) $q_n\to x$. [Continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) gives

$$
f(x)=\lim_n f(q_n)=\lim_n g(q_n)=g(x).
$$

Thus restriction embeds $C(\mathbb R,\mathbb R)$ into $\mathbb R^{\mathbb Q}$, which has the same [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md) as the real-sequence space because $\mathbb Q$ is countable. The previous part gives the [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $2^{\aleph_0}$, while [constant functions](../../../../../constant-function.md) give the matching lower bound. Hence

$$
\boxed{|C(\mathbb R,\mathbb R)|=2^{\aleph_0}.}
$$

This computes the [cardinality of continuous real functions](../../../../../cardinality-of-continuous-real-functions.md). All five [sets](../../../../../set-split.md) consequently have the same [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md). The [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) assumption is decisive for restriction to a [dense subset](../../../../../dense-set.md) to determine a [function](../../../../../function-split.md).
