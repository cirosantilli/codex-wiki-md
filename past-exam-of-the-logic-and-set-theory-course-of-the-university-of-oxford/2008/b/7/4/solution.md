<h1 id="b/7/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

[Constant sequences](../../../../../constant-sequence.md) give an [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) $\mathbb R\to{}^{\omega}\mathbb R$. For the opposite bound fix an enumeration $(q_m)_{m\in\omega}$ of the [rational numbers](../../../../../rational-number.md) and encode a [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $(x_n)$ by

$$
E((x_n))=\{(n,m)\in\omega\times\omega:q_m<x_n\}.
$$

Distinct [sequences](../../../../../sequence.md) differ at some coordinate $n$; density of the [rational numbers](../../../../../rational-number.md) then makes their encoded [sets](../../../../../set-split.md) differ at a pair $(n,m)$. Thus $E$ is [injective](../../../../../injective-function.md). The [Cartesian product](../../../../../cartesian-product.md) $\omega\times\omega$ is countable; for example the [Cantor pairing function](../../../../../cantor-pairing-function.md)

$$
\pi(n,m)=\frac{(n+m)(n+m+1)}2+m
$$

is a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) with $\omega$. Consequently $\mathcal P(\omega\times\omega)$ is [equinumerous](../../../../../equinumerosity.md) with $\mathcal P(\omega)$, already [equinumerous](../../../../../equinumerosity.md) with $\mathbb R$. Apply [Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein theorem](../../../../../cantor-schroder-bernstein-theorem.md) to obtain

$$
\boxed{|{}^{\omega}\mathbb R|=2^{\aleph_0}.}
$$
