↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

**This encoding works.** Use three distinct tags $0,1,2$ and the [ordered pair](../../../../../ordered-pair.md) construction already proved [injective](../../../../../injective-function.md). In the [set](../../../../../set-split.md)

$$
\{\langle0,a\rangle,\langle1,b\rangle,\langle2,c\rangle\},
$$

exactly one member has first coordinate $0$, exactly one has first coordinate $1$, and exactly one has first coordinate $2$. [Equality](../../../../../equality-mathematics.md) of two such [sets](../../../../../set-split.md) therefore matches the members carrying each tag. [Injectivity](../../../../../injective-function.md) of the [ordered pair](../../../../../ordered-pair.md) construction then gives [equality](../../../../../equality-mathematics.md) of $a$, of $b$ and of $c$, respectively.

This is a [tagged encoding of a finite tuple](../../../../../tagged-encoding-of-a-finite-tuple.md). It remains valid when coordinates coincide with one another or with tags: the [equality](../../../../../equality-mathematics.md) criterion for [ordered pairs](../../../../../ordered-pair.md) still distinguishes their first coordinates. [Finite set](../../../../../finite-set.md) formation using pairing and [union](../../../../../set-union.md) makes the encoding a [set](../../../../../set-split.md).
