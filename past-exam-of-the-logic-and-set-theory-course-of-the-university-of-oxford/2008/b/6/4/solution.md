<h1 id="b/6/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

**This encoding works**, interpreting the outer three-coordinate brackets by the nested [ordered pair](../../../../../ordered-pair.md) convention established in item 1. [Equality](../../../../../equality-mathematics.md) of the outer [ordered triples](../../../../../ordered-triple.md) gives

$$
\{0,a\}=\{0,a'\},\qquad \{1,b\}=\{1,b'\},\qquad \{2,c\}=\{2,c'\}.
$$

For any fixed [set](../../../../../set-split.md) $k$, the map $x\mapsto\{k,x\}$ is [injective](../../../../../injective-function.md). If $x=k$, [equality](../../../../../equality-mathematics.md) $\{k,x\}=\{k,y\}$ makes the right-hand side a [singleton](../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md) and forces $y=k$. If $x\ne k$, membership of $x$ in $\{k,y\}$ forces $x=y$. Applying this argument with $k=0,1,2$ recovers the respective coordinates.

The outer brackets must encode an [ordered triple](../../../../../ordered-triple.md), rather than an unordered [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of three doubletons. The source uses three-coordinate angle brackets here; using the successful convention of item 1 makes their meaning explicit and supplies a genuine construction.
