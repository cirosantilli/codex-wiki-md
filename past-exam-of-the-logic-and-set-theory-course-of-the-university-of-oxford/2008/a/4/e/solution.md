<h1 id="a/4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Let $T=\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb C)$ in the finite language of rings: it is the [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of all [first-order sentences](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) true in the [field](../../../../../field.md) of [complex numbers](../../../../../complex-number.md). It is satisfiable, with [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) $\mathbb C$, so the countable-language [Löwenheim-Skolem theorem](../../../../../lowenheim-skolem-theorem.md) gives an at most countable [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) $K$ of $T$.

All [field axioms](../../../../../field-axioms.md) belong to $T$, hence $K$ is a [field](../../../../../field.md). For every positive [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) $n$, the [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) saying that there are at least $n$ distinct elements is also true in $\mathbb C$ and belongs to $T$. Thus $K$ cannot be finite, and is countably infinite.

Finally, for each [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) $\sigma$, either $\sigma\in T$ or $\neg\sigma\in T$. Since $K$ satisfies all of $T$, it satisfies $\sigma$ exactly when $\mathbb C$ does. Therefore

$$
\boxed{|K|=\aleph_0,\qquad K\equiv\mathbb C.}
$$

[Elementary equivalence](../../../../../elementary-equivalence.md) concerns first-order [first-order sentences](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) and does not imply an [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md); the two [fields](../../../../../field.md) have different [cardinalities](../../../../../cardinality.md). The stronger downward form may even choose $K$ as a countable elementary subfield of $\mathbb C$.
