<h1 id="a/1/e/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

A [subformula](../../../../../../subformula.md) beginning at the first opening parenthesis equals the whole [logical formula](../../../../../../logical-formula.md) by [prefix-freeness of fully parenthesized formulas](../../../../../../prefix-freeness-of-fully-parenthesized-formulas.md). Every other [subformula](../../../../../../subformula.md) is the [logical formula](../../../../../../logical-formula.md) of a proper syntax subtree, by the [parsing](../../../../../../parsing.md) argument in part (iii). The two children of the root are precisely $\psi$ and $\chi$, so such a subtree lies entirely inside one of those children. In particular no [logical formula](../../../../../../logical-formula.md) substring can begin in $\psi$ and cross the principal [logical implication](../../../../../../logical-implication.md) symbol into $\chi$.

Conversely, each [subformula](../../../../../../subformula.md) of either child is visibly a substring of the whole expression, as is the whole expression itself. Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Sub}((\psi\to\chi))=\{(\psi\to\chi)\}\cup\operatorname{Sub}(\psi)\cup\operatorname{Sub}(\chi).}
$$

Together these cases identify the substring definition of a [subformula](../../../../../../subformula.md) with its usual syntax-tree definition.
