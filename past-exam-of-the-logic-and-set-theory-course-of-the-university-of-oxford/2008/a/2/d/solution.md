↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

There is a three-line Hilbert [formal proof](../../../../../formal-proof.md). Use A1 with $\alpha=\chi$, $\beta=\psi$; then use A3 with $\alpha=\chi$, $\beta=(\psi\to\chi)$:

$$
\begin{array}{rll}
1.&\chi\to(\psi\to\chi)&\text{A1},\\
2.&(\chi\to(\psi\to\chi))\to(\neg(\psi\to\chi)\to\neg\chi)&\text{A3},\\
3.&\neg(\psi\to\chi)\to\neg\chi&\text{modus ponens on 1,2}.
\end{array}
$$

This is a direct [logical derivation](../../../../../logical-derivation.md) in the displayed [Hilbert proof system](../../../../../hilbert-proof-system.md). Neither the [deduction theorem for propositional logic](../../../../../deduction-theorem-for-propositional-logic.md) nor a [completeness theorem for propositional logic](../../../../../completeness-theorem-for-propositional-logic.md) is needed: the desired [logical implication](../../../../../logical-implication.md) is just the contrapositive, supplied by A3, of the particular A1 instance on line 1.
