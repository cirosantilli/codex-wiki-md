<h1 id="a/2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**The requested derivation does not follow from the printed axiom set.** Use a two-valued matrix interpretation with ordinary material [logical implication](../../../../../../logical-implication.md) and designated value $1$, but interpret the unary symbol $\neg$ by the constant function $N(0)=N(1)=0$.

Every instance of the first axiom $\alpha\to(\beta\to\alpha)$ is valid. In the replacement axiom, both $N(\beta)\to\alpha$ and $N(\alpha)\to\beta$ have value $1$, so its whole [logical implication](../../../../../../logical-implication.md) is valid as well. [Modus ponens rule](../../../../../../implication-elimination-rule.md) preserves the designated value. The deduction rule is sound for semantic consequence under this interpretation because [logical implication](../../../../../../logical-implication.md) is the ordinary truth-functional [logical implication](../../../../../../logical-implication.md). Induction on derivations, allowing arbitrary sets of assumptions, therefore makes every theorem valid in this matrix.

But the old contraposition axiom has value

$$
(N(\beta)\to N(\alpha))\to(\alpha\to\beta)
=1\to(\alpha\to\beta),
$$

which is false when $\alpha=1$ and $\beta=0$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\text{The printed replacement system does not derive the old third axiom.}}
$$

This is a [truth-matrix countermodel for a Hilbert axiom set](../../../../../../truth-matrix-countermodel-for-a-hilbert-axiom-set.md). A nonstandard interpretation of a primitive symbol is legitimate for proving syntactic nonderivability: only preservation by the displayed axioms and rules is required. The source would need a stronger [negation](../../../../../../negation.md) axiom or an extra [negation](../../../../../../negation.md) principle to support its requested conclusion.
