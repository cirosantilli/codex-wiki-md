The [first-order terms](../../../../../../first-order-term.md) are the least collection containing every variable and constant and closed under formation of $f(t_1,\ldots,t_k)$ whenever $f$ has arity $k$ and the $t_j$ are terms. A [closed term](../../../../../../closed-term.md) has no variables.

The [first-order formulas](../../../../../../first-order-formula.md) are the least collection containing the [atomic formulas](../../../../../../atomic-formula.md) $t_1\dot=t_2$ and $P(t_1,\ldots,t_k)$ of the appropriate arities, and closed under [negation](../../../../../../negation.md), [logical implication](../../../../../../logical-implication.md), and [universal quantification](../../../../../../universal-quantification.md): from formulas $\phi,\psi$ one may form $\neg\phi$, $(\phi\to\psi)$, and $\forall x_i\phi$, with the chosen fully parenthesized grammar. Only finite expressions obtained by these rules are formulas. No additional strings belong to either collection.

Terms denote elements under an [assignment in a first-order structure](../../../../../../assignment-in-a-first-order-structure.md); formulas receive [truth values](../../../../../../truth-value.md) in a [first-order structure](../../../../../../first-order-structure.md). The two syntactic categories have different formation rules even when they use some of the same punctuation.
