**False.** Let $\phi$ be $x_i\dot=x_i$ and let $t=x_j$ with $j\ne i$. There is no quantifier that could capture a variable, so $t$ is free for $x_i$ in $\phi$. [Capture-avoiding substitution](../../../../../../capture-avoiding-substitution.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\phi[t/x_i]=x_j\dot=x_j.}
$$

The number of occurrences of $x_i$ falls from two to zero, contradicting the proposed strict increase. Even [capture-avoiding substitution](../../../../../../capture-avoiding-substitution.md) of $x_i$ itself would leave the formula unchanged and refute the strict inequality.
