<h1 id="a/3/d/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The theory is consistent because the rational order is a model. If some sentence $\theta$ were not decided by the theory, both extensions by $\theta$ and by $\neg\theta$ would be consistent. By [Gödel's completeness theorem](../../../../../../godel-s-completeness-theorem.md) and the countable-model consequence of [Löwenheim-Skolem theorem](../../../../../../lowenheim-skolem-theorem.md), each would have a [countable](../../../../../../countable-set.md) model. The two models would disagree on $\theta$, so could not be isomorphic. This contradicts part (iii).

Therefore the theory decides every sentence and is complete. Under the standard convention that a theory includes all its logical consequences, this makes it [maximal consistent](../../../../../../maximal-consistent-set-of-first-order-sentences.md).

If one takes only the displayed finite list of axioms as a raw set, that list is not literally maximal consistent: one can add further theorems without inconsistency. The conclusion applies to its deductive closure, which is the complete theory of dense linear order without endpoints.
