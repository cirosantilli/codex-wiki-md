<h1 id="a/3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose a structure satisfies every sentence of $\Sigma$. By the [soundness theorem for first-order logic](../../../../../../soundness-theorem-for-first-order-logic.md), every sentence derivable from $\Sigma$ is true in this structure. In particular it cannot derive both a sentence and its [negation](../../../../../../negation.md), because they cannot both be true under the usual semantics.

Therefore $\boxed{\Sigma\text{ satisfiable}\ \Longrightarrow\ \Sigma\text{ consistent}}$. This direction uses soundness alone, without constructing a model from a proof-theoretic assumption.
