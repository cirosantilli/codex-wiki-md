<h1 id="b/4/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

**True in ZF.** Let $A$ be a set that is not finite. For each [natural number](../../../../../natural-number.md) $n$, form

$$
E_n=\{B\subseteq A:|B|=n\}.
$$

Each $E_n$ is a subset of $\mathcal P(A)$ and is nonempty: a nonfinite set has an $n$-element subset for every finite $n$, by finite induction. The sets $E_n$ are pairwise disjoint, hence pairwise distinct. Consequently

$$
n\longmapsto E_n
$$

is an [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) from $\mathbb N$ into $\mathcal P(\mathcal P(A))$. This is a [double-power-set countability bound without choice](../../../../../double-power-set-countability-bound-without-choice.md).

Part (d), applied to the set $\mathcal P(A)$, says that its [power set](../../../../../power-set.md) is not countably infinite. Combining that result with the displayed [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\aleph_0<2^{2^\kappa}.}
$$

There is no hidden [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) choice: the proof uses the entire definable family of $n$-element subsets, rather than choosing one subset for each $n$.
