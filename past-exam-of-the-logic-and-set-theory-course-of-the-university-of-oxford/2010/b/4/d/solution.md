<h1 id="b/4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**True in ZF.** Suppose $\mathcal P(A)$ were countably infinite. The singleton [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) would inject $A$ into the [natural numbers](../../../../../natural-number.md). The set $A$ cannot be finite, since its [power set](../../../../../power-set.md) would then be finite. Its image is therefore an infinite subset of $\mathbb N$, which has a canonical increasing enumeration. This gives a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) $A\cong\mathbb N$ without choice.

But then $\mathcal P(A)\cong\mathcal P(\mathbb N)$, contradicting [Cantor's theorem](../../../../../cantor-s-theorem.md) that the latter is strictly larger than $\mathbb N$. Hence

$$
\boxed{2^\kappa\ne\aleph_0\quad\text{for every cardinal }\kappa.}
$$

The proof does not assume that an arbitrary infinite set contains a countably infinite subset; here the given [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) into the [natural numbers](../../../../../natural-number.md) supplies its [well-ordering](../../../../../well-order.md).
