<h1 id="b/6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The finite-stage calculation gives $\gamma_\omega=\sup_{n<\omega}n^2=\omega$, so the desired equality holds at the first infinite [ordinal](../../../../../ordinal-split.md). Apply [transfinite induction](../../../../../transfinite-induction.md) to all subsequent infinite [ordinals](../../../../../ordinal-split.md). The successor step is part (b), and the limit step is part (c). Thus

$$
|\gamma_\alpha|=|\alpha|\quad\text{for every infinite ordinal }\alpha.
$$

By the [well-ordering theorem](../../../../../well-ordering-theorem.md), any infinite cardinal in the question may be represented by an infinite [initial ordinal](../../../../../initial-ordinal.md) $\kappa$. The corrected recursion supplies a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) $g_\kappa:\kappa\times\kappa\to\gamma_\kappa$, and the just-proved equality gives $|\gamma_\kappa|=\kappa$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\kappa\cdot\kappa=\kappa.}
$$

The proof used only finite-copy interleaving before reaching this conclusion; using the square equality itself to estimate the successor or limit stages would have been circular.
