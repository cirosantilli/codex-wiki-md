↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

[Semantic consequence under an assignment](../../../../../../semantic-consequence-under-an-assignment.md) means that for every [first-order structure](../../../../../../first-order-structure.md) $\mathcal A$ with the relevant [first-order variable assignment](../../../../../../first-order-variable-assignment.md) $v$ into its domain,

$$
\boxed{\bigl(\mathcal A\models\gamma[v]\text{ for every }\gamma\in\Gamma\bigr)\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal A\models\phi[v].}
$$

More explicitly, in a fixed structure this is the satisfaction implication at $v$; logical consequence requires that implication in all structures and assignments. An assignment takes values in a particular domain, so a single set map is not silently shared between unrelated domains.

By induction on [first-order formulas](../../../../../../first-order-formula.md), their [truth values](../../../../../../truth-value.md) depend only on the values of their [free variables](../../../../../../free-variable.md). A [first-order sentence](../../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) has none. If all formulas here are sentences, every displayed satisfaction statement, and therefore the consequence condition, is independent of $v$.
