<h1 id="a/2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The [compactness theorem](../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) states that a set of [first-order sentences](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) has a model if every finite subset has a model.

For each $n\geq1$, let $\chi_n$ assert that at least $n$ distinct elements exist. Consider $T=\{\phi\}\cup\{\chi_n:n\geq1\}$. A finite subset requires only some finite lower bound $N$ on the size. Choose an odd integer $m\geq N$; the given property of $\phi$ supplies a model of that size, satisfying the finite subset. By the [compactness theorem](../../../../../compactness-theorem.md), $T$ has a model. Its domain has at least $n$ elements for every $n$, so **$\phi$ has an infinite model**. This argument applies to every sentence with the finite-model-size property in (b), irrespective of its behavior on infinite structures.
