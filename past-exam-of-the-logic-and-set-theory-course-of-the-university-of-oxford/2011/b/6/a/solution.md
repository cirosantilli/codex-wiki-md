↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The four principles are:

- [Axiom of choice](../../../../../axiom-of-choice.md): for every family $(A_i)_{i\in I}$ of nonempty sets, there exists a [function](../../../../../function-split.md) $c$ on $I$ with $c(i)\in A_i$ for all $i$.
- [Cardinal comparability principle](../../../../../cardinal-comparability-principle.md): for any sets $A,B$, there is an [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) $A\hookrightarrow B$ or an [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) $B\hookrightarrow A$.
- [Well-ordering theorem](../../../../../well-ordering-theorem.md): every set admits a [well-order](../../../../../well-order.md).
- [Zorn's lemma](../../../../../zorn-s-lemma.md): every nonempty [partially ordered set](../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md) in which every chain has an upper bound has a maximal element.

In the last assertion the upper bound belongs to the partially ordered set; it need not belong to the chain.
