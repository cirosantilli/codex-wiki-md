<h1 id="b/6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Assume the [well-ordering theorem](../../../../../well-ordering-theorem.md). Every set with a chosen [well-order](../../../../../well-order.md) has an [order isomorphism](../../../../../order-isomorphism.md) to a unique [ordinal](../../../../../ordinal-split.md). Any two [ordinals](../../../../../ordinal-split.md) are comparable, and the smaller embeds into the larger as an initial segment. Transporting that embedding back to the original sets proves the [cardinal comparability principle](../../../../../cardinal-comparability-principle.md).

Conversely, assume the [cardinal comparability principle](../../../../../cardinal-comparability-principle.md) and let $X$ be any set. The [Hartogs theorem](../../../../../hartogs-theorem.md), provable in [ZF](../../../../../zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory.md), supplies an [ordinal](../../../../../ordinal-split.md) $h(X)$ for which no [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) $h(X)\hookrightarrow X$ exists. Comparison forces an [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) $X\hookrightarrow h(X)$. Pulling back the [well-order](../../../../../well-order.md) on its image gives a [well-order](../../../../../well-order.md) on $X$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{ZF}\vdash\mathrm{CC}\leftrightarrow\mathrm{WO}.}
$$

The assumed ordinal facts are that every well-ordered set has an ordinal order type and that any two ordinals are comparable; the other assumed theorem is precisely the stated [Hartogs theorem](../../../../../hartogs-theorem.md).
