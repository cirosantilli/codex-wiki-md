<h1 id="b/4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein theorem](../../../../../cantor-schroder-bernstein-theorem.md) states that [injections](../../../../../injective-function.md) $A\hookrightarrow B$ and $B\hookrightarrow A$ imply a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) $A\cong B$.

Let $\mathfrak c=|\mathbb R|$ and $\mathcal D=\{S\subseteq\mathbb R:S\text{ is dense in }\mathbb R\}$. Clearly $|\mathcal D|\leq2^{\mathfrak c}$. For the reverse bound, let $C$ be the usual [Cantor set](../../../../../cantor-set.md), of [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md) $\mathfrak c$. It is closed with empty interior, so $D=\mathbb R\setminus C$ is dense. The map

$$
\mathcal P(C)\longrightarrow\mathcal D,\qquad A\longmapsto D\cup A
$$

is injective, because $A=(D\cup A)\cap C$. Applying the [Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein theorem](../../../../../cantor-schroder-bernstein-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{|\mathcal D|=2^{\mathfrak c}=2^{2^{\aleph_0}}.}
$$

One can see $|C|=\mathfrak c$ by coding [binary sequences](../../../../../bitstream.md) as ternary expansions using only digits zero and two, together with the usual $|\{0,1\}^{\omega}|=|\mathbb R|$. The same fixed $D$ works for all subsets. This is the [cardinality of the family of dense subsets of the reals](../../../../../cardinality-of-the-family-of-dense-subsets-of-the-reals.md).
