The assertion $\Gamma\vdash\phi$ means that there is a finite formal derivation ending in $\phi$ with its undischarged assumptions contained in $\Gamma$. In a context-labelled presentation, an axiom instance is available with empty context, a formula $\alpha$ is available from the context $\{\alpha\}$, [modus ponens](../../../../../implication-elimination-rule.md) combines derivations of $\alpha$ and $\alpha\to\beta$ to derive $\beta$, and [generalization](../../../../../universal-generalization-rule.md) derives $\forall x\alpha$ provided $x$ is not free in its open assumptions. Thinning enlarges a context without changing its conclusion.

Each line or node must specify an axiom instance, an assumption, or an application of a permitted rule to earlier lines or subderivations. The side conditions on substitution, quantification and contexts are part of correctness. In particular a derivation is a finite syntactic object, even when $\Gamma$ is infinite; it uses only finitely many assumptions before a final thinning to $\Gamma$ if necessary. Derivability $\vdash$ is thus distinct in definition from [semantic entailment](../../../../../semantic-entailment.md) $\models$, which quantifies over structures and assignments.
