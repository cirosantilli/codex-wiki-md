<h1 id="a/3/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Neither constants alone nor functions alone gives the same uniform adequacy on all nonempty carriers.** A one-element [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) has exactly one interpretation of each [function symbol](../../../../../../function-symbol.md) of any finite arity and of every [constant symbol](../../../../../../constant-symbol.md). In contrast it has two interpretations of a unary predicate: empty or full. Thus a fixed purely functional or constant-only language has only one structure on that [singleton](../../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md), and cannot encode both [truth values](../../../../../../truth-value.md) of $\exists xP(x)$ by a same-carrier truth-preserving translation with reconstruction. This is the [singleton truth-preservation obstruction to expressive completeness](../../../../../../singleton-truth-preservation-obstruction-to-expressive-completeness.md), even when [logical equality](../../../../../../logical-equality.md) is available.

Constants alone also cannot represent general variable-dependent relations on larger infinite carriers: a fixed formula mentions only finitely many named elements, and its remaining equality tests do not encode an arbitrary [binary relation](../../../../../../binary-relation.md). Functions offer more flexibility. On carriers with at least two elements, predicates can be encoded by [characteristic functions](../../../../../../characteristic-function-split.md) taking two distinguished values, and constants can be represented by constant-valued unary functions if explicit constant symbols are forbidden. Such a construction can work after adding axioms for distinct truth markers, but those axioms exclude the [singleton](../../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md) case. Enlarging the carrier or adding a Boolean sort is another possible encoding; it changes the same-carrier sense established in the preceding part and must not be silently identified with it.
