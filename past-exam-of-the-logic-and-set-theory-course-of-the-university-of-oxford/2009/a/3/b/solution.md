<h1 id="a/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

There are sixteen binary [Boolean functions](../../../../../boolean-function.md), one for each four-bit [truth table](../../../../../truth-table.md). **Exactly eight give an adequate basis together with [negation](../../../../../negation.md).**

The eight affine functions are $0,1,p,\neg p,q,\neg q,p\oplus q,\neg(p\oplus q)$. Each is affine over the field with two elements, and composition with other affine functions or [negation](../../../../../negation.md) remains affine. Since [conjunction](../../../../../conjunction-astronomy.md) is not affine, none of these eight bases is adequate.

Each remaining [truth table](../../../../../truth-table.md) has either one true row or three true rows. In the latter case negate its output to obtain a function with one true row. Negate selected inputs to move that row to $(1,1)$: the resulting function is [conjunction](../../../../../conjunction-astronomy.md). [Conjunction](../../../../../conjunction-astronomy.md) together with [negation](../../../../../negation.md) expresses every [disjunctive normal form](../../../../../disjunctive-normal-form.md), proving adequacy of all remaining eight. They are AND, NAND, OR, NOR, the two directed implications, and the two asymmetric conjunctions $p\land\neg q$, $\neg p\land q$. The count treats the binary connective by its truth function rather than by arbitrary choices of its printed symbol.
