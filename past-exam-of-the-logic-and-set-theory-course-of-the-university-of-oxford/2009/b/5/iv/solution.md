<h1 id="b/5/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Every finitely supported [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) vanishes from some index onward, since a finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md) of the [natural numbers](../../../../../natural-number.md) has a greatest element. Thus the [set](../../../../../set-split.md) is a union over $n<\omega$ of [sequences](../../../../../sequence.md) determined by their first $n$ real entries, with zeros afterwards. Each nontrivial piece has cardinal $\mathfrak c^n=\mathfrak c$, so the union has cardinal at most $\aleph_0\cdot\mathfrak c=\mathfrak c$.

Conversely $r\mapsto(r,0,0,\ldots)$ is an [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) from $\mathbb R$. Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak c.}
$$

No choice of an arbitrary infinite support is involved; it is the finite support that makes a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) union of finite-dimensional parameter spaces enough.
