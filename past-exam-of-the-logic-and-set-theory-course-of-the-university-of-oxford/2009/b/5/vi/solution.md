<h1 id="b/5/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

The [complex plane](../../../../../complex-plane.md) has a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) basis of disks with positive rational radii and centres with rational real and imaginary coordinates. Each [open set](../../../../../open-set.md) is the union of the basic disks it contains, so it is determined by a [subset](../../../../../subset.md) of this [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) basis. Therefore the number of [open sets](../../../../../open-set.md) is at most $2^{\aleph_0}=\mathfrak c$.

For the reverse inequality, the distinct disks $\{z:|z|<r\}$ for $r>0$ give $\mathfrak c$ different [open sets](../../../../../open-set.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\bigl|\{U\subseteq\mathbb C:U\text{ open}\}\bigr|=\mathfrak c.}
$$

The countable-basis restriction is decisive: there are $2^{\mathfrak c}$ arbitrary [subsets](../../../../../subset.md) of the plane, but only continuum many open ones.
