<h1 id="b/5/vii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vii](../vii.md)

For the [cardinality of total orders on a countably infinite set](../../../../../cardinality-of-total-orders-on-a-countably-infinite-set.md), a [total order](../../../../../total-order.md) on $\mathbb N$ is a [subset](../../../../../subset.md) of $\mathbb N\times\mathbb N$, so there are at most $2^{\aleph_0}=\mathfrak c$ such orders. To obtain the lower bound, associate to every [binary sequence](../../../../../bitstream.md) $(b_n)$ an order by placing the two-element blocks $\{2n,2n+1\}$ in increasing order of $n$. Inside block $n$, use $2n<2n+1$ when $b_n=0$ and reverse them when $b_n=1$.

This is a [total order](../../../../../total-order.md) for every [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md). Distinct [sequences](../../../../../sequence.md) differ on the comparison of some adjacent pair and therefore produce distinct relations. Thus [binary sequences](../../../../../bitstream.md) inject into the order [set](../../../../../set-split.md), proving

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak c.}
$$

The construction counts distinct orders on the labelled [set](../../../../../set-split.md), even though the resulting [order types](../../../../../order-type.md) are all isomorphic to the usual natural-number order.
