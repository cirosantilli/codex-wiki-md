# DNA synthesis labeling with EdU

↑ **Parent:** [DNA pulse labeling](dna-pulse-labeling.md)

[EdU labeling](dna-synthesis-labeling-with-edu.md) uses a modified [nucleoside](nucleoside.md), 5-ethynyl-2'-deoxyuridine, incorporated into newly synthesized [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md), then chemically couples it to a fluorescent reporter.

A 2008 experiment injected adult mice with $100\,\mathrm{\mu g}$ of this label and inspected intestinal sections 96 hours later. Fixed sections on slides received $10\,\mathrm{\mu M}$ Alexa568-azide for 10 minutes. Imaging with a [fluorescence microscope](fluorescence-microscope.md) combined the reporter image with a Hoechst [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) counterstain and a transmitted-light view. Labeled [cell nuclei](cell-nucleus.md) marked descendants of [biological cells](biological-cell-split.md) synthesizing [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) during exposure, rather than identifying current [S phase](s-phase.md) at collection. [A chemical method for fast and sensitive detection of DNA synthesis in vivo, Figure 4 legend: mouse pulse, section preparation and imaging](a-chemical-method-for-fast-and-sensitive-detection-of-dna-synthesis-in-vivo-by-adrian-salic-and-timothy-j-mitchison.md)

The same study's cultured-cell controls compared overnight $10\,\mathrm{\mu M}$ label, no label, and label with $10\,\mathrm{mM}$ [hydroxyurea](hydroxyurea.md). No-label samples had little reporter background; [hydroxyurea](hydroxyurea.md) strongly suppressed incorporation. The camera detected residual signal with a 3-second exposure versus 40 milliseconds under otherwise identical settings, demonstrating why acquisition settings matter. These controls support synthesis-dependent labeling, without proving that every labeled [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md) completes division. [Figure 2 legend, A–M: NIH 3T3 controls, fluorescence and differential interference contrast imaging, exposure-time comparison](a-chemical-method-for-fast-and-sensitive-detection-of-dna-synthesis-in-vivo-by-adrian-salic-and-timothy-j-mitchison.md)
