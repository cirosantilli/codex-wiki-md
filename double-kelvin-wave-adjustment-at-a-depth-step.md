# Double Kelvin-wave adjustment at a depth step

↑ **Parent:** [Step-trapped topographic Rossby wave](step-trapped-topographic-rossby-wave.md)

A long disturbance trapped on a depth step is sometimes called a double [Kelvin wave](kelvin-wave.md). With $H_+>H_-$, $f>0$ and $c_\pm=\sqrt{gH_\pm}$, cross-step [geostrophic balance](geostrophic-balance.md) and the along-step acceleration yield the transport equation shown for a background height jump $\eta_0\operatorname{sgn}(x)$. Its zero-initial-amplitude solution is $A=\eta_0[\operatorname{sgn}(x)-\operatorname{sgn}(x-(c_+-c_-)t)]$. The adjusted region diverts cross-step [volume flux](volumetric-flow-rate.md) along the step; the propagating front still carries flux. This outer approximation does not resolve fast adjustment at the fronts.
