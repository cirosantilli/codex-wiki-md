# Homomorphism of lattices

↑ **Parent:** [Lattice](lattice.md)

A homomorphism between [lattices](lattice.md) preserves binary joins and meets: $f(x\vee y)=f(x)\vee f(y)$ and $f(x\wedge y)=f(x)\wedge f(y)$. It is order preserving, since $x\leq y$ implies $x=x\wedge y$ and hence $f(x)=f(x)\wedge f(y)$. For a map into the two-element lattice, the inverse image of zero is a [lattice ideal](lattice-ideal.md), and meet preservation says this ideal is [prime](prime-number.md): if a meet belongs to it, at least one factor does. Conversely the indicator of the complement of a [prime](prime-number.md) [lattice ideal](lattice-ideal.md) preserves both operations. Preservation of distinguished top and bottom elements is an additional requirement for homomorphisms of bounded lattices.
