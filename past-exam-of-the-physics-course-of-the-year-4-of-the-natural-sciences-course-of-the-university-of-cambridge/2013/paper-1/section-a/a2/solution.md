<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

For independent [Poisson random variables](../../../../../poisson-distribution.md) with known expected counts $\mu_i$, the model variance is $\mu_i$. The [Pearson chi-squared statistic](../../../../../pearson-chi-squared-statistic.md) is therefore

$$
\chi_P^2=\sum_i\frac{(n_i-\mu_i)^2}{\mu_i}
=\frac{121}{20}+\frac{121}{35}+\frac{144}{44}+\frac{49}{36}+\frac{144}{24}+\frac{144}{11}
\simeq\boxed{33.23.}
$$

There are six independently specified bins and no parameters fitted from them, giving approximately six [degrees of freedom](../../../../../degree-of-freedom.md). This is far above its expected value six; the approximate [chi-squared distribution](../../../../../chi-squared-distribution.md) tail [probability](../../../../../probability.md) is $9.5\times10^{-6}$. **The discrepancies are much too large for a good Poisson-noise fit.** Replacing model variances by the observed-count estimates gives the alternative statistic $30.92$, with the same qualitative conclusion. There is no fitted normalization to justify subtracting a degree of freedom or rescaling the model to the observed total.
