<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

One conduction [electron](../../../../../electron.md) per atom gives number density $n=\rho N_A/M\simeq8.50\times10^{28}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$. Filling two spin states per [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) state in the three-dimensional [Fermi gas](../../../../../fermi-gas.md) gives $k_F=(3\pi^2n)^{1/3}$ and

$$
\boxed{E_F=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_e}(3\pi^2n)^{2/3}\simeq7.05\,\mathrm{eV},\qquad
T_F=E_F/k_B\simeq8.18\times10^4\,\mathrm K.}
$$

The [Fermi temperature](../../../../../fermi-temperature.md) greatly exceeds ordinary [temperatures](../../../../../temperature-split.md), explaining why these conduction [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) are degenerate rather than a classical Maxwell gas. The numerical result uses the stipulated free-electron band and bare [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [mass](../../../../../mass.md), rather than a detailed copper band structure.
