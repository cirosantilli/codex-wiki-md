<h1 id="section-b/b7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](../b7.md)

The [proper time](../../../../../proper-time.md) satisfies $dt/d\tau=\gamma(u)$. The collinear transformation from the Earth frame into the instantaneous rest frame gives proper acceleration $f=\gamma^3du/dt$, so

$$
\frac{du}{d\tau}=\frac{f}{\gamma^2}=f\left(1-\frac{u^2}{c^2}\right).
$$

Starting from rest, integration gives $\operatorname{artanh}(u/c)=f\tau/c$. Hence

$$
\boxed{u=c\tanh(f\tau/c),\qquad t=\frac cf\sinh(f\tau/c).}
$$

Since $dx/d\tau=u\gamma=c\sinh(f\tau/c)$, a further integration gives

$$
\boxed{x(\tau)=\frac{c^2}{f}\left[\cosh(f\tau/c)-1\right].}
$$

Using $\tau=10$ Julian years and $f=1\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ gives $f\tau/c\simeq1.053$, so the star is **about $5.77$ light years away** in the Earth frame. The Earth-frame travel time is about $11.95$ years and the final speed is about $0.783c$. The model accelerates toward the star throughout; adding a braking leg would be a different itinerary.
