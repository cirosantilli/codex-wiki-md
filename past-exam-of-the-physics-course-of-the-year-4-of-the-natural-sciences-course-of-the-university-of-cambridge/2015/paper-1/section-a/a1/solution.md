<h1 id="section-a/a1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A1](../a1.md)

For an infinite cubic [potential well](../../../../../potential-well.md) of side $L$, the one-particle [energy eigenvalues](../../../../../energy-eigenvalue-split.md) are $E_{n_xn_yn_z}=E_0(n_x^2+n_y^2+n_z^2)$, where $E_0=\pi^2\hbar^2/(2mL^2)$ and each quantum number is a positive integer. The lowest spatial orbital is $(1,1,1)$, with [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $3E_0$ and orbital degeneracy one. The next [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is $6E_0$, with three orbitals given by permutations of $(2,1,1)$.

Spin-zero identical particles are [bosons](../../../../../boson.md). Both can occupy the lowest orbital, so **the two-boson [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) is nondegenerate**. The first excited total [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is $9E_0$: one particle occupies the lowest orbital and one an excited orbital. Symmetrizing the two-particle [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) gives one state for each of the three excited orbitals, so **the first-excited degeneracy is three**.

Spin-half identical particles are [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md), so the total state must be antisymmetric. Two particles in the same lowest orbital have a symmetric spatial [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) and must form the unique [spin singlet state](../../../../../spin-singlet-state.md). Thus **the [fermion](../../../../../fermion.md) [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) is also nondegenerate**. At the first excited [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), each choice of excited spatial orbital permits a symmetric spatial state with a [singlet state](../../../../../singlet-state.md), or an antisymmetric spatial state with a three-dimensional [triplet state](../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{g_{\rm ground}=1,\qquad g_{\rm first}=3(1+3)=12\quad\text{for spin }\tfrac12.}
$$

The spin multiplicity is essential: counting only the three orbital choices would omit allowed triplet states. The result assumes ordinary spin-statistics, an unsplit spin degeneracy and the hard-wall cube model.
