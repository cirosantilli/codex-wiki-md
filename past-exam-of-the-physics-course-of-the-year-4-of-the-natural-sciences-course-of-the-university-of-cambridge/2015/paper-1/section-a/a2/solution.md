<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

The hard wall imposes $\psi(0)=0$, while the increasing linear potential requires decay as $x\to\infty$. Set

$$
\ell=\left(\frac{\hbar^2}{2m\beta}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
\xi=\frac x\ell-\frac E{\beta\ell}.
$$

The stationary [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) becomes $d^2\psi/d\xi^2-\xi\psi=0$. The growing [Airy function](../../../../../airy-function.md) solution is excluded, leaving

$$
\boxed{\psi_n(x)\propto\operatorname{Ai}(x/\ell-z_n),\quad
E_n=\beta\ell z_n,\quad\operatorname{Ai}(-z_n)=0.}
$$

This is the [quantum bouncer](../../../../../quantum-bouncer.md). For the first four states, $z_n\simeq2.3381,4.0879,5.5206,6.7867$. Their interior node counts are respectively **zero, one, two and three**. Each [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) starts at zero at the wall, oscillates in the classically allowed interval, and crosses smoothly into an exponentially decaying tail beyond the turning point $x_n=E_n/\beta=z_n\ell$. The turning point is not an extra hard wall: the [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) generally remains nonzero there and beyond it.
