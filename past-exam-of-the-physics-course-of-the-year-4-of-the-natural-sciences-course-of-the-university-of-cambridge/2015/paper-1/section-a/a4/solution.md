<h1 id="section-a/a4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A4](../a4.md)

For a homogeneous ideal gas in a two-dimensional box of area $A$, with dispersion $\epsilon=p^2/(2m)$ and one internal state, the [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md) is $g(\epsilon)=Am/(2\pi\hbar^2)$. The excited-particle density at [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) $T>0$ is

$$
n_{\rm ex}=\frac{m}{2\pi\hbar^2}\int_0^\infty\frac{d\epsilon}{z^{-1}e^{\epsilon/(k_BT)}-1}
=-\frac{mk_BT}{2\pi\hbar^2}\ln(1-z),\qquad z=e^{\mu/(k_BT)}<1.
$$

As $\mu\to0^-$, this diverges. Hence at every fixed positive [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) the [excited states](../../../../../excited-state.md) can accommodate any finite areal density with $\mu<0$; they never saturate at a finite critical density. **There is no finite-temperature [Bose-Einstein condensation](../../../../../bose-einstein-condensation.md) in the homogeneous two-dimensional ideal gas thermodynamic limit.**

The low-energy logarithmic divergence is the reason, not merely the small number of spatial dimensions. A finite box has a low-energy cutoff and a crossover; a trap changes the [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md) and can change the conclusion. Condensation at zero [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and an interacting gas's [Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition](../../../../../berezinskii-kosterlitz-thouless-transition.md) are separate issues. The proof assumes the uniform quadratic-dispersion model, rather than every conceivable two-dimensional trapping potential.
