<h1 id="section-a/a6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A6](../a6.md)

For total relativistic [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $E=\gamma mc^2$, the speed is $v=c\sqrt{1-(mc^2/E)^2}$. Both beam energies are enormously greater than the rest [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), so expanding the square root avoids obscuring the small difference:

$$
\Delta v\simeq\frac c2(mc^2)^2\left(\frac1{E_1^2}-\frac1{E_2^2}\right).
$$

With all energies in GeV, $mc^2=0.9383$, $E_1=4000$ and $E_2=6500$, giving

$$
\boxed{\Delta v\simeq5.12\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The deficits below the speed of light are about $8.25\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $3.12\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The large [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) increase therefore produces only a small speed increase; the [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) mainly changes the [Lorentz factor](../../../../../lorentz-factor.md). These numbers use the quoted beam energies as total energies. Interpreting them as kinetic energies requires adding the rest [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), with only a tiny correction at these scales.
