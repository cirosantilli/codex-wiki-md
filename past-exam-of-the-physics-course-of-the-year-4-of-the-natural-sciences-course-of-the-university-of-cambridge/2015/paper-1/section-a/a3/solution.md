<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

In the [sudden approximation](../../../../../sudden-approximation.md), the nuclear charge changes before the electronic [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) has time to evolve. Immediately afterward the state is still the original hydrogenic 1s [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md), now expanded in the eigenstates of the new Coulomb Hamiltonian. The final-ground-state [probability](../../../../../probability.md) is the squared overlap, rather than one minus an [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) change.

For initial and final charges $Z_1,Z_2$, the normalized 1s overlap is

$$
\langle1s,Z_2|1s,Z_1\rangle
=\frac{4(Z_1Z_2)^{3/2}}{a_0^3}\int_0^\infty r^2e^{-(Z_1+Z_2)r/a_0}\,dr
=\frac{8(Z_1Z_2)^{3/2}}{(Z_1+Z_2)^3}.
$$

Using $Z_1=1,Z_2=2$ gives

$$
\boxed{P_{1s}=\left(\frac{16\sqrt2}{27}\right)^2=\frac{512}{729}\simeq0.7023.}
$$

The remaining [probability](../../../../../probability.md) includes higher bound $ns$ states and the $s$-wave ionization continuum. In this central, instantaneous model the initial orbital has $l=0,m=0$, and angular orthogonality excludes states with $l\ne0$. The electronic spin is unchanged. Nuclear recoil and interactions with the emitted decay particles are outside this leading sudden-charge-change approximation.
