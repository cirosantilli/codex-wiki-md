<h1 id="section-a/a6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A6](../a6.md)

For the [focal length of a rotating liquid mirror](../../../../../focal-length-of-a-rotating-liquid-mirror.md), consider steady rigid rotation at [angular frequency](../../../../../angular-frequency.md) $\Omega=2\pi\nu$. The free surface is an [equipotential](../../../../../equipotential.md) of $gz-\Omega^2r^2/2$. Hence

$$
z-z_0=\frac{\Omega^2r^2}{2g}.
$$

A paraboloidal mirror has $z-z_0=r^2/(4f)$, giving

$$
\boxed{f=\frac{g}{2\Omega^2}=\frac{g}{8\pi^2\nu^2}\simeq3.1\,\mathrm m.}
$$

The numerical estimate uses the conventional standard acceleration $g_n=9.80665\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, rather than an asserted local measurement or a recovered handbook entry.[Footnote 23: conventional standard acceleration of free fall](../../../../../nist-guide-to-the-si-footnotes-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology.md) The mercury [density](../../../../../density.md) cancels; the ideal surface shape follows from gravity and rotation.
