<h1 id="section-a/a9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A9](../a9.md)

For [hyperfine coupling for a spin-one nucleus](../../../../../hyperfine-coupling-for-a-spin-one-nucleus.md), let the [electron spin](../../../../../electron-spin.md) be $s=1/2$ and the [nuclear spin](../../../../../nuclear-spin.md) $I=1$. First couple $\mathbf j=\mathbf L+\mathbf s$, giving $j=L\pm1/2$, then $\mathbf F=\mathbf j+\mathbf I$, giving $F=|j-I|,|j-I|+1,\ldots,j+I$.

| $L$ | $j$ | Allowed $F$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $1$ | $1/2$ | $1/2,3/2$ |
| $1$ | $3/2$ | $1/2,3/2,5/2$ |
| $2$ | $3/2$ | $1/2,3/2,5/2$ |
| $2$ | $5/2$ | $3/2,5/2,7/2$ |

For each $F$, the projection is $m_F=-F,-F+1,\ldots,F$; electron projections similarly satisfy $m_j=-j,\ldots,j$. Summing $2F+1$ gives 18 states for $L=1$ and 30 for $L=2$, agreeing with $(2L+1)(2s+1)(2I+1)$.

**Notation qualification:** if the printed leading “2” denotes the principal [quantum number](../../../../../quantum-number.md), a hydrogenic $2d$ bound state does not exist because $L\leq n-1$; a physical $d$ state needs $n\geq3$. If a doublet term symbol $^{2}P$ or $^{2}D$ was intended, its leading 2 instead means $2s+1$, and the angular-[momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) table applies without fixing $n$. The answers above use the explicitly specified $L$ values, while retaining this ambiguity.[Three-dimensional angular momentum and hydrogenic quantum-number restrictions](../../../../../quantum-mechanics-by-david-tong.md)
