<h1 id="section-b/b5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B5](../b5.md)

Nonrelativistic [momentum conservation](../../../../../momentum-conservation.md) gives $\mathbf u=\mathbf v_1+\mathbf v_2$, while an [elastic collision](../../../../../elastic-collision.md) conserves [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md), $u^2=v_1^2+v_2^2$. Squaring the [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) equation yields $\mathbf v_1\cdot\mathbf v_2=0$. **The two nonzero outgoing velocities are perpendicular.** In the degenerate case where one outgoing particle is at rest, its direction is undefined.

For relativistic collisions conserve total [four-momentum](../../../../../four-momentum.md), with $E=\gamma mc^2$, $\mathbf p=\gamma m\mathbf v$ and $E^2-p^2c^2=m^2c^4$. Rest energies must be included; for [photons](../../../../../photon.md) $E=pc$.

For the [symmetric relativistic equal-mass scattering angle](../../../../../symmetric-relativistic-equal-mass-scattering-angle.md), the incident [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) is $mc\sqrt{\alpha(\alpha+2)}$. Energy conservation gives each final particle $E_f=(1+\alpha/2)mc^2$, hence $p_f=(mc/2)\sqrt{\alpha(\alpha+4)}$. Transverse momenta cancel and $2p_f\cos\theta=p_{\rm in}$, giving

$$
\cos\theta=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha+4}},\qquad\boxed{\tan\theta=\sqrt{\frac2{\alpha+2}}.}
$$

The nonrelativistic limit has $\theta\to45^\circ$, so the outgoing angle is $90^\circ$ as above.

For the [symmetric two-photon annihilation angle](../../../../../symmetric-two-photon-annihilation-angle.md) in the specified [electron-positron annihilation](../../../../../electron-positron-annihilation.md), each [photon](../../../../../photon.md) has energy $E_\gamma=(\alpha+2)m_ec^2/2$. [Momentum conservation](../../../../../momentum-conservation.md) gives $2(E_\gamma/c)\cos\phi=m_ec\sqrt{\alpha(\alpha+2)}$, so

$$
\cos\phi=\sqrt{\frac\alpha{\alpha+2}},\qquad\boxed{\tan\phi=\sqrt{\frac2\alpha}\quad(\alpha>0).}
$$

As $\alpha\to0^+$ the two [photons](../../../../../photon.md) become back-to-back, transverse to the limiting incident direction; as $\alpha\to\infty$ they are forward collimated. Equal [photon](../../../../../photon.md) energies follow from the specified equal angles, and are not a general property of annihilation in a moving laboratory frame.
