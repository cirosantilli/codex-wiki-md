<h1 id="section-b/b2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Equating the initial and final [internal energies](../../../../../../internal-energy-split.md), with the same additive constant,

$$
\frac32Nk_BT_f-\frac{aN^2}{V_0}=\frac32Nk_BT_i-\frac{3aN^2}{V_0},
$$

so

$$
\boxed{T_f=T_i-\frac{4aN}{3k_BV_0}.}
$$

This is [cooling in free expansion of a van der Waals gas](../../../../../../cooling-in-free-expansion-of-a-van-der-waals-gas.md). The gas cools because separating attractive particles raises their potential energy at the expense of [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md). The excluded-volume coefficient $b$ cancels from this caloric model. The result assumes a homogeneous equilibrium gas with $V_0/3>Nb$ and positive $T_f$; a two-phase final state would require a coexistence calculation rather than this homogeneous formula. The ideal-gas limit $a=0$ has no [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) change.
