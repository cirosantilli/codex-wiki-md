<h1 id="section-b/b1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $y=g\mu_BB/(k_BT)$. Summing the [Boltzmann factors](../../../../../../boltzmann-factor.md) gives the one-atom [partition function](../../../../../../canonical-partition-function.md)

$$
Z_1=\sum_{m=-J}^Je^{-ym}=\frac{\sinh[(J+1/2)y]}{\sinh(y/2)}.
$$

Since $\langle m\rangle=-\partial_y\log Z_1$, the [Brillouin magnetization formula](../../../../../../brillouin-magnetization-formula.md) is

$$
\boxed{\langle\mu_z\rangle=g\mu_B\left[(J+\tfrac12)\coth((J+\tfrac12)y)-\tfrac12\coth(y/2)\right],\qquad M=N\langle\mu_z\rangle.}
$$

Here $M$ is the total [magnetic moment](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) used by the question; the [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) per unit volume is $M/V$. For independent distinguishable atoms, $Z_N=Z_1^N$, with no indistinguishability factor.

Putting $J=1/2$ yields

$$
\boxed{M=Ng\mu_B[\coth y-\tfrac12\coth(y/2)]=\frac{Ng\mu_B}{2}\tanh(y/2).}
$$

The apparent singularities of the hyperbolic cotangents at $B=0$ cancel; the finite sum makes the zero-field limit explicit.[Chapter 1: canonical partition functions and independent two-state systems](../../../../../../statistical-physics-by-david-tong.md)
