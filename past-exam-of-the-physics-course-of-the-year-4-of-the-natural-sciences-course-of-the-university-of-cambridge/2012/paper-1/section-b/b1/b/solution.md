<h1 id="section-b/b1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [transverse stiffness of a stretched spring](../../../../../../transverse-stiffness-of-a-stretched-spring.md) follows because a [spring](../../../../../../spring.md) of [length](../../../../../../length.md) $2l$ develops transverse [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) $T_0\Delta y/(2l)$ to first order. Its extension changes only at second order, so the leading transverse stiffness comes from its existing [tension](../../../../../../tension.md), not from that extension change. Here $T_0/(2l)=k/2$, giving

$$
m\begin{pmatrix}\ddot Y_1\\\ddot Y_2\end{pmatrix}
=-\frac k2\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}Y_1\\Y_2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\boxed{\omega_{y,+}=\sqrt{k/(2m)},\quad\omega_{y,-}=\sqrt{3k/(2m)}.}
$$

The mode vectors are again $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$. Treating the [springs](../../../../../../spring.md) as unstretched would incorrectly remove this linear restoring [force](../../../../../../force-split.md).
