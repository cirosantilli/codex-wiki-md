<h1 id="section-a/a5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A5](../a5.md)

The [geostationary gravitational clock gain](../../../../../geostationary-gravitational-clock-gain.md) uses only the potential contribution, as requested. In the weak field, $d\tau/dt\simeq1+\Phi/c^2$ and $\Phi=-GM/r$. The geostationary [radius](../../../../../radius.md) follows from $GM/r_g^3=\Omega_\oplus^2$. Taking $GM\simeq gR^2$ with $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, $R=6.4\times10^6\,\mathrm m$ and [sidereal day](../../../../../sidereal-day.md) $86164\,\mathrm s$ gives $r_g\simeq4.23\times10^7\,\mathrm m$.

$$
\frac{\Delta\dot\tau}{\dot\tau}\simeq\frac{GM}{c^2}\left(\frac1R-\frac1{r_g}\right)
=5.93\times10^{-10},\qquad
\boxed{\Delta\tau_{\rm day}\simeq+51\,\mathrm{\mu s}.}
$$

The satellite clock gains time because it is at higher [gravitational potential](../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md). This is not the combined gravitational-plus-kinematic correction; the orbital and surface rotational [velocities](../../../../../velocity.md) are deliberately outside the requested potential contribution.
