<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

For an [elastic eccentric impact of a thin disc](../../../../../elastic-eccentric-impact-of-a-thin-disc.md), take upward [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) as positive and let $J$ be the short upward [impulse](../../../../../impulse.md). The disc's [moment of inertia](../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) about a horizontal diameter is $I=Ma^2/4$. With impact offset $d=a/2$,

$$
v_C=-u+J/M,\qquad \omega=Jd/I.
$$

The contact point has upward [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) $v_C+d\omega$. Unit restitution against the fixed post requires that [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) to be $+u$, so

$$
J\left(\frac1M+\frac{d^2}I\right)=2u.
$$

Since $I=Md^2$, **$J=Mu$ and the centre's instantaneous [speed](../../../../../speed.md) is zero**. The disc rotates with $\omega=2u/a$; its [rotational kinetic energy](../../../../../rotational-kinetic-energy.md) $I\omega^2/2=Mu^2/2$ equals the entire initial [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). [Gravity](../../../../../gravity.md)'s [impulse](../../../../../impulse.md) during the impact is neglected. Using the polar-axis [moment of inertia](../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) would describe the wrong rotation axis.
