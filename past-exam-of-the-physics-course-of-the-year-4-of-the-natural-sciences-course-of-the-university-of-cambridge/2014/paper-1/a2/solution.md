<h1 id="a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

Let $a$ be the cube's side and $z$ a small upward displacement. Its displaced water [volume](../../../../volume.md) decreases by $a^2z$, so the change in [buoyancy](../../../../buoyancy.md) is $-\rho_wga^2z$. With cube [mass](../../../../mass.md) $m=0.9\rho_wa^3$,

$$
m\ddot z=-\rho_wga^2z,\qquad
\boxed{\omega^2=\frac{g}{0.9a},\qquad T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.9a}{g}}=0.426\,\mathrm s.}
$$

The displacement must be small enough that the upper face remains above water and the bottom remains submerged. This [buoyancy](../../../../buoyancy.md) estimate neglects fluid [added mass](../../../../added-mass.md), [damping](../../../../damping.md) and melting.
