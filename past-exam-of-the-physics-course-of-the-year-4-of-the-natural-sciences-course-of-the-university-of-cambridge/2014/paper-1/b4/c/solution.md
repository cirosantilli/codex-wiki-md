<h1 id="b4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For [proper-time acceleration by reflected laser light](../../../../../proper-time-acceleration-by-reflected-laser-light.md), the spacecraft receives [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) $(hf+hf_r)/c=2hf/[c(1+\beta)]$ per incident [photon](../../../../../photon.md). Combining with the impact rate gives

$$
F=\frac{2Rhf}{c}\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta},\qquad
\frac{d(\gamma\beta)}{dt}=\frac1{\tau_0}\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta},\qquad
\tau_0=\frac{Mc^2}{2Rhf}.
$$

Because $dt/d\tau=\gamma$ and $d(\gamma\beta)/d\beta=\gamma^3$,

$$
\gamma^2\frac{d\beta}{d\tau}=\frac1{\tau_0}\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{d\beta}{d\tau}=\frac{(1-\beta)^2}{\tau_0}.
$$

Integrating from rest gives $1/(1-\beta)-1=\tau/\tau_0$, hence

$$
\boxed{\beta(\tau)=\frac{\tau}{\tau+\tau_0}.}
$$

The mirror's [rest mass](../../../../../invariant-mass.md) is assumed constant and the laser illuminates it continuously; [diffraction](../../../../../diffraction.md) losses, incomplete interception and microscopic recoil are neglected.
