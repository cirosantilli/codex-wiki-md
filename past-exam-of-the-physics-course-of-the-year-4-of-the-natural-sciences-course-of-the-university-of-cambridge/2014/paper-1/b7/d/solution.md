<h1 id="b7/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Treat the transparent plate as a [phase plate](../../../../../phase-plate.md) at normal incidence. Its extra optical path is $(n-1)50\lambda$. An unchanged [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) pattern requires an integer number of full [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) cycles:

$$
50(n-1)=m\in\mathbb Z,\qquad n=1+\frac m{50}.
$$

For [full-cycle phase-plate invisibility](../../../../../full-cycle-phase-plate-invisibility.md) in an ordinary nontrivial material with $n>1$, the two smallest are **$n=1.02$ and $1.04$**. If the optically null case $n=1$ is admitted, the first two nondecreasing-$n\ge1$ values are $1$ and $1.02$. A different allowed range of [refractive indices](../../../../../refractive-index.md) would need to be specified before calling these the absolute smallest possibilities.
