<h1 id="a9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A9](../a9.md)

Approximate the beam as uniform over its circular cross section. Its mean [intensity](../../../../intensity-physics.md) is $I=P/[\pi(D/2)^2]=1.27\times10^6\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. For the [root mean square magnetic field from beam power](../../../../root-mean-square-magnetic-field-from-beam-power.md) in a vacuum electromagnetic wave, $I=cB_{\rm rms}^2/\mu_0$, so

$$
\boxed{B_{\rm rms}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_0P}{c\pi(D/2)^2}}=7.3\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm T.}
$$

This is an rms field, not the peak sinusoidal [amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md). A Gaussian-beam peak would need a stated diameter convention.
