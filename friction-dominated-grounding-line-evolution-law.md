# Friction-dominated grounding-line evolution law

↑ **Parent:** [Grounding line](grounding-line.md)

For a bed depth $b=\alpha x$, a thin [subglacial till](subglacial-till.md) layer of thickness $l$ is idealized by [Newtonian till lubrication](newtonian-till-lubrication.md), with [dynamic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) $\lambda\mu$ and basal drag $\lambda\mu u/l$. Neglecting longitudinal stress divergence in the bulk yields $u=-(l/\lambda)(g/\nu)Hh_x$. Retaining the [unbuttressed Newtonian grounding-line stress](unbuttressed-newtonian-grounding-line-stress.md) condition as boundary data and differentiating [ice-sheet flotation](ice-sheet-flotation.md) along the moving [grounding line](grounding-line.md) gives

$$
\left(\alpha\frac{\rho_w}{\rho}-H_x\right)\dot x_G=\frac l\lambda\frac g\nu Hh_x(h_x+\alpha)-\frac{g'H^2}{8\nu}.
$$

Indeed, [mass conservation](mass-conservation.md) gives $H_t=-uH_x-Hu_x$, $H_x=h_x+\alpha$, and [ice-sheet flotation](ice-sheet-flotation.md) gives $H_t=(\alpha\rho_w/\rho-H_x)\dot x_G$. This is a formal local friction closure, not a uniformly valid removal of membrane stress near every grounding line. If the coefficient of $\dot x_G$ vanishes, it is an implicit compatibility condition and cannot be divided to obtain a finite speed.
