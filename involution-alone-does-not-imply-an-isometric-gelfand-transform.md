# Involution alone does not imply an isometric Gelfand transform

↑ **Parent:** [Gelfand representation](gelfand-representation.md)

The complex [dual numbers](dual-number.md) $\mathbb C[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$, with norm $\|a+b\varepsilon\|=|a|+|b|$ and involution $a+b\varepsilon\mapsto\bar a+\bar b\varepsilon$, is a commutative unital [Banach algebra](banach-algebra-split.md) with an isometric involution. Its sole [character of an algebra](character-of-an-algebra.md) kills $\varepsilon$, so the [Gelfand transform](gelfand-representation.md) is neither injective nor isometric. The [C-star identity](c-star-identity.md) excludes this example and gives the [Commutative Gelfand--Naimark theorem](commutative-gelfand-naimark-theorem.md). Even preservation of the involution on characters can fail without that identity: on $\mathbb C^2$, the involution $(a,b)^*=(\bar b,\bar a)$ exchanges the two evaluation characters.
