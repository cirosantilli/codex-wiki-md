# Landau criterion

↑ **Parent:** [Superfluid](superfluid.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau_criterion)

The [Landau criterion](landau-criterion.md) asks whether creating an excitation can lower a moving [superfluid](superfluid.md)'s [energy](energy-split.md). For a weakly coupled, heavy obstacle moving through a uniform [liquid](liquid.md) initially at zero [temperature](temperature-split.md), let $\epsilon(p)>0$ be the excitation [energy](energy-split.md) in the fluid rest frame, with [momentum](momentum-split.md) magnitude $p$. Transferring [momentum](momentum-split.md) $\mathbf p$ from an obstacle moving at [velocity](velocity.md) $\mathbf v$ supplies, to leading order in its negligible recoil, [energy](energy-split.md) $\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf p$. Emission requires

$$
\epsilon(p)-\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf p\leq0.
$$

For an [isotropic](isotropy.md) [dispersion relation](dispersion-relation-split.md), the easiest direction is parallel to $\mathbf v$, giving the necessary kinematic condition

$$
v\geq v_L,\qquad v_L=\inf_{p>0}\frac{\epsilon(p)}p.
$$

An [infimum](infimum.md) includes cases in which the limiting slope occurs only as $p\to0$. A finite-mass obstacle of [mass](mass.md) $M$ instead loses $\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf p-p^2/(2M)$, so the corresponding emission inequality includes the extra positive recoil term $p^2/(2M)$ on the left. This is an [energy](energy-split.md) and [momentum](momentum-split.md) balance, not a calculation of an emission rate.

The [Landau criterion](landau-criterion.md) excludes spontaneous emission into the specified spectrum below its threshold. Above it, kinematic permission alone does not guarantee strong coupling or rapid decay. It is not a universal observed flow threshold: walls, channel geometry and [quantum vortex](quantum-vortex.md) nucleation can enable other decay routes, and finite [temperature](temperature-split.md) permits scattering from existing excitations. Using only bulk [phonons](phonon.md) and [rotons](roton.md) leaves those processes outside the model.

Landau's 1941 [energy](energy-split.md) and [momentum](momentum-split.md) argument used a [roton](roton.md) approximation different from the finite-[momentum](momentum-split.md) minimum illustrated below.[Landau, §§2,4, pp.500–504](the-theory-of-superfluidity-of-helium-ii-by-lev-davidovich-landau.md)

Landau received his [Nobel Prize in Physics in 1962](nobel-prize-in-physics-in-1962.md) while hospitalized in Moscow after a car accident.[Contemporary report, 12 December 1962, opening paragraph](soviet-jewish-scientist-presented-with-nobel-prize-in-hospital-bed-by-jewish-telegraphic-agency.md)

For a deliberately simple [Bose-Einstein condensation](bose-einstein-condensation.md) comparison, stipulate an ideal free-particle excitation law $\epsilon=p^2/(2m)$. Then $\epsilon/p=p/(2m)$ has [infimum](infimum.md) zero. Macroscopic one-particle occupation therefore supplies no positive emission threshold by this argument alone. By contrast a linear [phonon](phonon.md) law $\epsilon=cp$ yields $v_L=c$. The [dispersion relation](dispersion-relation-split.md) and the collective response contain information that the occupation criterion alone does not supply; [Bose-Einstein condensation](bose-einstein-condensation.md) and thermodynamic [superfluidity](superfluidity.md) must be tested separately.

**[Figure 5](#image-the-landau-criterion-minimizes-a-slope-from-the-origin). The Landau criterion minimizes a slope from the origin**. Original dimensionless [dispersion relation](dispersion-relation-split.md), not measured [helium](helium.md) data or a reconstruction of the 1941 spectrum. Define $q=p/p_*$, $e=\epsilon/E_*$ and $v_*=E_*/p_*$. The stipulated law is $e(q)=\min\{2q,\,0.6+2(q-2)^2\}$. Its sharp branch joins are deliberate simplifications. The [roton](roton.md) [energy](energy-split.md) minimum is at $q=2$, while the minimum of $e/q$ is at $q_L=\sqrt{4.3}\simeq2.07364$. The tangent slope gives $v_L/v_*=4(\sqrt{4.3}-2)\simeq0.294577$, below the low-[momentum](momentum-split.md) [phonon](phonon.md) slope 2.

To check the picture algebraically, on the stipulated [roton](roton.md) branch,

$$
\frac{d}{dq}\frac{e(q)}q
=\frac{qe'(q)-e(q)}{q^2},\qquad
qe'(q)-e(q)=2q^2-8.6.
$$

Thus tangency occurs at $q_L=\sqrt{4.3}$, slightly above the [roton](roton.md) [energy](energy-split.md) minimum. At an [energy](energy-split.md) minimum $e'=0$, but a tangent through the origin needs $e'=e/q>0$. Minimizing [energy](energy-split.md) and minimizing the [energy](energy-split.md)-to-[momentum](momentum-split.md) ratio are different operations. This geometric construction makes the criterion useful even when the full [dispersion relation](dispersion-relation-split.md) is supplied numerically rather than by a simple formula.
