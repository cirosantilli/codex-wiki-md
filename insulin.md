# Insulin

↑ **Parent:** [Protein primary structure](protein-primary-structure.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin)

[Insulin](insulin.md) is a [protein](protein-split.md) [hormone](hormone.md). Its mature form has two [polypeptide chains](polypeptide.md); identifying their sequences and [protein disulfide bonds](protein-disulfide-bond.md) provided the motivating example in [Sanger's insulin sequencing experiment](sanger-s-insulin-sequencing-experiment.md). See [Cooper: insulin as a two-chain hormone, figure 2.16](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9879/).

A meal illustrates the [insulin](insulin.md) side of [blood glucose](blood-sugar.md) [homeostasis](homeostasis.md): rising [glucose](glucose.md) stimulates [beta cells](beta-cell.md) in the [pancreatic islets](pancreatic-islets.md), and released [insulin](insulin.md) favors uptake and storage of fuel. [Alpha cells](alpha-cell.md) produce [glucagon](glucagon.md), while [delta cells](delta-cell.md) produce [somatostatin](somatostatin.md), which inhibits release of both [insulin](insulin.md) and [glucagon](glucagon.md). The [pancreas](pancreas.md) therefore contains interacting sensor and secretory [biological cells](biological-cell-split.md), rather than one undifferentiated source of [hormones](hormone.md). [17.9 The Endocrine Pancreas](anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/17-9-the-endocrine-pancreas.md)

[Insulin](insulin.md) and [glucagon](glucagon.md) are [peptide hormones](peptide-hormone.md), built from [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md) joined by [peptide bonds](peptide-bond.md); [glucose](glucose.md) is a small [carbohydrate](carbohydrate.md) [molecule](molecule-split.md). A [hormone](hormone.md) changes [signal transduction](signal-transduction-split.md), not the identity of the fuel it regulates. Human mature [insulin](insulin.md) has 51 [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md) in two [polypeptide chains](polypeptide.md). [Beta cells](beta-cell.md) process [proinsulin](proinsulin.md) to [insulin](insulin.md) and [C-peptide](c-peptide.md), storing the products in [secretory granules](secretory-granule.md) for [exocytosis](exocytosis.md). Increased [glucose](glucose.md) [metabolism](metabolism.md) raises the [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md)-to-[ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) [ratio](ratio.md), tending to close [ATP-sensitive potassium channels](atp-sensitive-potassium-channel.md); [depolarization](depolarization.md) and entry of [calcium](calcium.md) [ions](ion.md) then trigger [exocytosis](exocytosis.md). This is an important triggering pathway, with additional amplification and modulatory inputs.[pp. 1, 4–8, 10–11](endotext/insulin-biosynthesis-secretion-structure-and-structure-activity-relationships.md)

**[Figure 12](#image-insulin-and-glucagon-couple-pancreatic-islets-to-different-tissue-responses). Insulin and glucagon couple pancreatic islets to different tissue responses**. Original qualitative [physiology](physiology-split.md) circuit. Each outer return closes a [negative feedback](negative-feedback.md) loop for [blood glucose](blood-sugar.md); the arrows summarize direction, not measured gain or timing. [Insulin receptor](insulin-receptor.md) signaling changes [GLUT4](glut4.md) distribution in [skeletal muscle](skeletal-muscle.md) and [adipose tissue](adipose-tissue.md), while the [glucagon receptor](glucagon-receptor.md) pathway principally supports [liver](liver.md) [glucose](glucose.md) output. The local interactions within [pancreatic islets](pancreatic-islets.md) explain why the two [hormones](hormone.md) are not independent switches.

Binding to the [insulin receptor](insulin-receptor.md), a [receptor tyrosine kinase](receptor-tyrosine-kinase.md), starts [phosphorylation](phosphorylation.md) of the [receptor](receptor-biochemistry.md) and [insulin receptor substrates](insulin-receptor-substrate.md). Recruitment of [phosphoinositide 3-kinase](phosphoinositide-3-kinase.md) and activation of [Akt](protein-kinase-b.md) transmit a major metabolic branch. In [skeletal muscle](skeletal-muscle.md) and [adipose tissue](adipose-tissue.md), that branch helps move [GLUT4](glut4.md) to the [cell membrane](cell-membrane-split.md), increasing [glucose](glucose.md) uptake by [facilitated diffusion](facilitated-diffusion.md). [GLUT4](glut4.md) is a [membrane transport protein](membrane-transport-protein.md), not an [insulin receptor](insulin-receptor.md). [Insulin](insulin.md) also favors [glycogenesis](glycogenesis.md) in [skeletal muscle](skeletal-muscle.md) and the [liver](liver.md), and inhibits [lipolysis](lipolysis.md) in [adipose tissue](adipose-tissue.md). In the [liver](liver.md), suppression of [glucose](glucose.md) output involves [enzyme](enzyme-split.md) regulation, [gene expression](gene-expression.md), and indirect changes in [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) supply. Its relative direct and indirect contributions are more complex than a single [Akt](protein-kinase-b.md) arrow. [Insulin signaling in health and disease by Alan R. Saltiel](insulin-signaling-in-health-and-disease-by-alan-r-saltiel.md)

[Insulin](insulin.md) is not a universal permission slip for [glucose](glucose.md) entry: [red blood cells](red-blood-cell.md) and much of the nervous system use uptake mechanisms that are not acutely dependent on [insulin](insulin.md). Nor does the [liver](liver.md) use the same [GLUT4](glut4.md) switch as [skeletal muscle](skeletal-muscle.md). This is why reduced [insulin](insulin.md) action can change [blood glucose](blood-sugar.md) dramatically without stopping all [glucose](glucose.md) uptake. [Introduction to Diabetes](the-genetic-landscape-of-diabetes/introduction-to-diabetes.md)

[Diabetes](diabetes.md) can disrupt production, response, and counterregulation. [Type 1 diabetes](type-1-diabetes.md) usually involves destruction of [beta cells](beta-cell.md) and severe loss of endogenous [insulin](insulin.md); [type 2 diabetes](type-2-diabetes.md) combines [insulin resistance](insulin-resistance.md) with insufficient [beta cell](beta-cell.md) compensation. High circulating [insulin](insulin.md) can therefore coexist with impaired [insulin](insulin.md) action. Inappropriate [glucagon](glucagon.md) release can add to excessive [liver](liver.md) [glucose](glucose.md) output, and the response to low [blood glucose](blood-sugar.md) can also be impaired: "more [glucagon](glucagon.md)" is not a complete description of [diabetes](diabetes.md). These distinctions explain mechanisms, rather than prescribe treatment.
