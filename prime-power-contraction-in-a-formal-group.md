# Prime-power contraction in a formal group

↑ **Parent:** [Formal group law](formal-group-law.md)

Let $R$ be the [valuation ring](valuation-ring.md) of a complete [non-Archimedean valued field](non-archimedean-valued-field.md) whose residue field has characteristic $p$, and let $\mathfrak m=\{x:|x|<1\}$. For a one-dimensional [formal group law](formal-group-law.md) over $R$, repeated addition gives $[p](X)=pX+\sum_{j\ge2}c_jX^j$ with $c_j\in R$. Hence $|[p](x)|\le\max(|p||x|,|x|^2)$. The series converges for $x\in\mathfrak m$ because its [coefficients](coefficient.md) are bounded and $|x|<1$.

For $x_0=x$ and $x_{n+1}=[p](x_n)$, choose $\rho=\max(|p|,|x|)<1$. The values remain in the ball $|x_n|\le|x|$, so $|x_n|\le\rho^n|x|\to0$. Associativity identifies $x_n=[p^n](x)$. The argument also works in equal characteristic $p$, where the linear [coefficient](coefficient.md) vanishes, and does not require a [discrete valuation](discrete-valuation.md). It proves contraction of [prime](prime-number-split.md)-power multiplication near the formal identity without assuming a characteristic-zero formal logarithm.
