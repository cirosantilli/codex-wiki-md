# Vector curl form of the divergence theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md)

For a continuously differentiable [vector field](vector-field.md) $v$ on a bounded sufficiently regular region $V$, apply the [divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md) to $a\times v$ for an arbitrary constant [vector](vector.md) $a$. The [vector calculus](vector-calculus.md) identity $\nabla\cdot(a\times v)=-a\cdot(\nabla\times v)$ and the cyclic [scalar triple product](scalar-triple-product.md) identity show that the dot products of both sides of the displayed formula with $a$ agree. Since $a$ is arbitrary, the vector integrals agree. Here $n$ is the outward unit [normal vector](normal-vector.md). Reversing the order on the boundary gives the equivalent positive-sign formula $\int n\times v\,dS=\int\nabla\times v\,dV$.
