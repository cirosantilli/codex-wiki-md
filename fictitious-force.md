# Fictitious force

↑ **Parent:** [Reference frame](reference-frame.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fictitious_force)

An inertial force is an additional term used to express [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) in an accelerating [reference frame](reference-frame.md). For origin acceleration $\mathbf A$ and axes with [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) $\boldsymbol\Omega$, the apparent force on a particle is

$$
\mathbf F_{\rm inertial}=-m\mathbf A-2m\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v'-m\dot{\boldsymbol\Omega}\times\mathbf r-m\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r).
$$

Here $\mathbf r$ and $\mathbf v'$ are measured relative to that frame. The rotation terms are the [Coriolis force](coriolis-force.md), [Euler force](euler-force.md) and [centrifugal force](centrifugal-force.md). Their values depend on the frame rather than on an additional physical interaction.
