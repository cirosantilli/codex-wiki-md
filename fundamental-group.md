# Fundamental group

↑ **Parent:** [Algebraic topology](algebraic-topology-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_group)

The [fundamental group](fundamental-group.md) $\pi_1(X,x_0)$ consists of [based loops](based-loop.md) modulo [based homotopy](based-homotopy.md). Multiplication follows one [based loop](based-loop.md) and then the other; the constant [based loop](based-loop.md) represents the identity, and [path reversal](path-reversal.md) gives the inverse. Passing to [homotopy classes](homotopy-class.md) makes this operation associative, as explained by [well-defined loop concatenation](well-defined-loop-concatenation.md).

Identify the [circle](circle.md) with $S^1=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\}$ and choose basepoint $1$. Its [based loops](based-loop.md)

$$
\gamma_n(s)=e^{2\pi i n s},\qquad 0\leq s\leq1,\quad n\in\mathbb Z,
$$

have [winding number](winding-number.md) $n$: positive $n$ counts counterclockwise turns, negative $n$ counts clockwise turns, and $n=0$ gives the constant [based loop](based-loop.md). Every [based loop](based-loop.md) on the [circle](circle.md) is joined by a [based homotopy](based-homotopy.md) to exactly one $\gamma_n$. Consequently

$$
\pi_1(S^1,1)\cong(\mathbb Z,+),\qquad
[\gamma_m*\gamma_n]=[\gamma_{m+n}].
$$

This is Hatcher's Theorem 1.7 in [Algebraic Topology, §1.1, pp. 29–30](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch1.pdf#page=9). For example, two counterclockwise turns followed by one clockwise turn represent $2+(-1)=1$, rather than three turns. The winding graph in the figure above makes the signed arithmetic visible.

A proof outline uses the [covering map](covering-space.md) $q:\mathbb R\to S^1$, $q(u)=e^{2\pi i u}$. By the [path lifting theorem](path-lifting-theorem.md), a [based loop](based-loop.md) has a unique lift starting at zero and ending at an [integer](integer.md) $n$. Interpolating its lift to $s\mapsto ns$ produces a [based homotopy](based-homotopy.md) to $\gamma_n$. The [homotopy lifting theorem for a covering map](homotopy-lifting-theorem-for-a-covering-map.md) keeps that endpoint unchanged during a [based homotopy](based-homotopy.md), proving uniqueness.

In the filled [disk](disk-mathematics.md), every [based loop](based-loop.md) $\gamma$ at $x_0$ has a [based homotopy](based-homotopy.md) to the constant [based loop](based-loop.md):

$$
K(s,t)=(1-t)\gamma(s)+t x_0.
$$

Both endpoints remain $x_0$, and the [convex set](convex-set.md) property of the [disk](disk-mathematics.md) keeps every intermediate point inside. Thus $\pi_1(D^2,x_0)$ is the [trivial group](trivial-group.md). Together with the [deformation retraction](deformation-retraction.md) above, this computes three concrete answers: the filled [disk](disk-mathematics.md) has trivial [fundamental group](fundamental-group.md), while the [circle](circle.md) and the [annulus](annulus-mathematics.md) have [infinite cyclic groups](infinite-cyclic-group.md).

Maps have computable algebraic shadows too. The [continuous map](continuous-map.md) $p_n:S^1\to S^1$, $p_n(z)=z^n$, fixes $1$ and sends $\gamma_k$ to $\gamma_{nk}$. Its induced [group homomorphism](group-homomorphism.md) is therefore $k\mapsto nk$, and its [mapping degree](degree-of-a-continuous-mapping.md) is $n$. For $n=2$ the image consists of even [integers](integer.md), so this map cannot be a [homotopy equivalence](homotopy-equivalence.md). Composing $p_2$ with $p_{-3}$ gives $p_{-6}$: map composition multiplies degrees, whereas concatenating [based loops](based-loop.md) adds winding numbers. Distinct degrees distinguish these maps up to [homotopy](homotopy.md); this calculation does not classify arbitrary [topological spaces](topological-space.md).

Historically, Poincaré's 1895 Analysis Situs introduced the [fundamental group](fundamental-group.md); its original §12 explicitly names it. The [CNRS transcription of Poincaré's §12, Groupe fondamental](https://analysis-situs.pages.math.cnrs.fr/site/p-12-Groupe-fondamental.html) provides a direct view of this early formulation.
