# Series (mathematics)

↑ **Parent:** [Sequence and series](sequence-and-series.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series_(mathematics))

A [series](series-mathematics.md) adds the terms of a [sequence](sequence.md) in their specified order. Its infinite-sum notation is defined through finite [partial sums](partial-sum.md):

$$
S_N=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n,\qquad
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n=\lim_{N\to\infty}S_N
$$

when the displayed [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) exists as a finite number. Otherwise the [series](series-mathematics.md) is not a [convergent series](convergent-series.md) in the ordinary sense. Writing an infinite list of additions does not by itself assign it a finite sum. [Cesàro summation](cesaro-summation.md) is a different prescription, with its own definition.

Two positive [series](series-mathematics.md) make the distinction concrete. For $a_n=1/n$, the individual terms tend to zero but the [harmonic series](harmonic-series.md) has unbounded [partial sums](partial-sum.md). For $a_n=1/[n(n+1)]$, [partial fraction decomposition](partial-fraction-decomposition.md) reveals a [telescoping series](telescoping-series.md) whose [partial sums](partial-sum.md) tend to one. The useful question is how much all the remaining terms can contribute together, rather than only how small the next term looks.
