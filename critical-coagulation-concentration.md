# Critical coagulation concentration

↑ **Parent:** [Charge stabilization of colloids](charge-stabilization-of-colloids.md)

The critical coagulation concentration is the approximate electrolyte concentration separating slow, barrier-limited aggregation from rapid aggregation of a charge-stabilized [colloid](colloid.md). In the simplest [DLVO theory](dlvo-theory.md), increasing salt shortens the [Debye–Hückel screening length](debye-huckel-screening-length.md) and lowers the repulsive barrier until rapid aggregation is possible. It depends on particle charge, solvent, temperature and particularly counterion valence. It is a kinetic crossover, not a universal salt concentration or necessarily an equilibrium phase boundary. Aggregation just below this crossover can be reaction limited; exactly at or above the rapid-aggregation threshold, proximity to the threshold alone does not imply a small sticking probability.
