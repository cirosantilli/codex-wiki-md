# Ext-connected components determine blocks

↑ **Parent:** [Block of a finite-dimensional algebra](block-of-a-finite-dimensional-algebra.md)

For a finite-dimensional [associative algebra](associative-algebra-split.md), form a graph on its [simple modules](irreducible-module.md), joining $S,T$ when $\operatorname{Ext}^1(S,T)$ or $\operatorname{Ext}^1(T,S)$ is nonzero. Its connected components are exactly the [simple modules](irreducible-module.md) belonging to each [block of a finite-dimensional algebra](block-of-a-finite-dimensional-algebra.md). They are also the components generated by sharing a [Jordan–Hölder factor](jordan-holder-factor.md) occurrence in an [indecomposable representation](indecomposable-representation.md) that is a [projective module](projective-module.md).

Indeed, [Ext separation of finite-length modules](ext-separation-of-finite-length-modules.md) would split the regular module of a block into canonical summands if that block had two graph components. Right multiplication preserves these summands, so its projection supplies a nontrivial central [idempotent](idempotent.md), contradicting the definition of a block. An indecomposable projective belongs to one block. Finally, a nonsplit extension $0\to U\to V\to W\to0$ of [simple modules](irreducible-module.md) is a quotient of the [projective cover](projective-cover.md) of $W$: a lift onto $W$ must contain $U$, since otherwise the extension splits. Thus its two endpoints share factors in an indecomposable projective.
