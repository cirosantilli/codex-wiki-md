<h1 id="b/8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The simple [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) are indexed by the [p-regular partitions](../../../../../regular-partition.md) $\lambda$ of $n$, meaning no part is repeated $p$ or more times. Equip the [tabloid](../../../../../tabloid.md) [permutation module](../../../../../permutation-module.md) with the [bilinear form](../../../../../bilinear-form-split.md) invariant under the [symmetric group](../../../../../symmetric-group.md) making its [tabloid](../../../../../tabloid.md) [basis](../../../../../basis.md) orthonormal. The construction is

$$
D^\lambda=S^\lambda/(S^\lambda\cap(S^\lambda)^\perp).
$$

The standard modular Specht theorem says this quotient is nonzero precisely for p-regular $\lambda$, is then simple, and these quotients give all simple [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md).

To verify distinctness of labels, use the standard dominance triangularity theorem: if $D^\nu$ occurs as a composition factor of $S^\lambda$, then $\nu\ge\lambda$; for a p-regular label the diagonal multiplicity is one. If $D^\lambda\cong D^\mu$, then $D^\lambda$ occurs in $S^\mu$, so $\lambda\ge\mu$, and the symmetric argument gives $\mu\ge\lambda$. Antisymmetry of the [dominance order on partitions](../../../../../dominance-order-on-partitions.md) yields $\boxed{\lambda=\mu}$. These are the general representation-theoretic results allowed in this part.
