<h1 id="a/1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use [induction](../../../../../mathematical-induction.md) on $\dim V$. By part (b), there is a one-dimensional invariant [linear subspace](../../../../../vector-subspace.md) $U=Kv$ on which $L$ acts as zero. The induced operators on $V/U$ remain nilpotent, so [induction](../../../../../mathematical-induction.md) gives a simultaneous strictly upper triangular [basis](../../../../../basis.md) there. Part (c) lifts it together with $v$ to such a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) of $V$.

Thus all elements strictly lower one common complete flag. A nested [Lie bracket](../../../../../lie-bracket.md) of sufficiently many flag-lowering operators lowers it past zero. Consequently the [lower central series](../../../../../lower-central-series.md) of $L$ terminates, proving that **the [Lie algebra](../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) is nilpotent**. In fact its nilpotency class is at most $n-1$ for $n>1$; the zero algebra covers the one-dimensional case.
