<h1 id="b/8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Give $M^\lambda$ the invariant [symmetric bilinear form](../../../../../symmetric-bilinear-form.md) for which its [tabloid](../../../../../tabloid.md) [basis](../../../../../basis.md) is orthonormal. Define

$$
\boxed{D^\lambda=S^\lambda/(S^\lambda\cap(S^\lambda)^\perp).}
$$

In characteristic $p>0$, this quotient is nonzero and simple when $\lambda$ is $p$-regular, meaning that no part occurs $p$ or more times. For other [partitions](../../../../../integer-partition.md) it is zero, so the label “simple [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md)” includes the nonzero/$p$-regular qualification.

The [James submodule theorem](../../../../../james-submodule-theorem.md) states that for every $F\Sigma_n$-[submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) $U\subseteq M^\lambda$, either $S^\lambda\subseteq U$ or $U\subseteq(S^\lambda)^\perp$. Applied to [submodules](../../../../../submodule.md) of $S^\lambda$, it says that every proper [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) lies in the radical of the restricted form, giving the simplicity of the displayed nonzero quotient.
