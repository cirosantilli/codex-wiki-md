<h1 id="b/7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [permutation module](../../../../../permutation-module.md) $M^\lambda$ has a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) consisting of row [tabloids](../../../../../tabloid.md) of shape $\lambda$: tableaux are identified when each row has the same set of entries. A permutation acts by applying it to every entry, and the action is extended linearly over $F$. Its [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) is $n!/\prod_i\lambda_i!$.

Here the two singleton rows form an ordered pair of distinct entries, so

$$
\boxed{\dim M^{(n-2,1,1)}=n(n-1).}
$$

A [standard tableau](../../../../../standard-young-tableau.md) of the same shape must put one in its top-left corner. Choose two entries from $2,\ldots,n$ for the two remaining boxes of the first column, in increasing order; all other entries then fill the top row in increasing order. Thus the standard-[polytabloid](../../../../../polytabloid.md) [basis](../../../../../basis.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\dim S^{(n-2,1,1)}=\binom{n-1}2=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}2.}
$$
