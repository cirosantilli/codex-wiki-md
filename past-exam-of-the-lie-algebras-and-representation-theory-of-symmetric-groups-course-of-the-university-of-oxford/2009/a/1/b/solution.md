<h1 id="a/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Every subalgebra of a [nilpotent Lie algebra](../../../../../nilpotent-lie-algebra.md) is nilpotent, since its lower central series is contained term by term in the ambient lower central series.

Conversely, if $L$ were not nilpotent, part (a) would give $x$ for which $\operatorname{ad}x$ is not nilpotent. Over an [algebraically closed field](../../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md), such an endomorphism has a nonzero [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$ and [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) $y$. The vectors $x,y$ are independent because $[x,x]=0$ while $[x,y]=\lambda y\ne0$. Their span is a two-dimensional subalgebra, and its lower central series is $Ky$ at every stage after the first. It is therefore not nilpotent, a contradiction. Thus

$$
\boxed{L\text{ nilpotent}\iff\text{every two-dimensional subalgebra is nilpotent}.}
$$
