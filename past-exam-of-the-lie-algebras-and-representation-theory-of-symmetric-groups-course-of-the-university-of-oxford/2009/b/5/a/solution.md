<h1 id="b/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a tableau $t$, let $C_t$ be its column stabilizer and $e_t=\sum_{\sigma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\{\sigma t\}$ its [polytabloid](../../../../../polytabloid.md). The [Specht module](../../../../../specht-module.md) is the span of all such [polytabloids](../../../../../polytabloid.md) inside the [tabloid](../../../../../tabloid.md) permutation module.

A [tabloid](../../../../../tabloid.md) with second row of size $k$ is determined by that subset, so $\dim M^{(n-k,k)}=\binom nk$. The two-row hook formula gives

$$
\dim S^{(n-k,k)}=\frac{n!(n-2k+1)}{(n-k+1)!k!}=\binom nk-\binom n{k-1}.
$$

At $k=3$ this proves

$$
\boxed{\dim M^{(n-3,3)}=\dim M^{(n-2,2)}+\dim S^{(n-3,3)}.}
$$

By [Maschke's theorem](../../../../../maschke-s-theorem.md), characteristic-zero permutation modules are completely reducible, so their Specht submodules are summands. The nonzero sum of all three-subset tabloids is fixed by every permutation; its span is a copy of the trivial module $S^{(n)}$.
