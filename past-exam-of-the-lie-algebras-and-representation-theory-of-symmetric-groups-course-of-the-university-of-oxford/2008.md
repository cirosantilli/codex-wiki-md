<h3 id="a/1">1</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/1/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

Define $L_1=L$ and $L_{n+1}=[L,L_n]$, where brackets of subspaces mean their linear span. Each term is an [ideal](../lie-algebra.md#ideal-of-a-lie-algebra). Inductively, if $L_n$ is an [ideal](../lie-algebra.md#ideal-of-a-lie-algebra), the [Jacobi identity](../lie-algebra.md#jacobi-identity) gives

$$
[z,[x,y]]=[[z,x],y]+[x,[z,y]]\in[L,L_n]\quad(z,x\in L,\ y\in L_n),
$$

so $L_{n+1}$ is an [ideal](../lie-algebra.md#ideal-of-a-lie-algebra) as well. Also $L_{n+1}\subseteq L_n$. Nilpotence means that this descending series eventually reaches zero; class $c$ means $L_{c+1}=0$ with $L_c\ne0$ for nonzero $L$.

<h4 id="a/1/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/1/a)

The idealizer here is the normalizer $\operatorname{Id}_L(A)=\{x\in L:[x,A]\subseteq A\}$. Since $A$ is a subalgebra it contains its own brackets, so $A\subseteq\operatorname{Id}_L(A)$.

Because $L_1\not\subseteq A$ and the lower central series terminates, choose $j$ with $L_j\not\subseteq A$ but $L_{j+1}\subseteq A$. Take $x\in L_j\setminus A$. Then $[x,A]\subseteq[L_j,L]=L_{j+1}\subseteq A$. Thus $x$ belongs to the idealizer and not to $A$, proving $\boxed{A<\operatorname{Id}_L(A)}$.

<h5 id="a/1/b/solution">Solution</h5>

Start with $I_0=I$ and set $I_{k+1}=[L,I_k]$. Every term lies in $I$, and $I_k\subseteq L_{k+1}$, so the sequence eventually vanishes. Choose its last nonzero term $I_j$. Then $[L,I_j]=0$, hence $0\ne I_j\subseteq I\cap Z(L)$. Therefore $\boxed{I\cap Z(L)\ne\{0\}}$.

<h4 id="a/1/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/1/c)

Induct on $i$, proving the claim for all $j$ simultaneously. The case $i=1$ is the definition $[L,L_j]=L_{j+1}$. If it holds for $i$, Jacobi gives

$$
[L_{i+1},L_j]=[[L,L_i],L_j]\subseteq[L,[L_i,L_j]]+[L_i,[L,L_j]]\subseteq[L,L_{i+j}]+[L_i,L_{j+1}]\subseteq L_{i+j+1}.
$$

Both uses of the induction hypothesis have first index $i$, so the induction is valid. Thus $\boxed{[L_i,L_j]\subseteq L_{i+j}}$ for all positive indices.

<h3 id="a/2">2</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/2/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

The [Killing form](../lie-algebra.md#killing-form) is $\kappa_L(x,y)=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}_Lx\,\operatorname{ad}_Ly)$. Symmetry follows from $\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\operatorname{tr}(BA)$. Since $\operatorname{ad}[z,x]=[\operatorname{ad}z,\operatorname{ad}x]$, cyclicity of [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) gives the invariance identity

$$
\kappa_L([z,x],y)=-\kappa_L(x,[z,y]).
$$

If $x\in I^\perp$ and $y\in I$, then $[z,y]\in I$, so the right side is zero. Hence $[z,x]\in I^\perp$ for every $z$, proving that $\boxed{I^\perp\text{ is an ideal}}$.

For $x\in I$, use a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) of $I$ followed by a complementary [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) of $L$. Because $[x,L]\subseteq I$, the [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) of $\operatorname{ad}_Lx$ has blocks $\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{ad}_Ix&B_x\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$. Multiplying two such [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) shows

$$
\boxed{\kappa_L(x,y)=\kappa_I(x,y)\quad(x,y\in I).}
$$

The restriction statement has both arguments in $I$; it does not identify the two forms for arbitrary elements of $L$ outside $I$.

Now use the ordered [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) in the question for a central [ideal](../lie-algebra.md#ideal-of-a-lie-algebra) $J$. The images of the $u_j$ in the quotient have adjoint [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) $A$, so the $u$-coordinate block of their images is $A$; arbitrary $J$-components form $B$. Each $v_j$ is central, so its image under every adjoint map is zero. Thus the [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) is exactly $\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\B&0\end{pmatrix}$. Products have top-left block $A_xA_y$ and zero bottom-right block. Taking traces gives

$$
\boxed{\kappa_L(x,y)=\kappa_{L/J}(\bar x,\bar y).}
$$

Finally, a nonzero finite-dimensional [nilpotent Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#nilpotent-lie-algebra) has nonzero [centre](../lie-algebra.md#center-of-a-lie-algebra) by (1)(b). Induct on its [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space): the [Killing form](../lie-algebra.md#killing-form) descends to the quotient by its [centre](../lie-algebra.md#center-of-a-lie-algebra), which is again nilpotent of smaller [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space), and is therefore zero by induction. The zero algebra starts the induction. Apply this to the nilpotent [ideal](../lie-algebra.md#ideal-of-a-lie-algebra) $I$ and then use the restriction identity to obtain $\boxed{\kappa_L(x,y)=0\text{ for }x,y\in I}$. This avoids the false general claim that the product of two nilpotent linear maps must be nilpotent.

<h4 id="a/3/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

By [irreducible traceless matrix Lie algebra is semisimple](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#irreducible-traceless-matrix-lie-algebra-is-semisimple), let $R$ be the [solvable radical](../lie-algebra.md#radical-of-a-lie-algebra). [Lie's theorem](../lie-algebra.md#lie-s-theorem) triangularizes its action, with finitely many diagonal [characters](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) $\lambda_i:R\to\mathbb C$, and supplies a nonzero common [eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) for one of them. For $x\in L$, conjugation by $e^{tx}$ preserves $R$ and gives an equivalent [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) of it. Thus the multiset of [characters](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) $\lambda_i\circ e^{t\operatorname{ad}x}$ is constant. Each [character](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) varies continuously in $t$ and has values in that finite set of [characters](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation), so it is individually constant. Differentiating at zero gives $\lambda_i([x,r])=0$ for all $r\in R$.

Choose a common [eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) $V_\lambda\ne0$. For $v\in V_\lambda$,

$$
r(xv)=x(rv)+[r,x]v=\lambda(r)xv+\lambda([r,x])v=\lambda(r)xv.
$$

Thus $V_\lambda$ is $L$-invariant, so irreducibility makes it all of $\mathbb C^n$. Every element of $R$ acts as a scalar. Its [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) is zero because $L\subseteq\mathfrak{sl}_n$, so that scalar is zero. Faithfulness of the [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) inclusion gives $R=0$. Hence $\boxed{L\text{ is semisimple}}$.

One sufficient diagonal-[matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) condition for simplicity is that $L$ contain **all traceless [diagonal matrices](../linear-algebra.md#diagonal-matrix)**. Indeed invariance under their adjoint actions then decomposes $L$ into that diagonal algebra and some [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix)-unit spaces $\mathbb Ce_{ij}$. Put a directed edge $j\to i$ when $e_{ij}\in L$. Irreducibility makes the directed graph strongly connected; otherwise a terminal component spans a proper invariant coordinate subspace. Bracketing [matrix units](../vector-space.md#matrix-unit) along a simple directed path supplies the [matrix unit](../vector-space.md#matrix-unit) between its endpoints. Strong connectivity therefore supplies every off-[diagonal matrix](../linear-algebra.md#diagonal-matrix) unit, so $L=\mathfrak{sl}_n$, which is simple. The mere existence of a [diagonal matrix](../linear-algebra.md#diagonal-matrix) with distinct diagonal entries would not be a sufficient condition.

For the specified construction, $H$ has [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) two. Its diagonal adjoint action and the twelve distinct nonzero [weights](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) give a [direct sum](../vector-space.md#direct-sum) $L=H\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{12}\mathbb Cg_i$, of [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) fourteen. The stated bracket condition and the [weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation)-vector relations make it a [Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md). A subspace invariant under it is invariant under all the $g_i$ and hence their associative algebra, so it acts irreducibly on $\mathbb C^7$. The preceding result makes it semisimple.

Use [prime-dimensional faithful irreducible module forces simplicity](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#prime-dimensional-faithful-irreducible-module-forces-simplicity). To see this, decompose the algebra into simple [ideals](../lie-algebra.md#ideal-of-a-lie-algebra). Complete reducibility for one [ideal](../lie-algebra.md#ideal-of-a-lie-algebra) and commutation with the others make its isotypic component invariant under the whole algebra, hence all of the [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation). Writing that component as $W\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(W,V)$ and repeating produces a [tensor product](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor-product) of irreducibles for the simple [ideals](../lie-algebra.md#ideal-of-a-lie-algebra). Faithfulness makes every factor nontrivial, of [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) at least two. Their dimensions multiply to seven, so only one factor is possible. Thus the constructed $L$ is simple.

The diagonal algebra $H$ is toral, and its centralizer inside $L$ is just $H$, since no $g_i$ has zero [weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation). A maximal [toral subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#toral-subalgebra) containing $H$ lies in that centralizer, so equals $H$; it is a [Cartan subalgebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#cartan-subalgebra). Thus the rank is two and the twelve [weights](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) are the [roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system). Pair them as $\alpha_{i+6}=-\alpha_i$. The opposite-[root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) brackets have [weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) zero and belong to $H$. For distinct $i,j\le6$, the sum cannot be zero, and the supplied [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system)-bracket result says their bracket is nonzero exactly when $\alpha_i+\alpha_j$ is a [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system). This is equivalent to $-\alpha_i-\alpha_j$ being a [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system), proving the requested equivalence for the paired brackets.

Among rank-two [simple root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root) systems, $A_2,B_2,G_2$ have respectively six, eight and twelve [roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system). Therefore

$$
\boxed{L\text{ has type }G_2.}
$$

Choose [simple roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root) $\alpha$ short and $\beta$ long. In $G_2$, $\beta+\alpha$ and $\beta+2\alpha$ are [roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system). Renumber with $g_1\in L_\alpha$, $g_3\in L_\beta$, $g_4\in L_{\beta+\alpha}$ and $g_5\in L_{\beta+2\alpha}$, retaining opposite partners in the other six positions. [Root spaces](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-space) are one dimensional, and the allowed bracket equality gives

$$
\boxed{[g_3,g_1]=\mu_1g_4,\qquad[g_4,g_1]=\mu_2g_5,\qquad\mu_1\mu_2\ne0.}
$$

<h3 id="a/4">4</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/4/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#a/4)

Use the convention $A_{ij}=\langle\alpha_j,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=2(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)/(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)$. For a finite reduced crystallographic [root system](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-system), diagonal entries are two and off-diagonal entries belong to $\{0,-1,-2,-3\}$, with $A_{ij}A_{ji}\in\{0,1,2,3\}$. Transposing this convention merely transposes the [Cartan matrix](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#cartan-matrix).

The $\alpha$-string through $\beta$ has the consecutive [roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) $\beta-p\alpha,\ldots,\beta+q\alpha$, where $p,q\ge0$ and $p-q=\langle\beta,\alpha^\vee\rangle$. For distinct [simple roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root) $\alpha_i,\alpha_j$, the negative step is absent, so $p=0$ and $q=-A_{ij}$.

The [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) determines each simple [reflection](../linear-algebra.md#reflection-mathematics) on the simple-[root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) [basis](../vector-space.md#basis):

$$
s_i(\alpha_j)=\alpha_j-A_{ij}\alpha_i.
$$

It therefore determines their generated [Weyl group](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weyl-group) as linear transformations. Every [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) is in the Weyl orbit of a [simple root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root). For a positive nonsimple [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) $\beta=\sum c_i\alpha_i$, some $(\beta,\alpha_i)$ is positive because $\sum c_i(\beta,\alpha_i)=(\beta,\beta)>0$. Its [reflection](../linear-algebra.md#reflection-mathematics) subtracts a positive integer multiple of $\alpha_i$ and lowers height. It stays positive unless the [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) is simple, since the other simple-[root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) coefficients are unchanged and at least one is positive. Induction on height reaches a [simple root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root); negative [roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) follow by sign. Hence $\Phi=W\Delta$, which is completely reconstructed by those [reflection](../linear-algebra.md#reflection-mathematics) [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix). This determines the system up to [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system)-system isomorphism, rather than fixing an absolute length scale; irreducible components may be rescaled.

For [positive roots for a chain Cartan matrix](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#positive-roots-for-a-chain-cartan-matrix), symmetry and adjacency imply all [simple roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root) have equal length; normalize their squared lengths to two. Adjacent inner products are minus one and all others zero. Since every [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) is Weyl-conjugate to a [simple root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root), every [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) also has squared length two. Write a [positive root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#positive-root) as $\beta=\sum_{i=1}^lc_i\omega_i$ with nonnegative integers $c_i$, and set $c_0=c_{l+1}=0$. Then

$$
2=(\beta,\beta)=2\sum_{i=1}^lc_i^2-2\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}c_ic_{i+1}=\sum_{i=0}^l(c_{i+1}-c_i)^2.
$$

There must be exactly two nonzero integer differences, one $+1$ and one $-1$. Nonnegativity and the two zero endpoint values imply that the coefficients are one on one consecutive interval and zero elsewhere.

Conversely a single [simple root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root) is a [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system). If $\omega_i+\cdots+\omega_j$ is a [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) and $j<l$, its pairing with $\omega_{j+1}^\vee$ is minus one. Reflecting in $\omega_{j+1}$ therefore adds that [simple root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root), making the next longer interval a [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system). Induction constructs every interval. Thus

$$
\boxed{\Phi^+=\left\{\sum_{k=i}^j\omega_k:1\le i\le j\le l\right\}.}
$$

<h5 id="b/5/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/5/a)

Use the convention that insertion returns the insertion tableau $P$ first and the recording tableau $Q$ second. Successive row insertions of $3,4,1,6,7,5,2$ give

$$
\boxed{P=\begin{array}{llll}1&2&5&7\\3&4\\6\end{array},\qquad Q=\begin{array}{llll}1&2&4&5\\3&6\\7\end{array}.}
$$

For example inserting five bumps six to the second row, and the final insertion of two bumps four from the first row and six from the second, creating the third row. The newly created boxes at these steps receive recording labels six and seven respectively.

The deletion input is this pair with the two tableaux interchanged. The symmetry property of [RSK](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#robinson-schensted-knuth-correspondence) therefore gives the inverse permutation. One can also reverse-insert the boxes in descending recording-label order. The result is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{del}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&4&5&6&7\\3&7&1&2&6&4&5\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Indeed the displayed second row lists the positions of one through seven in the original permutation.

<h4 id="b/5/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/5/b)

Let $f^\lambda$ be the number of [standard tableaux](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#standard-young-tableau) of shape $\lambda$. First the associated [polytabloids](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid) are linearly independent. Order [tabloids](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) lexicographically by reading each row after sorting its entries. For a column-increasing tableau $t$, the term $\{t\}$ in $e_t$ has coefficient one; every other [tabloid](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) term has larger row word. To see this, take the first row changed by a nontrivial column permutation. Earlier rows are fixed entry by entry, since each entry belongs to one original column. The changed row can only receive entries from lower positions of those increasing columns, so after sorting it is lexicographically larger. In a linear relation between standard [polytabloids](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid), choose the smallest row word with a nonzero coefficient. Its own [tabloid](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) coefficient cannot be cancelled by a larger standard [polytabloid](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid), a contradiction. Thus $\dim S^\lambda\ge f^\lambda$.

Work first over $\mathbb C$. The [RSK](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#robinson-schensted-knuth-correspondence) bijection between permutations and pairs of [standard tableaux](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#standard-young-tableau) of the same shape gives $\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}(f^\lambda)^2=n!$. The assumed pairwise nonisomorphic irreducible [Specht modules](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#specht-module), and the decomposition of the [regular representation](../representation-theory.md#regular-representation) with irreducible multiplicities equal to their dimensions, give $\sum_\lambda(\dim S^\lambda)^2\le n!$. Combining the two inequalities forces equality term by term: $\dim S^\lambda=f^\lambda$. Hence the independent standard [polytabloids](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid) are a **[basis](../vector-space.md#basis)**.

The same conclusion holds over every characteristic-zero [field](../algebra.md#field). All [polytabloid](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid) coordinates in the [tabloid](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) are integers, so the ranks of their coordinate [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) are the same over any characteristic-zero [field](../algebra.md#field) as over $\mathbb C$. In particular the [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) equality and independence transfer without requiring an embedding of the whole [field](../algebra.md#field) into $\mathbb C$.

<h4 id="b/5/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/5/c)

The equality $\sum_\lambda(\dim S^\lambda)^2=n!$ exhausts the [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) of the [regular representation](../representation-theory.md#regular-representation). There is no room for another irreducible constituent, since it would contribute its positive squared [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space). Thus

$$
\boxed{\{S^\lambda:\lambda\vdash n\}\text{ is a complete irredundant family of irreducible representations in characteristic zero}.}
$$

Each has the standard-[polytabloid](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid) [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) just proved. The usual split characteristic-zero statement follows over any characteristic-zero [field](../algebra.md#field) because these integral Specht models remain irreducible after extension to an algebraic closure and already exhaust all dimensions there. In the literal wording of the source, [polytabloids](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid) themselves are vectors; it is the [Specht modules](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#specht-module) with those bases that form the family of [representations](../representation-theory.md#group-representation).

<h5 id="b/6/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/6/a)

If the cycle [partition](../combinatorics.md#integer-partition) contains a part $r$, the [Murnaghan–Nakayama rule](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#murnaghan-nakayama-rule) is

$$
\boxed{\chi^\lambda((r,\nu))=\sum_{\substack{\mu\subseteq\lambda\\\lambda/\mu\text{ a border strip of size }r}}(-1)^{\operatorname{ht}(\lambda/\mu)}\chi^\mu(\nu).}
$$

A [border strip](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#rim-hook) is a connected skew [Young diagram](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-diagram) with no $2\times2$ block; its height is its number of occupied rows minus one. The remaining boxes must be a [partition](../combinatorics.md#integer-partition) diagram. The recursion starts with [character](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) one for the empty diagram at the empty [cycle type](../finite-group-theory.md#cycle-type), and an empty sum is zero. Repeated removal for all cycle lengths computes the [character](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) value.

<h4 id="b/6/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/6/b)

Write the hook cycle [partition](../combinatorics.md#integer-partition) as $\mu=(k,1^{n-k})$, $1\le k\le n$. At the identity, the [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) is $n-1$: a [standard tableau](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#standard-young-tableau) of shape $(2,1^{n-2})$ is determined by the entry in its top-right box, any one of $2,\ldots,n$.

For $2\le k\le n-2$, the only removable [border strip](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#rim-hook) of length $k$ is the vertical bottom strip. Its height is $k-1$, and the remaining hook has size $n-k$ and [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) $n-k-1$. A strip of length $n-1$ would leave the top-left box but disconnect the removed top-right box from the removed column, so none exists and the value is zero. For $k=n$, the whole hook is a [border strip](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#rim-hook) of height $n-2$ and contributes $(-1)^{n-2}$. These cases combine as

$$
\boxed{\chi^{(2,1^{n-2})}(k,1^{n-k})=\begin{cases}n-1,&k=1,\\(-1)^{k-1}(n-k-1),&2\le k\le n.\end{cases}}
$$

In particular the formula includes zero at $k=n-1$ and the full-cycle sign at $k=n$.

<h4 id="b/6/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/6/c)

The two [partition](../combinatorics.md#integer-partition) diagrams are transposes, so their characteristic-zero [Specht modules](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#specht-module) differ by tensoring with the [sign representation](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#sign-representation). A [transposition](../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation) has sign minus one. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\chi^{(2,1^{n-2})}((i,j))=-\chi^{(n-1,1)}((i,j))=3-n.}
$$

The middle [character](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) is $n-3$, since the standard [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) is the point-permutation [representation](../representation-theory.md#group-representation) minus its trivial line, and a [transposition](../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation) fixes $n-2$ points.

<h3 id="b/7">7</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/7/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/7/a)

The [permutation module](../representation-theory.md#permutation-module) $M^\lambda$ has a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) consisting of row [tabloids](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) of shape $\lambda$: tableaux are identified when each row has the same set of entries. A permutation acts by applying it to every entry, and the action is extended linearly over $F$. Its [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) is $n!/\prod_i\lambda_i!$.

Here the two singleton rows form an ordered pair of distinct entries, so

$$
\boxed{\dim M^{(n-2,1,1)}=n(n-1).}
$$

A [standard tableau](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#standard-young-tableau) of the same shape must put one in its top-left corner. Choose two entries from $2,\ldots,n$ for the two remaining boxes of the first column, in increasing order; all other entries then fill the top row in increasing order. Thus the standard-[polytabloid](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid) [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) gives

$$
\boxed{\dim S^{(n-2,1,1)}=\binom{n-1}2=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}2.}
$$

<h4 id="b/7/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/7/b)

Write $v_{ij}$ for the [tabloid](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) with second-row entry $i$ and third-row entry $j$. Equivariance gives $\phi(v_{ij})=w_i+w_j$ for every distinct pair. For a tableau $t$, the only nontrivial column antisymmetrizer is on the three entries in its first column. In its alternating sum, pair each column permutation with the permutation obtained by interchanging the two singleton-row positions. Their images under $\phi$ are the same unordered sum of two $w$'s, while their signs are opposite. Hence $\phi(e_t)=0$ for every [polytabloid](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid), proving $\boxed{S^{(n-2,1,1)}\subseteq\ker\phi}$.

For distinct $i,j,k$,

$$
w_i=\frac12\big[(w_i+w_j)+(w_i+w_k)-(w_j+w_k)\big].
$$

Each summand is an image and two is invertible in [characteristic zero](../algebra.md#characteristic-zero). Thus every [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) vector of $M^{(n-1,1)}$ is in the image, proving [surjectivity](../algebra.md#surjective-function). [rank-nullity theorem](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md#rank-nullity-theorem) then gives

$$
\boxed{\dim\ker\phi=n(n-1)-n=n(n-2).}
$$

<h4 id="b/7/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/7/c)

The stabilizer of an ordered pair is the subgroup $\Sigma_{n-2}$ fixing both entries, so the [permutation module](../representation-theory.md#permutation-module) is $\operatorname{Ind}_{\Sigma_{n-2}}^{\Sigma_n}\mathbf1$. Induce in two stages through $\Sigma_{n-1}$. The first stage is $S^{(n-1)}\oplus S^{(n-2,1)}$. The characteristic-zero [induction branching rule for a symmetric group](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#induction-branching-rule-for-a-symmetric-group) adds one box in every possible [partition](../combinatorics.md#integer-partition) position, with multiplicity one. Thus the two second-stage decompositions are

$$
\operatorname{Ind}S^{(n-1)}=S^{(n)}\oplus S^{(n-1,1)},\qquad\operatorname{Ind}S^{(n-2,1)}=S^{(n-1,1)}\oplus S^{(n-2,2)}\oplus S^{(n-2,1,1)}.
$$

By transitivity of induction this yields

$$
\boxed{M^{(n-2,1,1)}\cong S^{(n)}\oplus2S^{(n-1,1)}\oplus S^{(n-2,2)}\oplus S^{(n-2,1,1)}.}
$$

The dimensions are $1$, $2(n-1)$, $n(n-3)/2$ and $(n-1)(n-2)/2$, whose sum is $n(n-1)$, checking the decomposition also at $n=4$.

<h3 id="b/8">8</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/8/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#b/8)

<h5 id="b/8/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/8/a)

Give $M^\lambda$ the invariant [symmetric bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#symmetric-bilinear-form) for which its [tabloid](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) is orthonormal. Define

$$
\boxed{D^\lambda=S^\lambda/(S^\lambda\cap(S^\lambda)^\perp).}
$$

In characteristic $p>0$, this quotient is nonzero and simple when $\lambda$ is $p$-regular, meaning that no part occurs $p$ or more times. For other [partitions](../combinatorics.md#integer-partition) it is zero, so the label “simple [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics)” includes the nonzero/$p$-regular qualification.

The [James submodule theorem](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#james-submodule-theorem) states that for every $F\Sigma_n$-[submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) $U\subseteq M^\lambda$, either $S^\lambda\subseteq U$ or $U\subseteq(S^\lambda)^\perp$. Applied to [submodules](../module-theory.md#submodule) of $S^\lambda$, it says that every proper [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) lies in the radical of the restricted form, giving the simplicity of the displayed nonzero quotient.

<h4 id="b/8/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#b/8)

<h5 id="b/8/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/8/b)

Choose the two [standard tableaux](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#standard-young-tableau)

$$
t_1=\begin{array}{ll}1&2\\3&4\end{array},\qquad t_2=\begin{array}{ll}1&3\\2&4\end{array},
$$

and use their [polytabloids](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid) $e_1,e_2$, with column-vector coordinates. Writing $[ab\mid cd]$ for the [tabloid](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) with those two row sets gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
e_1&=[12\mid34]-[23\mid14]-[14\mid23]+[34\mid12],\\
e_2&=[13\mid24]-[23\mid14]-[14\mid23]+[24\mid13].
\end{aligned}
$$

The coefficients at $[12\mid34]$ and $[13\mid24]$ prove independence over every [field](../algebra.md#field). Applying the generators directly gives

$$
(12)e_1=e_1-e_2,\quad(12)e_2=-e_2,\qquad(23)e_1=e_2,\quad(23)e_2=e_1,
$$

and $(34)$ acts in the same way as $(12)$. Their span is invariant under the generators, contains $e_1$, and contains its whole orbit, hence is the [Specht module](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#specht-module). Thus it is a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) without needing a characteristic-zero argument. The [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) are

$$
\boxed{\rho(12)=\rho(34)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\rho(23)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

These integral [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) may be reduced in the chosen characteristic.

<h4 id="b/8/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#b/8)

<h5 id="b/8/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/8/c)

For the [characteristic-three Specht module for the two-by-two partition](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#characteristic-three-specht-module-for-the-two-by-two-partition), reduce the [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) modulo three. The vector $e_1+e_2$ is fixed by every displayed [transposition](../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation): $(12)$ and $(34)$ send it to $e_1-2e_2=e_1+e_2$, and $(23)$ interchanges its two terms. Thus its span is a proper nonzero **trivial [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule)**.

On the quotient impose $e_2=-e_1$. Each generating [transposition](../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation) then acts as minus one, so the quotient is the **[sign representation](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#sign-representation)**. Consequently

$$
\boxed{0\longrightarrow\mathbf1\longrightarrow S^{(2,2)}\longrightarrow\operatorname{sgn}\longrightarrow0,}
$$

and the two [composition factors](../module-theory.md#composition-factor) are trivial and sign, each once. In the $D$ notation they are $D^{(4)}$ and $D^{(2,2)}$ respectively. Indeed the restricted [tabloid](../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) form has [Gram matrix](../inner-product.md#gram-matrix) $\begin{pmatrix}4&2\\2&4\end{pmatrix}$, whose characteristic-three radical is precisely the trivial line, so the simple quotient is $D^{(2,2)}$.

The extension does not split: the minus-one [eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) of $\rho(12)$ is the line through $e_2$, but $\rho(23)$ sends that line to the line through $e_1$. Thus there is no sign [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) complementing the trivial line, consistent with the failure of semisimplicity.
