# Adjacent-order spacing versus nearest-axis echelle interval

↑ **Parent:** [Free spectral range of an echelle grating](free-spectral-range-of-an-echelle-grating.md)

At a fixed camera-axis direction, the [grating equation](grating-equation.md) gives $m\lambda_m=K=d(\sin\alpha+\sin\beta_0)$, so adjacent central [wavelengths](wavelength.md) differ by $K/[m(m+1)]$. This differs from the exact [wavelength](wavelength.md) cell where an order is nearest the [optical camera](optical-camera.md) axis. For $\beta_0=0$ and $x=f\tan\beta$, equal distances of orders $m$ and $m+1$ mean opposite [diffraction](diffraction.md) [angles](angle.md); adding their [diffraction grating](diffraction-grating.md) equations gives $\lambda=K/(m+1/2)$. The full order-$m$ cell is bounded by $K/(m+1/2)$ and $K/(m-1/2)$, hence has width $K/(m^2-1/4)$ for $m\ge2$ and accessible neighbouring orders. Both widths approach $K/m^2$ at high order.
