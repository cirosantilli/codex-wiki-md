# Bernoulli differential equation

↑ **Parent:** [Ordinary differential equation](ordinary-differential-equation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_differential_equation)

A [Bernoulli differential equation](bernoulli-differential-equation.md) is a first-order [ordinary differential equation](ordinary-differential-equation.md) of the displayed form, with $P$ and $Q$ [continuous functions](continuous-function.md) on an [interval](interval-mathematics.md) and a fixed real exponent $n$. A nonlinear power can hide a [first-order linear differential equation](first-order-linear-differential-equation.md): on a suitable branch, the [Bernoulli power substitution](bernoulli-power-substitution.md) $z=y^{1-n}$ removes it exactly. The transformed [solution of a differential equation](solution-of-a-differential-equation.md) can be completely regular even when recovering $y$ produces [finite-time blowup](finite-time-blowup.md).

For example, the [reciprocal Bernoulli equation with finite-time blowup](reciprocal-bernoulli-equation-with-finite-time-blowup.md) $y'+y=e^xy^2$ becomes $z'-z=-e^x$ under $z=1/y$. The [initial conditions](initial-condition.md) $y(0)=1/2$ and $y(0)=-1$ select a positive trajectory blowing up at $x=2$ and a negative trajectory tending to zero as $x\to\infty$, respectively. The [integrating factor](integrating-factor.md) exposes both behaviors with the same short calculation.

The cases $n=0$ and $n=1$ are already [first-order linear differential equations](first-order-linear-differential-equation.md): respectively $y'+Py=Q$, with the zeroth-power term interpreted as the constant $1$, and $y'+(P-Q)y=0$. There is no need for a nonlinear change of variable; for $n=1$, $y^{1-n}=1$ would discard the unknown entirely. For other exponents, the substitution must respect the domain of [exponentiation](exponentiation.md) and may lose the zero [solution of a differential equation](solution-of-a-differential-equation.md).[Notes on Diffy Qs, §1.5.2 Bernoulli equations: substitution v equals y to the power 1 minus n; n equals 0 or 1 is already linear; Example 1.5.1 selects the positive root using its initial condition](notes-on-diffy-qs-differential-equations-for-engineers-by-jiri-lebl/1-5-substitution.md)
