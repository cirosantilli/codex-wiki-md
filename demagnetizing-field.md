# Demagnetizing field

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic susceptibility](magnetic-susceptibility.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demagnetizing_field)

The [demagnetizing field](demagnetizing-field.md) is the contribution to [magnetic field intensity](magnetic-field-intensity.md) produced by the sample's own [magnetization](magnetization.md). Inside a uniformly magnetized [ellipsoid](ellipsoid.md), it is uniform and has the form $\mathbf H_d=-\mathsf N\mathbf M$; the [demagnetizing factor](demagnetizing-factor.md) along a principal axis gives its longitudinal component. These conditions make the scalar correction in [field conventions for magnetic susceptibility](field-conventions-for-magnetic-susceptibility.md) useful.

A cylinder generally has spatially varying [magnetization](magnetization.md) and [demagnetizing field](demagnetizing-field.md). A volume-sensitive instrument and a short central pickup coil consequently need different effective corrections: one averages over the sample, while the other samples central flux. A shape factor copied from an [ellipsoid](ellipsoid.md) is not an exact correction for every cylinder, especially with nonlinear response or [hysteresis](hysteresis.md). [R. B. Goldfarb, Demagnetizing Factors, printed p. 104, §2 opening paragraphs: magnetometric versus fluxmetric sensing, nonuniform cylindrical fields and susceptibility-dependent factors](concise-encyclopedia-of-magnetic-and-superconducting-materials/demagnetizing-factors.md)
