# Gravitational mass degeneracy

↑ **Parent:** [Gravitational constant](gravitational-constant.md)

An [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) around an isolated source constrains its [standard gravitational parameter](standard-gravitational-parameter.md) $\mu=GM$ rather than independently measuring the source's [mass](mass.md) and the [gravitational constant](gravitational-constant.md). In the [test particle](test-particle.md) approximation, a [circular orbit](circular-orbit.md) of radius $a$ and [orbital period](orbital-period.md) $T$ satisfies

$$
\frac{4\pi^2a}{T^2}=\frac{GM}{a^2},\qquad \mu=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{T^2}.
$$

The left equation equates the required [centripetal acceleration](centripetal-acceleration.md) to the [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md). More generally, a [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md) depends on the same $\mu$. In the exact relative [two-body problem](two-body-problem.md), replace $GM$ by $G(M+m)$.

For any positive dimensionless number $q$, replacing $G$ by $qG$ and $M$ by $M/q$ leaves $GM$ and every predicted [test particle](test-particle.md) [trajectory](trajectory.md) unchanged. This is the [gravitational mass degeneracy](gravitational-mass-degeneracy.md): more observations of the same [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) can refine $GM$ without separating its factors. A laboratory source whose [mass](mass.md) is calibrated independently supplies the missing information.

Consequently an astronomical [mass](mass.md) expressed in kilograms as $M=\mu/G$ inherits [measurement uncertainty](measurement-uncertainty.md) from $G$, even if $\mu$ is known much more precisely. To first order,

$$
\frac{\delta M}{M}=\frac{\delta\mu}{\mu}-\frac{\delta G}{G}.
$$

When the relative [standard uncertainty](standard-uncertainty.md) of $\mu$ is negligible, that of $M$ is approximately $u(G)/G$. Conversely, the ratio of two source [masses](mass.md) inferred using the same $G$ is $M_1/M_2=\mu_1/\mu_2$, so this common factor cancels. That distinction explains how excellent [orbit determination](orbit-determination.md) can coexist with less precise absolute astronomical [masses](mass.md).
