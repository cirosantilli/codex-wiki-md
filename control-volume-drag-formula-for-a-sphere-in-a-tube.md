# Control-volume drag formula for a sphere in a tube

↑ **Parent:** [Lubrication resistance integrals for a nearly occluding sphere](lubrication-resistance-integrals-for-a-nearly-occluding-sphere.md)

Take a fluid [control volume](control-volume.md) spanning a nearly occluding [sphere](sphere.md). The [pressure](pressure.md) difference contributes $\pi a^2\Delta P$ upwards, while the outer-wall shear in the [sphere](sphere.md) frame contributes $2\pi a\mu\int u_y(h)\,dz$. Since [sphere-frame flux in a tube](sphere-frame-flux-in-a-tube.md) gives $u_y(h)=4U/h-6q/h^2$, the drag on the [sphere](sphere.md) is $F=\pi a^2\Delta P+2\pi a\mu(4UI_1-6qI_2)$. Using the wall shear avoids integrating traction over the strongly curved [sphere](sphere.md) directly. The remaining tube [pressure](pressure.md) drop is fixed by [Hagen-Poiseuille flow](hagen-poiseuille-equation.md).
