# Ferrimagnetism

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetism](magnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrimagnetism)

[Ferrimagnetism](ferrimagnetism.md) has opposing, generally unequal ordered [magnetic sublattices](magnetic-sublattice.md), with net [magnetization](magnetization.md) $|M_A-M_B|$ in a collinear model. [Louis Néel](louis-neel.md) used it to explain magnetic oxides.[§8, pp.328–330: unequal sublattice contributions](magnetism-and-the-local-molecular-field-by-louis-neel.md)

The counting example in [magnetic sublattice](magnetic-sublattice.md) gives $3(2m_0)-3(m_0)=3m_0$. Unequal populations also suffice: four sites carrying $+m_0$ and two carrying $-m_0$ give $2m_0$, even with identical individual magnitudes. Thus one must compare total sublattice contributions per common [volume](volume.md), rather than merely asking whether two atomic species carry different [magnetic moments](magnetic-dipole-moment.md).

Both [ferromagnetism](ferromagnetism.md) and [ferrimagnetism](ferrimagnetism.md) can produce a macroscopic magnet. Their internal organization differs: the latter retains opposing contributions. This makes [ferrimagnetism](ferrimagnetism.md) a useful bridge between net [magnetization](magnetization.md) and microscopic [magnetic order](magnetic-order.md): reducing one opposing contribution can increase the net sum, even though the total sum of moment magnitudes decreases.
