# Relaxation-controlled antidune instability

↑ **Parent:** [Sediment transport](sediment-transport.md)

In a reduced [sediment](sediment.md)-bed model with normalized discharge one, water momentum gives $s=1-u^{-1}+F^2(1-u^2)/2$, while [concentration](concentration.md) adaptation and bed exchange give $c_x=E(u)-c=-s_t$. Linearizing about $u=c=1,s=0$ yields $\widetilde s=(1-F^2)\widetilde u$ and $(1+ik)\widetilde c=E'(1)\widetilde u$. Elimination proves the displayed dispersion relation.

For $E'(1)>0$, the [growth rate](growth-rate.md) is $E'(1)k^2/[(F^2-1)(1+k^2)]$, positive exactly on the supercritical side $F>1$ for nonzero $k$. The [phase speed](phase-speed.md) is $-E'(1)/[(F^2-1)(1+k^2)]$, so unstable bedforms migrate upstream and are [antidunes](antidune.md). At high [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) growth approaches a finite plateau rather than becoming unbounded. This closure selects no finite fastest wavelength; short-scale selection needs additional physics. The critical limit $F=1$ and a vanishing [entrainment](fluid-entrainment.md) [derivative](derivative.md) are degenerate cases outside the displayed conclusion.
