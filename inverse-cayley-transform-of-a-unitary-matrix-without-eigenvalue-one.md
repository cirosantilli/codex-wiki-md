# Inverse Cayley transform of a unitary matrix without eigenvalue one

↑ **Parent:** [Cayley transform of a Hermitian matrix](cayley-transform-of-a-hermitian-matrix.md)

If $U$ is a [unitary matrix](unitary-matrix.md) with no [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) equal to one, the displayed inverse [Cayley transform of a Hermitian matrix](cayley-transform-of-a-hermitian-matrix.md) is Hermitian. Since $U^\dagger=U^{-1}$, taking the [Hermitian conjugate](hermitian-conjugation.md) gives $B^\dagger=-i(I-U^{-1})^{-1}(I+U^{-1})=i(I-U)^{-1}(I+U)=B$, using commutativity of rational functions of $U$. An eigenvalue $e^{i\theta}\ne1$ is mapped to the real number $-\cot(\theta/2)$. Conversely $U=(B-iI)(B+iI)^{-1}$, so this correspondence identifies Hermitian matrices with unitary matrices whose spectrum avoids one.
