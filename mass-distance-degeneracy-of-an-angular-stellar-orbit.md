# Mass-distance degeneracy of an angular stellar orbit

↑ **Parent:** [Galactic-centre point-mass orbit model](galactic-centre-point-mass-orbit-model.md)

Angular [astrometry](astrometry.md) determines the combination displayed by substituting $a=D\alpha$ into [Kepler's third law](kepler-s-third-law.md). Keeping the fitted shape, orientation and [orbital period](orbital-period.md) fixed, the rescaling

$$
D\mapsto\lambda D,\qquad a\mapsto\lambda a,\qquad \mu\mapsto\lambda^3\mu
$$

leaves the entire angular time series unchanged. A longer physical orbit around a larger mass can trace precisely the same angular path. More angular samples improve the fitted combination; by themselves they cannot break this scaling symmetry of the model.

The [radial-velocity semi-amplitude](radial-velocity-semi-amplitude.md) is

$$
K=\frac{2\pi a\sin i}{P\sqrt{1-e^2}}.
$$

For $\sin i\ne0$, combining it with the angular fit gives the [orbital parallax](orbital-parallax.md) distance

$$
D=\frac{KP\sqrt{1-e^2}}{2\pi\alpha\sin i}.
$$

The spectral [velocity](velocity.md) scale supplies the missing physical ruler. For a face-on orbit, $\sin i=0$, this radial signal vanishes and that method cannot supply a distance. In a partial or noisy data set, fitted orientation and systemic motion also correlate with the inferred scale.
