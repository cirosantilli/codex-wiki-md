# Sandwich form of the Hahn-Banach theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Hahn-Banach theorem](hahn-banach-theorem.md)

If $p$ is a [sublinear functional](sublinear-function.md), $q$ a [superlinear functional](superlinear-functional.md) and $q\leq p$ on a real vector space, there exists a linear functional $U$ between them. More generally, a linear functional $S$ on a subspace extends with this domination whenever $S(y)\leq p(x+y)-q(x)$ for all ambient $x$ and subspace $y$. In a one-dimensional extension the [admissible values for a sandwich extension](admissible-values-for-a-sandwich-extension.md) form a nonempty real interval. Positive homogeneity checks the extension on both signs of the new coordinate. Chain unions preserve linearity and the mixed domination, so [Zorn's lemma](zorn-s-lemma.md) gives a maximal extension, whose domain must be the whole space. Finally $x=0$ in the mixed inequality gives the upper bound; using the pair $(x,-x)$ gives the lower bound. Conversely, any linear $U$ between $q,p$ obeys the mixed inequality by $U(y)=U(x+y)-U(x)$.
