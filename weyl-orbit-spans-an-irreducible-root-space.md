# Weyl orbit spans an irreducible root space

↑ **Parent:** [Irreducible root system](irreducible-root-system.md)

For an [irreducible root system](irreducible-root-system.md) spanning $V$, the span $U$ of a nonzero root's [Weyl group](weyl-group.md) orbit is invariant, as is $U^\perp$. A root reflection preserving both subspaces must have its normal entirely in one of them: otherwise its rank-one correction mixes the subspaces. Thus a proper nonzero $U$ would partition all roots into mutually orthogonal subsets, contradicting irreducibility. Consequently some orbit root is nonorthogonal to any given root. Since [Weyl reflections](weyl-reflection.md) preserve lengths, the pairwise integrality restrictions for nonorthogonal roots also bound length ratios of orthogonal roots. All squared-length ratios lie in $\{1,2,1/2,3,1/3\}$; there cannot be three distinct lengths, since ratios two and three relative to a shortest root would yield the forbidden ratio $3/2$.
