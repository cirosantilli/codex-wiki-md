# Nonvanishing of a nonprincipal Dirichlet L-function at one

↑ **Parent:** [Analytic number theory](analytic-number-theory-split.md)

For every nonprincipal [Dirichlet character](dirichlet-character.md), $L(1,\chi)\ne0$. A nonreal character is covered by [Euler product positivity for L-function nonvanishing](euler-product-positivity-for-l-function-nonvanishing.md) at height zero, since its squared character is nonprincipal.

For a real character, suppose $L(1,\chi)=0$. The simple zeta pole would cancel, making $F(s)=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi)$ an [entire function](entire-function.md). Its [nonnegative zeta-times-real-L coefficients](nonnegative-zeta-times-real-l-coefficients.md) satisfy $a_n\ge0$ and $a_{m^2}\ge1$. Termwise derivatives at two give $(-1)^kF^{(k)}(2)=\sum_na_n(\log n)^kn^{-2}$. The [Taylor series](taylor-series.md) of this entire function at two converges at zero. Every term there is nonnegative, and [Tonelli theorem](tonelli-theorem.md) identifies its sum as

$$
F(0)=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{2^k}{k!}\sum_n\frac{a_n(\log n)^k}{n^2}=\sum_na_n.
$$

The last series diverges because all square coefficients are at least one. This contradiction proves the claim. It is the positive-coefficient mechanism behind the more general [Landau theorem for a Dirichlet series with nonnegative coefficients](landau-theorem-for-a-dirichlet-series-with-nonnegative-coefficients.md).
