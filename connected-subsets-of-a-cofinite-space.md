# Connected subsets of a cofinite space

↑ **Parent:** [Cofinite topology](cofinite-topology.md)

For a [set](set-split.md) $X$ with its [cofinite topology](cofinite-topology.md), a [subset](subset.md) $S$ is a [connected subset](connected-subset.md) exactly when $S$ is empty, a [singleton](singleton-mathematics.md), or infinite. Indeed, its [subspace topology](subspace-topology.md) is the [cofinite topology](cofinite-topology.md) on $S$: the traces of [open sets](open-set.md) are precisely $S\setminus F$ for finite $F\subseteq S$, together with the [empty set](empty-set.md). For infinite $S$, two nonempty [open sets](open-set.md) intersect, because their [complements](complement-of-a-set.md) have finite [union](set-union.md). Hence $S$ is a [hyperconnected space](hyperconnected-space.md) and therefore a [connected space](connected-space.md). A finite $S$ has the [discrete topology](discrete-space.md), which admits a [separation of a topological space](separation-of-a-topological-space.md) as soon as $S$ has at least two points.
