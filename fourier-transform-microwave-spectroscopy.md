# Fourier transform microwave spectroscopy

↑ **Parent:** [Rotational spectroscopy](rotational-spectroscopy.md)

[Fourier transform microwave spectroscopy](fourier-transform-microwave-spectroscopy.md) excites gaseous [molecules](molecule-split.md) with a pulse of [electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation-split.md), then records the emitted time-dependent [electric field](electric-field-split.md). A [Fourier transform](fourier-transform-split.md) extracts transition [frequencies](frequency.md) from the decaying signal. In a cavity implementation, two facing mirrors form a resonator; tuned excitation, receiver electronics and digitization replace the molecule-counting readout of [molecular beam electric resonance](molecular-beam-electric-resonance.md). The pulsed radiation prepares [quantum coherence](quantum-coherence-in-a-specified-basis.md) between rotational [quantum states](quantum-state.md); it does not set the whole gas spinning at one classical [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md).

For documented laboratory context, [Evolution of Microwave Spectroscopy at the National Bureau of Standards (NBS) and the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) by F. J. Lovas and others](evolution-of-microwave-spectroscopy-at-the-national-bureau-of-standards-nbs-and-the-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology-nist-by-f-j-lovas-and-others.md) (§5, printed pp. 280–282) describes the NIST cavity instrument, phase-locked sources, mirror tuning and detector-mixer modifications. That account explains one instrument family; it does not establish the electronics of every later commercial [spectrometer](spectrometer.md).
