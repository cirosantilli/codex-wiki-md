# Finite-time stability versus power boundedness

↑ **Parent:** [Eigenvalue stability analysis of a finite difference method](eigenvalue-stability-analysis-of-a-finite-difference-method.md)

Stability of the [partial differential equation](partial-differential-equation-split.md) requires a bound uniform over refining meshes and time steps on every fixed physical time interval. This is distinct from demanding an all-time bound for one fixed [matrix](matrix.md). A fixed [matrix](matrix.md) is power bounded exactly when all [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) are in the closed [unit disk](unit-disk.md) and its unit-modulus [Jordan blocks](jordan-block.md) have size one. A time-step family can nevertheless have harmless [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) physical-time growth: $G_\tau=I+\tau N$ with $N^2=0$ gives $G_\tau^n=I+n\tau N$, uniformly bounded for $n\tau\leq T$ despite its nontrivial unit-modulus [Jordan block](jordan-block.md). Mesh dependence and the scaling of the step must therefore be kept in an [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) argument.
