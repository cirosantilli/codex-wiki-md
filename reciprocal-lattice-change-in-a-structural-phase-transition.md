# Reciprocal lattice change in a structural phase transition

↑ **Parent:** [Reciprocal lattice](reciprocal-lattice.md)

The [reciprocal lattice](reciprocal-lattice.md) of a [body-centered cubic lattice](body-centered-cubic-lattice.md) is face-centred cubic, and that of a [face-centered cubic lattice](face-centered-cubic-lattice.md) is body-centred cubic. In both cases its conventional cubic side is $4\pi/a$ if the direct conventional side is $a$. Thus a direct BCC-to-FCC [phase transition](phase-transition.md) reverses the reciprocal lattice types. The reciprocal primitive cell volume is $(2\pi)^3/\Omega$, so [thermal expansion](thermal-expansion.md) contracts reciprocal spacings, while a discontinuous change in direct [primitive unit cell](primitive-unit-cell.md) volume gives an inverse change in reciprocal cell volume.

A first-order structural transition need not continuously deform one perfect reciprocal array into another. Phase coexistence, new orientation variants, domains and loss of single-crystal alignment can produce two sets of diffraction peaks or many differently oriented sets. The two endpoint lattice types alone do not specify the transformation path or orientation relationship.
