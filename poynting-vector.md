# Poynting vector

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic wave](electromagnetic-wave.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poynting_vector)

In vacuum the [Poynting vector](poynting-vector.md)

$$
\mathbf S=\frac1{\mu_0}\mathbf E\times\mathbf B
$$

is the local [energy flux](energy-flux.md), measured in watts per square metre. Its [surface integral](surface-integral.md) $P=\int_A\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf n\,dA$ gives signed [power](power.md) crossing a surface with unit [normal vector](normal-vector.md) $\mathbf n$. Positive flux follows the chosen normal; for a closed surface the usual convention is outward. [Power](power.md) depends on the normal component and area, rather than just the field magnitude.

The [Poynting vector](poynting-vector.md) applies to steady fields as well as [electromagnetic waves](electromagnetic-wave.md). In a driven resistive [electrical circuit](electrical-circuit.md), surrounding fields can carry [energy](energy.md) into the conductor, where electrical [work](work.md) becomes [Joule heating](joule-heating.md). This turns a circuit-level power balance into a spatial description of energy delivery. [Haus and Melcher, Electromagnetic Fields and Energy, chapter 11, §§11.0–11.3](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-001-electromagnetic-fields-and-energy-spring-2008/0f814aee1c72517df7d7e7e96b7365e1_11.pdf).
