# Dynamical systems

↑ **Parent:** [Branches of physics](branches-of-physics.md)

The study of dynamical systems asks how a state changes in [time](time-in-physics.md): which states persist, which [orbits](orbit-dynamical-system.md) repeat, and how a small [perturbation](perturbation.md) changes the future. A [dynamical system](dynamical-system.md) combines a [state space](state-space.md) with an evolution rule. For a seasonal population, the state might be its abundance and one step one generation; for a [harmonic oscillator](simple-harmonic-motion.md), a [state vector](state-vector.md) includes both position and velocity. Choosing a sufficient state is essential: position alone does not determine where an oscillator moves next.

The two basic descriptions are a [discrete dynamical system](discrete-dynamical-system-split.md), $x_{n+1}=F(x_n)$, and an [ordinary differential equation](ordinary-differential-equation.md), $\dot x=f(x)$. Repeated [iteration of a map](iterated-function.md) gives $x_n=F^n(x_0)$; an [autonomous differential equation](autonomous-system-mathematics.md) with unique solutions gives a [flow map](flow-map.md) $x(t)=\Phi_t(x_0)$. In either case, evolving for two successive durations agrees with evolving for their sum. The [logistic map](logistic-map.md) provides a particularly simple [discrete dynamical system](discrete-dynamical-system-split.md); the [Lorenz system](lorenz-system.md) supplies a continuous-time example with a very different geometry.

[Deterministic evolution](deterministic-evolution.md) says that the exact rule and exact [initial condition](initial-condition.md) specify one future. Predicting that future from measurements is a separate question. An instrument supplies a range of possible [initial conditions](initial-condition.md), and a computation adds small errors. Near an [asymptotically stable](asymptotic-stability.md) [fixed point](fixed-point.md), nearby [orbits](orbit-dynamical-system.md) converge and the long-term state can become easier to predict. With [sensitive dependence on initial conditions](butterfly-effect.md), arbitrarily close starting states can eventually lead to a visible disagreement, even though each follows the same rule.

Much of the usefulness of [dynamical systems](dynamical-systems-split.md) comes from describing outcomes without writing every solution explicitly. [Fixed points](fixed-point.md) identify possible resting states; [periodic orbits](periodic-orbit.md) identify repeating behaviour; [stability theory](stability-theory.md) asks whether those behaviours survive small disturbances. A [bifurcation diagram](bifurcation-diagram.md) shows how changing a [bifurcation parameter](bifurcation-parameter.md) changes the possible outcomes. This is useful in [population dynamics](population-dynamics.md), where a stable abundance and an alternating abundance have different consequences, and in [thermal convection](thermal-convection.md), where a steady flow and a changing flow transport heat differently.

The beauty of the subject is that a small formula can organize a large variety of behaviour. The [logistic map](logistic-map.md) moves from an attracting [fixed point](fixed-point.md) to attracting [periodic orbits](periodic-orbit.md) and then to parameter values supporting sensitive motion. Its [period-doubling cascade](period-doubling-cascade.md) has a recognizable structure across many [unimodal maps](unimodal-interval-map.md). Nevertheless, [Devaney chaos](devaney-chaos.md), [Glendinning chaos](glendinning-chaos.md), and a positive [Lyapunov exponent](lyapunov-exponent.md) express different properties; a claim of chaos should specify its definition, its [state space](state-space.md), and the parameter values under consideration.
