# Chemical shift in photoelectron spectroscopy

↑ **Parent:** [X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy](x-ray-photoelectron-spectroscopy.md)

A [chemical shift in photoelectron spectroscopy](chemical-shift-in-photoelectron-spectroscopy.md) is a difference in [binding energy](binding-energy.md) for the same kind of [core electron](core-electron.md) in different chemical surroundings, using comparable [energy](energy-split.md) references. Identifying a [chemical element](chemical-element.md) assigns its family of lines; resolving chemical shifts within that family asks how its [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) are bonded. For example, distinguishing a [metal](metal.md) from its chemically modified surface requires more than detecting that element in both places.

The removal [energy](energy-split.md) compares the initial electronic state with the state left after emission. Redistribution of the remaining [electrons](electron.md) changes that final state's [energy](energy-split.md); a measured shift therefore includes relaxation as well as initial bonding effects.[p. 69: frozen-orbital approximation and relaxation](electron-spectroscopy-for-atoms-molecules-and-condensed-matter-by-kai-manne-borje-siegbahn.md)

This makes [chemical shift in photoelectron spectroscopy](chemical-shift-in-photoelectron-spectroscopy.md) useful but conditional. Compare the same [electron shell](electron-shell.md), the same [photoelectron binding energy reference](photoelectron-binding-energy-reference.md), and compatible sample conditions. Agreement with a chemically appropriate reference supports an assignment; a line position by itself need not uniquely determine an [oxidation state](oxidation-state.md). An unresolved sum of two chemical components can even have a centroid where neither component lies. The following [Gaussian mixture distribution](gaussian-mixture-distribution.md) shows exactly how that happens without claiming a material identification.
