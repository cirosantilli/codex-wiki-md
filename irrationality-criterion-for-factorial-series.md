# Irrationality criterion for factorial series

↑ **Parent:** [Factorial series](factorial-series.md)

A [factorial series](factorial-series.md) with integral digits $0\leq a_n<n$ is an [irrational number](irrational-number.md) exactly when positive digits occur infinitely often and digits below their maximum occur infinitely often. Under these two conditions every scaled tail $N!\sum_{n>N}a_n/n!$ lies strictly between zero and one. If the sum were a [rational number](rational-number.md) $A/B$, choose $N\geq B$; then $N!A/B$ and the scaled finite head are [integers](integer.md), contradicting the strict tail bound. Conversely, an eventually zero digit sequence gives a finite [rational number](rational-number.md) sum, and an eventually maximal sequence gives a finite head plus $1/N!$, again a [rational number](rational-number.md).
