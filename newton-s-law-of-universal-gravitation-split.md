# Newton's law of universal gravitation

↑ **Parent:** [Classical mechanics](classical-mechanics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton's_law_of_universal_gravitation)

[Newton's law of universal gravitation](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation-split.md) gives the attractive [force](force-split.md) between two [point masses](point-mass.md). With source [mass](mass.md) $M$ at the origin and relative [position](position.md) $\mathbf r$ of [mass](mass.md) $m$, write $r=|\mathbf r|>0$:

$$
\mathbf F_{M\to m}=-\frac{GMm}{r^3}\mathbf r,\qquad |\mathbf F_{M\to m}|=\frac{GMm}{r^2}.
$$

Here $G$ is the [gravitational constant](gravitational-constant.md). The minus sign points toward $M$; the magnitude follows an [inverse-square law](inverse-square-law.md), so doubling the separation quarters the [force](force-split.md). The opposite [force](force-split.md) on $M$ satisfies [Newton's third law](newton-s-third-law.md). [OpenStax: mutual gravitational forces](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/13-1-newtons-law-of-universal-gravitation)

Both [masses](mass.md) accelerate. Applying [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) to each in the isolated [two-body problem](two-body-problem.md) gives

$$
\ddot{\mathbf r}=-\frac{\mu}{r^3}\mathbf r,\qquad \mu=G(M+m).
$$

The relative [standard gravitational parameter](standard-gravitational-parameter.md) includes both [masses](mass.md); replacing it by $GM$ requires the [test particle](test-particle.md) approximation $m\ll M$.

Historically, Newton's Book III, Proposition VII argues that gravitational attraction applies to all bodies and scales with their [masses](mass.md); its second corollary states the [inverse-square law](inverse-square-law.md) for individual particles. [Newton, Principia, Book III, Proposition VII, Motte's 1729 translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Principles_of_Natural_Philosophy_(1729)/Proposition_7:_Gravity_tending_to_all_Bodies)
