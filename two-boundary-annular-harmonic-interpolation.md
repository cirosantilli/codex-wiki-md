# Two-boundary annular harmonic interpolation

↑ **Parent:** [Harmonic Fourier expansions in planar concentric domains](harmonic-fourier-expansions-in-planar-concentric-domains.md)

For a [harmonic function](harmonic-function.md) on the planar [annulus](annulus-mathematics.md) $a<r<b$, the radial amplitude of a sine or cosine [Fourier mode](fourier-mode.md) of positive integer index $n$ is a linear combination of $r^n$ and $r^{-n}$. If its values at $a,b$ are $A_n,B_n$, respectively, the displayed hyperbolic-sine formula gives that combination. It follows either by solving the two boundary equations or by putting $s=\log(r/a)$, which changes the radial equation to $R_{ss}-n^2R=0$. The zero angular mode is instead affine in $\log r$:

$$
R_0(r)=A_0\frac{\log(b/r)}{\log(b/a)}+B_0\frac{\log(r/a)}{\log(b/a)}.
$$

Superposing the modes gives the [harmonic function](harmonic-function.md) with suitable prescribed [Dirichlet boundary conditions](dirichlet-boundary-condition.md). For a fixed interior radius, high angular modes from either boundary are exponentially suppressed. Piecewise smooth boundary data are attained at continuity points, while jumps have the usual mean-value Fourier limit.
