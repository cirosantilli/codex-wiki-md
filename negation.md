# Negation

↑ **Parent:** [Boolean operation](boolean-operation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negation)

[Logical negation](negation.md) exchanges zero and one: $\neg0=1$ and $\neg1=0$. For a numerical [bit](bit.md) $x$, this is $1-x$; applying [logical negation](negation.md) twice restores $x$. It acts on the entire [propositional formula](propositional-formula.md) in its scope. Parentheses therefore matter: $\neg(P\vee Q)$ says that neither condition holds, while $(\neg P)\vee Q$ also accepts every case where $Q$ holds. At $(P,Q)=(1,1)$ their values are zero and one.

On a [set](set-split.md), [logical negation](negation.md) becomes taking a [complement](complement-of-a-set.md) in a fixed [universal set](universal-set.md). In [Boolean operations on events](boolean-operations-on-events.md), “not even” selects $\{1,3,5\}$. “Not both even and at least four” selects $\{1,2,3,5\}$; it does not require both qualifications to fail. [De Morgan's laws](de-morgan-s-laws.md) make the scope of these compound negations precise.
