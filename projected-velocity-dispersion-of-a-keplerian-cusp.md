# Projected velocity dispersion of a Keplerian cusp

↑ **Parent:** [Projected spherical Jeans residual](projected-spherical-jeans-residual.md)

For a scale-free tracer $\nu\propto r^{-k}$ in a point-mass [gravitational potential](newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md), with $k>1$ and constant [velocity-anisotropy parameter](velocity-anisotropy-parameter.md), the [Spherical Jeans equation](spherical-jeans-equation.md) gives $\sigma_r^2=GM_\bullet/[(k+1-2\beta)r]$ when the outer pressure term vanishes. Project the tracer density and its second [stellar velocity moment](stellar-velocity-moment.md) along the [line of sight](line-of-sight.md). The substitution $r=R\sec\chi$ reduces both integrals to powers of $\cos\chi$ and hence the [beta function](beta-function.md), yielding the displayed expression. The ratio of the two numerator angular integrals is $k/(k+1)$. This makes the mass-anisotropy ambiguity explicit. At $k=2$ the [velocity-anisotropy parameter](velocity-anisotropy-parameter.md) dependence cancels and $\sigma_{\rm los}^2=2GM_\bullet/(3\pi R)$; that special slope does not remove the degeneracy in general. Physical use also requires a nonnegative tracer distribution and control of outer departures from the power law.
