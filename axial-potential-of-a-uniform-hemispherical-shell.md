# Axial potential of a uniform hemispherical shell

↑ **Parent:** [Newtonian gravitational potential](newtonian-gravitational-potential.md)

For constant [mass density](density.md) $\rho$ on the lower half of the [spherical shell](spherical-shell.md) $a\leq|\mathbf r'|\leq b$, with $0<a<b$, the displayed [Newtonian gravitational potential](newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) applies at $(0,0,h)$ for every $h>0$, with zero at infinity. Use source [spherical coordinates](spherical-coordinate-system.md) with polar angle $\alpha\in[\pi/2,\pi]$. For a fixed source radius $s$,

$$
\int_{\pi/2}^{\pi}\frac{\sin\alpha\,d\alpha}{\sqrt{s^2+h^2-2sh\cos\alpha}}
=\frac{s+h-\sqrt{s^2+h^2}}{sh}.
$$

Multiplication by $-2\pi G\rho s^2$ and [integration](integral-split.md) from $a$ to $b$ leaves the [antiderivative](antiderivative.md) $s^3/3+hs^2/2-(s^2+h^2)^{3/2}/3$, proving the formula. The entire positive axis is outside the matter, even for $a<h<b$, so there are no interior/exterior radial cases. The [limit](limit-of-a-function.md) at the origin is $-\pi G\rho(b^2-a^2)$; far away the leading term is $-GM/h$, with total [mass](mass.md) $M=2\pi\rho(b^3-a^3)/3$.
