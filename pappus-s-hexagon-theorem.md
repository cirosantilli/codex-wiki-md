<h1 id="pappus-s-hexagon-theorem">Pappus's hexagon theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Projective plane](projective-plane.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus's_hexagon_theorem)

For two triples of distinct points on two projective lines, the intersections $r_{ij}=p_iq_j\cap p_jq_i$ are collinear when the configuration is defined. Normalize a [projective frame](projective-frame.md) so $p_1=[0,1,0]$, $p_2=[1,1,0]$, $q_1=[0,0,1]$, $q_2=[1,0,1]$, and write $p_3=[1,\alpha,0]$, $q_3=[1,0,\beta]$. Then representatives of the three intersections are $(1,1,1)$, $(1,\alpha,\beta)$, and $(\alpha\beta-1,\alpha(\beta-1),\beta(\alpha-1))$. The last is $\alpha\beta$ times the first minus the second, proving collinearity by [linear dependence](linear-dependence.md). This gives a coordinate proof over any [field](field.md) when the normalizations are available.
