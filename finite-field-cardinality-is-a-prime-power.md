# Finite field cardinality is a prime power

↑ **Parent:** [Finite field](finite-field.md)

A [finite field](finite-field.md) has positive [characteristic](characteristic-of-a-field.md), because its repeated sums of $1$ cannot all be distinct. By the [characteristic of an integral domain](characteristic-of-an-integral-domain.md) theorem, this is a [prime number](prime-number-split.md) $p$. Its multiples of $1$ form a copy of the [prime field](prime-field.md) $\mathbb F_p$: a nonzero multiple $a1$ has inverse $b1$, where the [Bezout identity](bezout-s-identity.md) $ab+cp=1$ supplies $b$.

View $F$ as a [vector space](vector-space-split.md) over this [prime field](prime-field.md). Repeatedly extend a [linearly independent set](linear-independence.md) until it is maximal; the procedure terminates because $F$ is finite. Maximality makes it a [basis](basis.md). If the [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) is $d$, unique coordinates give exactly $p^d$ elements. Thus **the cardinality is a power of a single prime**, rather than an arbitrary composite integer.
