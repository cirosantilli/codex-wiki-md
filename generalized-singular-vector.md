# Generalized singular vector

↑ **Parent:** [Absolutely one-homogeneous functional](absolutely-one-homogeneous-functional.md)

Let $K:U\to V$ be a [bounded linear operator](continuous-linear-operator.md) from a real [Banach space](banach-space-split.md) to a real [Hilbert space](hilbert-space-split.md), and let $J$ be a proper lower-semicontinuous convex [absolutely one-homogeneous functional](absolutely-one-homogeneous-functional.md) on $U$. The normalization and source relation imply $\lambda=J(u_\lambda)$ by the [Euler identity for a convex one-homogeneous functional](euler-identity-for-a-convex-one-homogeneous-functional.md). For the [norm](norm.md) penalty $J(u)=\|u\|$ on a [Hilbert space](hilbert-space-split.md), its nonzero-point [subgradient](subgradient.md) is $u/\|u\|$. Hence $K^*Ku_\lambda=u_\lambda/\|u_\lambda\|^2$: $v=u_\lambda/\|u_\lambda\|$ is a [right singular vector](right-singular-vector.md) with [singular value](singular-value.md) $\sigma=1/\|u_\lambda\|$, and $Ku_\lambda$ is the corresponding [left singular vector](left-singular-vector.md). Thus the definition recovers the ordinary operator singular-vector relation using the one-homogeneous norm penalty. Uniqueness of the vector from its data requires extra hypotheses on $K$ and $J$.
