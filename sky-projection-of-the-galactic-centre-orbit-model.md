# Sky projection of the Galactic-centre orbit model

↑ **Parent:** [Galactic-centre point-mass orbit model](galactic-centre-point-mass-orbit-model.md)

Let $z$ increase away from the observer, with $x,y$ spanning the sky plane. A rotation $R=R_z(\Omega)R_x(i)R_z(\omega)$ sends the orbital-plane coordinates to $\mathbf r=R\mathbf r'$. Here $i$ is [orbital inclination](orbital-inclination.md), $\Omega$ the [longitude of ascending node](longitude-of-ascending-node.md), and $\omega$ the [argument of periapsis](argument-of-periapsis.md). For distance $D\gg a$, the small-angle model gives [angular positions](angular-position.md) $\theta_x=x/D$, $\theta_y=y/D$ in [radians](radian.md). [Doppler spectroscopy](doppler-spectroscopy.md) instead supplies the line-of-sight [velocity](velocity.md) $v_{\rm los}=\dot z+v_{\rm sys}$, with a fitted systemic offset $v_{\rm sys}$.

Define $\alpha=a/D$, the angular scale of the **deprojected** fitted [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md). It is not generally the semi-major axis of the apparent ellipse on the sky. Projection also need not send a true focus to a focus of the apparent ellipse. Fitting the rotated orbit preserves that distinction; measuring a bounding ellipse alone loses it.
