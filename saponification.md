# Saponification

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrolysis](hydrolysis.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saponification)

[Saponification](saponification.md) is [hydrolysis](hydrolysis.md) of an [ester](ester.md) under basic conditions. The [conductometric saponification assay](conductometric-saponification-assay.md) follows [ethyl acetate](ethyl-acetate.md) consumption by [hydroxide](hydroxide.md), producing [ethanol](ethanol.md) and an [acetate ion](acetate-ion.md); its equal-starting-[concentration](concentration.md) case provides a practical [second-order reaction](second-order-reaction.md) example.

[Saponification](saponification.md) is [ester](ester.md) [hydrolysis](hydrolysis.md) promoted by a [chemical base](base-chemistry.md), with net consumption of [hydroxide](hydroxide.md) to form a [carboxylate](carboxylate.md) and an [alcohol](alcohol.md). For a [carboxylic acid](carboxylic-acid.md)-derived [ester](ester.md), the net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) is

$$
\mathrm{RCOOR'}+\mathrm{OH^-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{RCOO^-}+\mathrm{R'OH}.
$$

The [hydroxide](hydroxide.md) is a [reactant](reactant.md), rather than merely a regenerated [catalyst](catalyst.md).[Organic Chemistry by John McMurry; §21.6, Conversion of Esters into Carboxylic Acids: Hydrolysis; Figure 21.8 discussion of carboxylate formation](organic-chemistry-by-john-mcmurry/21-6-chemistry-of-esters.md)

For [ethyl acetate](ethyl-acetate.md), the balanced [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) becomes

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOC_2H_5}+\mathrm{OH^-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH_3COO^-}+\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}.
$$

Both sides contain four [carbon](carbon.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), nine [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), three [oxygen](oxygen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), and net [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $-1$. A [sodium](sodium.md) [ion](ion.md) supplied by [sodium hydroxide](sodium-hydroxide.md) is a [spectator ion](spectator-ion.md); the [net ionic equation](net-ionic-equation.md) omits it without losing [electric charge conservation](charge-conservation.md).

In the usual [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md), [hydroxide](hydroxide.md) adds to the [carbonyl group](carbonyl-group.md), making a [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md). Departure of the [alcohol](alcohol.md)'s [conjugate base](conjugate-base.md) gives a [carboxylic acid](carboxylic-acid.md); transfer of a [proton](proton.md) then gives the [carboxylate](carboxylate.md) and the [alcohol](alcohol.md). Subsequent addition of a [chemical acid](acid-chemistry.md) gives the [carboxylic acid](carboxylic-acid.md) by [protonation](protonation.md):

$$
\mathrm{RCOO^-}+\mathrm{H_3O^+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{RCOOH}+\mathrm{H_2O}.
$$

This separate acidification changes the [reaction product](product-chemistry.md)'s [protonation](protonation.md) state.[Organic Chemistry by John McMurry; §21.6, Figure 21.8 discussion: addition, elimination, deprotonation and subsequent aqueous HCl treatment](organic-chemistry-by-john-mcmurry/21-6-chemistry-of-esters.md)

The [conductometric saponification assay](conductometric-saponification-assay.md) makes [hydroxide](hydroxide.md) consumption observable. A university procedure equilibrates separate [ethyl acetate](ethyl-acetate.md) and [sodium hydroxide](sodium-hydroxide.md) stocks in constant-[temperature](temperature-split.md) baths, then mixes them in a rinsed conductivity cell. Two [platinum](platinum.md) [electrodes](electrode.md) and an alternating-voltage [Wheatstone bridge](wheatstone-bridge.md) supply an [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) readout. A matched [sodium hydroxide](sodium-hydroxide.md)–[water](water.md) dilution checks the initial signal; [potassium chloride](potassium-chloride.md) calibrates the cell constant. Replacing [hydroxide](hydroxide.md) with less mobile [acetate ions](acetate-ion.md) lowers [electrical conductance](electrical-conductance.md), supporting an inference about changing [concentration](concentration.md). The manual supplies a procedure rather than measured outcomes. [Temperature](temperature-split.md) drift or additional [ions](ion.md) can change the signal; it cannot alone identify the full [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md).[Unit 7, Experiment 1, printed p. 63: electrodes, bridge and ionic conductance; Experiment 2, printed pp. 66–67: preparation, resistance readout and controls](manual-for-advanced-physical-chemistry-laboratory-ii-by-nasima-arshad.md)
