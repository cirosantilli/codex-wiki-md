<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Let $\Delta T=T-T_c$, with $a,c>0$ fixed. The equilibrium [phase diagram](../../../../phase-diagram.md) has boundaries

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Delta T&=0,&&b>0&&\text{continuous},\\
\Delta T&=\frac{3b^2}{16ac},&&b<0&&\text{first order}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

For either branch, larger $\Delta T$ is disordered and smaller $\Delta T$ is ordered. Both signs of the ordered [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) are symmetry-related [global minima](../../../../global-minimum.md) throughout the ordered region, rather than separate regions selected by temperature and $b$.

The branches meet at the [tricritical point](../../../../tricritical-point.md) $(\Delta T,b)=(0,0)$. Directly on $b=0$, minimizing $rm^2+cm^6/3$ gives

$$
\boxed{|m_{\rm eq}|=\left[\frac{a(T_c-T)}c\right]^{1/4}\quad(T<T_c),\qquad \beta_{\rm tri}=\frac14.}
$$

The transition there remains continuous, with a different [mean-field critical exponent](../../../../mean-field-critical-exponent.md). For $b<0$, the metastability boundaries are $\Delta T=0$ for the disordered state and $\Delta T=b^2/(4ac)$ for the ordered state. The [first-order phase transition](../../../../first-order-phase-transition.md) curve lies strictly between them, as shown in the figure. These spinodal curves describe possible hysteretic continuation of local minima, not equilibrium phase boundaries.
