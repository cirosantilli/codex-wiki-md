<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Insert the harmonic variables into the forward map and reduce the squared trigonometric functions. This gives

$$
\boxed{x(t)=A\cos\vartheta-
\frac{3\lambda A^2}{2m\omega^2}+
\frac{\lambda A^2}{2m\omega^2}\cos2\vartheta+O(\lambda^2),}
$$



$$
\boxed{p(t)=-m\omega A\sin\vartheta-
\frac{\lambda A^2}{\omega}\sin2\vartheta+O(\lambda^2).}
$$

Indeed $p=m\dot x$ at this order. The [second harmonic and mean displacement of a weakly cubic oscillator](../../../../../second-harmonic-and-mean-displacement-of-a-weakly-cubic-oscillator.md) show asymmetry of the motion: for positive $\lambda$ the mean shifts towards negative $x$. There is **no first-order [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) shift**, although the waveform is no longer purely sinusoidal.

The small parameter is $|\lambda|A/(m\omega^2)\ll1$. A purely cubic perturbation is unbounded in one direction, so these formulas describe small local [oscillations](../../../../../oscillation.md) around the stable origin, not globally confined motion at arbitrary [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). Amplitude $A$ labels the transformed orbit and must be inferred using the inverse map if physical initial position and [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) are supplied.
