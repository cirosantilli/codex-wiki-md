<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

In Cartesian components, $\partial L/\partial v_i=mv_i+eA_i$ and $\partial L/\partial x_i=ev_j\partial_iA_j-e\partial_i\phi$. The [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) therefore gives

$$
m\dot v_i=e[-\partial_i\phi-\partial_tA_i+v_j(\partial_iA_j-\partial_jA_i)]
=e[E_i+(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)_i].
$$

Thus **the minimally coupled particle [Lagrangian](../../../../lagrangian.md) yields the [Lorentz force](../../../../lorentz-force.md)**.

For an arbitrary smooth gauge function $\chi$, take $\mathbf A\to\mathbf A+\nabla\chi$ and $\phi\to\phi-\partial_t\chi$. Both fields are unchanged, while

$$
L\to L+e\left(\partial_t\chi+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla\chi\right)
=L+e\frac{d\chi}{dt}.
$$

The [action](../../../../action.md) changes only by an endpoint term, so fixed-endpoint variation gives the same motion. [Canonical momentum](../../../../canonical-momentum.md) shifts by $e\nabla\chi$, whereas [kinetic momentum](../../../../kinetic-momentum.md) $m\mathbf v$ and the [Lorentz force](../../../../lorentz-force.md) are gauge invariant. This is the relevant [gauge invariance](../../../../gauge-invariance.md) of the classical particle dynamics.
