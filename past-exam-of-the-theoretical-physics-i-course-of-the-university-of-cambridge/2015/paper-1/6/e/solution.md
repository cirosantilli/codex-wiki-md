<h1 id="6/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For uniform unconstrained [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md), the positive [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) coefficient favors a spatially constant minimizer. Put $u=|\mathbf m|^2\ge0$. The remaining free-energy difference is $a(T-T_c)u+bu^2$. Its minimum is

$$
\boxed{\begin{cases}
\mathbf m=0,&T\ge T_c,\\
\mathbf m=m_0\hat{\mathbf n},\quad
m_0=\sqrt{\dfrac{a(T_c-T)}{2b}},\quad |\hat{\mathbf n}|=1,&T<T_c.
\end{cases}}
$$

Below $T_c$ the amplitude is fixed but its direction is arbitrary; the set of uniform minima is a two-sphere. The free-energy minimum is $f_0-a^2(T_c-T)^2/(4b)$ below $T_c$ and $f_0$ above. The [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) vanishes continuously, so this is a continuous, second-order transition within [Landau theory](../../../../../landau-theory.md).

The [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) has internal $O(3)$ spin-rotation/reflection symmetry; a chosen nonzero vector leaves an $O(2)$ subgroup invariant. Restricting to proper rotations, this is **$SO(3)\to SO(2)$ [spontaneous symmetry breaking](../../../../../spontaneous-symmetry-breaking.md)**. There are two transverse orientation directions and a massive amplitude direction below the transition. Spatially varying directions cost [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) energy, and imposed boundaries or defects can prevent a uniform minimizer. In strictly two-dimensional equilibrium with short-range interactions, the fluctuation caveat of part (d) still applies: minimizing this polynomial functional at mean-field level does not overturn the absence of finite-temperature spontaneous [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md).
