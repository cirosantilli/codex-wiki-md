[Mean-field theory](../../../../../mean-field-theory.md) replaces fluctuating interactions with the average field produced by the other degrees of freedom. For an interacting [spin](../../../../../spin.md) system, write $\mathbf S_i=\mathbf S+\delta\mathbf S_i$ and neglect connected products $\delta\mathbf S_i\cdot\delta\mathbf S_j$. This reduces the many-body [partition function](../../../../../canonical-partition-function.md) to independent single-site factors coupled through a [self-consistency equation](../../../../../self-consistency-equation.md) for $\mathbf S$.

The approximation is most reliable when many effectively independent neighbors contribute, for sufficiently long-range interactions, or above the [upper critical dimension](../../../../../upper-critical-dimension.md) away from the fluctuation-dominated critical region. It usually becomes least reliable near a continuous transition in low dimensions, where correlations extend over long distances. It can estimate ordering scales and reveal candidate broken symmetries while giving incorrect [critical exponents](../../../../../critical-exponent.md) or even a spurious transition. The two-dimensional isotropic model in the later parts is an especially important example of this limitation.
