<h1 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An invertible change $(q,p)\mapsto(Q,P)$ is canonical when it preserves the [Poisson bracket](../../../../../../poisson-bracket.md). Its fundamental brackets must be

$$
\boxed{\{Q_i,Q_j\}=0,\qquad\{P_i,P_j\}=0,\qquad
\{Q_i,P_j\}=\delta_{ij}.}
$$

The [chain rule](../../../../../../chain-rule.md) then makes the bracket of any two [observables](../../../../../../observable.md) identical in the old and new coordinates. Consequently [Hamilton's equations](../../../../../../hamilton-s-equations.md) retain their canonical form. A time-dependent [canonical transformation](../../../../../../canonical-transformation.md) also requires the corresponding extra term in the transformed [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md); bracket preservation by itself concerns each fixed time.
