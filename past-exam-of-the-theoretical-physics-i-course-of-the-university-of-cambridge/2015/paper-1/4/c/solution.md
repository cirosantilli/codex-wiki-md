<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

An infinitesimal rotation about $x$ gives $\delta\psi_y=-\epsilon\psi_z$ and $\delta\psi_z=\epsilon\psi_y$. Both the squared transverse velocity and squared transverse slope are rotational invariants. [Noether's theorem](../../../../../noether-conserved-quantity-for-a-mechanical-point-symmetry.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\ell_x=\rho(\psi_y\dot\psi_z-\psi_z\dot\psi_y),\qquad
\mathcal J_x=-F(\psi_y\psi_z'-\psi_z\psi_y').}
$$

Differentiating and using the two [wave equations](../../../../../wave-equation-split.md) gives

$$
\partial_t\ell_x=F(\psi_y\psi_z''-\psi_z\psi_y'')
=-\partial_x\mathcal J_x.
$$

Consequently **$L_x=\int\ell_x\,dx$ is the [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) about the equilibrium string axis**, conserved when the net endpoint [torque](../../../../../torque.md) flux vanishes. For a finite segment, $dL_x/dt=-[\mathcal J_x]_{\rm left}^{\rm right}$. It is not momentum associated with translating the coordinate $x$; its symmetry rotates the two physical transverse displacement components into one another.
