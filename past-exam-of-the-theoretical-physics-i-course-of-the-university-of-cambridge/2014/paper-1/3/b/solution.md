<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The assumed relation makes the old and new actions differ by the endpoint term $G(t_f)-G(t_i)$. Fixed endpoint values of both coordinate sets make its variation zero. Therefore stationarity of the old [action](../../../../../action.md) is equivalent to stationarity of

$$
\int\left(\sum_iP_i\dot Q_i-\mathcal H(Q,P,t)\right)dt.
$$

Varying independent $Q_i,P_i$ and integrating $P_i\delta\dot Q_i$ by parts gives

$$
\delta S=\int\sum_i[(\dot Q_i-\mathcal H_{P_i})\delta P_i
-(\dot P_i+\mathcal H_{Q_i})\delta Q_i]dt.
$$

Both coefficients vanish: **the new variables satisfy [Hamilton's equations](../../../../../hamilton-s-equations.md)**, so a locally invertible transformation with this [action](../../../../../action.md) identity is canonical.
