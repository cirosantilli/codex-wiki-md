<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At mean-field level, positive gradient stiffness favours a spatially uniform [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md). Writing $r=|\phi|$, minimization of $f=ar^2+r^4/2$ gives

$$
\boxed{\phi=0\quad(a\ge0),\qquad
\phi=\sqrt{-a}\,e^{i\delta}\quad(a<0),}
$$

with arbitrary constant $\delta$. The minimized density is zero above the transition and $-a^2/2$ below it. The [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) vanishes continuously as $(-a)^{1/2}$; the first [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) of the minimized free [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is continuous while the second changes. This is the continuous mean-field transition.

The broken symmetry is **[global U(1) symmetry](../../../../../global-u-1-symmetry.md)**, not merely the Ising sign symmetry. Selecting one ordered phase breaks this continuous symmetry and leaves a soft phase direction. These are minima of the supplied [Landau free energy](../../../../../landau-free-energy.md), not an exact finite-temperature long-range-order claim in one dimension: phase fluctuations invalidate that inference for a short-range one-dimensional system.[§4.2.3, continuous symmetry, phase fluctuations and the Mermin-Wagner theorem](../../../../../statistical-field-theory-by-david-tong.md)
