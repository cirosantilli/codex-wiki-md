<h1 id="4/a/causal-support-and-contour-conditions">Causal support and contour conditions</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

A [retarded Green function](../../../../retarded-green-function.md) obeys $G(\tau)=0$ for $\tau<0$ and

$$
(\partial_\tau^2+1-\varepsilon\partial_\tau^3)G(\tau)=\delta(\tau).
$$

For a bounded or suitably tempered response represented by the real-axis Fourier integral, analyticity in the upper [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) half-plane is required, with suitable behaviour at infinity. For negative $\tau$, $e^{-i\omega\tau}$ decays on a large upper semicircle. If that half-plane has no singularities, contour closure gives zero response before the source. Lower-half-plane poles give factors decaying for positive time. Real poles are approached from below for a retarded prescription; at $\varepsilon=0$, for example, $G_R(\tau)=\Theta(\tau)\sin\tau$.

The [causality versus stability of a Green function](../../../../causality-versus-stability-of-a-green-function.md) distinction matters: an unstable initial-value problem can still have a strictly causal response that grows exponentially. Such a response has no ordinary real-axis [Fourier transform](../../../../fourier-transform-split.md). Its inverse [Laplace transform](../../../../laplace-transform.md) must run on a [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) contour above its growth poles. Thus “upper poles violate causality” is correct only with the stable/tempered real-axis Fourier assumptions, not as an unconditional theorem about differential equations.
