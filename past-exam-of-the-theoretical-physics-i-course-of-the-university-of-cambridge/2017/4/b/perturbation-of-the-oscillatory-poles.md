<h1 id="4/b/perturbation-of-the-oscillatory-poles">Perturbation of the oscillatory poles</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

At $\varepsilon=0$ the algebraic poles are $\boxed{\omega_\pm^{(0)}=\pm1}$; a retarded limiting prescription displaces them infinitesimally below the real axis. Set $\omega_s=s+\delta_s$, with $s=\pm1$ and $\delta_s=O(\varepsilon)$. The denominator becomes

$$
1-(s+\delta_s)^2-i\varepsilon(s+\delta_s)^3
=-2s\delta_s-i\varepsilon s^3+O(\varepsilon^2).
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\omega_\pm=\pm1-\frac{i\varepsilon}{2}+O(\varepsilon^2)}.
$$

For positive $\varepsilon$ these two poles move below the real axis, giving slowly damped oscillations. For negative $\varepsilon$ they move above it and the slow oscillations grow. This is only the slow part of the [radiation-reaction oscillator pole structure](../../../../radiation-reaction-oscillator-pole-structure.md); the third [pole](../../../../pole.md) must still be examined.
