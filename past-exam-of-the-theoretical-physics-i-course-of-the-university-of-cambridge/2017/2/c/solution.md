<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In a uniform-field region, $z$ is cyclic and $\gamma$ is constant. Its equation is $d(\gamma m\dot z)/dt=0$. The radial [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) is

$$
\frac{d}{dt}(\gamma m\dot\rho)=\gamma m\rho\dot\theta^2+qB\rho\dot\theta,
$$

so with $\dot\theta=-\Omega$ and $qB/(\gamma m)=2\Omega$,

$$
\boxed{\ddot z=0,\qquad\ddot\rho+\Omega^2\rho=0\quad\text{inside}}.
$$

The radial equation applies on intervals with a regular signed cylindrical-coordinate description; the Cartesian solution handles axis crossings.

In a field-free region, the equations are

$$
\boxed{\ddot z=0,\qquad\ddot\rho=\rho\dot\theta^2,\qquad
\frac{d}{dt}(\gamma m\rho^2\dot\theta)=0}.
$$

For this particle $p_\theta=0$, so $\dot\theta=0$ and $\ddot\rho=0$ outside, until a coordinate change is needed at an axis crossing.

**Axial [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) is constant within each uniform region, but not across the ends.** In a smooth fringe,

$$
\frac{d}{dt}(\gamma m\dot z)=\frac q2\rho^2\dot\theta B'(z).
$$

The entrance develops an azimuthal speed $|\Omega|\rho_0$ while radial [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) remains zero. Conservation of total speed gives

$$
\boxed{\dot z_{\rm in}=w=\sqrt{v^2-\Omega^2\rho_0^2}}.
$$

Forward passage presumes the radicand is positive. The exit fringe changes axial [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) again. Neglecting these small changes is a [paraxial approximation](../../../../paraxial-approximation.md), requiring $|\Omega|\rho_0\ll v$, rather than an exact consequence of cylindrical [symmetry](../../../../symmetry-physics.md).
