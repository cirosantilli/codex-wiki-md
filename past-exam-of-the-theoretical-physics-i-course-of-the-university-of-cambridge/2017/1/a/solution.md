<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write $A=\pi a^2$ for the cross-sectional area, and use $z$ consistently for the longitudinal coordinate: the paper's later $x$ is the same coordinate. The [canonical momentum](../../../../canonical-momentum.md) is a density per reference length,

$$
\boxed{\Pi(z,t)=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot\psi}=A\rho\dot\psi}.
$$

The [Legendre transform](../../../../convex-conjugate.md) gives the [Hamiltonian density](../../../../hamiltonian-density.md) in its proper canonical variables:

$$
\boxed{\mathcal H(\psi,\Pi)=\Pi\dot\psi-\mathcal L=\frac{\Pi^2}{2A\rho}+\frac{AK}{2}(\psi')^2}.
$$

The [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) is

$$
\partial_t(A\rho\dot\psi)+\partial_z(-AK\psi')=0,
\qquad \boxed{\ddot\psi=\frac K\rho\psi''}.
$$

For a twice-differentiable [wave packet](../../../../wave-packet.md) $\psi=f(z-ct)$, the derivatives are $\ddot\psi=c^2f''$ and $\psi''=f''$. Thus the nontrivial packet solves the [wave equation](../../../../wave-equation-split.md) when

$$
\boxed{c=\sqrt{K/\rho}}.
$$

The negative sign gives the opposite propagation direction. Here $\Pi$ has units $\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$, $\mathcal H$ has units $\mathrm{J\,m^{-1}}$, and $c$ has units $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The result follows from the supplied axial stiffness in the [Lagrangian](../../../../lagrangian.md). For a freely contracting thin elastic rod the relevant longitudinal modulus is normally [Young's modulus](../../../../young-s-modulus.md), rather than the [bulk modulus](../../../../bulk-modulus.md); the question's given [Lagrangian](../../../../lagrangian.md) fixes which coefficient is being used.
