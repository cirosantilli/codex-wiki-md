<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a rightgoing [wave packet](../../../../wave-packet.md), put $\xi=z-ct$ and assume $f'$ decays sufficiently rapidly. Then

$$
\Pi=-A\rho c f'(\xi),\qquad \mathcal H=A\rho c^2[f'(\xi)]^2.
$$

The shift charge becomes

$$
\boxed{Q_1=-A\rho c\int f'(\xi)d\xi=-A\rho c[f(+\infty)-f(-\infty)]}.
$$

It is time independent. It vanishes for a localized displacement packet that returns to the same displacement at both ends, but need not vanish for a localized strain pulse joining different asymptotic displacements.

For the dilation charge, substitute $z=\xi+ct$:

$$
Q_2=A\rho c\int(ct-z)[f'(\xi)]^2dz
=\boxed{-A\rho c\int\xi[f'(\xi)]^2d\xi}.
$$

The change of variables removes all time dependence. This charge also need not vanish; choosing an origin at the packet's [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) centroid can make it zero. Conservation assumes the integral exists and there is no boundary flux, rather than merely assuming that the [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is finite.
