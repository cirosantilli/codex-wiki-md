<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

In the negligible bead-mass limit the bead contribution to $\mathcal Q$ vanishes. Since the particle starts at rest, $\mathcal Q=0$, and hence $r^2\dot\theta+b\dot z=0$. Assume $b\ne0$ and a regular massless limit. Integrating along the trajectory gives

$$
\Delta z=-\frac1b\int_0^T r^2\dot\theta\,dt=-\frac1b\oint_C(x\,dy-y\,dx),
$$

where $C$ is the closed projection onto the plane perpendicular to the coil axis. The given final $r$ and $\theta$ ensure that this projection closes. [Green's theorem](../../../../../green-theorem.md) identifies

$$
A=\frac12\oint_C(x\,dy-y\,dx)=\frac12\oint_C r^2\,d\theta,\qquad \boxed{\Delta z=-\frac{2A}{b}}.
$$

Thus **$A$ is the [signed area](../../../../../signed-area.md) enclosed by the transverse projection**, positive for counterclockwise traversal viewed from positive $z$. Self-intersecting projections contribute their winding-weighted areas; it is neither an unsigned area nor an area of a surface in three dimensions. For $b>0$, a positive [signed area](../../../../../signed-area.md) gives negative axial displacement. This is [signed-area displacement under a helical constraint](../../../../../signed-area-displacement-under-a-helical-constraint.md): returning to the same projected coordinates can leave a nonzero axial displacement.
