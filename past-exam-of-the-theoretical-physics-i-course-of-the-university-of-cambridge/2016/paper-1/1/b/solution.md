<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Set $\Delta=\theta-\theta_B$ and $u=z-b\theta_B$. Applying the [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) separately to $r,\theta,z,\theta_B$ gives

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
m(\ddot r-r\dot\theta^2)&=-k(r-a\cos\Delta),\\
\frac{d}{dt}(mr^2\dot\theta)&=-kar\sin\Delta,\\
m\ddot z&=-ku,\\
M(a^2+b^2)\ddot\theta_B&=k(ar\sin\Delta+bu).
\end{aligned}}
$$

For example, $\partial L/\partial\theta=-kar\sin\Delta$ whereas $\partial L/\partial\theta_B=kar\sin\Delta+kb u$; these signs also follow from equal and opposite [spring](../../../../../spring.md) [forces](../../../../../force-split.md) projected on the permitted bead motion. The angular particle equation can equivalently be written $m(r^2\ddot\theta+2r\dot r\dot\theta)=-kar\sin\Delta$. The factor $M(a^2+b^2)$ is the bead's inertia with respect to the helical coordinate, rather than its [moment of inertia](../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) about the axis alone.
