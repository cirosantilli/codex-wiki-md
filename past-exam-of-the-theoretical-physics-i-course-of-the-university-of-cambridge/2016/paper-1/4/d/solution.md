<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Contract the two Cartesian indices and use the unit length of each molecular orientation:

$$
\operatorname{Tr}S=\sum_\alpha S_{\alpha\alpha}=\frac1N\sum_i\left(3\sum_{\alpha=x,y,z}s_{i\alpha}^2-\sum_{\alpha=x,y,z}\delta_{\alpha\alpha}\right)=\frac1N\sum_i(3-3)=\boxed{0}.
$$

This is exact for each configuration, with no [ensemble average](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) assumption. The [nematic order parameter](../../../../../nematic-order-parameter.md) is also a real [symmetric second-rank tensor](../../../../../symmetric-second-rank-tensor.md). Under a common [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) it transforms as $S\mapsto RSR^{\mathsf T}$, so its powers give [rotational invariants of a symmetric traceless tensor](../../../../../rotational-invariant-of-a-symmetric-traceless-tensor.md) by the [cyclic property of the trace](../../../../../cyclic-property-of-the-trace.md). It is unchanged by independent sign flips of the molecular [vectors](../../../../../vector.md), explaining why it is adapted to nematic rather than polar order.
