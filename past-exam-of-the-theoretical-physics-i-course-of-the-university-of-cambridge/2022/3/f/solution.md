<h1 id="3/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The coarse-grained [order parameter](../../../../order-parameter.md) is a [real scalar field](../../../../real-scalar-field.md) because the microscopic spin has one component. At $B=0$, [spin inversion symmetry](../../../../spin-inversion-symmetry.md) takes $m\mapsto-m$, so an analytic local [Landau free energy](../../../../landau-free-energy.md) contains only even powers. Near a [continuous phase transition](../../../../continuous-phase-transition.md), the quadratic coefficient changes sign linearly, represented by $a(T-T_c)$ with $a>0$. A positive quartic coefficient stabilizes the density when the quadratic term becomes negative, giving the two [minima](../../../../minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) $m=\pm\sqrt{a(T_c-T)/b}$ below $T_c$.

The ferromagnetic interaction favours neighbouring spins pointing alike, so spatial variation costs energy; its leading long-wavelength form is the positive [gradient](../../../../gradient.md) term $c(\partial_xm)^2$. The source term $-Bm$ favours alignment with the applied [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) and explicitly breaks [spin inversion symmetry](../../../../spin-inversion-symmetry.md) at fixed nonzero $B$. Finally $f_0(T)$ is the spin-independent background [free-energy density](../../../../free-energy-density.md).

The quartic sign can also be checked from part (b): its expansion contains

$$
\frac{F_{\rm MF}}N=-\frac{\log3}{\beta}+\left(2J-\frac{16\beta J^2}{3}\right)S^2+\frac{64\beta^3J^4}{9}S^4+O(S^6),
$$

whose quartic coefficient is positive. This is consistent with the proposed [Landau-Ginzburg theory](../../../../landau-ginzburg-theory.md), not a tricritical truncation requiring a stabilizing sixth-order term. The microscopic [square lattice](../../../../square-lattice.md) is two-dimensional; the written gradient describes a profile depending on one coordinate. For general profiles, replace it by $c[(\partial_xm)^2+(\partial_ym)^2]$.
