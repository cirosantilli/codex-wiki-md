<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [mean-field approximation](../../../../mean-field-approximation.md) replaces the fluctuating environment of each microscopic variable by a uniform average field. For spins, write $S_i=S+\delta S_i$ and drop products $\delta S_i\delta S_j$ between different sites. The resulting single-site problem can be solved independently, and its average spin must reproduce the assumed [order parameter](../../../../order-parameter.md) $S$: this is the [self-consistency equation](../../../../self-consistency-equation.md).

It is reliable when correlated [thermal fluctuations](../../../../thermal-fluctuation.md) about the mean are small, for example with many interacting neighbours, long-range interactions or high spatial dimension. Near a [thermodynamic critical point](../../../../thermodynamic-critical-point.md) the quantitative test is the [Ginzburg criterion](../../../../ginzburg-criterion.md). For short-range scalar ordering the [upper critical dimension](../../../../upper-critical-dimension.md) is four, with logarithmic qualifications at four; a two-dimensional nearest-neighbour lattice is below this and its asymptotic [critical region of a phase transition](../../../../critical-region-of-a-phase-transition.md) is not described accurately by simple [mean-field theory](../../../../mean-field-theory.md).[§2.2.4, The Upper Critical Dimension: correlation-volume fluctuations and the Ginzburg criterion](../../../../statistical-field-theory-by-david-tong.md)
