<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The velocity coupling changes the momenta and the undifferentiated field terms. Variation with respect to $\phi_2$ gives the equation for $\phi_1$:

$$
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi_2}=-\frac{\phi_{1,t}}{2\tau},\qquad\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi_{2,t}}=\phi_{1,t}+\frac{\phi_1}{2\tau},
$$

so the two [Euler-Lagrange field equations](../../../../euler-lagrange-field-equation.md) are

$$
\boxed{\phi_{1,tt}-c^2\phi_{1,xx}+\frac1\tau\phi_{1,t}=0,\qquad\phi_{2,tt}-c^2\phi_{2,xx}-\frac1\tau\phi_{2,t}=0.}
$$

They describe [damped and antidamped fields from an antisymmetric velocity coupling](../../../../damped-and-antidamped-fields-from-an-antisymmetric-velocity-coupling.md). With the convention $\phi_j=A_je^{i(k_jx-\omega_jt)}$, their characteristic equations are

$$
\omega_1^2+\frac i\tau\omega_1-c^2k_1^2=0,\qquad\omega_2^2-\frac i\tau\omega_2-c^2k_2^2=0.
$$

Thus the [dispersion relations](../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) and modes are

$$
\boxed{\omega_{1,\pm}=-\frac i{2\tau}\pm\sqrt{c^2k_1^2-\frac1{4\tau^2}},\qquad\phi_{1,\pm}=A_{1,\pm}e^{i(k_1x-\omega_{1,\pm}t)},}
$$



$$
\boxed{\omega_{2,\pm}=+\frac i{2\tau}\pm\sqrt{c^2k_2^2-\frac1{4\tau^2}},\qquad\phi_{2,\pm}=A_{2,\pm}e^{i(k_2x-\omega_{2,\pm}t)}.}
$$

For each spatial [Fourier mode](../../../../fourier-mode.md), the general solution is the [linear combination](../../../../linear-combination.md) of its two temporal branches; spatial superposition gives the general field. Take [real parts](../../../../real-part.md) or suitable [complex conjugate](../../../../complex-conjugate.md) pairs to obtain the required real fields. At the repeated-root threshold, the independent second temporal solution contains an extra factor $t$, as detailed next.
