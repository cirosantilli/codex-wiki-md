<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Interpret $\phi$ as the small transverse displacement of the [elastic beam](../../../../elastic-beam.md). The first term is the positive [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) density $\rho\phi_t^2/2$, with $\rho$ the mass per unit length. The [Lagrangian density](../../../../lagrangian-density.md) is kinetic minus potential energy, so the potential density encoded by the other two terms is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal V=-\frac\alpha2\phi_x^2+\frac\beta2\phi_{xx}^2.}
$$

For a compressed rod, transverse deflection releases compression energy: the small-slope geometric change in length is proportional to $\phi_x^2/2$, so the applied compressive load lowers the potential by $\alpha\phi_x^2/2$. This explains the positive sign of the $\alpha$ term in the Lagrangian. Tension would give the opposite sign and a restoring gradient term.

Bending costs [elastic energy](../../../../elastic-energy.md). At small slopes the [curvature](../../../../curvature.md) is $\phi_{xx}$, so positive [bending stiffness](../../../../bending-stiffness.md) $\beta$ gives potential $\beta\phi_{xx}^2/2$, and hence a negative term in the Lagrangian. For a uniform slender beam $\beta$ is its [flexural rigidity](../../../../bending-stiffness.md). The competition between destabilizing compression and stabilizing bending is responsible for [Euler buckling of an elastic filament](../../../../euler-buckling-of-an-elastic-filament.md).
