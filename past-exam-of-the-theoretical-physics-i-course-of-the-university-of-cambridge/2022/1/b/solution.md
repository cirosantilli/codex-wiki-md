<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $\theta$ and $\varphi$ be the lower and upper azimuthal angles, measured from a common fixed direction. The squared spring length is

$$
d^2=(a\cos\theta-A\cos\varphi)^2+(a\sin\theta-A\sin\varphi)^2+h^2=a^2+A^2+h^2-2aA\cos(\theta-\varphi).
$$

By [Hooke's law](../../../../hooke-s-law.md), a spring with zero natural length has [potential energy](../../../../potential-energy.md) $kd^2/2$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{L=\frac12ma^2\dot\theta^2+\frac12MA^2\dot\varphi^2-\frac k2[a^2+A^2+h^2-2aA\cos(\theta-\varphi)].}
$$

Constant gravitational energies can be omitted. It is useful to define the [moments of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) $I_1=ma^2$, $I_2=MA^2$ and the angular coupling $\kappa=kaA$. The part of the potential that affects motion is simply $-\kappa\cos(\theta-\varphi)$. In particular, $h$ changes the constant spring energy but not the angular equations, because the spring has zero natural length.
