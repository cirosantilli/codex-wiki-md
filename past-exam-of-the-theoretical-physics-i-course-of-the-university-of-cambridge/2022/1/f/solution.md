<h1 id="1/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

A common rotation changes neither the separation nor the spring energy. Its infinitesimal direction is $(1,1)$, exactly the zero-frequency [normal mode](../../../../normal-mode.md). Thus the continuum of rotated [equilibrium points](../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) requires that zero mode; a nonzero restoring frequency in that direction would contradict [rotational symmetry](../../../../rotational-symmetry.md).

The second mode has $I_1\eta_1+I_2\eta_2=0$, so it changes the relative angle while leaving the inertia-weighted common angle unchanged. It therefore carries zero perturbation in the conserved [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) associated with common motion. Indeed the coordinates

$$
q=\frac{I_1\theta+I_2\varphi}{I_1+I_2},\qquad\delta=\theta-\varphi
$$

separate the [Lagrangian](../../../../lagrangian.md) exactly:

$$
L=\frac12(I_1+I_2)\dot q^2+\frac12\frac{I_1I_2}{I_1+I_2}\dot\delta^2+\kappa\cos\delta-\text{constant}.
$$

The [cyclic coordinate](../../../../cyclic-coordinate.md) $q$ is free, while $\delta$ has restoring or inverted curvature. This also explains why all members of either equilibrium family have the same frequencies. Reflection symmetry makes the potential even in $\delta$, and the stable oscillatory mode is consistent with [time reversal in classical mechanics](../../../../time-reversal-in-classical-mechanics.md) through its two opposite frequency signs.
