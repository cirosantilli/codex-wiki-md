<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the downward $y$ direction of the PDF diagram. The bob's Cartesian coordinates are

$$
x=s+\ell\sin\theta,\qquad y=\ell\cos\theta.
$$

Thus

$$
\dot x=\dot s+\ell\dot\theta\cos\theta,\qquad
\dot y=-\ell\dot\theta\sin\theta.
$$

The hinge and rod are massless, so only the bob contributes [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md):

$$
T=\frac m2(\dot x^2+\dot y^2)
=\frac m2\left(\dot s^2+2\ell\dot s\dot\theta\cos\theta+\ell^2\dot\theta^2\right).
$$

Because gravity points along positive $y$, its [gravitational potential energy](../../../../gravitational-energy.md) is $-mgy=-mg\ell\cos\theta$. The zero-rest-length [spring](../../../../spring.md) has [elastic potential energy](../../../../elastic-energy.md) $ks^2/2$. Put $s=\ell\eta$ and divide $T-V$ by the positive constant $m\ell^2$. The equivalent [Lagrangian](../../../../lagrangian.md) is

$$
\boxed{L=\frac12\dot\eta^2+\dot\eta\dot\theta\cos\theta+\frac12\dot\theta^2
+\frac g\ell\cos\theta-\frac k{2m}\eta^2.}
$$

Multiplying the [action](../../../../action.md) by a nonzero constant leaves its stationary paths unchanged. This derivation is a direct use of [kinetic energy from Cartesian position vectors](../../../../kinetic-energy-from-cartesian-position-vectors.md); no independent kinetic term should be assigned to the massless support.
