<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

If $L'=L+dF(q,t)/dt$, the [action](../../../../action.md) changes by an endpoint term:

$$
S'[q]-S[q]=F(q(t_f),t_f)-F(q(t_i),t_i).
$$

In the [principle of stationary action](../../../../principle-of-stationary-action.md) with fixed endpoints, $\delta q(t_i)=\delta q(t_f)=0$ and the endpoint times are fixed. Therefore $\delta(S'-S)=0$, and the two actions give the same bulk [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md).

For a [Lagrangian density](../../../../lagrangian-density.md), adding $\partial_\mu K^\mu$ changes the [action](../../../../action.md) by $\int_{\partial\Omega}K^\mu n_\mu\,d\Sigma$, by the [divergence theorem](../../../../divergence-theorem.md). Its variation vanishes for compactly supported field variations, or when the appropriate boundary data are held fixed. These [total divergences in a variational principle](../../../../total-divergences-in-a-variational-principle.md) can therefore be discarded when deriving bulk equations under those conditions.

The qualification about boundaries matters: if $K^\mu$ depends on field derivatives, their variations on the boundary must also be controlled. A [total derivative](../../../../total-derivative.md) can change the canonical momenta or the chosen representative of a [Noether current](../../../../noether-current.md), even while leaving the bulk dynamics unchanged.
