<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a uniform constant [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) and zero [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md), the [mechanical momentum](../../../../mechanical-momentum.md) changes linearly even though the velocity does not:

$$
\mathbf p(t)=\mathbf p_0+e\mathbf E t,\qquad \mathbf p_0=m\gamma_0\mathbf v_0,\qquad
\gamma_0=(1-v_0^2/c^2)^{-1/2}.
$$

Invert $\mathbf p=m\gamma\mathbf v$ using $\gamma=\sqrt{1+p^2/(m^2c^2)}$. The result for arbitrary initial direction is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf v(t)=\frac{\gamma_0\mathbf v_0+(e\mathbf E/m)t}
{\sqrt{1+\left|\gamma_0\mathbf v_0+(e\mathbf E/m)t\right|^2/c^2}}.}
$$

Equivalently, $\mathbf v=c\mathbf p/\sqrt{m^2c^2+p^2}$. This gives $\mathbf v(0)=\mathbf v_0$ and guarantees $|\mathbf v|<c$ at every finite momentum. The transverse momentum stays constant, but its velocity component generally decreases because the [Lorentz factor](../../../../lorentz-factor.md) increases. Thus an initial transverse velocity must not be treated as constant during the electric acceleration; this is the [relativistic motion in a constant electric field with transverse momentum](../../../../relativistic-motion-in-a-constant-electric-field-with-transverse-momentum.md) effect.
