<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

If $e\mathbf E\ne0$, then $\mathbf p(t)=e\mathbf E t+O(1)$, and the [relativistic energy-momentum relation](../../../../energy-momentum-relation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbf v(t)=c\frac{e\mathbf E}{|e\mathbf E|}.}
$$

The particle approaches the [speed of light](../../../../speed-of-light.md) in the direction of the electric force, independently of its initial velocity. For positive $e$ the limiting direction is along $\mathbf E$; for negative $e$ it is opposite. A massive particle never reaches that limiting speed in finite time. If the force is zero, the velocity remains $\mathbf v_0$ instead.
