<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Interpret $\mathbf P=0$ as the zero-pseudomomentum sector, rather than assuming the mechanical [centre of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md) is at rest. Define $\mathbf u=\mathbf p+e^*\mathbf B\times\mathbf r$. [Hamilton's equations](../../../../../hamilton-s-equations.md) are

$$
\boxed{\dot{\mathbf R}=\frac eM\mathbf B\times\mathbf r,\quad
\dot{\mathbf P}=0,\quad
\dot{\mathbf r}=\frac{\mathbf u}{\mu},\quad
\dot{\mathbf p}=\frac{e^*}{\mu}\mathbf B\times\mathbf u
-\left(\frac{e^2B^2}{M}+\frac{e^2}{r^3}\right)\mathbf r.}
$$

For example, differentiating the relative kinetic term with respect to $\mathbf r$ gives $-e^*\mathbf B\times\mathbf u/\mu$ because the cross-product matrix is antisymmetric. Hamilton's minus sign then gives the positive cross-product term in $\dot{\mathbf p}$. The [Coulomb force](../../../../../coulomb-force.md) is attractive. A change to a moving frame need not preserve a pure magnetic external field, so setting $\mathbf P=0$ is a restriction on the chosen solution family.
