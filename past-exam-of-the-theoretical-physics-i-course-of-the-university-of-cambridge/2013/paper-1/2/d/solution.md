<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Differentiate $\mu\dot{\mathbf r}=\mathbf p+e^*\mathbf B\times\mathbf r$ and substitute the previous [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) equation:

$$
\mu\ddot{\mathbf r}=2e^*\mathbf B\times\dot{\mathbf r}
-\left(\frac{e^2B^2}{M}+\frac{e^2}{r^3}\right)\mathbf r.
$$

For a planar [vector](../../../../../vector.md), $\mathbf B\times\dot{\mathbf r}=B(-\dot y,\dot x)$, which gives the two component equations with the printed signs.

For [circular relative motion of a neutral Coulomb pair](../../../../../circular-relative-motion-of-a-neutral-coulomb-pair.md) of separation radius $R$, $\dot{\mathbf r}=\omega\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf r$ and $\ddot{\mathbf r}=-\omega^2\mathbf r$. Thus

$$
\mu\omega^2-2e^*B\omega-\frac{e^2B^2}{M}-\frac{e^2}{R^3}=0.
$$

Since $(e^*)^2+\mu e^2/M=e^2/4$, the two signed [frequencies](../../../../../frequency.md) are

$$
\boxed{\omega_\pm=\frac{e^*B}{\mu}\pm
\sqrt{\frac{e^2B^2}{4\mu^2}+\frac{e^2}{\mu R^3}}.}
$$

One is positive and the other negative. At $B=0$ they give the two senses of an ordinary Coulomb circular orbit; equal [masses](../../../../../mass.md) remove the term linear in $B$ from their average. With negligible Coulomb attraction and $eB>0$, the branches approach $eB/m_2$ and $-eB/m_1$, the appropriate signed free cyclotron limits.

The actual particle paths follow by integrating the centre-of-mass [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md):

$$
\mathbf R_{\rm cm}=\mathbf R_0+\frac{eB}{M\omega}\mathbf r,
\quad
\mathbf r_1=\mathbf R_0+\frac{eB/\omega-m_2}{M}\mathbf r,
\quad
\mathbf r_2=\mathbf R_0+\frac{eB/\omega+m_1}{M}\mathbf r.
$$

Both particles move on circles about the same fixed [guiding centre](../../../../../guiding-center.md) with a constant separation and common signed [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md), with a possible relative phase reversal encoded by a negative coefficient. Their mechanical [centre of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md) also generally circles. This is consistent with magnetic deflection plus a central mutual force; central interaction alone does not eliminate the external Lorentz forces.
