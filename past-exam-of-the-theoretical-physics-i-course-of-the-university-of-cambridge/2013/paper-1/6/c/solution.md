<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Differentiate the [equation of state](../../../../../equation-of-state.md) at fixed [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md):

$$
\chi=\frac1{a+3bm^2}.
$$

Above the transition, zero-field equilibrium has $m=0$. Below it the stable pure phases have $m^2=-a/b$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\chi(t)=\frac1{a_0t}\ (t>0),\qquad
\chi(t)=\frac1{2a_0|t|}\ (t<0),\qquad
\lim_{t\to0^+}\frac{\chi(t)}{\chi(-t)}=2.}
$$

This is the [mean-field magnetic-susceptibility amplitude ratio](../../../../../mean-field-magnetic-susceptibility-amplitude-ratio.md). Below the transition, take a one-sided response in a selected ordered phase: the globally minimizing [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) switches discontinuously between opposite phases when $h$ crosses zero. Its two-sided [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) at precisely zero field is therefore not the finite pure-phase [magnetic susceptibility](../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md) used in the ratio.
