# Dimensional analysis

↑ **Parent:** [Mathematical physics](mathematical-physics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensional_analysis)

Dimensional analysis checks the [physical dimensions](physical-dimension.md) of quantities and identifies combinations unchanged by the choice of units. It catches impossible equations and reveals scaling before a full dynamical calculation. Adding two quantities requires the same [physical dimension](physical-dimension.md); replacing metres by centimetres must change numerical values consistently while preserving physical predictions.

For the ideal [simple pendulum](simple-pendulum-split.md), suppose its [oscillation period](period-of-an-oscillation.md) $T$ depends only on mass $m$, [length](length.md) $\ell$, [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) $g$, and dimensionless angular [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) $\theta_0$. Write $T=m^a\ell^bg^c F(\theta_0)$. Since $[m]=M$, $[\ell]=L$, and $[g]=LT^{-2}$, matching [physical dimensions](physical-dimension.md) gives $a=0$, $b+c=0$, and $-2c=1$. Consequently

$$
T=\sqrt{\frac\ell g}\,F(\theta_0).
$$

The angular [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) is dimensionless when expressed in [radians](radian.md). Dimensional analysis establishes the length/gravity scaling and the absence of a mass factor under these assumptions, but cannot determine $F$. The [harmonic oscillator equation](simple-harmonic-motion.md) gives $F\to2\pi$ at small [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md); the full [simple pendulum](simple-pendulum-split.md) supplies its finite-amplitude correction. A model with drag or a finite massive support has additional parameters, so the same dimensional argument does not automatically apply.

The result is useful for comparison: at fixed angular [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) and $g$, lengths in the ratio $1:4$ give periods in the ratio $1:2$. The ratio $T\sqrt{g/\ell}$ should be the same for both in this ideal model. A failure to collapse onto the same value points to measurement error or an omitted parameter. Correct [physical dimensions](physical-dimension.md) are a necessary consistency check, while the coefficient and the adequacy of the model require more information.
