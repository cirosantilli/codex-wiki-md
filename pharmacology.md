#### Proof spirit

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmaceutical preparation](#pharmaceutical-preparation)

Historical aqueous [ethanol](metabolism.md#ethanol) of specified proof strength; it is not synonymous with [rectified spirit](#rectified-spirit).

#### Rectified spirit

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmaceutical preparation](#pharmaceutical-preparation)

Historical concentrated aqueous [ethanol](metabolism.md#ethanol) used as a pharmaceutical solvent; its specified strength depends on the applicable standard.

#### Maceration

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmaceutical preparation](#pharmaceutical-preparation)

Extraction by allowing divided material to stand in contact with a solvent; distinguished from passing fresh solvent through a packed bed in [percolation](percolation-theory.md).

#### Liquid extract

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmaceutical preparation](#pharmaceutical-preparation)

An extracted medicinal preparation supplied as a liquid of specified strength. The name alone does not impose a universal ratio of starting material to final liquid.

#### Infusion

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmaceutical preparation](#pharmaceutical-preparation)

A preparation obtained by allowing medicinal material to stand in a solvent for a specified time, at the prescribed [temperature](temperature.md), then straining or filtering.

#### Decoction

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmaceutical preparation](#pharmaceutical-preparation)

A preparation made by boiling medicinal material in a liquid, followed by the specified separation and volume adjustment. Suitability depends on the [solubility](ionic-bonding.md#solubility) and heat stability of the desired constituents.

Historical pharmaceutical preparation; its composition and clinical use must be identified by edition. [corresponding monograph and British preparation](pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

## Adverse drug reaction

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

A harmful or unintended response attributed to a medicine under specified conditions. Temporal association alone does not establish drug causation; disease, concomitant [treatments](causal-inference.md#treatment) and contextual influences must also be assessed.

## Ketamine

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

A compound with NMDA-receptor [antagonist](#antagonist) actions among other effects, used in some experiments to perturb [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) and perception. A transient pharmacological model does not reproduce an entire psychiatric disorder.

## D-cycloserine

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

A compound studied as a partial [agonist](protein.md#agonist) at the glycine site of the [NMDA receptor](neuron.md#nmda-receptor) and as a possible facilitator of [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning). An experimental adjunct to a [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) session is not equivalent to a general [anxiety](psychology.md#anxiety) suppressant.

## Antipsychotic

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

A medicine used for psychotic symptoms. [Receptor](protein.md#receptor-biochemistry) effects and symptom improvement do not by themselves explain a patient’s original disease cause or erase learned beliefs.

## Antagonist

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

A ligand that reduces [receptor](protein.md#receptor-biochemistry) activation by an [agonist](protein.md#agonist) or endogenous signal under specified conditions. Behavioural selectivity cannot be inferred solely from an in-vitro affinity label.

## Benzodiazepine

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

A drug class including positive allosteric modulators at susceptible [GABA-A receptors](protein.md#gaba-a-receptor). A behavioural effect can involve anxiolysis, sedation, altered [memory](neuroscience.md#memory-in-animals) or movement, which must be distinguished.

## Neuropsychopharmacology

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

Study of how pharmacological manipulations interact with [neural circuits](neuroscience.md#neural-circuit) and psychological functions. A change in task performance can arise through altered [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning), retrieval, [motivation](psychology.md#motivation), sensation or motor execution; the mechanism must be distinguished experimentally.

## Psychopharmacology

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

Study of how drugs affect psychological and behavioral functioning and of their clinical use. Treatment response to a drug does not alone identify a disorder's original cause.

### Risperidone

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopharmacology](#psychopharmacology)

### Imipramine

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopharmacology](#psychopharmacology)

### Clomipramine

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopharmacology](#psychopharmacology)

A tricyclic antidepressant with serotonin-reuptake-inhibiting action, studied in [obsessive-compulsive disorder](psychiatry.md#obsessive-compulsive-disorder). Comparative efficacy must be interpreted alongside tolerability and the treatment schedule; results for it do not automatically describe every [SSRI](#selective-serotonin-reuptake-inhibitor).

## Antidepressant

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

A medicine used to treat depressive illness. A demonstrated treatment effect does not, by itself, identify the biochemical abnormality that originally caused the illness.

### Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor

↑ **Parent:** [Antidepressant](#antidepressant)

An antidepressant class whose principal initial pharmacological action inhibits serotonin transport back into presynaptic cells. Initial transporter inhibition and subsequent clinical improvement need not have the same time course.

#### Fluoxetine

↑ **Parent:** [Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor](#selective-serotonin-reuptake-inhibitor)

A [selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor](#selective-serotonin-reuptake-inhibitor) studied in several [mental disorders](psychiatry.md#mental-disorder). A favorable treatment response does not by itself establish that a simple serotonin deficiency caused the condition.[2017; randomized pharmacotherapy comparison and qualified efficacy outcomes](psychology-bibliography.md#a-randomized-controlled-trial-of-medication-and-cognitive-behavioral-therapy-for-hypochondriasis-by-brian-a-fallon-and-others)

## Off-target drug effect

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

A drug effect mediated through an interaction other than the intended target. A residual phenotype after verified protection of the intended target is a useful candidate, but requires independent causal validation.

## Drug hypersensitivity

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

An increased response to a drug relative to a specified control. Cellular hypersensitivity does not uniquely imply higher molecular affinity for the intended target.

## Disc diffusion assay

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

A growth-inhibition assay in which a compound diffuses from a disc into a lawn of [biological cells](biological-cell.md). Halo size combines susceptibility with [molecular diffusion](thermodynamics.md#molecular-diffusion) and growth conditions and is not directly a molecular [binding affinity](protein.md#binding-affinity).

## Pharmacokinetics

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

### Drug absorption

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacokinetics](#pharmacokinetics)

Transfer of a [drug](#drug) from its administration site into the systemic circulation. Direct intravenous delivery bypasses this transfer step.

## Target engagement

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

Evidence that an intervention interacts with its intended molecular target in the biological setting being tested. A downstream phenotype alone is insufficient.

## Therapeutic window

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

A range of exposures giving useful benefit while keeping adverse effects acceptable. Biochemical target inhibition alone does not establish this range.

## Ipilimumab

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

A therapeutic antibody directed against [CTLA-4](protein.md#ctla-4), used as an immune checkpoint inhibitor in defined clinical contexts.

## Chemoresistance

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

Reduced susceptibility of cells or disease to a chemotherapy regimen. Survival can reflect pre-existing resistant states, induced changes or genetic evolution.

## Ulixertinib

↑ **Parent:** [Pharmacology](pharmacology.md)

A small-molecule inhibitor of [ERK](signal-transduction.md#erk) used experimentally to test pathway requirements. Developmental interpretation requires stage, dose, target-engagement and viability controls.
