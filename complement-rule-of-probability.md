# Complement rule of probability

↑ **Parent:** [Probability](probability.md)

For an [event](event.md) $A$ in a [sample space](sample-space.md) $\Omega$, the complementary [event](event.md) $A^c=\Omega\setminus A$ occurs exactly when $A$ does not occur. The [complement rule of probability](complement-rule-of-probability.md) follows from the [Kolmogorov axioms](kolmogorov-axioms.md): $A$ and $A^c$ are disjoint, their union is $\Omega$, and their [probabilities](probability.md) therefore add to $\mathbb P(\Omega)=1$.

This rule is useful when an [event](event.md) is easier to describe through its failure. For a nonnegative [integer](integer-split.md)-valued [random variable](random-variable-split.md) $K$, $\mathbb P(K\geq1)=1-\mathbb P(K=0)$ turns an at-least-one calculation into a single value of the [probability mass function](probability-mass-function.md). In [sampling without replacement](sampling-without-replacement.md), the [hypergeometric distribution](hypergeometric-distribution.md) gives the chance of missing all marked objects, so its complement gives the chance of finding at least one.
