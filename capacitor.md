# Capacitor

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor)

A [capacitor](capacitor.md) stores [electrostatic energy](electrostatic-energy.md) by separating [electric charge](electric-charge.md) between two [electrical conductors](electrical-conductor.md). In the ideal two-terminal model their [electric charges](electric-charge.md) are $+Q$ and $-Q$, with a vacuum or insulating gap preventing direct transfer between them. Write $V=\phi_+-\phi_-$ for the terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md), where $\phi_+$ and $\phi_-$ are the [electric potentials](electric-potential.md) of the two [electrical conductors](electrical-conductor.md). A linear [capacitor](capacitor.md) obeys

$$
Q=CV,
$$

where its [capacitance](capacitance.md) $C$ depends on the geometry and [permittivity](permittivity.md), and is independent of the applied [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) while these stay fixed. [OpenStax University Physics 2, §8.1: capacitors and capacitance](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/8-1-capacitors-and-capacitance).

For example, the [parallel-plate capacitor](parallel-plate-capacitor.md) below has [capacitance](capacitance.md) $C=88.54\,\mathrm{pF}$. At terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $V=10\,\mathrm V$, the [electric charge](electric-charge.md) magnitude on either plate is

$$
Q=CV=(8.854\times10^{-11})(10)\,\mathrm C
=8.854\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm C=0.8854\,\mathrm{nC}.
$$

The total [electric charge](electric-charge.md) of the device is $Q+(-Q)=0$: [charge neutrality](charge-neutrality.md) does not imply zero [capacitor energy](capacitor-energy.md). This [capacitor](capacitor.md) stores $4.427\,\mathrm{nJ}$ in its [electric field](electric-field.md). The schematic in [parallel-plate capacitor](parallel-plate-capacitor.md) shows the separation of [electric charge](electric-charge.md); the plots in [capacitor energy](capacitor-energy.md) show how storage grows with terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md).

The useful distinction is between storing net [electric charge](electric-charge.md) and storing separated [electric charge](electric-charge.md). A [capacitor](capacitor.md) can release [electrostatic energy](electrostatic-energy.md) into an [electrical circuit](electrical-circuit.md); in an ideal [RLC circuit](rlc-circuit.md), [energy](energy.md) moves between the [capacitor](capacitor.md)'s [electric field](electric-field.md) and the [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) associated with [inductance](inductance.md). An ideal disconnected [capacitor](capacitor.md) retains its separated [electric charges](electric-charge.md) and [capacitor energy](capacitor-energy.md) because there is no path for discharge.
