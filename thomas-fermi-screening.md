# Thomas-Fermi screening

↑ **Parent:** [Dielectric screening](dielectric-screening.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas–Fermi_screening)

In the long-wavelength static response of a degenerate conductor, a small electric potential $\phi$ changes the electron density by $\delta n=e g(E_F)\phi$, where $g(E_F)$ is the spin-summed [density of states](density-of-states.md) per volume per energy. The induced charge is therefore $-e^2g(E_F)\phi$. With background [permittivity](permittivity-split.md) $\varepsilon_b$, [Poisson equation](poisson-s-equation.md) becomes

$$
(\nabla^2-k_{\rm TF}^2)\phi=-\rho_{\rm ext}/\varepsilon_b,\qquad
k_{\rm TF}^2=e^2g(E_F)/\varepsilon_b.
$$

A point charge $Q$ then has $\phi(r)=Qe^{-k_{\rm TF}r}/(4\pi\varepsilon_b r)$. Larger electronic density of states gives stronger screening. This local response approximation does not capture the microscopic oscillatory tail of [Friedel oscillations](friedel-oscillations.md).[Sections 7.7.3–7.7.5: Thomas-Fermi screening and its nonlocal corrections](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/em/el6.pdf)
