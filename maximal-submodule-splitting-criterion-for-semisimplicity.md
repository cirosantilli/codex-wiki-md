# Maximal-submodule splitting criterion for semisimplicity

↑ **Parent:** [Semisimple module](semisimple-module.md)

For a finite-dimensional [module](module-mathematics.md), it suffices that every maximal proper [submodule](submodule.md) have a [submodule](submodule.md) complement in order that every [submodule](submodule.md) have one. Let $W$ be the sum of all [simple modules](irreducible-module.md) contained in the given [module](module-mathematics.md). If $W$ is proper, choose a maximal [submodule](submodule.md) $M$ containing it. A complement $C$ is isomorphic to the simple quotient $V/M$ and hence is itself simple. This forces $C\subseteq W\subseteq M$, contradicting the complement. Thus $W=V$. Greedily adding simple summands not already in the sum gives a [direct sum](direct-sum.md) in finitely many steps. Finally, given any $U$, choose a maximal sum of these simple summands disjoint from $U$. A missing simple summand has zero intersection with their sum plus $U$, contradicting maximality unless their sum complements $U$. This proves the [semisimple module](semisimple-module.md) criterion, including the zero [module](module-mathematics.md).
