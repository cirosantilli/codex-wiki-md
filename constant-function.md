# Constant function

↑ **Parent:** [Function](function-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constant_function)

On a nonempty [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) $X$, a constant [function](function-split.md) $f:X\to Y$ has one fixed value $c$ in its [codomain](codomain.md):

$$
\boxed{f(x)=c\qquad\text{for every }x\in X.}
$$

For example, $f(x)=2$ on the [real line](real-line.md) is a horizontal graph. Its [image of a function](image-of-a-function.md) is the [singleton](singleton-mathematics.md) $\{2\}$ even though its [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) has infinitely many inputs. A [constant function](constant-function.md) from the [real line](real-line.md) to the [real numbers](real-number.md) has [derivative](derivative.md) zero and every nonzero real [period of a function](period-of-a-function.md); there is no smallest positive [period](period-of-a-function.md). It supplies a reference level from which variation can be measured.

A constant map between [vector spaces](vector-space-split.md) is a [linear map](linear-map.md) only when its value is the [zero vector](zero-vector.md), since a [linear map](linear-map.md) must preserve zero. A nonzero constant map is still an [affine map](affine-map.md). In a [Fourier series](fourier-series-split.md), the constant term records the [period average](period-average.md), while the other terms describe oscillation around that level.
