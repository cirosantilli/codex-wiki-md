# Bond-dissociation energy

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond-dissociation_energy)

A [bond-dissociation energy](bond-dissociation-energy.md) specifies the [energy](energy-split.md) needed for a stated dissociation into separated fragments. An [energy](energy-split.md) from the lowest vibrational state, an [energy](energy-split.md) from the bottom of a [potential energy](potential-energy.md) well, and a finite-temperature molar dissociation [enthalpy](enthalpy-split.md) require different conventions; do not silently mix them in an [electronegativity](electronegativity-split.md) formula.

Which fragments, in which [quantum states](quantum-state.md), and at which [temperature](temperature-split.md)? For a neutral gaseous [molecule](molecule-split.md) with a specified two-electron [covalent bond](covalent-bond-split.md), the usual thermochemical convention is [homolysis](homolysis.md),

$$
\mathrm{A{-}B}(g)\longrightarrow\mathrm A^{\bullet}(g)+\mathrm B^{\bullet}(g).
$$

Each fragment receives one of the two bonding [electrons](electron.md); the dots denote unpaired [electrons](electron.md). The final fragments are isolated from each other, with their own relaxed [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md) and specified electronic [quantum states](quantum-state.md). The [energy](energy-split.md) cost includes this relaxation, rather than merely stretching the initial [chemical bond](chemical-bond-split.md) while freezing everything else. For a neutral parent, [heterolysis](heterolysis.md) instead produces $\mathrm A^+(g)+\mathrm B^-(g)$, or the opposite assignment of [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md); those are different final states and different [energies](energy-split.md). A value for gaseous neutral fragments cannot be transferred to [ions](ion.md), a [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md), or a [solid](solid.md) without accounting for those changed conditions.[definition of bond-dissociation energy: molar enthalpy for homolysis of a specified molecular entity](iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/bond-dissociation-energy.md)

The distinction is useful beyond terminology. [Pauling electronegativity](pauling-electronegativity.md) compares consistently defined [bond-dissociation energies](bond-dissociation-energy.md); [photodissociation](photodissociation.md) can reveal a specified ground-state threshold; a thermal [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md) balance requires compatible [enthalpies](enthalpy-split.md). A threshold is also different from an [activation energy](activation-energy.md): it compares initial and final [quantum states](quantum-state.md), whereas a barrier can obstruct the path between them.
