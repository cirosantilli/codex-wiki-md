# Log-radius slope of the circle log-mean

↑ **Parent:** [Circle log-mean of a holomorphic function](circle-log-mean-of-a-holomorphic-function.md)

Fix a [holomorphic function](holomorphic-function-split.md) on a neighbourhood of a closed [disk](disk-mathematics.md), with $f(0)\ne0$. On an interval of positive radii whose [circles](circle.md) contain no [zeros of a function](zero-of-a-function.md), differentiating [Jensen's formula](jensen-s-formula.md) with respect to $x=\log R$ gives

$$
\frac{d}{dx}L_f(e^x)=n_f(e^x).
$$

Each interior [zero of a function](zero-of-a-function.md) contributes slope one for each unit of its [multiplicity of a zero](multiplicity-of-a-zero.md). Between consecutive zero moduli, $L_f(e^x)$ is an [affine function](affine-function.md); passing the modulus of a zero of [multiplicity of a zero](multiplicity-of-a-zero.md) $m$ increases the adjacent slopes by $m$. The finite sum $\log|f(0)|+\sum_j\max(0,x-\log|a_j|)$ defines a continuous [convex function](convex-function.md) of $x$ whose values agree with the [circle log-mean of a holomorphic function](circle-log-mean-of-a-holomorphic-function.md) at every boundary-zero-free radius. This describes the adjacent line segments without asserting a boundary-singularity extension of the [integral](integral-split.md).
