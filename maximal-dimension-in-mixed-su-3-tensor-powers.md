<h1 id="maximal-dimension-in-mixed-su-3-tensor-powers">Maximal dimension in mixed SU(3) tensor powers</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Symmetric traceless SU(3) tensor representation](symmetric-traceless-su-3-tensor-representation.md)

For $d(p,q)=(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)/2$, the exact largest constituent [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) of a [mixed SU(3) tensor representation](mixed-su-3-tensor-representation.md) is

$$
D_{\max}(k,l)=\max\left\{\max_{0\le a\le\lfloor k/2\rfloor}d(k-2a,l+a),\ \max_{0\le b\le\lfloor l/2\rfloor}d(k+b,l-2b)\right\}.
$$

Every constituent [highest weight](highest-weight-of-a-representation.md) lies below $k\omega_1+l\omega_2$, so its labels have the form $(p,q)=(k-2a+b,l+a-2b)$ for nonnegative integers $a,b$. If both are positive, reducing each by their minimum increases both $p,q$ and strictly increases the [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md). The resulting boundary labels occur: antisymmetrize disjoint upper pairs into lower indices, or lower pairs into upper indices, then take the symmetric traceless component. This proves the maximum formula. For $(k,l)=(5,0)$, the fully symmetric [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) is 21 but $(3,1)$ has [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) 24. For $k=l$, the fully symmetric traceless [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) $(k+1)^3$ is the maximum.
