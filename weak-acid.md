# Weak acid

↑ **Parent:** [Acid (chemistry)](acid-chemistry.md)

A [weak acid](weak-acid.md) establishes a [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md) between its proton-donating form and its [conjugate base](conjugate-base.md) in [water](water.md). For a neutral [monoprotic acid](monoprotic-acid.md) $\mathrm{HA}$, the aqueous [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) is

$$
\mathrm{HA(aq)}+\mathrm{H_2O(l)}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{H_3O^+(aq)}+\mathrm{A^-(aq)}.
$$

[Water](water.md) accepts a [proton](proton.md) to become [hydronium](hydronium.md); $\mathrm A^-$ is the [conjugate base](conjugate-base.md). The double arrow denotes reversible exchange at [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md), not equal populations. The [acid dissociation constant](acid-dissociation-constant.md) and composition determine the [hydronium](hydronium.md) [concentration](concentration.md); substituting total [chemical acid](acid-chemistry.md) [concentration](concentration.md) into the [pH](ph.md) formula is generally incorrect.

“Weak” describes the tendency to transfer a [proton](proton.md) in a specified [solvent](solvent.md), rather than how much [chemical acid](acid-chemistry.md) was dissolved. A [weak acid](weak-acid.md) can be concentrated, and a [strong acid](strong-acid.md) can be dilute. Even the same [weak acid](weak-acid.md) can have a large [weak acid dissociation fraction](weak-acid-dissociation-fraction.md) after sufficient [dilution](dilution.md), while its [acid dissociation constant](acid-dissociation-constant.md) stays fixed at the same [temperature](temperature-split.md) and [standard state](standard-state.md) conventions.[Chemistry 2e by Paul Flowers and others; §14.3, acid ionization and concentration-dependent percent ionization](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/14-3-relative-strengths-of-acids-and-bases.md)
