# Completeness of convergence in measure on a finite measure space

↑ **Parent:** [Convergence in measure](convergence-in-measure.md)

Modulo equality almost everywhere, the displayed metric induces [convergence in measure](convergence-in-measure.md) and is complete. A sequence Cauchy in measure has a strictly increasing subsequence $f_{n_k}$ with $\mu(|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_k}|>2^{-k})<2^{-k}$. Subadditivity makes the limsup of these exceptional sets null, so the subsequence has summable pointwise successive differences almost everywhere. It therefore has a finite measurable limit. Almost-everywhere convergence implies convergence in measure for finite measure, and the triangle inequality plus the original Cauchy property extends convergence to the full sequence.

For the metric equivalence, $d(f_n,f)\leq\varepsilon\mu(E)+\mu(|f_n-f|>\varepsilon)$ for $0<\varepsilon<1$, while $\mu(|f_n-f|>\varepsilon)\leq d(f_n,f)/\min\{1,\varepsilon\}$. The same estimates compare the two Cauchy properties. Define the subsequence limit as zero where no finite limit exists; that exceptional set is measurable by the countable Cauchy criterion, so completeness of the underlying measure is unnecessary.
