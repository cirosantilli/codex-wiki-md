# Coefficient of kinetic friction

↑ **Parent:** [Kinetic friction](kinetic-friction.md)

The [coefficient of kinetic friction](coefficient-of-kinetic-friction.md) is the [dimensionless quantity](dimensionless-quantity.md) $\mu_k=|f_k|/N$ for sliding contact with positive [normal force](normal-force.md) $N$ in the ideal [kinetic friction](kinetic-friction.md) model. It characterizes the pair of contacting surfaces and their condition. A constant $\mu_k$ means the model neglects dependence on [contact slip](contact-slip.md) speed; unlike the [coefficient of static friction](coefficient-of-static-friction.md), it specifies a sliding [force](force-split.md) magnitude rather than a no-slip upper bound.

The ratio compares the tangential [kinetic friction](kinetic-friction.md) magnitude with the perpendicular [normal force](normal-force.md):

$$
\mu_k=\frac{|f_k|}{N},\qquad N>0,\quad |\mathbf w|>0.
$$

Here $\mathbf w$ is the tangential [relative velocity](relative-velocity.md) defining [contact slip](contact-slip.md). Both numerator and denominator have the same [physical dimension](physical-dimension.md), so the [coefficient of kinetic friction](coefficient-of-kinetic-friction.md) has no units. It belongs to a contact pair under specified conditions, rather than to one material alone. The [coefficient of static friction](coefficient-of-static-friction.md) instead describes the threshold $|f_s|_{\max}/N$ before [contact slip](contact-slip.md); a pull required to start motion cannot be substituted for a sliding measurement.[§6.2, “Magnitude of Static Friction” and “Magnitude of Kinetic Friction”, equations 6.1–6.2; unitless coefficient and dependence on the two surfaces](university-physics-volume-1-by-william-moebs-and-others.md)

As an original model example, stipulate $\mu_k=0.30$ and [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) $g=9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. An already sliding, nonrotating block of total [mass](mass.md) $m=2\,\mathrm{kg}$ is pulled horizontally across a stationary horizontal surface at constant [velocity](velocity.md). With no other vertical [force](force-split.md), [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) gives $N=mg=19.6\,\mathrm N$. Zero horizontal [acceleration](acceleration.md) then requires a pulling [force](force-split.md) $F=|f_k|=\mu_kmg=5.88\,\mathrm N$. Doubling the total [mass](mass.md) to $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ gives $N=39.2\,\mathrm N$ and $F=11.76\,\mathrm N$. The [force](force-split.md) doubles while the [coefficient of kinetic friction](coefficient-of-kinetic-friction.md) stays $0.30$ by assumption. These numbers are model predictions, not observations or a calibration for a particular material pair.

A constant [coefficient of kinetic friction](coefficient-of-kinetic-friction.md) is useful because one ratio can summarize many loads, as tested by [load dependence of kinetic friction](load-dependence-of-kinetic-friction.md). It is an empirical approximation over a stated range of [normal forces](normal-force.md), [speeds](speed.md) and surface conditions. It implies no universal independence from [speed](speed.md) or apparent contact [area](surface-area.md): a change of face or sliding regime can change the result. [Temperature](temperature-split.md), wear and lubrication are further conditions to report. [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) still applies when the constant-coefficient approximation fails.[§6.2, approximate empirical model; small nonzero speeds; exceptions for lubricated surfaces and high speeds; “Atomic-Scale Explanations of Friction”, apparent versus actual contact area](university-physics-volume-1-by-william-moebs-and-others.md)
