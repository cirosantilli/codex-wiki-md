# Ring of integers of a quadratic field

↑ **Parent:** [Ring of integers](ring-of-integers.md)

Let $d\ne0,1$ be a [square-free integer](square-free-integer.md), and let $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)$ be the associated [quadratic field](quadratic-field.md). Then

$$
\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\omega_d],\qquad
\omega_d=\begin{cases}(1+\sqrt d)/2,&d\equiv1\pmod4,\\\sqrt d,&d\equiv2,3\pmod4.\end{cases}
$$

The pair $1,\omega_d$ is an [integral basis](integral-basis.md). The [field discriminant](field-discriminant.md) is $d$ in the first case and $4d$ in the second. This statement requires $d$ to be a [square-free integer](square-free-integer.md): for example, $\mathbb Q(\sqrt8)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ but $\mathbb Z[\sqrt8]$ omits $\sqrt2$.[Conrad, Factoring in Quadratic Fields, Theorem 3.4](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/gradnumthy/quadraticgrad.pdf)

For $d=2$, the [integral basis](integral-basis.md) is $1,\sqrt2$, and the [field discriminant](field-discriminant.md) is $8$. For $d=5$, it is $1,\phi$ with $\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, and the [field discriminant](field-discriminant.md) is $5$. The identity $\phi^2=\phi+1$ makes $\mathbb Z[\phi]$ closed under multiplication. Since $\sqrt5=2\phi-1$, the smaller [order in a number field](order-in-a-number-field.md) $\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]$ consists precisely of $a+b\phi$ with even $b$. This computes its index two and shows concretely why adjoining a square root does not always supply every [algebraic integer](algebraic-integer.md).

In this second [integral basis](integral-basis.md), the [field norm](field-norm.md) is

$$
N_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)/\mathbb Q}(a+b\phi)=a^2+ab-b^2.
$$

In particular, $N(\phi)=-1$ and $\phi^{-1}=\phi-1$: the extra [algebraic integer](algebraic-integer.md) is also a [unit](unit-in-a-ring.md). The [units of the quadratic field Q square root of five](units-of-the-quadratic-field-q-square-root-of-five.md) describe all its powers.
