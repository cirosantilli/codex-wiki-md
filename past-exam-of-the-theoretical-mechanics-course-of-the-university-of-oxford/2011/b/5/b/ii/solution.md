<h1 id="b/5/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Linearize about a flat quiescent surface. The product $\phi_x\eta_x$ and the kinetic-energy term are quadratic, and the [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) can be evaluated at $z=0$ to first order. Thus $\eta_t=\phi_z$ and $\phi_t+g\eta=0$ there. Fourier transformation in $x$ changes $\partial_x^2$ into multiplication by $-k^2$, giving

$$
\boxed{\widehat\phi_{zz}=k^2\widehat\phi\quad(z<0),\qquad\widehat\phi_z\to0\quad(z\to-\infty),\qquad
\widehat\eta_t=\widehat\phi_z\quad(z=0),\qquad\widehat\phi_t+g\widehat\eta=0\quad(z=0).}
$$

The transform uses $e^{-ikx}$ in its forward definition; the inverse transform therefore uses $e^{ikx}/(2\pi)$. The zero-[wavenumber](../../../../../../wavenumber.md) potential has an irrelevant spatially constant gauge freedom.
