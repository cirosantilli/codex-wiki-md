<h1 id="b/5/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For [wavenumber selection by a moving observer of deep-water waves](../../../../../../wavenumber-selection-by-a-moving-observer-of-deep-water-waves.md), an observer with nonzero [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) $V$ follows $x=Vt$ up to a constant initial displacement. Splitting the cosine into exponentials gives phase [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md) $\psi_\pm(k)=kV\pm\sqrt{g|k|}$. Their stationary points satisfy

$$
V\pm\frac{\sqrt g\,\operatorname{sgn}k}{2\sqrt{|k|}}=0,
$$

so the selected wavenumbers are

$$
\boxed{k=\pm\frac g{4V^2}.}
$$

The two signs arise in the conjugate branches needed for a real [wave](../../../../../../wave-split.md). Equivalently the observer selects [wave](../../../../../../wave-split.md) packets whose [group velocity](../../../../../../group-velocity.md) equals $V$. Stationary-phase contributions decay as $t^{-1/2}$, whereas smooth nonstationary contributions decay more rapidly, so these are the dominant wavenumbers when the initial spectrum is nonzero there.

For real even data, one may make this explicit. Set $k_*=g/(4V^2)$. The stationary curvature has magnitude $2|V|^3/g$, yielding

$$
\eta(Vt,t)\sim\widehat\eta_0(k_*)\sqrt{\frac{g}{4\pi|V|^3t}}\cos\left(\frac{gt}{4|V|}-\frac\pi4\right)
$$

when its leading coefficient is nonzero. If that spectral value vanishes, the leading stationary term vanishes too. At $V=0$ there is no finite nonzero stationary [wavenumber](../../../../../../wavenumber.md) selected by this condition.
