<h1 id="b/6/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Write the shock as $x=s(t)$ and let the plus trace be on $x>s(t)$. Locally its discontinuous part can be represented with the Heaviside factor $H(x-s(t))$. Distributional differentiation gives $\partial_tH=-s'(t)\delta(x-s(t))$ and $\partial_xH=\delta(x-s(t))$. The singular part of the [conservation law](../../../../../../conservation-law.md) is therefore

$$
\bigl(-s'(t)[\mathbf P]_-^++[\mathbf Q]_-^+\bigr)\delta(x-s(t)).
$$

It must vanish, so the [Rankine-Hugoniot conditions](../../../../../../rankine-hugoniot-conditions.md) are

$$
\boxed{V[\mathbf P]_-^+=[\mathbf Q]_-^+,\qquad V=s'(t).}
$$

The smooth parts of the fields still satisfy the classical [conservation law](../../../../../../conservation-law.md) on each side. The jump condition ensures that the moving discontinuity creates no extra source of the conserved quantities.
