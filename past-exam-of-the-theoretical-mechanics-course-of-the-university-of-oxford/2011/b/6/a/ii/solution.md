<h1 id="b/6/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If the fields are continuously differentiable and obey the pointwise [conservation law](../../../../../../conservation-law.md), [integration by parts](../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) against a compactly supported [test function](../../../../../../test-function.md) gives the weak identity, with no boundary terms. Conversely, integrate the weak identity by parts for a continuously differentiable field:

$$
\iint(\partial_t\mathbf P+\partial_x\mathbf Q)\cdot\boldsymbol\varphi\,dx\,dt=0
$$

for every [test function](../../../../../../test-function.md). The [fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations](../../../../../../fundamental-lemma-of-the-calculus-of-variations.md), applied to each component, makes the continuous integrand vanish at every point. Hence **the classical and weak formulations are equivalent for continuously differentiable fields**.
