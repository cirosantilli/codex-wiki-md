<h1 id="b/6/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $v=u-V$, the [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) in the shock frame. The mass jump condition gives a common [mass flux](../../../../../../mass-flux.md) $j=\rho v$. Subtracting $V$ times the mass balance from the [momentum](../../../../../../momentum-split.md) balance gives

$$
\boxed{[\rho(u-V)]_-^+=0,\qquad[p+\rho(u-V)^2]_-^+=0.}
$$

For energy, transform the conserved energy to the shock frame by subtracting $V$ times [momentum](../../../../../../momentum-split.md) and adding $V^2/2$ times mass. The resulting energy [flux](../../../../../../flux.md) is

$$
\rho v\left(\frac{v^2}{2}+\frac{\gamma p}{(\gamma-1)\rho}\right).
$$

For a genuine shock, $j\ne0$; dividing its continuous energy [flux](../../../../../../flux.md) by the same nonzero $j$ on both sides gives

$$
\boxed{\left[\frac{(u-V)^2}{2}+\frac{\gamma p}{(\gamma-1)\rho}\right]_-^+=0.}
$$

The second term is the ideal-gas [specific enthalpy](../../../../../../specific-enthalpy.md). A [contact discontinuity](../../../../../../contact-discontinuity.md) has zero relative [mass flux](../../../../../../mass-flux.md) and does not in general require equal specific enthalpies; the division here is appropriate for the shock specified in the question.
