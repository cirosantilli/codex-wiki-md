<h1 id="b/6/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For a [piston-driven normal shock](../../../../../../piston-driven-normal-shock.md) with $U>0$, $c_0>0$ and $\gamma>1$, the quadratic has one positive and one negative root. The forward-moving shock uses

$$
\boxed{V=\frac{(\gamma+1)U+\sqrt{(\gamma+1)^2U^2+16c_0^2}}4.}
$$

Since the square root is greater than $(\gamma+1)U$,

$$
V-U>\frac{\gamma-1}{2}U>0.
$$

Therefore **there is always a forward root with shock speed greater than the gas and piston speed** under the stated compression assumptions. This also makes the [density](../../../../../../density.md) ratio $V/(V-U)$ positive and greater than one. Evaluating the quadratic at $V=c_0$ gives a negative value, so the positive root additionally has $V>c_0$: it is [supersonic](../../../../../../supersonic-flow.md) relative to the undisturbed gas. The negative root does not describe the shock driven into the region $x>0$.
