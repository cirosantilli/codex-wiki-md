<h1 id="b/6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let state zero be the undisturbed gas and state one the uniform gas between piston and [shock](../../../../../shock-wave.md). Their laboratory velocities are zero and $U$. In the [shock frame](../../../../../shock-frame.md) their speed magnitudes are $V$ and $V-U$. Mass and momentum jumps give

$$
\rho_1=\rho_0\frac V{V-U},\qquad p_1-p_0=\rho_0VU.
$$

Energy conservation gives $\gamma[p_1/\rho_1-p_0/\rho_0]/(\gamma-1)=VU-U^2/2$. Substitute the preceding relations and $\gamma p_0/\rho_0=c_0^2$, then divide by the nonzero $U$. The result is

$$
\boxed{V^2-\frac{\gamma+1}{2}UV-c_0^2=0.}
$$

The right-moving root is

$$
\boxed{V=\frac{\gamma+1}{4}U+\sqrt{c_0^2+\left(\frac{\gamma+1}{4}U\right)^2}.}
$$

The other root is negative and does not describe the outgoing [shock](../../../../../shock-wave.md). For $U>0$, the quadratic is negative at $V=U$, hence its positive root obeys $V>U$: the [shock](../../../../../shock-wave.md) stays ahead of the piston and the [density](../../../../../density.md) ratio is positive.
