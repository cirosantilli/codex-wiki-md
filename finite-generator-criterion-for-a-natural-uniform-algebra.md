# Finite-generator criterion for a natural uniform algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Natural uniform algebra](natural-uniform-algebra.md)

A [uniform algebra](uniform-algebra.md) is natural exactly when functions with no common zero generate the unit [ideal](ideal.md). If a generated [ideal](ideal.md) is proper, it is contained in a [maximal ideal](maximal-ideal.md). That [maximal ideal](maximal-ideal.md) is closed: the closure of a proper [ideal](ideal.md) stays proper, since an element within distance less than one of the identity is invertible by the [Neumann series](neumann-series.md). The [Gelfand-Mazur theorem](gelfand-mazur-theorem.md) identifies the maximal-ideal quotient with the complex numbers, yielding a [character of an algebra](character-of-an-algebra.md). Naturality would make that character a common-zero evaluation, a contradiction. Conversely, the asserted unit-ideal property makes the zero sets of every finite collection in a character's kernel intersect. Compactness gives a point common to all these zero sets; applying this to $f-\varphi(f)1$ proves $\varphi(f)=f(x)$. For $C(K)$, one can directly take $g_j=\overline{f_j}/\sum_k|f_k|^2$.
