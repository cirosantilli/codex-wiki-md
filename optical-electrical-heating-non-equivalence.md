# Optical-electrical heating non-equivalence

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical substitution radiometer](electrical-substitution-radiometer.md)

[Optical-electrical heating non-equivalence](optical-electrical-heating-non-equivalence.md) means that equal absorbed optical and electrical [powers](power.md) do not necessarily give identical [thermometer](thermometer.md) responses. [Light](light.md) and the heater may deposit [heat](heat-split.md) in different places, producing different [temperature gradients](temperature-gradient.md) and different routes of [thermal conduction](thermal-conduction.md) to the support. Holding one [thermometer](thermometer.md) reading fixed need not hold every part of the cavity at the same [temperature](temperature-split.md). This differs from incomplete [radiometric absorptance](radiometric-absorptance.md): one concerns where absorbed [heat](heat-split.md) goes, the other how much incident [light](light.md) becomes absorbed [heat](heat-split.md).

A useful accounting convention is

$$
P_{\rm in}=\frac{\Delta P_h+\Delta Q_{\rm out}-\Delta P_{\rm par}+\delta_{\rm ne}}{a},
$$

where the signed correction $\delta_{\rm ne}$ is determined by characterization of the instrument and represents the residual departure from the lumped [electrical substitution heat balance](electrical-substitution-heat-balance.md). It is not a universal extra source of [energy](energy-split.md). Varying the beam location or heater location, comparing equivalent temperature excursions, and checking repeatable dark returns can test the assumptions; no single small correction proves that every other correction vanishes.
