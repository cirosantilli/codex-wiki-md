# Locally vanishing principle for sheaf cohomology

↑ **Parent:** [Sheaf cohomology](sheaf-cohomology.md)

A positive-degree [sheaf cohomology](sheaf-cohomology.md) class restricts to zero on some neighbourhood of every point, because a representing cocycle in a [flasque resolution](flasque-resolution.md) is locally a boundary. If a basis closed under finite intersections has vanishing local cohomology in degrees $1,\ldots,i-1$, the neighbourhoods can be chosen in that basis so that the image of the class in $H^i(X,{}_U\mathcal F)$ is zero. Here ${}_U\mathcal F$ is the [direct image from an open restriction](direct-image-from-an-open-restriction.md). Lower local vanishing makes the edge map $H^i(X,{}_U\mathcal F)\to H^i(U,\mathcal F|_U)$ injective. Local vanishing of a class alone does not imply global vanishing.
