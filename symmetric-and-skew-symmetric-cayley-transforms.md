# Symmetric and skew-symmetric Cayley transforms

↑ **Parent:** [Cayley transform of a real skew-symmetric matrix](cayley-transform-of-a-real-skew-symmetric-matrix.md)

For a real [skew-symmetric matrix](skew-symmetric-matrix.md) $B$, its [Cayley transform of a real skew-symmetric matrix](cayley-transform-of-a-real-skew-symmetric-matrix.md) with the opposite sign convention has inverse $C^{-1}=(I+B)(I-B)^{-1}$. Since $C$ is an [orthogonal matrix](orthogonal-matrix.md), symmetry means $C=C^{-1}$, or $(I-B)^2=(I+B)^2$. This forces $B=0$. Skew-symmetry means $C=-C^{-1}$, or $(I-B)^2=-(I+B)^2$, which is equivalent to $B^2=-I$. In that case $C=-B$. Such examples exist in even dimension by taking two-dimensional right-angle [rotation matrices](rotation-matrix.md) in diagonal blocks; in odd real dimension the determinant equation $(\det B)^2=(-1)^n$ rules them out.
