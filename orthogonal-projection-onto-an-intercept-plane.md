# Orthogonal projection onto an intercept plane

↑ **Parent:** [Closest point on a plane](closest-point-on-a-plane.md)

For positive coordinate intercepts $A=(a,0,0)$, $B=(0,b,0)$ and $C=(0,0,c)$, their [plane equation](cartesian-equation-of-a-plane.md) is $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r=1$. The [closest point on a plane](closest-point-on-a-plane.md) to the origin is $\mathbf d=\mathbf n/\|\mathbf n\|^2$, with distance $1/\|\mathbf n\|$. Its [barycentric coordinate system](barycentric-coordinate-system.md) coefficients are proportional to $a^{-2},b^{-2},c^{-2}$ and sum to one.

The projection is also the [orthocenter](orthocenter.md) of triangle $ABC$. Indeed $\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b-\mathbf c)=0$, and $\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf b=\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf c$ because each equals $1/\|\mathbf n\|^2$. Hence $(\mathbf a-\mathbf d)\cdot(\mathbf b-\mathbf c)=0$. Cycling the vertices gives all three altitude directions. This connects an ambient-space [orthogonal projection](orthogonal-projection.md) with intrinsic triangle geometry.
