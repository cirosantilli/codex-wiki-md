# Low-redshift acceleration cosmography

↑ **Parent:** [Supernova discovery of accelerating expansion](supernova-discovery-of-accelerating-expansion.md)

A local derivation makes the acceleration sign transparent. Normalize $a(t_0)=1$ and put $\tau=t_0-t_e$. A [Taylor series](taylor-series.md) gives

$$
a(t_0-\tau)=1-H_0\tau-\frac12q_0H_0^2\tau^2+O(\tau^3),\qquad
z=H_0\tau+(1+q_0/2)H_0^2\tau^2+O(\tau^3).
$$

The [radial null geodesic in FLRW spacetime](radial-null-geodesic-in-flrw-spacetime.md) gives $D_C=c\int_{t_e}^{t_0}dt/a(t)=c[\tau+H_0\tau^2/2+O(\tau^3)]$. Invert the [redshift](redshift.md) series, use $d_L=(1+z)D_C$ through second order, and obtain the displayed [luminosity distance](luminosity-distance.md). [Spatial curvature of an FLRW universe](spatial-curvature-of-an-flrw-universe.md) first changes this relation at third order. Lower $q_0$ therefore means larger $d_L$ at a fixed sufficiently small positive [redshift](redshift.md) and common $H_0$.

At $z=0.05$, the quadratic approximation gives $222.4353\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ for $q_0=-0.55$, within $0.07\%$ of the full flat-model value $222.2891\,\mathrm{Mpc}$. At $z=0.5$ it gives $2971.1574\,\mathrm{Mpc}$, about $4.9\%$ too large. A useful sign argument at low [redshift](redshift.md) is not a precision replacement for the full distance integral in a distant [supernova](supernova.md) analysis.
