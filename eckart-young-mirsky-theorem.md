# Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Low-rank approximation](low-rank-approximation.md)

For a finite [matrix](matrix.md) with [singular values](singular-value.md) in nonincreasing order, truncating its [singular value decomposition](singular-value-decomposition.md) after $k$ terms minimizes the [Frobenius norm](frobenius-norm.md) error, whose square is the sum of the discarded squared [singular values](singular-value.md). It also minimizes the [operator norm](operator-norm.md) error, which equals the next [singular value](singular-value.md), or zero if none remain. See [Mazeika, Section 4.3: Low rank approximation](https://math.uchicago.edu/~may/REU2016/REUPapers/Mazeika.pdf).

The problem of replacing a [matrix](matrix.md) by one of lower [matrix rank](matrix-rank.md) is explicit in Eckart and Young's 1936 paper, [The approximation of one matrix by another of lower rank, Psychometrika 1, 211-218](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychometrika/article/approximation-of-one-matrix-by-another-of-lower-rank/B29672E1EDD0FA1B7611D4DFAFC321B3). The publication predates the modern [image compression](image-compression.md) example, showing how a general matrix question can support later applications.
