# Energy flow (ecology)

↑ **Parent:** [Ecosystem](ecosystem.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_flow_(ecology))

[Energy flow](energy-flow-ecology.md) tracks [energy](energy.md) entering an [ecosystem](ecosystem.md), passing through [organisms](organism.md) and leaving its boundary. To see its quantitative effect, assume net production at one [trophic level](trophic-level.md) is $P_1=1000\,\mathrm{kJ\,m^{-2}\,day^{-1}}$ and assume a transfer efficiency $\varepsilon=0.10$ at each of two successive steps. Here $P_i$ measures the [energy](energy.md) incorporated into new living material per unit [area](surface-area.md) per day, after that level's own [cellular respiration](cellular-respiration.md); it is not a count of [organisms](organism.md) or a standing stock. The [joule](joule.md) supplies the [energy](energy.md) unit, with $1\,\mathrm{kJ}=1000\,\mathrm J$.

$$
P_{i+1}=\varepsilon P_i,\qquad P_2=100,\qquad P_3=10\quad\mathrm{kJ\,m^{-2}\,day^{-1}}.
$$

**[Figure 24](#image-an-assumed-energy-flow-budget-1000-→-100-→-10-kj-per-square-metre-per-day). An assumed energy flow budget: 1000 → 100 → 10 kJ per square metre per day**. Original toy calculation with 10 percent transfer efficiency assumed at both steps. Bars use one linear scale; the labels retain visibility of the smallest bar. The differences 900 and 90 are production not transferred along the selected [food chain](food-chain.md), not measurements of [heat](heat.md) alone. The example is separate from the four-position illustrative [food web](food-web.md).

Only $10/1000=0.01$, or one percent, of the initial production reaches level 3 in this toy [food chain](food-chain.md). The first step leaves $1000-100=900$ and the second $100-10=90$ outside the next level's new production. These differences can include uneaten material, wastes, and [energy](energy.md) released as [heat](heat.md) during [cellular respiration](cellular-respiration.md); their separate amounts have not been assigned. [Detritus](detritus.md) can still carry [energy](energy.md) to [decomposers](decomposer.md). The 10 percent efficiency is an assumption, not a universal law: transfer efficiencies vary with the [organisms](organism.md), food and conditions. The production ratio $P_{i+1}/P_i$ also differs from the fraction of an individual meal converted into growth. See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 46.2: production and transfer efficiency](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/46-2-energy-flow-through-ecosystems).

The shrinking budget explains why supporting another [trophic level](trophic-level.md) requires enough production below it. It does not prove an exact maximum length for every [food chain](food-chain.md), particularly when a [food web](food-web.md) imports resources or has branching diets. Lindeman's 1942 paper grouped feeding relationships into [trophic levels](trophic-level.md), compared transfers and [cellular respiration](cellular-respiration.md), and emphasized the [ecosystem](ecosystem.md) as a unit connecting living and nonliving components. That viewpoint made different [organisms](organism.md) comparable through a common [energy](energy.md) budget. See [Lindeman, The Trophic-Dynamic Aspect of Ecology (1942)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1930126).

Material recycling and [energy flow](energy-flow-ecology.md) describe different bookkeeping. [Carbon](carbon.md), [nitrogen](nitrogen.md) and [phosphorus](phosphorus.md) can be reused through [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction.md) and transfers between [organisms](organism.md) and their surroundings. [Energy](energy.md) transferred as [heat](heat.md) is conserved, but is no longer the same store of chemical [energy](energy.md) available to a [consumer](consumer-food-chain.md); photosynthetic [primary producers](primary-producer.md) need continuing [light](light.md) input. A [decomposer](decomposer.md) helps recycle material and also releases [heat](heat.md) through [metabolism](metabolism.md). Thus loss from one feeding budget means transfer into other forms or routes, never destruction of [energy](energy.md). See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 46.3: material cycles](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/46-3-biogeochemical-cycles). [Conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) applies to the whole accounting.
