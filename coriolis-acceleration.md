# Coriolis acceleration

↑ **Parent:** [Coriolis force](coriolis-force.md)

The Coriolis acceleration is the [Coriolis force](coriolis-force.md) divided by the particle's [mass](mass.md):

$$
\mathbf a_{\rm cor}=-2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v_{\rm rot}.
$$

It is therefore independent of [mass](mass.md) for a specified relative [velocity](velocity.md) and [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md). This is one contribution to the [acceleration](acceleration.md) measured in a [rotating reference frame](rotating-reference-frame.md). For a fixed common origin with an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md) and constant $\boldsymbol\Omega$, the full [equation of motion in a rotating frame](equation-of-motion-in-a-rotating-frame.md) is

$$
\underbrace{\ddot{\mathbf r}_{\rm rot}}_{\text{full rotating acceleration}}
=\frac{\mathbf F_{\rm real}}m
\underbrace{-2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\dot{\mathbf r}_{\rm rot}}_{\text{Coriolis}}
\underbrace{-\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r)}_{\text{centrifugal}}.
$$

All [vectors](vector.md) here are expressed in the rotating axes; $\mathbf F_{\rm real}$ is the sum of actual [forces](force-split.md). An accelerating origin adds a translational apparent [force](force-split.md), and time-dependent [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) also adds the [Euler acceleration](euler-acceleration.md).

The factor two follows directly from the [rotating-frame derivative formula](rotating-frame-derivative-formula.md). First $\mathbf v_{\rm lab}=\dot{\mathbf r}_{\rm rot}+\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r$. Differentiate again in the [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md): differentiating $\dot{\mathbf r}_{\rm rot}$ contributes one $\boldsymbol\Omega\times\dot{\mathbf r}_{\rm rot}$, and differentiating $\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r$ contributes another. Move both terms to the rotating side of [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) to obtain the minus sign and the factor two. The [centrifugal acceleration](centrifugal-acceleration.md) comes from the remaining double [cross product](cross-product.md).
