# Vector area element of an implicit surface

↑ **Parent:** [Vector area element](vector-area-element.md)

For a regular implicit surface $f(x,y,z)=0$ with $f_z\ne0$, the [implicit function theorem](implicit-function-theorem.md) gives a local graph $z=h(x,y)$. Its upward [vector area element](vector-area-element.md) is $(-h_x,-h_y,1)dx\,dy$. Differentiating $f(x,y,h(x,y))=0$ yields $h_x=-f_x/f_z$, $h_y=-f_y/f_z$, proving the formula. Division by $f_z$ makes the final component positive independently of the sign of the original gradient. At a point where $f_z=0$, another projection or parametrization is needed.
