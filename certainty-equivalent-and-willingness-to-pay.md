# Certainty equivalent and willingness to pay

↑ **Parent:** [Certainty equivalent](certainty-equivalent.md)

A [certainty equivalent](certainty-equivalent.md) exchanges a [lottery](lottery.md) for a sure outcome on the same [utility function](utility-function-split.md) scale. A maximum purchase price compares wealth after paying with wealth without purchasing; its [utility indifference price](indifference-price.md) therefore depends on the initial wealth and on the admissible monetary domain. These two numbers are not generally interchangeable when the [utility function](utility-function-split.md) is nonlinear.

For example, suppose $u(x)=x^2$ is applied only on nonnegative wealth, $X\ge0$, $\mathbb EX=\mu$ and $\operatorname{Var}(X)=\sigma^2$. The [certainty equivalent](certainty-equivalent.md) is $\sqrt{\mu^2+\sigma^2}$. A purchase price satisfying the wealth comparison instead obeys

$$
(w-c+\mu)^2+\sigma^2=w^2,
\qquad c=w+\mu-\sqrt{w^2-\sigma^2},
$$

provided the square root exists and every resulting wealth is in the increasing domain. The sufficient condition $w\ge\sqrt{\mu^2+\sigma^2}$ ensures $w-c\ge0$. The formulas differ even though both use the same [expected utility](expected-utility.md) representation. Applying the square function indiscriminately to negative wealth would remove the usual preference for more money and invalidate that monotonic price interpretation.
