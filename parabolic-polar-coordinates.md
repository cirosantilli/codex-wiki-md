# Parabolic polar coordinates

↑ **Parent:** [Orthogonal coordinates](orthogonal-coordinates.md)

These plane [orthogonal coordinates](orthogonal-coordinates.md) use two families of confocal [parabolas](parabola.md). On the region $y\geq0$, take $u,v\geq0$ and define

$$
u=\sqrt{x+\sqrt{x^2+y^2}},\qquad v=\sqrt{-x+\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}.
$$

Then $x=(u^2-v^2)/2$ and $y=uv$. The coordinate [tangent vectors](tangent-vector-split.md) $(u,v)$ and $(-v,u)$ have zero [dot product](dot-product.md), and both have length $\sqrt{u^2+v^2}$. Thus the [Jacobian determinant](jacobian-determinant.md) is $u^2+v^2$, the [area element](area-element-of-a-surface.md) is $(u^2+v^2)\,du\,dv$, and these [orthogonal coordinates](orthogonal-coordinates.md) degenerate only at the origin. Fixed $u=a>0$ gives $x=(a^2-y^2/a^2)/2$; fixed $v=b>0$ gives $x=(y^2/b^2-b^2)/2$. This normalization is a rescaling of the plane section of [parabolic cylindrical coordinates](parabolic-cylindrical-coordinates.md).

Without a restricted [parameter domain](parameter-domain.md), simultaneous sign reversal $(u,v)\mapsto(-u,-v)$ preserves the image, so these [planar parabolic coordinates](parabolic-polar-coordinates.md) are globally two-to-one. The [inverse function theorem](inverse-function-theorem.md) supplies local inverse branches away from the origin. The nonnegative quadrant is one useful branch chart for the upper half-plane.
