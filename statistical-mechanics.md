# Statistical mechanics

↑ **Parent:** [Statistical physics](statistical-physics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics)

[Statistical mechanics](statistical-mechanics.md) connects [microstates](microstate.md) to [macrostates](macrostate.md) through [probability distributions](probability-distribution.md). Its [microcanonical ensemble](microcanonical-ensemble.md), [canonical ensemble](canonical-ensemble.md) and [grand canonical ensemble](grand-canonical-ensemble.md) specify different constraints on exchanges with the surroundings. An [ensemble average](expected-value.md) describes repeated preparations under those constraints; it need not be the value in every individual preparation.

This makes the subject useful whenever tracking every microscopic detail would obscure the question being asked. To predict a [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md), we need how the [expected value](expected-value.md) of [energy](energy.md) changes with [temperature](temperature.md), rather than a record of every unit's state. The [partition function](canonical-partition-function.md) packages that calculation into one reusable object.

In his 1902 book, J. Willard Gibbs systematically developed the [canonical ensemble](canonical-ensemble.md). His preface contrasts following one system through time with studying a collection of systems with different microscopic conditions. Chapter IV defines the [canonical ensemble](canonical-ensemble.md) through an [exponential function](exponential-function.md) of [energy](energy.md) and establishes its normalization. This supplied a general strategy for predicting collective measurements by averaging over possible microscopic conditions, with the [partition function](canonical-partition-function.md) organizing the calculation.
