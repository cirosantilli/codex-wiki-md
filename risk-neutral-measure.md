# Risk-neutral measure

↑ **Parent:** [Mathematical finance](mathematical-finance-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk-neutral_measure)

A [risk-neutral measure](risk-neutral-measure.md) $Q$ is a [probability measure](probability-measure.md) equivalent to the physical [probability measure](probability-measure.md) $P$, meaning they agree on which events have zero [probability](probability.md). For assets without intermediate payments, prices expressed in the chosen [numéraire](numeraire.md) must be [martingales](martingale-split.md) under $Q$. For a positive [bank account](bank-account.md) $B$ and a [stock](stock.md) without dividends, this requires $S/B$ to be a [martingale](martingale-split.md); with dividends, use [discounted dividend gains](discounted-dividend-gains.md). In an unrestricted finite-state one-period [financial market](financial-market.md), existence of such a measure is equivalent to absence of [arbitrage](arbitrage.md), by the [fundamental theorem of asset pricing](fundamental-theorem-of-asset-pricing-split.md).

Physical [probability](probability.md) $p=P(\text{up})$ describes the modeled frequency or belief about an up move. The [risk-neutral probability](risk-neutral-probability.md) $q=Q(\text{up})$ is constrained by traded prices. In the worked [binomial market](discrete-time-binomial-market.md), $q=5/8$ even if $p=1/2$ or $p=4/5$. Calling $Q$ risk-neutral does not assume that investors have no [risk aversion](risk-aversion.md); it changes the weights used for [risk-neutral pricing](risk-neutral-pricing.md).
