<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Put $e=E_+$ and $f=E_-$. The supplied identities give

$$
Hf^kv=(\chi-2k)f^kv,\qquad
ef^kv=k(\chi-k+1)f^{k-1}v.
$$

If no $f^kv$ vanishes, this is the first alternative. Otherwise choose the least $N\ge1$ with $f^Nv=0$. Applying $e$ gives $N(\chi-N+1)f^{N-1}v=0$. Since $f^{N-1}v\ne0$ and the field has characteristic zero, $\chi=N-1$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\chi\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\quad f^kv\ne0\ (0\le k\le\chi),\quad f^kv=0\ (k>\chi).}
$$

The endpoint must include zero: the trivial one-dimensional [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) has $\chi=0$ and $fv=0$, so the printed claim of strict positivity has that counterexample. All nonzero vectors in the displayed chain have distinct $H$-weights, and hence are linearly independent.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

For the intended [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics), index a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) by $v_k=a_{l-2k}$ for $0\le k\le l$, and use

$$
Hv_k=(l-2k)v_k,\qquad fv_k=v_{k+1},\qquad ev_k=k(l+1-k)v_{k-1},
$$

with $v_{-1}=v_{l+1}=0$. This makes the [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) endpoints distinct, identifies the lowering operator, and includes its missing raising action at $k=l$. These are the necessary indexing corrections to the printed display. The same definition for $l=0$ supplies the trivial [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) needed for classification.

Directly on $v_k$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
[H,f]v_k&=-2fv_k,\\
[H,e]v_k&=2ev_k,\\
[e,f]v_k&=((k+1)(l-k)-k(l+1-k))v_k=(l-2k)v_k.
\end{aligned}
$$

These hold at the endpoints too, proving the three defining $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ relations. Finally

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta v_k
&=\bigl(4k(l+1-k)+(l-2k)^2+2(l-2k)\bigr)v_k\\
&=\boxed{l(l+2)v_k.}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the [Casimir element](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#casimir-element) acts by that scalar on the whole [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Let $V\ne0$ be finite-dimensional. Choose an $H$-[eigenvector](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector) and apply $e$ repeatedly until the last nonzero vector $v$ in that chain. Such a last vector exists because $e$ raises the $H$-[eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) by two and $H$ has only finitely many eigenvalues. Then $ev=0$ and $Hv=lv$ for some $l$.

The nonzero $f$-chain cannot continue indefinitely, since its weights are distinct. Part (a), with its zero endpoint included, gives $l\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ and the [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) $v,fv,\ldots,f^lv$. The raising formula in part (a) shows its span is a [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) isomorphic to $A_l$.

To prove $A_l$ [irreducible](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation), a nonzero [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) stable under diagonal $H$ contains a [weight vector](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-vector): [polynomial interpolation](../interpolation.md#polynomial-interpolation) in the distinct eigenvalues projects onto individual [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) lines. Repeated raising reaches $v_0$, and repeated lowering then reaches every $v_k$. Thus the [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) is all of $A_l$. Distinct $l$ give different dimensions $l+1$, so the modules are nonisomorphic. Every finite-dimensional [irreducible](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) contains one of these nonzero simple submodules and hence equals it. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\{A_l:l\ge0\}\text{ is the complete list of finite-dimensional simple modules}.}
$$

The zero [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) has no nonzero simple [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) and is dealt with as the empty [direct sum](../vector-space.md#direct-sum) in part (d).

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

We first prove that every extension of two simple modules splits. Consider

$$
0\longrightarrow A_n\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow A_m\longrightarrow0.
$$

If $n\ne m$, the central [Casimir element](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#casimir-element) has distinct scalars $n(n+2)$ and $m(m+2)$. On $V$ its [minimal polynomial](../linear-operator-theory.md#minimal-polynomial) divides the product of the corresponding two distinct linear factors. Its eigenspaces are submodules, and the [eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) for $m(m+2)$ maps isomorphically onto the [quotient](../arithmetic.md#quotient). It is the required complement.

Now suppose $n=m=l$. Use [generalized weight spaces](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#generalized-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation) for $H$; their possible weights are $l,l-2,\ldots,-l$, because the [characteristic polynomial](../linear-operator-theory.md#characteristic-polynomial) on an extension is the product of those on its two factors. Lift the [quotient](../arithmetic.md#quotient)'s highest vector to $w$ in the [generalized weight space](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#generalized-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation) of [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) $l$. Then $ew=0$ and $f^{l+1}w=0$, since these would lie in [generalized weight spaces](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#generalized-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation) of weights $l+2$ and $-l-2$, which are absent. Also $(H-l)w=av_0$ for a highest vector $v_0$ in the [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) $A_l$. The commutator identity gives

$$
0=ef^{l+1}w=(l+1)f^l(H-l)w=(l+1)a f^lv_0.
$$

Thus $a=0$. The vector $w$ is an actual highest-[weight vector](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-vector). Its lowering chain maps to the full [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) of the [quotient](../arithmetic.md#quotient), so it generates an $A_l$ complementary to the original [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule). This proves splitting also when the two simple types coincide, including $l=0$.

The same splitting holds for a semisimple [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) and simple [quotient](../arithmetic.md#quotient) by induction on the number of [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) summands. Write the [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) as $U_1\oplus U_2$ with $U_1$ simple. Modulo $U_2$, split the extension by the simple [quotient](../arithmetic.md#quotient); the inverse image of that chosen [quotient](../arithmetic.md#quotient) copy is an extension with [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) $U_2$. Split it inductively, obtaining a [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) in $V$ meeting the entire original [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) trivially.

Finally induct on $\dim V$. A maximal proper [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) has smaller [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) and hence is semisimple by induction; the [quotient](../arithmetic.md#quotient) is simple by maximality. The just-proved splitting completes the induction. Hence

$$
\boxed{V\cong\bigoplus_{l\ge0}A_l^{\oplus m_l},\qquad m_l\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},}
$$

with only finitely many nonzero multiplicities. This proof does not assume the complete-reducibility theorem it is establishing.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Use a [triangular decomposition of a Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#triangular-decomposition-of-a-lie-algebra) $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak n_-\oplus\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n_+$. The [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) space of $M^\chi$ has [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) one. If $z$ is central, $zm_\chi$ is still of [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) $\chi-\rho$ and is killed by $\mathfrak n_+$, so

$$
zm_\chi=\theta_\chi(z)m_\chi.
$$

Since the [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) is generated by $m_\chi$ and $z$ commutes with $U(\mathfrak g)$, the same scalar acts on every vector. Addition, multiplication and the unit show that $z\mapsto\theta_\chi(z)$ is an [algebra homomorphism](../algebra-over-a-field.md#algebra-homomorphism-over-a-field) to $\mathbb C$.

For the [polynomial](../polynomial.md) assertion, the relevant variable for an element of $S(\mathfrak h)$ is $\chi$. The dependence on $z$ itself is linear, as already follows from the scalar action. Apply the [Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem](../lie-algebra.md#poincare-birkhoff-witt-theorem) ordering $U(\mathfrak n_-)U(\mathfrak h)U(\mathfrak n_+)$. Because $z$ has adjoint [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) zero, its surviving action on the highest vector is precisely the pure $U(\mathfrak h)$ component $p_z$; terms with a [positive root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#positive-root) factor kill that vector, and a term with only a nontrivial negative [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) factor could not have [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) zero. Since $U(\mathfrak h)=S(\mathfrak h)$,

$$
\boxed{\theta_\chi(z)=p_z(\chi-\rho),\qquad j_z(\chi):=p_z(\chi-\rho)\in S(\mathfrak h).}
$$

This specifies the intended [polynomial](../polynomial.md) despite the printed variable mismatch. The unadorned [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) symbol in that sentence is understood as $M^\chi$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Write $h_\alpha$ for the simple [coroot](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#coroot) and choose the corresponding normalized $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triple. The order condition means

$$
\chi-s_\alpha\chi=k\alpha\in Q_+,\qquad
k=\langle\chi,\alpha^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
$$

The highest vector has $h_\alpha$-[representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) $\langle\chi-\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle=k-1$. The raising-lowering identity gives

$$
E_\alpha E_{-\alpha}^km_\chi
=k((k-1)-k+1)E_{-\alpha}^{k-1}m_\chi=0.
$$

Other simple [positive root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#positive-root) vectors commute with $E_{-\alpha}$ and also kill this vector. Thus it is a highest vector of [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation)

$$
\chi-\rho-k\alpha=s_\alpha\chi-\rho.
$$

The universal property of the [Verma module](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#verma-module) gives the required homomorphism. It is injective: under the [Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem](../lie-algebra.md#poincare-birkhoff-witt-theorem) identification of each [Verma module](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#verma-module) with the free rank-one $U(\mathfrak n_-)$ [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics), it is right multiplication by $E_{-\alpha}^k$. The algebra $U(\mathfrak n_-)$ has no zero divisors, because its associated graded algebra is the [polynomial](../polynomial.md) domain $S(\mathfrak n_-)$. Hence

$$
\boxed{M^{s_\alpha\chi}\hookrightarrow M^\chi,\qquad
m_{s_\alpha\chi}\mapsto E_{-\alpha}^km_\chi.}
$$

For $k=0$ this is the identity map, so the equality case of the order is covered.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

For every $\chi$ on a hyperplane $\langle\chi,\alpha^\vee\rangle=k$, with $k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, part (b) embeds one [Verma module](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#verma-module) into the other. A central element acts on the nonzero image by both scalars, so $j_z(\chi)=j_z(s_\alpha\chi)$ there.

The difference of these two expressions is a [polynomial](../polynomial.md) on $\mathfrak h^*$. Fix all coordinates parallel to those hyperplanes and vary the remaining coordinate. It vanishes at every nonnegative integer, so as a [polynomial](../polynomial.md) in that coordinate it is identically zero. Its coefficient polynomials therefore vanish as well. Consequently the equality holds for all $\chi$. Since the simple reflections generate the [Weyl group](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weyl-group),

$$
\boxed{j_z(w\chi)=j_z(\chi)\qquad(w\in W).}
$$

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

In the shifted normalization of part (a), the [Harish-Chandra isomorphism](../lie-algebra.md#harish-chandra-isomorphism) is the algebra isomorphism

$$
\boxed{Z(U(\mathfrak g))\overset\sim\longrightarrow S(\mathfrak h)^W,\qquad z\longmapsto j_z.}
$$

Thus the centre is identified with the Weyl-invariant polynomials on $\mathfrak h^*$. Central characters satisfy $\theta_\chi=\theta_\xi$ exactly when $\chi$ and $\xi$ belong to the same Weyl orbit. The $\rho$ shift is already incorporated in $M^\chi$ having [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) $\chi-\rho$; without that shift the corresponding Weyl action is the dot action.

<h3 id="2/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#2/e)

The centre commutes with $\mathfrak h$, so every nonzero [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) component of the common [eigenvector](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector) $m$ has the same [central character](../module-theory.md#central-character-of-an-associative-algebra-module) $\theta$. Pick such a component $m_\lambda$. In [Category O](../lie-algebra.md#category-o), its $U(\mathfrak n_+)$-span is finite-dimensional and has finitely many weights. Choose a maximal [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) in that span and a nonzero vector $v$ of that [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation), say $\nu$. Every [positive root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#positive-root) operator kills $v$, since it would raise the [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) within the same finite span. Centrality ensures $zv=\theta(z)v$.

The universal property supplies a nonzero map $M^{\nu+\rho}\to M$ taking its highest vector to $v$. The centre acts on that image both by $\theta$ and by $\theta_{\nu+\rho}$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\theta=\theta_\xi,\qquad \xi=\nu+\rho,\quad\nu\in\operatorname{wt}(M).}
$$

<h3 id="2/f">f</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/f/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#2/f)

Use the standard defining properties of [Category O](../lie-algebra.md#category-o): finite generation over $U(\mathfrak g)$, a [direct sum](../vector-space.md#direct-sum) of finite-dimensional [weight spaces](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-space), and locally finite [positive root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#positive-root) action. In particular the weights lie in a finite union of downward [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) cones. Submodules and quotients retain the [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) bounds and finite [weight spaces](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-space); finite generation of submodules follows from the [Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem](../lie-algebra.md#poincare-birkhoff-witt-theorem) and the noetherian property of $U(\mathfrak g)$.

Choose finitely many [weight vector](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-vector) generators. The sum $T$ of their full [weight spaces](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-space) is finite-dimensional and preserved by the centre. The [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) of its central action has finite codimension in $Z(U(\mathfrak g))$ and kills the generators, hence, by centrality, all of $M$. Thus only finitely many central characters $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r$ can occur in any subquotient: they are the characters of a fixed finite-dimensional commutative [quotient](../arithmetic.md#quotient) algebra of the centre.

By the [Harish-Chandra isomorphism](../lie-algebra.md#harish-chandra-isomorphism) each $\theta_i$ determines one finite Weyl orbit of shifted weights. Let $S$ be the finite union of these orbits after subtracting $\rho$. Every nonzero subquotient has a nonzero highest-[weight vector](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-vector), using the bounded-above weights. Within its finite-dimensional [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) space, the commuting central operators have a common [eigenvector](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector). Part (e) and the central-character restriction show that its [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) belongs to $S$.

Define for any subquotient $N$

$$
d(N)=\sum_{\nu\in S}\dim N_\nu.
$$

It is finite, additive in short exact sequences, and positive whenever $N\ne0$. Hence each strict step of a [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) chain consumes at least one unit of $d(M)$. Refining a nonsimple factor into a proper [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) and [quotient](../arithmetic.md#quotient) cannot continue more than $d(M)$ times. It ends with simple factors, proving

$$
\boxed{M\text{ has a composition series of finite length, at most }d(M).}
$$

The argument proves the assertion for every object of $\mathcal O$, rather than just for a cyclic [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) object.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2010](2010.md)

For $f,g\in\mathcal E$, define their [convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#convolution) by

$$
(f*g)(\lambda)=\sum_{\mu+\nu=\lambda}f(\mu)g(\nu).
$$

The support restrictions make each sum finite: for fixed upper-cone representatives, the [simple root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root) coefficients in the two downward displacements are bounded nonnegative integers. This also proves closure in $\mathcal E$ and associativity by finite reordering at each coefficient. The unit is $\delta_0$, and $\delta_\mu*\delta_\nu=\delta_{\mu+\nu}$.

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

With the source's negative [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) partition function, interpret $Q_+=\sum_{\alpha\in B}\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\alpha$ and allow zero coefficients in the partitions. Write $e^\lambda$ for $\delta_\lambda$, with multiplication meaning [convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#convolution). Then

$$
K=\prod_{\gamma>0}\left(\sum_{n\ge0}e^{-n\gamma}\right).
$$

The denominator needed for the asserted character identities is

$$
D=\prod_{\gamma>0}(e^{\gamma/2}-e^{-\gamma/2})
=e^\rho\prod_{\gamma>0}(1-e^{-\gamma}).
$$

The repeated identical subscripts in the printed denominator would instead make it zero, so those literal factors cannot satisfy an inverse identity. Also the required inverse uses the negative $\rho$ shift. Geometric-series cancellation gives

$$
\boxed{K*\delta_{-\rho}*D=\delta_0.}
$$

With a positive shift the left side would be $\delta_{2\rho}$, which displays the second printed sign problem even after the denominator is repaired.

[Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem](../lie-algebra.md#poincare-birkhoff-witt-theorem) gives $\pi_{M^\xi}=\delta_{\xi-\rho}*K$, since each ordered negative [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) monomial lowers the [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) by its corresponding partition. Therefore

$$
\boxed{D*\pi_{M^\xi}=\delta_\xi.}
$$

The remaining parts use $D$ for the intended [Weyl denominator](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weyl-denominator), while the private TeX preserves the printed signs.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

For a finite-support [formal character](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#formal-character-of-a-weight-module), use the same pullback rule $(wu)(\xi)=u(w^{-1}\xi)$; equivalently $w(e^\lambda)=e^{w\lambda}$. This specifies the action on the denominator, which is a finite delta sum rather than a [polynomial](../polynomial.md) function. The whole infinite-support completion need not be Weyl-stable, so no such claim is required.

A simple [reflection](../linear-algebra.md#reflection-mathematics) $s_\alpha$ permutes the positive roots other than $\alpha$ and sends $\alpha$ to $-\alpha$. In the product defining $D$, all factors are therefore permuted except the $\alpha$ factor, whose sign reverses. Thus $s_\alpha D=-D$. Simple reflections generate $W$, and each has [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) $-1$, so

$$
\boxed{wD=(\det w)D.}
$$

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Use the intended finite-dimensional [irreducible](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) $A^\phi$, with dominant integral [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) $\phi$. Its [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) multiplicity is one. An arbitrary [direct sum](../vector-space.md#direct-sum) having that same largest [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) would not in general satisfy this formula. Set $f=\pi_{A^\phi}$ and use the corrected denominator $D$ of part (a).

Restriction to each [simple root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-root) $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ decomposes into symmetric finite [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) strings, so $f$ is Weyl-invariant. Consequently $Df$ is Weyl-antisymmetric. We prove that no lower Weyl alternants occur, rather than assuming the desired character formula.

First establish the denominator identity itself. The finite sum $D$ has support in $\rho-Q_+$ and coefficient one at $\rho$. Antisymmetry kills all singular [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) coefficients, since a [reflection](../linear-algebra.md#reflection-mathematics) fixing the [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) reverses their sign. Every remaining Weyl orbit has a unique regular dominant representative $\eta$. If it occurs in $D$, then $\eta\le\rho$. Since $\eta$ is integral and regular dominant, $\eta-\rho$ is dominant integral; it also belongs to $-Q_+$. A dominant vector $v=-\sum n_i\alpha_i$ satisfies $(v,v)=-\sum n_i(v,\alpha_i)\le0$, so it must be zero. Thus the only possible orbit is $W\rho$. The leading coefficient gives

$$
D=\sum_{w\in W}(\det w)e^{w\rho}.
$$

For the formal [Laplacian](../multivariable-calculus.md#laplacian) $\mathscr D(e^\lambda)=\|\lambda\|^2e^\lambda$, this implies $\mathscr D D=\|\rho\|^2D$.

Now choose [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) vectors dual under an invariant form, so $[E_\alpha,E_{-\alpha}]=H_\alpha$ with $H_\alpha$ corresponding to the vector $\alpha$, and take the quadratic Casimir

$$
C=\sum_iH_i^2+\sum_{\alpha>0}(E_\alpha E_{-\alpha}+E_{-\alpha}E_\alpha)
$$

for an orthonormal Cartan [basis](../vector-space.md#basis). On a highest vector its scalar is $c_\phi=\|\phi+\rho\|^2-\|\rho\|^2$, hence it has this scalar throughout the cyclic [highest weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics).

Here is the computation of the weighted [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) of its radial identity. For a [positive root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#positive-root), let $X$ be the formal weighted [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) of $E_{-\alpha}E_\alpha$ and $Y$ that of $E_\alpha E_{-\alpha}$. Cyclicity of [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) and [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) shifting give $Y=e^\alpha X$, while their difference is the weighted [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) of $H_\alpha$, namely $(\alpha,\nabla f)$. Thus

$$
X+Y=\frac{1+e^{-\alpha}}{1-e^{-\alpha}}(\alpha,\nabla f).
$$

Taking the [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) of $C$ yields

$$
c_\phi f=\mathscr D f+\sum_{\alpha>0}\frac{1+e^{-\alpha}}{1-e^{-\alpha}}(\alpha,\nabla f).
$$

The product expression for $D$ gives

$$
\nabla\log D=\rho+\sum_{\alpha>0}\frac{e^{-\alpha}}{1-e^{-\alpha}}\alpha.
$$

Using $2\rho=\sum_{\alpha>0}\alpha$ and the [product rule](../calculus.md#product-rule) for the formal [Laplacian](../multivariable-calculus.md#laplacian) therefore gives

$$
\mathscr D(Df)=c_\phi Df+f\mathscr DD
=\|\phi+\rho\|^2Df.
$$

Hence every [representation weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) in the support of $Df$ has [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) $\|\phi+\rho\|$. Its support is also contained in $\phi+\rho-Q_+$, and it is antisymmetric. Any occurring regular [dominant weight](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dominant-weight) $\eta$ thus satisfies $\eta\le\phi+\rho$. If the inequality were strict, then

$$
\|\phi+\rho\|^2-\|\eta\|^2
=(\phi+\rho-\eta,\phi+\rho+\eta)>0,
$$

because the first factor is a nonzero nonnegative sum of simple roots and the second is strictly dominant. This contradicts the equal-[norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) condition. Only the orbit $W(\phi+\rho)$ remains. Its leading coefficient is one, proving

$$
\boxed{D*\pi_{A^\phi}=\sum_{w\in W}(\det w)\delta_{w(\phi+\rho)}.}
$$

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

Convolve the identity of part (c) with the inverse denominator $K*\delta_{-\rho}$ found in part (a). Associativity gives

$$
\pi_{A^\phi}=\sum_{w\in W}(\det w)\,K*\delta_{w(\phi+\rho)-\rho}.
$$

Translation by a [Kronecker delta](../multilinear-algebra.md#kronecker-delta) obeys $(K*\delta_\lambda)(\psi)=K(\psi-\lambda)$. Taking the coefficient at $\psi$ therefore yields

$$
\boxed{\pi_{A^\phi}(\psi)=\sum_{w\in W}(\det w)
K\bigl(\psi+\rho-w(\phi+\rho)\bigr).}
$$

This is the [Kostant multiplicity formula](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#kostant-multiplicity-formula) in the paper's convention that $K$ counts negative [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) partitions. With a partition function counting [positive root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#positive-root) sums, each argument would have the opposite sign.
