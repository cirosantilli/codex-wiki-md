# Finite-hole neighborhoods of a planar compact set

↑ **Parent:** [Complex plane](complex-plane.md)

Given a [compact](compact-space.md) planar set $K$ and an [open](open-set.md) neighborhood $V$, a finite square-grid construction gives a [compact](compact-space.md) polygonal neighborhood inside $V$ with finitely many complementary components. Remove thin polygonal corridors disjoint from $K$ joining holes that lie in the same component of $\mathbb C\setminus K$, and joining holes in its unbounded component to the exterior. Corridors can also include a prescribed point from each of finitely many specified bounded components. There remains a neighborhood whose bounded holes lie in distinct bounded components of $\mathbb C\setminus K$, each containing its chosen point. Standard small polygonal rounding gives a finite union of planar surfaces with [boundary](boundary-of-a-set.md). The index map from its first integral [homology group](homology-group.md) to one integer per bounded hole is an isomorphism. Triangulate a surrounding disc: a one-cycle bounds a two-chain whose coefficients are constant in each complementary component. Zero hole coefficients give a filling supported inside the neighborhood; the face chain of each hole supplies a dual cycle. The cycles can be sums of boundary curves in different components. This uses finite triangulation, not the general [Jordan curve theorem](jordan-curve-theorem.md).
