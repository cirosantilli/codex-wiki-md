# Supremum

↑ **Parent:** [Infimum and supremum](infimum-and-supremum.md)

For a nonempty [set](set-split.md) $A\subseteq\mathbb R$, a real number $s$ is its [supremum](supremum.md) precisely when it is an [upper bound](upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) and no smaller real number is an [upper bound](upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). It is unique: two least [upper bounds](upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) must each be no greater than the other.

For $A=(0,1)$, every number at least one is an [upper bound](upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md), and none smaller than one bounds the whole [set](set-split.md). Thus $\sup A=1$, although $1\notin A$. For $A=[0,1]$, that same [supremum](supremum.md) is also the [maximum](maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md). The [approximation property of a supremum](approximation-property-of-a-supremum.md) supplies witnesses arbitrarily close to an unattained upper edge.
