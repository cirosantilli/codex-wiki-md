# Phase conjugation

↑ **Parent:** [Physical optics](physical-optics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_conjugation)

[Phase conjugation](phase-conjugation.md) creates a returning [monochromatic wave](monochromatic-wave.md) whose spatial [complex amplitude](complex-amplitude.md) is proportional to the [complex conjugate](complex-conjugate.md) of the incident field. Fix the time convention throughout:

$$
E(\mathbf r,t)=\operatorname{Re}\!\left\{U(\mathbf r)e^{-i\omega t}\right\},
\qquad U(\mathbf r)=A(\mathbf r)e^{i\phi(\mathbf r)},
\qquad U_{\mathrm c}(\mathbf r)=\gamma A(\mathbf r)e^{-i\phi(\mathbf r)}.
$$

Here $E$ is one scalar component of the [electric field](electric-field-split.md), $A\geq0$ is the [wave amplitude](wave-amplitude.md), $\omega>0$ is the [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md), and $\gamma$ is a spatially constant [complex number](complex-number.md) setting the return strength and reference [wave phase](phase-waves.md). The [complex conjugation](complex-conjugation.md) acts on $U$, including its spatial carrier, while the time factor remains $e^{-i\omega t}$. Conjugating both factors would merely give another representation of the same real field. With $\gamma=1$, the new real field equals $E(\mathbf r,-t)$ in this ideal [monochromatic wave](monochromatic-wave.md) model; that identity does not assert reversal of an arbitrary pulse or of the apparatus producing it.

The spatial [wave phase](phase-waves.md) becomes $-\phi+\arg\gamma$, so the local [wavevector](wavevector.md) changes from $\nabla\phi$ to $-\nabla\phi$. For example, $U=Ae^{i(k_xx+k_zz)}$ with $k_z>0$ becomes $\gamma Ae^{-i(k_xx+k_zz)}$: both components of the [wavevector](wavevector.md) reverse. In a transparent stationary medium with [isotropy](isotropy.md) and positive [refractive index](refractive-index.md), [light rays](light-ray.md) follow the [wavevector](wavevector.md), giving counterpropagation. The same [wavefronts](wavefront.md) now have opposite propagation normals. This is why [phase conjugation](phase-conjugation.md) can return a distorted beam toward its origin: it reverses the detailed spatial [wave phase](phase-waves.md), rather than requiring that [wave phase](phase-waves.md) to be flat.

The attractive application is correction of [optical aberrations](optical-aberration.md) accumulated on an outward trip. The return uses the distortion itself as the prescription for correction, as the original [thin phase-plate cancellation by phase conjugation](thin-phase-plate-cancellation-by-phase-conjugation.md) example makes explicit. An experimentally accessible implementation measures the spatial [wave phase](phase-waves.md) and uses a [spatial light modulator](spatial-light-modulator.md) to generate its negative; [digital optical phase conjugation](digital-optical-phase-conjugation.md) describes an inspected 2010 demonstration of recovery through [multiple scattering](multiple-scattering.md).
