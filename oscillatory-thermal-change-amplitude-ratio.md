# Oscillatory thermal-change amplitude ratio

↑ **Parent:** [Thermal damping of a sinusoidal density of states](thermal-damping-of-a-sinusoidal-density-of-states.md)

For the sinusoidal density-of-states model, divide the oscillatory [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) of $\Phi(T,\mu)-\Phi(0,\mu)$ by its smooth magnitude $g_0\pi^2(k_BT)^2/6$. This gives the title ratio. Its limit as $T\to0$ is $|\alpha|$, although both changes vanish at exactly zero [temperature](temperature-split.md). The expansion is $|\alpha|[1-7X^2/60+O(X^4)]$, and its large-$X$ envelope is $6|\alpha|/X^2$. A fixed [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) adds the actual [cosine](cosine.md) [phase](phase-waves.md), which can make the oscillatory contribution zero even when the envelope is nonzero. Exponential suppression of the full oscillatory potential therefore must not be confused with this algebraic thermal-change ratio.
