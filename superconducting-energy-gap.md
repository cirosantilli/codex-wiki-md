# Superconducting energy gap

↑ **Parent:** [BCS quasiparticle spectrum](bcs-quasiparticle-spectrum.md)

In this fully gapped isotropic [BCS quasiparticle spectrum](bcs-quasiparticle-spectrum.md), $\Delta$ is the minimum single-[quasiparticle](quasiparticle.md) excitation [energy](energy-split.md). The interval between $-\Delta$ and $+\Delta$ has width $2\Delta$. A process that creates two [quasiparticles](quasiparticle.md) from the [BCS ground state](bcs-ground-state.md) has an energetic threshold $2\Delta$, subject also to its transition-selection rules.[1972 lecture, excitation spectrum and correlated background, pp.78–80](microscopic-quantum-interference-effects-in-the-theory-of-superconductivity-by-leon-neil-cooper.md)

For example, two independently created excitations with $\xi_1=\xi_2=0$ cost $E_1+E_2=2\Delta$. With the chosen cutoff example this is $1.46574\ \mathrm{meV}$, while adding one excitation costs at least $0.732871\ \mathrm{meV}$. This factor of two matters when comparing [photon](photon.md) absorption with the [Giaever superconducting tunnelling experiment](giaever-gap-experiment.md). [Quantum tunnelling](quantum-tunnelling.md) from a normal metal can add one excitation to a sample exhibiting [superconductivity](superconductivity-split.md); with two gapped electrodes, their excitation costs add. A minimum excitation cost is an energetic condition; the measured onset can additionally depend on [temperature](temperature-split.md) and on the available transitions.
