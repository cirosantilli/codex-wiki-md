# Neutral buoyancy

↑ **Parent:** [Buoyancy](buoyancy.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_buoyancy)

[Neutral buoyancy](neutral-buoyancy.md) means a fully immersed body's upward [buoyancy](buoyancy.md) equals its downward [force](force.md) from [gravity](gravity.md). For a body of fixed [volume](volume.md) in a constant-[mass density](density.md) [fluid](fluid.md) under a uniform [gravitational field](gravitational-field.md), this occurs when its mean [mass density](density.md) equals the surrounding [mass density](density.md). A $0.10\ \mathrm m$ [cube](cube.md) of [mass density](density.md) $1000\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ in the stipulated [water](water.md) has [mass](mass.md) $1.000\ \mathrm{kg}$, so both [forces](force.md) are $9.81\ \mathrm N$.

Ideally it can remain at rest at any depth for which it stays fully immersed and clear of boundaries. Raising or lowering it changes the absolute [pressure](pressure.md), but neither its displaced [volume](volume.md) nor the difference between the vertical [pressure](pressure.md) [forces](force.md). There is therefore no vertical [restoring force](restoring-force.md) toward a preferred depth in this model. [Neutral buoyancy](neutral-buoyancy.md) is a vertical [force](force.md) balance, not a claim about resistance to tilting or about a compressible body whose [volume](volume.md) changes with [pressure](pressure.md).
