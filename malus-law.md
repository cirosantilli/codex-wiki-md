# Malus' law

↑ **Parent:** [Linear polarization](linear-polarization.md)

An ideal linear polarizer transmits the fraction $\cos^2\theta$ of incident linearly polarized light when its axis makes angle $\theta$ to the polarization. For a single photon, the corresponding conditional transmission [probability](probability.md) is $|\langle\theta|0\rangle|^2=\cos^2\theta$, by the [Born rule](born-rule.md); rejection has probability $\sin^2\theta$. The classical field calculation projects its electric-field amplitude onto the transmission direction and squares that amplitude to obtain [intensity](intensity-physics.md). This is a conditional probability given the incident polarization and is not automatically the joint probability of passing one analyzer and failing another.
