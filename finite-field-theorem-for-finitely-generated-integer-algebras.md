# Finite-field theorem for finitely generated integer algebras

↑ **Parent:** [Finite-type integer algebra](finite-type-integer-algebra.md)

A [field](field.md) that is a [finite-type integer algebra](finite-type-integer-algebra.md) has prime characteristic or zero. Prime characteristic and [Zariski lemma](zariski-s-lemma.md) make it a finite algebraic extension of a finite prime [field](field.md). In characteristic zero, the same lemma makes it a number [field](field.md); clearing finitely many coefficient denominators makes it integral over $\mathbb Z[1/N]$. The [integral field extension forces the base domain to be a field](integral-field-extension-forces-the-base-domain-to-be-a-field.md), but a prime not dividing $N$ is not invertible there. This contradiction excludes characteristic zero.
