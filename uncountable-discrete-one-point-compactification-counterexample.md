# Uncountable discrete one-point compactification counterexample

↑ **Parent:** [Alexandroff extension](alexandroff-extension.md)

The [one-point compactification](alexandroff-extension.md) $D\cup\{\infty\}$ of an uncountable [discrete space](discrete-space.md) is a [hereditarily normal space](hereditarily-normal-space.md) but not a [perfectly normal space](perfectly-normal-space.md). A subspace missing $\infty$ is discrete; one containing it is closed, hence compact Hausdorff and normal. However, every neighborhood of $\infty$ misses only finitely many points of $D$. A countable intersection misses at most countably many points, so the closed singleton $\{\infty\}$ is not a [G-delta set](g-delta-set.md).
