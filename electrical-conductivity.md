# Electrical conductivity

↑ **Parent:** [Ohm's law](ohm-s-law.md)

[Electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) $\sigma$ measures the local response of [current density](current-density.md) to [electric field](electric-field-split.md). In the rest frame of an [isotropic](isotropy.md) [Ohmic conductor](ohmic-conductor.md), the local form of [Ohm's law](ohm-s-law.md) is

$$
\mathbf J=\sigma\mathbf E,\qquad [\sigma]=\frac{\mathrm{A/m^2}}{\mathrm{V/m}}=(\Omega\,\mathrm m)^{-1}.
$$

Larger positive [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) gives larger [current density](current-density.md) at the same [electric field](electric-field-split.md). It is a bulk material coefficient, distinct from the geometry-dependent [electrical conductance](electrical-conductance.md) of a whole component and from a sheet's [surface conductivity](surface-conductivity.md). [Electrical resistivity](electrical-resistivity.md) is $\rho_{\mathrm{el}}=1/\sigma$ in this scalar model. [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, §9.3: conductivity and resistivity](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/9-3-resistivity-and-resistance).

The local and whole-wire forms fit together particularly neatly. For [uniform current in a straight homogeneous wire](uniform-current-in-a-straight-homogeneous-wire.md), with [length](length.md) $L$, [wire cross-sectional area](wire-cross-sectional-area.md) $A$, constant [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) and terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $V$, the axial [electric field](electric-field-split.md) has magnitude $E=V/L$. Thus

$$
I=jA=\sigma EA=\frac{\sigma A}{L}V,\qquad R=\frac{L}{\sigma A}.
$$

For the defined [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) example $\sigma=5\times10^5\,(\Omega\,\mathrm m)^{-1}$, $L=1\,\mathrm m$, $A=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $V=5\,\mathrm V$, the local values are $E=5\,\mathrm{V/m}$ and $j=2.5\times10^6\,\mathrm{A/m^2}$; integrating gives $I=2.5\,\mathrm A$. One material law therefore supplies both a spatial description and a terminal relation.

The scalar form requires [isotropy](isotropy.md). In an [anisotropic](anisotropy.md) material the response can instead be a [tensor](tensor.md), $J_i=\sum_j\sigma_{ij}E_j$, and [current density](current-density.md) need not be parallel to [electric field](electric-field-split.md); [uniaxial conductivity tensor](uniaxial-conductivity-tensor.md) is one existing example. These statements concern the material's rest frame. A moving [electrical conductor](electrical-conductor.md) instead uses the [moving-conductor Ohm law](moving-conductor-ohm-law.md).
