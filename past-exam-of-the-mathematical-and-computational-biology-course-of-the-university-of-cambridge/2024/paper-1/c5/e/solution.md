<h1 id="c5/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The observed [folded site frequency spectrum](../../../../../folded-site-frequency-spectrum.md) has a larger $3:1$ to $2:2$ ratio than either expectation in part (d), and is closer to the [Yule process](../../../../../yule-process.md) expectation than to the constant-size [Kingman's coalescent](../../../../../kingman-s-coalescent.md) expectation. Both models can nevertheless produce deviations in one finite sample, especially because all mitochondrial sites largely share one genealogy; this observation alone is not a formal model-selection result.

For the [Yule process](../../../../../yule-process.md), one possible violation is a **recent reduction in the per-lineage branching rate** rather than a constant $\lambda$. Lengthening the terminal four-branch interval adds one-descendant branch length and therefore preferentially increases the $3:1$ class.

For [Kingman's coalescent](../../../../../kingman-s-coalescent.md), one possible violation is **recent population expansion** rather than a constant [effective population size](../../../../../effective-population-size.md). Backward in time, coalescence is slow in the large recent population and faster in the smaller ancestral population. The resulting long external branches tend to increase the $3:1$ class relative to the $2:2$ class.
