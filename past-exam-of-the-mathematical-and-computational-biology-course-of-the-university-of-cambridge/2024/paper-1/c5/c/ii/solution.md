<h1 id="c5/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a haploid population with $N$ possible parents, each pair of ancestral lineages merges backward at rate $1/N$ in the [Kingman's coalescent](../../../../../../kingman-s-coalescent.md) approximation. With $k$ lineages there are $\binom{k}{2}$ pairs. Hence the mean [phylogenetic epoch](../../../../../../phylogenetic-epoch.md) duration is

$$
\boxed{E_k^{\rm coal}=\frac{N}{\binom{k}{2}}=\frac{2N}{k(k-1)}}.
$$

Thus $E_2=N$, $E_3=N/3$ and $E_4=N/6$. For an autosomal sample from $N$ diploid individuals, replace the number of gene copies by $2N$, giving $4N/[k(k-1)]$. A mitochondrial sample naturally uses a haploid maternal [effective population size](../../../../../../effective-population-size.md); the common factor cancels in the ratios below.
