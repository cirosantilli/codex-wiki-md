<h1 id="a1/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

Keep the true parameter fixed and imagine repeatedly collecting four [independent and identically distributed](../../../../../independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) observations under the same model. Compute the [maximum-likelihood estimate](../../../../../maximum-likelihood-estimator.md), its estimated [standard error](../../../../../standard-error.md), and the [Wald confidence interval](../../../../../wald-confidence-interval.md) afresh each time. The intended coverage statement is that approximately 95% of those random [confidence intervals](../../../../../confidence-interval.md) contain the fixed true parameter, provided the [normal approximation](../../../../../normal-approximation.md) is adequate.

After these particular observations have been collected, both endpoints are fixed. The frequentist statement does not assign 95% [probability](../../../../../probability.md) to the fixed parameter lying between these particular endpoints. Nor does the [confidence interval](../../../../../confidence-interval.md) describe the range containing 95% of future observations. The small sample makes the nominal 95% coverage an approximation whose accuracy is not guaranteed.
