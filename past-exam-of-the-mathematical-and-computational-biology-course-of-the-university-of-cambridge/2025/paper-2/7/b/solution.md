<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

First convert the [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) to SI units:

$$
\eta=0.1\frac{10^{-3}\ \mathrm{kg}}{10^{-2}\ \mathrm m\,\mathrm s}=0.01\ \mathrm{Pa\,s}.
$$

Using the stipulated effective-size version of [Stokes's law](../../../../../stokes-s-law.md) with $a=20\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm m$, the drag coefficient is

$$
\zeta=6\pi\eta a=6\pi(0.01)(20\times10^{-6})\approx3.76991\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{N\,s\,m^{-1}}.
$$

The speed is $v=10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, hence

$$
\boxed{F_{\mathrm{drag}}=\zeta v\approx3.76991\times10^{-13}\ \mathrm N=3.76991\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{nN}}.
$$

The drag acts opposite the velocity. Its magnitude is about $1.06\times10^5$ times smaller than the substrate force in (a); balancing surrounding-fluid drag alone therefore cannot account for the much larger substrate [traction](../../../../../traction.md). The calculation uses the size parameter exactly as supplied. An exact drag calculation for a cube moving close to a wall would need geometry and boundary corrections beyond this stipulated approximation.
