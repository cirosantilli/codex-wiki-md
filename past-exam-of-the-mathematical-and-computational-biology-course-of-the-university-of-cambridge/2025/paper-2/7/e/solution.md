<h1 id="7/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For the same concentration [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md), the front-to-back difference scales with cell length. A much smaller bacterial cell samples a correspondingly smaller spatial signal, while stochastic arrival and binding of individual [chemoattractant](../../../../../chemoattractant.md) molecules make [receptor](../../../../../receptor-biochemistry.md) measurements noisy. For illustration, a $2\ \mu\mathrm m$ cell in the $0.5$ cm capillary would experience only $0.04\ \mathrm{nM}$ across its length, compared with $0.4\ \mathrm{nM}$ for the longer cell. This illustrates a size penalty; it does not assume that bacterial and human detection thresholds are identical.

Many swimming bacteria, including [Escherichia coli](../../../../../escherichia-coli.md), use [temporal sensing in bacterial chemotaxis](../../../../../temporal-sensing-in-bacterial-chemotaxis.md): they compare recent receptor stimulation with an adapted baseline as they move. Increasing attractant suppresses reorientation and prolongs runs; decreasing attractant increases tumbling. Repeated runs and reorientations therefore generate a biased [random walk](../../../../../random-walk-split.md) up the [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md), without requiring a simultaneous comparison between the front and back. [Chemotaxis feedback regulation](../../../../../chemotaxis-feedback-regulation.md) supplies the adaptation needed to remain sensitive to changes rather than responding only to absolute concentration.

A discriminating experiment changes concentration rapidly and uniformly around the bacteria: a response then occurs without a spatial difference across the cell. The classic temporal-step experiments found increased tumbling after attractant decreases and smoother swimming after increases.[pp. 2509–2512, Abstract and temporal-gradient response experiments](../../../../../the-gradient-sensing-mechanism-in-bacterial-chemotaxis-by-robert-m-macnab-and-daniel-e-koshland-jr.md) In a new experiment, compare up-steps and down-steps with constant-concentration and sham-mixing controls, holding temperature and medium composition fixed. Measure run durations and tumble frequency, rather than only average swimming speed. Such a test supports temporal comparison but does not by itself identify every molecular component or show that all bacterial species use the same mechanism.
