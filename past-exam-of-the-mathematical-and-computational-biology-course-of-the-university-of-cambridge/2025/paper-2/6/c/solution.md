**No.** A [scale-free network](../../../../../scale-free-network.md) is characterized by an approximately power-law tail in its [degree distribution](../../../../../degree-distribution.md), $P(k)\propto k^{-\gamma}$ over a meaningful range. [clustering coefficients](../../../../../clustering-coefficient.md) and mean [graph distances](../../../../../distance-graph-theory.md) do not supply that distribution. One would need the [degrees of vertices](../../../../../degree-graph-theory.md), enough observations over a substantial range, and a fitted power-law model assessed against alternatives. Four [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) cannot establish such a tail, even if their degrees were supplied. [small-worldness](../../../../../small-worldness.md) alone does not imply a [scale-free network](../../../../../scale-free-network.md).
