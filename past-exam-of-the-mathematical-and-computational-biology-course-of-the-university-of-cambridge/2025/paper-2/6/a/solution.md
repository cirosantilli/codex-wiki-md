<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Average the four local [clustering coefficients](../../../../../clustering-coefficient.md) and the four supplied mean [graph distances](../../../../../distance-graph-theory.md), using the same weighting convention for the reference networks:

$$
C=\frac{0.8+0.7+0.6+0.8}{4}=0.725,\qquad L=\frac{3.6+3.7+3.6+3.8}{4}=3.675.
$$

The two reference values average to $C_{\mathrm{rand}}=0.35$ and $L_{\mathrm{rand}}=3.6$. The normalized [small-worldness](../../../../../small-worldness.md) is the relative clustering divided by the relative path length,

$$
\boxed{\sigma=\frac{C/C_{\mathrm{rand}}}{L/L_{\mathrm{rand}}}=\frac{0.725/0.35}{3.675/3.6}\approx2.02915}.
$$

This uses the standard ratio-of-ratios definition, with the supplied degree-matched [random graphs](../../../../../random-graph-split.md) as the chosen reference ensemble.[Results, New measures of small-world-ness, Definition 2 and the ratio of relative clustering to relative path length](../../../../../network-%E2%80%98small-world-ness%E2%80%99-a-quantitative-method-for-determining-canonical-network-equivalence-by-mark-d-humphries-and-others.md)
