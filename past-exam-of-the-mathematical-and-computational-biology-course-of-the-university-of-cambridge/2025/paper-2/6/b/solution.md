Under the conventional $\sigma>1$ criterion, **the supplied summaries classify it as a [small-world network](../../../../../small-world-network.md)**: clustering is about $2.071$ times the reference value, while path length is only about $1.021$ times the reference value. Two reference [random graphs](../../../../../random-graph-split.md) permit a numerical comparison but do not establish the variability or statistical significance of that classification.

There is a literal consistency problem with describing these summaries as those of a four-[vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) simple unweighted [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md). Any finite [graph distance](../../../../../distance-graph-theory.md) is at most three, whereas the supplied mean path lengths exceed three. A local [clustering coefficient](../../../../../clustering-coefficient.md) with at most three neighbours can only be $0$, $1/3$, $2/3$ or $1$ under the usual definition, not $0.7$ or $0.8$. Thus the result above is the intended calculation from the given summaries; **they cannot all describe an ordinary four-node simple unweighted network**. A larger network or a separately specified weighted convention would be needed for a literal network interpretation.
