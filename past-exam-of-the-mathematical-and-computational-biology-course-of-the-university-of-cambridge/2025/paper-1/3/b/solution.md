↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Using divisor $n=4$, the [covariance matrix](../../../../../covariance-matrix.md) is

$$
\boxed{\Sigma=\frac14X_c^{\mathsf T}X_c=
\begin{pmatrix}3&1&2\\1&3&2\\2&2&4\end{pmatrix}}.
$$

For example, the first [variance](../../../../../variance-split.md) is $(1+1+1+9)/4=3$, and the first-second [covariance](../../../../../covariance.md) is $(3-1-1+3)/4=1$. If “sample [covariance matrix](../../../../../covariance-matrix.md)” means the unbiased convention, use divisor $n-1=3$ instead: $S=(4/3)\Sigma$. This multiplies every [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) by $4/3$ and leaves all [principal component loadings](../../../../../principal-component-loading.md) and explained-[variance](../../../../../variance-split.md) fractions unchanged.
