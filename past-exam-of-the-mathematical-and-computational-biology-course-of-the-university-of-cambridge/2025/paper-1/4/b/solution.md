Conditional predictions for observed birds and years add their fitted [random intercepts](../../../../../random-intercept.md) to the fixed part of the [Gaussian linear mixed model](../../../../../gaussian-linear-mixed-model.md):

$$
\boxed{\widehat Y_{11,2012}=2.274+0.123(3)+0.383420+0.023624=3.050044},
$$



$$
\boxed{\widehat Y_{12,2014}=2.274+0.123(7)-0.426676-0.056633=2.651691}.
$$

These are predicted [expected values](../../../../../expected-value-split.md), so they need not be [integers](../../../../../integer-split.md). The first is likely more reliable: age three is within the displayed age range, whereas age seven requires [extrapolation](../../../../../extrapolation.md) beyond ages one to six. A quantitative comparison would need the [prediction intervals](../../../../../prediction-interval.md), including uncertainty in both fixed coefficients and [random effects](../../../../../random-effect.md); the displayed coefficients alone do not supply them.
