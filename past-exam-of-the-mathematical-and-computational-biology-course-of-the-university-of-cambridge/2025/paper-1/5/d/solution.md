<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At an [equilibrium](../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md), $H=rHe^{-aP}$ and $P=bH(1-e^{-aP})$. Taking $H=0$ forces $P=0$, giving extinction. If $H\ne0$, the first equation gives $e^{-aP}=1/r$ and hence

$$
\boxed{P_*=\frac{\log r}{a},\qquad H_*=
\frac{r\log r}{ab(r-1)}}\qquad(r\ne1).
$$

These are the only isolated candidates obtained from the equations. There is an exceptional parameter case: at $r=1$, $P=0$ and every $H\ge0$ is an [equilibrium](../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md). Thus the assertion of exactly two [equilibria](../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) needs $r\ne1$, and only the positive candidate belongs to the biological state space when $r>1$.
