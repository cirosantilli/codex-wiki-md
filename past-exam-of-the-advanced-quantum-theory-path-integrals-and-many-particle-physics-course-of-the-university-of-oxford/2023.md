<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

Assume $Q\ge2$ and $L\ge2$; the trivial exceptions are described below. In this [periodic Potts chain](../spin-model.md#periodic-potts-chain), every satisfied bond contributes $-J$ to the [energy](../energy.md), and no bond can contribute less. All $L$ bonds can be satisfied simultaneously by choosing the same state at every site. Conversely, satisfying every bond forces this uniform configuration. Therefore the [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) [energy](../energy.md) and [degeneracy factor](../statistical-physics.md#degeneracy-factor) are

$$
\boxed{E_0=-JL,\qquad d_0=Q.}
$$

The $Q$ choices are distinct configurations on the labelled lattice: a global relabelling of the states is a [symmetry](../physics.md#symmetry-physics), not an identification of microstates.

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

Let $w$ count the unequal neighbouring pairs, or [domain walls](../critical-phenomenon.md#domain-wall). The [energy](../energy.md) is $E=-JL+Jw$. A single [domain wall](../critical-phenomenon.md#domain-wall) is impossible with [periodic boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#periodic-boundary-conditions): following all the other, equal-state bonds around the ring would force its endpoints to be equal too. Two [domain walls](../critical-phenomenon.md#domain-wall) are possible, splitting the ring into two nonempty uniform arcs carrying different states. Thus the [first excited state](../energy-eigenvalue.md#first-excited-state) has

$$
\boxed{E_1=-JL+2J,\qquad E_1-E_0=2J.}
$$

Choose the two wall bonds in $\binom L2$ ways. For that choice, identify one of the resulting arcs by its smallest site label. Its state has $Q$ choices and the other arc has $Q-1$. This counts each configuration exactly once, giving the [two-domain-wall degeneracy of a Potts ring](../spin-model.md#two-domain-wall-degeneracy-of-a-potts-ring)

$$
\boxed{d_1=\binom L2Q(Q-1).}
$$

For example, $Q=2,L=4$ gives $d_0=2$ and $d_1=12$; the remaining two configurations are alternating and have four [domain walls](../critical-phenomenon.md#domain-wall). The formula also covers $L=2$, where the specified ring [energy](../energy.md) counts the two directed neighbouring bonds, and an unequal pair breaks both. If $Q=1$ or $L=1$, every allowed configuration is a [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) and there is no [first excited state](../energy-eigenvalue.md#first-excited-state). For $Q\ge3$, the number of [domain walls](../critical-phenomenon.md#domain-wall) need not be even: three distinct states on a three-site ring give three, although one remains impossible.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

The [Boltzmann factor](../canonical-ensemble.md#boltzmann-factor) factorizes over bonds. Define the [spin-chain transfer matrix](../spin-model.md#transfer-matrix-for-a-classical-spin-chain) by

$$
T_{ab}=e^{\beta J\delta_{ab}}
=1+(e^{\beta J}-1)\delta_{ab},\qquad a,b=1,\ldots,Q.
$$

Here $\delta_{ab}$ is the [Kronecker delta](../multilinear-algebra.md#kronecker-delta). Summing the product of these [matrix elements](../vector-space.md#matrix-element) over all configurations, and closing the final bond by [periodic boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#periodic-boundary-conditions), gives the [canonical partition function](../canonical-ensemble.md#canonical-partition-function)

$$
Z=\sum_{\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_L}
T_{\sigma_1\sigma_2}\cdots T_{\sigma_L\sigma_1}
=\operatorname{Tr}T^L.
$$

Hence, writing $I$ for the [identity matrix](../vector-space.md#identity-matrix) and $P$ for the [all-ones matrix](../vector-space.md#all-ones-matrix),

$$
\boxed{T=g(\beta J)I+P,\qquad g(\beta J)=e^{\beta J}-1.}
$$

Every element of $P^2$ is a sum of $Q$ ones, while every diagonal element of $P$ is one. Thus

$$
\boxed{P^2=QP,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}P=Q.}
$$

More explicitly, for $u=(1,\ldots,1)^T$, $Pu=Qu$. If $\sum_a v_a=0$, then $Pv=0$. These complementary [eigenspaces](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) have [dimensions](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) $1$ and $Q-1$. Equivalently, $P/Q$ is the [orthogonal projector](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection) onto the [linear span](../vector-space.md#linear-span) of $u$. The [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) and their [eigenvalue multiplicities](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue-multiplicity) are therefore

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
P:&\quad Q\ (1),\qquad 0\ (Q-1),\\
T:&\quad \lambda_+=e^{\beta J}+Q-1\ (1),\qquad
\lambda_-=e^{\beta J}-1\ (Q-1).
\end{aligned}}
$$

Since the two [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) share these [eigenvectors](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector), the exact finite-ring [canonical partition function](../canonical-ensemble.md#canonical-partition-function) is

$$
\boxed{Z=(e^{\beta J}+Q-1)^L+(Q-1)(e^{\beta J}-1)^L.}
$$

Both terms matter in the finite-ring low-[temperature](../temperature.md) limit: discarding the second would lose the correct [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) [degeneracy factor](../statistical-physics.md#degeneracy-factor) and introduce a spurious single-wall contribution.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Put $x=e^{-\beta J}$. Factoring out the [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) [Boltzmann factor](../canonical-ensemble.md#boltzmann-factor) from the exact [canonical partition function](../canonical-ensemble.md#canonical-partition-function) gives

$$
Z=e^{\beta JL}\left\{[1+(Q-1)x]^L+(Q-1)(1-x)^L\right\}.
$$

At fixed finite $L$, the [binomial theorem](../binomial-coefficient.md#binomial-theorem) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
[1+(Q-1)x]^L+(Q-1)(1-x)^L
&=1+(Q-1)\\
&\quad+[L(Q-1)-(Q-1)L]x\\
&\quad+\binom L2[(Q-1)^2+Q-1]x^2+O_{L,Q}(x^3)\\
&=Q\left[1+\binom L2(Q-1)x^2+O_{L,Q}(x^3)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Taking the [logarithm](../calculus.md#logarithm) yields the [finite-ring low-temperature expansion](../spin-model.md#finite-ring-low-temperature-expansion) of the [Helmholtz free energy](../thermodynamic-potential.md#helmholtz-free-energy),

$$
\boxed{F=-JL-\frac{\log Q}{\beta}
-\frac{L(L-1)(Q-1)}{2\beta}e^{-2\beta J}
+O_{L,Q}\!\left(\frac{e^{-3\beta J}}{\beta}\right).}
$$

The constant term is the [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) [energy](../energy.md). The term $-\beta^{-1}\log Q$ is the contribution of the [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) [degeneracy factor](../statistical-physics.md#degeneracy-factor). The first thermal correction follows directly by retaining the [first excited state](../energy-eigenvalue.md#first-excited-state) manifold:

$$
Z=e^{-\beta E_0}\left[d_0+d_1e^{-2\beta J}+\cdots\right],
\qquad \frac{d_1}{d_0}=\binom L2(Q-1).
$$

Each pair of [domain walls](../critical-phenomenon.md#domain-wall) costs $2J$, explaining the absence of an $e^{-\beta J}$ term. For $Q=2$, every configuration has an even number of [domain walls](../critical-phenomenon.md#domain-wall), so the displayed remainder improves to $O_L(e^{-4\beta J}/\beta)$.

This is an expansion for fixed $L$, not a uniform [thermodynamic limit](../statistical-physics.md#thermodynamic-limit). In particular, the small correction requires $\binom L2(Q-1)x^2\ll1$, with higher terms also controlled as $x\to0$ at fixed $L,Q$. Taking $L\to\infty$ first instead selects $\lambda_+$ and gives

$$
\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac FL=-J-\frac1\beta\log[1+(Q-1)x].
$$

Its leading correction is of order $x$. The finite-ring pair constraint and the extensive population of [domain walls](../critical-phenomenon.md#domain-wall) in an arbitrarily long chain explain the different ordering of these two limits.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Use $\hbar=1$ throughout. Let $N_a=a^\dagger a$, $N_b=b^\dagger b$ and $N=N_a+N_b$. Operators belonging to different [boson](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#boson) species commute. The proposed [Schwinger boson representation](../quantum-mechanics.md#schwinger-boson-representation) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
S^+S^-&=a^\dagger b b^\dagger a=N_a(N_b+1),\\
S^-S^+&=b^\dagger a a^\dagger b=N_b(N_a+1).
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently the first of the [spin commutation relations](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin-commutation-relations) is

$$
\boxed{[S^+,S^-]=N_a-N_b=2S^z.}
$$

The [number operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#number-operator) [commutators](../lie-algebra.md#commutator) $[N_a,a^\dagger]=a^\dagger$, $[N_a,a]=-a$ and their $b$ counterparts give

$$
\begin{aligned}
[S^z,S^+]&=\tfrac12([N_a,a^\dagger]b-a^\dagger[N_b,b])=a^\dagger b=S^+,\\
[S^z,S^-]&=-b^\dagger a=-S^-.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus all the [spin commutation relations](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin-commutation-relations) hold. Also $[N,S^\pm]=[N,S^z]=0$: each fixed-$N$ sector is invariant. To identify its [spin](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin), calculate the [Casimir operator](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#casimir-element):

$$
\begin{aligned}
S^2&=(S^z)^2+\tfrac12(S^+S^-+S^-S^+)\\
&=\tfrac14(N_a-N_b)^2+N_aN_b+\tfrac12(N_a+N_b)\\
&=\frac N2\left(\frac N2+1\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

The [irreducible spin representation](../angular-momentum-operator.md#irreducible-spin-representation) with prescribed $s$ therefore requires $N=2s$, and its normalized [Fock basis](../quantum-field-theory.md#fock-basis) correspondence is

$$
\boxed{|s,m\rangle\ \longleftrightarrow\ |s+m,s-m\rangle
=\frac{(a^\dagger)^{s+m}(b^\dagger)^{s-m}}
{\sqrt{(s+m)!(s-m)!}}|0,0\rangle.}
$$

Conversely, $s=(n_a+n_b)/2$ and $m=(n_a-n_b)/2$. The nonnegative integer [occupation numbers](../quantum-field-theory.md#occupation-number) give precisely $m=-s,-s+1,\ldots,s$, and there are $2s+1$ such states. As a check, the [spin raising operator](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin-raising-operator) acts as

$$
a^\dagger b|s+m,s-m\rangle
=\sqrt{(s+m+1)(s-m)}\,|s+m+1,s-m-1\rangle,
$$

which is the usual [spin ladder operator](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin-ladder-operator) coefficient $\sqrt{s(s+1)-m(m+1)}$.

For a lattice of fixed [spins](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin), the [local spin constraint in the Schwinger boson representation](../quantum-mechanics.md#local-spin-constraint-in-the-schwinger-boson-representation) is essential:

$$
\boxed{(a_n^\dagger a_n+b_n^\dagger b_n)|\psi\rangle=2s|\psi\rangle
\quad\text{at every site }n.}
$$

The full two-species [bosonic Fock space](../quantum-field-theory.md#bosonic-fock-space) also contains other local [irreducible spin representations](../angular-momentum-operator.md#irreducible-spin-representation); fixing only the total [boson](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#boson) number does not remove them.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

For the specified positive-exponent [discrete Fourier transform](../discrete-fourier-transform.md), the [inverse discrete Fourier transforms](../discrete-fourier-transform.md#inverse-discrete-fourier-transform) have negative exponents:

$$
\boxed{a_n=\frac1{\sqrt L}\sum_qe^{-iqn}A_q,\qquad
b_n=\frac1{\sqrt L}\sum_qe^{-iqn}B_q.}
$$

Every sum runs over $q=2\pi j/L$, $j=0,\ldots,L-1$. Indeed, [orthogonality of roots of unity](../algebra.md#orthogonality-of-roots-of-unity) gives

$$
\frac1L\sum_qe^{iq(r-n)}=\delta_{rn},
$$

so substituting the forward [discrete Fourier transform](../discrete-fourier-transform.md) into either proposed inverse recovers the original operator. Taking [Hermitian conjugates](../hilbert-space.md#hermitian-conjugation) supplies the inverse formulas for the [creation operators](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#creation-operator).

Multiplying the inverse [discrete Fourier transforms](../discrete-fourier-transform.md) gives the local [number operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#number-operator) combination

$$
\boxed{\frac12(a_n^\dagger a_n+b_n^\dagger b_n)
=\frac1{2L}\sum_{q,k}e^{i(q-k)n}
\left(A_q^\dagger A_k+B_q^\dagger B_k\right).}
$$

This local expression includes off-diagonal [momentum](../momentum.md) terms. Only after summing over sites does [orthogonality of roots of unity](../algebra.md#orthogonality-of-roots-of-unity) give

$$
\frac12\sum_n(a_n^\dagger a_n+b_n^\dagger b_n)
=\frac12\sum_q(A_q^\dagger A_q+B_q^\dagger B_q).
$$

In a physical fixed-$s$ state the first boxed operator acts as $s$ at every site, by the [local spin constraint in the Schwinger boson representation](../quantum-mechanics.md#local-spin-constraint-in-the-schwinger-boson-representation).

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Take $L\ge2$, so neighbouring operators act at distinct sites. The identity

$$
S_n^xS_{n+1}^x+S_n^yS_{n+1}^y
=\tfrac12(S_n^+S_{n+1}^-+S_n^-S_{n+1}^+)
$$

expresses the [Hamiltonian operator](../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics) of this [quantum XY chain](../spin-model.md#quantum-xy-chain) in [spin ladder operators](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin-ladder-operator). Substituting the [Schwinger boson representation](../quantum-mechanics.md#schwinger-boson-representation) gives the requested quartic expression:

$$
\boxed{H=-\frac J2\sum_n
\left(a_n^\dagger b_n b_{n+1}^\dagger a_{n+1}
+b_n^\dagger a_n a_{n+1}^\dagger b_{n+1}\right).}
$$

Operators at different sites commute, allowing [normal ordering](../perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#normal-ordering) without a correction here. For the first quartic term, the [inverse discrete Fourier transforms](../discrete-fourier-transform.md#inverse-discrete-fourier-transform) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_n a_n^\dagger b_{n+1}^\dagger b_n a_{n+1}
&=\frac1{L^2}\sum_{n,p,q,r,t}
e^{i(p+q-r-t)n}e^{i(q-t)}A_p^\dagger B_q^\dagger B_r A_t\\
&=\frac1L\sum_{p,q,r,t}
\delta^{(L)}_{p+q,r+t}e^{i(q-t)}A_p^\dagger B_q^\dagger B_r A_t.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\delta^{(L)}$ is one when its two [momenta](../momentum.md) agree modulo $2\pi$ and zero otherwise; it expresses lattice [momentum conservation](../momentum.md#momentum-conservation). The other quartic term has the same ordered operators with phase $e^{i(p-r)}$. On the support of this [Kronecker delta](../multilinear-algebra.md#kronecker-delta), $p-r=t-q$ modulo $2\pi$, so the two phases sum to $2\cos(q-t)$. Thus the [Fourier vertex of a Schwinger boson chain](../spin-model.md#fourier-vertex-of-a-schwinger-boson-chain) is

$$
\boxed{H=-\frac JL\sum_{p,q,r,t}
\delta^{(L)}_{p+q,r+t}\cos(q-t)\,
A_p^\dagger B_q^\dagger B_r A_t.}
$$

An equivalent three-sum expression sets $t=p+q-r$ modulo $2\pi$, so its coefficient is $\cos(r-p)$. The factor $1/L$ comes from four factors $L^{-1/2}$ and one site sum, while the factor of two from the two exchange terms cancels the original $1/2$. The formula is [Hermitian](../hilbert-space.md#hermitian-operator): conjugation exchanges $p$ with $t$ and $q$ with $r$, and the coefficient is unchanged after imposing lattice [momentum conservation](../momentum.md#momentum-conservation). It also preserves each local total [boson](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#boson) number in its real-space form, hence preserves the physical constrained subspace.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Use $\langle O\rangle=\langle G|O|G\rangle$. The given state is a [two-species uniform boson number state](../quantum-ideal-gas-statistics.md#two-species-uniform-boson-number-state): only the $q=0$ mode of each species is occupied. Applying an [annihilation operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#annihilation-operator) to an unoccupied mode gives zero, while $A_0^\dagger A_0$ has [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $n_A$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\langle A_q^\dagger A_k\rangle=n_A\delta_{q0}\delta_{k0},\qquad
\langle B_q^\dagger B_k\rangle=n_B\delta_{q0}\delta_{k0}.}
$$

The two species have independent fixed [occupation numbers](../quantum-field-theory.md#occupation-number), so mixed [expectation values](../quantum-system.md#expectation-value) factorize. The inverse [discrete Fourier transforms](../discrete-fourier-transform.md) yield

$$
\langle a_i^\dagger a_j\rangle=\frac{n_A}{L},\qquad
\langle b_i^\dagger b_j\rangle=\frac{n_B}{L},\qquad
\boxed{\langle a_n^\dagger a_n+b_n^\dagger b_n\rangle
=\frac{n_A+n_B}{L}.}
$$

In the momentum-space [Hamiltonian operator](../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics), only $p=q=r=t=0$ contributes: each [annihilation operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#annihilation-operator) must remove an occupied [boson](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#boson), and each [creation operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#creation-operator) must restore it to the same mode. Hence

$$
\langle A_p^\dagger B_q^\dagger B_r A_t\rangle
=n_An_B\delta_{p0}\delta_{q0}\delta_{r0}\delta_{t0},
\qquad
\boxed{\langle H\rangle=-\frac JLn_An_B.}
$$

There is no replacement of $n_B$ by $n_B+1$ in this [energy](../energy.md): in the real-space calculation the relevant neighboring-site [commutator](../lie-algebra.md#commutator) is zero. Both expressions assume distinct neighbouring sites, as in [ii](#2/b/ii).

To match the prescribed [spin](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) length in the mean, impose $n_A+n_B=2sL=:N$. At fixed integer $N$, the [energy](../energy.md) is minimized by maximizing $n_An_B=[N^2-(n_A-n_B)^2]/4$. Consequently the best choices within this relaxed family are

$$
\boxed{\{n_A,n_B\}=\{\lfloor sL\rfloor,\lceil sL\rceil\}.}
$$

When $N$ is even, $n_A=n_B=sL$ and $\langle H\rangle=-Js^2L$. When $N$ is odd, either assignment of $(N-1)/2,(N+1)/2$ gives $\langle H\rangle=-J(N^2-1)/(4L)$. Without fixing $N$, this auxiliary [boson](../tensor-product-of-quantum-systems.md#boson) [Hamiltonian operator](../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics) has no lower bound: taking $n_A=n_B\to\infty$ sends its [expectation value](../quantum-system.md#expectation-value) to $-\infty$.

Next, $S_i^x=(a_i^\dagger b_i+b_i^\dagger a_i)/2$. Each term changes the two species' [occupation numbers](../quantum-field-theory.md#occupation-number), producing a state [orthogonal](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) to $|G\rangle$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\langle S_i^x\rangle=0,\qquad \langle S_i^y\rangle=0,\qquad
\langle S_i^z\rangle=\frac{n_A-n_B}{2L}.}
$$

For distinct sites $i,j$ modulo $L$, expand the product of the two [spin operators](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin-operator). The terms $S_i^+S_j^+$ and $S_i^-S_j^-$ again have zero [expectation value](../quantum-system.md#expectation-value). The remaining terms give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle S_i^+S_j^-\rangle
&=\langle a_i^\dagger a_j\rangle\langle b_i b_j^\dagger\rangle
=\frac{n_A}{L}\frac{n_B}{L},\\
\langle S_i^-S_j^+\rangle&=\frac{n_An_B}{L^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\langle b_i b_j^\dagger\rangle=n_B/L+\delta_{ij}$ and the [Kronecker delta](../multilinear-algebra.md#kronecker-delta) vanishes at distinct sites. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\langle S_m^xS_{m+n}^x\rangle=\frac{n_An_B}{2L^2}
\quad(n\not\equiv0\pmod L).}
$$

The same holds for the $y$ components. At the balanced choice and as $L\to\infty$, each transverse [two-point correlation function](../critical-phenomenon.md#two-point-correlation-function) tends to $s^2/2$ independently of separation, whereas the one-point transverse [magnetization](../electromagnetism.md#magnetization) is zero. This is [transverse spin order in a number eigenstate](../statistical-physics.md#transverse-spin-order-in-a-number-eigenstate): the state has a definite total $S^z=(n_A-n_B)/2$ and retains the global [U(1)](../lie-theory.md#circle-group) [symmetry](../physics.md#symmetry-physics) of rotations about $z$, yet has the transverse correlations of an ordered state with no selected common azimuth. The sum of the $x$ and $y$ correlations tends to $s^2$, describing ferromagnetic [long-range order](../statistical-physics.md#long-range-order) within this approximation. The [thermodynamic limit](../statistical-physics.md#thermodynamic-limit) is understood with both the separation and $L$ growing, without identifying the two sites periodically.

**Fixed-spin qualification.** The proposed state is not actually in the physical fixed-$s$ [Hilbert space](../hilbert-space.md) for $s>0,L>1$. It satisfies the [local spin constraint in the Schwinger boson representation](../quantum-mechanics.md#local-spin-constraint-in-the-schwinger-boson-representation) only in [expectation value](../quantum-system.md#expectation-value). For example, with $L=2,s=1/2,n_A=n_B=1$,

$$
|G\rangle=\tfrac12(a_0^\dagger+a_1^\dagger)(b_0^\dagger+b_1^\dagger)|\mathrm{vac}\rangle.
$$

The nonzero component $a_0^\dagger b_0^\dagger|\mathrm{vac}\rangle/2$ has local total [occupation numbers](../quantum-field-theory.md#occupation-number) $(2,0)$ and hence local [spins](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) $(1,0)$, instead of $(1/2,1/2)$. More generally the local total [number operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#number-operator) $N_n$ has [variance](../variance.md)

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(N_n)
=\frac{n_A+n_B}{L}\left(1-\frac1L\right)
=2s\left(1-\frac1L\right)>0.}
$$

Each species contributes a [binomial distribution](../discrete-probability-distribution.md#binomial-distribution) at a given site, with success probability $1/L$, and the two contributions are independent. In the physical subspace that [variance](../variance.md) must be zero. The proposed state even has

$$
\langle S_n^2\rangle=s(s+1)+\tfrac14\operatorname{Var}(N_n),
$$

by the [Casimir operator](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#casimir-element) identity from [a](#2/a). Thus the calculated [energy](../energy.md) is a relaxed [mean-field theory](../statistical-physics.md#mean-field-theory) estimate, not a [Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle](../linear-operator-theory.md#rayleigh-ritz-variational-principle) bound for the stated fixed-$s$ [quantum XY chain](../spin-model.md#quantum-xy-chain). Projecting onto $N_n=2s$ at every site would give a physical trial state and generally change its [expectation values](../quantum-system.md#expectation-value); in the two-site example the projected state has [energy](../energy.md) $-J$, whereas the unprojected value is $-J/2$. The calculated [long-range order](../statistical-physics.md#long-range-order) characterizes the supplied approximation and does not establish [long-range order](../statistical-physics.md#long-range-order) in the exact one-dimensional [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state).
