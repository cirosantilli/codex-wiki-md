<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $w$ count the unequal neighbouring pairs, or [domain walls](../../../../../domain-wall.md). The [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is $E=-JL+Jw$. A single [domain wall](../../../../../domain-wall.md) is impossible with [periodic boundary conditions](../../../../../periodic-boundary-conditions.md): following all the other, equal-state bonds around the ring would force its endpoints to be equal too. Two [domain walls](../../../../../domain-wall.md) are possible, splitting the ring into two nonempty uniform arcs carrying different states. Thus the [first excited state](../../../../../first-excited-state.md) has

$$
\boxed{E_1=-JL+2J,\qquad E_1-E_0=2J.}
$$

Choose the two wall bonds in $\binom L2$ ways. For that choice, identify one of the resulting arcs by its smallest site label. Its state has $Q$ choices and the other arc has $Q-1$. This counts each configuration exactly once, giving the [two-domain-wall degeneracy of a Potts ring](../../../../../two-domain-wall-degeneracy-of-a-potts-ring.md)

$$
\boxed{d_1=\binom L2Q(Q-1).}
$$

For example, $Q=2,L=4$ gives $d_0=2$ and $d_1=12$; the remaining two configurations are alternating and have four [domain walls](../../../../../domain-wall.md). The formula also covers $L=2$, where the specified ring [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) counts the two directed neighbouring bonds, and an unequal pair breaks both. If $Q=1$ or $L=1$, every allowed configuration is a [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) and there is no [first excited state](../../../../../first-excited-state.md). For $Q\ge3$, the number of [domain walls](../../../../../domain-wall.md) need not be even: three distinct states on a three-site ring give three, although one remains impossible.
