# Covariant transversality of a vacuum plane wave

↑ **Parent:** [Plane electromagnetic wave](plane-electromagnetic-wave.md)

For a source-free [plane electromagnetic wave](plane-electromagnetic-wave.md) in [vacuum](vacuum.md), let $n^\mu=(1,\mathbf s)$ be its null propagation [Lorentz four-vector](four-vector.md). In signature $(+---)$, take $F^{0i}=-E_i/c$, $F^{ij}=-\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$, and define the [dual electromagnetic field tensor](dual-electromagnetic-field-tensor.md) by $\widetilde F^{0i}=-B_i$, $\widetilde F^{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}E_k/c$. Lower both indices with the [Minkowski metric](minkowski-metric.md). Direct contraction gives

$$
n^\mu F_{\mu\nu}=\left(-\frac{\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf E}{c},\frac{\mathbf E}{c}+\mathbf s\times\mathbf B\right),\qquad
n^\mu\widetilde F_{\mu\nu}=\left(-\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf B,\mathbf B-\frac{\mathbf s\times\mathbf E}{c}\right).
$$

Both contractions vanish exactly when the transverse [plane electromagnetic wave](plane-electromagnetic-wave.md) [Maxwell equations](maxwell-equations-split.md) hold. Being zero [covectors](covector.md), they remain zero in every [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md). Thus the transformed spatial [wavevector](wavevector.md) is perpendicular to both transformed fields, even though the separate three-dimensional [dot products](dot-product.md) are not themselves [Lorentz scalars](lorentz-scalar.md) for arbitrary fields.

One contraction alone is insufficient without the other [Maxwell equations](maxwell-equations-split.md). In particular, adding a [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) parallel to $\mathbf s$ leaves $n^\mu F_{\mu\nu}$ unchanged but violates $\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf B=0$. Conversely the two scalar perpendicularity conditions alone do not impose the required relation $\mathbf B=\mathbf s\times\mathbf E/c$ between field strengths and directions.
