# Countability from vanishing averages of distinct sequences

↑ **Parent:** [Countable set](countable-set.md)

If every [sequence](sequence.md) of distinct elements of a positive-real [set](set-split.md) $A$ has arithmetic averages tending to zero, then $A$ is a [countable set](countable-set.md). For each positive [integer](integer.md) $n$, the threshold [set](set-split.md) $\{a\in A:a\ge1/n\}$ must be finite: an infinite sequence inside it would have averages at least $1/n$. The [Archimedean property](archimedean-property.md) makes $A$ their union, so the [countable union of countable sets](countable-union-of-countable-sets.md) theorem applies. The hypothesis concerns every distinct-element sequence, not merely one enumeration.
