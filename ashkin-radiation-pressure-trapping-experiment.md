# Ashkin radiation-pressure trapping experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Optical tweezers](optical-tweezers.md)

[Arthur Ashkin](arthur-ashkin.md)'s 1970 first experiment suspended transparent latex [spheres](sphere.md) in [water](water.md) inside a $120\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ glass cell. A horizontal $514.5\,\mathrm{nm}$ argon [laser](laser-split.md) beam, focused to a $6.2\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ radius, illuminated individual beads viewed through a [microscope](microscope.md). An off-centre $2.68\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ bead moved toward the beam axis and downstream until reaching the cell wall; blocking the beam released it into [Brownian motion](brownian-motion-split.md). Transparent particles and liquid reduced competing thermal effects. This observed sequence separates transverse attraction from downstream [radiation pressure](radiation-pressure.md): wall-assisted confinement is not free three-dimensional confinement by one beam.[A. Ashkin, Acceleration and Trapping of Particles by Radiation Pressure, 1970, p. 156 and Fig. 1(a)](acceleration-and-trapping-of-particles-by-radiation-pressure-by-arthur-ashkin.md)

The beam-blocking comparison is particularly informative: persistence against a wall while illuminated and release when illumination stops test an optical cause. To infer a [force](force-split.md) from the downstream [velocity](velocity.md), one additionally needs a [linear drag](linear-drag.md) model and the bead's distance from the wall; the observed motion alone does not calibrate an unrestricted [harmonic optical trap](harmonic-optical-trap.md).
