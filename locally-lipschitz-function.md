# Locally Lipschitz function

↑ **Parent:** [Lipschitz continuity](lipschitz-continuity.md)

A [function](function-split.md) between [metric spaces](metric-space.md) is locally Lipschitz if every point has a [neighbourhood](neighbourhood-mathematics.md) on which one finite [Lipschitz bound](lipschitz-bound.md) works for every pair. The neighbourhood and bound may depend on the point. A [continuously differentiable](continuously-differentiable-function.md) real function on an open [interval](interval-mathematics.md) has this property: continuity of its [derivative](derivative.md) bounds that derivative on a sufficiently small closed interval, and the [mean value theorem](mean-value-theorem.md) bounds every secant slope there.

The [function](function-split.md) $x^2$ is locally Lipschitz at every real point but has no single bound on the [real line](real-line.md). The [square root](square-root.md) is locally Lipschitz on $(0,\infty)$, and has the bound $1/(2\sqrt a)$ on $[a,b]$ for $0<a<b$. Adding zero to its domain changes the conclusion at that point: the ratios in the [Lipschitz continuity](lipschitz-continuity.md) example become unbounded, so it is not locally Lipschitz at zero. This distinction is what permits the delayed, nonunique ODE solutions illustrated under [Picard-Lindelöf theorem](picard-lindelof-theorem.md).
