# Length contraction

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz factor](lorentz-factor.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Length_contraction)

A rod of rest length $L_0$ moving parallel to its length has simultaneous laboratory length $L=L_0/\gamma$, where $\gamma$ is the [Lorentz factor](lorentz-factor.md). Here $L_0$ is the rod's [proper length](proper-length.md) measured in its [rest frame](rest-frame.md). Transverse lengths are unchanged by the corresponding [Lorentz transformation](lorentz-transformation.md).

For a rod at rest in $S'$ with endpoints $x'=0$ and $x'=10\,\mathrm m$, let $S'$ move at $v=0.6c$ relative to the laboratory $S$. Record both endpoint [positions](position.md) at laboratory time $t=0$. The [Lorentz transformation](lorentz-transformation.md) then gives $x'=1.25x$, so their recorded positions are $0$ and $8\,\mathrm m$: the laboratory length is $10/1.25=8\,\mathrm m$. This is an operational definition: synchronized laboratory detectors register the endpoints at the same laboratory time. A photograph collects light emitted at different times and need not show this length. [Einstein (1905), sections 2 and 4: measuring moving rods](https://fourmilab.ch/etexts/einstein/specrel/www/).

For those same two endpoint [spacetime events](event-relativity.md), $\Delta t'=-1.25(0.6c)(8\,\mathrm m)/c^2=-6\,\mathrm m/c$. Thus the front-end measurement occurs earlier in $S'$ by about $20\,\mathrm{ns}$. [Length contraction](length-contraction.md) compares simultaneous endpoint measurements in each frame, which select different pairs of [spacetime events](event-relativity.md); it does not mean that the rod has been compressed in its own [rest frame](rest-frame.md).
