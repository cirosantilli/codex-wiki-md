# Stern-Gerlach historical and modern interpretations

↑ **Parent:** [Stern-Gerlach experiment](stern-gerlach-experiment.md)

The 1922 interpretation concerned [space quantization](space-quantization.md) of atomic [magnetic moments](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) in the old [quantum theory](quantum-theory-split.md). The modern electron [spin](spin.md) proposal came in 1925; the original observers did not identify intrinsic electron [spin](spin.md) as the cause of their splitting. An orbital interpretation then seemed plausible, but the later [spin](spin.md) explanation gives a different account of the same two-component evidence.[Pakvasa, pp. 1–3: original orbital interpretation, 1925 spin proposals and later reinterpretation](the-stern-gerlach-experiment-and-the-electron-spin-by-sandip-pakvasa.md)

Modern atomic data assign neutral [silver](silver.md) the [ground state](ground-state.md) [electron configuration](electron-configuration.md) $[\mathrm{Kr}]4d^{10}5s$ and electronic term ${}^2S_{1/2}$. The filled [electron shells](electron-shell.md) contribute zero net electronic [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md); the outer [unpaired electron](unpaired-electron.md) has [orbital angular momentum](orbital-angular-momentum.md) $L=0$, [spin](spin.md) $S=1/2$, and electronic total [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) $J=1/2$. The [magnetic quantum number](magnetic-quantum-number.md) has two values $m_J=\pm1/2$.[Handbook of Basic Atomic Spectroscopic Data by Jean E. Sansonetti and William C. Martin; Ag I ground-state configuration and term; electronic J, distinct from isotope nuclear-spin rows](handbook-of-basic-atomic-spectroscopic-data-by-jean-e-sansonetti-and-william-c-martin/atomic-data-for-silver-ag.md)

In the effective electronic description,

$$
\mu_z=-g_J\mu_B\frac{J_z}{\hbar},\qquad
J_z|J,m_J\rangle=\hbar m_J|J,m_J\rangle,\qquad
\mu_z\simeq\mp\mu_B\quad(g_J\simeq2).
$$

The minus sign matters: for positive $G$, the component with $m_J=+1/2$ has negative electronic [magnetic moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) and deflects toward negative $z$ in our coordinates. Calling the upper spot “spin up” without specifying the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) and channel calibration can therefore reverse the assignment. The [total angular momentum operator](total-angular-momentum-operator.md) here sums electronic [orbital angular momentum](orbital-angular-momentum.md) and electronic [spin](spin.md); $J$ is not a claim that the [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md) has no [spin](spin.md). [Hyperfine structure](hyperfine-structure.md) and nuclear [magnetic moments](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) can require additional internal levels, so a two-channel electronic approximation must be justified for the field and resolution being used.

This is a neutral [atomic beam](atomic-beam.md), not a beam of free [electrons](electron.md). The deflected [mass](mass.md) in the flight equations is the entire [silver](silver.md) [chemical atom](atom-physics.md); its dominant electronic [magnetic moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) supplies the state-dependent [force](force-split.md). Replacing that atom by a free [electron](electron.md) would also introduce the charged particle's [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md) and a different preparation problem. The ideal two-state model is useful because it isolates the connection between discrete internal components and spatially resolved outputs; it does not retroactively turn the 1922 deposit into a direct contemporary identification of intrinsic electron [spin](spin.md).
