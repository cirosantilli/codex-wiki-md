# Manganese dioxide peroxide contact demonstration

↑ **Parent:** [Heterogeneous catalysis](heterogeneous-catalysis.md)

The University of Washington's [Decomposition of H₂O₂ with MnO₂](https://chem.washington.edu/lecture-demos/decomposition-h2o2-mno2) describes a supervised demonstration using [solid](solid.md) [manganese dioxide](manganese-dioxide.md) and [hydrogen peroxide](hydrogen-peroxide.md) listed as 30%. Its cylinder version uses a spatula to introduce the [solid](solid.md); the stated readout is immediate [molecular oxygen](molecular-oxygen.md) bubbles and [heat](heat-split.md) production. An alternate bottle version replaces the contents of a tea bag with the [solid](solid.md) and suspends it above the liquid. Releasing the bag brings it into contact with the liquid. The bag-above-liquid stage supplies a before-contact comparison; the page gives no timed gas collection, replicated blank trials or quantitative [reaction rates](reaction-rate.md).[Chemicals and Solutions; Materials; Procedure; Alternate Procedure: Genie in a bottle, apparatus and steps 3–6](decomposition-of-h%E2%82%82o%E2%82%82-with-mno%E2%82%82-by-university-of-washington-department-of-chemistry.md)

The balanced net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) is $2\mathrm{H_2O_2}\longrightarrow2\mathrm{H_2O}+\mathrm{O_2}$. Appearance of gas after contact supports a contact-dependent acceleration of decomposition under these conditions. It does not distinguish surface conversion from a contribution of dissolved material, establish regeneration, or measure an [activation energy](activation-energy.md). The simultaneous [heat](heat-split.md) release also means that [temperature](temperature-split.md) can change after contact. A matched no-[solid](solid.md) comparison and an empty-bag comparison would test background decomposition and the bag's contribution; these are proposed controls, not reported trials. The [catalysed peroxide comparison](catalysed-peroxide-comparison.md) supplies a complementary inspected teaching experiment with separate single-addition and paired-addition comparisons.
