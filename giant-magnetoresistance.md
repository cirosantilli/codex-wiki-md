# Giant magnetoresistance

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetoresistance](magnetoresistance.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_magnetoresistance)

[Giant magnetoresistance](giant-magnetoresistance.md) connects the relative [magnetizations](magnetization.md) of adjacent magnetic layers to their electrical transport. In a suitable magnetic-metal/nonmagnetic-metal stack, aligning the layers can leave one [spin](spin.md) channel with an easy conducting path through both magnetic layers; opposite alignments make each channel encounter a more resistive layer. [Albert Fert](albert-fert.md)'s account traces the spin-dependent-conduction idea to [Nevill Mott](nevill-francis-mott.md) and distinguishes the later device geometries.[pp.1517–1520, spin-dependent conduction and CIP/CPP geometry](nobel-lecture-origin-development-and-future-of-spintronics-by-albert-fert.md)

The beauty is that a magnetic arrangement becomes an electrical readout. Preparing reproducible layers, establishing their [magnetizations](magnetization.md), and measuring [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) are separate parts of that inference. The [Fe-Cr giant magnetoresistance experiments](fe-cr-giant-magnetoresistance-experiments.md) show how those parts were joined; the [two-current resistor model](two-current-resistor-model.md) isolates a simple transport mechanism under stated assumptions.
