# Lambertian reflectance

↑ **Parent:** [Optics](optics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambertian_reflectance)

[Lambertian reflectance](lambertian-reflectance.md) is the ideal diffuse-reflection model with direction-independent outgoing [radiance](radiance.md), the [frequency](frequency.md)-integrated [specific intensity](spectral-radiance.md). If a fraction $0\leq\rho\leq1$ of incident [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) is reflected, a surface receiving [irradiance](irradiance.md) $E$ has reflected [radiance](radiance.md) $L=\rho E/\pi$. The reflected outward [power](power.md) per [area](surface-area.md) is $\pi L=\rho E$ by the same hemispherical integral used for [radiant exitance](radiant-exitance.md). Its [radiant intensity](radiant-intensity.md) still acquires the [projected area](projected-area.md) factor in [Lambert's cosine law](lambert-s-cosine-law.md).

For $E=100\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ and $\rho=0.80$, the reflected [radiance](radiance.md) is $80/\pi\approx25.4648\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,sr^{-1}}$. A patch of [area](surface-area.md) $0.020\,\mathrm{m^2}$ reflects $1.6\,\mathrm W$ in total, and its [radiant intensities](radiant-intensity.md) at $0^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ are respectively $1.6/\pi$ and $0.8/\pi\,\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}}$. The [radiance](radiance.md) stays fixed as the view changes, provided the illumination stays fixed. Changing the incident [angle](angle.md) can change [irradiance](irradiance.md) and therefore the reflected [radiance](radiance.md).
