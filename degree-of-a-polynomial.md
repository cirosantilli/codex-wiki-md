# Degree of a polynomial

↑ **Parent:** [Polynomial](polynomial-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_of_a_polynomial)

The degree of a nonzero [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) is the largest exponent of a [monomial](monomial.md) with a nonzero [coefficient](coefficient.md). Thus $X^3-3X^2+4$ has degree three, while any nonzero constant [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) has degree zero. The zero [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) is treated separately in degree statements.

Over an [integral domain](integral-domain.md), [polynomial degrees](degree-of-a-polynomial.md) add under [multiplication](multiplication.md): $\deg(pq)=\deg p+\deg q$ for nonzero factors, because the product of their [leading coefficients](leading-coefficient-of-a-polynomial.md) cannot vanish. Under [addition](addition.md), cancellation may lower the degree: $(X^2+1)+(-X^2+X)=X+1$. This explains why degree is reliable for counting factors and bounding [polynomial roots](root-of-a-polynomial.md), but cannot always be inferred from the degrees of summands alone.
