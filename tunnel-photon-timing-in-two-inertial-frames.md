# Tunnel photon timing in two inertial frames

↑ **Parent:** [Relativity of simultaneity](relativity-of-simultaneity.md)

A runner enters a stationary tunnel of [length](length.md) $L$ at the tunnel-frame instant a photon is emitted towards them from the far end. The interception fraction is $f=\beta/(1+\beta)$, where $\beta=v/c$. In the runner frame, with entry at the origin, emission is at $(x',t')=(\gamma L,-\gamma vL/c^2)$ and the entrance clock simultaneously reads $-vL/c^2$. The entrance is then ahead by $\gamma\beta^2L$, so the photon travels $\gamma L$, not the contracted tunnel [length](length.md) $L/\gamma$. Subtracting that initial entrance gap from the entrance's motion during the photon flight gives the same interception fraction. This is a concrete application of [relativity of simultaneity](relativity-of-simultaneity.md), [length contraction](length-contraction.md) and the [Lorentz transformation](lorentz-transformation.md).
