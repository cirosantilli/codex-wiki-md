## Stellar mass determination

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)

Orbital dynamics gives the most direct stellar masses: a resolved binary orbit and distance determine the relative semimajor axis, and [Kepler's third law](physics.md#kepler-s-third-law) gives the total mass. The barycentric size or radial-velocity amplitude ratio gives individual masses. An [eclipsing binary](#eclipsing-binary) inclination removes the sine-cubed projection factor from spectroscopic masses; a single-lined [binary mass function](#binary-mass-function) alone does not uniquely determine both masses. Surface-gravity/radius and stellar-track estimates are model-dependent and are calibrated against dynamical binary measurements.

## Mass loss (astrophysics)

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)

Loss of matter from a star or other astronomical system, for example through a [stellar wind](#stellar-wind) or explosive ejection. [Mass loss rate](physics.md#mass-loss-rate) conventions must distinguish the positive ejected rate $|\dot M|$ from the negative change of the retained [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass), $\dot M$. In a [binary star](#binary-star), the fate of an orbit also depends on the [specific angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#specific-angular-momentum) carried away.

## Stellar rotation

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_rotation)

The spin of a star about its axis, which can be [solid-body rotation](classical-mechanics.md#solid-body-rotation) or vary through the interior. [Angular momentum transport](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum-transport) and rotational mixing can change the [stellar composition profile](stellar-structure.md#stellar-composition-profile) and available fuel. An observed surface rotation rate does not determine the entire interior profile.

### Gyroscopic pumping in a star

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar rotation](#stellar-rotation)

In a steady axisymmetric [star](#star), a torque due to [Reynolds stress](turbulence.md#reynolds-stress), [Maxwell stress tensor](electromagnetism.md#maxwell-stress-tensor) or [viscosity](fluid-mechanics.md#dynamic-viscosity) must balance advection of [specific angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#specific-angular-momentum) by [stellar meridional circulation](stellar-structure.md#meridional-circulation-in-a-star). Writing the nonadvective [angular momentum transport](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum-transport) flux as $\mathbf F_J$ gives the displayed balance. Thus stresses that maintain [differential rotation](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#differential-rotation) also drive a meridional response. The [stellar thermal-wind balance](#stellar-thermal-wind-balance) supplies a separate leading force constraint, not a replacement for this torque equation.

### Stellar thermal-wind balance

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar rotation](#stellar-rotation)

Taking the [curl](calculus.md#curl) of axisymmetric [hydrostatic equilibrium](statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) with centrifugal acceleration gives $\varpi\partial_z\Omega^2=(\nabla p\times\nabla\rho)_\phi/\rho^2$. For an approximately spherical [ideal gas](thermodynamics.md#ideal-gas) with constant composition, use $\nabla\rho=\nabla p/c_s^2-\rho\nabla S/c_p$ to obtain the displayed relation. Latitudinal [entropy](thermodynamics.md#entropy) differences can support noncylindrical [differential rotation](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#differential-rotation). A [barotropic fluid](fluid-mechanics.md#barotropic-fluid) gives cylindrical rotation in this leading balance; the relation does not determine the transport stresses or circulation rate.

## Stellar gravity

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)

Outside a spherical [star](#star), its gravitational acceleration is $-GM_\star\mathbf r/r^3$. It provides the dominant central force for [Kepler orbits](classical-mechanics.md#kepler-orbit) in a star-dominated planetary system.

## Stellar population

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_population)

A stellar population is an ensemble of [stars](#star) described by its formation times, initial [masses](classical-mechanics.md#mass), composition and present [stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution) states. Number fractions must be distinguished from mass-weighted fractions. Shared formation times can correlate the states of components of a [binary star](#binary-star) even when their initial masses have [independence](random-variable.md#independent-random-variables).

### Population III star

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar population](#stellar-population)

A Population III star is a first-generation star formed from essentially metal-free primordial gas. Limited cooling generally raises its characteristic fragmentation mass and favors a more top-heavy [initial mass function](#initial-mass-function) than in enriched star formation.

### Population II star

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar population](#stellar-population)

A Population II star is an old metal-poor star formed from gas enriched by earlier stellar generations. Metal and dust cooling let enriched gas fragment to lower masses than primordial gas under otherwise comparable conditions.

### Age-metallicity degeneracy

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar population](#stellar-population)

Integrated spectra can confuse older [stellar populations](#stellar-population) with more metal-rich ones because both can redden the light and change [absorption lines](#absorption-line). Line strengths depend on [stellar metallicity](galaxy.md#stellar-metallicity), temperatures, ages, and abundance ratios. Weak metal lines in a young population dominated by hot bright [stars](#star) need not indicate low [stellar metallicity](galaxy.md#stellar-metallicity).

### Evolutionary-state selection by stellar lifetimes

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar population](#stellar-population)

Given a birth-mass distribution and age distribution, an evolutionary state occupies the mass-age set where its starting lifetime has elapsed and its ending lifetime has not. The state number fraction is the [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) measure of this set. [Initial mass function](#initial-mass-function) tail coordinates and the [probability integral transform](probability-theory.md#probability-integral-transform) can turn these weighted regions into simple geometric areas.

#### Stellar lifetime regions in a mass-age diagram

↑ **Parent:** [Evolutionary-state selection by stellar lifetimes](#evolutionary-state-selection-by-stellar-lifetimes)

A phase bounded by mass-dependent lifetimes $\tau_a(M)$ and $\tau_b(M)$ occupies $\tau_a(M)<t<\tau_b(M)$, clipped to the population age range. Its boundaries can be mapped into uniform [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) coordinates to evaluate evolutionary-state fractions.

### Birth-age distribution under constant star formation

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar population](#stellar-population)

If a population forms at a constant number rate for duration $T$ and every birth object is retained in the census, its present ages are uniform on $[0,T]$. A fixed [initial mass function](#initial-mass-function) with finite mean birth mass also makes constant mass-based [star formation rate](galaxy.md#star-formation-rate) equivalent to constant number rate.

## Stellar atmosphere

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_atmosphere)

A [stellar atmosphere](#stellar-atmosphere) is the outer region that determines the escaping spectrum and matches the interior [stellar structure](stellar-structure.md) to its radiation boundary conditions. A [photosphere](stellar-structure.md#photosphere) is the layer near unit [optical depth](astrophysics.md#optical-depth), not a zero-temperature boundary. The inward intensity at the outer surface vanishes when there is no external illumination.

### Stellar chemical abundance

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar atmosphere](#stellar-atmosphere)

A stellar abundance can be expressed as an elemental or isotopic mass fraction, or as a number ratio to [hydrogen](chemistry.md#hydrogen). Inferring it from a [spectral line](#spectral-line) requires a model for level populations, [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity), radiation and velocity fields. Agreement between suitable ionization stages and weak photospheric lines tests whether the adopted [stellar atmosphere](#stellar-atmosphere) is adequate.

#### Metallicity

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar chemical abundance](#stellar-chemical-abundance)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallicity)

The abundance of [chemical elements](chemistry.md#chemical-element) heavier than [helium](chemistry.md#helium) in astronomical material. A [metal mass fraction](#metal-mass-fraction) measures their combined [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass); spectroscopic abundance ratios are other measures of metallicity and need not be numerically identical to that fraction.

##### Metal mass fraction

↑ **Parent:** [Metallicity](#metallicity)

The fraction of total [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) in [chemical elements](chemistry.md#chemical-element) heavier than [helium](chemistry.md#helium), so the [hydrogen mass fraction](#hydrogen-mass-fraction), [helium mass fraction](#helium-mass-fraction) and metal mass fraction satisfy $X+Y+Z=1$. Despite the astronomical name, this includes elements such as [carbon](chemistry.md#carbon) and [oxygen](chemistry.md#oxygen) that are not chemical metals.

### Non-LTE stellar atmosphere

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar atmosphere](#stellar-atmosphere)

When radiative rates compete with or dominate collisions, level populations need statistical equilibrium coupled to [radiative transfer](astrophysics.md#radiative-transfer), rather than thermal population laws at the local [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature). Radiation from other depths can make the local radiation field differ from the [Planck function](astrophysics.md#planck-function). This changes ionization and spectral-line strengths, so a geometrically thin atmosphere may still require non-LTE abundance modelling.

### Chromosphere

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar atmosphere](#stellar-atmosphere)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosphere)

A heated layer above a photosphere can have an outward [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature) increase. Strong lines formed there may show emission where an outward-cooling photosphere would produce absorption. The energy maintaining the [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature) inversion is additional atmospheric heating; the exact height and transfer regime vary between stars.

### Spectral line

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar atmosphere](#stellar-atmosphere)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_line)

A narrow spectral feature records enhanced emission or absorption near a transition frequency. In a [stellar atmosphere](#stellar-atmosphere), its strength depends on atomic populations, [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity), velocity fields and the [source function](astrophysics.md#radiative-transfer-source-function). Lines therefore diagnose composition as well as the physical atmosphere in which they form.

#### Pressure broadening

↑ **Parent:** [Spectral line](#spectral-line)

Collisions and fluctuating neighboring-particle electric fields alter atomic energy levels and coherence times, broadening a [spectral line](#spectral-line). In a simple impact treatment the coherence-decay rate gives a Lorentzian width of order $\Gamma/(2\pi)$; the precise definition of full or half width must be stated. Unlike [Doppler broadening](statistical-physics.md#doppler-broadening), this depends primarily on collision rates and perturber density. In stellar [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity) calculations it alters line overlap and the low-opacity windows entering the [Rosseland mean opacity](astrophysics.md#rosseland-mean-opacity).

#### Voigt profile

↑ **Parent:** [Spectral line](#spectral-line)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voigt_profile)

A Voigt profile is the convolution of a [Gaussian distribution](probability-theory.md#normal-distribution) and a [Cauchy distribution](probability-theory.md#cauchy-distribution). For an [absorption line](#absorption-line), thermal [Doppler broadening](statistical-physics.md#doppler-broadening) contributes the Gaussian core while finite excited-state lifetime contributes the Lorentzian wings. Instrumental broadening and unresolved motions must also be modeled when inferring [column density](statistical-physics.md#column-density) and [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature).

#### Emission line

↑ **Parent:** [Spectral line](#spectral-line)

An [emission line](#emission-line) is a localized excess of [specific intensity](astrophysics.md#specific-intensity) above the continuum. Heated chromospheric material can give a line source above the photospheric continuum source. Optically thin circumstellar gas can also add line photons through recombination or collisional excitation. Such line formation need not obey a thermal photospheric source prescription.

#### Absorption line

↑ **Parent:** [Spectral line](#spectral-line)

An [absorption line](#absorption-line) is a deficit of observed spectral [specific intensity](astrophysics.md#specific-intensity) relative to the surrounding continuum. In a photosphere with [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature) falling outward, increased line [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity) makes photons emerge from cooler layers than the continuum. For an absorption-dominated [LTE](astrophysics.md#local-thermodynamic-equilibrium) source, the [Planck function](astrophysics.md#planck-function) then supplies a smaller line [specific intensity](astrophysics.md#specific-intensity). A foreground layer with source below the incident continuum gives another direct transfer description.

## Star

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star)

### Low-mass main-sequence star

↑ **Parent:** [Star](#star)

A low-mass star maintained by sustained [hydrogen burning](#hydrogen-burning), rather than the continuing cooling of a [brown dwarf](#brown-dwarf). Near the [hydrogen-burning minimum mass](#hydrogen-burning-minimum-mass) its radius is of order a Jovian radius. Stars below roughly $0.35M_\odot$ are approximately fully convective, with a radiative photosphere; mass, composition and age affect the precise transition.

### Solar radius

↑ **Parent:** [Star](#star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_radius)

The solar radius is the conventional unit of length based on the radius of the Sun. The nominal value is $R_\odot=6.957\times10^8\,\mathrm m$.

### Sun

↑ **Parent:** [Star](#star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun)

#### Solar corona

↑ **Parent:** [Sun](#sun)

The solar corona is the [Sun](#sun)'s hot, dilute outer atmosphere. Its optically thin atomic lines diagnose temperature, density and composition through [collisional-radiative models](physics.md#collisional-radiative-model) and ionization balance. Its plasma extends into the [solar wind](astrophysics.md#solar-wind); the [coronal approximation](physics.md#coronal-approximation) describes useful low-density equilibrium limits rather than every coronal condition.

#### Supergranulation

↑ **Parent:** [Sun](#sun)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergranulation)

A larger-scale horizontal cellular flow pattern on the [Sun](#sun)'s surface. Transport by these flows helps arrange small magnetic elements into a [solar magnetic network](#solar-magnetic-network) at cell boundaries. The observed pattern does not establish a unique thermal driving depth or an exact hierarchy of isolated steady eddies.

#### Solar granule

↑ **Parent:** [Sun](#sun)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_granule)

A solar granule is a photospheric [convection cell](fluid-mechanics.md#convection-cell), with relatively hot rising material in its interior and cooler descending material near its edges. The pattern formed by their bright interiors and darker edges is [solar granulation](#solar-granulation). Their evolving horizontal outflows can sweep weak [magnetic flux](electromagnetism.md#magnetic-flux) toward surrounding lanes.

##### Solar granulation

↑ **Parent:** [Solar granule](#solar-granule)

The evolving cellular intensity pattern on the [Sun](#sun)'s [photosphere](stellar-structure.md#photosphere), produced by [solar granules](#solar-granule). Its radiative appearance and flow pattern are related but are not identical observables. Near-surface horizontal flow can redistribute weak magnetic field into the surrounding [intergranular lanes](#intergranular-lane).

###### Intergranular lane

↑ **Parent:** [Solar granulation](#solar-granulation)

An intergranular lane is a region between [solar granules](#solar-granule), commonly associated with cooler material and descending flow. Horizontal convergence can concentrate approximately vertical [magnetic flux](electromagnetism.md#magnetic-flux) there. The field's geometry, diffusivity and feedback determine the actual concentration rather than the lane label alone.

#### Solar neutrino

↑ **Parent:** [Sun](#sun)

A solar neutrino is a [neutrino](standard-model.md#neutrino) produced by a [nuclear reaction](physics.md#nuclear-reaction) in the [Sun](#sun), principally through the [proton–proton chain](#proton-proton-chain) and [CNO cycle](#cno-cycle). Its weak interactions allow it to escape directly from the [stellar core](stellar-structure.md#stellar-core). Accounting for [neutrino oscillation](standard-model.md#neutrino-oscillation) allows measurements of the different detected flavors to test the nuclear reaction power and rates independently of escaping [photons](quantum-mechanics.md#photon).

#### Tachocline

↑ **Parent:** [Sun](#sun)

The solar tachocline is the rotational transition near the base of the convection zone, approximately at $0.7$ solar radii, between differential rotation above and more nearly uniform rotation in the radiative interior. Its shear can contribute to the [Omega effect](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#omega-effect), and stable stratification can help store strong [toroidal magnetic fields](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#toroidal-magnetic-field). It is a proposed site for important parts of the [solar dynamo](#solar-dynamo), rather than a demonstrated unique location for all field generation.

#### Solar magnetic field

↑ **Parent:** [Sun](#sun)

The solar magnetic field is generated and maintained by electrically conducting flows through [solar dynamo](#solar-dynamo) action. It includes a large-scale approximately dipolar component near activity minimum and strong, intermittent fields in active regions and [sunspots](#sunspot). Its organized polarity reversals define the magnetic [solar cycle](#solar-cycle). Magnetic stresses modify the generating flows, so a saturated dynamo requires nonlinear feedback as well as magnetic induction.

##### Solar magnetic network

↑ **Parent:** [Solar magnetic field](#solar-magnetic-field)

A spatially extended pattern of magnetic concentrations near boundaries of [supergranulation](#supergranulation). Surface transport and convergence redistribute magnetic elements into this network; flux emergence, cancellation and diffusion also continually modify it. It is distinct from the much smaller-scale field pattern in individual [intergranular lanes](#intergranular-lane) and from the organized strong fields of [sunspots](#sunspot).

##### Solar dynamo

↑ **Parent:** [Solar magnetic field](#solar-magnetic-field)

The solar dynamo is the induction and regeneration of the [solar magnetic field](#solar-magnetic-field) by conducting flows powered ultimately by solar convection and rotation. In a [mean-field dynamo](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#mean-field-dynamo), differential rotation supplies the [Omega effect](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#omega-effect) and helical correlations or the [Babcock-Leighton mechanism](#babcock-leighton-mechanism) regenerate a [poloidal magnetic field](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#poloidal-magnetic-field). The mean induction equation is

$$
\partial_t\overline{\mathbf B}=\nabla\times\left(\overline{\mathbf U}\times\overline{\mathbf B}+\boldsymbol{\mathcal E}-\eta\nabla\times\overline{\mathbf B}\right),
$$

where $\boldsymbol{\mathcal E}=\langle\mathbf u\times\mathbf b\rangle$ is the [mean-field electromotive force](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#mean-field-electromotive-force). A model must close this correlation and include feedback to predict a saturated magnetic cycle; prescribing a shear alone does not close the regeneration loop.

###### Stellar rotation-activity feedback

↑ **Parent:** [Solar dynamo](#solar-dynamo)

Rotation influences [convection](fluid-mechanics.md#convection), its helicity and [differential rotation](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#differential-rotation), which feed [dynamo action](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#dynamo-action). The generated [magnetic field](electromagnetism.md#magnetic-field) produces surface activity and strengthens the angular-momentum lever arm of a [stellar wind](#stellar-wind). Wind braking lowers the rotation rate and ordinarily reduces unsaturated activity. [Lorentz force](electromagnetism.md#lorentz-force) feedback also changes the differential rotation and convective motions, saturating the dynamo on shorter time scales. Secular evolution, cyclic polarity reversal and irregular modulation are distinct time scales of this coupled system.

###### Interface solar dynamo

↑ **Parent:** [Solar dynamo](#solar-dynamo)

An interface solar dynamo separates the region of strong differential rotation from the region of poloidal-field regeneration, often placing shear near the [tachocline](#tachocline) and an [alpha effect](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#alpha-effect) in the convection zone. Magnetic transport across the interface couples the two regions. The separation can provide toroidal-flux storage in a relatively stable layer, but growth, period and migration depend on the transport, diffusivity contrast, and signs of the regeneration and shear coefficients.

###### Equal-diffusivity interface dynamo

↑ **Parent:** [Interface solar dynamo](#interface-solar-dynamo)

An ideal planar [alpha-Omega dynamo](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#alpha-omega-dynamo) places poloidal regeneration above an interface and shear-generated toroidal induction below it. With equal constant [diffusivities](brownian-motion.md#diffusion-coefficient), decay away from the interface and ordinary electromagnetic matching, its surface-wave branch grows when $D>32$ for positive $k,\alpha,V$. The model separates the two regeneration regions while allowing diffusive coupling. The threshold refers to this model, not every solar interface dynamo or a full isotropic alpha closure.

###### Dispersion relation of an equal-diffusivity interface dynamo

↑ **Parent:** [Equal-diffusivity interface dynamo](#equal-diffusivity-interface-dynamo)

Let $p=s-i\omega$ and $q^2=k^2+p/\eta$, with $\operatorname{Re}q>0$. The upper poloidal amplitude is $(A_0+Cz)e^{-qz}$ and the lower toroidal amplitude is $(B_0+cz)e^{qz}$. Their forcing equations give $2\eta qC=\alpha B_0$ and $-2\eta qc=ikVA_0$; interface matching gives $C=2qA_0$ and $c=-2qB_0$. Eliminating amplitudes yields the [dispersion relation](wave-equation.md#dispersion-relation). Its potentially growing branch has $s=\eta k^2(\sqrt{D/32}-1)$ and $\omega=-\eta k^2\sqrt{D/32}$ with the convention $e^{ikx-i\omega t}$. The opposite branch decays.

###### Electromagnetic matching for an alpha-Omega interface

↑ **Parent:** [Equal-diffusivity interface dynamo](#equal-diffusivity-interface-dynamo)

For a planar interface, equal permeability and no singular sheet current make all magnetic components continuous. A nonzero horizontal Fourier wavenumber then gives the first three conditions in the title. [Continuity](calculus.md#continuous-function) of the tangential [electric field](electromagnetism.md#electric-field) supplies the last: continuous mean velocity cancels its advective contribution, and the retained alpha [mean-field electromotive force](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#mean-field-electromotive-force) is along the toroidal direction, so the other tangential electric component is $-U\partial_xA-\eta\partial_zB$. The given alpha-Omega equations retain only toroidal-to-poloidal alpha generation; adding a full isotropic alpha contribution would alter both the bulk equation and the matching problem.

###### Flux-transport solar dynamo

↑ **Parent:** [Solar dynamo](#solar-dynamo)

A flux-transport solar dynamo couples toroidal and poloidal field generation through meridional advection, turbulent magnetic diffusion and possibly turbulent pumping. A common model uses the surface [Babcock-Leighton mechanism](#babcock-leighton-mechanism), differential rotation near the [tachocline](#tachocline), poleward surface circulation and an equatorward deep return flow. If advection dominates, circulation strongly influences the cycle period and migration; if diffusion dominates, diffusive coupling is important instead. Agreement with a butterfly pattern depends on the assumed transport geometry and coefficients, not merely on including a circulation term.

###### Babcock-Leighton mechanism

↑ **Parent:** [Solar dynamo](#solar-dynamo)

The Babcock-Leighton mechanism regenerates large-scale poloidal solar flux through the emergence and dispersal of systematically tilted bipolar active regions. With [Joy's law](#joy-s-law) tilt, opposite polarities have different latitudes: cancellation near the equator and poleward transport of surviving following-polarity flux alter and reverse the polar field. Thus a toroidal field that produces rising bipolar regions feeds back into a [poloidal magnetic field](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#poloidal-magnetic-field). The source is intrinsically nonlocal between the interior flux reservoir and surface emergence and is stochastic because active-region fluxes and tilts vary.

##### Sunspot

↑ **Parent:** [Solar magnetic field](#solar-magnetic-field)

A sunspot is a relatively cool, dark photospheric region associated with concentrated magnetic flux. The strong [magnetic field](electromagnetism.md#magnetic-field) inhibits convective heat transport, reducing the emergent local intensity. Spots commonly emerge in bipolar active regions, whose polarity and systematic tilt follow [Hale's polarity law](#hale-s-polarity-law) and [Joy's law](#joy-s-law). Their number and latitude distribution are visible tracers of the [solar cycle](#solar-cycle).

<h6 id="joy-s-law">Joy's law</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Sunspot](#sunspot)

The axis of a bipolar solar active region is statistically tilted relative to the local east-west direction, with its following polarity typically farther from the equator and a mean tilt that grows with latitude. Scatter is substantial. The separation in latitude of the two polarities allows their surface dispersal to generate a net poloidal field through the [Babcock-Leighton mechanism](#babcock-leighton-mechanism).

##### Solar cycle

↑ **Parent:** [Solar magnetic field](#solar-magnetic-field)

The solar cycle is the recurrent variation of magnetic activity, with a characteristic sunspot-number period of about eleven years. The large-scale magnetic polarity reverses from one activity cycle to the next, giving an approximately twenty-two-year magnetic cycle. Activity cycles vary in length and amplitude. A satisfactory [solar dynamo](#solar-dynamo) model must account for polarity reversal, equatorward activity-belt migration in the [solar butterfly diagram](#solar-butterfly-diagram), the phase relation between polar and active-region fields, and long-term modulation including [solar grand minima](#solar-grand-minimum).

###### Solar grand minimum

↑ **Parent:** [Solar cycle](#solar-cycle)

A solar grand minimum is a prolonged interval, lasting several ordinary cycles, of strongly reduced sunspot activity. Historical records identify the [Maunder minimum](#maunder-minimum); cosmogenic carbon-14 and beryllium-10 provide longer indirect records because stronger heliospheric magnetic shielding generally reduces cosmic-ray production of these isotopes. Weak activity does not imply a zero [solar magnetic field](#solar-magnetic-field). Proposed causes include stochastic weakening of poloidal-field generation, nonlinear dynamo modulation, and intermittency near an excitation threshold.

###### Maunder minimum

↑ **Parent:** [Solar grand minimum](#solar-grand-minimum)

The Maunder minimum is the interval approximately 1645–1715 when recorded sunspot activity was exceptionally low. It is the best-known historical [solar grand minimum](#solar-grand-minimum). Historical coverage and cosmogenic-isotope records require care in inferring its precise magnetic amplitude; evidence for weak modulation need not imply that the normal spot-producing dynamo disappeared completely.

<h6 id="hale-s-polarity-law">Hale's polarity law</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Solar cycle](#solar-cycle)

Bipolar solar active regions preferentially have one leading magnetic polarity in a given hemisphere and cycle, the opposite leading polarity in the other hemisphere, and reversed assignments in the next cycle. These statistical polarity rules reveal the ordered toroidal component of the [solar magnetic field](#solar-magnetic-field). Individual active regions can violate the preferred pattern, so the law is a statistical regularity rather than an exact rule for every spot pair.

###### Solar butterfly diagram

↑ **Parent:** [Solar cycle](#solar-cycle)

A solar butterfly diagram plots sunspot occurrence or magnetic flux against latitude and time. New-cycle sunspot belts appear at middle latitudes and migrate toward the equator, producing successive wing-shaped patterns. Magnetic butterfly diagrams additionally show poleward flux transport and polar-field reversals. The diagram constrains both [Parker dynamo wave](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#parker-dynamo-wave) propagation and magnetic transport in [flux-transport solar dynamos](#flux-transport-solar-dynamo).

## Cepheid variable

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cepheid_variable)

A Cepheid variable is a pulsating star whose period is tightly related to its luminosity, allowing its distance to be inferred from its measured period and [apparent magnitude](astrophysics.md#apparent-magnitude).

### Cepheid period-luminosity relation

↑ **Parent:** [Cepheid variable](#cepheid-variable)

Calibrating the relation with stars of known distance allows an observed period and corrected [apparent magnitude](astrophysics.md#apparent-magnitude) to give a distance. Extinction and metallicity corrections matter for a precise [cosmic distance ladder](cosmology.md#cosmic-distance-ladder).

## Supernova

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernova)

A supernova is a luminous stellar explosion produced by thermonuclear disruption or the collapse of a stellar core.

<h3 id="type-1-5-supernova">Type 1.5 supernova</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Supernova](#supernova)

A hypothetical explosion in which a degenerate [carbon-oxygen core](#carbon-oxygen-core) within a single evolved star grows toward the [Chandrasekhar mass](#chandrasekhar-limit) and ignites explosive [carbon burning](#carbon-burning) while retaining an envelope. This differs from accretion onto an already exposed [white dwarf](#white-dwarf) in standard [Type Ia supernova](#type-ia-supernova) scenarios. [Evolutionary calculations by Doherty et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5431) explain the strong dependence of this proposed channel on envelope [mass loss](#mass-loss-astrophysics); their adopted winds remove the carbon-oxygen envelope before this outcome. It is a proposed possibility under different assumptions, rather than an established endpoint for normal $5M_\odot$ stars.

### Core-collapse supernova

↑ **Parent:** [Supernova](#supernova)

Collapse of a massive stellar core can launch an explosion that ejects material processed by hydrostatic and explosive burning. Whether an explosion succeeds, and how much material escapes instead of falling back, depend on the core and envelope structure. A [failed supernova](#failed-supernova) can retain much of the nucleosynthetic core; initial stellar mass alone is not a complete prediction.

#### Failed supernova

↑ **Parent:** [Core-collapse supernova](#core-collapse-supernova)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Failed_supernova)

Core collapse can fail to produce an ordinary successful ejecting [supernova](#supernova), leaving a [black hole](general-relativity.md#black-hole) and retaining much of the processed core. Some envelope material can still escape, depending on the event; failure is not equivalent to a universal zero-yield prescription.

### Pair-instability supernova

↑ **Parent:** [Supernova](#supernova)

In a hot radiation-supported stellar core, [electron-positron thermal pair abundance](statistical-physics.md#electron-positron-thermal-pair-abundance) can grow as radiation energy is converted into pair rest energy. The [stellar adiabatic exponent](stellar-structure.md#stellar-adiabatic-exponent) can fall below $4/3$, permitting contraction and rapid heating. If the resulting explosive [nuclear fusion](physics.md#nuclear-fusion) releases enough energy, it disrupts the star in a pair-instability supernova. Weaker events can produce pulses rather than full disruption, and still heavier cores can collapse without an explosion. Thus the thermal relativity scale alone neither establishes the instability nor predicts its outcome.

### Type Ia supernova

↑ **Parent:** [Supernova](#supernova)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_Ia_supernova)

A Type Ia supernova is a thermonuclear supernova whose standardized luminosity makes it a useful astronomical distance indicator.

#### Radioactively powered Type Ia supernova light curve

↑ **Parent:** [Type Ia supernova](#type-ia-supernova)

The nickel-56 to cobalt-56 to iron-56 decay chain supplies gamma rays and charged particles that heat supernova ejecta. Early expansion and [photon](quantum-mechanics.md#photon) diffusion delay the escaping optical light; after a peak on a scale of weeks, the [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity) falls as decay power declines and gamma rays escape more readily. With comparable diffusion times and deposition efficiencies, peak [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity) scales approximately with the nickel-56 [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass). Using one fixed absolute [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity) without accounting for composition-dependent yields can therefore bias standard-candle distances.

#### Metallicity dependence of nickel-56 yield

↑ **Parent:** [Type Ia supernova](#type-ia-supernova)

Approximate a fixed [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) of iron-group freeze-out ashes by nickel-56 and iron-54, with no further weak neutronization and negligible residual light particles. Their electron fractions are $1/2$ and $26/54$. Conservation of the initial [electron fraction in stellar matter](physics.md#electron-fraction-in-stellar-matter), $1/2-X_{22}/22$, then gives the displayed [nickel](chemistry.md#nickel) [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) fraction. Its argument is the initial neon-22 [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) fraction, which need not equal the earlier [nitrogen](chemistry.md#nitrogen) metallicity. The two-isotope mass-budget approximation is not an exact full nuclear equilibrium with all free-particle densities set to zero.

## Brown dwarf

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_dwarf)

A brown dwarf is a self-gravitating object too light to sustain ordinary hydrogen fusion. Electron degeneracy limits its contraction, leaving radii near that of Jupiter across a broad mass range.

### CoRoT-3b

↑ **Parent:** [Brown dwarf](#brown-dwarf)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoRoT-3b)

### Deuterium-burning mass

↑ **Parent:** [Brown dwarf](#brown-dwarf)

The conventional deuterium-burning boundary is near $13$ Jupiter masses, with composition-dependent variation. It is a useful classification scale rather than a sharp formation-based distinction between planets and brown dwarfs.

### Hydrogen-burning minimum mass

↑ **Parent:** [Brown dwarf](#brown-dwarf)

The hydrogen-burning minimum mass is the lowest mass that can sustain core hydrogen fusion, about $0.075$--$0.08$ solar masses for near-solar composition. Less massive brown dwarfs cool continually after any deuterium burning ends.

#### Contraction-limited central temperature

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen-burning minimum mass](#hydrogen-burning-minimum-mass)

A fixed [stellar homology](stellar-structure.md#stellar-homology) profile with $P=\rho\mathcal RT/\mu_i+K_{\rm nr}\rho^{5/3}$ gives

$$
T_c=\frac{\mu_i}{\mathcal R}\left(AGM/R-BK_{\rm nr}M^{2/3}/R^2\right),
$$

where $A,B>0$. As a function of $1/R$ this is a concave quadratic; its maximum is $A^2G^2\mu_iM^{4/3}/(4BK_{\rm nr}\mathcal R)$, at $R=2BK_{\rm nr}M^{-1/3}/(AG)$. At the maximum, the central [electron degeneracy pressure](statistical-physics.md#electron-degeneracy-pressure) and thermal pressure are equal. If this temperature is insufficient for sustained [hydrogen burning](#hydrogen-burning), the object becomes a [brown dwarf](#brown-dwarf). Only the positive-temperature segment represents a physical sequence.

##### Mass-averaged temperature maximum in a partially degenerate polytrope

↑ **Parent:** [Contraction-limited central temperature](#contraction-limited-central-temperature)

For a homologous [stellar polytrope](stellar-structure.md#stellar-polytrope) of index $3/2$, take $P=K\rho^{5/3}+\mathcal R\rho T/\mu$ and zero surface pressure. The [stellar virial theorem](stellar-structure.md#stellar-virial-theorem), with $\Omega=-6GM^2/(7R)$, gives

$$
\overline T_M=\frac{2\mu GM}{7\mathcal RR}-\frac{\mu A M^{2/3}}{3\mathcal RR^2},\qquad A=3K\int_0^1\left(\frac{3\rho}{4\pi\bar\rho}\right)^{2/3}dq.
$$

The normalized density profile fixes $A$ independently of mass and radius at fixed composition. Writing $\overline T_M=\alpha/R-\delta/R^2$ shows that its maximum is $\alpha^2/(4\delta)=3\mu G^2M^{4/3}/(49\mathcal RA)$, at $R=7A M^{-1/3}/(3G)$. Below this radius contraction increases the fraction of support provided by [electron degeneracy pressure](statistical-physics.md#electron-degeneracy-pressure) and lowers the thermal temperature. A fixed normalized profile also fixes the ratio of central to mass-averaged temperature, giving a [hydrogen-burning minimum mass](#hydrogen-burning-minimum-mass).

## Star formation

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_formation)

Star formation is the gravitational collapse and fragmentation of cold interstellar gas into protostars and eventually stars. Cooling, turbulence, magnetic fields, rotation, and feedback determine its efficiency and characteristic masses.

### Protostar

↑ **Parent:** [Star formation](#star-formation)

A protostar is a forming [star](#star) whose energy comes chiefly from gravitational contraction and [accretion](astrophysics.md#accretion), before sustained core [hydrogen burning](#hydrogen-burning) supplies its [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity). A fixed-mass contraction model approximates the stage after most accretion has ended; an actively accreting protostar need not have fixed [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) or the same [Hayashi track](#hayashi-track).

### Gravitational fragmentation

↑ **Parent:** [Star formation](#star-formation)

[Gravitational fragmentation](#gravitational-fragmentation) divides a self-gravitating gas distribution into condensations that can undergo separate collapse. Pressure support, cooling and rotation influence the scales and outcome; it is not equivalent to an instantaneous equal-mass fission of a rigidly rotating sphere. [Isothermal fragmentation](#isothermal-fragmentation) is a useful limiting case in which thermal adjustment keeps the gas temperature approximately fixed.

<h3 id="kennicutt-schmidt-law">Kennicutt–Schmidt law</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Star formation](#star-formation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennicutt–Schmidt_law)

The Kennicutt–Schmidt law relates star-formation-rate surface density to gas surface density, commonly as $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}=A\Sigma_g^N$. Disk-averaged total-gas measurements give $N$ near $1.4$, while resolved molecular-gas relations are often closer to linear.

#### Gas-depletion time

↑ **Parent:** [Kennicutt–Schmidt law](#kennicutt-schmidt-law)

The gas-depletion time is the time required to consume a gas reservoir at its current star-formation rate, $t_{\rm dep}=M_g/\dot M_*$. It ignores replenishment, returned stellar mass, and changes in the rate.

##### Star-formation efficiency per free-fall time

↑ **Parent:** [Gas-depletion time](#gas-depletion-time)

The star-formation efficiency per free-fall time is the fraction of gas converted into stars during one gravitational free-fall time, $\epsilon_{\rm ff}=t_{\rm ff}/t_{\rm dep}$. Molecular clouds commonly have values of order one per cent.

### Jeans mass

↑ **Parent:** [Star formation](#star-formation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeans_mass)

The Jeans mass is the characteristic gas mass above which self-gravity overcomes pressure support. Up to the convention used to define the collapsing region,

$$
M_J\propto\frac{c_s^3}{G^{3/2}\rho^{1/2}}
\propto T^{3/2}\rho^{-1/2}
$$

for an ideal gas of fixed composition.

#### Isothermal fragmentation

↑ **Parent:** [Jeans mass](#jeans-mass)

During isothermal collapse, $T$ is constant and the [Jeans mass](#jeans-mass) decreases as $\rho^{-1/2}$. Successively denser regions can therefore become independently unstable and fragment to smaller masses.

#### Adiabatic suppression of fragmentation

↑ **Parent:** [Jeans mass](#jeans-mass)

For adiabatic monatomic gas, $T\propto\rho^{2/3}$ and the [Jeans mass](#jeans-mass) increases as $\rho^{1/2}$. Once cooling fails and collapse becomes adiabatic, further fragmentation is suppressed.

### Initial mass function

↑ **Parent:** [Star formation](#star-formation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_mass_function)

The initial mass function is the distribution of stellar birth masses. It records the mass scales selected by cloud fragmentation, accretion, feedback, and stellar multiplicity.

#### Power-law initial-mass-function tail coordinate

## Stellar nuclear fusion

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_nuclear_fusion)

Stellar nuclear fusion converts light nuclei into more tightly bound nuclei and supplies most main-sequence stellar luminosity.

### Thermonuclear runaway

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

Temperature-sensitive [nuclear fusion](physics.md#nuclear-fusion) can heat material faster than cooling and expansion can regulate it, accelerating the burning. In a [classical nova](#classical-nova), [hydrogen](chemistry.md#hydrogen) accumulated on a [white dwarf](#white-dwarf) ignites; initial [electron degeneracy pressure](statistical-physics.md#electron-degeneracy-pressure) helps suppress the usual expansion response. The eruption can eject the envelope without destroying the underlying [white dwarf](#white-dwarf).

### Thermonuclear reaction rate

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

A thermal distribution of relative velocities weights a nuclear cross-section to determine the reaction rate per volume. Identical reactants require the denominator two to avoid counting each pair twice. Specific deposited power is $r_{12}Q_{\rm deposited}/\rho$. Charged-particle [quantum tunnelling](quantum-mechanics.md#quantum-tunnelling) through a [Coulomb barrier](physics.md#coulomb-barrier) produces a [Gamow peak](#gamow-peak), while resonances and plasma screening can alter the rate.

#### Local temperature exponent of a thermonuclear reaction

↑ **Parent:** [Thermonuclear reaction rate](#thermonuclear-reaction-rate)

If a charged-particle rate coefficient has $\lambda\propto\eta^2e^{-\eta}$ with $\eta\propto T^{-1/3}$, its logarithmic [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature) derivative is $(\eta-2)/3$. This is a local slope evaluated at fixed composition. It increases as [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature) falls, so extrapolating a single power law across a large [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature) range can be inaccurate.

#### Gamow peak

↑ **Parent:** [Thermonuclear reaction rate](#thermonuclear-reaction-rate)

For a nonresonant charged-particle [thermonuclear reaction rate](#thermonuclear-reaction-rate), the thermal high-energy tail favors low $E$ while the [Coulomb barrier](physics.md#coulomb-barrier) penetration factor favors high $E$. Their product peaks in an intermediate energy window. Resonant reactions such as the [Triple-alpha process](#triple-alpha-process) require an additional resonance treatment.

##### Gaussian approximation to a nonresonant thermonuclear rate

↑ **Parent:** [Gamow peak](#gamow-peak)

For a slowly varying nuclear factor and penetration factor $\exp[-2\sqrt{E_B/E}]$, the thermal integral is $\int_0^\infty e^{-\Phi(E)}dE$, where $\Phi(E)=E/(k_BT)+2\sqrt{E_B/E}$. The [Gamow peak](#gamow-peak) occurs at $E_0=[E_B(k_BT)^2]^{1/3}$, with $\Phi(E_0)=3E_0/(k_BT)$ and $\Phi''(E_0)=3/(2k_BTE_0)$. The [Gaussian integral](calculus.md#gaussian-integral) gives $\sqrt{4\pi k_BTE_0/3}e^{-3E_0/(k_BT)}$. Combined with the Maxwellian prefactor $(k_BT)^{-3/2}$ this produces the displayed [thermonuclear reaction rate](#thermonuclear-reaction-rate) at fixed reactant density. The approximation assumes $E_B\gg k_BT$ and excludes strong resonances or rapidly varying nuclear factors.

### Silicon burning

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

At very high temperatures, [photodisintegration](physics.md#nuclear-photodisintegration) and proton, neutron and alpha captures rearrange silicon-group material through networks approaching quasi-statistical equilibrium. The result is an iron-group mixture determined by electron fraction and weak-reaction timescales. Direct silicon-plus-silicon fusion is not the usual mechanism. The approach to maximal [nuclear binding energy](physics.md#nuclear-binding-energy) per nucleon ends sustained hydrostatic fusion power.

### Oxygen burning

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

Oxygen fusion in sufficiently hot evolved stellar cores has several reaction channels, including the displayed silicon-plus-alpha channel and channels producing phosphorus and protons. Its ashes feed [silicon burning](#silicon-burning), while [stellar neutrino energy loss](stellar-structure.md#stellar-neutrino-energy-loss) strongly affects the evolution timescale.

### Neon burning

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

High-temperature [photodisintegration](physics.md#nuclear-photodisintegration) of neon-20 releases an alpha particle and oxygen-16. Capture of that alpha particle by another neon-20 nucleus produces magnesium-24. The breakup consumes energy, but the combined reactions can release net energy. Other captures modify the ashes in a real network.

### Helium-burning shell

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

A helium-burning shell converts [helium](chemistry.md#helium) into [carbon](chemistry.md#carbon) and [oxygen](chemistry.md#oxygen) outside a helium-exhausted stellar core. Thin-shell burning on the [Asymptotic giant branch](#asymptotic-giant-branch) can develop a [thermal pulse of an asymptotic-giant-branch star](#thermal-pulse-of-an-asymptotic-giant-branch-star).

### Helium mass fraction

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

The helium mass fraction is the fraction of a material's mass carried by [helium](chemistry.md#helium) nuclei. [Hydrogen burning](#hydrogen-burning) raises it, whereas [core helium burning](#core-helium-burning) lowers it while producing [carbon](chemistry.md#carbon) and [oxygen](chemistry.md#oxygen).

### Stellar energy-generation rate

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

The stellar energy-generation rate $\epsilon$ is power produced per unit mass. Spherical energy conservation is $dL_r/dr=4\pi r^2\rho\epsilon$.

### Hydrogen mass fraction

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

The hydrogen mass fraction is the fraction $X$ of a stellar material's mass carried by hydrogen nuclei.

### Hydrogen burning

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

Hydrogen burning converts hydrogen into helium through the [proton–proton chain](#proton-proton-chain) or [CNO cycle](#cno-cycle).

#### Hydrogen-burning shell

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen burning](#hydrogen-burning)

A hydrogen-burning shell converts [hydrogen](chemistry.md#hydrogen) into [helium](chemistry.md#helium) in a layer surrounding a hydrogen-exhausted stellar core. It moves outward in [enclosed mass](stellar-structure.md#enclosed-mass) as it supplies fresh helium to that core; its movement in radius need not have the same sign.

### Triple-alpha process

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple-alpha_process)

The triple-alpha process fuses three helium-4 nuclei into carbon-12 through an unstable beryllium-8 intermediate. Its steep temperature dependence can make helium-shell burning thermally unstable.

#### Hoyle state

↑ **Parent:** [Triple-alpha process](#triple-alpha-process)

This excited carbon-12 resonance lies close to the three-alpha threshold and enables the [Triple-alpha process](#triple-alpha-process) to make carbon efficiently. A transient beryllium-8 nucleus captures an alpha particle into the resonance; electromagnetic decay then stabilizes carbon-12. A small radiative branching probability still matters because the resonance enhances formation.

### CNO mass fraction

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

The CNO mass fraction is the combined mass fraction of carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen nuclei available to catalyse the CNO cycle.

<h3 id="proton-proton-chain">Proton–proton chain</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton–proton_chain)

The proton–proton chain converts hydrogen to helium and dominates hydrogen burning in stars near and below the Sun's mass. Near $2\times10^7\,\mathrm K$, its specific energy generation is roughly proportional to $\rho T^4$.

#### Proton-proton chain branches

↑ **Parent:** [Proton–proton chain](#proton-proton-chain)

The [proton–proton chain](#proton-proton-chain) first produces deuterium by a weak reaction and then helium-3 by proton capture. pp I ends with ${}^3\mathrm{He}+{}^3\mathrm{He}\to{}^4\mathrm{He}+2p$. pp II passes through beryllium-7 [electron capture](physics.md#electron-capture) to lithium-7 and then proton capture. pp III passes through boron-8 [beta-plus decay](physics.md#beta-plus-decay) and beryllium-8 breakup. Each branch produces one net helium-4 nucleus from four protons, but the [neutrino](standard-model.md#neutrino) spectrum and deposited heat differ. In pp II the net weak reactions involve one emitted positron and one captured electron.

##### Solar hydrogen and helium-3 abundance profiles

↑ **Parent:** [Proton-proton chain branches](#proton-proton-chain-branches)

Present solar [hydrogen](chemistry.md#hydrogen) is most depleted in the central burning region and rises toward the envelope. [helium-3](chemistry.md#helium-3) is efficiently destroyed in the hot centre, increases outward as its equilibrium abundance rises, and develops an off-centre maximum where equilibration becomes slow. Still farther out its production becomes weak, leaving a smaller envelope abundance. Convective mixing tends to flatten the outer profiles. This qualitative pattern is not itself a quantitative solar model.

##### Effective proton-proton reaction network

↑ **Parent:** [Proton-proton chain branches](#proton-proton-chain-branches)

An effective [Proton–proton chain](#proton-proton-chain) network can track [protons](physics.md#proton), [deuterium](chemistry.md#deuterium), [helium-3](chemistry.md#helium-3), [helium-4](chemistry.md#helium-4) and lithium-7 after eliminating a rapidly adjusting beryllium intermediate. Identical-reactant event rates include a factor one half. Stoichiometric changes must count both [helium-4](chemistry.md#helium-4) products of lithium-7 [proton](physics.md#proton) capture. The weighted baryon [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) $n_1+2n_2+3n_3+4n_4+7n_7$ is conserved by the nuclear reactions at fixed volume.

###### Helium-3 equilibrium abundance

↑ **Parent:** [Effective proton-proton reaction network](#effective-proton-proton-reaction-network)

With $A=\lambda_{33}$, $B=\lambda_{34}n_4$ and $C=\lambda_{11}n_1^2/2$, [helium-3](chemistry.md#helium-3) evolves as $\dot n_3=C-An_3^2-Bn_3$ after [deuterium](chemistry.md#deuterium) elimination. The positive root is its unique attracting equilibrium for a fixed background. In the pp-I limit it reduces to $n_1\sqrt{\lambda_{11}/(2\lambda_{33})}$, making equilibrium abundance strongly [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature) dependent.

###### Helium-3 relaxation time

↑ **Parent:** [Helium-3 equilibrium abundance](#helium-3-equilibrium-abundance)

A small abundance displacement from the [helium-3 equilibrium abundance](#helium-3-equilibrium-abundance) obeys $\dot x=-x/\tau$ to linear order. Rapid relaxation relative to background evolution allows the abundance to track its equilibrium. If the equilibrium drifts, the displacement equation also contains $-\dot n_{3e}$. In the pp-I limit the rate is $n_1\sqrt{2\lambda_{11}\lambda_{33}}$, so both production and destruction [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature) dependences matter.

###### Temperature for helium-3 freeze-out

↑ **Parent:** [Helium-3 relaxation time](#helium-3-relaxation-time)

Equilibrium fails where the [helium-3 relaxation time](#helium-3-relaxation-time) becomes comparable to stellar age. In a fixed-density pp-I scaling with reaction exponents four and sixteen, the equilibrium abundance scales as $T^{-6}$ and the relaxation time as $T^{-10}$. Central normalization then places the transition near six million kelvin; using the full supplied exponential factors gives a somewhat higher estimate. A precise radius or [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature) requires a stellar [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density)/composition profile and the second branch contribution.

###### Helium-3 freeze-out estimate with Gamow factors

↑ **Parent:** [Temperature for helium-3 freeze-out](#temperature-for-helium-3-freeze-out)

In the pp-I limit, [helium-3 relaxation time](#helium-3-relaxation-time) is $\tau^{-1}=n_1\sqrt{2\lambda_{11}\lambda_{33}}$. At fixed proton [number density](statistical-physics.md#number-density), each [thermonuclear reaction rate](#thermonuclear-reaction-rate) coefficient obeys $\lambda(T)/\lambda(T_0)=(T/T_0)^{-2/3}\exp[-\eta(T_0)((T_0/T)^{1/3}-1)]$. Taking their geometric mean gives the displayed relaxation ratio with $\bar\eta=[\eta_{11}(T_0)+\eta_{33}(T_0)]/2$. A local power-law approximation instead gives $\tau\propto T^{-(\alpha+\beta)/2}$. These are estimates rather than a complete radial model: the proton [number density](statistical-physics.md#number-density), composition and pp-II branch change along a real [stellar composition profile](stellar-structure.md#stellar-composition-profile).

###### Deuterium quasi-equilibrium in proton-proton burning

↑ **Parent:** [Effective proton-proton reaction network](#effective-proton-proton-reaction-network)

[deuterium](chemistry.md#deuterium) [proton](physics.md#proton) capture is much faster than the weak reaction that produces [deuterium](chemistry.md#deuterium). Its abundance therefore adjusts so that $\lambda_{21}n_2n_1\simeq\lambda_{11}n_1^2/2$. Substituting this balance eliminates the fast intermediate from the slower [proton](physics.md#proton) and [helium-3](chemistry.md#helium-3) evolution equations. This is a [quasi-steady approximation](mathematics.md#quasi-steady-approximation), not an assumption that [hydrogen](chemistry.md#hydrogen) itself is in nuclear equilibrium.

### CNO cycle

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNO_cycle)

The CNO cycle catalyses hydrogen fusion through carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen nuclei. Near $2\times10^7\,\mathrm K$, its specific energy generation is approximately proportional to $\rho T^{17}$ and is therefore strongly concentrated near a star's centre.

#### CNO crossover temperature

↑ **Parent:** [CNO cycle](#cno-cycle)

If [proton–proton chain](#proton-proton-chain) heating scales as $X_H^2\rho T^{\nu_{pp}}$ and [CNO cycle](#cno-cycle) heating as $X_HZ_{\mathrm{CNO}}\rho T^{\nu_{\mathrm{CNO}}}$, their ratio at fixed hydrogen abundance scales as $Z_{\mathrm{CNO}}T^{\nu_{\mathrm{CNO}}-\nu_{pp}}$. Equating the rates proves the displayed crossover-temperature relation. It applies to a local power-law approximation, with unchanged coefficient normalizations apart from the catalyst abundance.

#### CNO-II cycle

↑ **Parent:** [CNO cycle](#cno-cycle)

A branch of the [CNO cycle](#cno-cycle) proceeds through proton captures on nitrogen-15 and oxygen-16, [beta-plus decay](physics.md#beta-plus-decay) of fluorine-17, and ${}^{17}\mathrm O(p,\alpha){}^{14}\mathrm N$. Returning through oxygen-15 and nitrogen-15 closes the cycle. It also catalyzes the conversion of four protons into helium-4, with branch-dependent [neutrino](standard-model.md#neutrino) loss.

#### CNO-I cycle

↑ **Parent:** [CNO cycle](#cno-cycle)

The principal [CNO cycle](#cno-cycle) follows ${}^{12}\mathrm C(p,\gamma){}^{13}\mathrm N$, [beta-plus decay](physics.md#beta-plus-decay) to carbon-13, proton capture to nitrogen-14, proton capture to oxygen-15, [beta-plus decay](physics.md#beta-plus-decay) to nitrogen-15, and ${}^{15}\mathrm N(p,\alpha){}^{12}\mathrm C$. The carbon seed is regenerated. The ordinary bottleneck is ${}^{14}\mathrm N(p,\gamma){}^{15}\mathrm O$, so nitrogen-14 accumulates in cycle equilibrium.

### Stellar thermostat

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

The stellar thermostat is the negative feedback between temperature-sensitive nuclear burning and hydrostatic expansion: excess burning expands and cools a star, while deficient burning permits contraction and heating.

### Core helium burning

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)

Core helium burning begins when a hydrogen-exhausted stellar core becomes hot enough for the [Triple-alpha process](#triple-alpha-process). Subsequent alpha capture on carbon produces oxygen, leaving a carbon-oxygen core after central helium exhaustion.

#### Helium flash

↑ **Parent:** [Core helium burning](#core-helium-burning)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium_flash)

A helium flash is a thermally unstable ignition of [helium](chemistry.md#helium) in an electron-degenerate stellar core. A nondegenerate intermediate-mass core can expand and regulate ignition, so a usual isolated star of five [solar masses](#solar-mass) begins [core helium burning](#core-helium-burning) without this flash.

### Carbon burning

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_burning)

Carbon burning fuses carbon nuclei at temperatures of order $10^9\,\mathrm K$. In intermediate-mass stars it can ignite off-centre under partial electron degeneracy and propagate inward as a burning flame.

### s-process

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/s-process)

The slow neutron-capture process builds nuclei heavier than iron when neutron captures occur more slowly than typical beta decays. Thermal pulses in asymptotic-giant-branch stars produce characteristic enhancements of elements such as strontium, zirconium, and barium.

## Main sequence

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_sequence)

A main-sequence star is in a long-lived phase of core hydrogen burning.

### Main-sequence turnoff

↑ **Parent:** [Main sequence](#main-sequence)

The main-sequence turnoff is the location in a coeval population's [Hertzsprung-Russell diagram](#hertzsprung-russell-diagram) where stars are leaving the [main sequence](#main-sequence) after exhausting their core hydrogen. More massive stars generally have larger [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity) per unit [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) and shorter nuclear lifetimes, so the most massive stars still on the main sequence constrain the population's age. In a simple model, equating the [stellar nuclear timescale](#stellar-nuclear-timescale) $t\sim qM/L(M)$ to the common age determines a turnoff mass; detailed calibration requires composition and stellar models.

### Terminal-age main sequence

↑ **Parent:** [Main sequence](#main-sequence)

The terminal-age main sequence denotes the end of central [hydrogen burning](#hydrogen-burning). It does not mark complete exhaustion of hydrogen throughout the star; a [hydrogen-burning shell](#hydrogen-burning-shell) can subsequently supply its luminosity.

### Lower main sequence

↑ **Parent:** [Main sequence](#main-sequence)

The lower main sequence consists of relatively cool, low-mass hydrogen-burning stars whose luminosity is supplied mainly by the proton–proton chain.

#### Red dwarf

↑ **Parent:** [Lower main sequence](#lower-main-sequence)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_dwarf)

A red dwarf is a cool low-mass main-sequence star. The lowest-mass red dwarfs are fully convective.

### Zero-age main sequence

↑ **Parent:** [Main sequence](#main-sequence)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-age_main_sequence)

The zero-age main sequence is the locus where stars begin stable core hydrogen burning with nearly their initial composition.

## Hertzsprung-Russell diagram

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertzsprung-Russell_diagram)

A Hertzsprung-Russell diagram plots stellar luminosity against effective temperature or spectral type. By convention, temperature usually decreases from left to right.

### Instability strip

↑ **Parent:** [Hertzsprung-Russell diagram](#hertzsprung-russell-diagram)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instability_strip)

#### Cepheid instability strip

↑ **Parent:** [Instability strip](#instability-strip)

The Cepheid instability strip is the region of the [Hertzsprung-Russell diagram](#hertzsprung-russell-diagram) where [Cepheid variables](#cepheid-variable) are unstable to pulsation driven by the [opacity mechanism](#kappa-mechanism).

## Stellar evolution

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_evolution)

Stellar evolution is the change of a star's structure and composition as nuclear reactions, energy transport, mass loss, and interactions proceed.

### Post-asymptotic giant branch evolution

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)

After substantial envelope loss on the [Asymptotic giant branch](#asymptotic-giant-branch), a low- or intermediate-mass star's exposed core heats while its radius shrinks, often at approximately constant [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity) during continued shell burning. It may ionize the expelled envelope as a [planetary nebula](#planetary-nebula). After the remaining nuclear fuel is depleted, its luminosity decreases and it joins a [white dwarf](#white-dwarf) cooling track. Evolutionary tracks on the [Hertzsprung-Russell diagram](#hertzsprung-russell-diagram) connect the central-star temperature and luminosity with core mass and elapsed time.

### Stellar nuclear timescale

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)

The time for [stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion) to consume the accessible fuel at characteristic [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity) $L$. If a fraction $f$ of a star's [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) $M$ supplies fuel abundance $X$ and released [energy](classical-mechanics.md#energy) per unit fuel [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) $E_H$, [hydrogen burning](#hydrogen-burning) gives $t_{\rm nuc}\sim fXME_H/L$. Accessible core fuel, rather than the entire star, sets the lifetime unless mixing is complete. This is longer than a structural [Kelvin-Helmholtz cooling time](#kelvin-helmholtz-cooling-time) when the nuclear reservoir greatly exceeds the gravitational contraction reservoir.

### Stellar remnant

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)

A [stellar remnant](#stellar-remnant) is the object left after a [star](#star) finishes its principal nuclear-burning evolution, such as a [white dwarf](#white-dwarf), [neutron star](#neutron-star) or [black hole](general-relativity.md#black-hole). [Stellar remnants](#stellar-remnant) retain mass while often contributing little optical light, affecting an old [stellar population](#stellar-population)'s [mass-to-light ratio](galaxy.md#mass-to-light-ratio).

### Stellar age

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)

The stellar age is the elapsed time since a [star](#star) formed. A constant number formation rate on a finite interval gives a [uniform distribution](continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution) of present ages if every formed object remains counted, including stellar remnants. An age distribution inferred only from surviving [main sequence](#main-sequence) stars generally differs because their lifetimes depend on [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass).

### Planetary nebula

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_nebula)

#### Zanstra method

↑ **Parent:** [Planetary nebula](#planetary-nebula)

The Zanstra method estimates a central star's effective temperature by comparing its observed nonionizing continuum with the absorbed ionizing-photon rate inferred from nebular recombination lines. If $\Phi_{E_0}(T)=\int_{E_0/h}^\infty B_\nu(T)/(h\nu)d\nu$, then $[F_\ell/(h\nu_\ell F_{\nu_o,*})][\beta/\alpha_\ell^{\rm eff}]=\Phi_{E_0}(T_s)/B_{\nu_o}(T_s)$ when the line traces the full appropriate ionizing budget. Both the distance and stellar radius cancel. Density inhomogeneity also cancels from the integrated recombination estimate when the coefficient ratio is known; photon leakage, dust and mixed-species transfer can invalidate the simple budget.

### Carbon-oxygen core

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)

A carbon-oxygen core is the product of [core helium burning](#core-helium-burning). After central helium exhaustion it has little [hydrogen](chemistry.md#hydrogen) or [helium](chemistry.md#helium), while helium-rich and hydrogen-rich layers surround it.

### Blue loop

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_loop)

A blue loop is an excursion to higher [effective temperature](stellar-structure.md#effective-temperature), followed by a return redward, during [core helium burning](#core-helium-burning) in some evolved stars. Its extent depends on core and envelope structure, composition gradients and mixing. Only loops reaching the [Cepheid instability strip](#cepheid-instability-strip) produce the associated additional [Cepheid variable](#cepheid-variable) crossings.

### Hertzsprung gap

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertzsprung_gap)

The Hertzsprung gap is a sparsely populated region of the [Hertzsprung-Russell diagram](#hertzsprung-russell-diagram) traversed rapidly by many stars evolving away from the [main sequence](#main-sequence) toward the [red giant](#red-giant) region.

### Kelvin-Helmholtz mechanism

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin–Helmholtz_mechanism)

Kelvin-Helmholtz contraction releases gravitational binding energy as a self-gravitating body cools and shrinks. It powers young stars and giant planets before nuclear burning or degeneracy dominates their thermal evolution.

#### Gravitational energy generation in a homologously contracting ideal-gas star

↑ **Parent:** [Kelvin-Helmholtz mechanism](#kelvin-helmholtz-mechanism)

For a [monatomic gas](thermodynamics.md#monatomic-gas) obeying the [ideal gas](thermodynamics.md#ideal-gas) law, the [specific internal energy](thermodynamics.md#specific-internal-energy) is $e=3P/(2\rho)$. With no [stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion) heating, the [first law of thermodynamics](thermodynamics.md#first-law-of-thermodynamics) makes the effective [stellar energy-generation rate](#stellar-energy-generation-rate) $\epsilon=-\partial_te-P\partial_t(1/\rho)$, with the [derivatives](calculus.md#derivative) taken at fixed enclosed [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass). Differentiating gives $\epsilon=-3\partial_tP/(2\rho)+5P\partial_t\rho/(2\rho^2)$. In a fixed-mass [stellar homology](stellar-structure.md#stellar-homology) [Kelvin-Helmholtz contraction](#kelvin-helmholtz-mechanism), $\rho\propto R^{-3}$ and $P\propto R^{-4}$ at each [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) label. Hence $\epsilon=-3P\dot R/(2\rho R)>0$ when $\dot R<0$. This includes [internal energy](thermodynamics.md#internal-energy) storage as well as [pressure](thermodynamics.md#pressure) work; using the gravitational potential-energy decrease alone would overestimate the outgoing [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity).

#### Intrinsic planetary luminosity

↑ **Parent:** [Kelvin-Helmholtz mechanism](#kelvin-helmholtz-mechanism)

The intrinsic planetary luminosity $L_{\rm int}$ is the power supplied by interior cooling, contraction, nuclear reactions if present, or internal heating, excluding reflected or passively reradiated stellar light. For fixed [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) and no accretion or nuclear source, global [conservation of energy](physics.md#conservation-of-energy) gives $L_{\rm int}=Q_{\rm deep}-d(U+\Omega)/dt$, where $Q_{\rm deep}$ is any additional power deposited in the interior, such as [tidal heating](planetary-science.md#tidal-heating) or [Joule heating](electromagnetism.md#joule-heating). For $Q_{\rm deep}=0$ and a gas-supported quasi-static sphere of fixed [specific-heat ratio](thermodynamics.md#heat-capacity-ratio) $\gamma$ and negligible surface [pressure](thermodynamics.md#pressure), the [stellar virial theorem](stellar-structure.md#stellar-virial-theorem) gives $\Omega=-3(\gamma-1)U$, hence

$$
L_{\rm int}=-\frac{3\gamma-4}{3(\gamma-1)}\frac{d\Omega}{dt}.
$$

For a dynamically stable gas-supported model take $\gamma>4/3$. For $\gamma=5/3$ this is half the gravitational release. Degenerate giants require a different internal energy reservoir; the half-factor is not universal.

##### Entropy loss of an isolated convective planet

↑ **Parent:** [Intrinsic planetary luminosity](#intrinsic-planetary-luminosity)

With negligible nuclear or external heating, the [stellar structure equations](stellar-structure.md#stellar-structure-equations) give $L_{\rm int}=-\int_0^M T\,\partial s/\partial t\,dm$. A nearly isentropic convective interior therefore has $\dot s=-L_{\rm int}/\int T\,dm<0$ whenever it radiates. Decreasing [specific entropy](thermodynamics.md#specific-entropy) does not require temperature at every depth to decrease immediately: contraction of an [ideal gas](thermodynamics.md#ideal-gas) can raise its central temperature while total energy is lost.

##### Power-law planetary cooling

↑ **Parent:** [Intrinsic planetary luminosity](#intrinsic-planetary-luminosity)

If otherwise comparable isolated [planets](planetary-science.md#planet) of one [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) follow $L_{\rm int}=Ct^{-\alpha}$, their intrinsic [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity) ratio is $L_{\rm int}(t_1)/L_{\rm int}(t_2)=(t_2/t_1)^\alpha$. The normalization depends on composition and initial [entropy](thermodynamics.md#entropy). Strong stellar irradiation, deep heating, or accretion can invalidate extrapolating the same cooling law.

#### Kelvin-Helmholtz cooling time

↑ **Parent:** [Kelvin-Helmholtz mechanism](#kelvin-helmholtz-mechanism)

The Kelvin-Helmholtz cooling time estimates how long a self-gravitating body can radiate a reservoir of energy of order its gravitational binding energy. It uses intrinsic luminosity $L_{\rm int}$, excluding reradiated stellar light. At fixed radius and structure, this reservoir scales as $M^2$; the approximation changes when degeneracy or other internal physics changes the accessible energy.

### Pre-main-sequence star

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-main-sequence_star)

A pre-main-sequence star contracts toward the main sequence before sustained core hydrogen burning supplies its luminosity. Convection, accretion, and deuterium burning shape its track across the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram.

#### Henyey track

↑ **Parent:** [Pre-main-sequence star](#pre-main-sequence-star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henyey_track)

A [pre-main-sequence star](#pre-main-sequence-star) with a developing [radiative core](stellar-structure.md#radiative-core) can contract along a relatively horizontal path toward higher [effective temperature](stellar-structure.md#effective-temperature) in the [Hertzsprung-Russell diagram](#hertzsprung-russell-diagram). This differs from the nearly vertical fully convective [Hayashi track](#hayashi-track).

#### Hayashi track

↑ **Parent:** [Pre-main-sequence star](#pre-main-sequence-star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayashi_track)

A Hayashi track is the nearly vertical path of a fully convective pre-main-sequence star on the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram. Photospheric opacity fixes a weak relation between luminosity and effective temperature.

##### Hayashi relation with eighth-power temperature opacity

↑ **Parent:** [Hayashi track](#hayashi-track)

Match a fully convective [stellar polytrope](stellar-structure.md#stellar-polytrope) of index $3/2$ to the [grey-atmosphere convection onset with eighth-power temperature opacity](stellar-structure.md#grey-atmosphere-convection-onset-with-eighth-power-temperature-opacity). At the fixed matching optical depth, $T_b\propto T_e$ and $P_b\propto g^{2/3}T_e^{-16/3}$. The ideal-gas adiabat $P=K\rho^{5/3}$ gives $K\propto T_b^{5/3}P_b^{-2/3}\propto T_e^{47/9}g^{-4/9}$. The [polytropic mass-radius relation](stellar-structure.md#polytropic-mass-radius-relation) gives $K\propto RM^{1/3}$; with $g\propto M/R^2$, this yields $T_e^{47}\propto RM^7$. The [Stefan–Boltzmann law](thermodynamics.md#stefan-boltzmann-law) then gives the displayed luminosity. Composition and opacity normalization are held fixed between models.

##### Hayashi relation with thirteenth-power opacity

↑ **Parent:** [Hayashi track](#hayashi-track)

Match an efficient, [monatomic gas](thermodynamics.md#monatomic-gas), fully convective star to the [grey-atmosphere convection onset with thirteenth-power opacity](stellar-structure.md#grey-atmosphere-convection-onset-with-thirteenth-power-opacity). Its convective relation is $P=K_TT^{5/2}$. At the matching [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature) $T_b\propto T_e$, the [stellar atmosphere](#stellar-atmosphere) gives $K_T^2\propto ML^{-1}T_e^{-13}\propto MR^{13/2}L^{-17/4}$ using the [Stefan–Boltzmann law](thermodynamics.md#stefan-boltzmann-law). The ideal-gas relation converts this to $P=K_\rho\rho^{5/3}$ with $K_\rho=(\mathcal R/\mu)^{5/3}K_T^{-2/3}$. The [polytropic mass-radius relation](stellar-structure.md#polytropic-mass-radius-relation) at index $3/2$ gives $K_\rho\propto GM^{1/3}R$, hence $K_T^2\propto M^{-1}R^{-3}$. Equating the two expressions proves $L^{17/4}\propto M^2R^{19/2}$, or $L\propto M^{8/17}R^{38/17}$. The entropy coefficient is constant throughout the [convective envelope](stellar-structure.md#convective-envelope), but can change between models.

##### Convective contraction with power-law opacity

↑ **Parent:** [Hayashi track](#hayashi-track)

For a monatomic fully convective [ideal gas](thermodynamics.md#ideal-gas) star, uniform [specific entropy](thermodynamics.md#specific-entropy) gives $P=KT^{5/2}$ with $K\propto\mu^{-5/2}M^{-1/2}R^{-3/2}$. Combining this with the [stellar surface boundary condition](stellar-structure.md#stellar-surface-boundary-condition) $\kappa P=2g/3$ and $\kappa=\kappa_0\rho^aT^b$ gives

$$
T_e^{(3a+5)/2+b}\propto
\mu^{(3a+5)/2}M^{(a+3)/2}R^{(3a-1)/2}.
$$

All composition comparisons here hold $\kappa_0$ fixed. For $a=1,b=4$, $T_e^8\propto\mu^4M^2R$, and the [Stefan–Boltzmann law](thermodynamics.md#stefan-boltzmann-law) gives $L\propto\mu^2MR^{5/2}$. The [stellar virial theorem](stellar-structure.md#stellar-virial-theorem) and [Kelvin-Helmholtz contraction](#kelvin-helmholtz-mechanism) then give $R^{-7/2}-R_0^{-7/2}\propto\mu^2M^{-1}(t-t_0)$, with a positive coefficient. Once the initial-radius term is negligible, $R\propto\mu^{-4/7}M^{2/7}(t-t_0)^{-2/7}$.

###### Contraction law with density-linear fourth-power stellar opacity

↑ **Parent:** [Convective contraction with power-law opacity](#convective-contraction-with-power-law-opacity)

For a fixed-mass, fully convective [monatomic gas](thermodynamics.md#monatomic-gas) [protostar](#protostar), write $P=KT^{5/2}$ and $K=K_0\mu^{-5/2}M^{-1/2}R^{-3/2}$. Matching the [photosphere](stellar-structure.md#photosphere) to $\kappa P=2g/3$ with $\kappa=\kappa_0\rho T^4$ gives $T_e^8=2G\mathcal R\mu^4M^2R/(3\kappa_0K_0^2)$. The [Stefan–Boltzmann law](thermodynamics.md#stefan-boltzmann-law) gives $L=\pi ac[2G\mathcal R/(3\kappa_0)]^{1/2}K_0^{-1}\mu^2MR^{5/2}$. The [stellar virial theorem](stellar-structure.md#stellar-virial-theorem) gives total energy $-3GM^2/(7R)$, so $L=-3GM^2\dot R/(7R^2)$. Integrating proves $C=(49\pi ac/6)[2\mathcal R/(3\kappa_0G)]^{1/2}K_0^{-1}$. This holds before [stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion), changing [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity) or [electron degeneracy pressure](statistical-physics.md#electron-degeneracy-pressure) invalidates the assumed contraction law.

###### Hayashi contraction with seventeenth-power opacity

↑ **Parent:** [Convective contraction with power-law opacity](#convective-contraction-with-power-law-opacity)

Match an $n=3/2$ [stellar polytrope](stellar-structure.md#stellar-polytrope) to the [grey-atmosphere convection onset with seventeenth-power opacity](stellar-structure.md#grey-atmosphere-convection-onset-with-seventeenth-power-opacity). At each instant the convective entropy coefficient obeys $P=KT^{5/2}$, $K\propto M^{-1/2}R^{-3/2}$ at fixed composition, while the boundary gives $K\propto g^{1/2}T_e^{-21/2}$. Thus $T_e^{21/2}\propto MR^{1/2}$ and the first displayed [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity) law follows. The [stellar virial theorem](stellar-structure.md#stellar-virial-theorem) for a monatomic gas gives total energy $-3GM^2/(7R)$, so [Kelvin-Helmholtz contraction](#kelvin-helmholtz-mechanism) has $\dot R=-7LR^2/(3GM^2)$. Integrating at fixed mass gives $R^{-67/21}-R_0^{-67/21}\propto t$. The second displayed relation holds after the initial-radius term is negligible. The entropy coefficient is spatially constant but changes during contraction.

###### Composition shift of a convective pre-main-sequence track

↑ **Parent:** [Convective contraction with power-law opacity](#convective-contraction-with-power-law-opacity)

For [convective contraction with power-law opacity](#convective-contraction-with-power-law-opacity) $\kappa\propto\rho T^4$ at fixed stellar mass and opacity normalization, $T_e\propto\mu^{1/2}R^{1/8}$ and $L\propto\mu^2R^{5/2}$. Eliminating $R$ gives $L\propto\mu^{-8}T_e^{20}$. Thus a larger [mean molecular weight](thermodynamics.md#mean-molecular-weight) moves the track to larger [effective temperature](stellar-structure.md#effective-temperature) at a given [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity), with $T_e|_L\propto\mu^{2/5}$. Contraction decreases both temperature and luminosity for this particular opacity exponent.

#### Accretion luminosity

↑ **Parent:** [Pre-main-sequence star](#pre-main-sequence-star)

Matter accreting at rate $\dot M$ onto a star of mass $M$ and radius $R$ releases gravitational power of order

$$
L_{\rm acc}=\frac{GM\dot M}{R},
$$

up to an efficiency and an inner-boundary correction.

#### Deuterium-burning energy-generation rate

↑ **Parent:** [Pre-main-sequence star](#pre-main-sequence-star)

At the temperatures relevant to pre-main-sequence stars, a useful steep power-law approximation to the specific deuterium-burning rate is $\epsilon_D\propto\rho T^{15}$. Its sensitivity to central temperature makes deuterium burning act as a temporary thermostat.

### Subgiant

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgiant)

A subgiant has begun evolving away from the [main sequence](#main-sequence) after central hydrogen exhaustion, increasing its radius before the extended [red giant](#red-giant) stage.

### Red giant

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_giant)

A red giant is an evolved star with a large radius and low [effective temperature](stellar-structure.md#effective-temperature). On the first giant branch, a low- or intermediate-mass star is powered primarily by shell [hydrogen burning](#hydrogen-burning) around a helium-rich core.

#### Fixed-radius-core shell-burning luminosity relation

↑ **Parent:** [Red giant](#red-giant)

Use a [Kramers radiative-zero envelope around a stellar core](stellar-structure.md#kramers-radiative-zero-envelope-around-a-stellar-core) and specific heating $\epsilon=\epsilon_0\rho T^{67/4}$. Since $\rho\propto CT^{13/4}$ and $T\propto M_c/r$, $\epsilon\propto Cr^{-20}$ and $\epsilon(1.05R_c)/\epsilon(R_c)=1.05^{-20}\simeq0.377$. The volume heating is $\rho\epsilon\propto C^2T^{93/4}$. Integrating $4\pi r^2\rho\epsilon$ from $R_c$ outwards gives $L\propto C^2M_c^{93/4}R_c^{-81/4}$, while radiative transport gives $C^2\propto M_c/L$. Therefore $L^2\propto M_c^{97/4}R_c^{-81/4}$, or $L\propto M_c^{97/8}R_c^{-81/8}$. For a core sequence with fixed $R_c$, the [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) exponent is $97/8$. The steep integrand confines most heating to a thin shell; using the exterior constant-luminosity profile throughout it estimates the coefficient rather than resolving the shell structure.

#### Degenerate-core shell-burning mass-luminosity relation

↑ **Parent:** [Red giant](#red-giant)

In a thin-shell giant model with a nonrelativistic degenerate core, $R_c\propto M_c^{-1/3}$. For a gas-pressure radiative layer with $\kappa\propto\rho T^{-3}$ and specific energy generation $\epsilon\propto\rho T^{10}$, the deep profile has $P\propto T^4$, $\rho\propto T^3$ and $T\propto M_c/r$. Integrating the strongly concentrated shell power gives $L\propto C^2M_c^{16}R_c^{-13}$, while radiative transport gives $C^2\propto M_c/L$. Therefore $L^2\propto M_c^{17}R_c^{-13}$, yielding the displayed exponent. This is a model-specific scaling, not a universal core-mass law; it fails when envelope stripping removes the shell fuel or the confining structure.

##### Constant-opacity radiative-zero giant envelope

↑ **Parent:** [Degenerate-core shell-burning mass-luminosity relation](#degenerate-core-shell-burning-mass-luminosity-relation)

A negligible-mass [radiative envelope](stellar-structure.md#radiative-envelope) surrounding a core of mass $M_c$, carrying constant luminosity and having constant [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity), obeys $dP/dT=16\pi acGM_cT^3/(3\kappa_0L)$. Neglecting the outer pressure integration constant gives $\Lambda=4\pi acGM_c/(3\kappa_0L)$. The [gas pressure](thermodynamics.md#gas-pressure) fraction $\beta$ is constant because $P_{\rm rad}=aT^4/3$, and $\rho=\mu\beta\Lambda T^3/\mathcal R$. Hydrostatic balance gives $T=T_s+\mu\beta GM_c(1/r-1/R_s)/(4\mathcal R)$; dropping this outer-boundary constant gives the displayed deep-envelope approximation. A positive gas-pressure fraction requires $L<4\pi cGM_c/\kappa_0$.

###### High-power shell burning in a constant-opacity giant envelope

↑ **Parent:** [Constant-opacity radiative-zero giant envelope](#constant-opacity-radiative-zero-giant-envelope)

With specific heating $\epsilon=\epsilon_0\rho T^{16}$, the [constant-opacity radiative-zero giant envelope](#constant-opacity-radiative-zero-giant-envelope) has $T\propto\mu\beta M_c/r$ and $\rho\propto\mu\beta M_cT^3/L$. Therefore the shell power $4\pi r^2\rho\epsilon$ is proportional to $(\mu\beta M_c)^{24}L^{-2}r^{-20}$. Integrating from the core radius gives $L^3\propto(\mu\beta M_c)^{24}R_c^{-19}$. The steep radial dependence justifies a thin-shell estimate dominated by the base. Using the outer radiative profile throughout the emitting layer is an approximation; a resolved shell can change the numerical coefficient. Also $\beta=1-\kappa_0L/(4\pi cGM_c)$, so retaining radiation pressure makes the relation implicit in luminosity.

### Stellar rejuvenation

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)

Stellar rejuvenation occurs when mixing brings fresh hydrogen into the core of a mass-gaining or merged [main sequence](#main-sequence) star, making its remaining nuclear lifetime resemble that of a younger star. The amount of rejuvenation depends on the accretor's internal mixing and prior composition.

### Helium star

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)

A helium star has lost most of its hydrogen-rich envelope and is made predominantly of helium. If massive enough, its core can ignite helium; a lower-mass stripped core instead becomes a [helium white dwarf](#helium-white-dwarf).

### Asymptotic giant branch

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_giant_branch)

An asymptotic-giant-branch star has an inert carbon-oxygen core surrounded by helium- and hydrogen-burning shells and a deep convective envelope. Alternating shell burning produces thermal pulses and dredge-up, while strong mass loss removes the envelope.

#### AGB envelope-loss and core-growth timescales

↑ **Parent:** [Asymptotic giant branch](#asymptotic-giant-branch)

The fate of an [Asymptotic giant branch](#asymptotic-giant-branch) star depends on whether its envelope is removed before its core reaches the next ignition or instability threshold. The approximate envelope-loss time is $t_{\rm env}=M_{\rm env}/|\dot M|$, while the time to add a required core [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) $\Delta M_c$ is $t_{\rm grow}=\Delta M_c/\dot M_c$. Shell [stellar nuclear fusion](#stellar-nuclear-fusion) grows the core; [third dredge-up](#third-dredge-up) can reduce the net growth. If $t_{\rm env}\ll t_{\rm grow}$, shell fuel disappears first and a [white dwarf](#white-dwarf) remnant is favored. The opposite ordering permits further core growth but does not by itself specify whether [carbon burning](#carbon-burning) is stable, explosive, or followed by further evolution. Both rates change with time, so the estimates guide rather than replace a stellar model.

#### Hot-bottom burning

↑ **Parent:** [Asymptotic giant branch](#asymptotic-giant-branch)

When the bottom of an [Asymptotic giant branch](#asymptotic-giant-branch) star's convective envelope becomes hot enough for [hydrogen burning](#hydrogen-burning), the [CNO cycle](#cno-cycle) can convert newly dredged-up [carbon](chemistry.md#carbon) into [nitrogen](chemistry.md#nitrogen). Its onset and strength depend on mass, composition and envelope structure; it is not a universal property of all stars at one initial mass.

#### Stellar dredge-up

↑ **Parent:** [Asymptotic giant branch](#asymptotic-giant-branch)

Stellar dredge-up occurs when a convective envelope penetrates into layers altered by nuclear burning and transports their material to the surface.

##### First dredge-up

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar dredge-up](#stellar-dredge-up)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_dredge-up)

First dredge-up occurs as a star ascends the red-giant branch. Its deepening convective envelope exposes material processed by hydrogen burning, especially enhanced helium and nitrogen and depleted carbon.

##### Second dredge-up

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar dredge-up](#stellar-dredge-up)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_dredge-up)

Second dredge-up occurs after core helium exhaustion in sufficiently massive intermediate-mass stars. The convective envelope moves inward, mixes helium- and hydrogen-burning products to the surface, and reduces the hydrogen-exhausted core mass.

##### Third dredge-up

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar dredge-up](#stellar-dredge-up)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_dredge-up)

Third dredge-up follows a thermal pulse on the asymptotic giant branch, when the convective envelope reaches into the intershell and can carry freshly made carbon and slow-neutron-capture elements to the surface.

#### Thermal pulse of an asymptotic-giant-branch star

↑ **Parent:** [Asymptotic giant branch](#asymptotic-giant-branch)

A thermal pulse is a helium-shell flash caused by thermally unstable burning in a thin shell. It briefly drives intershell convection, expands and cools the hydrogen-burning shell, and can be followed by third dredge-up.

<h5 id="harm-schwarzschild-instability">Härm–Schwarzschild instability</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Thermal pulse of an asymptotic-giant-branch star](#thermal-pulse-of-an-asymptotic-giant-branch-star)

The Härm–Schwarzschild instability is the thin-shell thermal instability of helium burning in an asymptotic-giant-branch star. Heating greatly raises the nuclear rate while the geometrically thin shell initially expands too little to provide stabilizing cooling.

#### Super-AGB star

↑ **Parent:** [Asymptotic giant branch](#asymptotic-giant-branch)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super-AGB_star)

A super-AGB star is massive enough to ignite carbon and form a degenerate oxygen-neon core, but initially too light to proceed directly through all advanced burning stages. Its fate depends on competition between core growth and envelope mass loss.

##### Oxygen-neon-magnesium core

↑ **Parent:** [Super-AGB star](#super-agb-star)

An oxygen-neon-magnesium core is the degenerate product of carbon burning, composed mainly of oxygen and neon with a smaller magnesium abundance. It can become an oxygen-neon white dwarf or collapse after electron captures if it approaches the [Chandrasekhar mass](#chandrasekhar-limit).

##### Electron-capture supernova

↑ **Parent:** [Super-AGB star](#super-agb-star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron-capture_supernova)

An electron-capture supernova occurs when electron captures, especially on neon and magnesium nuclei in a degenerate oxygen-neon core, remove pressure support and trigger collapse before ordinary iron-core formation.

### Stellar wind

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_wind)

A stellar wind is a continuous outflow of gas from a star. Radiation, gas pressure, waves, or magnetic fields can supply its acceleration, and the resulting mass loss can determine a star's final remnant.

#### AGB superwind

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar wind](#stellar-wind)

An episode of particularly strong [mass loss](#mass-loss-astrophysics) near the end of [Asymptotic giant branch](#asymptotic-giant-branch) evolution, capable of removing the remaining envelope rapidly compared with its prior evolution. Radiation acting on dust can transfer momentum to the gas; pulsations help lift material into cooler dust-forming layers. Envelope removal terminates sustained [hydrogen-burning shell](#hydrogen-burning-shell) and [helium-burning shell](#helium-burning-shell) activity.

#### Wind-driven magnetic braking of a solar-type star

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar wind](#stellar-wind)

A magnetized [stellar wind](#stellar-wind) extracts angular momentum through matter transport and [Maxwell stress tensor](electromagnetism.md#maxwell-stress-tensor). The [Alfvén radius](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#alfven-radius) supplies an effective lever arm larger than the stellar radius, with geometry factors absorbed into an averaged lever arm. [Dynamo action](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#dynamo-action) couples the torque to rotation by supplying the magnetic field. For a simplified radial wind of fixed speed, $R_A^2$ scales as $B_*^2R_*^4/(\mu_0\dot M_wv_w)$, so unsaturated $B_*\propto\Omega$ gives a torque proportional to $-\Omega^3$. Magnetic topology and wind thermodynamics can alter this scaling.

##### Skumanich rotation law

↑ **Parent:** [Wind-driven magnetic braking of a solar-type star](#wind-driven-magnetic-braking-of-a-solar-type-star)

If the moment of inertia I changes slowly and [wind-driven magnetic braking of a solar-type star](#wind-driven-magnetic-braking-of-a-solar-type-star) has torque $-K\Omega^3$, then $\Omega^{-2}(t)=\Omega^{-2}(t_0)+2K(t-t_0)/I$. At sufficiently late times within this unsaturated approximation, rotation scales as $t^{-1/2}$. This empirical main-sequence trend is not a universal law for saturated rapid rotators, changing stellar structure or every magnetic geometry.

#### Hydrodynamic transfield equation for an axisymmetric wind

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar wind](#stellar-wind)

Using an [axisymmetric hydrodynamic mass flux function](fluid-mechanics.md#axisymmetric-hydrodynamic-mass-flux-function), set $s=s(\psi)$, $\ell=Ru_\phi=\ell(\psi)$ and $\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\psi)$. The azimuthal [vorticity](fluid-mechanics.md#vorticity) is $\omega_\phi=-R\nabla\cdot(\nabla\psi/(\rho R^2))$. The meridional projection of the [Crocco form of the unsteady Euler equation](fluid-mechanics.md#crocco-form-of-the-unsteady-euler-equation) gives the displayed equation. The [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) also satisfies the Bernoulli relation $\varepsilon=|\nabla\psi|^2/(2\rho^2R^2)+\ell^2/(2R^2)+w+\Phi$, so an [equation of state](thermodynamics.md#equation-of-state) is part of closing the system.

#### Polytropic stellar wind

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar wind](#stellar-wind)

A steady spherical [polytropic flow](compressible-flow.md#polytropic-flow) escaping a point-mass potential conserves the outward [mass loss rate](physics.md#mass-loss-rate) and the [Bernoulli function](fluid-mechanics.md#bernoulli-function) $C=u^2/2+c_s^2/(\gamma-1)-GM/r$. For positive terminal speed, a finite-radius smooth [sonic point](compressible-flow.md#sonic-point) requires $C>0$ and $1<\gamma<5/3$. Its sound speed obeys $c_{s,c}^2=2C(\gamma-1)/(5-3\gamma)$, and evaluating the conserved flux there selects the transonic [mass loss rate](physics.md#mass-loss-rate). Other parameter limits need separate treatment.

##### Minimum matching at a polytropic sonic point

↑ **Parent:** [Polytropic stellar wind](#polytropic-stellar-wind)

Put $y=\rho r^{4/(\gamma+1)}$ and $\alpha=4(\gamma-1)/(\gamma+1)$. The [polytropic stellar wind](#polytropic-stellar-wind) equations become $F(r)=Cr^\alpha+GMr^{\alpha-1}=G(y)=\gamma Ky^{\gamma-1}/(\gamma-1)+\dot M^2/(32\pi^2y^2)$. At a regular [sonic point](compressible-flow.md#sonic-point) both derivatives vanish. Matching their minima allows the two positive-$y$ branches to join with finite slope; unequal minima either leave a forbidden radial interval or separate the subsonic and supersonic branches. This reduces a singular differential-equation crossing to a geometric minimum comparison.

#### Gas and radiation pressure stellar wind equation

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar wind](#stellar-wind)

In an optically thick steady spherical [stellar wind](#stellar-wind) of constant [mean molecular weight](thermodynamics.md#mean-molecular-weight) $\mu_{\rm mol}$, let $v$ be the outward speed and $a_g^2=k_BT/(\mu_{\rm mol}m_u)$ the [isothermal sound speed](compressible-flow.md#isothermal-sound-speed) squared. Mass conservation gives $\rho'/\rho=-2/r-v'/v$, where a prime denotes differentiation with respect to radius $r$. Use $P_{\rm gas}=\rho a_g^2$ and [radiative diffusion](astrophysics.md#radiative-diffusion) to write $P_{\rm rad}'/\rho=-g\Gamma$, where $g=GM_r/r^2$ and $\Gamma=L_r/L_{\rm Edd}$ uses the local [Eddington luminosity](stellar-structure.md#eddington-luminosity) $L_{\rm Edd}=4\pi cGM_r/\kappa$ and [opacity](stellar-structure.md#opacity) $\kappa$. Substituting into the momentum equation yields the displayed wind equation. Smooth [sonic point](compressible-flow.md#sonic-point) crossing requires both its denominator and numerator to vanish. The radiation-pressure gradient already supplies radiative acceleration; adding that force separately would count it twice.

##### Sonic-point obstruction at the Eddington luminosity

↑ **Parent:** [Gas and radiation pressure stellar wind equation](#gas-and-radiation-pressure-stellar-wind-equation)

For an optically thick diffusion-controlled wind, put $\beta=P_{\rm gas}/(P_{\rm gas}+P_{\rm rad})$. Diffusion gives $(\log T)'=-g\Gamma\beta/[4a_g^2(1-\beta)]$. At $\Gamma=1$, the numerator of the [gas and radiation pressure stellar wind equation](#gas-and-radiation-pressure-stellar-wind-equation) is $2a_g^2/r+g\beta/[4(1-\beta)]>0$ for finite-temperature gas and $0<\beta<1$. A subsonic branch then decelerates and cannot pass a regular [sonic point](compressible-flow.md#sonic-point). This statement is specific to subsonically launched winds in this transport model, not to every supersonic solution or every form of stellar-wind driving.

### Neutron star

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_star)

A neutron star is a compact stellar remnant whose gravity compresses roughly a stellar mass into a radius of order ten kilometres. Degenerate matter supplies most of its pressure support, while rapid rotation and intense magnetic fields can power observable emission.

#### Pulsar

↑ **Parent:** [Neutron star](#neutron-star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsar)

A pulsar is a rotating magnetized neutron star whose directed emission is observed as periodic pulses. Rotation supplies the energy of an ordinary radio pulsar.

##### Pulsar magnetosphere

↑ **Parent:** [Pulsar](#pulsar)

A pulsar magnetosphere is the plasma and electromagnetic field surrounding a pulsar. Close to the star, charges tend to screen electric fields parallel to the magnetic field and approximately corotate; near the light cylinder, relativistic motion and field distortion become essential.

###### Corotating pulsar magnetosphere

↑ **Parent:** [Pulsar magnetosphere](#pulsar-magnetosphere)

In an idealized corotating pulsar magnetosphere, plasma moves with $\mathbf u=\boldsymbol\Omega\mathbin\times\mathbf r$. In Gaussian units, ideal magnetohydrodynamics then gives $\mathbf E=-\mathbf u\mathbin\times\mathbf B/c$.

###### Goldreich-Julian density

↑ **Parent:** [Corotating pulsar magnetosphere](#corotating-pulsar-magnetosphere)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldreich-Julian_density)

The Goldreich-Julian density is the charge density needed to support corotation in an idealized pulsar magnetosphere. Where exterior currents vanish,

$$
\rho_{\rm GJ}=-\frac{\boldsymbol\Omega\mathbin\cdot\mathbf B}{2\pi c}
$$

in Gaussian units.

###### Light cylinder

↑ **Parent:** [Pulsar magnetosphere](#pulsar-magnetosphere)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_cylinder)

The light cylinder is the notional cylindrical surface on which rigid corotation with angular speed $\Omega$ would reach the speed of light.

###### Light cylinder radius

↑ **Parent:** [Light cylinder](#light-cylinder)

The light cylinder radius is $R_{\rm LC}=c/\Omega$. A nonrelativistic corotation model must fail as cylindrical radius approaches this scale.

###### Pulsar polar cap

↑ **Parent:** [Pulsar magnetosphere](#pulsar-magnetosphere)

A pulsar polar cap is the region on the stellar surface threaded by magnetic field lines that reach the outer magnetosphere rather than closing close to the star.

###### Polar-cap opening angle

↑ **Parent:** [Pulsar polar cap](#pulsar-polar-cap)

For an aligned dipole whose last relevant field line reaches equatorial radius $R_e$, the polar-cap opening angle obeys $\sin^2\theta_*=R_*/R_e$. It is approximately $\sqrt{R_*/R_e}$ for a small cap.

### White dwarf

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_dwarf)

A white dwarf is a compact stellar remnant supported mainly by electron degeneracy pressure.

#### Carbon-oxygen white dwarf

↑ **Parent:** [White dwarf](#white-dwarf)

A carbon-oxygen white dwarf is a [white dwarf](#white-dwarf) whose core is predominantly [carbon](chemistry.md#carbon) and [oxygen](chemistry.md#oxygen), retained after [core helium burning](#core-helium-burning) when the star loses its envelope without sustained [carbon burning](#carbon-burning).

#### Helium white dwarf

↑ **Parent:** [White dwarf](#white-dwarf)

A helium white dwarf is a [white dwarf](#white-dwarf) whose core is primarily helium. Stripping a low-mass evolved star before helium ignition can produce one on a much shorter timescale than isolated stellar evolution.

#### Nonrelativistic white dwarf

↑ **Parent:** [White dwarf](#white-dwarf)

A cold nonrelativistic white dwarf supported by [electron degeneracy pressure](statistical-physics.md#electron-degeneracy-pressure) has a [polytropic equation of state](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#polytropic-equation-of-state) $P=K_{\rm NR}\rho^{5/3}$, so its [polytropic index](stellar-structure.md#polytropic-index) is $3/2$. The [Lane-Emden mass formula](stellar-structure.md#lane-emden-mass-formula) and [polytropic mass-radius relation](stellar-structure.md#polytropic-mass-radius-relation) give $R\propto K_{\rm NR}M^{-1/3}$ at fixed composition. As density rises, relativistic corrections soften the exponent toward $4/3$ and lead toward the [Chandrasekhar mass](#chandrasekhar-limit) regime.

#### Chandrasekhar limit

↑ **Parent:** [White dwarf](#white-dwarf)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrasekhar_limit)

The Chandrasekhar mass is the limiting mass of a cold nonrotating white dwarf supported by relativistic electron degeneracy pressure, about $1.4M_\odot$ for ordinary composition.

##### Chandrasekhar mass from central polytropic constants

↑ **Parent:** [Chandrasekhar limit](#chandrasekhar-limit)

An ultrarelativistic [degenerate electron gas](statistical-physics.md#degenerate-electron-gas) has $P=K\rho^{4/3}$, so it forms an index-three [stellar polytrope](stellar-structure.md#stellar-polytrope). Insert $P_c=c_3GM^2/R^4$ and $\rho_c=3a_3M/(4\pi R^3)$ into that equation of state: the radius cancels, leaving the displayed mass. For fully ionized helium, $n_e=\rho/(2m_p)$ and $K=(3/(2\pi))^{1/3}hc/(16m_p^{4/3})$. This is the ideal limiting [Chandrasekhar mass](#chandrasekhar-limit), rather than a claim that every ordinary [white dwarf](#white-dwarf) has that mass.

<h4 id="mestel-s-cooling-law">Mestel's cooling law</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [White dwarf](#white-dwarf)

Mestel's cooling law matches a degenerate isothermal white-dwarf core to a nondegenerate radiative envelope. With Kramers opacity it gives $L/M\propto T_c^{7/2}$ in terms of core temperature.

##### Radiative-zero white-dwarf envelope

↑ **Parent:** [Mestel's cooling law](#mestel-s-cooling-law)

In a negligible-mass [white dwarf](#white-dwarf) envelope, take enclosed mass and [luminosity](astrophysics.md#luminosity) constant, neglect [radiation pressure](thermodynamics.md#radiation-pressure), and use an [ideal gas](thermodynamics.md#ideal-gas) with the [Kramers' opacity law](stellar-structure.md#kramers-opacity-law). Dividing [hydrostatic pressure support equation](stellar-structure.md#hydrostatic-pressure-support-equation) by [radiative diffusion in a star](stellar-structure.md#radiative-diffusion-in-a-star) gives $P\,dP\propto(M/L)T^{15/2}dT$. Neglecting the surface integration constant produces the displayed [pressure](thermodynamics.md#pressure) and [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) laws. It is a deep-envelope approximation, not a finite-temperature photospheric boundary condition.

###### Geometrical thickness of a white-dwarf radiative envelope

↑ **Parent:** [Radiative-zero white-dwarf envelope](#radiative-zero-white-dwarf-envelope)

Integrating the [hydrostatic pressure support equation](stellar-structure.md#hydrostatic-pressure-support-equation) with the [radiative-zero white-dwarf envelope](#radiative-zero-white-dwarf-envelope) law yields $T=4GM(1/r-1/R)/(17\mathcal R)$. Matching $T=T_c$ at $r_b$ gives $s=R-r_b$ as displayed. A strongly degenerate bulk core has thermal energy small compared with its [Fermi energy](statistical-physics.md#fermi-energy), making $q\ll1$ and hence $s\ll R$. The inequality $T_c\gg T_{\rm eff}$ alone does not establish geometrical thinness.

###### White-dwarf envelope thickness-luminosity relation

↑ **Parent:** [Geometrical thickness of a white-dwarf radiative envelope](#geometrical-thickness-of-a-white-dwarf-radiative-envelope)

For fixed mass, degenerate radius and composition, the [matching of a white-dwarf envelope to a degenerate core](#matching-of-a-white-dwarf-envelope-to-a-degenerate-core) gives $L\propto T_c^{7/2}$, while the [geometrical thickness of a white-dwarf radiative envelope](#geometrical-thickness-of-a-white-dwarf-radiative-envelope) gives $s\propto T_c$ to leading thin-layer order. Cooling therefore thins this idealized envelope. At finite thickness the ratio additionally contains $(1+q_2)/(1+q_1)$.

###### Matching of a white-dwarf envelope to a degenerate core

↑ **Parent:** [Radiative-zero white-dwarf envelope](#radiative-zero-white-dwarf-envelope)

Continuity of [temperature](thermodynamics.md#temperature), [pressure](thermodynamics.md#pressure) and [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) matches the [ideal gas](thermodynamics.md#ideal-gas) envelope [pressure](thermodynamics.md#pressure) $\mathcal R\rho_bT_c$ to the nonrelativistic [electron degeneracy pressure](statistical-physics.md#electron-degeneracy-pressure) $K_{\rm NR}\rho_b^{5/3}$. Substituting this base [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) into the [radiative-zero white-dwarf envelope](#radiative-zero-white-dwarf-envelope) law gives the luminosity-temperature scaling behind [Mestel's cooling law](#mestel-s-cooling-law). The matched point is a transition approximation; the cold degeneracy law does not represent finite-temperature corrections there exactly.

#### Hydrogen-burning white-dwarf envelope

↑ **Parent:** [White dwarf](#white-dwarf)

A hydrogen-burning white-dwarf envelope is a thin nondegenerate surface layer in which accreted or residual hydrogen can undergo nuclear fusion above a degenerate core.

##### Inverse-square-opacity burning envelope

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen-burning white-dwarf envelope](#hydrogen-burning-white-dwarf-envelope)

In a thin nondegenerate [hydrogen-burning white-dwarf envelope](#hydrogen-burning-white-dwarf-envelope), fix gravity $g$ and use $P=g\Sigma$, $\rho=B\Sigma/T$. With opacity $\kappa=\kappa_0\rho T^{-2}$ and specific heating $\epsilon=\epsilon_0\rho T^{15}$, set $y=T^7$, $x=\Sigma^2/2$. Then $y'=AF$ and $F'=-\epsilon_0By^2$, where $A=21\kappa_0B/(4ac)$ and $\omega^2=A\epsilon_0B$. Radiative-zero surface and zero core-flux base conditions give $(y')^2=(2\omega^2/3)(y_b^3-y^3)$. Thus $T_b\propto F_s^{2/21}$ and $F_s=12I^3/(A\omega^2\Sigma_b^6)$, with $I=\int_0^1(1-t^3)^{-1/2}dt$. At fixed column the specific heating varies as $T^{14}$ while radiative cooling varies as $T^7$, giving [thin-shell instability](#thin-shell-instability) in this approximation.

##### Thin-shell instability

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen-burning white-dwarf envelope](#hydrogen-burning-white-dwarf-envelope)

Thin-shell instability is a thermal runaway of nuclear burning in a geometrically thin layer whose pressure is constrained by overlying weight, weakening expansion-driven cooling.

###### Classical nova

↑ **Parent:** [Thin-shell instability](#thin-shell-instability)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_nova)

A classical nova is a thermonuclear runaway in hydrogen-rich material accreted onto a white dwarf, producing a sudden luminous outburst and ejecting part of the envelope.

###### Nova-induced binary detachment

↑ **Parent:** [Classical nova](#classical-nova)

An idealized [classical nova](#classical-nova) that slowly ejects [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) $\delta m$ isotropically from the accretor, compared with the [orbital period](classical-mechanics.md#orbital-period), but quickly compared with the mass-transfer timescale, expands a circular [binary star](#binary-star)'s [Roche lobe](#roche-lobe). The ejecta carry accretor [specific angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#specific-angular-momentum) $j_1/J=M_2/(M_1M)=q/M$. Taking $\delta J/J=-q\delta m/M$, $\delta M_1=-\delta m$ and $\delta M_2=0$ in $J=M_1M_2\sqrt{Ga/M}$ proves $\delta a/a=\delta m/M$. The lobe law $R_L\propto a(M_2/M)^{1/3}$ then gives $\delta R_L/R_L=4\delta m/(3M)$. A donor whose radius is unchanged moves inside its lobe.

For constant external loss $\Gamma=-\dot J>0$ and a donor sequence $R_2\propto M_2^\zeta$, the fractional gap closes at rate $2\Gamma/J$, giving $t_d=2J\delta m/(3M\Gamma)$. The [binary mass-transfer contact equation](#binary-mass-transfer-contact-equation) gives accumulation time $t_s=J\delta m(\zeta+5/3-2q)/(2M_2\Gamma)$. Therefore

$$
\frac{t_d}{t_s}=\frac{4q}{(1+q)(3\zeta+5-6q)}.
$$

This first-order result requires $\delta m/M\ll1$, a positive contact denominator and a fixed donor radius during the eruption. At $\zeta=1$ it reduces to $2q/[(1+q)(4-3q)]$.

<h3 id="schonberg-chandrasekhar-limit">Schönberg-Chandrasekhar limit</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar evolution](#stellar-evolution)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schönberg-Chandrasekhar_limit)

The Schönberg-Chandrasekhar limit is the maximum fractional mass of an isothermal inert core that can support a surrounding hydrogen-burning envelope in hydrostatic and thermal equilibrium.

## Binary star

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_star)

A binary star is a gravitationally bound pair orbiting their common centre of mass.

### X-ray binary

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_binary)

An X-ray binary has a compact [mass-gaining star](#mass-gaining-star) or [black hole](general-relativity.md#black-hole) accreting from a companion, so that much of its emitted power appears as [X-rays](electromagnetism.md#x-ray). Gravitational binding energy supplies an [accretion luminosity](#accretion-luminosity) of order $GM\dot M/R$ when there is a surface or an appropriate inner disk radius. The [donor star](#donor-star) may feed an [accretion disk](astrophysics.md#accretion-disk) through [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow) or a [stellar wind](#stellar-wind).

#### Type I X-ray burst

↑ **Parent:** [X-ray binary](#x-ray-binary)

A Type I X-ray burst is a transient [thermonuclear runaway](#thermonuclear-runaway) in accreted [hydrogen](chemistry.md#hydrogen) or [helium](chemistry.md#helium) on a [neutron star](#neutron-star) surface. It rises rapidly and cools after the fuel burns. A [black hole](general-relativity.md#black-hole) has no material surface on which the same accumulated surface-fuel event can occur. This is distinct from an accretion-powered X-ray pulse or an accretion instability.

#### Accreting X-ray pulsar

↑ **Parent:** [X-ray binary](#x-ray-binary)

An accreting X-ray pulsar contains a magnetized [neutron star](#neutron-star) whose [magnetic field](electromagnetism.md#magnetic-field) channels the accreting gas toward its magnetic poles. Rotation carries the bright accretion regions through the observer's view and produces pulses. The input [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum) can change the spin period; this differs from the rotational energy source of an isolated [pulsar](#pulsar).

#### High-mass X-ray binary

↑ **Parent:** [X-ray binary](#x-ray-binary)

A high-mass [X-ray binary](#x-ray-binary) has a massive [donor star](#donor-star) supplying an accreting [neutron star](#neutron-star) or [black hole](general-relativity.md#black-hole). A strong [stellar wind](#stellar-wind) often feeds it, although [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow) and donor equatorial outflows also occur. The short lifetime of the donor associates these systems with young stellar populations.

#### Low-mass X-ray binary

↑ **Parent:** [X-ray binary](#x-ray-binary)

In a low-mass [X-ray binary](#x-ray-binary), the [donor star](#donor-star) is relatively low mass. [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow) usually feeds an [accretion disk](astrophysics.md#accretion-disk) around a [neutron star](#neutron-star) or [black hole](general-relativity.md#black-hole). The low-mass label concerns the donor rather than the total binary mass. Accumulated fuel on a [neutron star](#neutron-star) can produce a [Type I X-ray burst](#type-i-x-ray-burst).

### Neutron star merger

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_star_merger)

Coalescence of a binary containing two [neutron stars](#neutron-star) can eject neutron-rich material and power a [kilonova](#kilonova). [Rapid neutron-capture process](physics.md#r-process) reactions in the ejecta produce heavy elements. This connects compact-binary dynamics to chemical enrichment.

#### Kilonova

↑ **Parent:** [Neutron star merger](#neutron-star-merger)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilonova)

An optical and infrared transient powered by radioactive decay in material ejected from a compact-object merger. Its evolving spectrum and light curve probe ejecta composition and [rapid neutron-capture process](physics.md#r-process) production.

### Synchronous fission model for binary formation

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

In this idealized model, a critically rotating spherical cloud splits into two equal-mass homologous spheres of radius $a/2$ that share the orbital frequency of their touching circular binary. Their combined orbital and spin inertia is $(1+\alpha)Ma^2/4$. Equating post-fission angular momentum to $\alpha M\sqrt{GMR_c}$ gives the displayed separation relation. A compact outcome $a<R_c$ requires $0<\alpha<1/3$. The model is an angular-momentum budget, not a hydrodynamic proof that stellar fission occurs.

#### Repeated fission hierarchy

↑ **Parent:** [Synchronous fission model for binary formation](#synchronous-fission-model-for-binary-formation)

If each daughter subsequently conserves its own spin while contracting at fixed outer separation, its next critical radius is $a/8$. A local repetition of the [synchronous fission model for binary formation](#synchronous-fission-model-for-binary-formation) gives the displayed inner-to-outer separation ratio. Continuous [tidal locking](planetary-science.md#tidal-locking) throughout the contraction would instead keep the spin frequency fixed and decrease its centrifugal-to-gravity ratio as the cube of its radius; the repeated fission would not follow. Spin-orbit torque assumptions must therefore be explicit.

### Circular-binary orbital angular momentum

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

For a [circular Kepler orbit](classical-mechanics.md#circular-kepler-orbit), neglecting stellar spin, the orbital [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum) of a [binary star](#binary-star) is $J=\mu a^2\Omega=\mu\sqrt{GMa}$, where $M$ is total mass, $\mu$ is the [reduced mass](classical-mechanics.md#reduced-mass), and $a$ is separation. [Kepler's third law](physics.md#kepler-s-third-law) also gives $J=G^{2/3}P^{1/3}M_1M_2/[(2\pi)^{1/3}M^{1/3}]$. Distances from the [center of mass](classical-mechanics.md#center-of-mass) are $aM_2/M$ and $aM_1/M$; adding their individual orbital [angular momenta](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum) proves the formula.

### Binary mass ratio

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

A binary [binary mass ratio](#binary-mass-ratio) is the quotient of its component masses. For mass-transfer stability, specify the donor-to-accretor convention $q=M_d/M_a$ explicitly, since the inverse convention also occurs. Mass-ratio reversal changes which component is more massive.

### Detached binary

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

A detached binary has both components lying inside their respective [Roche lobes](#roche-lobe), so neither currently transfers matter by [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow). A detached system may still exchange matter through a [stellar wind](#stellar-wind).

### Coeval binary population

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

A coeval binary has components formed at the same time. A population of such [binary stars](#binary-star) can still have a distribution of system ages. If component masses have [independence](random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) and the state at age $t$ is determined separately for each component, the states have [conditional independence](random-variable.md#conditional-independence) given $t$. If $g(t)$ and $w(t)$ are the conditional [probabilities](probability-theory.md#probability) of two states, the unconditional unordered pair fractions are $\mathbb E[g(t)^2]$ and $2\mathbb E[g(t)w(t)]$, not generally products of marginal state fractions.

#### Continuous-birth binary state fractions

↑ **Parent:** [Coeval binary population](#coeval-binary-population)

Suppose binary ages have uniform coordinate $y\in[0,1]$ and independently sampled component masses give disjoint conditional state fractions $g(y)=ay$ and $w(y)=by$. Integrating the conditional pair probabilities gives $P(GG)=a^2/3$, $P(GW)=2ab/3$, and $P(WW)=b^2/3$. The fraction with at least one $G$ is $a-a^2/3$, and the fraction of those systems with a second evolved component is $(a^2+2ab)/(3a-a^2)$. Components share their age, so their unconditional states are correlated even when their birth masses have [independence](random-variable.md#independent-random-variables).

#### Giant-pair fraction in an instantaneous stellar burst

↑ **Parent:** [Coeval binary population](#coeval-binary-population)

In an instantaneous burst all binary components have a fixed common age. If the initial component masses are independent, the [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) of two [red giants](#red-giant) is the square of the individual giant [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) at that age. This need not equal the age-averaged pair fraction in a continuously forming population.

#### Conditional evolved-companion fraction in a coeval binary population

↑ **Parent:** [Coeval binary population](#coeval-binary-population)

If $g(t)$ is the conditional giant fraction and $w(t)$ a second evolved-state fraction at common age $t$, independent component masses give the fraction of giant-containing systems with another evolved component as $\mathbb E[g^2+2gw]/\mathbb E[2g-g^2]$. Averaging must follow conditioning on age, rather than multiplying marginal state [probabilities](probability-theory.md#probability).

### Semidetached binary

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

A semidetached binary has one component filling its [Roche lobe](#roche-lobe) while the other remains inside its own lobe. [Algol binaries](#algol-binary) and most ordinary [cataclysmic variables](#cataclysmic-variable) are examples.

#### RT Lac

↑ **Parent:** [Semidetached binary](#semidetached-binary)

RT Lac is a named interacting binary used in studies of [binary star](#binary-star) evolution. Conservative-progenitor tests and [nonconservative mass transfer](#nonconservative-binary-mass-transfer) provide ways to interpret a stripped, mass-losing component.

### Eclipsing binary

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

An eclipsing binary has an orbital orientation that lets one star pass in front of the other as seen by the observer, producing periodic changes in received [radiative flux](astrophysics.md#radiative-flux).

#### Algol variable

↑ **Parent:** [Eclipsing binary](#eclipsing-binary)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algol_variable)

An Algol variable is an [eclipsing binary](#eclipsing-binary) with well-separated eclipse minima and relatively little variation between eclipses. This light-curve classification includes detached systems as well as interacting [Algol binaries](#algol-binary); it does not by itself imply [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow).

### Donor star

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

A donor star loses matter to its companion through [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow) or a [stellar wind](#stellar-wind).

### Mass-gaining star

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

A mass-gaining star accretes material from a companion. Its changing mass and composition can alter its [main sequence](#main-sequence) location and cause [stellar rejuvenation](#stellar-rejuvenation).

### Binary mass function

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_mass_function)

For a single-lined circular spectroscopic binary whose visible component has radial-velocity amplitude $K_1$ and orbital period $P$, the binary mass function is

$$
F=\frac{PK_1^3}{2\pi G}
=\frac{M_2^3\sin^3i}{(M_1+M_2)^2}.
$$

#### Isotropic binary inclination distribution

↑ **Parent:** [Binary mass function](#binary-mass-function)

Randomly oriented binary angular-momentum vectors are uniform in $\cos i$. After identifying $i$ with $\pi-i$, their normalized inclination density on $0\leq i\leq\pi/2$ is $p(i)=\sin i$.

### Recycled pulsar

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recycled_pulsar)

A recycled pulsar is a neutron star spun up by accretion from a binary companion. Accreted angular momentum can reduce its spin period to milliseconds, while donor-envelope removal often leaves a white-dwarf companion.

#### Binary millisecond pulsar

↑ **Parent:** [Recycled pulsar](#recycled-pulsar)

A binary millisecond pulsar is a rapidly rotating recycled neutron star that remains in a binary, commonly with the white-dwarf remnant of its donor.

### Wind mass transfer in a binary star

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

Wind mass transfer occurs when one star accretes part of a companion's stellar wind without the donor necessarily filling its Roche lobe.

#### Fast-wind accretion fraction in a circular binary

↑ **Parent:** [Wind mass transfer in a binary star](#wind-mass-transfer-in-a-binary-star)

For a fast isotropic cold wind, the [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) at a companion at separation $a$ is $(-\dot M_d)/(4\pi a^2v_w)$. The ballistic capture model gives $\dot M_a/(-\dot M_d)=G^2M_a^2/(a^2v_w^4)$. Fast relative to orbital motion also makes the capture radius small compared with the separation.

#### Wind Roche-lobe overflow

↑ **Parent:** [Wind mass transfer in a binary star](#wind-mass-transfer-in-a-binary-star)

Wind Roche-lobe overflow occurs when a slow dense wind nearly fills the donor's Roche lobe and is gravitationally focused through the inner Lagrange region. It can transfer a much larger fraction of the outflow than a uniform fast-wind Bondi--Hoyle estimate.

#### Tidally enhanced stellar wind

↑ **Parent:** [Wind mass transfer in a binary star](#wind-mass-transfer-in-a-binary-star)

A tidally enhanced stellar wind is a model in which a companion increases a giant's wind mass loss as the giant approaches its [Roche lobe](#roche-lobe). Removing envelope mass before contact can reduce or reverse the mass ratio and make subsequent [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow) more stable. This is one proposed route to wide systems with Algol-like reversed evolutionary ordering. [Van der Sluys's compact-binary study, section 5.7.1](https://www.astro.ru.nl/~sluys/Thesis/pdfs/vdSluys_thesis_screen.pdf) discusses the enhanced-wind scenario and its effect on mass-transfer stability.

#### Jeans-mode mass loss

↑ **Parent:** [Wind mass transfer in a binary star](#wind-mass-transfer-in-a-binary-star)

In Jeans-mode mass loss, an isotropic wind escapes from one binary component carrying that star's specific orbital angular momentum. If the companion accretes negligibly, a circular binary obeys $aM=\text{constant}$ and $PM^2=\text{constant}$.

#### Barium star

↑ **Parent:** [Wind mass transfer in a binary star](#wind-mass-transfer-in-a-binary-star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barium_star)

A barium star is a main-sequence or giant star enriched in carbon and slow-neutron-capture elements despite being too unevolved to have made them itself. It was polluted by an asymptotic-giant-branch companion that is now a white dwarf.

#### S-type star

↑ **Parent:** [Wind mass transfer in a binary star](#wind-mass-transfer-in-a-binary-star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-type_star)

An S-type star has strong zirconium-oxide bands and enhanced slow-neutron-capture elements. Intrinsic S-type stars are thermally pulsing asymptotic giants, while extrinsic S-type stars were polluted by a former asymptotic-giant-branch companion.

### Cataclysmic variable

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cataclysmic_variable)

A cataclysmic variable is a close binary in which a Roche-lobe-filling low-mass star transfers matter to a white dwarf. Angular-momentum loss drives long-term transfer, while unstable burning of accumulated hydrogen produces classical novae.

#### Nova-like variable

↑ **Parent:** [Cataclysmic variable](#cataclysmic-variable)

A [cataclysmic variable](#cataclysmic-variable) whose persistent luminous disk resembles a [dwarf nova](#dwarf-nova) in outburst. A sufficiently high transfer rate can keep its [accretion disk](astrophysics.md#accretion-disk) on the hot stable branch of the [hydrogen-ionization disk instability](astrophysics.md#hydrogen-ionization-disk-instability), avoiding ordinary dwarf-nova cycles.

#### Dwarf nova

↑ **Parent:** [Cataclysmic variable](#cataclysmic-variable)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_nova)

A [cataclysmic variable](#cataclysmic-variable) with recurrent disk outbursts associated with the [hydrogen-ionization disk instability](astrophysics.md#hydrogen-ionization-disk-instability). Enhanced [accretion](astrophysics.md#accretion) powers the bright phase; the event does not require [thermonuclear runaway](#thermonuclear-runaway) on the [white dwarf](#white-dwarf).

#### Nonmagnetic cataclysmic variable

↑ **Parent:** [Cataclysmic variable](#cataclysmic-variable)

A [cataclysmic variable](#cataclysmic-variable) whose [white dwarf](#white-dwarf) [magnetic field](electromagnetism.md#magnetic-field) does not dominate the large-scale [accretion](astrophysics.md#accretion) flow. It normally supports an [accretion disk](astrophysics.md#accretion-disk) fed through [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow). The term does not rule out a magnetized [donor star](#donor-star) or [magnetic braking of a binary star](#magnetic-braking-of-a-binary-star).

##### Accretion-disk stream-impact hot spot

↑ **Parent:** [Nonmagnetic cataclysmic variable](#nonmagnetic-cataclysmic-variable)

The bright region where the stream from [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow) strikes an [accretion disk](astrophysics.md#accretion-disk). Dissipation of relative [kinetic energy](classical-mechanics.md#kinetic-energy) produces a localized source of light whose visibility changes around the [orbital period](classical-mechanics.md#orbital-period).

#### Cataclysmic-variable orbital-period distribution

↑ **Parent:** [Cataclysmic variable](#cataclysmic-variable)

Ordinary hydrogen-rich [cataclysmic variables](#cataclysmic-variable) occur on both sides of a [cataclysmic-variable period gap](#cataclysmic-variable-period-gap) at approximately two to three hours, with a [cataclysmic-variable period minimum](#cataclysmic-variable-period-minimum) near 82 minutes. Selection effects change the observed proportions. [Knigge, Baraffe and Patterson's donor-based evolutionary study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.2440) summarizes these empirical landmarks.

##### Cataclysmic-variable period minimum

↑ **Parent:** [Cataclysmic-variable orbital-period distribution](#cataclysmic-variable-orbital-period-distribution)

For a [Roche lobe](#roche-lobe)-filling donor with local radius response $R_2\propto M_2^\zeta$, the [Roche-lobe-filling period-density relation](#roche-lobe-filling-period-density-relation) gives $P\propto M_2^{(3\zeta-1)/2}$. As mass is lost, the period falls for $\zeta>1/3$ and rises for $\zeta<1/3$, with a minimum at the transition. The reversal is called a period bounce. Slow evolution near the turning point produces an accumulation in the period distribution: for a steady flow of systems along one evolutionary branch, $N(P)\propto1/|\dot P|$.

###### Cataclysmic-variable period bounce

↑ **Parent:** [Cataclysmic-variable period minimum](#cataclysmic-variable-period-minimum)

The reversal from decreasing to increasing [orbital period](classical-mechanics.md#orbital-period) during continuing donor mass loss. The [Roche-lobe-filling period-density relation](#roche-lobe-filling-period-density-relation) gives $d\log P/d\log M_d=(3\zeta-1)/2$, where $\zeta$ is the effective [stellar radius response exponent](#stellar-radius-response-exponent) along the mass-losing sequence. A crossing from $\zeta>1/3$ to $\zeta<1/3$ gives a local [cataclysmic-variable period minimum](#cataclysmic-variable-period-minimum).

##### Cataclysmic-variable period gap

↑ **Parent:** [Cataclysmic-variable orbital-period distribution](#cataclysmic-variable-orbital-period-distribution)

The cataclysmic-variable period gap is the observed deficit of accreting, ordinary hydrogen-rich [cataclysmic variables](#cataclysmic-variable) with periods around two to three hours. The [disrupted magnetic braking model](#disrupted-magnetic-braking-model) interprets it as a detached phase after the donor contracts inside its [Roche lobe](#roche-lobe).

#### Magnetic braking of a binary star

↑ **Parent:** [Cataclysmic variable](#cataclysmic-variable)

A magnetized wind removes spin angular momentum from a cool donor star. Tidal synchronization replenishes that spin from the orbit, so the wind indirectly removes orbital angular momentum and drives the binary toward shorter separation.

##### Disrupted magnetic braking model

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic braking of a binary star](#magnetic-braking-of-a-binary-star)

In this model, strong [magnetic braking of a binary star](#magnetic-braking-of-a-binary-star) inflates the mass-losing donor above the [cataclysmic-variable period gap](#cataclysmic-variable-period-gap). A reduction in the torque near the [fully convective star](stellar-structure.md#fully-convective-star) transition allows thermal contraction and detachment. [Gravitational-wave emission from a binary system](#gravitational-wave-emission-from-a-binary-system) continues to shrink the orbit until contact returns below the gap. The model requires a braking decrease and a previously inflated donor, not disappearance of stellar magnetism. [Knigge's review](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.4716) describes this evolutionary picture and observational tests.

#### Isotropic re-emission from a binary star

↑ **Parent:** [Cataclysmic variable](#cataclysmic-variable)

In isotropic re-emission, transferred mass is expelled from the vicinity of the accretor and carries the accretor's specific orbital angular momentum. The total binary mass and orbital angular momentum then decrease even though the accretor retains no net mass.

### Supernova kick in a binary star

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

Asymmetric ejecta or neutrino emission can impart a natal kick to a compact remnant. The impulsive change in relative velocity and total mass can alter the binary's semimajor axis and eccentricity or unbind the system.

#### Kick-direction binary survival criterion

↑ **Parent:** [Supernova kick in a binary star](#supernova-kick-in-a-binary-star)

For an impulsive [supernova kick in a binary star](#supernova-kick-in-a-binary-star) initially in a [circular Kepler orbit](classical-mechanics.md#circular-kepler-orbit), let $\beta$ be the remaining total mass fraction and $\alpha$ the kick speed divided by the original relative speed. The displayed post-kick [specific orbital energy](classical-mechanics.md#specific-orbital-energy) is negative for a bound pair and positive for escape. Over all directions, the velocity factor ranges from $(1-\alpha)^2$ to $(1+\alpha)^2$. Some direction leaves a bound pair exactly when $2\beta>(1-\alpha)^2$; some direction permits positive-energy escape exactly when $2\beta<(1+\alpha)^2$. Equalities are marginal [parabolic Kepler orbits](classical-mechanics.md#parabolic-trajectory). The circular initial orbit and impulsive approximation are essential.

##### Asymptotic relative speed of a disrupted binary

↑ **Parent:** [Kick-direction binary survival criterion](#kick-direction-binary-survival-criterion)

A disrupted [binary star](#binary-star) with positive post-impulse [specific orbital energy](classical-mechanics.md#specific-orbital-energy) $\varepsilon'$ has relative speed $V=\sqrt{2\varepsilon'}$ at infinite separation, because the [Newtonian gravitational potential energy](classical-mechanics.md#newtonian-gravitational-potential-energy) tends to zero. With the notation of the [kick-direction binary survival criterion](#kick-direction-binary-survival-criterion), $V=v\sqrt{1+2\alpha\cos\psi+\alpha^2-2\beta}$. This is not a systemic recoil velocity. A marginal [parabolic Kepler orbit](classical-mechanics.md#parabolic-trajectory) has $V=0$ at infinity.

### Gravitational-wave emission from a binary system

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

An accelerating binary mass quadrupole emits gravitational waves. The resulting energy and angular-momentum loss shrinks and circularizes a detached compact binary, with the evolution accelerating sharply as its separation decreases.

#### Circular gravitational-wave inspiral

↑ **Parent:** [Gravitational-wave emission from a binary system](#gravitational-wave-emission-from-a-binary-system)

For fixed component [masses](classical-mechanics.md#mass), total [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) $M=M_1+M_2$ and [reduced mass](classical-mechanics.md#reduced-mass) $\mu=M_1M_2/M$, in the weak-field slow-motion [circular orbits](classical-mechanics.md#circular-orbit), $E=-GM\mu/(2a)$ and $J=\mu\sqrt{GMa}$ obey $dE/dJ=\Omega$. The [gravitational-wave energy and angular-momentum balance](general-relativity.md#gravitational-wave-energy-and-angular-momentum-balance) makes the secular loss tangent to this circular family. Substituting $v^2=GM/a$ into the [instantaneous quadrupole luminosity of a Kepler binary](general-relativity.md#instantaneous-quadrupole-luminosity-of-a-kepler-binary) and differentiating $E$ gives the displayed inspiral equation. Integration gives

$$
a^4=a_0^4-\frac{256G^3M^2\mu}{5c^5}(t-t_0),\qquad t_{\rm coal}-t_0=\frac{5c^5a_0^4}{256G^3M^2\mu}.
$$

The orbit is quasicircular, with small radial drift. The formal coalescence time is not a valid extension of the weak-field point-mass model into the final strong-field or stellar-contact phase.

### Equipotential stellar-structure approximation

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)

The equipotential stellar-structure approximation replaces distorted closed equipotentials in a synchronized binary by one-dimensional shells labeled by enclosed volume and mass.

#### Volume-equivalent radius

↑ **Parent:** [Equipotential stellar-structure approximation](#equipotential-stellar-structure-approximation)

The volume-equivalent radius of a closed surface enclosing volume $V$ is defined by $V=4\pi r'^3/3$.

### Roche lobe

↑ **Parent:** [Binary star](#binary-star)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roche_lobe)

A Roche lobe is the region around one component of a circular binary inside the critical effective-potential surface through the inner Lagrange point.

#### Roche-lobe-filling period-density relation

↑ **Parent:** [Roche lobe](#roche-lobe)

If $R_2=R_L\simeq Ca(M_2/M)^{1/3}$, [Kepler's third law](physics.md#kepler-s-third-law) gives

$$
P=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{GC^3}}\left(\frac{R_2^3}{M_2}\right)^{1/2}.
$$

Thus a [Roche lobe](#roche-lobe)-filling star's period measures its inverse square-root mean [mass density](fluid-mechanics.md#density), largely independently of the companion mass in this approximation. A local [mass-radius relation](exoplanet.md#mass-radius-relation) $R_2\propto M_2^\zeta$ gives $P\propto M_2^{(3\zeta-1)/2}$.

##### Roche-lobe period-mass relation for a linear donor radius law

↑ **Parent:** [Roche-lobe-filling period-density relation](#roche-lobe-filling-period-density-relation)

For contact with $R_d/R_\odot=M_d/M_\odot$ and $R_L/a=f(M_d/M)^{1/3}$, [Kepler's third law](physics.md#kepler-s-third-law) gives $P_0=2\pi\sqrt{R_\odot^3/(f^3GM_\odot)}$. The total binary mass cancels. The linear period relation depends on that particular equilibrium radius sequence; a donor with a different [stellar radius response exponent](#stellar-radius-response-exponent) follows a different mass-period relation.

##### Pre-giant Roche-lobe-filling period limit

↑ **Parent:** [Roche-lobe-filling period-density relation](#roche-lobe-filling-period-density-relation)

For $R_L=Ca(M_d/M)^{1/3}$, the period when a donor reaches radius $R$ is $P=2\pi\sqrt{R^3/(C^3GM_d)}$. Requiring overflow before a specified evolutionary radius therefore bounds the initial period independently of companion mass in this lobe approximation. If that radius scales as $M_d^{5/3}$, the bound scales as $M_d^2$.

###### Conservative pre-giant period-ratio minimum

↑ **Parent:** [Pre-giant Roche-lobe-filling period limit](#pre-giant-roche-lobe-filling-period-limit)

For fixed total solar-unit mass $m$ and conserved orbital [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum), [conservative binary mass transfer](#conservative-binary-mass-transfer) preserves $K=P[m_d(m-m_d)]^3$. Hence $P/m_d^2=K/[m_d^5(m-m_d)^3]$. Its logarithmic derivative is $-5/m_d+3/(m-m_d)$, vanishing at $5m/8$ with positive second derivative. The ratio diverges at both endpoints, so this is its global minimum. Comparing this minimum to a pre-giant bound $P_0$ tests whether any such conservative antecedent can overflow before the giant branch; satisfying it is necessary, not a proof of an entire evolutionary history.

#### Roche-lobe overflow

↑ **Parent:** [Roche lobe](#roche-lobe)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roche-lobe_overflow)

Roche-lobe overflow begins when a star fills its Roche lobe and matter flows through the inner Lagrange point toward its companion.

##### Nonconservative binary mass transfer

↑ **Parent:** [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow)

In nonconservative binary evolution, matter can escape the system and/or external processes change its orbital [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum). Escaping overflow, a [stellar wind](#stellar-wind) and a [common envelope](#common-envelope) can invalidate the fixed-total-mass and period-product invariants of [conservative mass transfer](#conservative-binary-mass-transfer).

###### Nuclear-driven Roche-overfill relaxation

↑ **Parent:** [Nonconservative binary mass transfer](#nonconservative-binary-mass-transfer)

Let $x=\log(R/R_L)$, $q=M_1/M_2$, $\dot M_2=-f\dot M_1=M_1x/t_{\rm dyn}$, and $d\log R/dt=n\dot M_1/M_1+t_{\rm nuc}^{-1}$. Under the [binary period invariant for a constant wind angular-momentum factor](#binary-period-invariant-for-a-constant-wind-angular-momentum-factor) and $R_L=0.46a(M_1/M)^{1/3}$, the restoring coefficient is $A(q)=n+5/3-2fq-2(3\lambda+1)(1-f)q/[3(1+q)]$. Locally freezing the masses gives an attracting overfill $x_*=ft_{\rm dyn}/[A(q)t_{\rm nuc}]$ precisely when $A(q)>0$. If $A$ is of order one and $t_{\rm dyn}\ll t_{\rm nuc}$, transfer is driven slowly by nuclear expansion. A negative $A$ gives positive feedback on the short overfill timescale; detailed outcomes then exceed this linear contact model.

###### Binary period invariant for a constant wind angular-momentum factor

↑ **Parent:** [Nonconservative binary mass transfer](#nonconservative-binary-mass-transfer)

If an escaping [stellar wind](#stellar-wind) carries specific [orbital angular momentum](quantum-mechanics.md#orbital-angular-momentum) $\lambda J/M$ with constant $\lambda$, then $\dot J/J=\lambda\dot M/M$ and $J\propto M^\lambda$. Combining this with [circular-binary orbital angular momentum](#circular-binary-orbital-angular-momentum) gives the displayed period invariant. It is a different prescription from [donor-wind angular-momentum loss](#donor-wind-angular-momentum-loss), whose specific angular momentum generally varies with mass ratio.

###### Donor-wind angular-momentum loss

↑ **Parent:** [Nonconservative binary mass transfer](#nonconservative-binary-mass-transfer)

An isotropic escaping [stellar wind](#stellar-wind) has the [donor star](#donor-star)'s mean specific orbital [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum). With $\dot M_d<0$, retained fraction $f$, negligible spin and no additional wind torque, $\dot J=(1-f)\dot M_da_d^2\Omega$. The retained matter redistributes [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum) internally. Unlike [isotropic re-emission from a binary star](#isotropic-re-emission-from-a-binary-star), the escaping material originates at the donor rather than the accretor. With no retention this reduces to [Jeans-mode mass loss](#jeans-mode-mass-loss).

###### Donor-wind period invariant with fixed retention fraction

↑ **Parent:** [Donor-wind angular-momentum loss](#donor-wind-angular-momentum-loss)

In a circular [binary star](#binary-star) let the [donor star](#donor-star) lose [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) $\dot M_1<0$, with constant retained fraction $f$ and $\dot M_2=-f\dot M_1$. A [donor star](#donor-star) [stellar wind](#stellar-wind) escaping with the [donor star](#donor-star)'s orbital specific [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum) has $\dot J/J=(1-f)\dot M_1M_2/[M_1(M_1+M_2)]$. Differentiating $J=G^{2/3}(P_{\rm orb}/2\pi)^{1/3}M_1M_2(M_1+M_2)^{-1/3}$ and using this loss law gives

$$
\frac{\dot P_{\rm orb}}{P_{\rm orb}}=-3f\frac{\dot M_1}{M_1}-3\frac{\dot M_2}{M_2}-2\frac{\dot M_1+\dot M_2}{M_1+M_2}.
$$

For constant $f$ this integrates to $P_{\rm orb}M_1^{3f}M_2^3(M_1+M_2)^2=\mathrm{constant}$. If $f$ varies, the differential relation remains valid but the first factor must be replaced by $\exp(3\int f\,d\log M_1)$; treating a time-dependent exponent as a fixed power introduces an extra $\dot f\log M_1$ term.

###### Donor-wind Roche-lobe response

↑ **Parent:** [Donor-wind angular-momentum loss](#donor-wind-angular-momentum-loss)

Under [donor-wind angular-momentum loss](#donor-wind-angular-momentum-loss) and the [Roche lobe](#roche-lobe) approximation $R_L=0.46a(M_d/M)^{1/3}$, the [Roche-lobe radius response exponent](#roche-lobe-radius-response-exponent) is affine in retained fraction: $\zeta_L=(1-f)\zeta_0+f\zeta_1$, where $q=M_d/M_a$, $\zeta_0=1/3-4q/[3(1+q)]$ and $\zeta_1=2q-5/3$. Differentiate $\log R_L=\log a+(\log M_d-\log M)/3+\mathrm{constant}$ and the [circular-binary orbital angular momentum](#circular-binary-orbital-angular-momentum) to obtain this expression.

###### Wind-driven Roche-lobe overflow

↑ **Parent:** [Donor-wind Roche-lobe response](#donor-wind-roche-lobe-response)

A fast escaping [Jeans-mode mass loss](#jeans-mode-mass-loss) wind can drive a giant into [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow) because its radius changes as it loses mass, even though the wind itself is not substantially captured. This differs from slow [Wind Roche-lobe overflow](#wind-roche-lobe-overflow). For $R_d\propto M_d^{-n}$ and $R_L\propto a(M_d/M)^{1/3}$, wind alone gives $d\log(R_d/R_L)/dt=\dot M[3(1-n)q-(1+3n)]/[3M_d(1+q)]$, where $q=M_d/M_a$ and $\dot M<0$ is the escaping wind rate. The displayed inequality is exactly the condition that overfill increases.

###### Contact transfer rate with Jeans-mode wind loss

↑ **Parent:** [Wind-driven Roche-lobe overflow](#wind-driven-roche-lobe-overflow)

Let [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow) transfer mass conservatively within a circular binary while a separate fast wind escapes with the donor's specific orbital [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum). Then $\dot M_d=\dot M-\dot M_a$ and $\dot a/a=-\dot M/M+2\dot M_a(1/M_d-1/M_a)$. Differentiating the donor and [Roche lobe](#roche-lobe) radii and enforcing contact gives the displayed rate. It is positive and stabilizing when the wind drives overfill and $6q<5-3n$. When the latter inequality is reversed, transfer increases the overfill, producing runaway rather than a steady negative-transfer solution.

###### Feasibility of donor-wind binary contact

↑ **Parent:** [Donor-wind Roche-lobe response](#donor-wind-roche-lobe-response)

A [donor star](#donor-star) with [stellar radius response exponent](#stellar-radius-response-exponent) $\zeta_*$ can maintain exact contact in the [donor-wind Roche-lobe response](#donor-wind-roche-lobe-response) model for some retained fraction $0\le f\le1$ precisely when $\zeta_*$ lies between $\zeta_0$ and $\zeta_1$. This follows because the attainable [Roche-lobe radius response exponents](#roche-lobe-radius-response-exponent) form that closed interval. If its endpoints coincide, all fractions work when $\zeta_*$ equals the common value and none work otherwise. With negative donor mass-loss rate, $d\log(R_d/R_L)/dt=(\zeta_*-\zeta_L)\dot M_d/M_d$ distinguishes detachment from increasing overfill. [Dynamical stability of binary mass transfer](#dynamical-stability-of-binary-mass-transfer) requires the adiabatic donor response.

###### Period invariant for constant-fraction donor-wind mass loss

↑ **Parent:** [Donor-wind angular-momentum loss](#donor-wind-angular-momentum-loss)

For a slowly evolving [circular orbit](classical-mechanics.md#circular-orbit) with constant retained fraction $f$ and [donor-wind angular-momentum loss](#donor-wind-angular-momentum-loss), logarithmic differentiation of the [circular-binary orbital angular momentum](#circular-binary-orbital-angular-momentum) gives $d\log P=-3f\,d\log M_d-3\,d\log M_a-2\,d\log(M_d+M_a)$. Integration gives the displayed invariant. At $f=1$ it becomes the [period-product invariant for conservative mass transfer](#period-product-invariant-for-conservative-mass-transfer), while at $f=0$ it becomes $PM^2=\mathrm{constant}$. A varying retained fraction does not give this simple power-law invariant.

##### Case B mass transfer

↑ **Parent:** [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow)

Case B mass transfer starts after core hydrogen exhaustion and before core helium ignition. It is subdivided by donor structure; [early case B mass transfer](#early-case-b-mass-transfer) occurs before a deep convective giant envelope develops.

###### Early case B mass transfer

↑ **Parent:** [Case B mass transfer](#case-b-mass-transfer)

Early case B overflow occurs in an early post-main-sequence donor, commonly crossing the Hertzsprung gap with a largely radiative envelope. A stabilizing mass-loss response can allow transfer at [binary mass ratios](#binary-mass-ratio) that would destabilize a convective giant. This is an evolutionary-stage classification, distinct from recombination terminology.

##### Case A mass transfer

↑ **Parent:** [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow)

Case A mass transfer starts while the [donor star](#donor-star) is on the [main sequence](#main-sequence), with core hydrogen burning. Its onset precedes giant-envelope formation; donor structure and the [binary mass ratio](#binary-mass-ratio) still determine stability.

##### Binary mass-transfer contact equation

↑ **Parent:** [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow)

For a [circular orbit](classical-mechanics.md#circular-orbit), fixed total [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass), transfer from $M_2$ to $M_1$, negligible spin and $R_L\propto a(M_2/M)^{1/3}$, the orbital [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum) $J=M_1M_2\sqrt{Ga/M}$ gives

$$
\frac{\dot R_L}{R_L}=2\frac{\dot J}{J}+\left(2q-\frac53\right)\frac{\dot M_2}{M_2},\qquad q=\frac{M_2}{M_1}.
$$

If the donor follows a radius sequence with [stellar radius response exponent](#stellar-radius-response-exponent) $\zeta$, has no additional radius change at fixed mass, and maintains contact, equating its radius derivative to this expression proves the displayed contact equation. Negative external $\dot J$ drives donor mass loss when $\zeta+5/3-2q>0$. The appropriate equilibrium or adiabatic response and its timescale must be specified separately; the equation alone is not a universal dynamical-stability criterion.

##### Conservative binary mass transfer

↑ **Parent:** [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow)

Conservative binary mass transfer moves matter between the components while preserving total binary mass and orbital angular momentum.

###### Period-product invariant for conservative mass transfer

↑ **Parent:** [Conservative binary mass transfer](#conservative-binary-mass-transfer)

Negligible spin and a circular orbit give $J=M_1M_2\sqrt{Ga/M}$. Fixed $M$ and $J$ imply $a(M_1M_2)^2$ is constant. [Kepler's third law](physics.md#kepler-s-third-law) then gives the period-product invariant $P(M_1M_2)^3$.

###### Minimum period-to-donor-mass ratio for conservative evolution

↑ **Parent:** [Period-product invariant for conservative mass transfer](#period-product-invariant-for-conservative-mass-transfer)

At fixed total mass $M$ and period-product invariant, $P/M_d^2$ is proportional to $[M_d^5(M-M_d)^3]^{-1}$. Its unique global minimum occurs at donor mass $5M/8$ and accretor mass $3M/8$. It is useful for testing all possible conservative progenitors against a pre-overflow period bound.

###### Dynamical stability of binary mass transfer

↑ **Parent:** [Conservative binary mass transfer](#conservative-binary-mass-transfer)

Binary mass transfer is dynamically stable when the donor's radius decreases relative to its Roche-lobe radius after a small mass loss, reducing overflow instead of amplifying it.

###### Conservative mass-transfer critical mass ratio

↑ **Parent:** [Dynamical stability of binary mass transfer](#dynamical-stability-of-binary-mass-transfer)

With fixed total mass and orbital [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum) and $R_L\propto a(M_d/M)^{1/3}$, the [Roche-lobe radius response exponent](#roche-lobe-radius-response-exponent) is $2q-5/3$. A donor with [stellar radius response exponent](#stellar-radius-response-exponent) $\zeta_*$ is stable to infinitesimal mass loss when $\zeta_*>2q-5/3$, giving $q<(\zeta_*+5/3)/2$. The threshold depends on the donor response.

###### Stellar radius response exponent

↑ **Parent:** [Dynamical stability of binary mass transfer](#dynamical-stability-of-binary-mass-transfer)

The stellar radius response exponent measures how a star's radius changes as it loses mass. The adiabatic exponent $\zeta_{\rm ad}$ describes loss too rapid for thermal adjustment; the equilibrium exponent $\zeta_{\rm eq}$ follows a sequence of thermally relaxed stars. Compare $\zeta_{\rm ad}$ with the [Roche-lobe radius response exponent](#roche-lobe-radius-response-exponent) to test dynamical stability.

###### Roche-lobe radius response exponent

↑ **Parent:** [Dynamical stability of binary mass transfer](#dynamical-stability-of-binary-mass-transfer)

For [conservative binary mass transfer](#conservative-binary-mass-transfer) and $R_L\propto a(M_d/M)^{1/3}$,

$$
\zeta_L=2q-\frac53,\qquad q=\frac{M_d}{M_a}.
$$

The [conservation of angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#conservation-of-angular-momentum) gives $d\log a/d\log M_d=-2(1-q)$. Since a mass loss has $d\log M_d<0$, the donor moves inside its lobe when $\zeta_{\rm ad}>\zeta_L$. An ideal fully convective donor with $\zeta_{\rm ad}=-1/3$ is therefore stable for $q<2/3$ and unstable for $q>2/3$ under these assumptions.

###### Roche-lobe overflow feedback with a constant wind torque factor

↑ **Parent:** [Roche-lobe radius response exponent](#roche-lobe-radius-response-exponent)

Let $q=M_1/M_2$, hold total binary mass fixed, use $R_L\propto a q^{3/10}$, and impose $d\log J=f\,d\log M_1$. The [circular-binary orbital angular momentum](#circular-binary-orbital-angular-momentum) then gives $d\log a/d\log M_1=2(q-1+f)$, whereas mass conservation gives $d\log q/d\log M_1=1+q$. Thus $\zeta_L=(23q-17+20f)/10$. If the donor obeys $R_1=KM_1^\beta$ with slowly varying $K$, loss of donor mass increases its overfill when $\zeta_L>\beta$, or $q>(17-20f+10\beta)/23$. This is a feedback criterion on the time scale of the assumed donor response, not automatically a dynamical instability for every choice of $\beta$. For $\beta=1/2$, the threshold is $(22-20f)/23$; if $f\geq11/10$, every positive mass ratio satisfies it.

<h5 id="white-dwarf-mass-orbital-period-relation">White-dwarf mass--orbital-period relation</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow)

Stable envelope stripping of a low-mass red giant leaves a helium white dwarf. Because the giant radius is determined mainly by its core mass and fixes its Roche-lobe radius at detachment, the final binary orbital period is tightly related to the white-dwarf mass.

##### Common envelope

↑ **Parent:** [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_envelope)

A common-envelope phase occurs when unstable binary interaction engulfs both stellar cores in one envelope. Orbital energy and angular momentum may eject the envelope, leaving a compact binary, or the cores may merge.

###### Common-envelope energy formalism

↑ **Parent:** [Common envelope](#common-envelope)

The common-envelope energy formalism equates a fraction $\alpha_{\rm CE}$ of the released orbital energy to the envelope binding energy, often written $E_{\rm bind}=GM_dM_{\rm env}/(\lambda R_d)$. It estimates the post-envelope separation but compresses uncertain hydrodynamics into $\alpha_{\rm CE}$ and $\lambda$.

###### Double-degenerate Type Ia supernova scenario

↑ **Parent:** [Common envelope](#common-envelope)

The double-degenerate scenario forms a close pair of carbon--oxygen white dwarfs, usually through mass transfer and one or more common-envelope phases. Gravitational radiation then shrinks the orbit until merger or unstable transfer triggers thermonuclear disruption.

##### Algol binary

↑ **Parent:** [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow)

An Algol binary is a [semidetached binary](#semidetached-binary) in which an evolved, less massive [donor star](#donor-star) fills its [Roche lobe](#roche-lobe) while its more massive [mass gainer](#mass-gaining-star) remains near the [main sequence](#main-sequence). [Binary mass-ratio reversal](#binary-mass-ratio-reversal) resolves the [Algol paradox](#algol-paradox). Eclipsing members commonly have [Algol variable](#algol-variable) light curves, but that observational class also includes detached systems.

###### Algol paradox

↑ **Parent:** [Algol binary](#algol-binary)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algol_paradox)

The Algol paradox is the apparently inconsistent evolutionary ordering in an [Algol binary](#algol-binary): the less massive star is more evolved, whereas higher-mass isolated [main sequence](#main-sequence) stars normally exhaust core fuel sooner. [Binary mass-ratio reversal](#binary-mass-ratio-reversal) resolves it by identifying the present donor as the initially more massive star.

###### Binary mass-ratio reversal

↑ **Parent:** [Algol binary](#algol-binary)

During binary mass-ratio reversal, an initially more massive [donor star](#donor-star) loses enough material that its companion becomes the more massive member. The stars retain their different prior nuclear evolution, resolving the [Algol paradox](#algol-paradox). [Conservative binary mass transfer](#conservative-binary-mass-transfer) shrinks the orbit before equal masses and widens it after reversal, because $a\propto(M_dM_a)^{-2}$ at fixed total mass and orbital [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum).

###### Algol mass-ratio stability limit

↑ **Parent:** [Algol binary](#algol-binary)

A simplified conservative model with a fully convective donor gives long-lived stable [Roche-lobe overflow](#roche-lobe-overflow) only for donor-to-accretor mass ratio $q<2/3$. This follows from the [Roche-lobe radius response exponent](#roche-lobe-radius-response-exponent) and the donor's adiabatic [stellar radius response exponent](#stellar-radius-response-exponent) $-1/3$. The boundary is model-dependent; radiative donors, evolved core structure and mass or angular-momentum loss change it. [Van Rensbergen and collaborators' Algol population study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2620) includes reported Algol mass ratios above $0.7$ and discusses observational uncertainties.
