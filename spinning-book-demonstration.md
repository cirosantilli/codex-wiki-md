# Spinning-book demonstration

↑ **Parent:** [Intermediate axis theorem](intermediate-axis-theorem.md)

Clemson's documented apparatus is a book held tightly closed with several rubber bands. Toss it freely with spin about each [principal axis](principal-axis.md) in turn: the stated visual readout is steady orientation for the smallest and largest [principal moments of inertia](principal-moment-of-inertia.md), but a flip before the catch for the intermediate one. The same book and the two extreme-axis throws provide comparison controls; the bands support the [rigid body](rigid-body-dynamics-split.md) approximation by restraining the pages. This is a documented qualitative demonstration, not a measurement of $\lambda_2$.[Spinning Book by Clemson University, physics lecture demonstrations, 1Q60.40: Purpose, Description and Equipment](spinning-book-by-clemson-university.md)

For a rectangular book with length $a$, width $b$ and thickness $c$, where $a>b>c$, a uniform [mass](mass.md) model gives [principal moments of inertia](principal-moment-of-inertia.md) $M(b^2+c^2)/12$, $M(a^2+c^2)/12$ and $M(a^2+b^2)/12$. The intermediate [principal axis](principal-axis.md) runs across the width through the [centre of mass](center-of-mass.md); the maximum is normal to the cover. A marked cover or end helps distinguish ordinary spin from reversal of a directed body axis.

Interpret only the interval after release and before the catch: hand contact applies [torque](torque.md), whereas [gravity](gravity.md) in a [uniform gravitational field](uniform-gravitational-field.md) has zero net [torque](torque.md) about the [centre of mass](center-of-mass.md) because $\int\mathbf r\,dm=0$. Air resistance, page motion, an imperfect release and short flight limit the approximation. A toss ending before the [perturbation](perturbation.md) grows need not reveal instability. Comparable initial [angular speeds](angular-speed.md) and flight durations would improve the comparison; these are suggested controls, not quantified controls reported by Clemson.
