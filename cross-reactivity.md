# Cross-reactivity

↑ **Parent:** [Antibody specificity](antibody-specificity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-reactivity)

[Cross-reactivity](cross-reactivity.md) occurs when an [antibody](antibody.md) also recognizes another [antigen](antigen.md) beyond the one against which it was raised. This may involve related features, but obvious overall similarity is not required. [Immunobiology, 5th edition, Appendix I, antiserum generation: cross-reactive antigens and absorption](immunobiology-the-immune-system-in-health-and-disease-5th-edition-by-charles-a-janeway-jr-and-others/appendix-i-immunologists-toolbox.md)

For an imagined example, a [paratope](paratope.md) contacts a small surface patch shared by two otherwise different [proteins](protein.md). Both can bind even though their remaining surfaces differ. Increasing [antibody](antibody.md) [concentration](concentration.md) can reveal weak binding that was previously undetectable. Conversely, an extra [Western blot](western-blot.md) band need not prove [cross-reactivity](cross-reactivity.md): the intended [protein](protein.md) may occur in multiple forms, or detection reagents may contribute background. The target-deleted and no-[primary antibody](primary-antibody.md) comparisons under [Western blot antibody specificity controls](western-blot-antibody-specificity-control.md) test different explanations.
