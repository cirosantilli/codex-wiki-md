# Bernstein falling-factorial identity

↑ **Parent:** [Bernstein polynomial](bernstein-polynomial.md)

At the sampling point $j/n$, the polynomial inside the [Bernstein polynomial](bernstein-polynomial.md) has value $n^{-m}j(j-1)\cdots(j-m+1)$. The [falling factorial](falling-factorial.md) identity $j^{\underline m}\binom nj=n^{\underline m}\binom{n-m}{j-m}$ and the [binomial theorem](binomial-theorem.md) prove the displayed formula. For fixed $m$, both the input and its scalar factor tend uniformly to $x^m$ as $n$ grows. The [supremum norm](supremum-norm.md) contraction of the [Bernstein polynomial](bernstein-polynomial.md) therefore gives $\|B_n(x^m)-x^m\|_\infty\le m(m-1)/n$ for $n\ge m$.
