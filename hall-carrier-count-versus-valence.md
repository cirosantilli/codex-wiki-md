# Hall carrier count versus valence

↑ **Parent:** [Hall coefficient](hall-coefficient.md)

In a one-carrier isotropic [Drude model](drude-model.md), $R_H=1/(nq)$ and an inferred [carrier density](carrier-density.md) is $n_H=1/(e|R_H|)$. Dividing by the atomic [number density](number-density.md) gives an effective Hall count per atom. A noninteger result need not mean a literal fractional count of [valence electrons](valence-electron.md): multiple [energy bands](energy-band.md), anisotropic [Fermi surfaces](fermi-surface.md) and different [charge carrier mobilities](charge-carrier-mobility.md) can change the [Hall coefficient](hall-coefficient.md). In a two-carrier isotropic approximation,

$$
R_H=\frac{p\mu_h^2-n\mu_e^2}{e(p\mu_h+n\mu_e)^2}.
$$

Its sign identifies the dominant Hall response rather than necessarily the most numerous [charge carrier](charge-carrier.md).
