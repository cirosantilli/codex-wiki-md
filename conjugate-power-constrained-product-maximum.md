# Conjugate-power constrained product maximum

↑ **Parent:** [Lagrange multiplier](lagrange-multiplier.md)

For [conjugate exponents](conjugate-exponents.md) $p,q>1$, the constraint set is [compact](compact-space.md), so the continuous product has a maximum. Boundary endpoints have product zero, while $(1,1)$ is feasible with product one. An interior maximum therefore satisfies the [Lagrange multiplier](lagrange-multiplier.md) equations $y=\lambda x^{p-1}$ and $x=\lambda y^{q-1}$. Multiplying by $x$ and $y$ respectively gives $x^p=y^q$, since the multiplier is nonzero. Substitution in the constraint makes both powers equal one. Thus the maximum is one, attained uniquely at $(1,1)$. The boundary check and compactness are what turn the necessary multiplier equations into a global conclusion.
