# Axial moment of inertia of a solid of revolution

↑ **Parent:** [Moment of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md)

A uniform solid with circular cross-sectional radius $r(z)$ can be sliced into thin [discs](topological-disc.md). A slice of thickness $dz$ has [mass](mass.md) $dm=\rho\pi r(z)^2dz$ and axial [moment of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md) $dI_z=\tfrac12r(z)^2dm$. Thus

$$
M=\rho\pi\int r(z)^2dz,\qquad I_z=\frac{\rho\pi}{2}\int r(z)^4dz.
$$

All slice axes coincide, so no [parallel axis theorem](parallel-axis-theorem.md) correction is needed. For a linearly tapered section of length $l$ with end radii $r_0,r_1$, direct [integration](integral-split.md) gives $M=\rho\pi l(r_0^2+r_0r_1+r_1^2)/3$ and $I_z=\rho\pi l(r_0^4+r_0^3r_1+r_0^2r_1^2+r_0r_1^3+r_1^4)/10$.
