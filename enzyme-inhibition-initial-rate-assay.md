# Enzyme inhibition initial-rate assay

↑ **Parent:** [Enzyme inhibitor](enzyme-inhibitor.md)

The protocol used in the [trypsin colorimetric assay](trypsin-colorimetric-assay.md) requires $40$–$60\%$ inhibition and rejects hazy or yellow [BAPNA](bapna.md) solution.[A6012, SPBAPN02.001, revised 22 December 1997, p. 3, Notes 1–2: target inhibition range and substrate-solution quality](enzymatic-assay-of-aprotinin-sigma-quality-control-test-procedure.md)

The [trypsin colorimetric assay](trypsin-colorimetric-assay.md) explains blank subtraction and conversion to a [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) using the [Beer-Lambert law](beer-lambert-law.md). This protocol is an activity comparison at one [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) level, not evidence that [aprotinin](aprotinin.md) obeys any particular curve above. To test the model, an experimental extension would vary both free inhibitor and free [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) [concentrations](concentration.md), estimate early linear [reaction rates](reaction-rate.md) before appreciable depletion, and fit the whole collection by [nonlinear regression](nonlinear-regression.md). Include a no-inhibitor series and inhibitor-matched blanks: inhibitor-dependent [absorbance](absorbance.md) drift must not be mistaken for changed [catalysis](catalysis.md).

There is a mathematical reason to collect more than one [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md). At $s=20\,\mathrm{\mu M}$ with the chosen uninhibited parameters, measuring $v=25\,\mathrm{\mu M\,min^{-1}}$ would imply only $\alpha+\alpha'=4$. Both $(\alpha,\alpha')=(2,2)$ and $(3,1)$ satisfy that observation, although their high-$s$ limits differ. A broader [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) range tests which model fits. Even a fitted curve leaves binding geometry unresolved. Check activity recovery after reducing inhibitor [concentration](concentration.md) separately; the simple equation also ceases to apply when free inhibitor is substantially depleted by binding or inhibitor exchange has not equilibrated during the measured interval.
