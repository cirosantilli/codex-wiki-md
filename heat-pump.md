# Heat pump

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md)

A heat pump uses [work](work.md) to deliver [heat](heat-split.md) to the region we want to keep warm. Its useful output is the hot-side delivery $Q_h$. The same operating machine can be described as a [refrigerator](refrigerator.md) when the cold-side extraction $Q_c$ is the objective: which output is useful changes the [coefficient of performance](coefficient-of-performance.md), while the [energy](energy-split.md) balance stays $Q_h=Q_c+W$. Heating a water bath by taking [heat](heat-split.md) from a colder water bath makes this distinction visible in the [heat-pump calorimetry](heat-pump-calorimetry.md) experiment below. [University of Tennessee, Refrigerators, air conditioners, and heat pumps: heating objective](https://labs.phys.utk.edu/mbreinig/phys136core/modules/m3/refrigerators.html)

In the illustrative budget from [refrigerator](refrigerator.md), supplying $1\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of [work](work.md) delivers $4\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of useful [heat](heat-split.md), giving a heating [coefficient of performance](coefficient-of-performance.md) of $4$. The additional $3\,\mathrm{kJ}$ came from the colder region. Compare an ideal electrical heater whose entire input [work](work.md) becomes [heat](heat-split.md) in the target region: it delivers $1\,\mathrm{kJ}$ for that input. The appeal of a [heat pump](heat-pump.md) is this ability to move existing [energy](energy-split.md), while the [Second law of thermodynamics](second-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) limits the [work](work.md) saving for a given pair of [temperatures](temperature-split.md).

One energy budget, two useful outputs, and Carnot's theorem bounds on the coefficient of performance**. Original quantitative diagram, with stipulated $Q_c=3\,\mathrm{kJ}$, $W=1\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and $Q_h=4\,\mathrm{kJ}$. Arrow widths follow the $3:1:4$ transfer magnitudes. The curves are reversible bounds for fixed cold [temperature](temperature-split.md) $T_c=280\,\mathrm K$ and varying hot [temperature](temperature-split.md) $T_h=T_c+\Delta T$; they are not measured performance. At $\Delta T=40\,\mathrm K$, the bounds are $7$ for cooling and $8$ for heating, above the illustrative machine's $3$ and $4$.
