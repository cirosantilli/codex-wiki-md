# Endosymbiotic theory

↑ **Parent:** [Endosymbiosis](endosymbiosis.md)

[Endosymbiotic theory](endosymbiotic-theory.md) explains the bacterial origins of [mitochondria](mitochondria.md) and [plastids](plastid.md) through enduring internal associations and subsequent integration. Survival inside a [biological cell](biological-cell.md) is only part of the problem: exchange through [membrane transport](membrane-transport.md), inherited division, [gene expression](gene-expression.md) and delivery of required [proteins](protein.md) must work together. The elegance is evolutionary reuse of an existing cellular capability, followed by changes that make the partners operate as one system. [Abstract and “How to tame your symbiont”: metabolic, genetic and division integration](origin-and-evolution-of-plastids-and-photosynthesis-in-eukaryotes-by-geoffrey-i-mcfadden.md)

The historical [On the origin of mitosing cells by Lynn Sagan](on-the-origin-of-mitosing-cells-by-lynn-sagan.md) (1967, abstract and Introduction, pp. 225–226) synthesized a symbiotic account of [eukaryotic](eukaryote.md) origins and proposed tests using molecular evidence. Its Introduction explicitly acknowledged earlier proposals. It also hypothesized origins for other structures; the modern evidence for [mitochondria](mitochondria.md) and [plastids](plastid.md) should be assessed separately from the rest of that historical scheme.
