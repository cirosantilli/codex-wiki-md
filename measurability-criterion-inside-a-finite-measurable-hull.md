# Measurability criterion inside a finite measurable hull

↑ **Parent:** [Regular outer measure](regular-outer-measure.md)

For a [regular outer measure](regular-outer-measure.md) and measurable $M$, the forward implication is [countable additivity](countable-additivity.md). For the reverse, choose a measurable hull of $A$ and intersect it with $M$ to obtain $A\subseteq B\subseteq M$ and $\mu(B)=\mu^*(A)$. [Measurability](measurability.md) of $B$ splits $M\setminus A$ into $M\setminus B$ and $B\setminus A$, so

$$
\mu^*(M\setminus A)=\mu(M\setminus B)+\mu^*(B\setminus A).
$$

The assumed equality and finiteness force $\mu^*(B\setminus A)=0$. Every outer-[measure](measure-split.md)-null [set](set-split.md) is measurable by [Caratheodory measurability criterion](caratheodory-s-criterion.md), so $A=B\setminus(B\setminus A)$ is measurable. Finiteness prevents invalid subtraction of infinite masses.
