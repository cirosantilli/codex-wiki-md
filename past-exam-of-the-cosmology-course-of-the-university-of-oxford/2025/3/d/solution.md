<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The $z\simeq0.3$ sample is expected to show the stronger late [Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect](../../../../integrated-sachs-wolfe-effect.md) correlation, for comparable [galaxy bias](../../../../galaxy-bias.md) and selection quality. In the stated flat [Lambda-CDM model](../../../../lambda-cdm-model.md), neglecting [radiation in cosmology](../../../../radiation-in-cosmology.md),

$$
\Omega_m(z)=\frac{0.3(1+z)^3}{0.3(1+z)^3+0.7},\qquad
\Omega_m(0.3)\simeq0.485,\qquad \Omega_m(4)\simeq0.982.
$$

At $z\simeq4$ the universe is nearly [matter-dominated universe](../../../../matter-domination.md), $D\propto a$ and $f\simeq1$, so the [cosmological Newtonian potential](../../../../cosmological-newtonian-potential.md) $\psi\propto D/a$ is nearly constant. A [photon](../../../../photon.md) then receives little net [Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect](../../../../integrated-sachs-wolfe-effect.md) shift. At $z\simeq0.3$, [dark energy](../../../../dark-energy.md) has substantially suppressed the [linear growth factor](../../../../linear-growth-factor.md) relative to $a$, so $f<1$ and the potentials decay appreciably.

For a quantitative indication, the approximation $f\simeq\Omega_m(z)^{0.55}$ gives $1-f\simeq0.328$ at $z=0.3$ and $1-f\simeq0.0101$ at $z=4$. Thus fractional potential evolution, $d\ln|\psi|/d\ln a=f-1$, is much larger for the nearby sample. The high-[cosmological redshift](../../../../cosmological-redshift.md) [matter power spectrum](../../../../matter-power-spectrum.md) also has a smaller growth amplitude. The entire correlation still depends on $H$, [galaxy bias](../../../../galaxy-bias.md), $p(z)$, and the [matter power spectrum](../../../../matter-power-spectrum.md) at the sampled wavenumber; redshift alone does not fix a universal numerical ratio or guarantee an ordering at every multipole. The robust expectation is

$$
\boxed{\text{the low-redshift sample is the better tracer of the late integrated Sachs-Wolfe signal.}}
$$
