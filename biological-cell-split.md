# Biological cell

↑ **Parent:** [Biology](biology-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology))

A [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md) is a membrane-bounded basic unit of living organization. In a cell-level experiment it can be an [experimental unit](experimental-unit.md), provided that treatment assignment and relevant interactions are handled at that level.

The shared architecture of cellular life combines a selective [cell membrane](cell-membrane-split.md), internal [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md), inherited information in [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md), and [ribosomes](ribosome.md) that assemble [proteins](protein-split.md). [Metabolism](metabolism.md) supplies transformations of matter and [energy](energy-split.md) needed for maintenance and growth. These components form a coupled system: a boundary retains useful [molecules](molecule-split.md), [enzymes](enzyme-split.md) organize [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction-split.md), and inherited information supports rebuilding the machinery. See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e: prokaryotic cells](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-2-prokaryotic-cells).

A worked structural comparison: in a typical [prokaryote](prokaryote.md), [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) occupies a region without a surrounding [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md); in a typical nucleated [eukaryote](eukaryote.md), much of the [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) lies inside a membrane-bounded [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md). Both still have a [cell membrane](cell-membrane-split.md), [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md) and [ribosomes](ribosome.md). The extra boundary compartmentalizes processes; it does not create a different basic principle of living organization. See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e: eukaryotic cells](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells).

![](cell-compartments.png)

**[Figure 1](#image-shared-cellular-architecture-with-different-dna-compartmentalization). Shared cellular architecture with different DNA compartmentalization**. Original simplified schematics of a [prokaryote](prokaryote.md) and a nucleated [eukaryote](eukaryote.md). Colors identify corresponding components; sizes and component counts are illustrative, and additional structures are omitted.

Shared architecture describes cellular life and its developmental machinery, rather than requiring every specialized [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md) to retain every component. A mature mammalian [red blood cell](red-blood-cell.md), for example, lacks a [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md), although it develops from nucleated precursors. See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e: components of blood](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/40-2-components-of-the-blood). This distinction makes cellular unity compatible with striking specialization.
