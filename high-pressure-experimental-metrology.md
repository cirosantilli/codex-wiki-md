# High-pressure experimental metrology

↑ **Parent:** [Pressure measurement](pressure-measurement.md)

High-pressure experimental [metrology](metrology.md) connects three tasks: producing [pressure](pressure-split.md), retaining it without leakage, and assigning a calibrated value to the [pressure](pressure-split.md) experienced by a sample. A large applied [force](force-split.md) alone establishes none of the last two. The useful result is a reproducible material property at specified [pressure](pressure-split.md), [temperature](temperature-split.md) and [stress](stress.md) conditions, with stated [measurement uncertainty](measurement-uncertainty.md).

[Percy Williams Bridgman](percy-williams-bridgman.md) identified leakage as an earlier experimental limit. His 1946 lecture describes packing that tightens as [pressure](pressure-split.md) rises, ultimately leaving vessel strength as a limit.[General survey of certain results in the field of high-pressure physics, delivered 11 December 1946, p. 53 opening and Figure 1 caption](general-survey-of-certain-results-in-the-field-of-high-pressure-physics-by-percy-williams-bridgman.md)

The [Bridgman unsupported-area seal](bridgman-unsupported-area-seal.md) turns the very [pressure](pressure-split.md) that would drive a leak into an extra seating [force](force-split.md). Its appeal is a feedback between load and sealing, rather than an assumption of perfectly fitted rigid surfaces. The [piston-cylinder pressure apparatus](piston-cylinder-pressure-apparatus.md) below explains that feedback; [pressure calibration](pressure-calibration.md) then separates the applied load from the sample's inferred [pressure](pressure-split.md).
