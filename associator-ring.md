# Associator ring

↑ **Parent:** [Ring](ring.md)

An associator ring is a [ring](ring.md) in which equality of the [principal ideals](principal-ideal.md) generated by two elements forces them to be [associate elements](associate-element.md): $(a)=(b)$ implies $a=ub$ for a [unit in a ring](unit-in-a-ring.md) $u$. The reverse implication always holds, since $a=ub$ and $b=u^{-1}a$ give both [ideal](ideal.md) inclusions. The property separates equality of generated [ideals](ideal.md) from equality of generators up to an invertible factor.

Every [integral domain](integral-domain.md) is an associator ring. If $a\ne0$ and $(a)=(b)$, write $a=rb$ and $b=sa$. Then $(1-rs)a=0$, so cancellation gives $rs=1$ and $a=rb$ with $r$ a [unit](unit-in-a-ring.md). The zero case is immediate. The converse fails: $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ has generator classes $\{0\}$, $\{2\}$ and $\{1,3\}$ for its three [ideals](ideal.md), and $1,3$ differ by a [unit](unit-in-a-ring.md), although $2$ is a nonzero [zero divisor](zero-divisor.md).

Finite [direct product of rings](direct-product-of-rings.md) preserve this property. Equality of [principal ideals](principal-ideal.md) is coordinatewise, and a tuple of [units](unit-in-a-ring.md) is a [unit](unit-in-a-ring.md). Every [quotient ring](quotient-ring.md) of a [principal ideal domain](principal-ideal-domain.md) is also an associator ring: first, modulo a prime power $p^k$, every nonzero class is a power $p^e$, $e<k$, times a [unit](unit-in-a-ring.md), and distinct powers generate distinct [ideals](ideal.md). Next factor a nonzero nonunit modulus into prime powers and apply the [Chinese remainder theorem](chinese-remainder-theorem.md). The zero modulus gives the original [integral domain](integral-domain.md), while a unit modulus gives the [zero ring](zero-ring.md).
