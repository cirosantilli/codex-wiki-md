# Discharge spectra and the Bohr model

↑ **Parent:** [Bohr model](bohr-model.md)

In [On the Constitution of Atoms and Molecules, Part I](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786441308634955) (1913), pp. 8–9, [Niels Bohr](niels-bohr.md) interprets existing [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) discharge and astronomical spectra. Electrical discharge in a vacuum tube removes [electrons](electron.md); recombination supplies [spectral lines](spectral-line.md). This is a theoretical interpretation, without a reported [spectrograph](spectrograph.md) of his own.[Part I, section 2, pp. 8–9: existing spectra](on-the-constitution-of-atoms-and-molecules-part-i-by-niels-bohr.md)

An actual optical obstacle appears in [Theodore Lyman](theodore-lyman.md)'s measurements: [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) discharge light entered an evacuated [spectrograph](spectrograph.md) directly through a slit, avoiding a transmitting window. A concave reflection [diffraction grating](diffraction-grating.md) dispersed the light onto photographic film. Pumping preserved the vacuum; a second slit supplied a known spectral displacement for [wavelength](wavelength.md) calibration. Gas contamination and vacuum leaks limited the measurement. This apparatus resolved ultraviolet [spectral lines](spectral-line.md); it did not image [orbits](orbit-dynamical-system.md).[Paul Horowitz, Theodore Lyman, Pioneer of Ultraviolet Vacuum Spectroscopy, pp. 3–4, figures 2–5: windowless discharge connection, grating, film, pumping and dual-slit calibration](theodore-lyman-pioneer-of-ultraviolet-vacuum-spectroscopy-by-paul-horowitz.md)

For a separate original calibration exercise, suppose an idealized [spectrograph](spectrograph.md) returns synthetic vacuum line positions $x_n=\lambda_{n\to2}+b$ for $n=3,4,5,6$, with chosen additive offset $b=0.200\,\mathrm{nm}$. Assume its dispersion scale is already known and only this offset is uncertain. Compare the finite-[mass](mass.md) prediction with the fixed-nucleus prediction $\lambda_n^{(\infty)}=(\mu/m_e)\lambda_{n\to2}$. Calibrate each model using only the $3\to2$ line: $\widehat b=x_3-\lambda_3^{\rm model}$. Then hold that offset fixed and test the other lines with residuals

$$
d_n=x_n-\widehat b-\lambda_n^{\rm model}.
$$

For the finite-[mass](mass.md) model every $d_n=0$. The fixed-nucleus model has $\widehat b=0.55733\,\mathrm{nm}$ and residuals $d_4=-0.09264\,\mathrm{nm}$, $d_5=-0.12100\,\mathrm{nm}$, $d_6=-0.13400\,\mathrm{nm}$. Algebraically $d_n=(1-\mu/m_e)(\lambda_n-\lambda_3)$: one additive calibration cannot absorb a multiplicative change of the [wavelengths](wavelength.md). Allowing an unknown multiplicative dispersion factor as well would make the two models degenerate in this exercise, so independent scale calibration matters.
