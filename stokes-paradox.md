# Stokes' paradox

↑ **Parent:** [Stokes flow](stokes-flow-split.md)

The unbounded two-dimensional [Stokes flow](stokes-flow-split.md) past a no-slip circular cylinder has no solution approaching a nonzero uniform stream. With radius and speed scaled to one, the forced angular mode has [stream function](stream-function.md) $\psi=f(r)\sin\theta$. The [biharmonic equation](biharmonic-equation.md) gives $f=Ar^3+Br\log r+Cr+D/r$. Uniform flow at infinity forces $A=B=0$, $C=1$; impermeability forces $D=-1$, but then $f'(1)=2$ violates no slip. The failure belongs to a nonuniform small-[Reynolds number](reynolds-number.md) limit. In a distant outer region inertia must be retained through an [Oseen approximation](oseen-approximation.md) or the full [Navier-Stokes equations](navier-stokes-equation.md), matched to an inner Stokes region. A finite outer wall changes the problem and removes this particular infinity obstruction.
