# Finite-evaluation inner product on polynomials

↑ **Parent:** [Inner product](inner-product-split.md)

At distinct real nodes $x_0,\ldots,x_n$ with positive weights $w_j$, the displayed expression is an [inner product](inner-product-split.md) on the real [vector space](vector-space-split.md) of [polynomials](polynomial-split.md) of degree at most $n$. Symmetry and bilinearity follow from finite sums. If its squared [norm](norm.md) vanishes, positivity of every weight forces $p(x_j)=0$ at every node. The root bound for a nonzero [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) then forces $p=0$, proving positive definiteness.

More generally, on any [vector subspace](vector-subspace.md) of real [functions](function-split.md), the evaluation form is an [inner product](inner-product-split.md) exactly when the evaluation map $p\mapsto(\sqrt{w_j}p(x_j))_j$ is injective. On [polynomials](polynomial-split.md) of degree at most $n+1$ it fails: the nonzero node [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) $\prod_j(x-x_j)$ vanishes at all nodes. This gives a positive semidefinite form with a nontrivial kernel. On the smaller polynomial space, the [Lagrange interpolation polynomial](lagrange-polynomial.md) identifies evaluation coordinates with $\mathbb R^{n+1}$, and [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) follows either from the Euclidean inequality in those coordinates or from minimizing $\|p-tq\|^2$ over real $t$.
