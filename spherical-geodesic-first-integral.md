# Spherical-geodesic first integral

↑ **Parent:** [Clairaut first integral for a surface of revolution](clairaut-first-integral-for-a-surface-of-revolution.md)

For a [sphere](sphere.md) with metric $a^2(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2)$, physical [arc length](arc-length.md) $s$ gives $h=\sin^2\theta\,d\phi/ds$. The [cyclic coordinate](cyclic-coordinate.md) $\phi$ proves conservation of $a^2h$, while unit-speed normalization gives

$$
\left(\frac{d\theta}{d\phi}\right)^2=\frac{\sin^4\theta}{a^2h^2}-\sin^2\theta.
$$

This azimuthal form requires $h\ne0$. The solutions are [great circles](great-circle.md); meridians have $h=0$ and require another parameter.
