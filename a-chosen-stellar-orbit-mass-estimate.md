# A chosen stellar-orbit mass estimate

↑ **Parent:** [Galactic-centre point-mass orbit model](galactic-centre-point-mass-orbit-model.md)

Choose $D=7.5\,\mathrm{kpc}$, $\alpha=0.110$ [arcseconds](arcsecond.md), $P=18$ years, $e=0.70$ and $i=55$ degrees. The small-angle conversion gives **$a=825\,\mathrm{AU}$**. Taking one model year as $365.25$ [days](day.md) gives $\mu\simeq2.30\times10^{26}\,\mathrm{m^3s^{-2}}$ and $K\simeq1566\,\mathrm{km/s}$.

Dividing by the [nominal solar mass parameter](nominal-solar-mass-parameter.md), $(GM)_\odot^{\rm N}=1.3271244\times10^{20}\,\mathrm{m^3s^{-2}}$, gives **$1.73\times10^6$ nominal solar masses**. The nominal parameter is an exact conversion convention, rather than an assertion that the [Sun](sun.md)'s measured [mass](mass.md) is exact.[PDF pp.2–3, exact nominal solar mass parameter and conversion recommendation](resolution-b3-on-recommended-nominal-conversion-constants-for-selected-solar-and-planetary-properties-by-international-astronomical-union.md)

The chosen [periapsis](periapsis.md) radius is $r_p=a(1-e)=247.5\,\mathrm{AU}$. Its corresponding [Schwarzschild radius](schwarzschild-radius.md), $r_s=2\mu/c^2$, is about $0.0342\,\mathrm{AU}$. Thus this illustrative orbit remains roughly $7200$ Schwarzschild radii from the centre at closest approach. Inferring a very concentrated mass from such an orbit is different from directly resolving its [event horizon](event-horizon.md). Every number here follows from the chosen orbit, not from a fit to the actual Galactic-centre measurements.
