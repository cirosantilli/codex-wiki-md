# Mass spectrometry

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrometry)

[Mass spectrometry](mass-spectrometry.md) separates and detects [ions](ion.md) according to [mass-to-charge ratio](mass-to-charge-ratio.md). It can distinguish [isotopes](isotope.md) which belong to the same [chemical element](chemical-element.md) but have different [atomic masses](atomic-mass.md). The instrument measures [ions](ion.md): interpreting the result as a neutral [atomic mass](atomic-mass.md) requires accounting for [Electron](electron.md) removal and its [ionization energy](ionization-energy.md). For a singly positive [ion](ion.md) in its [ground state](ground-state.md), $m_+=m_{\rm a}-m_e+I/c^2$, where $m_e$ is the [Electron](electron.md) [rest mass](invariant-mass.md), $I$ is the [ionization energy](ionization-energy.md), and $c$ is the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md).

For a simple illustrative analyzer, positive [ions](ion.md) of [mass](mass.md) $m$ and [electric charge](electric-charge.md) $q=ze$ gain [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) $qV=mv^2/2$ across an [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $V$ before entering a transverse uniform [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) $B$. The [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md) supplies [centripetal acceleration](centripetal-acceleration.md), so

$$
qvB=\frac{mv^2}{r},\qquad\frac{m}{q}=\frac{B^2r^2}{2V}.
$$

At fixed $V$ and $B$, the measured bending radius $r$ separates [mass-to-charge ratios](mass-to-charge-ratio.md), not [masses](mass.md) independently of [electric charge](electric-charge.md). This ideal model assumes negligible initial [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and speeds small compared with the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md); the historical focusing [mass spectrograph](mass-spectrograph.md) used the geometry described below.
