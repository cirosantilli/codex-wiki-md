# Gravitational acceleration

↑ **Parent:** [Newtonian gravitational field](newtonian-gravitational-field.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_acceleration)

Gravitational acceleration is the [acceleration](acceleration.md) of a [test particle](test-particle.md) in [free fall](free-fall.md) in a [Newtonian gravitational field](newtonian-gravitational-field.md). It is a vector pointing along that field; $g$ commonly denotes its positive magnitude. Near Earth's surface, the magnitude is approximately constant over shallow vertical scales. For a fixed spherical source it instead varies as $g(r)=\mu/r^2$, where $\mu$ is the [standard gravitational parameter](standard-gravitational-parameter.md).

This is distinct from [gravitational weight](weight-physics.md), the [force](force-split.md) $m\mathbf g$, and from [apparent weight](apparent-weight.md), the support force a scale measures. An orbiting body can have substantial [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) while its [apparent weight](apparent-weight.md) is zero.
