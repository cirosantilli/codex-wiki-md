# Kelvin equation

↑ **Parent:** [Phase coexistence curve](phase-coexistence-curve.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin_equation)

In the ideal-vapor, incompressible-liquid capillarity approximation, a spherical droplet of radius $R$ has the displayed equilibrium vapor-pressure relation. Here $v_l$ is liquid volume per molecule, $\Gamma$ is [surface tension](surface-tension-split.md), and $p_{\rm sat}$ is the flat-interface saturation pressure at the same [temperature](temperature-split.md). The [Young–Laplace equation](young-laplace-equation.md) raises liquid pressure by $2\Gamma/R$, increasing its [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) by approximately $2\Gamma v_l/R$. Matching this with the ideal-vapor chemical-potential increase $k_BT\ln(p/p_{\rm sat})$ proves the formula. The liquid contribution from the vapor-pressure change itself is neglected relative to its curvature pressure, and bulk properties are taken independent of radius. A convex small drop therefore requires a higher ambient vapor pressure than a flat interface.
