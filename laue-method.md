# Laue method

↑ **Parent:** [X-ray diffraction](x-ray-diffraction.md)

In the [Laue method](laue-method.md), a fixed single [crystal structure](crystal-structure.md) receives a collimated beam containing many [wavelengths](wavelength.md). Each [reciprocal lattice](reciprocal-lattice.md) vector can select its own [wavelength](wavelength.md); waves with different [wavelengths](wavelength.md) need not interfere with one another. For $\mathbf G\ne0$, the [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md) constraint gives the particularly useful test

$$
\lambda_{\mathbf G}=-\frac{4\pi\,\mathbf s_{\rm in}\cdot\mathbf G}{|\mathbf G|^2}.
$$

A candidate reflection requires $\lambda_{\mathbf G}>0$ within the source spectrum and an outgoing ray that reaches the detector. Varying [wavelength](wavelength.md) changes the radius and center of the [Ewald sphere](ewald-sphere.md) while leaving the [reciprocal lattice](reciprocal-lattice.md) fixed. A [monochromatic wave](monochromatic-wave.md) instead fixes the radius: rotating the [crystal structure](crystal-structure.md) or varying the incident [wavelength](wavelength.md) in a scan finds reflections sequentially. A white-beam photograph integrates many spectral selections in one exposure; it is not an angular scan at one known [wavelength](wavelength.md).

The upper ray slopes are schematic; the lower coordinates are calculated.

The [Laue method](laue-method.md) is useful for orienting a single [crystal structure](crystal-structure.md): tilting it changes which [reciprocal lattice](reciprocal-lattice.md) vectors meet the spectral and detector conditions. Absolute scale has an ambiguity if the [wavelengths](wavelength.md) are unknown. Scaling all direct [lattice translations](lattice-translation.md) by $c>0$ and all selected [wavelengths](wavelength.md) by $c$ leaves every outgoing direction unchanged. Independently known spectral information or length calibration is needed to remove this ambiguity.
