# Second-harmonic generation

↑ **Parent:** [Nonlinear optics](nonlinear-optics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-harmonic_generation)

[Second-harmonic generation](second-harmonic-generation.md) converts an incident optical [frequency](frequency.md) $\nu$ into $2\nu$ through a material's nonlinear response. In the [photon](photon.md) description of an ideal lossless conversion, two input [photons](photon.md) supply one output [photon](photon.md):

$$
2h\nu=h(2\nu),\qquad \omega=2\pi\nu.
$$

Here $h$ is the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md). The relation states [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md); the material mediates the interaction. It does not say that each input [photon](photon.md) independently splits its [wavelength](wavelength.md) in half.

For [vacuum](vacuum.md) [wavelengths](wavelength.md), $\lambda_{0,\omega}=c/\nu$ and $\lambda_{0,2\omega}=c/(2\nu)$, where $c$ is the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). Hence

$$
\lambda_{0,2\omega}=\frac{\lambda_{0,\omega}}{2},\qquad
\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{med},2\omega}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{med},\omega}}=\frac{n_\omega}{2n_{2\omega}}.
$$

The second relation uses the [refractive index](refractive-index.md) for each propagating mode and [frequency](frequency.md). Within a medium with [optical dispersion](dispersion-optics.md), the [wavelength](wavelength.md) need not halve. For an invented example, $\lambda_{0,\omega}=800\,\mathrm{nm}$, $n_\omega=1.5$, and $n_{2\omega}=1.6$ give $400\,\mathrm{nm}$ in [vacuum](vacuum.md), but in the material the two [wavelengths](wavelength.md) are $533.33\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $250\,\mathrm{nm}$; their ratio is $0.46875$.
