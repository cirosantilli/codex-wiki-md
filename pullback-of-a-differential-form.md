# Pullback of a differential form

↑ **Parent:** [Differential form](differential-form-split.md)

A smooth map $\phi:M\to N$ pulls a [differential form](differential-form-split.md) on $N$ back to one on $M$ by applying its [differential](differential-of-a-smooth-map.md) $d\phi$ to each [tangent vector](tangent-vector-split.md) argument. In particular, a parametrization of a [curve](curve.md) pulls a [one-form](one-form.md) back to an ordinary integrand on an interval.

For $\alpha=x\,dy$ and the [parabola](parabola.md) $\phi(t)=(t,t^2)$, $0\leq t\leq1$, substitute $x=t$ and $dy=2t\,dt$ to obtain

$$
\phi^*\alpha=2t^2\,dt,\qquad \int_\phi\alpha=\int_0^1 2t^2\,dt=\boxed{\frac23}.
$$

The same endpoints gave $0$ and $1$ along the two square-edge paths in the introduction, so this third answer is consistent with path dependence. By contrast, $\phi^*d(xy)=d(t^3)=3t^2\,dt$ gives integral $1$, the same endpoint difference as before.

The [pullback of a differential form](pullback-of-a-differential-form.md) commutes with the [wedge product of differential forms](wedge-product-of-differential-forms.md) and the [exterior derivative](exterior-derivative.md): $\phi^*(\alpha\wedge\beta)=\phi^*\alpha\wedge\phi^*\beta$ and $\phi^*(d\alpha)=d(\phi^*\alpha)$. These identities make changes of coordinates and parametrizations compatible with differentiation and integration.

It is the alternating-tensor case of [pullback](pullback-differential-geometry.md).
