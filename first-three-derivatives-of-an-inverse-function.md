# First three derivatives of an inverse function

↑ **Parent:** [Derivative of a local inverse map](derivative-of-a-local-inverse-map.md)

Let a three-times [continuously differentiable](continuously-differentiable-function.md) real [function](function-split.md) $f(g)$ have $df/dg\ne0$. The [inverse function theorem](inverse-function-theorem.md) gives a local inverse $g(f)$. Writing $p=df/dg$, $q=d^2f/dg^2$ and $r=d^3f/dg^3$, its [derivatives](derivative.md) are

$$
\frac{dg}{df}=\frac1p,\qquad
\frac{d^2g}{df^2}=-\frac q{p^3},\qquad
\frac{d^3g}{df^3}=\frac{3q^2-pr}{p^5}.
$$

Indeed $d/df=p^{-1}d/dg$ by the [chain rule](chain-rule.md). Apply this operator successively to $g$, $p^{-1}$ and $-qp^{-3}$. Every forward derivative on the right is evaluated at the inverse image $g(f)$.
