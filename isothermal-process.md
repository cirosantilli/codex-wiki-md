# Isothermal process

↑ **Parent:** [Carnot cycle](carnot-cycle.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isothermal_process)

An [isothermal process](isothermal-process.md) changes a [thermodynamic system](thermodynamic-system.md) while keeping its [temperature](temperature-split.md) constant. It need not keep its [pressure](pressure-split.md), [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) or [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md) constant, and it does not generally have zero [heat](heat-split.md) transfer. An [adiabatic process](adiabatic-process.md) specifies zero [heat](heat-split.md) transfer instead. For example, a slowly expanding [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) can absorb [heat](heat-split.md) from a [heat bath](heat-bath.md) and deliver the same amount of [work](work.md) while its [temperature](temperature-split.md) stays fixed.

For a fixed [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) and composition, [internal energy of an ideal gas](internal-energy-of-an-ideal-gas.md) depends only on [temperature](temperature-split.md). With $Q>0$ for [heat](heat-split.md) entering and $W_{\rm by}>0$ for [work](work.md) delivered to the surroundings, the [first law of thermodynamics](first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) therefore gives

$$
\Delta U=Q-W_{\rm by}=0,\qquad Q=W_{\rm by}.
$$

This equality does not determine either transfer until the path is specified. [Isothermal ideal-gas work](isothermal-ideal-gas-work.md) gives the reversible value; an insulated [free expansion](free-expansion.md) has a different value even when its equilibrium endpoints have the same [temperature](temperature-split.md). The [closed system](closed-system.md) convention here excludes changes in bulk [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and external [potential energy](potential-energy.md).

A conducting cylinder in a large, stirred [heat bath](heat-bath.md) approximates an [isothermal process](isothermal-process.md) when the piston moves slowly enough for [thermal conduction](thermal-conduction.md) to keep up. A [quasistatic process](quasistatic-process.md) keeps the [gas](gas.md) close to internal [thermodynamic equilibrium](thermodynamic-equilibrium.md); a [reversible thermodynamic process](reversible-thermodynamic-process.md) additionally requires negligible [friction](friction.md) and vanishing driving differences in [pressure](pressure-split.md) and [temperature](temperature-split.md). Exact equality of the [gas](gas.md) and [heat bath](heat-bath.md) [temperatures](temperature-split.md) gives no finite [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) rate through a finite [thermal resistance](thermal-resistance.md). The reversible description is a limit of arbitrarily small differences and arbitrarily slow motion, illustrated by the [finite-rate isothermal piston model](finite-rate-isothermal-piston-model.md).[§3.4, “Isothermal Processes”, Figure 3.9 and the discussion of removing piston weights in infinitesimal steps](university-physics-volume-2-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others/3-4-thermodynamic-processes.md)

For a [real gas](real-gas.md), [internal energy at constant temperature](internal-energy-at-constant-temperature.md) can change with [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) because molecular interaction [potential energy](potential-energy.md) changes. In one smooth [thermodynamic phase](thermodynamic-phase.md) of a fixed-composition [simple compressible system](simple-compressible-system.md),

$$
\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial V}\right)_{T,n}
=T\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial T}\right)_{V,n}-p.
$$

Thus an [isothermal process](isothermal-process.md) generally has $Q=\Delta U+W_{\rm by}$, with no reason to set $\Delta U=0$. The right-hand side vanishes for the [ideal gas law](ideal-gas-law.md), but not generally for another [equation of state](equation-of-state.md).[Equations 6.151–6.154: the volume contribution to internal-energy changes](thermodynamics-and-statistical-mechanics-by-richard-fitzpatrick/free-expansion-of-gas.md)
