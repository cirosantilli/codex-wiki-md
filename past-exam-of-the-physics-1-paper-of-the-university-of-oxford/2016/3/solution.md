<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

In the frame following the turn, the outward inertial [force](../../../force-split.md) $mv^2/R$ acts at height $h$ and tends to unload the inner rail. The two upward [normal forces](../../../normal-force.md) satisfy

$$
N_{\mathrm{out}}+N_{\mathrm{in}}=mg,\qquad\frac d2(N_{\mathrm{out}}-N_{\mathrm{in}})=\frac{mv^2h}{R}.
$$

The second equation is [moment balance](../../../moment-balance.md) about the midpoint between the rails. Thus

$$
N_{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{mg}{2}-\frac{mv^2h}{Rd},\qquad N_{\mathrm{out}}=\frac{mg}{2}+\frac{mv^2h}{Rd}.
$$

Full contact requires a nonnegative inner [normal force](../../../normal-force.md). Its limiting value is zero, giving the [toppling speed on level rails](../../../toppling-speed-on-level-rails.md)

$$
\boxed{v_0=\sqrt{\frac{gRd}{2h}}.}
$$

For strictly positive contact force on both rails, $v<v_0$. This ideal result assumes level rails, negligible suspension motion and enough lateral contact force to follow the curve; lateral sliding could impose a different limit.
