<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Let $\theta$ describe the direction _towards the star_, measured from the Earth's positive direction of motion. The arriving [photon](../../../photon.md) propagates in the opposite direction, so in the stellar frame its [four-momentum](../../../four-momentum.md) has

$$
p_x=-\frac{E_\gamma}{c}\cos\theta,\qquad p_y=-\frac{E_\gamma}{c}\sin\theta.
$$

A [Lorentz transformation](../../../lorentz-transformation.md) into the Earth's frame, moving at $\beta c$ along $x$, gives

$$
E_\gamma'=\gamma E_\gamma(1+\beta\cos\theta),\qquad p_x'=-\frac{\gamma E_\gamma}{c}(\cos\theta+\beta),\qquad p_y'=p_y.
$$

The observed stellar bearing is opposite to the transformed photon [momentum](../../../momentum-split.md). Since a [photon](../../../photon.md) satisfies $|\mathbf p'|=E_\gamma'/c$,

$$
\boxed{\cos\theta'=\frac{\cos\theta+\beta}{1+\beta\cos\theta},\qquad\sin\theta'=\frac{\sin\theta}{\gamma(1+\beta\cos\theta)}.}
$$

This is [relativistic aberration](../../../relativistic-aberration.md). For small $\beta$, $\theta'=\theta-\beta\sin\theta+O(\beta^2)$: stellar directions shift towards the direction of the observer's motion. Keeping the distinction between stellar bearing and photon propagation direction explains the plus signs.
