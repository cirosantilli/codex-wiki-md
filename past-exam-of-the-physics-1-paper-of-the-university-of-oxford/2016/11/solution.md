<h1 id="11/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [11](../11.md)

Write $m_e$ for the electron [rest mass](../../../invariant-mass.md) and $\varepsilon=m_ec^2$. The incident positron has total [energy](../../../energy-split.md) $E$ and satisfies $p^2c^2=E^2-\varepsilon^2$. Adding the target electron's [four-momentum](../../../four-momentum.md), the squared total [centre-of-momentum energy](../../../centre-of-momentum-energy.md) is

$$
W^2=(E+\varepsilon)^2-p^2c^2=2\varepsilon(E+\varepsilon).
$$

At the [fixed-target production threshold](../../../fixed-target-production-threshold.md), all three final particles are at rest relative to one another, so $W=3M_0c^2$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{E_{\min}=\frac{9M_0^2c^4}{2m_ec^2}-m_ec^2.}
$$

This is the positron's total energy, not its [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md). It assumes $3M_0\ge2m_e$ and addresses kinematic allowance; other [selection rules](../../../selection-rule.md) could still forbid a proposed reaction. For $M_0c^2=135\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and $m_ec^2\simeq0.510999\,\mathrm{MeV}$, $E_{\min}\simeq160.494\,\mathrm{GeV}$.

At threshold the three products share the [centre-of-momentum frame](../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md)'s laboratory [velocity](../../../velocity.md). Their common [Lorentz factor](../../../lorentz-factor.md) and speed are

$$
\gamma_{\mathrm{CM}}=\frac{E_{\min}+\varepsilon}{3M_0c^2}=\frac{3M_0}{2m_e}\simeq396.283,\qquad\boxed{v=c\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_e^2}{9M_0^2}}\simeq0.999996816\,c.}
$$

For the subsequent [two-photon decay](../../../two-photon-decay.md), each [photon](../../../photon.md) has rest-frame energy $E_\gamma^*=M_0c^2/2$. A [Lorentz transformation](../../../lorentz-transformation.md) of a photon emitted at rest-frame angle $\theta^*$ to the boost gives $E_\gamma=\gamma_{\mathrm{CM}}E_\gamma^*(1+\beta_{\mathrm{CM}}\cos\theta^*)$. Therefore the kinematically allowed laboratory range is

$$
\boxed{\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{CM}}M_0c^2}{2}(1-\beta_{\mathrm{CM}})\le E_\gamma\le\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{CM}}M_0c^2}{2}(1+\beta_{\mathrm{CM}}),}
$$

or numerically

$$
\boxed{0.08517\,\mathrm{MeV}\ \lesssim E_\gamma\lesssim\ 5.3498\times10^4\,\mathrm{MeV}.}
$$

The two photon energies in a single event are correlated: their sum is the parent's laboratory energy $\gamma_{\mathrm{CM}}M_0c^2$. When one attains the upper endpoint, the other attains the lower endpoint. Angular distributions and detector acceptance can affect which part of this range is observed.
