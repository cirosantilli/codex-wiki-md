<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

During the short [collision](../../../collision.md), the pivot's [impulse](../../../impulse.md) has zero [torque](../../../torque.md) about the pivot, so [angular momentum conservation](../../../conservation-of-angular-momentum.md) about that point gives

$$
mvL=I\Omega,\qquad I=\frac{ML^2}{3}.
$$

The bullet stops rather than becoming embedded: immediately after impact only the rod contributes to the [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md). Because this is an [elastic collision](../../../elastic-collision.md),

$$
\frac12mv^2=\frac12I\Omega^2.
$$

Eliminating $\Omega$ yields $mL^2=I$: the projectile mass equals the [effective impact mass of a pivoted body](../../../effective-impact-mass-of-a-pivoted-body.md), $I/L^2$. Hence **the bullet has one third of the rod's mass**, and

$$
\boxed{m=\frac M3,\qquad \Omega=\frac vL.}
$$

Ordinary [momentum conservation](../../../momentum-conservation.md) for the bullet and rod together would be incorrect: the fixed pivot supplies an external [impulse](../../../impulse.md). The short-impact approximation neglects the change in [gravitational potential energy](../../../gravitational-energy.md) during impact.
