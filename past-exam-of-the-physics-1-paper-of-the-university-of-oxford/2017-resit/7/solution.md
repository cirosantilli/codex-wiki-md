<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

Choose upward [velocity](../../../velocity.md) positive during ascent. Gravity and [linear drag](../../../linear-drag.md) both point downward, giving the [linear differential equation](../../../linear-differential-equation.md)

$$
m\dot v=-mg-\alpha v,\qquad
v(t)=\left(v_0+\frac{mg}{\alpha}\right)e^{-\alpha t/m}-\frac{mg}{\alpha}.
$$

At the highest point $v=0$, so

$$
\boxed{t_{\max}=\frac m\alpha\log\left(1+\frac{\alpha v_0}{mg}\right).}
$$

The [vertical ascent under linear drag](../../../vertical-ascent-under-linear-drag.md) time is smaller than $v_0/g$ for $\alpha>0$, because $\log(1+x)<x$. Its zero-drag [limit](../../../limit-of-a-function.md) is $v_0/g$, as required by constant-gravity [projectile motion](../../../projectile-motion.md).
