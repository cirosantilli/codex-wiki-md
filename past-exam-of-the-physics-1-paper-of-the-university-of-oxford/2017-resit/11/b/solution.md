<h1 id="11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a uniform disk, the surface [mass density](../../../../density.md) is $\sigma=m/(\pi a^2)$. Integrate concentric annuli of [mass](../../../../mass.md) $dm=2\pi\sigma r\,dr$ about the central perpendicular axis:

$$
I_O=\int_0^a r^2(2\pi\sigma r\,dr)
=\frac{\pi\sigma a^4}{2}
=\boxed{\frac12ma^2.}
$$

The result is specific to a uniform thin disk and that axis; it is not the diameter-axis [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md), which is $ma^2/4$ by planar symmetry and the [perpendicular axis theorem](../../../../perpendicular-axis-theorem.md).
