<h1 id="9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For [masses](../../../../mass.md) $m$ and $2m$, the constant [centre-of-mass velocity](../../../../center-of-mass-velocity.md) is $(V/3)\mathbf e_x$. Initially the laboratory [velocities](../../../../velocity.md) are $V\mathbf e_x$ and zero, while the [centre-of-mass frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md) [velocities](../../../../velocity.md) are $(2V/3)\mathbf e_x$ and $-(V/3)\mathbf e_x$. After the [elastic collision](../../../../elastic-collision.md) they are

$$
\mathbf v_1^*=\frac{2V}{3}(\cos\chi,\sin\chi),\qquad
\mathbf v_2^*=-\frac V3(\cos\chi,\sin\chi).
$$

Adding $(V/3,0)$ produces the lower-left panel's laboratory [velocity vectors](../../../../velocity-vector.md). The given $30^\circ$ laboratory angle implies

$$
\tan30^\circ=\frac{2\sin\chi}{1+2\cos\chi},\qquad
\boxed{\chi=30^\circ+\arcsin(1/4)\simeq44.48^\circ.}
$$

The [centre-of-mass frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md) scattering angle is therefore different from the laboratory angle. Reflection across the $x$-axis gives the equally possible negative-angle configuration.
