<h1 id="10/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Alison's outbound [proper time](../../../../proper-time.md) is $(13/5)(12/13)=12/5$ years. Her return Earth-frame duration is $T-13/5=26/7$ years, with [time dilation](../../../../time-dilation.md) factor $\sqrt{1-(12/13)^2}=5/13$. Adding the two segments,

$$
\boxed{\tau_A=\frac{12}{5}+\frac{26}{7}\frac5{13}
=\frac{134}{35}\,\mathrm{yr}\simeq3.8286\,\mathrm{yr}.}
$$

**Alison is two years younger than Belinda at reunion.** Her turnaround changes [inertial frames](../../../../inertial-frame.md), and the faster return segment accumulates less [proper time](../../../../proper-time.md) per unit Earth-frame time. The [twin paradox](../../../../twin-paradox.md) is resolved by comparing the complete [worldlines](../../../../world-line.md), not by applying one constant [time dilation](../../../../time-dilation.md) factor to Alison's whole journey.
