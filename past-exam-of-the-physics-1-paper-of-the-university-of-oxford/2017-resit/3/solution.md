<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

For planets with negligible [mass](../../../mass.md) compared with the [Sun](../../../sun.md), [Kepler's third law](../../../kepler-s-third-law.md) gives $T^2\propto a^3$, where $a$ is the [semi-major axis](../../../semi-major-axis.md). Relative to [Earth](../../../earth.md),

$$
\boxed{\frac{a_{\rm Mercury}}{a_\oplus}=0.24^{2/3}\simeq0.386,\qquad
\frac{a_{\rm Jupiter}}{a_\oplus}=11.9^{2/3}\simeq5.21.}
$$

Thus the requested orbital-distance estimates are **about 0.39 and 5.2 times Earth's**. Strictly, the period fixes the [semi-major axis](../../../semi-major-axis.md), not a time-averaged radius of an arbitrary [eccentric orbit](../../../eccentric-orbit.md); identifying the two as a mean distance is the usual elementary orbital-size convention.
