<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [central force](../../../../central-force.md) exerts zero [torque](../../../../torque.md) about its centre, so [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) is conserved and the motion is planar. In [plane polar coordinates](../../../../plane-polar-coordinates.md), $J=mr^2\dot\theta$ and

$$
T=\frac12m(\dot r^2+r^2\dot\theta^2),\qquad
\boxed{E=\frac12m\dot r^2+\frac{J^2}{2mr^2}+U(r).}
$$

Use $F=-U'$ and either the radial [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) or [differentiation](../../../../differentiation.md) of the [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) away from [turning points](../../../../turning-point.md) to get

$$
\boxed{m\ddot r=F(r)+\frac{J^2}{mr^3}.}
$$

The radial [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) is $\ddot r-r\dot\theta^2$, so this is exactly $m(\ddot r-r\dot\theta^2)=F(r)$, not an additional physical outward force in an inertial frame. The variational derivation remains valid at [turning points](../../../../turning-point.md) where dividing an [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) by $\dot r$ would be illegitimate.
