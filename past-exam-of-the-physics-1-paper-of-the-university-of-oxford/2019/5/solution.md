<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

With metric convention $(+---)$, the invariant interval is $c^2\Delta\tau^2=c^2\Delta t^2-|\Delta\mathbf r|^2$ along an inertially moving clock. For constant [speed](../../../speed.md) $v$ in the ground frame, $|\Delta\mathbf r|=v\Delta t$, so

$$
\boxed{\Delta t=\gamma\Delta\tau,\qquad\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}.}
$$

The aircraft records [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) $\Delta\tau=1000\,\mathrm s$. The ground duration is measured by a network of [clocks](../../../clock.md) at rest in the ideal ground frame, with [Einstein synchronisation](../../../einstein-synchronisation.md). Its excess is

$$
\Delta t-\Delta\tau=1000(\gamma-1)\,\mathrm s
\simeq1000\frac{300^2}{2c^2}\,\mathrm s
\simeq5.0\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm s.
$$

Thus **the ground-frame duration is longer by about $0.50\,\mathrm{ns}$**. This is the special-relativistic [time dilation](../../../time-dilation.md) contribution under the stated constant-speed assumptions; gravitational potential differences and Earth's rotation are outside this model.
