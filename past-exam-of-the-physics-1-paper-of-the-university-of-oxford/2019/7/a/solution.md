<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

During $dt$, the remaining rocket has [mass](../../../../mass.md) $M+dM$ and [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) $v+dv$, while the expelled positive [mass](../../../../mass.md) $-dM$ has [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) $v-u$ to first order. Subtract the initial [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) $Mv$ from their final [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) to obtain $M\,dv+u\,dM$. Equating this to the external [impulse](../../../../impulse.md) gives the [rocket equation](../../../../rocket-equation.md)

$$
F=M\dot v+u\dot M.
$$

With $F=-Mg-kMv$ and $\dot M/M=-b$, this reduces to $\dot v+kv=ub-g$. Therefore, for initial [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) $v_0$ and $k>0$,

$$
v(t)=\frac{ub-g}{k}+\left(v_0-\frac{ub-g}{k}\right)e^{-kt}.
$$

For a launch from rest,

$$
\boxed{v(t)=\frac{ub-g}{k}(1-e^{-kt}),\qquad b>g/u\text{ for upward launch}.}
$$

The inequality makes the initial upward [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) positive. Equality gives no upward launch from rest. In the zero-drag limit, $v=(ub-g)t$. These expressions apply while the prescribed burn law and constant-$g$ approximation remain valid.
