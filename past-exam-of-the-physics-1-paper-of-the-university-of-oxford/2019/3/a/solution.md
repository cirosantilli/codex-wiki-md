<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Measure position $x$ along the ideal thin rod from its [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md). Its perpendicular distance from the inclined axis is $|x|\sin\alpha$, and its [linear mass density](../../../../linear-mass-density.md) is $M/L$. The [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) is therefore

$$
I=\frac ML\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}x^2\sin^2\alpha\,dx
=\boxed{\frac{ML^2}{12}\sin^2\alpha.}
$$

This tends to zero for an axis along an infinitely thin rod and to $ML^2/12$ for a perpendicular central axis. A finite rod thickness would add a contribution absent from the stated thin-rod model.
