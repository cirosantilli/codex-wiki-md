<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Write $r=r_0+\eta$ and keep $L$ fixed. The [small radial oscillations in a power-law potential](../../../../small-radial-oscillations-in-a-power-law-potential.md) have squared [angular frequency](../../../../angular-frequency.md)

$$
\omega_r^2=\frac{V_{\mathrm{eff}}''(r_0)}m
=\frac{3L^2}{m^2r_0^4}+\frac{\beta k(k-1)}m r_0^{k-2}
=\frac{\beta k(k+2)}m r_0^{k-2}.
$$

The circular orbital [angular frequency](../../../../angular-frequency.md) has magnitude $\Omega=|L|/(mr_0^2)$, so $\Omega^2=(\beta k/m)r_0^{k-2}$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\omega_r=\Omega\sqrt{k+2},\qquad\frac{\omega_r}{\Omega}=\sqrt{k+2},\qquad
f_r=\frac{\Omega}{2\pi}\sqrt{k+2}.}
$$

A restoring radial oscillation exists for $k>-2$, subject to the attractive-force condition in part (b). For $k<-2$ the radial stationary point is unstable; $k=-2$ is the degenerate boundary. This agrees with [circular-orbit stability for a power-law central force](../../../../circular-orbit-stability-for-a-power-law-central-force.md), since the actual force is proportional to $-r^{k-1}$.
