<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

To escape the [Solar system](../../../../solar-system.md) in the single-Sun approximation, the heliocentric [specific orbital energy](../../../../specific-orbital-energy.md) must reach zero. At Earth's orbital radius, the required heliocentric [escape speed](../../../../escape-velocity.md) is $\sqrt2\,v_E$. The smallest change from Earth's initial [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) is prograde, parallel to its motion:

$$
w_{\mathrm{escape}}=(\sqrt2-1)v_E.
$$

Combining the terrestrial and solar [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) requirements gives

$$
\boxed{u_{\mathrm{escape,min}}=\sqrt{v_{e,E}^2+(\sqrt2-1)^2v_E^2}\simeq16.65\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The asymptotic heliocentric trajectory is a [parabolic Kepler orbit](../../../../parabolic-trajectory.md); larger launch [speeds](../../../../speed.md) can give positive solar [orbital energy](../../../../orbital-energy.md).
