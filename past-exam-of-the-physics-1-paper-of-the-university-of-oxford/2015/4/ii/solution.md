<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Direct fall towards a point Sun.** If the [Sun](../../../../sun.md) is treated as a point target, the projectile must lose its heliocentric [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md). The cheapest change is retrograde, cancelling Earth's tangential orbital [velocity](../../../../velocity.md). It then starts its radial fall with zero heliocentric [speed](../../../../speed.md), so $w=v_E$ and

$$
\boxed{u_{\mathrm{radial\ fall}}=\sqrt{v_{e,E}^2+v_E^2}\simeq31.81\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

This is the usual radial-fall estimate. Merely launching towards the [Sun](../../../../sun.md) while retaining Earth's tangential [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) does not remove the [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) barrier.

**Minimum for impact on a finite Sun.** A literal collision with the solar surface does not require zero [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md). The limiting [solar impact by a retrograde launch](../../../../solar-impact-by-a-retrograde-launch.md) instead has [apocentre](../../../../apoapsis.md) $a$ at Earth's orbit and [pericentre](../../../../periapsis.md) $R_\odot$ at the solar surface. The [vis-viva equation](../../../../vis-viva-equation.md) with [semi-major axis](../../../../semi-major-axis.md) $(a+R_\odot)/2$ gives the heliocentric departure [speed](../../../../speed.md)

$$
v_a=v_E\sqrt{\frac{2R_\odot}{a+R_\odot}}.
$$

It remains prograde about the [Sun](../../../../sun.md), but the Earth-relative departure is retrograde with $w=v_E-v_a$. Therefore the surface launch [speed](../../../../speed.md) for grazing the finite solar surface is

$$
\boxed{u_{\mathrm{impact,min}}=\sqrt{v_{e,E}^2+v_E^2\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{2R_\odot}{a+R_\odot}}\right)^2}\simeq29.15\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

Here $a\simeq149.598\times10^6\,\mathrm{km}$ and $R_\odot\simeq695700\,\mathrm{km}$.[Orbital parameters: semimajor axis 149.598 million km](../../../../earth-fact-sheet-by-national-aeronautics-and-space-administration.md)[Bulk parameters: volumetric mean radius 695700 km](../../../../sun-fact-sheet-by-national-aeronautics-and-space-administration.md) Slightly reducing the departure [speed](../../../../speed.md) below $v_a$ makes the trajectory enter the solar surface rather than just graze it. The $31.81$ and $29.15\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ values correspond to the explicitly different point-target and finite-radius idealizations, not to two ways of adding escape speeds. These are direct-flight estimates; planetary gravity assists and Earth's rotation are outside the model.
