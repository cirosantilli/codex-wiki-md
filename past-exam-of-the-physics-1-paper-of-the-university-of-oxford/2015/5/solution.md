<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Let the projectile's initial laboratory [velocity](../../../velocity.md) be $u\hat{\mathbf x}$. The [centre-of-momentum frame](../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md) moves at

$$
V=\frac{Mu}{M+m}.
$$

In that frame, the projectile's initial [speed](../../../speed.md) is $w=u-V=mu/(M+m)$. An [elastic collision](../../../elastic-collision.md) preserves that [speed](../../../speed.md), but can change its direction. Transforming the outgoing projectile [velocity](../../../velocity.md) back by [Galilean velocity addition](../../../galilean-velocity-addition.md) gives

$$
\mathbf u_{\mathrm{out}}=V\hat{\mathbf x}+w\mathbf n,\qquad |\mathbf n|=1.
$$

This is the [velocity-circle construction for elastic scattering](../../../velocity-circle-construction-for-elastic-scattering.md): the outgoing tip lies on a [circle](../../../circle.md) of radius $w$, centred a distance $V$ from the origin in the velocity plane. Since $M>m$, $V>w$ and the origin lies outside this circle. The largest [scattering angle](../../../scattering-angle.md) occurs when the ray from the origin is tangent to it. The resulting [right triangle](../../../right-triangle.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\sin\theta_{\max}=\frac wV=\frac mM,\qquad 0\leq\theta\leq\arcsin\frac mM.}
$$

Equality is kinematically possible; in the [centre-of-momentum frame](../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md) it corresponds to $\cos\chi=-w/V=-m/M$, where $\chi$ is the outgoing projectile [angle](../../../angle.md) to its initial direction. In particular, a heavier projectile cannot reverse its laboratory direction in an isolated [elastic collision](../../../elastic-collision.md) with an initially stationary lighter target.
