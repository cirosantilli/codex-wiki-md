<h1 id="10/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**Constant azimuth.** If $\phi$ is constant, $\dot\phi=0$ and the motion is a planar [simple pendulum](../../../../simple-pendulum-split.md):

$$
\boxed{\ddot\theta+\frac gl\sin\theta=0.}
$$

For the [small-angle approximation](../../../../small-angle-approximation.md), $\sin\theta\simeq\theta$, giving [simple harmonic motion](../../../../simple-harmonic-motion.md) with

$$
\boxed{\omega_{\mathrm{plane}}=\sqrt{\frac gl},\qquad T_{\mathrm{plane}}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac lg}.}
$$

The harmonic [angular frequency](../../../../angular-frequency.md) requires small [amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md). At finite libration amplitude $\theta_{\max}<\pi$, the exact [oscillation period](../../../../period-of-an-oscillation.md) is $4\sqrt{l/g}\,K(\sin(\theta_{\max}/2))$, where $K$ is the [complete elliptic integral of the first kind](../../../../complete-elliptic-integral-of-the-first-kind.md); the nonlinear pendulum does not have the same amplitude-independent period.

**Constant polar angle.** If $\theta=\theta_0$ is nonzero and constant, the bob traces a horizontal [circle](../../../../circle.md): this is a [conical pendulum](../../../../conical-pendulum.md). Setting $\ddot\theta=0$ and dividing by $\sin\theta_0$ gives

$$
\Omega^2\cos\theta_0=\frac gl,\qquad
\boxed{\dot\phi=\Omega=\pm\sqrt{\frac{g}{l\cos\theta_0}},\qquad 0<\theta_0<\frac\pi2.}
$$

The sign distinguishes the two senses of rotation. The exactly downward configuration $\theta_0=0$ is stationary; its azimuth is undefined and it must not be obtained by dividing by $\sin\theta_0$. A nontrivial constant-angle orbit of this gravity-only system needs $\cos\theta_0>0$ even though the supporting rod can also push.
