<h1 id="11/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [11](../11.md)

**Energy–momentum transformations and invariant.** Let the primed [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md) move at signed [velocity](../../../velocity.md) $u$ along the unprimed $x$ axis. With $\Gamma=(1-u^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$, a [Lorentz transformation](../../../lorentz-transformation.md) of [four-momentum](../../../four-momentum.md) gives

$$
\boxed{E'=\Gamma(E-up_x),\qquad p_x'=\Gamma\left(p_x-\frac{uE}{c^2}\right),\qquad p_y'=p_y,\quad p_z'=p_z.}
$$

Use $p^2=p_x^2+p_y^2+p_z^2$. The longitudinal terms satisfy

$$
E'^2-c^2p_x'^2
=\Gamma^2\left[(E-up_x)^2-c^2\left(p_x-\frac{uE}{c^2}\right)^2\right]
=\Gamma^2(1-u^2/c^2)(E^2-c^2p_x^2)
=E^2-c^2p_x^2.
$$

The transverse [momenta](../../../momentum-split.md) are unchanged, so

$$
\boxed{E'^2-c^2p'^2=E^2-c^2p^2.}
$$

For one [electron](../../../electron.md), the [relativistic energy-momentum relation](../../../energy-momentum-relation.md) identifies this [Lorentz invariant](../../../lorentz-invariance.md) as $E_0^2=m_e^2c^4$.

**Symmetric electron scattering.** Assume an isolated [elastic collision](../../../elastic-collision.md) of the two [electrons](../../../electron.md), with no emitted radiation. The incident electron has total [energy](../../../energy-split.md) $E$ and [momentum](../../../momentum-split.md) magnitude $p$, so $p^2c^2=E^2-E_0^2$; the target electron initially contributes [rest energy](../../../rest-energy.md) $E_0$ and zero [momentum](../../../momentum-split.md). In the nondegenerate configuration with final track [angles](../../../angle.md) $+\theta$ and $-\theta$, transverse [momentum conservation](../../../momentum-conservation.md) gives

$$
p_1\sin\theta=p_2\sin\theta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_1=p_2=p_f.
$$

Because both final particles have the same [rest mass](../../../invariant-mass.md), they also have equal total [energies](../../../energy-split.md). [Conservation of energy](../../../conservation-of-energy.md) gives

$$
E_f=\frac{E+E_0}{2},\qquad
p_f^2c^2=E_f^2-E_0^2=\frac{(E-E_0)(E+3E_0)}{4}.
$$

Longitudinal [momentum conservation](../../../momentum-conservation.md) is $p=2p_f\cos\theta$. Combining these expressions, with $E>E_0$, proves the [symmetric relativistic equal-mass scattering angle](../../../symmetric-relativistic-equal-mass-scattering-angle.md) relation:

$$
\cos^2\theta=\frac{E^2-E_0^2}{(E-E_0)(E+3E_0)}=\frac{E+E_0}{E+3E_0},
\qquad
\boxed{\theta=\arccos\sqrt{\frac{E+E_0}{E+3E_0}}
=\arctan\sqrt{\frac{2E_0}{E+E_0}}.}
$$

The low-[kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md) limit has $\theta\to45^\circ$, recovering the familiar right angle between the outgoing tracks in nonrelativistic equal-[mass](../../../mass.md) scattering. Relativistic incident [energies](../../../energy-split.md) give $\theta<45^\circ$, approaching zero at very high [energy](../../../energy-split.md).

**Accelerating potential.** For $\theta=30^\circ$, $\tan^2\theta=1/3=2E_0/(E+E_0)$, so $E=5E_0$. The acceleration from rest supplies [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md), not total [energy](../../../energy-split.md). If $e>0$ is the [elementary charge](../../../elementary-charge.md), the required accelerating [electric potential difference](../../../electric-potential-difference.md) has magnitude

$$
\boxed{|V|=\frac{E-E_0}{e}=\frac{4E_0}{e}\simeq2.044\times10^6\,\mathrm V=2.044\,\mathrm{MV},}
$$

using electron [rest energy](../../../rest-energy.md) $E_0\simeq0.511\,\mathrm{MeV}$. The [electric potential difference](../../../electric-potential-difference.md) is oriented to accelerate the negative [electron](../../../electron.md); the result above specifies its magnitude.
