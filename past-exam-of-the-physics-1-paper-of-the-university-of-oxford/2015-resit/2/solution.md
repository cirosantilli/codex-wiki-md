<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The disc carries [mass](../../../mass.md) $M_d=2M/3$ and the thin rim carries $M_r=M/3$. About the wheel's central normal axis, add the [moment of inertia of a uniform disc](../../../moment-of-inertia-of-a-uniform-disc.md) and the thin rim's [moment of inertia](../../../moment-of-inertia.md):

$$
\boxed{I_z=\frac12M_dR^2+M_rR^2=\frac12\frac{2M}{3}R^2+\frac M3R^2=\frac23MR^2.}
$$

Treat the wheel as a thin [planar lamina](../../../planar-lamina.md), as implied by the disc-and-thin-rim model. Interpret the requested perpendicular axis as perpendicular to the wheel's original axis and tangent to the rim: if the wheel lies in the $xy$ [plane](../../../plane.md), choose the tangent line $x=R,z=0$, parallel to the $y$ diameter. By [rotational symmetry](../../../rotational-symmetry.md), the two diametral [moments of inertia](../../../moment-of-inertia.md) are equal. The [perpendicular axis theorem](../../../perpendicular-axis-theorem.md) therefore gives

$$
I_x=I_y=\frac{I_z}{2}=\frac13MR^2.
$$

The [parallel axis theorem](../../../parallel-axis-theorem.md) then gives the [tangent-axis inertia of an axisymmetric lamina](../../../tangent-axis-inertia-of-an-axisymmetric-lamina.md):

$$
\boxed{I_{\mathrm{tangent,in\ plane}}=I_y+MR^2=\frac43MR^2.}
$$

The axis direction matters. If “perpendicular” were intended to mean perpendicular to the wheel's plane, an axis through a rim point would instead be parallel to the original wheel axis, giving $I_{\mathrm{rim,normal}}=I_z+MR^2=5MR^2/3$. The $4MR^2/3$ answer uses a tangent axis perpendicular to the original axis; the $5MR^2/3$ value belongs to the different, explicitly identified normal axis. Finite wheel thickness would require additional information for the in-plane [moment of inertia](../../../moment-of-inertia.md).
