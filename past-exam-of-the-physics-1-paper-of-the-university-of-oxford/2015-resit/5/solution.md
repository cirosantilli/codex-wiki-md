<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Take the initial internal [pressure](../../../pressure-split.md) as negligible and the external [atmospheric pressure](../../../atmospheric-pressure.md) as $p_0=1.01325\times10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The window has frontal [area](../../../surface-area.md) $A=\pi(0.400\,\mathrm m)^2=0.50265\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Before appreciable air has entered, its inward pressure [force](../../../force-split.md) is approximately $p_0A=5.09\times10^4\,\mathrm N$. [Newton's second law](../../../newton-s-second-law.md) therefore gives the initial collective inward [acceleration](../../../acceleration.md)

$$
\boxed{a_0\simeq\frac{p_0A}{M}=4.63\times10^2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\simeq47\,g.}
$$

Here $M=110\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $g\simeq9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. Neglect gravity in this much larger pressure-driven [acceleration](../../../acceleration.md), together with supports and elastic stresses once the glass has broken. The given thickness $d=0.080\,\mathrm m$ implies a plausible average glass [mass density](../../../density.md) $M/(Ad)\simeq2.74\times10^3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. Equivalently, a fragment retaining the original thickness has [acceleration](../../../acceleration.md) $p_0/(\rho_gd)$, independent of its frontal [area](../../../surface-area.md) while it experiences the full pressure difference.

For a [vacuum-window implosion energy estimate](../../../vacuum-window-implosion-energy-estimate.md), the ideal pressure-work scale for filling vessel [volume](../../../volume.md) $V=1.5\,\mathrm{m^3}$ is $W\simeq p_0V$. Assigning all that [pressure-volume work](../../../pressure-volume-work.md) to the glass's [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md) gives

$$
\frac12Mv_{\mathrm{est}}^2\simeq p_0V,
\qquad
\boxed{v_{\mathrm{est}}\simeq\sqrt{\frac{2p_0V}{M}}\simeq5.26\times10^1\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The same result follows from retaining [acceleration](../../../acceleration.md) $a_0$ over an effective swept distance $V/A\simeq2.98\,\mathrm m$, so that $v_{\mathrm{est}}^2=2a_0V/A$. This is an ideal upper energy-budget estimate for the common [speed](../../../speed.md), or [mass](../../../mass.md)-weighted [root mean square](../../../root-mean-square.md) [speed](../../../speed.md), of all the glass. It assumes an atmospheric reservoir maintains the driving pressure and that the whole vessel's pressure-work budget can accelerate the glass. In practice, air motion, fragmentation, heating and collisions share that [energy](../../../energy-split.md), the pressure difference falls, and fragments may hit the vessel after a much shorter path. The unspecified vessel shape and fragment trajectories prevent a unique actual maximum [speed](../../../speed.md); concentrating [energy](../../../energy-split.md) into one small fragment also prevents interpreting this collective estimate as a strict bound on every individual fragment.
