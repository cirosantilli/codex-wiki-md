<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Let $\mathbf r$ be the orbiting [point mass](../../../point-mass.md)'s [position](../../../position.md) relative to the attracting body, and let its [velocity](../../../velocity.md) be $\mathbf v=\dot{\mathbf r}$. Its [angular momentum](../../../angular-momentum.md) is

$$
\mathbf L=m\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r}.
$$

[Conservation of angular momentum](../../../conservation-of-angular-momentum.md) keeps both the magnitude and direction of $\mathbf L$ fixed. Since $\mathbf L$ is perpendicular to $\mathbf r$, a nonradial orbit lies in a fixed [plane](../../../plane.md). During a short interval $dt$, the radius vector sweeps a thin [triangle](../../../triangle.md) of [area](../../../surface-area.md)

$$
dA=\frac12|\mathbf r\times d\mathbf r|=\frac12|\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r}|\,dt.
$$

Thus the [areal velocity](../../../areal-velocity.md) is constant:

$$
\boxed{\frac{dA}{dt}=\frac{L}{2m},\qquad \Delta A=\frac{L}{2m}\,\Delta t.}
$$

Equal [time](../../../time-in-physics.md) intervals give equal swept [areas](../../../surface-area.md): this is [Kepler's second law](../../../kepler-s-second-law.md). In [plane polar coordinates](../../../plane-polar-coordinates.md), the same statement is $L=mr^2\dot\phi$ and $\dot A=r^2\dot\phi/2$. The attracting body is at the relevant focus of the [ellipse](../../../ellipse.md), not its geometric centre. The area argument needs conserved [angular momentum](../../../angular-momentum.md), not specifically an [ellipse](../../../ellipse.md); a [central force](../../../central-force.md) provides this conservation because its [torque](../../../torque.md) $\mathbf r\times\mathbf F$ vanishes.
