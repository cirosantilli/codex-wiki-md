<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a uniform thin disc, the [surface mass density](../../../../projected-surface-mass-density.md) is $\sigma=m/(\pi a^2)$. An annulus of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$ has [mass](../../../../mass.md) $dm=2\pi\sigma r\,dr$, all at distance $r$ from the normal central axis. Its contribution to the [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) is $r^2dm$, so

$$
I=\int_0^a r^2(2\pi\sigma r\,dr)
=\frac{2m}{a^2}\left[\frac{r^4}{4}\right]_0^a,
\qquad\boxed{I=\frac12ma^2.}
$$

This establishes the [moment of inertia of a uniform disc](../../../../moment-of-inertia-of-a-uniform-disc.md); the normal axis and uniform [surface mass density](../../../../projected-surface-mass-density.md) are part of the result.
