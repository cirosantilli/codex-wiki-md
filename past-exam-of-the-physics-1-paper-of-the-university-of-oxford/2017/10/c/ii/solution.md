<h1 id="10/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $y''=0$ over the subsequent segment, its slope is a constant $s$ and the [equation of motion](../../../../../equation-of-motion.md) reduces to

$$
\boxed{\ddot x=-\frac{gs}{1+s^2}.}
$$

There is uniform horizontal [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md), not generally zero [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md). Starting that segment at $(t_0,x_0,\dot x_0)$,

$$
x(t)=x_0+\dot x_0(t-t_0)-\frac{gs}{2(1+s^2)}(t-t_0)^2.
$$

For a downhill straight track with $s=-\tan\theta$, this gives $\ddot x=g\sin\theta\cos\theta$, agreeing with [b](../../b.md). Only $s=0$ gives constant horizontal [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md).

The condition $y''\to0$ must describe an actually straight limiting segment, with bounded [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md), for that subsequent constant-[acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) conclusion. If $y''$ vanishes only at an isolated inflection point, the boxed expression describes the instantaneous [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) there; it does not make the rest of the track straight. Likewise, a vanishing $y''$ alone does not make the product $y''\dot x^2$ negligible in a limit where $\dot x$ diverges.
