<h1 id="10/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take positive $x$ horizontally toward the downhill end of the plane, with $0\leq\theta<\pi/2$. The [normal force](../../../../normal-force.md) is $N=mg\cos\theta$, since there is no [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) perpendicular to the stationary plane. Along the plane, the only tangential [force](../../../../force-split.md) is $mg\sin\theta$, so [Newton's second law](../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) gives $a_\parallel=g\sin\theta$.

Its horizontal projection is therefore

$$
\boxed{a_x=g\sin\theta\cos\theta.}
$$

Equivalently, the [normal force](../../../../normal-force.md) has horizontal component $N\sin\theta$ while gravity has none, giving $ma_x=N\sin\theta$. For a plane whose height increases in the positive $x$ direction, the answer instead carries a minus sign. A horizontal plane gives zero [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md); the horizontal projection also tends to zero for a vertical plane.
