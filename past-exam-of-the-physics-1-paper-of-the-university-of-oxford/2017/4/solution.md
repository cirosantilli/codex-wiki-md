<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Use the [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md) in which the departure point and destination are stationary, with separation $d$. The astronauts' elapsed [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) is $\tau=t/\gamma$, whereas $d=vt$. Hence

$$
\gamma\beta=\frac d{c\tau}=\frac45,\qquad
\gamma^2-(\gamma\beta)^2=1,
\qquad \gamma=\frac{\sqrt{41}}5,\qquad \beta=\frac4{\sqrt{41}}.
$$

The Earth-frame [travel time](../../../travel-time.md) is $t=\sqrt{41}\,\mathrm{yr}\simeq6.40\,\mathrm{yr}$ and the [speed](../../../speed.md) is approximately $0.625c$. The spacecraft's [relativistic kinetic energy](../../../relativistic-kinetic-energy.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{K=(\gamma-1)mc^2
=\left(\frac{\sqrt{41}}5-1\right)(10^5\,\mathrm{kg})c^2
\simeq2.52\times10^{21}\,\mathrm J.}
$$

The five years are not the time to use in $v=d/t$ in the departure frame. This is the cruising [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md), with departure and arrival [acceleration](../../../acceleration.md) intervals omitted, rather than a complete propulsion [energy](../../../energy-split.md) budget.
