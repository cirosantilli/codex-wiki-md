<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [parallel axis theorem](../../../../parallel-axis-theorem.md) relates the [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) $I$ about an axis to $I_{\rm CM}$ about a parallel axis through the [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md), separated by perpendicular distance $d$:

$$
\boxed{I=I_{\rm CM}+M_{\rm tot}d^2.}
$$

In the defining mass [integral](../../../../integral-split.md), expanding the squared distance produces a cross term whose first mass moment about the [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md) is zero.

For a thin planar [rigid body](../../../../rigid-body-dynamics-split.md), the [perpendicular axis theorem](../../../../perpendicular-axis-theorem.md) applies to two perpendicular axes $x,y$ in its plane and the perpendicular axis $z$, all meeting at the same point:

$$
\boxed{I_z=I_x+I_y.}
$$

Indeed, $I_x=\int y^2\,dm$, $I_y=\int x^2\,dm$, and $I_z=\int(x^2+y^2)\,dm$. The planar condition is essential; this is not an identity for an arbitrary three-dimensional [rigid body](../../../../rigid-body-dynamics-split.md).
