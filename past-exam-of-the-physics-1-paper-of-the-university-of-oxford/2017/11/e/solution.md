<h1 id="11/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Take Jupiter's heliocentric [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) as $\mathbf V_J=13\hat{\mathbf x}\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. [Conservation of energy](../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) in the Jupiter frame makes the incoming and outgoing asymptotic [speeds](../../../../speed.md) both $10\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. For the $90^\circ$ encounter, an outgoing [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) in Jupiter's direction requires the incoming Jupiter-frame [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) to be perpendicular to it:

$$
\mathbf u_{\rm in}=\pm10\hat{\mathbf y}\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}},\qquad
\mathbf u_{\rm out}=10\hat{\mathbf x}\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}.
$$

Use a [Galilean transformation](../../../../galilean-transformation.md) back to the Sun frame:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf v_{\rm in}=13\hat{\mathbf x}\pm10\hat{\mathbf y}\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}},\qquad
\mathbf v_{\rm out}=23\hat{\mathbf x}\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The heliocentric [speeds](../../../../speed.md) are therefore

$$
\boxed{v_{\rm in}=\sqrt{13^2+10^2}\simeq16.4\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}},\qquad
v_{\rm out}=23.0\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The incoming direction makes an angle $\arctan(10/13)\simeq37.6^\circ$ with Jupiter's motion; its two signs describe mirror-image encounter planes. The [gravitational assist](../../../../gravitational-assist.md) transfers orbital [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) from Jupiter to the spacecraft: the spacecraft's specific [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) increases by $\tfrac12(23^2-16.4^2)\,\mathrm{km^2\,s^{-2}}$, or, using the unrounded squared incoming [speed](../../../../speed.md), exactly $130\,\mathrm{km^2\,s^{-2}}=1.30\times10^8\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$. There is no gain in asymptotic [speed](../../../../speed.md) in the Jupiter frame; the gain is a consequence of changing frames while the planet is moving.
