<h1 id="11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use a [reference frame](../../../../reference-frame.md) moving with Jupiter, neglect the spacecraft's effect on the planet and neglect the Sun during the short encounter. The Newtonian [central force](../../../../central-force.md) makes the [orbit](../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) planar and conserves the [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) $J=mr^2\dot\vartheta$. In plane [polar coordinates](../../../../polar-coordinates.md), the [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is $m(\dot r^2+r^2\dot\vartheta^2)/2$. Choosing zero [gravitational potential energy](../../../../gravitational-energy.md) at infinity gives

$$
\boxed{E=\frac12m\dot r^2+\frac{J^2}{2mr^2}-\frac{GmM_J}{r}.}
$$

The terms are respectively radial [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md), transverse [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md), and attractive [gravitational potential energy](../../../../gravitational-energy.md). The second term is often combined with the third into the radial [effective potential](../../../../effective-potential.md). Far from Jupiter,

$$
\boxed{E=\frac12mv_0^2,\qquad J=mbv_0.}
$$

Here $b$ is the perpendicular [impact parameter](../../../../impact-parameter.md) of the incoming asymptote, not the eventual [periapsis distance](../../../../pericentre-distance.md).
