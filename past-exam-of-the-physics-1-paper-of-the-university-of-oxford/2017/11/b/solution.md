<h1 id="11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put $\mu_J=GM_J$. At the [periapsis distance](../../../../pericentre-distance.md), the radial [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) vanishes. Insert $E=mv_0^2/2$ and $J=mbv_0$ into [a](../a.md):

$$
\frac12v_0^2=\frac{b^2v_0^2}{2r_{\min}^2}-\frac{\mu_J}{r_{\min}},
\qquad v_0^2r_{\min}^2+2\mu_Jr_{\min}-b^2v_0^2=0.
$$

The positive root is

$$
\boxed{r_{\min}=\frac{\sqrt{\mu_J^2+b^2v_0^4}-\mu_J}{v_0^2}
=\sqrt{b^2+\left(\frac{\mu_J}{v_0^2}\right)^2}-\frac{\mu_J}{v_0^2}.}
$$

Rationalizing gives the numerically stable alternative $r_{\min}=b^2v_0^2/[\sqrt{\mu_J^2+b^2v_0^4}+\mu_J]$. Attraction makes $r_{\min}<b$. This is a point-planet [scattering](../../../../scattering.md) calculation; a noncolliding physical encounter must also have $r_{\min}$ outside Jupiter's surface. At $b=0$, the limiting formula gives a radial collision, not a flyby.
