<h1 id="9/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a massive free particle with [rest mass](../../../../../invariant-mass.md) $m$ and [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) $\mathbf v$,

$$
\mathbf p=\gamma m\mathbf v,\qquad E=\gamma mc^2,
\qquad\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}.
$$

The [relativistic energy-momentum relation](../../../../../energy-momentum-relation.md) follows directly:

$$
E^2-c^2|\mathbf p|^2
=\gamma^2m^2c^4(1-v^2/c^2)
=\boxed{m^2c^4.}
$$

This is the squared Minkowski norm of the [four-momentum](../../../../../four-momentum.md) multiplied by $c^2$. The [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) preserves that norm, so it has the same value in every [inertial frame](../../../../../inertial-frame.md). In the particle's [rest frame](../../../../../rest-frame.md), the [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) is zero and the total [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is its [rest energy](../../../../../rest-energy.md) $mc^2$.
