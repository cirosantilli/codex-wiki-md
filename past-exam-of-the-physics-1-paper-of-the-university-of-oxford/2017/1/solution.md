<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Measure $\theta$ from the downward vertical. The falling [mass](../../../mass.md) loses [gravitational potential energy](../../../gravitational-energy.md) $3mgl(1-\cos\theta)$. The fixed pivot does no [work](../../../work.md), so [conservation of energy](../../../conservation-of-energy.md) gives its incident [speed](../../../speed.md) $u$:

$$
3mgl(1-\cos\theta)=\frac12(3m)u^2,
\qquad u=\sqrt{2gl(1-\cos\theta)}.
$$

At the bottom, the rod is vertical and the impact is horizontal. Its impulsive reaction therefore has no horizontal component. Let $U$ and $w$ be the subsequent horizontal [velocities](../../../velocity.md) of the heavy and light balls. Horizontal [momentum conservation](../../../momentum-conservation.md) and unit [coefficient of restitution](../../../coefficient-of-restitution.md) give

$$
3mu=3mU+mw,\qquad w-U=u.
$$

Thus $U=u/2$, and the light ball's outgoing [speed](../../../speed.md) is

$$
\boxed{w=\frac32\sqrt{2gl(1-\cos\theta)}
=3\sqrt{gl}\,\left|\sin\frac\theta2\right|.}
$$

The calculation neglects the sizes of the balls, treats the rod as massless, and uses an [elastic collision](../../../elastic-collision.md). The expression vanishes for release at the bottom; a ball placed exactly at the unstable upright [equilibrium](../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) requires a perturbation before it can fall.
