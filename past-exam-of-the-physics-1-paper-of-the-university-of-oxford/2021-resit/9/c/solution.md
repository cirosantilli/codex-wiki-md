<h1 id="9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $D$ be the separation of the [Earth](../../../../earth.md) and [Moon](../../../../moon.md), and let $x$ be the spacecraft's distance from the Earth's centre towards the Moon. On $0<x<D$, the two [gravitational forces](../../../../gravitational-force.md) oppose each other. Their equality, by [Newton's law of universal gravitation](../../../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation-split.md), is

$$
\frac{GM_E}{x^2}=\frac{GM_M}{(D-x)^2}.
$$

Taking the positive [square root](../../../../square-root.md) gives

$$
\boxed{x=\frac{D\sqrt{M_E}}{\sqrt{M_E}+\sqrt{M_M}}
=\frac{D}{1+\sqrt{M_M/M_E}}.}
$$

The [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md) of the two bodies is at $x_G=DM_M/(M_E+M_M)$ from the Earth's centre. Thus the [gravitational field null between two point masses](../../../../gravitational-field-null-between-two-point-masses.md) lies a distance

$$
\boxed{x-x_G=D\left[\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{M_M/M_E}}
-\frac{M_M}{M_E+M_M}\right]}
$$

from their [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md), on the Moon's side. With $M_E/M_M=81$, $x=9D/10$ and $x_G=D/82$, so

$$
\boxed{x=3.456\times10^8\,\mathrm m,\qquad
x-x_G=\frac{182}{205}D\simeq3.40917\times10^8\,\mathrm m.}
$$

The point is $3.84\times10^7\,\mathrm m$ from the Moon. It is an instantaneous zero of the physical [gravitational field](../../../../gravitational-field.md), not a co-rotating [Lagrange point](../../../../lagrange-point.md): a spacecraft remaining fixed relative to the Earth-Moon line would need a nonzero [centripetal acceleration](../../../../centripetal-acceleration.md). With zero net [force](../../../../force-split.md) it cannot follow that circular co-rotation.
