<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Take positive [velocity](../../../velocity.md) towards $B$, and interpret $100\,\mathrm{MHz}$ as the emitted [frequency](../../../frequency.md) in the probe's rest frame. The [relativistic velocity-addition formula](../../../velocity-addition-formula.md) gives the probe's station-frame [velocity](../../../velocity.md):

$$
u_P=\frac{0.5c+0.5c}{1+(0.5)(0.5)}=0.8c.
$$

For reception at the station, photons propagate backwards while the probe recedes. The longitudinal [relativistic Doppler effect](../../../relativistic-doppler-effect.md) gives

$$
\boxed{f_{\rm station}=100\sqrt{\frac{1-0.8}{1+0.8}}\,\mathrm{MHz}
=\frac{100}{3}\,\mathrm{MHz}\simeq33.33\,\mathrm{MHz}.}
$$

Before the probe overtakes $B$, their separation decreases. The probe's [velocity](../../../velocity.md) in $B$'s [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md) is

$$
u_{P|B}=\frac{0.8c-0.4c}{1-(0.8)(0.4)}=\frac{10}{17}c.
$$

The approaching-source [relativistic Doppler effect](../../../relativistic-doppler-effect.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{f_B=100\sqrt{\frac{1+10/17}{1-10/17}}\,\mathrm{MHz}
=100\sqrt{\frac{27}{7}}\,\mathrm{MHz}\simeq196.40\,\mathrm{MHz}.}
$$

An independent frame calculation gives the same result: forward photons have station-frame [frequency](../../../frequency.md) $300\,\mathrm{MHz}$, and a receiver moving at $0.4c$ measures $300\sqrt{0.6/1.4}\,\mathrm{MHz}$. If reception occurs after the probe passes $B$, backwards photons instead give $f_B=100\sqrt{7/27}\,\mathrm{MHz}\simeq50.92\,\mathrm{MHz}$. The direction of propagation and the reception geometry are essential to choosing the [Doppler factor](../../../doppler-factor.md).
