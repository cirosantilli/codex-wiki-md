<h1 id="8/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Set $q=1$ in the [laboratory scattering circle](../../../../laboratory-scattering-circle.md) formula. For $M_1>M_2$, a real outgoing [speed](../../../../speed.md) requires

$$
\cos^2\theta_1-1+\left(\frac{M_2}{M_1}\right)^2\ge0,
\qquad \sin^2\theta_1\le\left(\frac{M_2}{M_1}\right)^2.
$$

Also $v_1\ge0$ excludes the backward-angle interval: the square root is smaller than $|\cos\theta_1|$ when $M_2/M_1<1$, so both roots would be negative for $\cos\theta_1<0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\theta_1^{\max}=\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{M_2}{M_1}\right)<\frac\pi2.}
$$

At this boundary the two possible [speeds](../../../../speed.md) coincide, with $v_1/u_1=\sqrt{(M_1-M_2)/(M_1+M_2)}$. Geometrically the outgoing ray is tangent to the [laboratory scattering circle](../../../../laboratory-scattering-circle.md). As $M_2/M_1\to0$ the maximum tends to zero, while it approaches $\pi/2$ as the masses approach equality from $M_1>M_2$.
