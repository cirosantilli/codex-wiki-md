<h1 id="8/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For equal [masses](../../../../mass.md) and an [elastic collision](../../../../elastic-collision.md), $q=1$ and

$$
\frac{v_1}{u_1}=\frac12(\cos\theta_1\pm|\cos\theta_1|).
$$

Thus the nonzero outgoing branch is

$$
\boxed{v_1=u_1\cos\theta_1,\qquad 0\le\theta_1<\frac\pi2,}
$$

and the other nonnegative algebraic root is $v_1=0$. The latter is the head-on transfer of the incoming [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) and [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) to particle 2: $v_2=u_1$. A stationary particle has no defined outgoing direction, so this root is not a separate physical outcome for every chosen $\theta_1$.

On the nonzero branch, [momentum conservation](../../../../momentum-conservation.md) gives

$$
\mathbf v'_2=u_1\widehat{\mathbf x}-\mathbf v'_1,
\qquad v_2=u_1\sin\theta_1.
$$

The two nonzero outgoing [velocities](../../../../velocity.md) are perpendicular: squaring [momentum conservation](../../../../momentum-conservation.md) and comparing with [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) conservation gives $\mathbf v'_1\cdot\mathbf v'_2=0$. For $\cos\theta_1<0$, the nonzero root is negative and must be rejected; backward scattering of a moving incident particle is impossible in this equal-mass, initially stationary-target case.
