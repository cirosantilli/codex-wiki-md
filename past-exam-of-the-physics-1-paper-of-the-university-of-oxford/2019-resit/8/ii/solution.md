<h1 id="8/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For an [elastic collision](../../../../elastic-collision.md) with $M_2>M_1$, the positive root in the [laboratory scattering circle](../../../../laboratory-scattering-circle.md) formula is the only nonnegative one. With $\varepsilon=M_1/M_2\ll1$,

$$
\frac{v_1}{u_1}
=\frac{\varepsilon\cos\theta_1+\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2\sin^2\theta_1}}{1+\varepsilon}
=1-\varepsilon(1-\cos\theta_1)+O(\varepsilon^2).
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{v_1\longrightarrow u_1\quad\text{as }M_2/M_1\to\infty.}
$$

The heavy target can absorb a finite [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) with negligible [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) and negligible [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md). Thus the projectile may change direction substantially while retaining its [speed](../../../../speed.md); a backward reflection also has $v_1\simeq u_1$.
