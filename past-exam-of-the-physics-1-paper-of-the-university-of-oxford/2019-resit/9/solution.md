<h1 id="9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9](../9.md)

For a particle of [rest mass](../../../invariant-mass.md) $m_0$ and [velocity](../../../velocity.md) $\mathbf u$, the [relativistic energy](../../../relativistic-energy.md) and [momentum](../../../momentum-split.md) are

$$
\boxed{E=\gamma_um_0c^2,\qquad\mathbf p=\gamma_um_0\mathbf u,\qquad
\gamma_u=(1-u^2/c^2)^{-1/2}.}
$$

For a [photon](../../../photon.md) of wavelength $\lambda$ travelling along $\widehat{\mathbf n}$,

$$
\boxed{E_\gamma=\frac{hc}{\lambda},\qquad\mathbf p_\gamma=\frac h\lambda\widehat{\mathbf n},\qquad E_\gamma=c|\mathbf p_\gamma|.}
$$

Here $h$ is [Planck constant](../../../planck-constant.md) and $c$ is the [speed of light](../../../speed-of-light.md).

Let $\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon'$ be the incident and scattered [photon](../../../photon.md) [energies](../../../energy-split.md). The stationary [electron](../../../electron.md) initially has [energy](../../../energy-split.md) $m_ec^2$. By [four-momentum conservation](../../../four-momentum-conservation.md), its final [energy](../../../energy-split.md) and [momentum](../../../momentum-split.md) are

$$
E_e'=m_ec^2+\varepsilon-\varepsilon',\qquad
\mathbf p_e'=\frac{\varepsilon}{c}\widehat{\mathbf n}
-\frac{\varepsilon'}c\widehat{\mathbf n}',
\quad\widehat{\mathbf n}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf n}'=\cos\theta.
$$

Use the electron's [relativistic energy-momentum relation](../../../energy-momentum-relation.md), rather than assuming its recoil is nonrelativistic:

$$
(m_ec^2+\varepsilon-\varepsilon')^2
=m_e^2c^4+\varepsilon^2+\varepsilon'^2-2\varepsilon\varepsilon'\cos\theta.
$$

Cancelling common terms gives

$$
m_ec^2(\varepsilon-\varepsilon')
=\varepsilon\varepsilon'(1-\cos\theta),\qquad
\frac1{\varepsilon'}-\frac1\varepsilon=\frac{1-\cos\theta}{m_ec^2}.
$$

Since $\lambda=hc/\varepsilon$, the [Compton wavelength shift](../../../compton-wavelength-shift.md) is

$$
\boxed{\lambda'-\lambda=\frac{h}{m_ec}(1-\cos\theta).}
$$

The word elastic here means that the final [electron](../../../electron.md) has the same [rest mass](../../../invariant-mass.md) as initially and the complete photon-electron system conserves total [energy](../../../energy-split.md); the [photon](../../../photon.md) alone loses [energy](../../../energy-split.md) to recoil.

For backscattering, $1-\cos\theta=2$, and hence

$$
\boxed{\varepsilon'=\frac{\varepsilon}{1+2\varepsilon/(m_ec^2)}
=\frac{0.511}{2.511}\,\mathrm{MeV}=0.2035\,\mathrm{MeV}.}
$$

Choose the incident direction as $+x$, so that the scattered [photon](../../../photon.md) travels in the $-x$ direction. An observer moving with [velocity](../../../velocity.md) $+\beta c\widehat{\mathbf x}$ meets that [photon](../../../photon.md) head-on. The [Lorentz transformation](../../../lorentz-transformation.md) of [four-momentum](../../../four-momentum.md) gives the observed [energy](../../../energy-split.md)

$$
\varepsilon_{\rm obs}=\gamma_\beta(\varepsilon'-\beta c p_x')
=\gamma_\beta\varepsilon'(1+\beta)
=\varepsilon'\sqrt{\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}}.
$$

This is the approaching-observer [relativistic Doppler effect](../../../relativistic-doppler-effect.md). Set $D=\varepsilon/\varepsilon'=2.511/0.511$ and solve:

$$
\boxed{\beta=\frac{D^2-1}{D^2+1}=0.9205\ \text{approximately}.}
$$

The observer must move **opposite to the scattered photon**, along the original incident direction, at approximately $0.9205c$. Moving with the scattered [photon](../../../photon.md) would redshift it instead.
