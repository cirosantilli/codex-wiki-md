<h1 id="11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

About a fixed origin in an [inertial frame](../../../../inertial-frame.md), the [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) and [torque](../../../../torque.md) are

$$
\boxed{\mathbf L=\mathbf r\times m\mathbf v,\qquad
\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf r\times\mathbf F.}
$$

For a specified fixed axis with unit vector $\widehat{\mathbf n}$, use the projections $L_n=\widehat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf L$ and $\tau_n=\widehat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\tau$. The [mass](../../../../mass.md) is constant. Differentiating and applying [Newton's second law](../../../../newton-s-second-law.md),

$$
\frac{d\mathbf L}{dt}
=\mathbf v\times m\mathbf v+\mathbf r\times m\dot{\mathbf v}
=0+\mathbf r\times\mathbf F
=\boxed{\boldsymbol\tau}.
$$

Hence $\dot L_n=\tau_n$ for the fixed axis. If the net [torque](../../../../torque.md) vanishes, the corresponding [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) is conserved. Fixing the origin and axis is essential to this unmodified form of the law.
