<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

Let $L_0=100\,\mathrm m$ be the train's [proper length](../../../proper-length.md), $D=90\,\mathrm m$ the tunnel's length in the track [reference frame](../../../reference-frame.md), and $v=0.99c$. The [Lorentz factor](../../../lorentz-factor.md) is $\gamma=7.0888$. At one track-frame [time](../../../time-in-physics.md), [length contraction](../../../length-contraction.md) makes the train

$$
L=\frac{L_0}{\gamma}=14.107\,\mathrm m<D.
$$

**Yes, the whole train fits inside at a single track-frame time.** Set $t=0$ when the rear enters the near portal. Until the front reaches the far portal, the rear and front are at $vt$ and $vt+L$, respectively. Complete containment lasts

$$
0\le t\le\frac{D-L}{v},
$$

a track-frame interval of approximately $256\,\mathrm{ns}$, counting boundary contact.

In the train's [reference frame](../../../reference-frame.md), its length is $100\,\mathrm m$, while the moving tunnel has simultaneous length $D/\gamma=12.696\,\mathrm m$. **No single train-frame time has the whole train between the portals.** The two answers refer to different simultaneity slices.

For an explicit check, denote the rear-entry event by $A$ and the front-exit event by $B$. In track coordinates,

$$
\Delta t=\frac{D-L}{v}>0,\qquad \Delta x=D.
$$

The [Lorentz transformation](../../../lorentz-transformation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\Delta t'=\gamma\left(\Delta t-\frac{vD}{c^2}\right)
=\frac{D/\gamma-L_0}{v}<0.}
$$

Thus the front leaves before the rear enters in train coordinates. These spatially separated events have a frame-dependent order, as allowed by [relativity of simultaneity](../../../relativity-of-simultaneity.md); neither answer asserts a different local event at a portal. No collision with closed doors has been stipulated.
