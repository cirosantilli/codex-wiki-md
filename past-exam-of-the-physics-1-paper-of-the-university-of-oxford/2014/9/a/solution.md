<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the [eccentric circular-hole disk inertia](../../../../eccentric-circular-hole-disk-inertia.md), let $D=\pi\rho R^2$ be the [mass](../../../../mass.md) of the complete disc before removing material, $h=\pi\rho r_o^2$ the missing [mass](../../../../mass.md), and $W=D-h$ the wheel's remaining [mass](../../../../mass.md). Denote the complete disc's centre by C and the axle/hole centre by H; $CH=r_o$. Assume the hole fits inside the disc, $0<r_o<R/2$. The [parallel axis theorem](../../../../parallel-axis-theorem.md) gives the full disc's [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) about H as $\tfrac12DR^2+Dr_o^2$. Subtract the absent little disc about its own centre H:

$$
\boxed{I_H=\frac12DR^2+Dr_o^2-\frac12hr_o^2=\frac{\pi\rho}{2}\left(R^4+2R^2r_o^2-r_o^4\right).}
$$

The axle is a support, not part of the rotating wheel's stated [mass](../../../../mass.md). Its frictionless bearing does not alter this [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md). For fixed-axis [angular velocity](../../../../angular-velocity.md) $\omega$,

$$
\boxed{T_H=\frac12I_H\omega^2=\frac{\pi\rho\omega^2}{4}\left(R^4+2R^2r_o^2-r_o^4\right).}
$$

The missing material must be subtracted with its actual displacement relative to the axis; treating the wheel as a concentric annulus would give the wrong result.
