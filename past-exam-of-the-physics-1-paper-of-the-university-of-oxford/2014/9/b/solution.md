<h1 id="9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a freely rolling rigid body, decompose the [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) into translation of its [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md) G and rotation about G. The [centre of mass after removing material](../../../../centre-of-mass-after-removing-material.md) lies on the side opposite H:

$$
\overrightarrow{CG}=-\frac hW\overrightarrow{CH},\qquad HG=r_o\left(1+\frac hW\right)=\frac{Dr_o}{W}.
$$

Another use of the [parallel axis theorem](../../../../parallel-axis-theorem.md) gives

$$
I_G=I_H-W(HG)^2=I_H-\frac{D^2r_o^2}{W}.
$$

Thus at the same [angular speed](../../../../angular-speed.md),

$$
\boxed{T_{\rm rot,G}=\frac12I_G\omega^2<T_H,\qquad T_H-T_{\rm rot,G}=\frac{D^2r_o^2\omega^2}{2W}>0.}
$$

This compares the rotational component, not the total rolling [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md). The total also contains $\tfrac12Wv_G^2$ and need not be smaller than $T_H$. Describing all rolling motion as instantaneous rotation about its contact point includes that translational contribution; it is not the rotational component intended by this comparison.
