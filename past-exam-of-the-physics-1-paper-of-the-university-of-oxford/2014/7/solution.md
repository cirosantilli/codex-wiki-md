<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

Let $\theta$ measure the spoke's displacement from downward equilibrium. The total [moment of inertia](../../../moment-of-inertia.md) about the axle is $I=MR_2^2+mR_1^2$: the rim is a thin [thin hoop](../../../thin-hoop.md), and the attached body is a point [mass](../../../mass.md). The rim's weight has no axle [torque](../../../torque.md), while the attached [mass](../../../mass.md) gives restoring [torque](../../../torque.md) $-mgR_1\sin\theta$. Hence

$$
\boxed{(MR_2^2+mR_1^2)\ddot\theta+mgR_1\sin\theta=0,\quad \theta(0)=\theta_0,\quad\dot\theta(0)=0.}
$$

Equivalently [conservation of energy](../../../conservation-of-energy.md) gives $\tfrac12I\dot\theta^2=mgR_1(\cos\theta-\cos\theta_0)$. For small [angles](../../../angle.md), $\sin\theta\simeq\theta$, so the motion is approximately $\theta=\theta_0\cos(\omega t)$ with $\omega^2=mgR_1/I$. The small-amplitude period is

$$
\boxed{T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{MR_2^2+mR_1^2}{mgR_1}}.}
$$

At finite amplitude the nonlinear pendulum equation remains valid but this period approximation does not. The $M\to0$ limit recovers a simple pendulum of length $R_1$.
