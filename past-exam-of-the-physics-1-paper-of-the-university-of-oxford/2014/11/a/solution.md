↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Here $G$ is the [gravitational constant](../../../../gravitational-constant.md), $r$ is the separation of the orbiting object from the central body's centre, and $J$ is the magnitude of the object's conserved [orbital angular momentum](../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md), not angular momentum per unit [mass](../../../../mass.md). Approximate the much heavier central body as fixed, neglect other forces, and use the Newtonian gravitational [potential energy](../../../../potential-energy.md) $U=-GMm_0/r$, with zero at infinity.

A [central force](../../../../central-force.md) confines the motion to a fixed [orbital plane](../../../../orbital-plane.md). In plane polar coordinates its [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is $T=\tfrac12m_0(\dot r^2+r^2\dot\phi^2)$. Conservation of [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) gives $J=m_0r^2\dot\phi$; eliminating $\dot\phi$ yields

$$
\boxed{E=\frac12m_0\dot r^2+V_{\rm eff}(r),\qquad V_{\rm eff}(r)=\frac{J^2}{2m_0r^2}-\frac{GMm_0}{r}.}
$$

The centrifugal term belongs to the radial [effective potential](../../../../effective-potential.md); it is the eliminated transverse [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md), not an additional physical interaction. If recoil of the central body mattered, the relative-coordinate reduction would use the [reduced mass](../../../../reduced-mass.md).
