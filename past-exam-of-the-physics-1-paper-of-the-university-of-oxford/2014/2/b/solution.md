<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A straight diameter at nonzero [angle](../../../../angle.md) $\chi$ to the rotation axis moves the body toward that axis during the inward half of its trip. With no external axial [torque](../../../../torque.md), the total axial [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) of the [Earth](../../../../earth.md) and body is conserved. The body's contribution to the [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) decreases toward the centre, so the [Earth](../../../../earth.md) spins slightly faster; the reverse happens on the outward half, restoring the original rotation rate at the far surface.

For a body of [mass](../../../../mass.md) $m$ constrained to follow a tunnel fixed in the [Earth](../../../../earth.md), an illustrative angular-momentum balance is

$$
\boxed{\Omega(x)=\Omega_0\frac{I_E+mr_0^2\sin^2\chi}{I_E+mx^2\sin^2\chi}.}
$$

Here $I_E$ is the [Earth](../../../../earth.md)'s axial [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md), treated as constant, and $x$ is the signed distance along the diameter. A frictionless guide can still exert normal forces: these supply the transverse acceleration and exchange axial [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) with the rotating [Earth](../../../../earth.md). Simply allowing an unconstrained body to fall would not keep it inside a narrow nonpolar straight tunnel. The effect vanishes for $\chi=0$ and is largest for an equatorial diameter.
