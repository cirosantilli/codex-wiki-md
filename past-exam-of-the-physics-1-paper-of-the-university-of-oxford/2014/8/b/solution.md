<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $q$ be the transferred fuel [mass](../../../../mass.md). Before any engine reversal, rocket A has [mass](../../../../mass.md) $a=m_A+M-q$ and [velocity](../../../../velocity.md)

$$
v_A(q)=u_0\ln\frac{m_A+M}{m_A+M-q}.
$$

For [exhaust collection by a second rocket](../../../../exhaust-collection-by-a-second-rocket.md), under the stipulated eventual capture of all exhaust, final [conservation of momentum](../../../../momentum-conservation.md) for the complete initially stationary system gives $m_Av_{Af}+(m_B+M)v_{Bf}=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{v_{Af}=u_0\ln\frac{m_A+M}{m_A},\quad v_{Bf}=-\frac{m_A}{m_B+M}v_{Af},\quad v_{\rm rel}=u_0\frac{m_A+m_B+M}{m_B+M}\ln\frac{m_A+M}{m_A}.}
$$

Capture is inelastic: no [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) conservation for B is assumed. Finite exhaust flight time changes the intermediate sharing of [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) but not this endpoint balance if the stated complete capture actually occurs.

The [ballistic exhaust interception condition](../../../../ballistic-exhaust-interception-condition.md) is a physical restriction on that idealization. For purely ballistic exhaust to reach a receding collector to the rear, its laboratory [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) must be more negative than B's while interception occurs. In the negligible-flight-time approximation this requires $v_A-u_0<v_B$. Very large fuel ratios need not meet that condition throughout a complete burn; the boxed endpoint formula is conditional on the prompt's complete-collection stipulation, not an unconditional prediction for every ballistic two-rocket geometry.
