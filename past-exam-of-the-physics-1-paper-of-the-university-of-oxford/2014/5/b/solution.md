<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take the light to travel along positive x, while the water moves with signed [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) $-v$. In the water's rest frame its light [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md) is $c/n$. The [relativistic velocity addition](../../../../velocity-addition-formula.md) law from [a](../a.md) gives the laboratory [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md)

$$
w=\frac{c/n-v}{1-v/(nc)},\qquad
n'=\frac cw=n\frac{1-v/(nc)}{1-nv/c}.
$$

Expanding to first order in $v/c$, with $nv/c\ll1$, yields

$$
\boxed{n'\simeq n\left(1+\frac{nv}{c}-\frac{v}{nc}\right).}
$$

Equivalently $w\simeq c/n-v(1-1/n^2)$: the counterflow reduces the forward light [speed](../../../../speed.md). This is the nondispersive [Fresnel drag coefficient](../../../../fresnel-drag-coefficient.md). The [refractive index](../../../../refractive-index.md) is taken as constant over the relevant frequencies; [dispersion](../../../../wave-dispersion.md) and its frequency-shift corrections are outside this approximation.
