<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the inverse [Lorentz transformation](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md), with $\gamma_u=(1-u^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$:

$$
x=\gamma_u(x'+ut'),\qquad t=\gamma_u\left(t'+\frac{ux'}{c^2}\right).
$$

Taking differentials along the object's trajectory gives

$$
\boxed{w=\frac{dx}{dt}=\frac{v+u}{1+uv/c^2}.}
$$

This is the collinear [relativistic velocity addition](../../../../velocity-addition-formula.md) law. It concerns the [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) of one object measured in two frames. By contrast the rate of change of the distance between two objects in one [laboratory frame](../../../../laboratory-frame.md) can be as large as

$$
\boxed{\frac{dD}{dt}=2c,}
$$

attained by oppositely directed light signals; for massive objects it is approached but not attained. This is not an object's [speed](../../../../speed.md) in the other's rest frame and cannot transmit a signal faster than $c$.
