<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) of a uniform thin ring about its perpendicular central axis is $M_aa^2$. Its two in-plane diameters are equivalent by [rotational symmetry](../../../../rotational-symmetry.md), so the [perpendicular axis theorem](../../../../perpendicular-axis-theorem.md) gives $M_aa^2/2$ about either diameter. The central rod contribution about an axis perpendicular to its length is $M_ll^2/12$.

The requested axis is parallel to these central axes and passes through the whole racquet's [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md). Applying the [parallel axis theorem](../../../../parallel-axis-theorem.md) separately gives

$$
\boxed{I_{\mathrm{CM}}=\frac12M_aa^2+M_aX_{\mathrm{CM}}^2
+\frac1{12}M_ll^2+M_l\left(a+\frac l2-X_{\mathrm{CM}}\right)^2.}
$$

An equivalent reduced expression is

$$
I_{\mathrm{CM}}=\frac12M_aa^2+\frac1{12}M_ll^2
+\frac{M_aM_l}{M_a+M_l}\left(a+\frac l2\right)^2.
$$

Both refer to the in-plane axis perpendicular to the handle; the ring contribution would be different for an axis normal to the racquet face.
