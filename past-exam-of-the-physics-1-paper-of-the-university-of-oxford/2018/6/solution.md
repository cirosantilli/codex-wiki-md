<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Interpret the quoted muon [energy](../../../energy-split.md) as total [relativistic energy](../../../relativistic-energy.md), and neglect energy loss in the atmosphere. The [Lorentz factor](../../../lorentz-factor.md), [speed](../../../speed.md) and Earth-frame [travel time](../../../travel-time.md) are

$$
\gamma=\frac{6000\,\mathrm{MeV}}{106\,\mathrm{MeV}}=56.60,\qquad
\beta=\sqrt{1-\gamma^{-2}}=0.999844,\qquad
t=\frac{h}{\beta c}.
$$

The [muon decay](../../../muon-decay.md) law is exponential in [proper time](../../../proper-time.md). Equivalently, [time dilation](../../../time-dilation.md) makes the Earth-frame mean lifetime $\gamma\tau_0$. The [exponential survival probability](../../../exponential-survival-probability.md) over the flight is

$$
P=\exp\!\left(-\frac{t}{\gamma\tau_0}\right)
=\exp\!\left(-\frac{h}{c\tau_0\sqrt{\gamma^2-1}}\right).
$$

With $h=15000\,\mathrm m$ and $\tau_0=2.2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm s$, the elapsed [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) is $8.841\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm s$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P\simeq e^{-0.40186}\simeq0.669,\quad\text{about }67\%.}
$$

This uses the stated single incident [energy](../../../energy-split.md) and vertical trajectories. If the number were a [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md), one would instead use $\gamma=1+T/(mc^2)$; the distinction is not silently absorbed into the [rest energy](../../../rest-energy.md).
