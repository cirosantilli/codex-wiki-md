<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take $\beta>0$. At a radial [turning point](../../../../turning-point.md), $\dot r=0$, and the [effective potential](../../../../effective-potential.md) gives

$$
E=\frac{L^2}{2mr^2}-\frac\beta r,\qquad
\boxed{Er_{\min}^2+\beta r_{\min}-\frac{L^2}{2m}=0.}
$$

For $L\ne0$, the smaller positive radial [turning point](../../../../turning-point.md) is conveniently written without division by $E$:

$$
\boxed{r_{\min}=\frac{L^2}{m\beta+\sqrt{m^2\beta^2+2mEL^2}}.}
$$

This includes $E=0$, where $r_{\min}=L^2/(2m\beta)$. For $E<0$ there is also an outer radial [turning point](../../../../turning-point.md); the two coincide for the [circular orbit](../../../../circular-orbit.md) at $E=-m\beta^2/(2L^2)$. Smaller [energies](../../../../energy-split.md) are inaccessible at that $L$. For $E>0$ there is just one positive radial [turning point](../../../../turning-point.md). If $L=0$, there is no [centrifugal barrier](../../../../centrifugal-barrier.md) and an inward trajectory can reach the singular centre; its limiting closest distance is zero.

**The requested omission of $\beta$ is not possible.** For example, at $E=0$ and fixed nonzero $L,m$, doubling $\beta$ halves $r_{\min}$. Thus $E,L,m$ alone cannot determine closest approach.
