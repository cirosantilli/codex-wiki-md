<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

By [time dilation](../../../../time-dilation.md), the laboratory [mean lifetime](../../../../mean-lifetime-physics.md) is $\tau=\gamma\tau_0$, where $\tau_0=2.6\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm s$. The [exponential decay](../../../../exponential-decay.md) law evaluated at travel time $d/v$ gives the surviving [pion](../../../../pion.md) fraction

$$
\frac n{n_0}=\exp\left(-\frac{d}{\gamma v\tau_0}\right).
$$

Use the exact product $\gamma v=(400/0.94)c$ from part (a), rather than approximating the [speed](../../../../speed.md) separately. With $c=2.99792458\times10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$,

$$
\gamma v\tau_0\simeq3317\,\mathrm m,\qquad
\boxed{\frac n{n_0}=\exp\left[-\frac{400}{(400/0.94)c(2.6\times10^{-8})}\right]
\simeq0.886.}
$$

Thus about $88.6\%$ emerge without decaying, and about $11.4\%$ decay inside the pipe. This assumes the given common longitudinal [speed](../../../../speed.md) and neglects losses from other interactions.
