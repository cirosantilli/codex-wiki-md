<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [circular orbit](../../../../circular-orbit.md) has constant $r=r_0>0$. Both its initial radial [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) and radial [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) must vanish, giving

$$
\boxed{r(0)=r_0,\quad\dot r(0)=0,\quad
V_{\mathrm{eff}}'(r_0)=V'(r_0)-\frac{J^2}{mr_0^3}=0.}
$$

Equivalently, the initial tangential [speed](../../../../speed.md) and [angular velocity](../../../../angular-velocity.md) obey

$$
v_{\theta,0}^2=\frac{r_0f(r_0)}m,\qquad
\dot\theta(0)=\frac{J}{mr_0^2},\qquad
J^2=mr_0^3f(r_0).
$$

The initial [polar angle](../../../../polar-angle.md) is arbitrary, and the [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is fixed at $E=V_{\mathrm{eff}}(r_0)$. Either orientation is possible. These conditions give an exact [circular orbit](../../../../circular-orbit.md) even if it is unstable; stability requires an additional positive second [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) of the [effective potential](../../../../effective-potential.md).
