<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the [power law](../../../../power-law.md) [central potential](../../../../central-potential.md) $V=\beta r^k$, differentiate the [effective potential](../../../../effective-potential.md):

$$
V_{\mathrm{eff}}'(r)=\beta k r^{k-1}-\frac{J^2}{mr^3}.
$$

The [circular orbit](../../../../circular-orbit.md) condition is $m\beta k r_0^{k+2}=J^2$. Thus, when $J\ne0$, $\beta k>0$ and $k\ne-2$,

$$
\boxed{r_0=\left(\frac{J^2}{m\beta k}\right)^{1/(k+2)}.}
$$

The sign condition $\beta k>0$ expresses attraction. At $k=0$ the [force](../../../../force-split.md) vanishes and there is no nontrivial [circular orbit](../../../../circular-orbit.md). At $k=-2$, attraction requires $\beta<0$ and the condition reduces to $J^2=-2m\beta$. In that exceptional case the [effective potential](../../../../effective-potential.md) is identically zero and every $r_0>0$ is a possible circular radius for the tuned $J$; there is no isolated preferred radius. A small radial [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) then gives $\ddot r=0$, rather than an oscillation.
