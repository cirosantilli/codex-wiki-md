<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Choose the emitted beam direction as positive $x$. In the original laboratory frame the sensor moves with [velocity](../../../velocity.md) $-0.2c$; let the laser recede from it with [velocity](../../../velocity.md) $-\beta c$. The collinear [relativistic Doppler shift](../../../relativistic-doppler-effect.md) from source to laboratory and then to sensor gives

$$
\frac{\nu_{\rm sensor}}{\nu_0}=\sqrt{\frac{1.2}{0.8}}\sqrt{\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}.
$$

At the detection threshold this ratio is $633/1000$. Hence

$$
q=\frac{(633/1000)^2}{1.5}=0.267126,\qquad
\boxed{\beta=\frac{1-q}{1+q}\simeq0.578375.}
$$

The minimum collinear speed is therefore **about $0.578c$, away from the approaching sensor**, while the laser is still aimed towards it. The source must recede fast enough to overcome the sensor's approach-induced blueshift. A larger recession speed gives a longer received [wavelength](../../../wavelength.md), if the detector has no additional upper cutoff.

As a check, the required relative recession speed is $(1-0.633^2)c/(1+0.633^2)$; [relativistic velocity addition](../../../velocity-addition-formula.md) gives this same value from $(\beta-0.2)c/(1-0.2\beta)$.
