<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

The initial total [momentum](../../../momentum-split.md) is zero, so the outgoing [neutron](../../../neutron-split.md) recoils with momentum magnitude $E_\gamma/c$. Put $W=(m_p+m_\pi)c^2$. Conservation of [relativistic energy](../../../relativistic-energy.md) gives

$$
W=E_\gamma+\sqrt{m_n^2c^4+E_\gamma^2},\qquad
\boxed{E_\gamma=\frac{W^2-m_n^2c^4}{2W}\simeq129.4\,\mathrm{MeV}.}
$$

For example the rest energies $938.2721$, $139.5704$ and $939.5654\,\mathrm{MeV}$ for the proton, charged pion and neutron give this value.[Charged-pion, proton and neutron listings, page 1 of each: mass values in MeV](../../../review-of-particle-physics-by-particle-data-group-2022.md) The result is smaller than the initial-to-final rest-energy difference of about $138.3\,\mathrm{MeV}$, because some released [energy](../../../energy-split.md) becomes neutron recoil [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md). Atomic binding of a captured pion is neglected in the stated stationary-particle model.
