<h1 id="8/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The symbols in the question are diameters: write drum radius $a=R/2$, axle radius $b=r/2$. For a thin hollow drum, $I=Ma^2$. A rightward horizontal [impulse](../../../../impulse.md) $J$ applied at height $h$ above the centre gives $v=J/M$ and clockwise [angular velocity](../../../../angular-velocity.md) $\omega=Jh/I$. Rolling on the axle requires $v=b\omega$, so

$$
\boxed{h=\frac I{Mb}=\frac{a^2}{b}=\frac{R^2}{2r}.}
$$

The frictionless rails do not create the required rotation; the initial impulse must give exactly this ratio of linear to angular motion. For the usual case $r<R$, the required height is greater than the drum radius. It cannot be reached by striking the bare cylindrical surface horizontally; an extension or equivalent applied couple would be needed. This geometric limitation should accompany the formal result.
