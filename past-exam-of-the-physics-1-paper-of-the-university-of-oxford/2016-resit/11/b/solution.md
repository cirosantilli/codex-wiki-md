<h1 id="11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In the original rest frame each leg takes $d/c$, so the round trip takes $2d/c$. For the moving frame, use the coordinate differences between the same three events. The [Lorentz transformation](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\Delta t'_{AB}=\frac{\gamma d}{c}(1-\beta),\quad\Delta t'_{BC}=\frac{\gamma d}{c}(1+\beta),\quad\Delta t'_{AC}=\frac{2\gamma d}{c}.}
$$

The outbound leg is shorter because the mirror approaches the outgoing ray; the inbound leg is longer because the source retreats from the returning ray. Their sum is larger than the original round-trip [proper time](../../../../proper-time.md) by the [time dilation](../../../../time-dilation.md) factor $\gamma$.

These are coordinate times for return to the original source, not a claim that the pulse returns to the second observer's body at event $C$. If that observer starts at $A$, it intercepts the returning light at a different event, earlier than $C$, at its own time $2\gamma d(1-\beta)/c$. Distinguishing those events is necessary when interpreting “seen” or “return”.
