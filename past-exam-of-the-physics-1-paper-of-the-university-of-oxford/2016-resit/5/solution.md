<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Centre the square on the $xy$ plane, with uniform surface [mass density](../../../density.md) $m/a^2$. For an in-plane axis at angle $\alpha$ to the $x$ direction, the perpendicular distance of $(x,y)$ from the axis is $-x\sin\alpha+y\cos\alpha$. Therefore its [moment of inertia](../../../moment-of-inertia.md) is

$$
I_\alpha=\frac m{a^2}\int_{-a/2}^{a/2}\int_{-a/2}^{a/2}(-x\sin\alpha+y\cos\alpha)^2\,dx\,dy.
$$

The mixed integral vanishes by symmetry, while the $x^2$ and $y^2$ integrals are equal. Thus

$$
\boxed{I_\alpha=\frac{ma^2}{12}(\sin^2\alpha+\cos^2\alpha)=\frac{ma^2}{12}.}
$$

This is the in-plane isotropy of the square's [inertia tensor](../../../inertia-tensor.md): its two central in-plane principal moments coincide. It does not assert isotropy about arbitrary three-dimensional axes, nor about parallel axes that miss the centre.
