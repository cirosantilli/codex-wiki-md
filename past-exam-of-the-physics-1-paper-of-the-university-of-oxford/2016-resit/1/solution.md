<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Take upward [velocity](../../../velocity.md) as positive. The linear [drag force](../../../drag-physics.md) is $-mkv$, so it opposes the motion both during ascent and descent. [Newton's second law](../../../newton-s-second-law.md) gives $\dot v+kv=-g$. With $v(0)=u$, the [integrating factor](../../../integrating-factor.md) $e^{kt}$ yields

$$
\boxed{v(t)=\left(u+\frac gk\right)e^{-kt}-\frac gk,\qquad k>0.}
$$

The [terminal velocity](../../../terminal-velocity.md) is $-g/k$. The limit $k\to0$ at fixed $t$ recovers $u-gt$; expanding the exponential is necessary to see the cancellation of the apparently singular terms.
