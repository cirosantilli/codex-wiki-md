<h1 id="10/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $V=v_{\max}$ and let $u_c=\sqrt{GM/r_0}$ be the [circular orbit](../../../../circular-orbit.md) speed. Take the captured half to move in the original tangential direction, the minimum-energy choice. Conservation of [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) during the short explosion gives the other half speed $2V-u_c$. The gravitational [potential energy](../../../../potential-energy.md) is unchanged at the instant of separation, so the necessary generated [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is the increase in [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md):

$$
\boxed{E_{\rm explosion}=\frac m4[u_c^2+(2V-u_c)^2]-\frac m2V^2=\frac m2(V-u_c)^2.}
$$

The captured half has zero radial velocity and exactly the required tangential speed, so it can enter the stable Newtonian [circular orbit](../../../../circular-orbit.md) at this radius. Internal self-binding and any mass lost to radiation are neglected.
