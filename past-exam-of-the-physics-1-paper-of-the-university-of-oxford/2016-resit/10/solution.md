<h1 id="10/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [10](../10.md)

Use the [test-particle approximation](../../../test-particle-approximation.md), neglecting the planet's recoil. The conserved specific [angular momentum](../../../angular-momentum.md) is $j=bv$ and the specific [energy](../../../energy-split.md) at infinity is $v^2/2$. At closest approach the radial [velocity](../../../velocity.md) vanishes, so the [effective potential](../../../effective-potential.md) gives

$$
\frac{v^2}{2}=\frac{b^2v^2}{2r_0^2}-\frac{GM}{r_0}.
$$

The positive root and the tangential speed are

$$
\boxed{r_0=\frac{\sqrt{G^2M^2+b^2v^4}-GM}{v^2}=\frac{b^2v^2}{GM+\sqrt{G^2M^2+b^2v^4}},\quad v_{\max}=\frac{bv}{r_0}=\sqrt{v^2+\frac{2GM}{r_0}}.}
$$

The second form for $r_0$ avoids subtracting two nearly equal numbers for a small impact parameter. These formulas describe a nonradial orbit; $b=0$ gives a collision rather than a finite nonzero [pericentre](../../../periapsis.md) in the point-planet model.
