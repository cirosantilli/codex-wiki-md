<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The familiar right-angle result requires an **elastic collision** with conserved horizontal [momentum](../../../momentum-split.md) and the appropriate treatment of [rotational kinetic energy](../../../rotational-kinetic-energy.md). In the ideal smooth-sphere model the spins do not change during the collision, so the translational [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md) is separately conserved. For equal masses, with initial [velocity](../../../velocity.md) $\mathbf u$ and outgoing [velocities](../../../velocity.md) $\mathbf v_1,\mathbf v_2$,

$$
\mathbf u=\mathbf v_1+\mathbf v_2,\qquad u^2=v_1^2+v_2^2.
$$

Squaring the first equality and using the second gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf v_1\cdot\mathbf v_2=0.}
$$

Thus the two nonzero outgoing directions are **perpendicular**. In the head-on limiting case one sphere stops, so an angle for its zero velocity is undefined. An ideal elastic model that enforces [rolling without slipping](../../../rolling-without-slipping.md) at both ends gives the same energy equation: each solid sphere has total [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md) $7mv^2/10$, and the common factor cancels.

Elasticity is not stated explicitly in the question. It cannot be omitted from the argument: for a smooth equal-mass collision with normal incoming component $u_n$ and [coefficient of restitution](../../../coefficient-of-restitution.md) $e<1$, the outgoing scalar product is $(1-e^2)u_n^2/4>0$. Moreover, after a smooth impact the spheres need not still be rolling; subsequent frictional relaxation can change their directions. The right-angle statement therefore concerns the specified ideal elastic collision, not arbitrary later rolling trajectories.
