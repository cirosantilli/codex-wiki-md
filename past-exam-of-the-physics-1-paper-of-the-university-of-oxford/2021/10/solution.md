<h1 id="10/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [10](../10.md)

The laboratory is the parent's [rest frame](../../../rest-frame.md). In this [relativistic two-body decay](../../../relativistic-two-body-decay.md), [conservation of momentum](../../../momentum-conservation.md) gives equal and opposite daughter [momenta](../../../momentum-split.md), of magnitude $p$. Their total [relativistic energies](../../../relativistic-energy.md) satisfy

$$
E_1+E_2=4mc^2,\qquad
E_1^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4,\qquad
E_2^2=p^2c^2+4m^2c^4.
$$

Subtracting the [relativistic energy-momentum relations](../../../energy-momentum-relation.md) eliminates $p$:

$$
(E_2-E_1)(E_2+E_1)=3m^2c^4,
\qquad E_2-E_1=\frac34mc^2.
$$

Solving the two [linear equations](../../../linear-equation.md) gives

$$
E_1=\frac{13}{8}mc^2,\qquad E_2=\frac{19}{8}mc^2,\qquad
p=\frac{\sqrt{105}}8mc.
$$

Since [three-momentum](../../../three-momentum.md) obeys $pc^2=Ev$, the daughter [speeds](../../../speed.md) are

$$
\boxed{v_1=\frac{\sqrt{105}}{13}c=0.7882c,\qquad
v_2=\frac{\sqrt{105}}{19}c=0.5393c.}
$$

Their directions are opposite; the initial state does not specify their common axis. The [kinetic energies](../../../kinetic-energy.md) are $K_1=5mc^2/8$ and $K_2=3mc^2/8$. Their sum $mc^2$ is the loss of [rest energy](../../../rest-energy.md) when the total daughter [rest mass](../../../invariant-mass.md) is $3m$ instead of $4m$.

The [proper mean lifetime](../../../proper-mean-lifetime.md) $\tau_0$ is the [mean lifetime](../../../mean-lifetime-physics.md) measured in the unstable particle's own [rest frame](../../../rest-frame.md), or equivalently the mean elapsed [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) before decay. In the [exponential decay](../../../exponential-decay.md) model, its survival [probability](../../../probability.md) to [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) $\tau$ is $e^{-\tau/\tau_0}$. For the daughter of [rest mass](../../../invariant-mass.md) $m$, the [Lorentz factor](../../../lorentz-factor.md) is $\gamma_1=E_1/(mc^2)=13/8$, so [time dilation](../../../time-dilation.md) gives a laboratory [mean lifetime](../../../mean-lifetime-physics.md) of $\gamma_1\tau_0=3.25\,\mu\mathrm s$.

At constant [speed](../../../speed.md) $v_1$, travelling a [distance](../../../distance.md) $L$ requires laboratory time $L/v_1$ and [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) $L/(\gamma_1v_1)$. Its [mean decay length](../../../mean-decay-length.md) is

$$
\ell_d=\gamma_1v_1\tau_0
=\frac{\sqrt{105}}8c\tau_0
=768.0\,\mathrm m
$$

for $\tau_0=2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm s$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(\text{distance before decay}>1000\,\mathrm m)
=\exp\!\left[-\frac{1000\,\mathrm m}{\ell_d}\right]
=0.272\quad\text{approximately}.}
$$

The [mean lifetime](../../../mean-lifetime-physics.md) sets the scale of an [exponential distribution](../../../exponential-distribution.md); it is not a fixed deadline, so travelling beyond one [mean decay length](../../../mean-decay-length.md) has a nonzero [probability](../../../probability.md).
