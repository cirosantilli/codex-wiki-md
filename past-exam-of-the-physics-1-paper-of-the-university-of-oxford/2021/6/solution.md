<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Use the [Earth](../../../earth.md)'s [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md), with the [Earth](../../../earth.md) at $x=0$ and the star at $x=D=4.2$ light years, neglecting their relative motion. Take the stated relative [velocity](../../../velocity.md) of $B$ to be forward along the same direction as $A$. The [relativistic velocity addition](../../../velocity-addition-formula.md) law gives

$$
\boxed{v_B=\frac{0.25c+0.50c}{1+(0.25)(0.50)}=\frac23c.}
$$

In units of light years and years, $c=1$ and the spacecraft [worldlines](../../../world-line.md) are $x_A=t/4$ and $x_B=2t/3$. Their passages at the star occur at

$$
t_{B*}=\frac{4.2}{2/3}=6.3\,\mathrm{yr},\qquad
t_{A*}=\frac{4.2}{1/4}=16.8\,\mathrm{yr}.
$$

The return [radio wave](../../../radio-wave.md) from $B$ follows the leftward light [worldline](../../../world-line.md)

$$
x=4.2-(t-6.3)=10.5-t.
$$

Intersecting this with $A$'s [worldline](../../../world-line.md) gives $t/4=10.5-t$, hence

$$
\boxed{t_{A\leftarrow B}=8.4\,\mathrm{yr},\qquad x_{A\leftarrow B}=2.1\,\text{light years}.}
$$

This is the [Earth](../../../earth.md)-frame coordinate time of reception on $A$, as requested; it is not the reading of $A$'s own clock. If an observer on the [Earth](../../../earth.md) watched that reception through a returning light signal, they would see it later.

Each transmission from the star takes another $D/c=4.2$ years to reach the [Earth](../../../earth.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{t_{\oplus\leftarrow B}=10.5\,\mathrm{yr},\qquad
 t_{\oplus\leftarrow A}=21.0\,\mathrm{yr}.}
$$

All times are measured from the common passage by the [Earth](../../../earth.md); the spacecraft continue at their stated [velocities](../../../velocity.md) during the calculation.
