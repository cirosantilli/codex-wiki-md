<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For [maximum projectile range from an elevated launch](../../../maximum-projectile-range-from-an-elevated-launch.md), let $X$ be horizontal [projectile range](../../../projectile-range.md) from the tower and $\alpha$ the launch angle above horizontal. The [projectile motion](../../../projectile-motion.md) trajectory is

$$
y=h+X\tan\alpha-\frac{gX^2}{2u^2}(1+\tan^2\alpha).
$$

Put $z=\tan\alpha$. For a fixed $X$, its largest possible $y$ occurs at $z=u^2/(gX)$; the envelope is $y_{\max}=h+u^2/(2g)-gX^2/(2u^2)$. The furthest attainable ground point satisfies $y_{\max}=0$, so

$$
\boxed{X_{\max}=\frac ug\sqrt{u^2+2gh},\qquad \tan\alpha_{\rm opt}=\frac{u}{\sqrt{u^2+2gh}}.}
$$

The optimum angle is less than $45^\circ$ for $h>0$ and approaches $45^\circ$ as $h\to0$. The result is the maximum horizontal distance, using uniform gravity and no [drag](../../../drag-physics.md).
