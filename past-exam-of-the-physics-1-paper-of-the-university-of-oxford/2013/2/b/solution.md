<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Define the [energy function](../../../../energy-function.md) $\mathcal E=\sum_i\dot q_i p_i-L$. Along solutions of the [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md),

$$
\frac{d\mathcal E}{dt}=\sum_i\left[\ddot q_i p_i+\dot q_i\dot p_i-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}\dot q_i-\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\ddot q_i\right]-\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}=-\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\partial L/\partial t=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathcal E=\text{constant}.}
$$

For an ordinary isolated [mechanical system](../../../../mechanical-system.md) with $L=T-U$, a time-independent quadratic [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) and a velocity-independent [potential energy](../../../../potential-energy.md), $\sum_i\dot q_i p_i=2T$, so $\mathcal E=T+U$. For a more general [Lagrangian](../../../../lagrangian.md), the conserved quantity is the displayed [energy function](../../../../energy-function.md); identifying it with $T+U$ requires these assumptions.
