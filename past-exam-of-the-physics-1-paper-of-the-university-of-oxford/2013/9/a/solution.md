<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $\theta$ measure the rim [point mass](../../../../point-mass.md)'s displacement from vertically below the fixed axis. The disk has [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) $I_D=\tfrac12(2M)R^2=MR^2$, and the rim [mass](../../../../mass.md) contributes another $MR^2$. Its varying [gravitational potential energy](../../../../gravitational-energy.md) is $-MgR\cos\theta$. Thus

$$
\boxed{L=MR^2\dot\theta^2+MgR\cos\theta.}
$$

The disk's [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md) remains fixed, so its own gravitational contribution is a constant and has been omitted. The total fixed-axis [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) is $2MR^2$.
