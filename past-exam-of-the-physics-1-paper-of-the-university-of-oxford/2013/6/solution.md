<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Choose the positive axis from the approaching source towards the observer. The photon that reaches the observer is emitted forward, with source-frame [four-momentum](../../../four-momentum.md) $(E/c,E/c,0,0)$. Boosting to the observer frame gives $E_{\rm obs}=\gamma E(1+\beta)$, where $\beta=v/c$. Since photon [energy](../../../energy-split.md) is $h_{\rm P}\nu$,

$$
\boxed{\nu=\frac E{h_{\rm P}}\gamma(1+\beta)=\frac E{h_{\rm P}}\sqrt{\frac{1+v/c}{1-v/c}}.}
$$

This is the approaching-source [relativistic Doppler effect](../../../relativistic-doppler-effect.md). A photon emitted in a general source-frame direction would instead have the factor $\gamma(1+\beta\cos\theta_*)$; forward emission is the geometry of the photon directed to the observer here.
