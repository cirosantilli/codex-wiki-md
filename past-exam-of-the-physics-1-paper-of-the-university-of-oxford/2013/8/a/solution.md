<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [geostationary orbit](../../../../geostationary-orbit.md) has the same [angular velocity](../../../../angular-velocity.md) as the [Earth](../../../../earth.md). Let $M_E=4\pi\rho R^3/3$ and $r_g=\eta R$. Balancing the required [centripetal acceleration](../../../../centripetal-acceleration.md) with the exterior [gravitational field](../../../../gravitational-field.md) gives

$$
\omega^2r_g=\frac{GM_E}{r_g^2},\qquad
\boxed{\eta=\left(\frac{GM_E}{\omega^2R^3}\right)^{1/3}=\left(\frac{4\pi G\rho}{3\omega^2}\right)^{1/3}.}
$$

The [orbital plane](../../../../orbital-plane.md) must be equatorial and the motion prograde for the satellite to remain over one point. The uniform density assumption supplies $M_E$ in terms of the specified $\rho$ and $R$.
