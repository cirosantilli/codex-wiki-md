<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the [uniform unloaded space-elevator cable](../../../../uniform-unloaded-space-elevator-cable.md), let the constant [linear mass density](../../../../linear-mass-density.md) be $\lambda$ and the outer endpoint be $b=\eta'R$. In the co-rotating frame the outward effective [force](../../../../force-split.md) per unit length is $\lambda(\omega^2r-GM_E/r^2)$. The cable is just clear of the surface, with neither a ground load nor an endpoint counterweight, so both ends have zero applied [tension](../../../../tension.md). The integrated effective [force](../../../../force-split.md) must vanish:

$$
0=\lambda\int_R^b\left(\omega^2r-\frac{GM_E}{r^2}\right)dr=\lambda\left[\frac{\omega^2}{2}(b^2-R^2)+GM_E\left(\frac1b-\frac1R\right)\right].
$$

Using $GM_E=\omega^2\eta^3R^3$, this becomes

$$
0=(\eta'-1)\left[\frac{\eta'+1}{2}-\frac{\eta^3}{\eta'}\right].
$$

The root $\eta'=1$ is a zero-length cable. The physical nonzero cable has

$$
\boxed{\eta'=\frac{\sqrt{1+8\eta^3}-1}{2}\simeq23.6.}
$$

It extends well beyond the [geostationary orbit](../../../../geostationary-orbit.md) so its outer [centrifugal force](../../../../centrifugal-force.md) balances the inner segment's weight. If a cable is anchored with a nonzero endpoint load or has a counterweight, that load changes the integrated balance; its length is not fixed by the zero-end-load formula.
