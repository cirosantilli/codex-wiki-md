<h1 id="10/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Launch and arrival occur on the capsule's own worldline, so its clock measures the [proper time](../../../../../proper-time.md). In the ship frame its [Lorentz factor](../../../../../lorentz-factor.md) is $\gamma_{c/s}=(1-0.9^2)^{-1/2}$; therefore

$$
\boxed{\Delta\tau_c=\frac{\Delta t_s}{\gamma_{c/s}}=\Delta t_s\sqrt{1-0.9^2}\simeq0.131211\,\mathrm{day}\simeq3.1491\,\mathrm h.}
$$

This uses the station-frame distance convention stated in [i](../i.md). With a one-light-day distance in the ship frame instead, it becomes $\Delta\tau_c=\sqrt{0.19}/1.75\,\mathrm{day}\simeq0.249080\,\mathrm{day}$. The uncertainty is a distance-frame convention in the wording, not a missing figure or an unanswered mathematical part.
