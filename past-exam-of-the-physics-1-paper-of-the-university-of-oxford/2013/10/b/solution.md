<h1 id="10/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The berth moves at $0.85c$ in the ship's initial [rest frame](../../../../rest-frame.md), so it too is subject to [length contraction](../../../../length-contraction.md):

$$
\boxed{L_b'=\frac{L_b}{\gamma_s}=100(1-0.85^2)+4\sqrt{1-0.85^2}\simeq29.8571\,\mathrm m.}
$$

The ship remains $100\,\mathrm m$ long in this frame. The two length comparisons use different simultaneous endpoint events; this is the same [relativity of simultaneity](../../../../relativity-of-simultaneity.md) that resolves the [ladder paradox](../../../../ladder-paradox.md). Physical docking also requires deceleration, whose later effects are outside these initial inertial measurements.
