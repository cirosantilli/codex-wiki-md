<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

Treat the carriage as translating along the circular track, neglecting wheel [rotational kinetic energy](../../../rotational-kinetic-energy.md) and its finite size. At the top, gravity and the inward normal [force](../../../force-split.md) supply the [centripetal acceleration](../../../centripetal-acceleration.md):

$$
mg+N=\frac{mv_{\rm top}^2}{r}.
$$

For unilateral contact, $N\ge0$, so the limiting [speed](../../../speed.md) is $v_{\rm top}^2=gr$. [Conservation of energy](../../../conservation-of-energy.md) from rest at height $h$ then gives

$$
mgh=2mgr+\tfrac12mgr,\qquad \boxed{h_{\min}=\tfrac52r.}
$$

This top contact condition is the most restrictive: at angle $\phi$ measured from the bottom, $N/(mg)=2h/r-2+3\cos\phi$, whose minimum occurs at the top. A mechanically captive carriage able to sustain an outward rail reaction has a different constraint; the result here uses the usual requirement of positive normal contact.
