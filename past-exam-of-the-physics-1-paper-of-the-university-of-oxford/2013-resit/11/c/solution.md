<h1 id="11/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

As $m_1/m_2\to0$, [a](../a.md) gives $v_1\to3u$. The upper ball then rises through height $v_1^2/(2g)$ above the near-ground mutual encounter:

$$
\boxed{H_{\rm rebound}\longrightarrow\frac{9u^2}{2g}=9H.}
$$

The rise above its original release level is therefore $8H$ in this idealization. Although its [speed](../../../../speed.md) triples, the light ball's fraction of the total [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) tends to zero, $f(r)\sim9r$. This distinguishes maximum rebound height from the maximum energy-transfer fraction in [b](../b.md). With finite radii, $9H$ is the leading approximation in drop height relative to the collision height.
