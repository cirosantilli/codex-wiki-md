<h1 id="11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $r=m_1/m_2>0$. The upper ball's fraction of the available [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) after the separated [elastic collisions](../../../../elastic-collision.md) is

$$
f(r)=\frac{\tfrac12m_1v_1^2}{\tfrac12(m_1+m_2)u^2}=\frac{r(3-r)^2}{(1+r)^3},\qquad
1-f(r)=\frac{(1-3r)^2}{(1+r)^3}\ge0.
$$

Thus the maximal possible fraction is unity, attained at

$$
\boxed{\frac{m_1}{m_2}=\frac13,\qquad f_{\max}=1,\qquad v_1=2u,\quad v_2=0.}
$$

The identity proves the global bound without selecting a spurious stationary point. At the optimum the upper ball receives all the initial translational [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md). Its subsequent rise is $4H$ above the mutual-collision level. For arbitrary large mass ratios further ground encounters may occur; the unity bound is already attained in the stated two-impact sequence.
