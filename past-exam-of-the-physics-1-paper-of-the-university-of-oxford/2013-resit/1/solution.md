<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Take the upward [velocity](../../../velocity.md) as positive during ascent. [Newton's second law](../../../newton-s-second-law.md) with [linear drag](../../../linear-drag.md) gives

$$
\dot v+kv=-g,\qquad v(0)=v_0.
$$

Multiplication by the [integrating factor](../../../integrating-factor.md) $e^{kt}$ and integration give

$$
v(t)=\left(v_0+\frac gk\right)e^{-kt}-\frac gk.
$$

The maximum height occurs at the first zero of this [velocity](../../../velocity.md), hence

$$
\boxed{t_{\rm top}=\frac1k\log\left(1+\frac{kv_0}{g}\right).}
$$

Here $k$ has units of inverse [time](../../../time-in-physics.md). The limit $k\to0$ gives $v_0/g$, and positive [drag](../../../drag-physics.md) shortens the ascent relative to motion in a vacuum. The expression applies only up to the turning point; the [drag force](../../../drag-physics.md) opposes the subsequent downward motion.
