<h1 id="10/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At a fixed launch radius the escape threshold is zero total [mechanical energy](../../../../mechanical-energy.md). It depends on the initial [speed](../../../../speed.md) alone:

$$
\boxed{v_{\rm esc}=\sqrt{2GM/R}\quad\text{independent of outward launch direction in the nonrotating model}.}
$$

A direction aimed into the planet can of course cause impact before escape. If the planet rotates, the ground-relative launch [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) must be added to $\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf R$ to obtain the inertial [velocity](../../../../velocity.md). The condition becomes $|\mathbf u+\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf R|^2\ge2GM/R$: prograde launch needs less ground-relative [speed](../../../../speed.md) than retrograde launch. For a spherical planet the threshold expressed as inertial [speed](../../../../speed.md) at the same radius remains independent of direction.
