<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the shorter uniform rod, the [parallel axis theorem](../../../../parallel-axis-theorem.md) gives $I=m(a^2/12+x^2)$. Its equivalent [simple pendulum](../../../../simple-pendulum-split.md) length is $I/(mx)=x+a^2/(12x)$. The rod of length $2a$ pivoted at an end has equivalent length $2(2a)/3=4a/3$. Matching the [periods](../../../../period-of-a-function.md) therefore requires

$$
x+\frac{a^2}{12x}=\frac{4a}{3},\qquad 12x^2-16ax+a^2=0,
$$



$$
x=\frac{a}{6}(4\pm\sqrt{13}).
$$

A pivot on the shorter rod requires $0<x\le a/2$, leaving

$$
\boxed{x=\frac{4-\sqrt{13}}6a\simeq0.0657415a.}
$$

Either side of the rod's [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md) gives this distance. The other algebraic root is beyond the rod and would require a support extension of negligible [mass](../../../../mass.md).
