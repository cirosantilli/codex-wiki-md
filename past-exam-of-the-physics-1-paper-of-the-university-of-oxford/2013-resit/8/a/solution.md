<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose parallel axes along $z$, one through the [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md) and one displaced transversely by $\mathbf a$. Write the perpendicular position as $\mathbf r_\perp=\mathbf r'_{\perp}+\mathbf a$. Direct expansion of the [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) gives

$$
I=\int|\mathbf r'_\perp+\mathbf a|^2\,dm=I_G+2\mathbf a\cdot\int\mathbf r'_\perp\,dm+ma^2.
$$

The integral in the cross term is zero by the definition of the [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md). Therefore the [parallel axis theorem](../../../../parallel-axis-theorem.md) is

$$
\boxed{I=I_G+ma^2.}
$$

The axes must be parallel and the reference axis must pass through the [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md); the formula is not generally valid between arbitrary parallel axes using either axis as the reference.
