<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $\theta=0$ place the [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md) vertically below a horizontal pivot axis. The restoring [torque](../../../../torque.md) and [rotational kinetic energy](../../../../rotational-kinetic-energy.md) are $-mgh\sin\theta$ and $I\dot\theta^2/2$, so

$$
I\ddot\theta+mgh\sin\theta=0.
$$

For small angles, $\sin\theta=\theta+O(\theta^3)$, and the equation reduces to [simple harmonic motion](../../../../simple-harmonic-motion.md). Thus the [physical pendulum](../../../../physical-pendulum.md) has

$$
\boxed{\omega_0=\sqrt{\frac{mgh}{I}},\qquad T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{mgh}}.}
$$

This assumes negligible friction and a horizontal axis, with $h>0$. For a tilted axis the projected gravitational restoring [torque](../../../../torque.md) changes; an axis through the [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md) has no gravitational restoring [torque](../../../../torque.md).
