<h1 id="8/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the general [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md) just obtained, the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) gives

$$
I\ddot\theta+A\sin\theta=0.
$$

Using the [small-angle approximation](../../../../../small-angle-approximation.md) $\sin\theta\simeq\theta$, this is [simple harmonic motion](../../../../../simple-harmonic-motion.md) with $\omega_0^2=A/I$. Thus

$$
\boxed{T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l(m+M_r/3)}{g(m+M_r/2)}}\quad\longrightarrow\quad
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac lg}\ \text{for a negligible-mass rod}.}
$$

The endpoint [mass](../../../../../mass.md) cancels in the light-rod limit. A concrete comparison shows why the finite rod cannot silently be included: the same specified endpoint $m$ with $M_r=m$ gives $T=2\pi\sqrt{8l/(9g)}$, distinct from the negligible-rod result. This is a [physical pendulum](../../../../../physical-pendulum.md) when the rod contributes, and a [simple pendulum](../../../../../simple-pendulum-split.md) in the light-rod limit. Both expressions require small oscillation amplitude; the full sine equation remains nonlinear.
