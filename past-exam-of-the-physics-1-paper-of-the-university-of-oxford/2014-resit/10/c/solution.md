<h1 id="10/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Using [rotational kinetic energy](../../../../rotational-kinetic-energy.md) $K=I\omega^2/2$ at the two steady endpoint configurations,

$$
\boxed{K_i=155.150\,\mathrm J,\qquad K_f=539.593\,\mathrm J,\qquad
\Delta K=384.443\,\mathrm J.}
$$

At fixed [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) $J$, $K=J^2/(2I)$ increases when the [moment of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) decreases. The skater supplies [work](../../../../work.md) by pulling the arms inward; chemical energy in the muscles is converted into additional [rotational kinetic energy](../../../../rotational-kinetic-energy.md), with possible extra dissipation in a real skater. This does not require an external axial [torque](../../../../torque.md).

For the ideal rotating [point masses](../../../../point-mass.md), an inward radial [force](../../../../force-split.md) supplies the required centripetal acceleration. During contraction both that inward [force](../../../../force-split.md) and the radial displacement point inward, so their scalar product gives positive [work](../../../../work.md). At the initial and final steady configurations radial motion has ceased; any transient radial [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) must also be included in a full intermediate-time balance. The quoted difference compares the rotational endpoint energies only.
