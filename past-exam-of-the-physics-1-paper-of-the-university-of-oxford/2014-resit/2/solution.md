<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

For the attractive [central force](../../../central-force.md) $\mathbf F=-k\mathbf r$, with $k>0$, the [potential energy](../../../potential-energy.md) is $U(r)=kr^2/2$ up to an additive constant. Conservation of [angular momentum](../../../angular-momentum.md) confines the motion to a plane; writing its magnitude as $J=mr^2\dot\varphi$, the [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md) is

$$
T=\frac12m\dot r^2+\frac12mr^2\dot\varphi^2=\frac12m\dot r^2+\frac{J^2}{2mr^2}.
$$

At fixed $J$, the radial [energy](../../../energy-split.md) equation becomes $E=m\dot r^2/2+U_{\rm eff}(r)$, where

$$
\boxed{U_{\rm eff}(r)=\frac{J^2}{2mr^2}+\frac12kr^2.}
$$

The [effective potential](../../../effective-potential.md) combines the true [potential energy](../../../potential-energy.md) with the angular-motion contribution. Its negative derivative gives the radial equation $m\ddot r=-U_{\rm eff}'=J^2/(mr^3)-kr$; the first term is the centrifugal contribution arising from elimination of the angular coordinate.

A [circular orbit](../../../circular-orbit.md) is a fixed radial position at a stationary point:

$$
\boxed{r_0=\left(\frac{J^2}{mk}\right)^{1/4},\qquad
\Omega=\frac J{mr_0^2}=\sqrt{\frac km}.}
$$

For $J\ne0$, $U_{\rm eff}''(r_0)=4k>0$, so this is a stable minimum. Small radial disturbances have [angular frequency](../../../angular-frequency.md) $2\sqrt{k/m}$. For $J=0$, there is no nonzero-radius [circular orbit](../../../circular-orbit.md).
