<h1 id="9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In the parent's rest frame, the identical daughters have equal and opposite [momenta](../../../../momentum-split.md), and each has total [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) $110\,\mathrm{GeV}$. With daughter rest energy $Q=90\,\mathrm{GeV}$, each speed in this frame is

$$
\beta_*c=c\sqrt{1-\frac{Q^2}{110^2}},\qquad \beta_*=0.5749596.
$$

Transform to the rest frame of one daughter using the collinear [relativistic velocity addition](../../../../velocity-addition-formula.md) law. The other daughter then has speed

$$
\boxed{\frac wc=\frac{2\beta_*}{1+\beta_*^2}=\frac{44\sqrt{10}}{161}=0.864225.}
$$

An independent [invariant mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md) check is useful. In the rest frame of one daughter the other's total [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) satisfies $W^2=2Q^2+2QE'$, so $E'=178.8889\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and the relative [Lorentz factor](../../../../lorentz-factor.md) is $\gamma_{\rm rel}=E'/Q=161/81$. Hence $w/c=\sqrt{1-\gamma_{\rm rel}^{-2}}$, the same result.

The answer is unchanged if the parent is produced by a fixed-target collision. Its laboratory [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) then differs, but its rest [mass](../../../../mass.md) and two-body decay kinematics do not. The scalar product of the daughters' [four-momenta](../../../../four-momentum.md), equivalently their relative [Lorentz factor](../../../../lorentz-factor.md), depends only on $W$ and $Q$. A common boost cannot change the speed of one measured in the rest frame of the other.[Invariant relative Lorentz factor and associated relative speed](../../../../relative-speed-from-four-velocity-contraction.md)
