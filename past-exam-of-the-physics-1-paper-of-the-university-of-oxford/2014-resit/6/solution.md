<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Let the initial [velocity](../../../velocity.md) be $\mathbf v$ and the two final [velocities](../../../velocity.md) be $\mathbf u,\mathbf w$. In the stated nonrelativistic [elastic collision](../../../elastic-collision.md), [conservation of momentum](../../../momentum-conservation.md) and [conservation of energy](../../../conservation-of-energy.md) give

$$
\mathbf v=\mathbf u+\mathbf w,\qquad
v^2=u^2+w^2.
$$

Squaring the first equation and comparing it with the second yields

$$
v^2=u^2+w^2+2\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf w,
\qquad \boxed{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf w=0.}
$$

If both final [velocities](../../../velocity.md) are nonzero, their directions are perpendicular. If one is zero, the moving particle retains the initial [velocity](../../../velocity.md) and a scattering angle for the stationary particle is undefined. This includes the zero-deflection and head-on exchange limits rather than incorrectly assigning a direction to a zero vector. Equal [masses](../../../mass.md) and a stationary initial target are essential to the dot-product cancellation.
