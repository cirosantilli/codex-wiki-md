<h1 id="11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The first term, $m\dot r^2/2$, is radial [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md). The second, $L^2/(2mr^2)$, is tangential [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md), since conservation of [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) gives $L=mr^2\dot\varphi$. The final term, $-GMm/r$, is the gravitational [potential energy](../../../../potential-energy.md), with zero at infinite separation. Thus

$$
E=\frac12m\dot r^2+U_{\rm eff}(r),\qquad
\boxed{U_{\rm eff}(r)=\frac{L^2}{2mr^2}-\frac{GMm}{r}.}
$$

The second and third terms together form the radial [effective potential](../../../../effective-potential.md) for a [Kepler orbit](../../../../kepler-orbit.md). At an apsis the radial [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) vanishes, so $E=U_{\rm eff}$; for a bound noncircular orbit the two accessible roots give the nearest and furthest radii.
