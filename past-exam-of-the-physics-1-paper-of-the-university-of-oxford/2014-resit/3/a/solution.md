<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the [centre-of-mass frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md), the two equal incoming [rest masses](../../../../invariant-mass.md) have opposite [momenta](../../../../momentum-split.md) and total [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) $2E$. The squared total [four-momentum](../../../../four-momentum.md) fixes the composite [invariant mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md) $\mathcal M$:

$$
\mathcal M^2c^4=E_{\rm tot}^2-c^2|\mathbf P_{\rm tot}|^2=4E^2,
\qquad \boxed{\mathcal M=\frac{2E}{c^2}.}
$$

A change of [inertial frame](../../../../inertial-frame.md), including going to either particle's rest frame, cannot change this [invariant mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md). It includes the internal [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) and is generally larger than $2M_1$.

There is a terminology convention worth making explicit. The older “relativistic mass” means frame-dependent total [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) divided by $c^2$. To stop particle 1 requires a boost with [Lorentz factor](../../../../lorentz-factor.md) $\gamma=E/(M_1c^2)$, so the system has total [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) $2\gamma E=2E^2/(M_1c^2)$ in that frame. Under that alternative convention, its relativistic mass would be $2E^2/(M_1c^4)$. This is not its [invariant mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md); both quantities coincide only when $E=M_1c^2$ and the incoming particles already have zero relative motion.
