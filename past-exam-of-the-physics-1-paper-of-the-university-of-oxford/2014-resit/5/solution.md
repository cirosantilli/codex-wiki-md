<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Measure the vertical displacement $x$ downward from the spring's unstretched position. The [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md) is $m\dot x^2/2$, while the spring and gravitational [potential energies](../../../potential-energy.md) are $kx^2/2$ and $-mgx$. Thus the [Lagrangian](../../../lagrangian.md) is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal L=\frac12m\dot x^2-\frac12kx^2+mgx.}
$$

Applying the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md), $d(\partial\mathcal L/\partial\dot x)/dt-\partial\mathcal L/\partial x=0$, gives

$$
\boxed{m\ddot x+kx=mg.}
$$

Gravity shifts the equilibrium to $x_*=mg/k$. For displacement $\xi=x-x_*$ from that equilibrium, the [equation of motion](../../../equation-of-motion.md) becomes $\ddot\xi+(k/m)\xi=0$, giving [simple harmonic motion](../../../simple-harmonic-motion.md) with [angular frequency](../../../angular-frequency.md) $\omega=\sqrt{k/m}$. Choosing an upward coordinate instead reverses the gravitational term, but leaves the equilibrium-relative [angular frequency](../../../angular-frequency.md) unchanged.
