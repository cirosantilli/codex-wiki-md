<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use two central perpendicular axes in the plate. The [perpendicular axis theorem](../../../../perpendicular-axis-theorem.md) and the preceding [moments of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) give

$$
\boxed{I_{z,G}=\frac{Ma^2}{12}+\frac{Ma^2}{12}=\frac{Ma^2}{6}.}
$$

Alternatively, axes along adjacent edges have [moments of inertia](../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) $Ma^2/3$ each. The normal axis through their shared corner therefore has $2Ma^2/3$. It lies a distance $a/\sqrt2$ from the parallel normal axis through the centre, so the [parallel axis theorem](../../../../parallel-axis-theorem.md) yields $I_{z,G}=2Ma^2/3-M(a^2/2)=Ma^2/6$. This normal central result is independent of the in-plane axis-location ambiguity.
