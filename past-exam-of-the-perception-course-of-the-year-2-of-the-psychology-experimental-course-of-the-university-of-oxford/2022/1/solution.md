**Individual differences show that [color vision](../../../color-vision.md) depends on receptor signals, their neural interpretation and the task used to measure them.** They provide natural perturbations of the system, but differences in genes, matching, discrimination, naming and subjective appearance must not be treated as interchangeable.

A useful starting point is the [principle of univariance](../../../principle-of-univariance.md). For a [cone cell](../../../cone-cell.md) class $j$, a simplified [receptor quantum catch](../../../receptor-quantum-catch.md) is $Q_j=\int N(\lambda)a_j(\lambda)\,d\lambda$, where $N$ is the incident photon distribution, including optical filtering, and $a_j$ is effective absorptance. One receptor's absorption count does not uniquely identify [wavelength](../../../wavelength.md): increasing [intensity](../../../intensity-physics.md) can compensate for a change in [spectral sensitivity](../../../spectral-sensitivity.md). Comparing classes supplies spectral information. With three classes, distinct spectra can satisfy three equal-catch constraints and therefore be [metamers](../../../metamer.md). This is information loss: if a spectrum is represented by more than three independent [wavelength](../../../wavelength.md) bins, its three-channel sampling matrix has a nontrivial [kernel](../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md). Around a strictly positive spectrum, sufficiently small changes along that [kernel](../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) preserve nonnegative stimulus values and all three catches. Thus the observer, not [wavelength](../../../wavelength.md) alone, defines a [color](../../../color.md) match. With [luminance](../../../luminance.md) fixed, three independent receptor coordinates leave at most two independent chromatic coordinates.

Inherited differences test that account. [Dichromacy](../../../dichromacy.md) removes one independent class of cone input; [anomalous trichromacy](../../../anomalous-trichromacy.md) retains three classes but with altered spectral separation, often weakening particular discriminations. L and M are overlapping long- and middle-wave sensitivities, not detectors that individually label light “red” and “green.” Molecular work by [Jeremy Nathans](../../../jeremy-nathans.md) and colleagues identified cone pigment genes, then related inherited red–green variation to changes in the corresponding gene arrays.[Science 232, pp. 193–202, pigment-gene identification](../../../molecular-genetics-of-human-color-vision-the-genes-encoding-blue-green-and-red-pigments-by-jeremy-nathans-and-others.md)[Science 232, pp. 203–210, genotype–phenotype comparison](../../../molecular-genetics-of-inherited-variation-in-human-color-vision-by-jeremy-nathans-and-others.md) The important inference is a relation between biological variation and available spectral signals, not a claim that all observers with one label have identical vision.

A [Rayleigh color match](../../../rayleigh-color-match.md) makes that inference measurable. An observer adjusts a mixture of two narrowband lights to match a comparison light, with brightness adjusted separately. The accepted mixture and matching range constrain pigment sensitivities and discriminability. A [color discrimination](../../../color-discrimination.md) task instead measures whether two stimuli can be distinguished, ideally with [luminance](../../../luminance.md) cues controlled and a fitted [psychometric function](../../../psychometric-function.md). Plate tests and everyday [color](../../../color.md) names are less specific: learned labels or a brightness difference can support a correct response without normal chromatic discrimination. Conversely, reduced discrimination does not imply a world without [color](../../../color.md).

Individual differences also reveal compensation. [Heidi Hofer](../../../heidi-hofer.md) and colleagues used [adaptive optics](../../../adaptive-optics.md) retinal imaging with selective pigment bleaching and retinal densitometry to classify [cone cells](../../../cone-cell.md) in living eyes. In their small sample, men passing normal color-vision tests had L:M cone ratios spanning approximately 1.1:1 to 16.5:1; the paper's corrected abstract supplies the lower value. The ratio was not fixed even within the normal category.[Methods and abstract, eight observers; corrected L:M ratio; J Neurosci 25, pp. 9669–9679](../../../organization-of-the-human-trichromatic-cone-mosaic-by-heidi-hofer-and-others.md) This rules out a simple requirement for one universal cone proportion. For example, [opponent color processing](../../../opponent-color-processing.md) could compare gain-adjusted cone signals rather than raw cell counts. It is compatible with post-receptoral gain adjustment and [chromatic adaptation](../../../chromatic-adaptation.md), but normal test scores do not prove identical [subjective experiences](../../../subjective-experience.md) or one uniquely identified compensatory mechanism.

A stronger question is whether more receptor classes create more perceptual dimensions. Heterozygous carriers of certain X-linked pigment variants can, through [X-chromosome inactivation](../../../x-chromosome-inactivation.md), have a [retina](../../../retina.md) containing more than three spectrally distinct cone types. Yet four types need not supply four functionally independent signals to behavior. [Gabriele Jordan](../../../gabriele-jordan.md) and colleagues combined forced-choice matching, multidimensional scaling and genetic analysis. One of 24 obligate carriers of deuteranomaly showed behavior consistent with [tetrachromacy](../../../tetrachromacy.md) across their tests; most did not.[Abstract and behavioral/genetic tests, J Vis 10(8):12](../../../the-dimensionality-of-color-vision-in-carriers-of-anomalous-trichromacy-by-gabriele-jordan-and-others.md) This distinction between potential and demonstrated extra dimensionality is crucial. The selected sample cannot establish population prevalence, and additional [opsin](../../../opsin.md) variants alone cannot establish enhanced discrimination or richer experience.

Finally, optical and experiential differences must be controlled. Lens and macular filtering change the spectrum reaching the [cone cells](../../../cone-cell.md); age, adaptation, illumination and task [learning](../../../learning.md) can therefore mimic or modify receptor differences. Repeated measurements should separate within-person variability from stable between-person variation. Matching should be compared with discrimination, naming and appearance judgments rather than assuming that one measures all the others. [Color](../../../color.md) naming can change without changing the receptor information; matching can change without entailing a particular verbal category.

The strongest conclusions arise when genetics, retinal measurements and controlled behavior converge. **Variation establishes both the constraints imposed by receptor sampling and the flexibility of later processing; it does not directly disclose another person's [color](../../../color.md) qualia.**
