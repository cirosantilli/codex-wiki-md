# Radius change at constant angular momentum

↑ **Parent:** [Conservation of angular momentum](conservation-of-angular-momentum.md)

Place two equal [point masses](point-mass.md) of $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ each on opposite sides of a fixed origin, at equal [radius](radius.md) $r$, and let them share a signed [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) $\omega=\dot\theta$ in a plane. Their total [mass](mass.md) is $M=2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and their [moment of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md) about the perpendicular axis is $I=Mr^2$. Radial pulling [forces](force.md) have zero [torque](torque.md) about that origin, so

$$
L_z=I\omega=Mr^2\omega=\mathrm{constant},\qquad
K_{\mathrm{rot}}=\frac12I\omega^2=\frac{L_z^2}{2I}.
$$

Take an initial [radius](radius.md) $r_i=1\,\mathrm m$ and [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) $\omega_i=1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, and a final [radius](radius.md) $r_f=0.5\,\mathrm m$. Then

$$
I_i=2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2},\quad I_f=0.5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2},\quad
L_z=2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s},\quad \omega_f=4\,\mathrm{rad/s},
$$

and

$$
K_i=1\,\mathrm J,\qquad K_f=4\,\mathrm J,\qquad W=K_f-K_i=+3\,\mathrm J.
$$

These are endpoint states with $\dot r_i=\dot r_f=0$. During the pull, the full [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) also contains radial motion: $K=M\dot r^2/2+K_{\mathrm{rot}}$. Assume the pulling apparatus supplies all net [work](work.md) to the two [point masses](point-mass.md); the [work-energy theorem](work-energy-theorem.md) then fixes its net [work](work.md) at $+3\,\mathrm J$, including any radial acceleration and braking. Each [mass](mass.md) spirals inward during the pull, allowing its inward [force](force.md) to do positive [work](work.md). [Conservation of angular momentum](conservation-of-angular-momentum.md) therefore predicts the final [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) without requiring [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) conservation. For an apparatus included in a larger system, its stored [energy](energy.md) supplies the increase consistently with [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md). OpenStax uses the same mechanism to explain a skater pulling her arms inward. [OpenStax, section 11.3: work during a change of moment of inertia](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/11-3-conservation-of-angular-momentum)
