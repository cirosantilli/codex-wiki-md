# Freedman-Clauser experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Bell test](bell-test.md)

In 1972, [Stuart J. Freedman](stuart-jay-freedman.md) and [John F. Clauser](john-francis-clauser.md) excited a [calcium](calcium.md) beam from an oven with a filtered [deuterium](deuterium.md) arc lamp. A [calcium](calcium.md) emission cascade supplied paired [photons](photon.md); an [optical lens](lens-optics.md) system, [optical filters](optical-filter.md), removable glass-plate [polarizers](polarizer.md) and cooled [photomultipliers](photomultiplier.md) selected their [linear polarizations](linear-polarization.md). They compared [coincidence circuit](coincidence-circuit.md) rates at relative angles $22.5^\circ$ and $67.5^\circ$, normalized with [polarizers](polarizer.md) removed. Alternating configurations controlled drift; delayed counts estimated [accidental coincidences](accidental-coincidence.md). The violation agreed with [quantum mechanics](quantum-mechanics-split.md), conditional on their explicit assumption that passage through a [polarizer](polarizer.md) did not change a photon's detection [probability](probability.md). This was a static-analyzer test with a [detection loophole](detection-loophole.md), rather than the ideal four-output [CHSH inequality](chsh-inequality.md) statistic.[Printed pp. 2-7, assumption (3), Eq. (3), Figs. 1-3](experimental-test-of-local-hidden-variable-theories-by-stuart-jay-freedman-and-john-francis-clauser.md)
