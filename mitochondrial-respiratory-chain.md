# Mitochondrial respiratory chain

↑ **Parent:** [Electron transport chain](electron-transport-chain.md)

[NADH](nadh.md) supplies two [electrons](electron.md) to [respiratory complex I](respiratory-complex-i.md), then [ubiquinone](ubiquinone.md), [respiratory complex III](respiratory-complex-iii.md), [cytochrome c](cytochrome-c.md) and [cytochrome c oxidase](cytochrome-c-oxidase.md); terminal [oxygen](oxygen.md) [reduction](reduction-chemistry.md) forms [water](water.md). [Succinate dehydrogenase](succinate-dehydrogenase.md), or [respiratory complex II](succinate-dehydrogenase.md), supplies another entry to [ubiquinone](ubiquinone.md). [Respiratory complex I](respiratory-complex-i.md), [respiratory complex III](respiratory-complex-iii.md) and [cytochrome c oxidase](cytochrome-c-oxidase.md) move [protons](proton.md) outward; [respiratory complex II](succinate-dehydrogenase.md) does not.[§7.3, Citric Acid Cycle, succinate oxidation; §7.4, Figure 7.12, Complex I, Q and Complex II, Complex III and Complex IV: routes and proton transport](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others.md)

**[Figure 9](#image-electron-and-proton-routes-in-the-mitochondrial-respiratory-chain). Electron and proton routes in the mitochondrial respiratory chain**. Original qualitative schematic. Blue arrows follow reducing equivalents; orange arrows denote outward [proton](proton.md) transfer and green arrows denote return through [ATP synthase](atp-synthase.md). The [inner mitochondrial membrane](inner-mitochondrial-membrane.md) separates the [mitochondrial matrix](mitochondrial-matrix.md) from the [intermembrane space](intermembrane-space.md). Arrow widths encode no [reaction rates](reaction-rate.md) or [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md). The chemical [protons](proton.md) consumed in forming [water](water.md) are distinct from pumped [protons](proton.md); the sketch omits transport of [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) and [inorganic phosphate](inorganic-phosphate.md).

For an original [electron](electron.md) balance, supply exactly $6\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of [NADH](nadh.md) to an ideal preparation with no other [electron donor](electron-donor.md) or competing [electron acceptor](electron-acceptor.md). The donor supplies $12\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of [electrons](electron.md). Complete [reduction](reduction-chemistry.md) of one [oxygen](oxygen.md) [molecule](molecule-split.md) needs four [electrons](electron.md), so the corresponding [oxygen](oxygen.md) consumption is $3\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$. Adding the two formal [half-reactions](half-reaction.md) makes the result explicit:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\,\mathrm{NADH}&\longrightarrow2\,\mathrm{NAD}^{+}+2\,\mathrm H^{+}+4e^-,\\
\mathrm O_2+4\,\mathrm H^{+}+4e^-&\longrightarrow2\,\mathrm{H_2O},\\
2\,\mathrm{NADH}+\mathrm O_2+2\,\mathrm H^{+}&\longrightarrow2\,\mathrm{NAD}^{+}+2\,\mathrm{H_2O}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The cancelling [electrons](electron.md) determine the donor-to-[oxygen](oxygen.md) ratio. These chemical equations do not count transmembrane [protons](proton.md) or determine an [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) yield. Chemical [protons](proton.md) consumed in [water](water.md) formation and transported [protons](proton.md) have different roles in the ledger.
