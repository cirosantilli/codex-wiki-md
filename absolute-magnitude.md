# Absolute magnitude

↑ **Parent:** [Astronomical magnitude](astronomical-magnitude.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_magnitude)

Absolute magnitude $M$, in the stellar convention used here, is the [apparent magnitude](apparent-magnitude.md) a source would have at $10$ [parsecs](parsec.md), with [interstellar extinction](interstellar-extinction.md) removed. The hypothetical observation uses the specified [photometric passband](photometric-passband.md) and [magnitude zero point](magnitude-zero-point.md), preserving the source's emitted spectrum and [luminosity](luminosity.md). Moving every source to this common distance lets us compare intrinsic brightness without confusing it with distance. [Caltech Ay 20, Lecture 3: Apparent versus absolute magnitudes](https://sites.astro.caltech.edu/~george/ay20/Ay20-Lec3x.pdf).

For a fixed source with [isotropy](isotropy.md) of emission, in transparent space with negligible relativistic and cosmological effects, the [inverse-square law](inverse-square-law.md) gives the hypothetical measurement $F_{10}$ and [absolute magnitude](absolute-magnitude.md)

$$
F_{10}=F(d)\left(\frac{d}{10\,\mathrm{pc}}\right)^2,\qquad M=-2.5\log_{10}\left(\frac{F_{10}}{F_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right).
$$

The factor applies in the same [photometric passband](photometric-passband.md), with unchanged emitted spectrum. Thus [absolute magnitude](absolute-magnitude.md) compares [luminosity](luminosity.md) in that response, rather than automatically the total [bolometric luminosity](luminosity.md) over all [wavelengths](wavelength.md).

Consider a toy source with $M=0$. Its [apparent magnitude](apparent-magnitude.md) is $m=0$ at $10$ [parsecs](parsec.md), $m=5$ at $100$ [parsecs](parsec.md), and $m=10$ at $1000$ [parsecs](parsec.md), neglecting [interstellar extinction](interstellar-extinction.md). Its [absolute magnitude](absolute-magnitude.md) remains $M=0$ throughout: moving the observer changes received [radiative flux](radiative-flux.md), not emitted [luminosity](luminosity.md). Another toy source with $M=-5$ has $100$ times its passband output under the same [isotropy](isotropy.md) and calibration assumptions. Negative [absolute magnitudes](absolute-magnitude.md) are allowed too; "absolute" names the reference-distance convention, not an [absolute value](absolute-value.md).

A calibrated [standard candle](standard-candle.md) reverses this reasoning: its known [absolute magnitude](absolute-magnitude.md) and measured [apparent magnitude](apparent-magnitude.md) give a [distance modulus](distance-modulus.md). This is why estimating intrinsic brightness is so useful for measuring astronomical distances. [Fraknoi, Morrison and Wolff, Astronomy 2e, 19.3: Intrinsic brightness and distance](https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/19-3-variable-stars-one-key-to-cosmic-distances).
