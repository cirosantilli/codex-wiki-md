# Strong local minimizer of a variational integral

↑ **Parent:** [Calculus of variations](calculus-of-variations-split.md)

For a fixed-endpoint variational integral, an admissible function $u$ is a strong local minimizer if $I(v)\geq I(u)$ for all admissible $v$ whose [uniform norm](supremum-norm.md) distance from $u$ is sufficiently small. Derivatives can be far apart. Hence strong local minimality implies [weak local minimality](weak-local-minimizer-of-a-variational-integral.md), while a global minimizer implies both. Small narrow bumps and rapid low-amplitude oscillations explain why the reverse implication can fail.
