<h1 id="lenz-s-law">Lenz's law</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Faraday's law of induction](faraday-s-law-of-induction.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenz's_law)

[Lenz's law](lenz-s-law.md) expresses the minus sign in [Faraday's law of induction](faraday-s-law-of-induction.md): the induced [electromotive force](electromotive-force.md) drives a passive resistive loop's [electric current](electric-current.md) in the direction whose own [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) opposes the imposed change in [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md). **It opposes the change of flux, not invariably the external magnetic field.** Choose an [oriented surface](oriented-surface.md) and its positive boundary direction first, find the sign of $d\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}/dt$, and then use the [right-hand rule](right-hand-rule.md) to translate the signed [electric current](electric-current.md) into a physical direction. [OpenStax University Physics 2, §13.2: current direction and changing flux](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/13-2-lenzs-law).

In the fixed-loop example in [Faraday's law of induction](faraday-s-law-of-induction.md), out-of-page [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md) increases at $0.0060\,\mathrm{Wb/s}$. With counterclockwise positive, $I=-0.0030\,\mathrm A$ is clockwise, producing an in-page [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) through the loop. Reverse the ramp to $B(t)=0.40\,\mathrm T-(0.30\,\mathrm{T/s})t$ on the same one-second interval: now $\mathcal E=+0.0060\,\mathrm V$ and $I=+0.0030\,\mathrm A$. The counterclockwise [electric current](electric-current.md) produces an out-of-page [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md), supporting the decreasing external [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md). A steady out-of-page [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) with fixed loop geometry gives no induced [electromotive force](electromotive-force.md) in either direction.

For the increasing-area example in [motional electromotive force](motional-electromotive-force.md), clockwise [electric current](electric-current.md) runs down the right-hand rod. Its [magnetic force on a current-carrying wire](magnetic-force-on-a-current-carrying-wire.md) points left, opposing the imposed motion to the right. Writing $I_{\mathrm{mag}}=B\ell v/R$ for the current magnitude, the [power](power.md) required to maintain constant [speed](speed.md) is

$$
P_{\mathrm{drive}}=(I_{\mathrm{mag}}\ell B)v
=\frac{(B\ell v)^2}{R}=I_{\mathrm{mag}}^2R=P_{\mathrm{Joule}}.
$$

Thus the sign agrees with [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md): the external drive supplies the [Joule heating](joule-heating.md). The [magnetic forces do no work](magnetic-forces-do-no-work.md) on individual charges; mechanical [work](work.md) by the drive supplies the electrical [energy](energy-split.md). This also explains the usefulness of [electromagnetic braking of a sliding rod](electromagnetic-braking-of-a-sliding-rod.md) and the [terminal speed of a falling conducting loop](terminal-speed-of-a-falling-conducting-loop.md). [OpenStax University Physics 2, §13.3: mechanical power and resistive heating](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/13-3-motional-emf).

The sign of induced [electromotive force](electromotive-force.md) alone does not determine an arbitrary circuit's instantaneous [electric current](electric-current.md). Retaining constant [self-inductance](self-inductance.md) $L$ gives $L\,dI/dt+RI=-d\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}/dt$, so a pre-existing [electric current](electric-current.md) can persist while the induced drive opposes its change. The distinction matters in the [lossless limit of magnetic loop braking](lossless-limit-of-magnetic-loop-braking.md). The resistive toy examples use $L=0$ as an approximation, not a general consequence of [Lenz's law](lenz-s-law.md).
