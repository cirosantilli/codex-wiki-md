# Random phase approximation

↑ **Parent:** [Linear response theory](linear-response-theory.md)

The [random phase approximation](random-phase-approximation.md) treats the independent-particle response to the self-consistent total field, including the induced mean [Coulomb potential](coulomb-potential-energy.md). With a positive static number-response coefficient $\Pi_0=-\delta n/\delta U$, a potential $U=-e\phi$ gives $\delta n=e\Pi_0\phi_{\rm tot}$. Combining induced charge $-e\delta n$ with [Poisson equation](poisson-s-equation.md) gives $\epsilon=1+e^2\Pi_0/(\epsilon_0q^2)$. Exchange-correlation vertex corrections beyond this self-consistent response are omitted. It is a response approximation, not a literal assumption that every microscopic electron phase is independently randomized.
