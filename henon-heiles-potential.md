<h1 id="henon-heiles-potential">Hénon-Heiles potential</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Hénon-Heiles system](henon-heiles-system.md)

The [potential energy](potential-energy.md) $V(x,y)=\tfrac12(x^2+y^2)+x^2y-y^3/3$ in the [Hénon-Heiles system](henon-heiles-system.md) has the following expression in [polar coordinates](polar-coordinates.md):

$$
V=\frac12r^2+\frac13r^3\sin(3\theta),
$$

so it is invariant under rotation through $2\pi/3$. Solving $\nabla V=0$ gives a strict [local minimum](local-minimum.md) at the origin and three [saddle points](saddle-point.md), at $(0,1)$ and $(\pm\sqrt3/2,-1/2)$. All three [saddle points](saddle-point.md) have value $1/6$. Their common [level set](level-set.md) factors exactly:

$$
6V-1=(2y+1)\bigl[3x^2-(y-1)^2\bigr].
$$

Thus this [level set](level-set.md) is the union of the three lines $y=-1/2$ and $y=1\pm\sqrt3x$. They bound an equilateral triangle containing the [local minimum](local-minimum.md). The [Hessian matrix](hessian-matrix.md) is the identity at the origin; at each of the other three [stationary points](stationary-point.md) it has [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) $3$ and $-1$. For energies $0\leq E<1/6$, the central accessible region is bounded; above that value the three saddle passages allow paths from the centre to arbitrarily large radius. This follows directly from the radial potential: in each direction with $\sin(3\theta)<0$, its radial maximum occurs at $r=-1/\sin(3\theta)$, with value $1/[6\sin^2(3\theta)]\geq1/6$.
