# Adenosine triphosphate

↑ **Parent:** [Nucleotide](nucleotide.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenosine_triphosphate)

[ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) is a [nucleotide](nucleotide.md) consisting of [adenosine](adenosine.md), itself [adenine](adenine.md) joined to [ribose](ribose.md), carrying three [phosphate](phosphate.md) groups. From the [ribose](ribose.md) outward, these groups are labelled $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$. [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) connects [energy](energy.md)-supplying processes to [energy coupling](energy-coupling.md) for synthesis and [active transport](active-transport.md); the same carrier can be regenerated and reused. [OpenStax Biology 2e, 6.4: ATP composition and recycling](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/6-4-atp-adenosine-triphosphate).

Both sides retain [adenine](adenine.md) and [ribose](ribose.md); [hydrolysis](hydrolysis.md) consumes [water](water.md), whereas the reverse net [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md) forms [water](water.md). The [energy](energy.md) labels describe transfers, not extra material [reactants](reactant.md) or [reaction products](product-chemistry.md).

The net [hydrolysis](hydrolysis.md) commonly used in [energy coupling](energy-coupling.md) is

$$
\mathrm{ATP}+\mathrm{H_2O}\longrightarrow\mathrm{ADP}+\mathrm P_i.
$$

Here $\mathrm P_i$ denotes [inorganic phosphate](inorganic-phosphate.md). At buffered [pH](ph.md) $7$, this biochemical shorthand groups the appropriate [protonation](protonation.md) states into pools and leaves [proton](proton.md) exchange with the [buffer solution](buffer-solution.md) implicit. An explicit, charge-balanced choice is $\mathrm{ATP}^{4-}+\mathrm{H_2O}\longrightarrow\mathrm{ADP}^{3-}+\mathrm{HPO_4}^{2-}+\mathrm H^+$, without implying these are the only [chemical species](chemical-species.md) present. See the [IUBMB recommendations for biochemical thermodynamics](https://iubmb.qmul.ac.uk/thermod2/).

The quoted textbook biochemical [standard state](standard-state.md) value is approximately

$$
\Delta G^{\circ\prime}=-30.5\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

This convention uses fixed [pH](ph.md) $7$, [temperature](temperature.md) $298.15\,\mathrm K$ ($25\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$), reference [pressure](pressure.md) $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$ ($1\,\mathrm{bar}$), unit reference [thermodynamic activities](thermodynamic-activity.md) for dissolved [reactants](reactant.md) and [reaction products](product-chemistry.md), and [water](water.md) [thermodynamic activity](thermodynamic-activity.md) approximated by one. The prime indicates the fixed-[pH](ph.md) biochemical convention. See [OpenStax Biology 2e, 6.4: standard ATP hydrolysis value](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/6-4-atp-adenosine-triphosphate) and [Biology 2e, 6.2: biochemical standard conditions](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/6-2-potential-kinetic-free-and-activation-energy). This is a reference value, rather than a universal value inside a [biological cell](biological-cell.md); precise tables must also specify [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) conditions and any [magnesium](magnesium.md) [ion](ion.md) binding convention. Different reference conventions and data treatments give different tabulated values, as discussed in [Li et al., 2010: a database of thermodynamic quantities for biochemical reactions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3299200/).

An [exergonic reaction](exergonic-reaction.md) has a negative change in [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md), so the overall [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) [hydrolysis](hydrolysis.md) is favourable under those reference conditions. Breaking a [chemical bond](chemical-bond.md) by itself costs [energy](energy.md); forming new [chemical bonds](chemical-bond.md) can release [energy](energy.md). The net change compares all [reactants](reactant.md) with all [reaction products](product-chemistry.md), including their interactions with [water](water.md) and the [entropy](entropy.md) contribution. Calling the phosphate connection a "high-energy bond" describes a large favourable change in [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md) on [hydrolysis](hydrolysis.md), rather than [energy](energy.md) released by bond breaking alone. See [7.5 Strengths of Ionic and Covalent Bonds](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/7-5-strengths-of-ionic-and-covalent-bonds.md) and [Cooper, The Cell: ATP and metabolic energy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9903/).

Composition changes the driving force. At the same fixed [pH](ph.md), [temperature](temperature.md), [pressure](pressure.md), and binding convention, write the molar reaction change as

$$
\Delta G=\Delta G^{\circ\prime}+RT\ln Q,\qquad
Q=\frac{a_{\mathrm{ADP}}a_{\mathrm P_i}}{a_{\mathrm{ATP}}}.
$$

Here $R$ is the [molar gas constant](molar-gas-constant.md), $T$ the absolute [temperature](temperature.md), and $a$ a [dimensionless](dimensionless-quantity.md) [thermodynamic activity](thermodynamic-activity.md) for a biochemical pool. Constant [water](water.md) [thermodynamic activity](thermodynamic-activity.md) is absorbed into the reference term; fixed [pH](ph.md) accounts for buffered [protons](proton.md). The shorthand $\Delta G$ means transformed molar reaction [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md) change, $\Delta_rG'$, rather than a finite sample's total change. The [natural logarithm](natural-logarithm.md) acts on a [dimensionless](dimensionless-quantity.md) [reaction quotient](reaction-quotient.md), never on [concentrations](concentration.md) with units. See the [IUBMB biochemical thermodynamics recommendations](https://iubmb.qmul.ac.uk/thermod2/) and the general [equilibrium constant](equilibrium-constant.md) relation.

For a stipulated ideal dilute [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md), let $a_i=[i]/c^\circ$, with $c^\circ=1\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. Choose $[\mathrm{ATP}]=5\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$, $[\mathrm{ADP}]=0.5\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$, and $[\mathrm P_i]=1\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$, ignoring [activity coefficient](activity-coefficient.md) corrections and [magnesium](magnesium.md) binding. These are illustrative inputs, not a claim about a particular [biological cell](biological-cell.md). Then

$$
Q=\frac{(0.0005)(0.001)}{0.005}=10^{-4},\qquad
RT=(0.008314462618)(298.15)=2.47895703\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}},
$$

and

$$
\Delta G=-30.5+2.47895703\ln(10^{-4})
\approx-53.332\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

Increasing $Q$ tenfold raises $\Delta G$ by $RT\ln10\approx5.708\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ at this [temperature](temperature.md). Thus the [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) to [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) ratio and available [inorganic phosphate](inorganic-phosphate.md) matter, even with the same [standard state](standard-state.md) value. These are local molar predictions at the stipulated composition, rather than a constant total yield as an isolated sample reacts.
