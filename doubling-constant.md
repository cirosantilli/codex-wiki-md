# Doubling constant

↑ **Parent:** [Additive combinatorics](additive-combinatorics-split.md)

The [doubling constant](doubling-constant.md) of a nonempty [finite set](finite-set.md) $S\subseteq G$ in an [abelian group](abelian-group.md) $G$ is

$$
K(S)=\frac{|S+S|}{|S|}.
$$

It measures the [cardinality](cardinality.md) of the [sumset](sumset.md) relative to the original [set](set-split.md). For the [integer](integer-split.md) examples, $K(A)=7/4$ and $K(B)=10/4=5/2$. A nonconstant [arithmetic progression](arithmetic-progression.md) with $n$ members has [doubling constant](doubling-constant.md) $(2n-1)/n$, which stays below two as $n$ grows. This is the sense in which its [sumset](sumset.md) has small growth.

In any [abelian group](abelian-group.md), $K(S)\geq1$: fixing $s_0\in S$ puts a translated copy $s_0+S$ inside $S+S$. The stronger [integer](integer-split.md) bound gives $K(S)\geq2-1/|S|$ only in that setting. A finite [subgroup](subgroup.md) $H$ has $K(H)=1$, as does every [coset](coset.md) $x+H$, since its [sumset](sumset.md) is $2x+H$. In particular $H=\{0,2,4\}\subseteq\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$ has [doubling constant](doubling-constant.md) one.

Small [doubling constant](doubling-constant.md) is useful because it constrains repeated growth, as in the [Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality](plunnecke-ruzsa-inequality.md). It signals structure such as [arithmetic progressions](arithmetic-progression.md) and [subgroups](subgroup.md); precise descriptions depend on the ambient [abelian group](abelian-group.md) and are the subject of the [Freiman-Ruzsa theorem](freiman-ruzsa-theorem.md). The [additive energy](additive-energy.md) bound below gives a simpler immediate consequence: $E(S)\geq|S|^3/K(S)$.
