<h1 id="rontgen-x-ray-discovery-experiment">Röntgen X-ray discovery experiment</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [X-ray](x-ray.md)

[Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen](wilhelm-conrad-rontgen.md)'s 1895 first communication describes an [induction coil](induction-coil.md) driving an evacuated [gas-discharge tube](gas-discharge-tube.md), enclosed in black cardboard in darkness. A paper screen coated with [barium platinocyanide](barium-platinocyanide.md) showed [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) even with its uncoated side facing the tube, visible two metres away. The covering excluded ordinary illumination.[Röntgen, On a New Kind of Rays, §§1–2; inspected reprint p. 153](on-a-new-kind-of-rays-by-wilhelm-conrad-rontgen.md)

He interposed materials: a thousand-page book transmitted; $1.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ lead was practically opaque. A stepped stack of foil produced a [photographic plate](photographic-plate.md) record. Different materials were not equivalent at equal density and thickness. [Photographic plates](photographic-plate.md) checked visual observations while remaining covered against ordinary illumination.[First communication, §§2–6; reprint pp. 153–154: absorption tests and photographic controls](on-a-new-kind-of-rays-by-wilhelm-conrad-rontgen.md)

Section 14 lists photographs of hand bones, wire on a bobbin, boxed weights, an enclosed compass, and a lead-painted door. It does not identify the hand's owner. Magnetic deflection moved the [cathode ray](cathode-ray.md) impact point and thus the source, without bending the emerging [X-rays](x-ray.md). Their nature remained unsettled.[First communication, §§11–14 and 17; reprint pp. 156–157](on-a-new-kind-of-rays-by-wilhelm-conrad-rontgen.md)

The observations belong to 1895; the contemporary English translation appeared in Nature on 23 January 1896.[Catalogue, On a new kind of rays: notes identify the Nature translation and its publication date](on-a-new-kind-of-rays-by-wilhelm-conrad-rontgen.md) The experimental logic separates production, transmission and detection. A visibly opaque covering can transmit [X-rays](x-ray.md); [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) then converts deposited [energy](energy-split.md) into visible [light](light.md). A shadow measures a difference between paths through an object, rather than a direct view of its surface. Neither a screen's brightness nor photographic darkness alone measures an absolute [X-ray](x-ray.md) [energy](energy-split.md) or establishes the [X-ray attenuation](x-ray-attenuation.md) law below.
