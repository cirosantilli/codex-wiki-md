# Coronagraph pupil-stop observing experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Lyot coronagraph](lyot-coronagraph.md)

Schmid et al. tested the [Lyot stop](lyot-stop.md) on sky using the close binary $\alpha$ Hydri with SPHERE/ZIMPOL at the Very Large Telescope. They kept the CLC-S-WF [coronagraphic focal plane mask](coronagraphic-focal-plane-mask.md), the NB730 filter and pupil-stabilized imaging mode, and compared observations without a pupil stop and with stopB1\_2. Each configuration used ten 8-second exposures. One camera imaged the [optical pupil](optical-pupil.md) while the other simultaneously imaged the [focal plane](focal-plane.md), connecting the blocked pupil features to the image improvement. [Schmid et al., 2018, §4.2, figures 19–20 and table 10, test A](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833620).

Bright rings occurred at the inner and outer pupil boundaries; the stop covered them and other unwanted features. To compare [point spread functions](point-spread-function-split.md), the authors multiplied the no-stop image by $0.68$, accounting for lost off-axis transmission. The companion's reported detection significance rose from $5.2$ to $9.7$. This demonstrates a useful gain despite lost companion [light](light.md). It measures the complete stop, including obstruction, support-vane and faulty-actuator blockers; it does not isolate outer-rim clipping alone or establish a universal suppression factor. The figures here are independent toys, not reproductions of those observations. [Schmid et al., 2018, §4.2 and table 10](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833620).
