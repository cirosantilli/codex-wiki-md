# Unbounded linear terminal-wealth utility

↑ **Parent:** [Expected utility maximization](expected-utility-maximization.md)

If a positive terminal [state-price density](state-price-density.md) has essential infimum zero, let $A_n=\{\zeta_T<1/n\}$ and $X_n=w_0\mathbf1_{A_n}/\mathbb E[\zeta_T\mathbf1_{A_n}]$. Whenever $\mathbb P(A_n)>0$, these claims cost $w_0$ and satisfy $\mathbb EX_n>nw_0$. In a Brownian [complete market](complete-market.md) with constant nonzero [market price of risk](market-price-of-risk.md), $\zeta_T$ has a [lognormal distribution](log-normal-distribution.md) and all these events have positive probability. Thus bounded market coefficients and an increasing [concave](concave-function.md) utility do not alone ensure a finite attained optimum: the linear utility $U(x)=x$ already fails. Usual inverse-marginal-utility formulas require further utility and integrability hypotheses.
