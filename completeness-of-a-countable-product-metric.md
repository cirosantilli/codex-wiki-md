# Completeness of a countable product metric

↑ **Parent:** [Countable product metric](countable-product-metric.md)

If every factor is a [complete metric space](complete-metric-space.md), the [countable product metric](countable-product-metric.md) is complete. For a product-Cauchy sequence, the inequality $2^{-i}\phi(d_i(x_i^{(m)},x_i^{(n)}))\leq D(x^{(m)},x^{(n)})$, with $\phi(t)=t/(1+t)$, makes each coordinate sequence [Cauchy](cauchy-sequence.md). Its coordinate limits define a product point. Coordinatewise convergence and the uniformly small summable tail imply convergence to that point in $D$. It matters that the original distances are bounded before they are summed: summing arbitrary unbounded metrics directly need not be finite.
