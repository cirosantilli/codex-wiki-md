# Gaussian random walk spends zero density of time in bounded sets

↑ **Parent:** [Random walk](random-walk.md)

For independent $N(0,1)$ increments, a [random walk](random-walk.md) has $S_j\sim N(0,j)$. If $B$ is a bounded measurable set, its [occupation time](occupation-time-of-a-continuous-time-markov-chain.md) [probability](probability.md) obeys $\mathbb P(S_j\in B)\leq |B|/\sqrt{2\pi j}$. Consequently $\mathbb E\sum_j\mathbf1_B(S_j)/j<\infty$, and [Tonelli's theorem](tonelli-theorem.md) makes this series finite [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md). [Kronecker lemma](kronecker-lemma.md) then gives the displayed zero occupation density. This also shows why its empirical occupation [probabilities](probability.md) cannot converge to a [probability](probability.md) measure simultaneously on all bounded intervals and the whole real line: [continuity](continuous-function.md) from below would contradict the preserved total mass one.
