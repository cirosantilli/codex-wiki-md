# Antiproton

↑ **Parent:** [Antiparticle](antiparticle.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiproton)

The [antiproton](antiproton.md) is the [antiparticle](antiparticle.md) of the [proton](proton.md): it has the same [rest mass](invariant-mass.md) and [spin](spin.md) $1/2$, with opposite [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) and [baryon number](baryon-number.md). A negative [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) alone cannot identify an [antiproton](antiproton.md), because a negative [pion](pion.md) also bends in that direction in a [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md). Combining [magnetic rigidity](magnetic-rigidity.md) with [velocity](velocity.md) determines a [rest mass](invariant-mass.md); agreement with the [proton](proton.md) [rest mass](invariant-mass.md) then makes the identification much more discriminating.

The [mode expansion of a Dirac field](mode-expansion-of-a-dirac-field.md) explains how independent particle and [antiparticle](antiparticle.md) excitations enter a relativistic field description. It supplies a theoretical language for the [antiproton](antiproton.md), while the [Chamberlain-Segrè antiproton identification](chamberlain-segre-antiproton-identification.md) supplies an experimental test. The [proton](proton.md) is a composite [baryon](baryon.md), so a free [Dirac field](dirac-field.md) is not a complete account of its internal dynamics or of [antiproton](antiproton.md) production in a target.
