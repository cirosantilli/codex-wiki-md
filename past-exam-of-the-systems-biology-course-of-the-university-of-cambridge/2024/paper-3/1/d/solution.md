<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At a conventional 5% [significance level](../../../../../significance-level.md), **there is evidence for an association with sex after adjusting for weight**, because its two-sided [p-value](../../../../../p-value.md) is $0.00131<0.05$. There is **insufficient evidence for a weight association after adjusting for sex**, because its [p-value](../../../../../p-value.md) is $0.62392>0.05$.

These [Student t-tests](../../../../../student-s-t-test.md) test whether the corresponding [regression coefficients](../../../../../regression-coefficient.md) are zero within the additive [multiple linear regression](../../../../../multiple-linear-regression.md). Failure to reject the weight hypothesis does not establish an exactly zero effect; these associations alone also do not justify a [causal inference](../../../../../causal-inference-split.md). The later model with a [statistical interaction](../../../../../interaction-statistics.md) asks a different question, allowing the weight association to differ by sex.
