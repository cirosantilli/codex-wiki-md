<h1 id="section-i/1-1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1.1](../1-1.md)

Write the parameters as **$\pi_0,\pi_1,\mu_0,\mu_1,\sigma_0,\sigma_1$**, where $\pi_j$ is the fraction of molecules in component $j$, $\mu_j$ is its [mean](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) current, and $\sigma_j>0$ is its current [standard deviation](../../../../../standard-deviation.md). The [mixture weights](../../../../../mixture-weight.md) satisfy $\pi_j\geq0$ and $\pi_0+\pi_1=1$. Thus six displayed parameters represent **five independent degrees of freedom**.

Assigning component 1 to modified molecules makes $\pi_1$ the inferred modification fraction. That biological assignment needs a reference or other identifying information: exchanging both component labels leaves the [Gaussian mixture distribution](../../../../../gaussian-mixture-distribution.md) unchanged. In the illustrated multi-sample model, component locations can be shared while the sample-specific fractions differ; this is different from fitting unrelated [means](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) to every sample.
