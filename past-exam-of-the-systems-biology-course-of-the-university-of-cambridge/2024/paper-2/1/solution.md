A [reference genome](../../../../reference-genome.md) turns sequence reads into measurements with biological coordinates. It supplies the scaffold on which to place [genes](../../../../gene.md), regulatory regions and [genetic variants](../../../../genetic-variant.md), so experiments in an unfamiliar organism can address mechanisms rather than only produce disconnected sequence fragments. Across the tree of life, its greatest value comes from combining a reliable assembly with comparative sampling and experimental information.

**Reconstructing evolutionary history.** [Phylogenomics](../../../../phylogenomics.md) compares many loci to infer relationships and the history of [gene duplication](../../../../gene-duplication.md), [gene loss](../../../../gene-loss.md) and chromosomal change. A [reference genome](../../../../reference-genome.md) helps distinguish corresponding [genes](../../../../gene.md) through [orthology](../../../../orthology.md) and conserved [synteny](../../../../synteny.md). Multiple loci also expose conflicts: a [gene tree](../../../../gene-tree.md) need not equal the species history because of [incomplete lineage sorting](../../../../incomplete-lineage-sorting.md) or [horizontal gene transfer](../../../../horizontal-gene-transfer.md). Dense sampling across distant and closely related lineages can localize when a change occurred; sequencing many almost identical species answers a different question from covering a previously unsampled major lineage.

**Connecting sequence to function.** In [comparative genomics](../../../../comparative-genomics.md), regions that change unusually slowly can indicate functional constraint, while lineage-specific changes suggest candidates for adaptation. [Gene duplication](../../../../gene-duplication.md) can create new biochemical functions; changes in noncoding regulation can alter where a conserved [gene](../../../../gene.md) is expressed. A useful design compares multiple independent origins of a phenotype and tests candidate changes, rather than attributing every difference between two species to their most conspicuous phenotypic difference. Conserved sequence supports a functional hypothesis, not its complete molecular explanation.

A concrete example is the Zoonomia study, which supplied new mammalian [genome assemblies](../../../../genome-assembly.md) and a whole-genome alignment spanning 240 species. Its broad sampling enabled analysis of evolutionary constraint and provided resources relevant to conservation, illustrating how a comparative collection can have more value than isolated reference sequences.[Abstract; sections on phylogenetic diversity, evolutionary constraint and conservation](../../../../a-comparative-genomics-multitool-for-scientific-discovery-and-conservation-by-zoonomia-consortium.md)

**Making experiments possible outside traditional model organisms.** A [reference genome](../../../../reference-genome.md) permits reliable read mapping for [RNA sequencing](../../../../rna-sequencing.md), improved [genome annotation](../../../../genome-annotation.md), identification of complete coding sequences, and design of targeted [genetic perturbations](../../../../genetic-perturbation.md). It helps place alternative transcripts, discriminate duplicated loci and connect measurements of [gene expression](../../../../gene-expression.md) to candidate regulatory regions. It also supplies a species-specific [protein](../../../../protein-split.md) sequence database for [mass spectrometry](../../../../mass-spectrometry.md) identification. Predicted [protein](../../../../protein-split.md) sequences can reveal candidate enzymes for useful [metabolic pathways](../../../../metabolic-pathway.md), but expression and biochemical activity still need validation.

**Conservation, ecology and applications.** A [reference genome](../../../../reference-genome.md) supports variant discovery across populations, allowing [conservation genetics](../../../../conservation-genetics.md) to investigate genetic diversity, [inbreeding](../../../../inbreeding.md) and connectivity. These quantities can inform breeding and management when combined with ecology and demographic history; a single sequenced animal cannot establish population diversity. Reference sequences also help assign reads from [environmental DNA](../../../../environmental-dna.md) or [metagenomics](../../../../metagenomics.md) experiments to organisms, investigate host–microbe interactions and track pathogens. In agriculture and biotechnology, they support trait mapping, breeding, disease-resistance studies and discovery of useful enzymes or biosynthetic pathways. Such uses extend naturally to plants, fungi and microbes rather than privileging mammals alone.

**Quality and representation determine what can be concluded.** An assembly should be checked for contamination, missing sequence, collapsed repeats and duplicated haplotypes. [Genome annotation](../../../../genome-annotation.md) benefits from [RNA sequencing](../../../../rna-sequencing.md) and comparative evidence; an unannotated or incorrectly assembled region can masquerade as [gene loss](../../../../gene-loss.md). A [reference genome](../../../../reference-genome.md) from one individual also creates [reference bias](../../../../reference-bias.md) when reads from divergent individuals are mapped to it. [Pangenomes](../../../../pangenome.md) and additional population samples represent sequence absent from that individual. Low sequencing cost does not remove the costs of sample identification, high-quality material, computation, curation or preserving voucher information.

**The scientific aim is a comparative, testable resource**, with open, well-documented data and appropriate agreements about sample access and benefit sharing. A [reference genome](../../../../reference-genome.md) enables hypotheses about evolution and mechanism; it is not a substitute for population sampling, phenotyping or experiments.
