# Line-vortex trajectory in a quarter-plane

↑ **Parent:** [Image vortex at a plane wall](image-vortex-at-a-plane-wall.md)

In the quadrant $x,y>0$, a [line vortex](line-vortex.md) of [circulation](circulation-physics.md) $\Gamma$ at $(x,y)$ has three images: circulations $-\Gamma$ at $(-x,y)$ and $(x,-y)$, and $+\Gamma$ at $(-x,-y)$. Their induced velocity is

$$
\dot x=\frac{\Gamma x^2}{4\pi y(x^2+y^2)},\qquad\dot y=-\frac{\Gamma y^2}{4\pi x(x^2+y^2)}.
$$

In [polar coordinates](polar-coordinates.md), these equations give $\dot r=\Gamma\cot(2\theta)/(2\pi r)$ and $\dot\theta=-\Gamma/(4\pi r^2)$. Direct [differentiation](differentiation.md) yields $\frac d{dt}(r\sin2\theta)=0$, so the trajectory obeys $r\sin2\theta=\text{constant}$.
