# Volumetric flask

↑ **Parent:** [Weak acid dissociation measurement](weak-acid-dissociation-measurement.md)

A [volumetric flask](volumetric-flask.md) prepares a [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) at a specified total [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md), using a calibration mark.

Its mark specifies contained [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) under its calibration conditions. In contrast, a [volumetric pipette](volumetric-pipette.md) specifies a delivered portion. The narrow neck makes a small change of [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) visible as a larger change of liquid height. The final [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) includes the [solute](solute.md); “dilute to $100\,\mathrm{mL}$” does not mean “add $100\,\mathrm{mL}$ of [water](water.md)”.[Laboratory Experiment 1. Calibration of Volumetric Glassware, p. 1, distinction between contained and delivered volumes and flask calibration principle](calibration-of-volumetric-glassware-buffalo-state-laboratory-handout.md)

For an original [dilution](dilution.md) example, a fully transferred $10.00\,\mathrm{mL}$ [aliquot](aliquot.md) of $0.100\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ acid contains $1.000\,\mathrm{mmol}$. Making the final total $100.0\,\mathrm{mL}$ gives [analytical concentration](analytical-concentration.md) $0.0100\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. A subsequent $10.00\,\mathrm{mL}$ [aliquot](aliquot.md) contains $0.1000\,\mathrm{mmol}$ and needs $1.000\,\mathrm{mL}$ of $0.100\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ base for one-to-one [neutralization](neutralization.md). The entire diluted flask still needs $10.00\,\mathrm{mL}$ of that base: [dilution](dilution.md) preserves the selected inventory but sampling selects a fraction of it.

Overfilling lowers the intended [molar concentration](molar-concentration.md). Removing some well-mixed [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) to restore the mark also removes [solute](solute.md), so it does not recover the intended inventory. Full transfer, known final [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md), and mixing are separate requirements.
