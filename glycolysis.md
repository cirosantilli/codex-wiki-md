# Glycolysis

↑ **Parent:** [Metabolic pathway](metabolic-pathway.md)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Energy metabolism](energy-metabolism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycolysis)

[Glycolysis](glycolysis.md) converts one [glucose](glucose.md) into two [pyruvates](pyruvate.md), yielding two net [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) and reducing two [NAD+](nad-plus.md) to two [NADH](nadh.md) in the canonical pathway. It first spends two [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md), then produces four by [substrate-level phosphorylation](substrate-level-phosphorylation.md). The pathway itself consumes no molecular [oxygen](oxygen.md); sustained operation nevertheless requires regeneration of [NAD+](nad-plus.md).[§7.2, First Half of Glycolysis, step 5; Second Half of Glycolysis, steps 6–10; Outcomes of Glycolysis](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others.md)

The useful organizing idea is three separate ledgers: [carbon](carbon.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), transfers between [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) and [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md), and [redox](redox.md) transfers between [NAD+](nad-plus.md) and [NADH](nadh.md). Splitting the [carbon](carbon.md) skeleton doubles the number of downstream conversions; recycling a [cofactor](cofactor-biochemistry.md) permits repeated operation. These counts explain the yield without memorizing a drawing of every [enzyme](enzyme-split.md). The two net [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) belong to [glycolysis](glycolysis.md) alone, not to complete [cellular respiration](cellular-respiration.md).
