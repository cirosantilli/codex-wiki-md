# Model organism

↑ **Parent:** [Biology](biology-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_organism)

A [model organism](model-organism.md) is an [organism](organism-split.md) used to investigate biological processes through accessible, repeatable experiments. Its value combines [experimental tractability](experimental-tractability.md) with a carefully delimited relationship to other [organisms](organism-split.md). For example, a [gene deletion](gene-deletion.md) in [Saccharomyces cerevisiae](saccharomyces-cerevisiae.md) can test whether a [gene](gene.md) is required for growth under specified conditions; adding another [gene](gene.md) can test [functional complementation](functional-complementation.md). Such interventions support [causal inference](causal-inference-split.md) more directly than merely noticing that a [gene](gene.md) and a [phenotype](phenotype.md) occur together. [Duina, Miller and Keeney (2014), introduction and Experimental Toolkit and Related Resources: manipulation of genes and phenotypic effects](budding-yeast-for-budding-geneticists-a-primer-on-the-saccharomyces-cerevisiae-model-system-by-andrea-a-duina-and-others.md)

The striking opportunity is to ask two experimentally separate questions: what does this component do here, and which part of that function survives a change of [species](species.md) or conditions? The [yeast cross-species complementation experiment](yeast-cross-species-complementation-experiment.md) makes both questions concrete. [Model organism evidence transfer](model-organism-evidence-transfer.md) requires specifying the function being compared; a useful [model organism](model-organism.md) need not reproduce every feature of another [organism](organism-split.md).
