# Metric topology

↑ **Parent:** [Metric space](metric-space.md)

The [metric topology](metric-topology.md) consists of the [sets](set-split.md) $U$ such that, for every $x\in U$, some [open ball](open-ball.md) $B_d(x,r)$ lies in $U$. Thus [open sets](open-set.md) describe which sufficiently small changes remain inside a region; they do not specify the numerical size of those changes.

For $v\in\mathbb R^2$, the [L1 norm](l1-norm.md), [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md), and [maximum norm](supremum-norm.md) satisfy

$$
\|v\|_\infty\leq\|v\|_2\leq\|v\|_1\leq\sqrt2\,\|v\|_2\leq2\|v\|_\infty.
$$

These bounds make them [equivalent norms](equivalent-norms.md). For example, $B_\infty(a,r/2)\subseteq B_1(a,r)\subseteq B_\infty(a,r)$: every [open ball](open-ball.md) contains a sufficiently small [open ball](open-ball.md) for either other [metric](metric.md). Hence all three define the same [open sets](open-set.md), [convergent sequences](convergent-sequence.md), and [continuous maps](continuous-map.md). More generally, any two [norms](norm.md) on a real or complex [finite-dimensional vector space](finite-dimensional-vector-space.md) are [equivalent norms](equivalent-norms.md); see [H. A. Priestley, Functional Analysis I, §§0.5–0.7 and Theorem 4.3](https://www.maths.ox.ac.uk/system/files/attachments/B4.1%20Lecture%20Notes.pdf).

The [metric topology](metric-topology.md) therefore does not determine numerical distances. Multiplying a [metric](metric.md) by $100$ preserves its [open sets](open-set.md) while multiplying every distance by $100$. Nor does the general [equivalence of metrics](equivalence-of-metrics.md) require constant-factor bounds: that stronger comparison holds for the three [norms](norm.md) used here.
