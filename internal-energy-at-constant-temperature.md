# Internal energy at constant temperature

↑ **Parent:** [Internal energy](internal-energy.md)

Constant [temperature](temperature.md) does not generally mean constant [internal energy](internal-energy.md). At fixed amount and composition, within one smooth [thermodynamic phase](thermodynamic-phase.md) of a [simple compressible system](simple-compressible-system.md), an equilibrium identity is

$$
dU=C_V\,dT+\left[T\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial T}\right)_V-p\right]dV.
$$

The bracket vanishes for the [ideal gas law](ideal-gas-law.md) $pV=nRT$. It need not vanish for a [real gas](real-gas.md): changing the [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) changes the average interaction [potential energy](potential-energy.md), even when the random-motion contribution stays at the same [temperature](temperature.md). The identity follows by writing $dU=T\,dS-p\,dV$ and using a [Maxwell relation](maxwell-relations.md); it does not require the actual process to be reversible. [Free Expansion of Gas](thermodynamics-and-statistical-mechanics-by-richard-fitzpatrick/free-expansion-of-gas.md)

For a [Van der Waals equation](van-der-waals-equation.md) model with [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) per [mole](mole-unit.md) $v=V/n$, $p=RT/(v-b)-a/v^2$ gives $(\partial U/\partial V)_{T,n}=a/v^2$. Thus an [isothermal expansion](isothermal-expansion.md) from $v_1$ to $v_2>v_1$, at fixed [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) $n$, increases [internal energy](internal-energy.md) by

$$
\Delta U=na\left(\frac1{v_1}-\frac1{v_2}\right)>0.
$$

Separating attractive particles makes their interaction [potential energy](potential-energy.md) less negative. For stipulated $n=1\,\mathrm{mol}$, $a=0.10\,\mathrm{Pa\,m^6\,mol^{-2}}$, $b=10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3\,mol^{-1}}$, $T=300\,\mathrm K$, $v_1=0.010$ and $v_2=0.020\,\mathrm{m^3\,mol^{-1}}$, the calculated increase for this one-[mole](mole-unit.md) sample is $5\,\mathrm J$, despite $\Delta T=0$. This dilute stable-branch model example is not a measurement of a named [gas](gas.md). [Van der Waals Gas](thermodynamics-and-statistical-mechanics-by-richard-fitzpatrick/van-der-waals-gas.md)

A [phase transition](phase-transition.md) gives another reason. During equilibrium vaporization of a pure substance at fixed [pressure](pressure.md), the [temperature](temperature.md) can stay at its [phase coexistence](phase-coexistence.md) value while the [phase fraction](phase-fraction.md) changes. Molecular separation changes interaction [potential energy](potential-energy.md).[§1.5, figure 1.18 and equations 1.7–1.8: molecular attractions and constant-temperature phase conversion](university-physics-volume-2-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others/1-5-phase-changes.md) If the [closed system](closed-system.md) does only [pressure](pressure.md)-[volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) [work](work.md), its [latent heat](latent-heat.md) input obeys

$$
Q_{\rm in}=\Delta H,\qquad
\Delta U=\Delta H-p\Delta V.
$$

Here $H=U+pV$ is [enthalpy](enthalpy.md). A vaporization [latent heat](latent-heat.md) therefore is not automatically the [internal energy](internal-energy.md) increment: some input supplies expansion [work](work.md). A fixed [temperature](temperature.md) reading alone cannot tell how much [internal energy](internal-energy.md) changed.
