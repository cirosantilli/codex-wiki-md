# Brownian recurrence proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Fundamental theorem of algebra](fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md)

If a nonconstant complex polynomial were zero-free, its reciprocal would be an entire bounded function. The real part of its value along planar [Brownian motion](brownian-motion-split.md) is a bounded martingale, hence converges almost surely. If that real part were nonconstant, continuity would give two disks with separated value ranges, and [recurrence of planar Brownian motion](recurrence-of-planar-brownian-motion.md) would force alternate visits to both at arbitrarily large times, contradicting convergence. It is therefore constant; the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](cauchy-riemann-equations.md) then make the reciprocal constant, a contradiction.
