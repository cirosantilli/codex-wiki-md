# Sagnac effect

↑ **Parent:** [Special relativity](special-relativity-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagnac_effect)

The [Sagnac effect](sagnac-effect.md) is a difference in return times, or an associated [interference fringe](interference-fringe.md) displacement, for [light](light.md) sent in opposite directions around a closed optical path in a rotating apparatus. The [light](light.md) leaves one [beam splitter](beam-splitter.md), travels around the same loop in opposite senses, and returns to the rotating [beam splitter](beam-splitter.md) and readout. For a slowly rotating apparatus in flat [spacetime](spacetime.md), the leading laboratory arrival-time difference is

$$
\Delta t=t_+-t_-\simeq\frac{4\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\mathbf A}{c^2},\qquad
\mathbf A=\frac12\oint_C\mathbf r\times d\mathbf r.
$$

Here $c$ is the vacuum [speed of light](speed-of-light.md), $\boldsymbol\Omega$ is the apparatus's [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) relative to an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md), and $\mathbf A$ is the [vector area](vector-area.md) oriented by the chosen positive direction around the [closed curve](closed-curve.md) $C$. With positive [rotation](rotation-mathematics.md) about the upward [normal vector](normal-vector.md), the counterclockwise beam is labelled $+$ and arrives later. Reversing [rotation](rotation-mathematics.md), or reversing the orientation convention alone, changes the sign of $\Delta t$.

The surprise is that a small loop can reveal its [rotation](rotation-mathematics.md) by comparing two beams locally, even though each local vacuum [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) measurement gives $c$. A [rotating reference frame](rotating-reference-frame.md) is not a single [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md): a beam must catch the moving receiver after a full circuit. The [Sagnac area law](sagnac-area-law.md) makes this difference a practical way to measure [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md), while the [Relativistic Sagnac delay on a circular ring](relativistic-sagnac-delay-on-a-circular-ring.md) distinguishes laboratory coordinate time from the receiver's [proper time](proper-time.md).
