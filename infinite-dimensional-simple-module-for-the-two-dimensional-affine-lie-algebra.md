# Infinite-dimensional simple module for the two-dimensional affine Lie algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Lie's theorem](lie-s-theorem.md)

The [solvable Lie algebra](solvable-lie-algebra.md) $\mathbb Cx\oplus\mathbb Cy$, with $[x,y]=y$, has an infinite-dimensional [Irreducible Lie algebra representation](irreducible-lie-algebra-representation.md) on $\mathbb C[t]$: $xf(t)=tf(t)$ and $yf(t)=f(t-1)$. Indeed $[x,y]f=yf$. An [invariant subspace](invariant-subspace.md) is an ideal $p(t)\mathbb C[t]$ because it is invariant under multiplication by $t$. Shift invariance gives $p(t)\mid p(t-1)$, hence $p(t-1)=p(t)$ and $p$ is constant. This shows why the [Lie theorem](lie-s-theorem.md) needs finite-dimensional representations, even when the algebra itself is finite-dimensional.
