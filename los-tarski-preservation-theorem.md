<h1 id="los-tarski-preservation-theorem">Łoś-Tarski preservation theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Universal first-order theory](universal-first-order-theory.md)

A [first-order theory](first-order-theory.md) has its class of [first-order models](model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) closed under [substructures of a first-order structure](substructure-of-a-first-order-structure.md) exactly when it is equivalent to its universal consequences $T_\forall$. If $A\models T_\forall$, then $T\cup\operatorname{Diag}(A)$ is consistent. Otherwise a finite [logical conjunction](logical-conjunction.md) $\delta$ of signed [diagram of a structure](diagram-mathematical-logic.md) sentences yields a universal consequence $\forall\mathbf x\,\neg\delta(\mathbf x)$ false in $A$. The [compactness theorem](compactness-theorem.md) gives a [first-order model](model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of $T$ containing an embedded copy of $A$, and closure under [substructures of a first-order structure](substructure-of-a-first-order-structure.md) implies $A\models T$. The converse is preservation of [universal sentences](universal-sentence.md). The [diagram of a structure](diagram-mathematical-logic.md) must include negated as well as positive atomic sentences so that the resulting map is an embedding, not just a homomorphism.
