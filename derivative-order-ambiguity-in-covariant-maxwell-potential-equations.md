# Derivative-order ambiguity in covariant Maxwell potential equations

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](maxwell-equations.md)

Use the negative curvature convention defined in [curvature-sign convention in Killing derivative identities](curvature-sign-convention-in-killing-derivative-identities.md), and $C_{ab}=C^c{}_{acb}$. Covariant derivatives of a vector obey $[\nabla_b,\nabla^a]A^b=-C^a{}_bA^b$. Hence the flat-space equivalent expressions $\Box A^a-\partial_b\partial^aA^b$ and $\Box A^a-\partial^a\partial_bA^b$ acquire a difference $+C^a{}_bA^b$ under the [comma-to-semicolon prescription](comma-to-semicolon-prescription.md). Covariantizing $F_{ab}=2\nabla_{[a}A_{b]}$ and its [Maxwell equations](maxwell-equations.md) gives $\Box A^a-\nabla^a(\nabla_bA^b)+C^a{}_bA^b=\mu_0J^a$. This form respects [gauge invariance](gauge-invariance.md). Dropping the Ricci term is generally not gauge invariant. The two expressions coincide in a Ricci-flat [vacuum spacetime](vacuum-spacetime.md); vanishing matter alone with nonzero [cosmological constant](cosmological-constant.md) does not imply Ricci flatness.
