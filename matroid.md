# Matroid

↑ **Parent:** [Combinatorics](combinatorics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroid)

A [matroid](matroid.md) is an [independence system](independence-system.md) in which a smaller admissible selection can always grow using an element of a larger admissible selection. This exchange rule abstracts [linear independence](linear-independence.md) of [vectors](vector.md) and the selection of [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) forming a [forest](forest.md). It forces all [matroid bases](basis-of-a-matroid.md) to have the same [cardinality](cardinality.md) and makes the [matroid greedy algorithm](matroid-greedy-algorithm.md) correct for maximizing their total weight. The useful surprise is that local choices become globally optimal because of a structural rule about admissible [subsets](subset.md), rather than a special numerical coincidence.

The [weighted three-vector matroid](weighted-three-vector-matroid.md) below lets us inspect every [subset](subset.md) and every possible size comparison. Its three [vectors](vector.md) give three two-element [matroid bases](basis-of-a-matroid.md), yet their total weights differ. The [graphic matroid](graphic-matroid.md) of a [graph triangle](triangle-in-a-graph.md) has exactly the same admissible labels. A [pregeometry](pregeometry.md) is a finitary closure-theoretic extension to possibly infinite [sets](set-split.md).
