<h1 id="a/1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a two-by-two matrix over a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md), its first [Smith normal form](../../../../../smith-normal-form.md) invariant is the [greatest common divisor](../../../../../greatest-common-divisor.md) of its entries, while the product of its two invariants is its [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md), up to a unit. Here

$$
1=3-(1-i)(1+i),
$$

so the entries generate the unit ideal in the [Gaussian integers](../../../../../gaussian-integer.md). The first invariant is therefore $1$. Also

$$
\det B=3(1+i)-(2+2i)(5+2i)=-3-11i.
$$

Hence a valid [Smith normal form](../../../../../smith-normal-form.md) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-3-11i\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Multiplying the second diagonal entry by a unit $\pm1,\pm i$ gives an equivalent normal form. The divisibility condition holds because $1$ divides every [Gaussian integer](../../../../../gaussian-integer.md).
