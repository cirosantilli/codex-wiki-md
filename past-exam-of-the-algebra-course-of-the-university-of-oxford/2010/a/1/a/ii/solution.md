<h1 id="a/1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain](../../../../../../structure-theorem-for-finitely-generated-modules-over-a-principal-ideal-domain.md) applies because every [Euclidean domain](../../../../../../euclidean-domain.md) is a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md). In its cyclic decomposition form, every [finitely generated module](../../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md) has a decomposition

$$
\boxed{M\cong R^r\oplus R/(d_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus R/(d_t),\qquad d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_t,}
$$

where the $d_i$ are nonzero nonunits. The nonnegative integer $r$ and the [invariant factors of a finitely generated module](../../../../../../invariant-factor-of-a-finitely-generated-module.md) $d_i$ are unique, with each $d_i$ determined up to multiplication by a unit. The zero module corresponds to no summands. Equivalently, factor the $d_i$ into powers of prime elements and use the [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) to obtain a direct sum of cyclic prime-power [torsion modules](../../../../../../torsion-module.md) and a free summand.
