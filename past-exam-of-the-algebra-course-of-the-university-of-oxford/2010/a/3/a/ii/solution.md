<h1 id="a/3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Given a [group representation](../../../../../../group-representation.md) $\rho:G\to\operatorname{GL}_n(k)$, define an action on $k^n$ by

$$
\boxed{\left(\sum_ga_gg\right)\cdot v=\sum_ga_g\rho(g)v.}
$$

It is bilinear. The identity acts as $I_n$, and

$$
\left(\sum_ga_g\rho(g)\right)\left(\sum_hb_h\rho(h)\right)=\sum_{g,h}a_gb_h\rho(gh),
$$

because $\rho$ is a [group homomorphism](../../../../../../group-homomorphism.md). Hence the product action agrees with successive action, proving the [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) axioms. Thus every [group representation](../../../../../../group-representation.md) extends uniquely by linearity to a $kG$-[module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md).
