<h1 id="a/3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**True.** If every [simple module](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md) over $\mathbb CG$ were one-dimensional, the [Maschke theorem](../../../../../../maschke-s-theorem.md) and the [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](../../../../../../artin-wedderburn-theorem.md) would make $\mathbb CG$ a product of copies of $\mathbb C$, hence commutative. But its [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) elements multiply as in $G$, so commutativity of $\mathbb CG$ implies commutativity of $G$. This contradicts the assumption. Therefore a non-[Abelian](../../../../../../abelian-group.md) finite [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) has a [simple module](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md) of [dimension](../../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) greater than one.
