<h1 id="a/3/c/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**True; there are exactly five.** On a simple [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md), the central element $Y$ acts as zero: its [image](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) is a [submodule](../../../../../../submodule.md), and surjectivity would contradict $Y^6=0$. The [nilpotent central elements annihilate simple modules](../../../../../../nilpotent-central-elements-annihilate-simple-modules.md) argument shows that the action factors through

$$
\mathbb C[X,Y]/(X^5-1,Y)\cong\mathbb C[X]/(X^5-1)\cong\mathbb C^5
$$

by the [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md). Therefore the simple [modules](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) are one-dimensional with

$$
\boxed{X\mapsto\zeta_5^j,\qquad Y\mapsto0,\qquad j=0,\ldots,4.}
$$

This also rules out hypothetical infinite-dimensional simple [modules](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md), rather than counting only finite-dimensional ones.
