<h1 id="a/3/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**True.** Every [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) of order four is [Abelian](../../../../../../abelian-group.md): if it has an element of order four it is cyclic; otherwise every nonidentity element has order two and $(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}=ba$, while $(ab)^{-1}=ab$, so $ab=ba$.

Over $\mathbb C$, the [Maschke theorem](../../../../../../maschke-s-theorem.md) and the [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](../../../../../../artin-wedderburn-theorem.md) give

$$
\mathbb CG\cong\prod_{j=1}^rM_{d_j}(\mathbb C).
$$

Since $G$ is [Abelian](../../../../../../abelian-group.md), this [algebra](../../../../../../algebra-split.md) is commutative, forcing every $d_j=1$. Its [dimension](../../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) is four, so $r=4$. Each factor contributes one simple [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md); therefore there are exactly **four** up to [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md).
