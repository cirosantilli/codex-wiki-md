<h1 id="b/4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If $|G|=p^am$ with $p\nmid m$, a [Sylow p-subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) has order $p^a$. The [Sylow theorems](../../../../../sylow-theorems.md) say that such subgroups exist; every [p-subgroup](../../../../../p-subgroup.md) is contained in one; they are all conjugate; and their number $n_p$ divides $m$ and satisfies $n_p\equiv1\pmod p$.

Suppose $|G|=p^2q$ with distinct primes and $G$ were simple. A [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of order $q$ has order $q$, and $n_q>1$ divides $p^2$, so $n_q=p$ or $p^2$. If $n_q=p$, conjugation gives a nontrivial homomorphism $G\to S_p$. Its [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) is normal and, by simplicity, trivial. Hence $p^2q$ would divide $p!$ by [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), impossible because $p!$ has only one factor of $p$.

If $n_q=p^2$, distinct [Sylow subgroups](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of order $q$ meet only in the identity and together contain $p^2(q-1)$ nonidentity elements. Only $p^2$ elements of $G$ remain, including the identity. Every [Sylow p-subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) lies entirely in that remaining set and itself has $p^2$ elements. They must therefore all be the same subgroup, making it normal, again a contradiction. Thus **no group of order $p^2q$ is simple**. If $p=q$, the preceding [p-group](../../../../../p-group.md) argument applies.

For distinct $p,q$, a finite [Abelian group](../../../../../abelian-group.md) is the [direct product of groups](../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md) of its primary subgroups. A group of order $p^2$ is either cyclic or has every nonidentity element of order $p$, in which case choosing two independent elements gives $C_p\times C_p$. Consequently the possibilities are

$$
\boxed{C_{p^2q},\qquad C_p\times C_{pq}\quad(p\ne q).}
$$

If the primes coincide, the [Fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-finitely-generated-abelian-groups.md) gives instead $C_{p^3}$, $C_{p^2}\times C_p$, and $C_p^3$. This classification is used only for the requested list, not for the proof above.
