<h1 id="b/5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In a finite [group](../../../../../group-split.md), choose a maximal chain of [normal subgroups](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) from $1$ to $G$. Finiteness ensures such a chain exists, and maximality leaves no [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $G$ strictly between successive terms. This is a [chief series](../../../../../chief-series.md).

The [infinite cyclic group has no chief series](../../../../../infinite-cyclic-group-has-no-chief-series.md). In any finite chain starting at zero, the first nonzero subgroup is $n\mathbb Z$. But $2n\mathbb Z$ is a nonzero proper subgroup of it and is normal in $\mathbb Z$. Thus zero is not maximal among the normal subgroups in that first term, contradicting the definition.

For any [chief series](../../../../../chief-series.md), $G_{n-1}$ is a maximal proper [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $G$. The [subgroup correspondence theorem](../../../../../subgroup-correspondence-theorem.md) identifies normal subgroups of $G/G_{n-1}$ with normal subgroups of $G$ containing $G_{n-1}$. Consequently **the top chief factor is simple**.

Other [chief factors](../../../../../chief-factor.md) need only have no proper nonzero subgroup invariant under the action of the whole [group](../../../../../group-split.md). In $A_4$,

$$
\boxed{1<V_4<A_4}
$$

is a [chief series](../../../../../chief-series.md), where $V_4=\{1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}$. Conjugation in $A_4$ permutes the three involutions transitively, so none of its three order-two subgroups is normal in $A_4$. Thus $V_4$ is a minimal nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md); the top quotient is $C_3$. But the bottom [chief factor](../../../../../chief-factor.md) $V_4/1\cong C_2\times C_2$ is **not simple**, since it has proper order-two normal subgroups as an abstract [group](../../../../../group-split.md).
