<h1 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A direct [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) computation gives

$$
\det(tI-D)=t^2(t-4),\qquad \operatorname{rank}D=2.
$$

The zero [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) has algebraic multiplicity two but a one-dimensional [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md), so it gives one [Jordan block](../../../../../jordan-block.md) of size two. The other block is $J_1(4)$. For an explicit [Jordan basis](../../../../../jordan-basis.md), take

$$
v=(1,-3,1)^T,\quad u=(4,-7,0)^T,\quad w=(1,1,1)^T.
$$

These are linearly independent and satisfy $Dv=0$, $Du=v$, $Dw=4w$. Thus

$$
\boxed{{}_AM\cong A/(X^2)\oplus A/(X-4).}
$$

The first summand has the chain $u\mapsto v\mapsto0$ and is an [indecomposable module](../../../../../indecomposable-module.md); the other is simple.
