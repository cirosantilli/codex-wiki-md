<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A two-dimensional [indecomposable module](../../../../indecomposable-module.md) over $\mathbb C[X]$ is exactly $A/((X-\lambda)^2)$ for some $\lambda\in\mathbb C$. Write its operator as $T=\lambda I+N$ with $N^2=0$ and $N\ne0$. On [restriction of scalars](../../../../restriction-of-scalars.md),

$$
Y=T^2=\lambda^2I+2\lambda N.
$$

For $\lambda=0$ this is the zero operator on a two-dimensional space, which splits as two one-dimensional [submodules](../../../../submodule.md). For $\lambda\ne0$, its [nilpotent](../../../../nilpotent.md) part is nonzero of square zero, so it is one [Jordan block](../../../../jordan-block.md) and is indecomposable. Therefore the full list is

$$
\boxed{A/((X-\lambda)^2),\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}.}
$$

Different $\lambda$ give nonisomorphic $A$-[modules](../../../../module-mathematics.md); the restricted $B$-[modules](../../../../module-mathematics.md) for $\lambda$ and $-\lambda$ are isomorphic.
