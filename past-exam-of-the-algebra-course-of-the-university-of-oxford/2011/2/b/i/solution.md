<h1 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $e_{ij}$ for the [matrix units](../../../../../matrix-unit.md). Left multiplication by $a=\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\0&t\end{pmatrix}$ gives

$$
ae_{11}=re_{11},\qquad ae_{12}=re_{12},\qquad ae_{22}=se_{12}+te_{22}.
$$

Thus a [composition series of a module](../../../../../composition-series-of-a-module.md) is

$$
\boxed{0\subset\mathbb Ce_{11}\subset\mathbb Ce_{11}\oplus\mathbb Ce_{12}\subset A.}
$$

Each quotient is one-dimensional, hence a [simple module](../../../../../irreducible-module.md). The required [direct sum](../../../../../direct-sum.md) is

$$
\boxed{{}_AA=Ae_{11}\oplus Ae_{22}
=\mathbb Ce_{11}\oplus(\mathbb Ce_{12}\oplus\mathbb Ce_{22}).}
$$

The first summand is simple. The second has a unique nonzero proper [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md), $\mathbb Ce_{12}$: any vector $ue_{12}+ve_{22}$ with $v\ne0$ generates $e_{12}$ by applying $e_{12}$ and then generates $e_{22}$. A decomposition into two nonzero summands would require a second one-dimensional [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md), which is impossible. Hence this summand is an [indecomposable module](../../../../../indecomposable-module.md).
