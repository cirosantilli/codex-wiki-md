<h1 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [Jacobson radical](../../../../../jacobson-radical.md) is $J=\mathbb Ce_{12}$. Indeed $J^2=0$, so $J$ is contained in the [Jacobson radical](../../../../../jacobson-radical.md), and $A/J\cong\mathbb C\times\mathbb C$ is a [semisimple algebra](../../../../../semisimple-algebra.md), giving the reverse inclusion. The [radical of a module](../../../../../radical-of-a-module.md) of the [left regular module](../../../../../left-regular-module.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rad}({}_AA)=\mathbb Ce_{12}.}
$$

Its [socle](../../../../../socle-mathematics.md) is the sum of its [simple modules](../../../../../irreducible-module.md). The displayed left action shows that its $S_1$-[submodules](../../../../../submodule.md) span $\mathbb Ce_{11}\oplus\mathbb Ce_{12}$. There is no $S_2$-[submodule](../../../../../submodule.md): if $av=tv$ for every $a$, applying $e_{11}$ forces the first row of $v$ to vanish, and applying $e_{12}$ then forces the remaining $e_{22}$ coefficient to vanish. Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{soc}({}_AA)=\mathbb Ce_{11}\oplus\mathbb Ce_{12}.}
$$

A homomorphism from $S_1$ is determined by any image $\alpha e_{11}+\beta e_{12}$ of $s_1$. Thus $V_{S_1}$ is the two-dimensional row [right module](../../../../../right-module.md) with action

$$
(\alpha,\beta)\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\0&t\end{pmatrix}
=(\alpha r,\alpha s+\beta t).
$$

Its line $\mathbb Ce_{12}$ is a nonzero proper [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md), so **$V_{S_1}$ is not simple**. Since $S_2$ has no copy in the [socle](../../../../../socle-mathematics.md), any homomorphism $S_2\to A$ is zero. Thus **$V_{S_2}=0$ is not simple either**, since a [simple module](../../../../../irreducible-module.md) is required to be nonzero.
