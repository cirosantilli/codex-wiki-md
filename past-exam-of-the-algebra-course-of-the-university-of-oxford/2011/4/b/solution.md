<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [quaternion group](../../../../quaternion-group.md) of order eight has a unique [involution](../../../../involution.md), which generates its [center of a group](../../../../center-of-a-group.md). A nontrivial intersection $S\cap S'$ is a nontrivial finite $2$-[group](../../../../group-split.md), so by [Cauchy's theorem for finite groups](../../../../cauchy-theorem-for-groups.md) it contains an [involution](../../../../involution.md). This must be both the unique [involution](../../../../involution.md) $t$ of $S$ and the unique [involution](../../../../involution.md) of $S'$. Thus $t\in Z(S')$.

It follows that $S'\leq C_G(t)=N_G(S)$. But $S$ is a normal [Sylow subgroup](../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of its [normalizer](../../../../normalizer.md), so it is that [normalizer](../../../../normalizer.md)'s unique [Sylow subgroup](../../../../sylow-subgroup.md). Hence $\boxed{S'=S}$. Distinct [Sylow subgroups](../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) therefore intersect trivially. From the preceding result,

$$
n_2\equiv1\pmod8,\qquad n_2\mid168/8=21.
$$

Of $1,3,7,21$, only $1$ is congruent to $1$ modulo $8$. Therefore **$n_2=1$, so $S$ is normal in $G$**.
