<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](../../../../artin-wedderburn-theorem.md) says that a finite-dimensional [semisimple algebra](../../../../semisimple-algebra.md) over $\mathbb C$ is a finite product $\prod_jM_{d_j}(\mathbb C)$: the division algebras in the general theorem reduce to $\mathbb C$ because the base field is algebraically closed. Each factor has [center of an associative algebra](../../../../center-of-an-associative-algebra.md) $\mathbb C I_{d_j}$. Consequently a central element corresponds to $(\lambda_j I_{d_j})_j$. If it is [nilpotent](../../../../nilpotent.md), then $\lambda_j^n=0$ for every $j$, whence every $\lambda_j=0$. Therefore $\boxed{z=0}$. The centrality assumption matters: a full [matrix algebra](../../../../matrix-algebra.md) has nonzero [nilpotent](../../../../nilpotent.md) elements.
