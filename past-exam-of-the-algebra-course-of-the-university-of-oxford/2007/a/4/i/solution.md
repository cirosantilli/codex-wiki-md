<h1 id="a/4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An $R$-[module isomorphism](../../../../../module-isomorphism.md) $T:V_\alpha\to V_\beta$ is a $K$-linear isomorphism satisfying $T\alpha=\beta T$, by applying it to multiplication by $X$. Conversely that identity implies $Tf(\alpha)=f(\beta)T$ for every [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md), so a similarity is an $R$-[module isomorphism](../../../../../module-isomorphism.md).

The cyclic decomposition theorem over the [principal ideal domain](../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md) $R=K[X]$ states that any [finitely generated module](../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md) is

$$
R^r\oplus R/(d_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus R/(d_s),\qquad d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_s,
$$

with nonunit monic invariant factors, uniquely determined, and free rank $r$ uniquely determined. A finite-dimensional $K$-space has $r=0$. In each cyclic summand the basis $1,X,\ldots,X^{\deg d_i-1}$ makes multiplication by $X$ the [companion matrix](../../../../../companion-matrix.md) of $d_i$. Their block diagonal sum is the [rational canonical form](../../../../../rational-canonical-form.md).

Here the two [polynomials](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) are already in divisibility order. With the companion convention having ones below the diagonal,

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{RCF}(\alpha)=\operatorname{diag}\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\1&0&-3\\0&1&3\end{pmatrix}\right).}
$$

Integer coefficients are interpreted in $K$, so this remains valid in characteristics two and three.

In the cyclic case there is one companion block $C(d)$. Its defining [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is both characteristic and minimal: the vector represented by $1$ is cyclic and a [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) annihilates it exactly when divisible by $d$. Thus **the [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) is read directly from that block**.
