## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

The [center of an associative algebra](../associative-algebra.md#center-of-an-associative-algebra) is the [subalgebra](../algebra-over-a-field.md#subalgebra)

$$
Z(A)=\{z\in A:za=az\text{ for every }a\in A\}.
$$

For a pair $(z_1,z_2)$, commuting with every $(a_1,a_2)$ means $z_1a_1=a_1z_1$ and $z_2a_2=a_2z_2$. Varying the two coordinates independently proves both inclusions, hence

$$
\boxed{Z(A_1\times A_2)=Z(A_1)\times Z(A_2).}
$$

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Use the usual unital convention for an [algebra over a field](../algebra-over-a-field.md). For $a,b,x\in A$ and $c\in K$,

$$
\phi_{a+cb}(x)=\phi_a(x)+c\phi_b(x),\qquad
\phi_{ab}(x)=a(bx)=(\phi_a\circ\phi_b)(x),\qquad \phi_1(x)=x.
$$

Passing to matrices in one fixed [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) preserves these identities: $M_{a+cb}=M_a+cM_b$, $M_{ab}=M_aM_b$ and $M_1=I$. Thus $\psi$ is an [algebra homomorphism](../algebra-over-a-field.md#algebra-homomorphism-over-a-field). If $M_a=0$, evaluating $\phi_a$ at $1_A$ gives $a=0$. Its [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is zero, so **the left regular representation is faithful**.

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

**No.** Take $A=M_2(K)$ and its diagonal [subalgebra](../algebra-over-a-field.md#subalgebra) $B$. The [algebra over a field](../algebra-over-a-field.md) $B$ is commutative, so $Z(B)=B$. But $e_{11}\in B$ does not commute with $e_{12}$:

$$
e_{11}e_{12}=e_{12},\qquad e_{12}e_{11}=0.
$$

Hence $e_{11}\notin Z(A)$, although $e_{11}\in Z(B)$. Commutation inside a [subalgebra](../algebra-over-a-field.md#subalgebra) places no condition on elements outside it.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

The [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](../associative-algebra.md#artin-wedderburn-theorem) says that a finite-dimensional [semisimple algebra](../associative-algebra.md#semisimple-algebra) over $\mathbb C$ is a finite product $\prod_jM_{d_j}(\mathbb C)$: the division algebras in the general theorem reduce to $\mathbb C$ because the base field is algebraically closed. Each factor has [center of an associative algebra](../associative-algebra.md#center-of-an-associative-algebra) $\mathbb C I_{d_j}$. Consequently a central element corresponds to $(\lambda_j I_{d_j})_j$. If it is [nilpotent](../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent), then $\lambda_j^n=0$ for every $j$, whence every $\lambda_j=0$. Therefore $\boxed{z=0}$. The centrality assumption matters: a full [matrix algebra](../associative-algebra.md#matrix-algebra) has nonzero [nilpotent](../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent) elements.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

The [Jordan–Hölder theorem](../finite-group-theory.md#jordan-holder-theorem) applies to a [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) of finite length: if

$$
0=M_0\subsetneq M_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_r=M
$$

is a [composition series of a module](../module-theory.md#composition-series-of-a-module), its successive quotients are [simple modules](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module). Any two such [composition series of a module](../module-theory.md#composition-series-of-a-module) have the same length $r$, and their [composition factors](../module-theory.md#composition-factor) are pairwise isomorphic after a permutation, with multiplicities preserved. Every finite-dimensional [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) over $A$ has finite length, since strict inclusions increase vector-space [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space). A finite-dimensional [algebra over a field](../algebra-over-a-field.md) does not by itself make every possible [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) finite-dimensional, so the finite-length hypothesis should be retained in the statement.

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

For $a\in A$, $m\in M$,

$$
(f\cdot x)(am)=f(am)x=af(m)x=a(f\cdot x)(m).
$$

Additivity and complex linearity follow from those of $f$ and multiplication in $A$, so $f\cdot x$ is an [module homomorphism](../module-theory.md#module-homomorphism). In particular the action stays inside $V$. Associativity gives

$$
((f\cdot x)\cdot y)(m)=f(m)xy=(f\cdot(xy))(m),\qquad (f\cdot1)(m)=f(m).
$$

Both distributive laws hold pointwise. These are exactly the axioms for a [right module](../module-theory.md#right-module), proving **$\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,A)$ is a right $A$-[module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics)**.

<h4 id="2/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/a/iii)

By the [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](../associative-algebra.md#artin-wedderburn-theorem), a finite-dimensional complex [simple algebra](../associative-algebra.md#simple-algebra) is $M_d(\mathbb C)$, with a unique [simple module](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module) up to isomorphism, namely the column [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) $S=\mathbb C^d$. Its [left regular module](../module-theory.md#left-regular-module) is $S^{\oplus d}$. The [Schur lemma](../representation-theory.md#schur-s-lemma) gives $\operatorname{End}_A(S)=\mathbb C$, so

$$
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb C}V_M=d=\dim_{\mathbb C}M=\sqrt{\dim_{\mathbb C}A}.}
$$

The right action can be seen explicitly. For a row vector $r$, the map $f_r(m)=mr$ is $A$-linear, and all such maps are obtained this way, since the row space and $V_M$ both have [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) $d$. Moreover $f_r\cdot x=f_{rx}$. Any nonzero row generates the whole row space under right multiplication by matrices: if $r_j\ne0$, suitable [matrix units](../vector-space.md#matrix-unit) send it to each coordinate row. Thus **$V_M$ is a simple right $A$-[module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics)**.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Write $e_{ij}$ for the [matrix units](../vector-space.md#matrix-unit). Left multiplication by $a=\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\0&t\end{pmatrix}$ gives

$$
ae_{11}=re_{11},\qquad ae_{12}=re_{12},\qquad ae_{22}=se_{12}+te_{22}.
$$

Thus a [composition series of a module](../module-theory.md#composition-series-of-a-module) is

$$
\boxed{0\subset\mathbb Ce_{11}\subset\mathbb Ce_{11}\oplus\mathbb Ce_{12}\subset A.}
$$

Each quotient is one-dimensional, hence a [simple module](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module). The required [direct sum](../vector-space.md#direct-sum) is

$$
\boxed{{}_AA=Ae_{11}\oplus Ae_{22}
=\mathbb Ce_{11}\oplus(\mathbb Ce_{12}\oplus\mathbb Ce_{22}).}
$$

The first summand is simple. The second has a unique nonzero proper [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule), $\mathbb Ce_{12}$: any vector $ue_{12}+ve_{22}$ with $v\ne0$ generates $e_{12}$ by applying $e_{12}$ and then generates $e_{22}$. A decomposition into two nonzero summands would require a second one-dimensional [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule), which is impossible. Hence this summand is an [indecomposable module](../module-theory.md#indecomposable-module).

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Choose bases $S_1=\mathbb Cs_1$, $S_2=\mathbb Cs_2$ and set

$$
\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\0&t\end{pmatrix}s_1=rs_1,
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\0&t\end{pmatrix}s_2=ts_2.
$$

Both are [simple modules](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module). They are nonisomorphic because $e_{11}$ acts as the identity on $S_1$ and as zero on $S_2$. In the ascending [composition series of a module](../module-theory.md#composition-series-of-a-module) above, the factors are, in order,

$$
\boxed{S_1,\ S_1,\ S_2.}
$$

The last factor is represented by $e_{22}$ modulo the span of $e_{11},e_{12}$.

<h4 id="2/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/b/iii)

The [Jacobson radical](../noncommutative-algebra.md#jacobson-radical) is $J=\mathbb Ce_{12}$. Indeed $J^2=0$, so $J$ is contained in the [Jacobson radical](../noncommutative-algebra.md#jacobson-radical), and $A/J\cong\mathbb C\times\mathbb C$ is a [semisimple algebra](../associative-algebra.md#semisimple-algebra), giving the reverse inclusion. The [radical of a module](../module-theory.md#radical-of-a-module) of the [left regular module](../module-theory.md#left-regular-module) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rad}({}_AA)=\mathbb Ce_{12}.}
$$

Its [socle](../module-theory.md#socle-mathematics) is the sum of its [simple modules](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module). The displayed left action shows that its $S_1$-[submodules](../module-theory.md#submodule) span $\mathbb Ce_{11}\oplus\mathbb Ce_{12}$. There is no $S_2$-[submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule): if $av=tv$ for every $a$, applying $e_{11}$ forces the first row of $v$ to vanish, and applying $e_{12}$ then forces the remaining $e_{22}$ coefficient to vanish. Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{soc}({}_AA)=\mathbb Ce_{11}\oplus\mathbb Ce_{12}.}
$$

A homomorphism from $S_1$ is determined by any image $\alpha e_{11}+\beta e_{12}$ of $s_1$. Thus $V_{S_1}$ is the two-dimensional row [right module](../module-theory.md#right-module) with action

$$
(\alpha,\beta)\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\0&t\end{pmatrix}
=(\alpha r,\alpha s+\beta t).
$$

Its line $\mathbb Ce_{12}$ is a nonzero proper [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule), so **$V_{S_1}$ is not simple**. Since $S_2$ has no copy in the [socle](../module-theory.md#socle-mathematics), any homomorphism $S_2\to A$ is zero. Thus **$V_{S_2}=0$ is not simple either**, since a [simple module](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module) is required to be nonzero.

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

Every finite-dimensional $\mathbb C[X]$-[module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) is a finitely generated torsion [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) over the [principal ideal domain](../commutative-algebra.md#principal-ideal-domain) $\mathbb C[X]$. The [structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain](../module-theory.md#structure-theorem-for-finitely-generated-modules-over-a-principal-ideal-domain) gives

$$
M\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^r\mathbb C[X]/(p_j),\qquad
p_1\mid p_2\mid\cdots\mid p_r,
$$

where the $p_j$ are uniquely determined monic nonconstant [polynomials](../polynomial.md). Equivalently, factoring over $\mathbb C$ and using the [Chinese remainder theorem](../mathematics.md#chinese-remainder-theorem) gives

$$
\boxed{M\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb C}\ \bigoplus_j
\mathbb C[X]/((X-\lambda)^{m_{\lambda j}}).}
$$

There are finitely many summands, and the multisets of positive integers $m_{\lambda j}$ are uniquely determined. Each displayed primary cyclic summand is an [indecomposable module](../module-theory.md#indecomposable-module).

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

Apply the decomposition to the [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) on which $X$ acts as $\alpha$. On $\mathbb C[X]/((X-\lambda)^m)$ put $t=X-\lambda$ and use the ordered [basis](../vector-space.md#basis)

$$
t^{m-1},\ t^{m-2},\ldots,\ t,\ 1.
$$

Multiplication by $X=\lambda+t$ has diagonal entries $\lambda$ and superdiagonal entries $1$, so its [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) is the [Jordan block](../linear-operator-theory.md#jordan-block) $J_m(\lambda)$. Concatenating the bases of the [direct sum](../vector-space.md#direct-sum) gives a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) of $V$ in which

$$
\boxed{[\alpha]=\bigoplus_{\lambda,j}J_{m_{\lambda j}}(\lambda).}
$$

This proves existence of [Jordan normal form](../linear-operator-theory.md#jordan-normal-form); uniqueness of the primary cyclic decomposition also proves uniqueness of its blocks up to order.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

The universal property of the [polynomial ring](../commutative-algebra.md#polynomial-ring) gives the unique complex [algebra homomorphism](../algebra-over-a-field.md#algebra-homomorphism-over-a-field)

$$
\iota:\mathbb C[Y]\longrightarrow\mathbb C[X],\qquad
\sum_jc_jY^j\longmapsto\sum_jc_jX^{2j}.
$$

Distinct monomials $X^{2j}$ are linearly independent, so $\iota$ is injective. [Restriction of scalars](../module-theory.md#restriction-of-scalars) defines $b\cdot m=\iota(b)m$ for $b\in B$ and $m\in M$; its [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) axioms follow from those of the original action and the [algebra homomorphism](../algebra-over-a-field.md#algebra-homomorphism-over-a-field). In particular **$Y$ acts as the square of the operator defining the $X$ action**.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/c/i)

A direct [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) computation gives

$$
\det(tI-D)=t^2(t-4),\qquad \operatorname{rank}D=2.
$$

The zero [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) has algebraic multiplicity two but a one-dimensional [eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace), so it gives one [Jordan block](../linear-operator-theory.md#jordan-block) of size two. The other block is $J_1(4)$. For an explicit [Jordan basis](../linear-operator-theory.md#jordan-basis), take

$$
v=(1,-3,1)^T,\quad u=(4,-7,0)^T,\quad w=(1,1,1)^T.
$$

These are linearly independent and satisfy $Dv=0$, $Du=v$, $Dw=4w$. Thus

$$
\boxed{{}_AM\cong A/(X^2)\oplus A/(X-4).}
$$

The first summand has the chain $u\mapsto v\mapsto0$ and is an [indecomposable module](../module-theory.md#indecomposable-module); the other is simple.

<h4 id="3/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/c/ii)

On the same [basis](../vector-space.md#basis), $Y=D^2$ kills $v,u$ and sends $w$ to $16w$. Explicitly,

$$
D^2=\begin{pmatrix}7&4&5\\7&4&5\\7&4&5\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Thus all three [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) lines are [submodules](../module-theory.md#submodule) after [restriction of scalars](../module-theory.md#restriction-of-scalars), and

$$
\boxed{{}_BM\cong B/(Y)\oplus B/(Y)\oplus B/(Y-16).}
$$

Each summand is a [simple module](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module), hence an [indecomposable module](../module-theory.md#indecomposable-module). Squaring has destroyed the length-two [nilpotent](../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent) [Jordan chain](../linear-operator-theory.md#jordan-chain).

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

A two-dimensional [indecomposable module](../module-theory.md#indecomposable-module) over $\mathbb C[X]$ is exactly $A/((X-\lambda)^2)$ for some $\lambda\in\mathbb C$. Write its operator as $T=\lambda I+N$ with $N^2=0$ and $N\ne0$. On [restriction of scalars](../module-theory.md#restriction-of-scalars),

$$
Y=T^2=\lambda^2I+2\lambda N.
$$

For $\lambda=0$ this is the zero operator on a two-dimensional space, which splits as two one-dimensional [submodules](../module-theory.md#submodule). For $\lambda\ne0$, its [nilpotent](../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent) part is nonzero of square zero, so it is one [Jordan block](../linear-operator-theory.md#jordan-block) and is indecomposable. Therefore the full list is

$$
\boxed{A/((X-\lambda)^2),\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}.}
$$

Different $\lambda$ give nonisomorphic $A$-[modules](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics); the restricted $B$-[modules](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) for $\lambda$ and $-\lambda$ are isomorphic.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/a)

If $|G|=p^am$ with $p\nmid m$, a [Sylow subgroup](../finite-group-theory.md#sylow-subgroup) for $p$ has order $p^a$. Let $P,Q$ be two such [subgroups](../group.md#subgroup). The action of $P$ by left translation on $G/Q$ has [orbits](../dynamical-systems.md#orbit-dynamical-system) of powers of $p$, while $|G/Q|=m$ is prime to $p$. There is a fixed coset $gQ$, giving $g^{-1}Pg\leq Q$; equality follows from their orders. Thus **all Sylow $p$-[subgroups](../group.md#subgroup) are conjugate**.

Let $P$ now act by conjugation on their set. If it fixes $Q$, then $P\leq N_G(Q)$, so $PQ$ is a [subgroup](../group.md#subgroup) of $p$-power order and must equal $Q$; hence $P=Q$. The unique fixed point is $P$ itself and all other [orbits](../dynamical-systems.md#orbit-dynamical-system) have size divisible by $p$. Consequently $\boxed{n_p\equiv1\pmod p}$.

For the strengthened conclusion, the [stabilizer](../group-action.md#stabilizer-subgroup) of $Q\ne P$ is $H=P\cap N_G(Q)$. The product $HQ$ is a [subgroup](../group.md#subgroup) of $p$-power order, so $H\leq Q$. If distinct [Sylow subgroups](../finite-group-theory.md#sylow-subgroup) meet trivially, then $H\leq P\cap Q=1$. Every other [orbit](../dynamical-systems.md#orbit-dynamical-system) therefore has the full size $|P|$, giving

$$
\boxed{n_p\equiv1\pmod{|P|}.}
$$

Conjugacy also gives $n_p=[G:N_G(P)]$, a divisor of $[G:P]$.

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

The [quaternion group](../finite-group-theory.md#quaternion-group) of order eight has a unique [involution](../group-theory.md#involution), which generates its [center of a group](../group-theory.md#center-of-a-group). A nontrivial intersection $S\cap S'$ is a nontrivial finite $2$-[group](../group.md), so by [Cauchy's theorem for finite groups](../finite-group-theory.md#cauchy-theorem-for-groups) it contains an [involution](../group-theory.md#involution). This must be both the unique [involution](../group-theory.md#involution) $t$ of $S$ and the unique [involution](../group-theory.md#involution) of $S'$. Thus $t\in Z(S')$.

It follows that $S'\leq C_G(t)=N_G(S)$. But $S$ is a normal [Sylow subgroup](../finite-group-theory.md#sylow-subgroup) of its [normalizer](../group-action.md#normalizer), so it is that [normalizer](../group-action.md#normalizer)'s unique [Sylow subgroup](../finite-group-theory.md#sylow-subgroup). Hence $\boxed{S'=S}$. Distinct [Sylow subgroups](../finite-group-theory.md#sylow-subgroup) therefore intersect trivially. From the preceding result,

$$
n_2\equiv1\pmod8,\qquad n_2\mid168/8=21.
$$

Of $1,3,7,21$, only $1$ is congruent to $1$ modulo $8$. Therefore **$n_2=1$, so $S$ is normal in $G$**.

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

The [class equation](../group-action.md#class-equation) implies that a nontrivial finite $p$-[group](../group.md) has nontrivial [center of a group](../group-theory.md#center-of-a-group). Here a center of order $9$ would make $G/Z(G)$ cyclic; a [group](../group.md) with cyclic quotient by its [center of a group](../group-theory.md#center-of-a-group) is abelian, contradicting the hypothesis. Thus

$$
\boxed{|Z(G)|=3,\qquad G/Z(G)\cong C_3\times C_3.}
$$

The quotient of order nine must be noncyclic for the same reason. For $x\notin Z(G)$, $\langle x,Z(G)\rangle$ has order at least nine and lies in the proper [centralizer](../group-action.md#centralizer) $C_G(x)$. Lagrange's theorem therefore forces $\boxed{|C_G(x)|=9}$. Also $1\ne G'\leq Z(G)$, so $G'=Z(G)$ and all [commutators](../lie-algebra.md#commutator) are central of order dividing three.

**Classification and splitting.** First suppose $a$ has order nine. Then $N=\langle a\rangle$ is normal of index three and $Z(G)=\langle a^3\rangle$. For $b\notin N$, conjugation on $N$ is nontrivial, since otherwise $a,b$ would generate an abelian [group](../group.md). Its order divides three, so, after replacing $b$ by its inverse if needed, $bab^{-1}=a^4$. Write $b^3=a^{3k}$. Central [commutators](../lie-algebra.md#commutator) and $3\mid\binom32$ give $(a^ib)^3=a^{3i}b^3$; choose $i=-k$ modulo three to obtain an element $c=a^ib$ of order three outside $N$. This proves

$$
\boxed{G\cong C_9\rtimes C_3,\qquad cac^{-1}=a^4.}
$$

There is a unique resulting isomorphism type, since the two nontrivial actions of order three are interchanged by reversing the complement's generator.

If there is no element of order nine, every nonidentity element has order three. Choose $a,b$ whose images form a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) of $G/Z(G)$ and put $c=[a,b]$. These elements generate $G$, and $c$ generates $Z(G)$. The [subgroup](../group.md#subgroup) $N=\langle a,c\rangle\cong C_3^2$ has index three and is normal. The line $\langle b\rangle$ meets $N$ trivially and complements it. Conjugation by $b$ fixes $c$ and sends $a$ to $ac$ or $ac^{-1}$; choosing the central generator removes this sign. Thus there is one isomorphism type,

$$
\boxed{G\cong C_3^2\rtimes C_3\cong\operatorname{UT}_3(\mathbb F_3),}
$$

with the nontrivial unipotent action on $\mathbb F_3^2$. These presentations are realized by the indicated [semidirect products](../group-theory.md#semidirect-product) and have order 27. Their exponents are nine and three respectively, so they are not isomorphic. This proves both existence and completeness of the two cases.

**Their ordinary [character tables](../representation-theory.md#character-table) are the same**, up to relabelling. In either [group](../group.md) each of the 24 noncentral elements has [conjugacy class](../group-action.md#conjugacy-class) of size three, giving eight noncentral classes and three central singleton classes. Each noncentral class is $xZ(G)$, since conjugates of $x$ differ from $x$ by central [commutators](../lie-algebra.md#commutator) and its class has size three. There are nine [linear characters](../representation-theory.md#linear-character), arising from $G/G'\cong C_3^2$, and eleven [irreducible characters](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-character) altogether. The two remaining degrees satisfy $d_1^2+d_2^2=27-9=18$, so both are three.

Put $Z(G)=\langle z\rangle$ and $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$. Label the noncentral classes by nonzero $(i,j)\in\mathbb F_3^2$. For $(r,s)\in\mathbb F_3^2$ the nine [linear characters](../representation-theory.md#linear-character) have values

$$
\psi_{rs}(1)=\psi_{rs}(z)=\psi_{rs}(z^2)=1,
\qquad \psi_{rs}(i,j)=\omega^{ri+sj}.
$$

At a noncentral element, [column orthogonality](../representation-theory.md#column-orthogonality) gives a squared column norm of nine. The nine [linear characters](../representation-theory.md#linear-character) already contribute nine, so both degree-three [characters](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) vanish there. By the [Schur lemma](../representation-theory.md#schur-s-lemma), a central element has value $3\omega^k$ in an irreducible representation of degree three. The central [character](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) cannot be trivial, since that would factor the representation through the abelian quotient. Orthogonality of the identity and $z$ columns then forces the two nontrivial central [characters](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) to occur once each. The final two rows, on $1,z,z^2$ and the eight other columns, are

$$
(3,3\omega,3\omega^2,0,\ldots,0),\qquad
(3,3\omega^2,3\omega,0,\ldots,0).
$$

This description gives the same entire [character table](../representation-theory.md#character-table) in both cases. The differing element orders are additional power-map data, which an ordinary [character table](../representation-theory.md#character-table) need not determine.

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="6/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#6/a)

The identity column gives the [group order](../group.md#order-of-a-finite-group) as the sum of squared [irreducible character degrees](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-character-degree):

$$
\boxed{|G|=1+4\cdot7^2+8^2+3\cdot9^2=504.}
$$

By [column orthogonality](../representation-theory.md#column-orthogonality), the squared norm of a column is the corresponding [centralizer](../group-action.md#centralizer) order. In source order these orders and the resulting [conjugacy class](../group-action.md#conjugacy-class) sizes are

$$
\boxed{|C_G(g)|=(504,8,9,9,9,9,7,7,7),}
$$



$$
|g^G|=(1,63,56,56,56,56,72,72,72).
$$

For example the fourth column has squared norm $1+1+(|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2+|\gamma|^2)+1=9$, while the seventh has squared norm $1+1+(|\xi|^2+|\eta|^2+|\phi|^2)=7$.

An element's order divides its [centralizer](../group-action.md#centralizer) order. [Cauchy's theorem for finite groups](../finite-group-theory.md#cauchy-theorem-for-groups) provides an [involution](../group-theory.md#involution), and only $C_2$ has an even [centralizer](../group-action.md#centralizer) order. Hence that entire class consists of [involutions](../group-theory.md#involution). The last three classes consist of elements of order seven. In $C_3,C_4,C_5,C_6$ the possibilities are three and nine. The three real numbers $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ are roots of $t^3-3t+1$, which has no rational root and is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. A real [character](../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) value at an element of order three belongs to $\mathbb Q(e^{2\pi i/3})\cap\mathbb R=\mathbb Q$. Therefore the irrational real entries in $C_4,C_5,C_6$ rule out order three there. Cubing any of these order-nine elements produces an element of order three, which must belong to the sole remaining class $C_3$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}(C_1),\ldots,\operatorname{ord}(C_9)
=(1,2,3,9,9,9,7,7,7).}
$$

The three deductions below give nine [Sylow subgroups](../finite-group-theory.md#sylow-subgroup) for $2$ with pairwise trivial intersections. Their conjugation action is transitive. Fix one, $S$. If $T\ne S$, put $H=S\cap N_G(T)$. The product $HT$ is a $2$-[subgroup](../group.md#subgroup), so maximality of $T$ gives $H\leq T$. Trivial intersection then gives $H=1$. Thus the [orbit](../dynamical-systems.md#orbit-dynamical-system) of $T$ under $S$ has size eight, exhausting the other eight points. The point [stabilizer](../group-action.md#stabilizer-subgroup) $N_G(S)$ is therefore transitive on the remaining points: **the action is doubly transitive**.

For a doubly transitive action, its [permutation character](../representation-theory.md#permutation-character) has the form $1+\theta$ with $\theta$ irreducible. Here $\theta(1)=8$, and the unique degree-eight row is $\chi_6$. Therefore the numbers of fixed points are the entries of

$$
\boxed{\pi=\chi_1+\chi_6=(9,1,0,0,0,0,2,2,2).}
$$

Every nonidentity element consequently fixes at most two points. This also shows that the action is faithful.

<h4 id="6/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#6/a)

<h5 id="6/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#6/a/i)

A [Sylow subgroup](../finite-group-theory.md#sylow-subgroup) for $2$ has order eight. Every one of its nonidentity elements lies in the only even-order class, so has order two. In a [group](../group.md) of exponent two,

$$
xy=(xy)^{-1}=y^{-1}x^{-1}=yx.
$$

It is therefore an [elementary abelian group](../group.md#elementary-abelian-group) and $\boxed{S\cong C_2^3}$.

<h4 id="6/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#6/a)

<h5 id="6/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#6/a/ii)

If [Sylow subgroups](../finite-group-theory.md#sylow-subgroup) $S,T$ share $t\ne1$, they are abelian by the preceding deduction, so $S,T\leq C_G(t)$. But $|C_G(t)|=8$ and both [subgroups](../group.md#subgroup) have order eight. Thus $S=C_G(t)=T$. Consequently **distinct Sylow $2$-[subgroups](../group.md#subgroup) intersect only in the identity**.

<h4 id="6/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#6/a)

<h5 id="6/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#6/a/iii)

All 63 [involutions](../group-theory.md#involution) lie in [Sylow subgroups](../finite-group-theory.md#sylow-subgroup) for $2$. Each such [subgroup](../group.md#subgroup) has exactly seven nonidentity elements and distinct ones have no nonidentity overlap. Hence

$$
\boxed{n_2=63/7=9.}
$$

<h3 id="6/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#6/b)

Over $\mathbb F_q$, the first nonzero column of an invertible $2\times2$ [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) has $q^2-1$ choices and the second has $q^2-q$ choices. The surjective [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) homomorphism onto $\mathbb F_q^\times$ has [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) $\operatorname{SL}_2(q)$. Thus

$$
\boxed{|\operatorname{SL}_2(8)|=\frac{(8^2-1)(8^2-8)}{8-1}=504.}
$$

It acts on the nine one-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb F_8^2$, namely the [projective line](../finite-group-theory.md#projective-line) $\mathbb F_8\cup\{\infty\}$, by fractional linear transformations. Any element acting trivially fixes $0,\infty,1$, which forces it to be scalar. A scalar [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) in $\operatorname{SL}_2(8)$ has $a^2=1$; in characteristic two this implies $a=1$. Thus the action is faithful.

The action is transitive: any nonzero vector can be completed to a [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant)-one [basis](../vector-space.md#basis), so any line can be reached. The [stabilizer](../group-action.md#stabilizer-subgroup) of $\infty=[1:0]$ is

$$
\boxed{\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}:
 a\in\mathbb F_8^\times,\ b\in\mathbb F_8\right\}
\cong (C_2)^3\rtimes C_7.}
$$

On finite points this is $x\mapsto a^2x+ab$. Squaring permutes the seven nonzero field elements, so these are exactly the affine maps $x\mapsto ux+v$. In particular translations act transitively on all eight finite points. Thus the full action is doubly transitive. The point [stabilizer](../group-action.md#stabilizer-subgroup) has order $8\cdot7=56$, and all point [stabilizers](../group-action.md#stabilizer-subgroup) are conjugate.
