<h1 id="a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A [simple module](../../../../irreducible-module.md) is a nonzero module with no proper nonzero [submodule](../../../../submodule.md). For a finite-dimensional simple complex module $S$, any [endomorphism](../../../../endomorphism.md) $\varphi$ has a complex [eigenvalue](../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$. The nonzero [kernel](../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) of $\varphi-\lambda I$ is an $A$-[submodule](../../../../submodule.md), hence all of $S$. Thus **$\varphi=\lambda I$**, proving [Schur's lemma](../../../../schur-s-lemma.md).

More generally a nonzero map between two simple modules is an isomorphism, because its [kernel](../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) is zero and its [image](../../../../image-of-a-function.md) is the whole target. The [endomorphism](../../../../endomorphism.md) [algebra](../../../../algebra-split.md) of $S_1\oplus S_2$ is the block [algebra](../../../../algebra-split.md) with entries $\operatorname{Hom}_A(S_j,S_i)$. For nonisomorphic summands its off-diagonal entries vanish and its diagonal entries are scalars, giving $\mathbb C\times\mathbb C$. For isomorphic summands, choose one fixed isomorphism to identify them; every block is scalar and block composition is matrix multiplication, giving $M_2(\mathbb C)$.

A finite-dimensional [algebra](../../../../algebra-split.md) is a [semisimple algebra](../../../../semisimple-algebra.md) when its [left regular module](../../../../left-regular-module.md) is a [direct sum](../../../../direct-sum.md) of simple modules. The complex [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](../../../../artin-wedderburn-theorem.md) gives $A\cong\prod_iM_{d_i}(\mathbb C)$, with the multiset of positive integers $d_i$ uniquely determined. Its [dimension](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) is $\sum_i d_i^2$. For [dimension](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) eight only sizes one and two can occur; the number of size-two blocks is zero, one, or two. Thus there are **exactly three isomorphism types**:

$$
\boxed{\mathbb C^8,\quad M_2(\mathbb C)\times\mathbb C^4,\quad M_2(\mathbb C)\times M_2(\mathbb C).}
$$

They are distinct by the uniqueness clause, or by their numbers of one-dimensional simple modules. If $G$ is nonabelian, $\mathbb CG$ is [noncommutative](../../../../noncommutativity.md) because its group elements form a basis, so the first type is excluded. The augmentation $\sum a_gg\mapsto\sum a_g$ provides a one-dimensional [simple module](../../../../irreducible-module.md), excluding the third type. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathbb CG\cong\mathbb C^4\times M_2(\mathbb C).}
$$

There is one [simple module](../../../../irreducible-module.md) for each matrix block, of [dimension](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) the block size, so exactly four are one-dimensional. Every nonabelian group of order eight therefore has this same complex [group algebra](../../../../group-algebra.md).
