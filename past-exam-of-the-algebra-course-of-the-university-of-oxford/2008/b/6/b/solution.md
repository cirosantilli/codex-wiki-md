<h1 id="b/6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The quotient $PN/N$ is a $p$-group, being the [image](../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) of $P$, and

$$
[G/N:PN/N]=[G:PN]\mid[G:P].
$$

Its index is [prime](../../../../../prime-number-split.md) to $p$, so **$PN/N$ is Sylow in $G/N$**.

If $P\leq A$ and $Q\leq B$ are Sylow $p$-subgroups, their product has the full $p$-part of $|A\times B|$, hence is Sylow. Conversely, for any Sylow $R\leq A\times B$, its two projections are $p$-subgroups and lie in some Sylows $P,Q$. Then $R\subseteq P\times Q$, and equality follows by order. Distinct pairs give distinct subgroups because their projections recover $P$ and $Q$. Thus

$$
\boxed{n_p(A\times B)=n_p(A)n_p(B)=mn.}
$$
