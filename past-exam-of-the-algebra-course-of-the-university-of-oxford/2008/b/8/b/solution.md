<h1 id="b/8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For order $105=3\cdot5\cdot7$, Sylow's congruence and divisibility conditions give

$$
n_5\in\{1,21\},\qquad n_7\in\{1,15\}.
$$

If neither [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) were a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), there would be $21\cdot4=84$ elements of order five and $15\cdot6=90$ elements of order seven. Distinct subgroups of either [prime](../../../../../prime-number-split.md) order intersect only at the identity, and elements of the two orders are disjoint. This would give at least $84+90+1=175$ elements in a group of order $105$, a contradiction. Thus **a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) of order five or seven is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md)**, and the group is not simple.
