<h1 id="b/8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the preliminary index assertion, the [coset action](../../../../../coset-action.md) gives a homomorphism $G\to S_n$. Its [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) and cannot be all of $G$, because the action is transitive on $n>1$ points. Simplicity makes the [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) trivial. The [sign homomorphism](../../../../../sign-homomorphism.md) of this faithful action must be trivial: otherwise simplicity would identify $G$ with $C_2$, impossible for a transitive action of degree at least three. Thus $G$ embeds in $A_n$, and Lagrange gives

$$
\boxed{|G|\mid n!/2.}
$$

A group of order $112=16\cdot7$ has a Sylow two-[subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) of index seven. If the group were simple, the preceding result would give $112\mid7!/2=2520$. This fails because $112$ is divisible by $16$ while $2520$ has only three factors of two. Thus **there is no [simple group](../../../../../simple-group.md) of order $112$**.

For order $56$, Sylow gives $n_7\in\{1,8\}$. If it is one, the order-seven Sylow is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md). Otherwise its eight Sylows of order seven intersect only at the identity, giving $8\cdot6=48$ nonidentity elements of order seven. Only eight elements remain including the identity. Every Sylow two-[subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) has order eight and consists entirely of these remaining elements. Hence all Sylow two-subgroups coincide and the order-eight [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md). Either case gives a nontrivial proper [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), so **no group of order $56$ is simple**.
