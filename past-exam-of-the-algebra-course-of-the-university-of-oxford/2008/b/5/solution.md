<h1 id="b/5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Write $C=\langle c\rangle$ with $|c|=n$. For each divisor $k$ the [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) $\langle c^{n/k}\rangle$ has order $k$. Conversely, if $H$ has order $k$, Lagrange gives $k\mid n$, and every $c^j\in H$ satisfies $(c^j)^k=1$, so $n\mid jk$. Therefore $n/k\mid j$ and $H\subseteq\langle c^{n/k}\rangle$. Equality follows from the common order. Thus **there is exactly one [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) for each divisor of $n$**.

For the Abelian assertion, take a surjection $\mathbb Z^n\to A$ and let $L$ be the inverse [image](../../../../image-of-a-function.md) of $B$. Every [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) of $\mathbb Z^n$ has at most $n$ generators, by induction: its projection onto the first coordinate is either zero, reducing to $\mathbb Z^{n-1}$, or $d\mathbb Z$ for some $d>0$. In the latter case choose one element with first coordinate $d$; subtracting its multiples reduces every element to the [kernel](../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md), generated by at most $n-1$ elements by induction. The base case is the elementary description of subgroups of $\mathbb Z$ by their least positive element. Images of these generators generate $B$, proving the claim.

In $S_6$, the commuting disjoint transpositions $(12),(34),(56)$ generate $C_2^3$. This is a three-dimensional [vector space](../../../../vector-space-split.md) over $\mathbb F_2$, so it requires three generators and cannot be generated by two.

For each $n\geq3$, take $G=S_{2n+2}$, generated by $(12)$ and the cycle $(1\,2\,\ldots\,2n+2)$. Its [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) generated by

$$
(12),(34),\ldots,(2n+1\,2n+2)
$$

is $C_2^{n+1}$. It requires $n+1$ generators by the same [dimension](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) argument, giving **a two-generated group with a [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) requiring more than $n$ generators**.
