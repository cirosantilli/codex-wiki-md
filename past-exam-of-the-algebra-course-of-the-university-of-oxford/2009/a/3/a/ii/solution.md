<h1 id="a/3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Choose $s\ne0$ in the [simple module](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md) $S$. The map $\phi:A\to S$, $a\mapsto as$, is an $A$-module homomorphism whose [image](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) is the nonzero [submodule](../../../../../../submodule.md) $As$, hence all of $S$. Thus the [first isomorphism theorem for modules](../../../../../../first-isomorphism-theorem-for-modules.md) gives

$$
\boxed{S\cong A/\ker\phi.}
$$

The kernel is a maximal left ideal because the quotient is simple. Whenever the regular [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) has finite length, as it does for a finite-dimensional [algebra](../../../../../../algebra-split.md), take a composition series of the kernel and append $\ker\phi<A$. Its last factor is $S$, so $S$ occurs as a [composition factor](../../../../../../composition-factor.md) of the regular [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md); the [Jordan–Hölder theorem for modules](../../../../../../jordan-holder-theorem-for-modules.md) makes that independent of the chosen series.

The quotient conclusion holds for every unital [algebra](../../../../../../algebra-split.md). If an arbitrary infinite-dimensional regular [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) has no finite composition series, the universally valid statement is that $S$ is a simple subquotient; a claim about the factors of a finite series requires finite length.
