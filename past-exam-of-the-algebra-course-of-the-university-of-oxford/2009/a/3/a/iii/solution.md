<h1 id="a/3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A [semisimple module](../../../../../../semisimple-module.md) is a direct sum of [simple modules](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md). An [algebra](../../../../../../algebra-split.md) is semisimple when all its [modules](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) are semisimple. Suppose the left regular [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $A$ is semisimple. Every [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $M$ is a quotient of a free [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $\bigoplus_{j\in J}A$, by sending its basis elements to a generating set of $M$. This free [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is a direct sum of simple [modules](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md).

A quotient of a direct sum of simple [modules](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is semisimple: the images of its simple summands are simple or zero and span the quotient. Choose a maximal direct sum among these simple images, allowing all simple submodules in the choice. If it does not span the whole quotient, some simple image is not contained in it. Its intersection with that direct sum is zero by simplicity, so it could be added, contradicting maximality. For infinite index sets, [Zorn's lemma](../../../../../../zorn-s-lemma.md) supplies the maximal direct sum; in finite dimension the construction terminates directly.

Hence every $M$ is semisimple, and **$A$ is a [semisimple algebra](../../../../../../semisimple-algebra.md)**. This proves [semisimplicity from the regular module](../../../../../../semisimplicity-from-the-regular-module.md).
