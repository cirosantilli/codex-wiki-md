<h1 id="a/1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In the finite-dimensional setting of the preceding part, let $S$ be a [simple module](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md). Each action map $s\mapsto as$ is an $A$-module endomorphism because $A$ is commutative. The complex [Schur's lemma](../../../../../../schur-s-lemma.md) makes every such action a scalar. Therefore every [linear subspace](../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) of $S$ is an [submodule](../../../../../../submodule.md); simplicity forces

$$
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb C}S=1.}
$$

Equivalently, a simple [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) over a commutative [ring](../../../../../../ring.md) is a quotient by a [maximal ideal](../../../../../../maximal-ideal.md), and a finite-dimensional field extension of $\mathbb C$ is just $\mathbb C$.

The finite-dimensional context matters. If the clause is read as allowing an arbitrary commutative complex [algebra](../../../../../../algebra-split.md), the unrestricted assertion is false: $A=\mathbb C(t)$ is simple as a [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) over itself and is infinite-dimensional over $\mathbb C$. This is [simple module of a finite-dimensional commutative complex algebra](../../../../../../simple-module-of-a-finite-dimensional-commutative-complex-algebra.md). Thus **the one-dimensional conclusion uses the finite-dimensional algebra context**, or an alternative hypothesis such as finite dimensionality of the simple [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md).
