↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Maschke theorem](../../../../../maschke-s-theorem.md) says that for a finite [group](../../../../../group-split.md) over a [field](../../../../../field.md) whose characteristic does not divide its order, every finite-dimensional [group representation](../../../../../group-representation.md) is semisimple; equivalently its [group algebra](../../../../../group-algebra.md) is a [semisimple algebra](../../../../../semisimple-algebra.md). This applies over $\mathbb C$.

The [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](../../../../../artin-wedderburn-theorem.md) says that a finite-dimensional complex [semisimple algebra](../../../../../semisimple-algebra.md) is a product $\prod_iM_{d_i}(\mathbb C)$, with one simple [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $\mathbb C^{d_i}$ for each factor, up to [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md). Applying it to $\mathbb CG$ yields

$$
\mathbb CG\cong\prod_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)}M_{\chi(1)}(\mathbb C).
$$

The [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) of the left-hand side is $|G|$, while a matrix algebra $M_d(\mathbb C)$ has [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) $d^2$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{|G|=\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)}\chi(1)^2.}
$$
