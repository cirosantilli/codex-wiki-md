<h1 id="b/4/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), a nonidentity element in a group of order four has order two or four. In the latter case the group is cyclic. Otherwise every nonidentity element has order two, and $(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}=ba$ while $(ab)^{-1}=ab$, so the group is [Abelian](../../../../../../abelian-group.md). Choose distinct nonidentity elements $a,b$. Their cyclic subgroups meet trivially and $ab$ is the fourth element, so $(i,j)\mapsto a^ib^j$ gives an [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md) $C_2\times C_2\to G$. Hence

$$
\boxed{G\cong C_4\quad\text{or}\quad C_2\times C_2.}
$$

No classification of finite [Abelian groups](../../../../../../abelian-group.md) is used.
