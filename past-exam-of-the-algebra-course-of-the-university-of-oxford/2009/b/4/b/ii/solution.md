<h1 id="b/4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

By [Cauchy theorem for groups](../../../../../../cauchy-theorem-for-groups.md), choose $a$ of order $p$ and $b$ of order two. The subgroup $P=\langle a\rangle$ has index two, hence is normal, and $b\notin P$. Thus $a,b$ generate $G$. Conjugation by $b$ has $bab^{-1}=a^r$ with $r\not\equiv0\pmod p$ and $r^2\equiv1\pmod p$. Since $p$ is prime, $r\equiv1$ or $-1$.

If $r=1$, the two generators commute and $ab$ has order $2p$, so $G$ is cyclic. If $r=-1$, then $(ab)^2=e$ and $ab\ne b$. The two involutions $b,ab$ generate $G$, because $(ab)b=a$. Hence $G$ is dihedral in the stated convention. Thus

$$
\boxed{G\cong C_{2p}\quad\text{or}\quad D_{2p},}
$$

where $D_{2p}$ denotes the dihedral group of order $2p$. In both cases $P$ is a proper nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), so **$G$ is not simple**.
