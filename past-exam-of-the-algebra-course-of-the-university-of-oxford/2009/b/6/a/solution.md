<h1 id="b/6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [commutator subgroup](../../../../../commutator-subgroup.md) is $G'=\langle xyx^{-1}y^{-1}:x,y\in G\rangle$. It is characteristic and hence normal, because every automorphism sends a commutator to a commutator. If $G'\leq H$, then $ghg^{-1}h^{-1}\in H$ for every $g\in G,h\in H$, so $H$ is normal. Every commutator becomes trivial in $G/H$, making that quotient [Abelian](../../../../../abelian-group.md). Conversely, the quotient homomorphism to an [Abelian](../../../../../abelian-group.md) group kills each commutator, and hence kills $G'$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{G'\leq H\iff H\triangleleft G\text{ and }G/H\text{ is Abelian}.}
$$

Set $G^{(0)}=G$, $G^{(i+1)}=(G^{(i)})'$. The [group](../../../../../group-split.md) is [soluble](../../../../../solvable-group.md) exactly when this [derived series](../../../../../derived-series.md) reaches the identity after finitely many steps.

If a nontrivial simple [group](../../../../../group-split.md) is soluble, its normal subgroup $G'$ is either $1$ or $G$. The latter would make the derived series constant and nontrivial, so $G'=1$ and $G$ is [Abelian](../../../../../abelian-group.md). Every nonidentity element then generates $G$, since its cyclic subgroup is normal. An infinite cyclic group is not simple, and a finite cyclic group is simple exactly when its order is prime. This proves [soluble simple group has prime order](../../../../../soluble-simple-group-has-prime-order.md).
