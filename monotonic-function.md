# Monotonic function

↑ **Parent:** [Calculus](calculus-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonic_function)

A [real-valued function](real-valued-function.md) $f:D\to\mathbb R$, with [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) $D\subseteq\mathbb R$ in the usual order of the [real numbers](real-number.md), is a [monotonic function](monotonic-function.md) if it is either a [nondecreasing function](nondecreasing-function.md) throughout $D$ or a [nonincreasing function](nonincreasing-function.md) throughout $D$. The same direction must work for every pair $x<y$ in $D$. Being a [strictly increasing function](strictly-increasing-function.md) on one part and a [strictly decreasing function](strictly-decreasing-function.md) on another does not make the whole [function](function-split.md) [monotone](monotonic-function.md): for example, $x^2$ on the [real line](real-line.md) changes direction at zero. A [constant function](constant-function.md) is both a [nondecreasing function](nondecreasing-function.md) and a [nonincreasing function](nonincreasing-function.md).

The word "increasing" is ambiguous across textbooks: some allow equal outputs and some require unequal outputs. Use [nondecreasing function](nondecreasing-function.md) for the weak inequality and [strictly increasing function](strictly-increasing-function.md) for the strict inequality; use [nonincreasing function](nonincreasing-function.md) and [strictly decreasing function](strictly-decreasing-function.md) in the other direction. Neither [continuity](continuous-function.md) nor [differentiability](differentiability.md) is part of the definition of a [monotonic function](monotonic-function.md).

Strict [monotonicity](monotonic-function.md) gives [injectivity](injective-function.md), so an equation $f(x)=b$ has at most one solution and an [inverse function](inverse-function.md) exists on the [image of a function](image-of-a-function.md). Weak [monotonicity](monotonic-function.md) permits repeated outputs and jumps. In [probability theory](probability-theory-split.md), a [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md) is a [nondecreasing function](nondecreasing-function.md); its [quantile function](quantile-function.md) uses a generalized inverse to handle those flat parts and jumps. [Monotonicity](monotonic-function.md) alone does not guarantee [continuity](continuous-function.md), even for a [strictly increasing function](strictly-increasing-function.md).
