# Occupation of an interacting localized electron level

↑ **Parent:** [Impurity level in a crystal](impurity-level-in-a-crystal.md)

A localized orbital with two [electron spin](electron-spin.md) states has [energies](energy-split.md) $E_0=0$, $E_1=\epsilon$ and $E_2=2\epsilon+U$. Its zero-[temperature](temperature-split.md) equilibrium occupation minimizes $\mathcal E_N=E_N-\mu N$, where $\mu$ is the [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md). For repulsion $U>0$, the occupation is successively $0,1,2$ as $\mu$ crosses $\epsilon$ and $\epsilon+U$. For attraction $U<0$, the singly occupied state is never the minimum: the occupation jumps from $0$ to $2$ at $\mu=\epsilon+U/2$. Exactly at a crossing the ground-state occupation is not unique.

At positive [temperature](temperature-split.md), its [grand canonical partition function](grand-canonical-partition-function.md) is $\Xi=1+2e^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}+e^{-\beta(2\epsilon+U-2\mu)}$, where $\beta=1/(k_BT)$. The [expected value](expected-value-split.md) of the occupation follows by differentiating: $\langle N\rangle=k_BT\,\partial_\mu\ln\Xi$. The factor two counts the two singly occupied [microstates](microstate.md), not an additional pair state.
