# Pressure calibration

↑ **Parent:** [High-pressure experimental metrology](high-pressure-experimental-metrology.md)

[Pressure calibration](pressure-calibration.md) assigns [pressure](pressure-split.md) values to a sensor's output by comparison with a reference under specified conditions. It is not the same as making the output more finely resolved. A smooth [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) curve can be repeatable yet have an incorrect [pressure](pressure-split.md) scale.

Bridgman used pressure-dependent [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) of [manganin](manganin.md) and established fixed points, including a [bismuth](bismuth.md) transition. He reported poorer accuracy above the better calibrated range.[p. 56, pressure-measurement paragraph: manganin gauge, bismuth fixed point, and different accuracy estimates below and above 30000 kg/cm²](general-survey-of-certain-results-in-the-field-of-high-pressure-physics-by-percy-williams-bridgman.md)

A fixed-point specimen and a property-measurement sample have distinct roles: one helps assign the scale, while the other is studied on that scale. The reference transition must itself have an assigned [pressure](pressure-split.md) at the relevant [temperature](temperature-split.md). Agreement at a single point does not determine a nonlinear sensor response elsewhere. Comparing increasing and decreasing [pressure](pressure-split.md) checks [hysteresis](hysteresis.md); returning to a reference checks drift. These are checks one can design from the measurement model, not additional historical controls asserted for the lecture's experiments.
