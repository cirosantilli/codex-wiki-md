# Negative part of a finite convex function is Lipschitz

↑ **Parent:** [Convex function](convex-function.md)

For a finite [convex function](convex-function.md) $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$, its negative part $f^-=\max(-f,0)$ is globally [Lipschitz continuous](lipschitz-continuity.md). The negative sublevel set is an interval. If bounded, local Lipschitz continuity of finite convex functions bounds the slopes on its closure. If it is a left ray, any negative slope there would force $f$ positive far to the left by the supporting-line inequality; all slopes on that ray are therefore nonnegative and bounded above near its finite endpoint. For a right ray the analogous slopes are nonpositive and bounded below. If $f$ is negative everywhere, convexity and boundedness above force it to be constant. These cases bound the slopes of $f^-$ globally, including its zero extension outside the interval. In particular, integrability of $f(Z)$ implies integrability of the negative part of every fixed translate $f(Z+x)$.
