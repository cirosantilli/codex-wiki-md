# Nuclear rotational spectroscopy evidence

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear rotational band](nuclear-rotational-band.md)

[Aage Niels Bohr](aage-niels-bohr.md)'s 1975 lecture presents a [plutonium](plutonium.md) ${}^{238}\mathrm{Pu}$ spectrum deduced from the fine structure of [alpha decay](alpha-decay.md) of [curium](curium.md) ${}^{242}\mathrm{Cm}$. The readout was decay-line [energy](energy-split.md); the comparison fixed the rotor scale from the first excitation and tested the remaining levels against $I(I+1)$. The lecture reports spectroscopy, rather than a laboratory setup operated by its author.[p. 217, Fig. 3 caption, plutonium spectrum and one-scale comparison](rotational-motion-in-nuclei-by-aage-niels-bohr.md)

His measured [erbium](erbium.md) ${}^{166}\mathrm{Er}$ $K=0$ band has excitation [energies](energy-split.md) $0$, $80.56$, $265.0$, $545.4$ and $911.2\,\mathrm{keV}$ for $I=0,2,4,6,8$. The same lecture analyzes interband [electric quadrupole transitions](electric-quadrupole-transition.md), adding tests beyond level positions.[p. 225, Fig. 9 measured levels and following discussion of Fig. 10 transition matrix elements](rotational-motion-in-nuclei-by-aage-niels-bohr.md)

Using those first two excitations gives $265.0/80.56\simeq3.28947$, close to the ideal $10/3$, and distinct from the ideal harmonic value $2$. Fixing the rotor scale with $80.56\,\mathrm{keV}$ predicts $268.53\,\mathrm{keV}$ at $I=4$ and $563.92\,\mathrm{keV}$ at $I=6$. The measured values lie about $1.32\%$ and $3.28\%$ below these predictions. This is a useful limit of the simple model: the pattern supports a rotational interpretation while revealing departures from constant inertia. It does not by itself determine a unique [nuclear charge distribution](nuclear-charge-distribution.md). Reconstructing a level scheme also requires assigning which transitions connect which states; raw peak positions alone do not provide all [spins](spin.md), [parities](parity.md) or transition strengths.
