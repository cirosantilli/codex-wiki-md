# Complex derivative

↑ **Parent:** [Complex differentiability at a point](complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md)

The complex derivative exists when this [limit](limit-of-a-function.md) has the same finite value for increments $h$ approaching zero from every complex direction. For $f=u+iv$, real and imaginary increments give the necessary [Cauchy-Riemann equations](cauchy-riemann-equations.md). Existence of those [partial derivatives](partial-derivative.md) at one point alone is not sufficient for [complex differentiability at a point](complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md); continuous [partial derivatives](partial-derivative.md) in a neighbourhood, together with the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](cauchy-riemann-equations.md) there, give a standard sufficient condition for $f$ to be a [holomorphic function](holomorphic-function-split.md).
