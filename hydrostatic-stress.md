# Hydrostatic stress

↑ **Parent:** [High-pressure experimental metrology](high-pressure-experimental-metrology.md)

With tension taken positive, [hydrostatic stress](hydrostatic-stress.md) is the [stress tensor](cauchy-stress-tensor.md) $\boldsymbol{\sigma}=-p\mathbf I$, where $\mathbf I$ is the [identity matrix](identity-matrix.md). Every plane has the same compressive normal [stress](stress.md), and no tangential traction. More generally,

$$
p=-\frac13\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\sigma},\qquad
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=-p\mathbf I+\mathbf s,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}\mathbf s=0,
$$

where $\mathbf s$ is [deviatoric stress](deviatoric-stress.md). Two samples can share the same mean [pressure](pressure-split.md) while experiencing different [deviatoric stresses](deviatoric-stress.md). A stationary [liquid](liquid.md) cannot sustain equilibrium shear [stress](stress.md); a [solid](solid.md) transmitting medium can support it. Consequently, a load divided by [surface area](surface-area.md) does not by itself establish hydrostatic sample conditions. A transmitting medium's state at the actual [pressure](pressure-split.md) and [temperature](temperature-split.md), and contact between sample and walls, matter.

Bridgman's Figure 4 compares mild [steel](steel.md) fractured in tension at [atmospheric pressure](atmospheric-pressure.md) with [steel](steel.md) stretched further in a [liquid](liquid.md) at $25000\,\mathrm{kgf\,cm^{-2}}$.[p. 56, Figure 4 caption: atmospheric-pressure tensile fracture versus greater reduction of area in pressurized liquid; historical kg/cm² rendered explicitly as force per area](general-survey-of-certain-results-in-the-field-of-high-pressure-physics-by-percy-williams-bridgman.md) The imposed tension remains a directional load: the experiment combines confinement with deformation, rather than measuring a [hydrostatic stress](hydrostatic-stress.md) alone. Its comparison motivates recording the loading path and the surrounding medium as well as [pressure](pressure-split.md); it does not establish a universal fracture threshold for all [steels](steel.md).
