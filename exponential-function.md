# Exponential function

↑ **Parent:** [Calculus](calculus-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_function)

The real [exponential function](exponential-function.md) $\exp(x)=e^x$ converts addition into multiplication:

$$
\exp(x+y)=\exp(x)\exp(y),\qquad \exp(0)=1,\qquad \frac{d}{dx}\exp(x)=\exp(x).
$$

Its values are positive, and it defines a [bijection](bijection.md) from the [real numbers](real-number.md) to the positive [real numbers](real-number.md). The [natural logarithm](natural-logarithm.md) is its [inverse function](inverse-function.md). These properties connect arithmetic with [calculus](calculus-split.md): adding an amount to the input multiplies the output by a fixed factor, and the local rate of change equals the current value. The [exponential series](exponential-series.md) gives one construction; an equivalent characterization is the unique [solution of a differential equation](solution-of-a-differential-equation.md) $y'=y$, $y(0)=1$.

For a concrete example, $y(t)=10e^{0.2t}$ starts at $10$ and satisfies $y'=0.2y$. The absolute [derivative](derivative.md) increases with $y$, while the relative rate $y'/y$ stays $0.2$. At $t=5$, $y=10e\approx27.1828$ and $y'\approx5.43656$. The [exponential function](exponential-function.md) therefore models accumulation proportional to what is already present, rather than addition of a fixed amount at every step. A positive constant rate gives [exponential growth](exponential-growth.md); a negative one gives [exponential decay](exponential-decay.md).

Every positive real base $b\ne1$ fits the same construction through $b^x=e^{x\ln b}$. Thus its [derivative](derivative.md) is $(\ln b)b^x$: base $e$ is special because $\ln e=1$. The [exponential function](exponential-function.md) is also unchanged in form by [differentiation](differentiation.md) and [integration](integral.md), helping explain its repeated appearance in [linear ordinary differential equations](linear-ordinary-differential-equation.md). Its real definition, inverse relationship, and derivative rules are developed in [OpenStax, integrals, exponential functions, and logarithms](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/6-7-integrals-exponential-functions-and-logarithms).
