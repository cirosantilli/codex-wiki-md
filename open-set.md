# Open set

↑ **Parent:** [Topology](topology-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_set)

An open set is a member of the [topology](topology-split.md) on a [topological space](topological-space.md). In a [metric space](metric-space.md), a [subset](subset.md) $U$ is an [open set](open-set.md) precisely when every $x\in U$ has an [open ball](open-ball.md) $B(x,r)$ contained in $U$ for some $r>0$. For $U=(0,1)$, one can take $r=\tfrac12\min(x,1-x)$. At $x=0.2$, this gives $r=0.1$ and $B(x,r)=(0.1,0.3)\subset U$. The radius depends on the point; no uniform radius is required. [MIT Real Analysis, Lecture 13](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-100b-real-analysis-spring-2025/resources/ocw_18100b-lec13-2025apr03_mp4/)

Openness depends on the ambient [topological space](topological-space.md). In $A=[0,1]$ with its [subspace topology](subspace-topology.md), $[0,1/2)=A\cap(-1,1/2)$ is an [open set](open-set.md) in $A$, although it is not an [open set](open-set.md) in the [real line](real-line.md). Similarly, the whole [closed interval](closed-real-interval.md) $A$ is both an [open set](open-set.md) and a [closed set](closed-set.md) in itself. These relative [open sets](open-set.md) are what the [continuity](continuous-function.md) test uses for maps involving a [subset](subset.md) with its [subspace topology](subspace-topology.md).

Arbitrary [intersections](set-intersection.md) of [open sets](open-set.md) need not be [open sets](open-set.md): $\bigcap_{n\geq1}(-1/n,1/n)=\{0\}$, a [singleton](singleton-mathematics.md) that is not an [open set](open-set.md) in the usual [real line](real-line.md). Any point $x\ne0$ is excluded once $n>1/|x|$. This is why the [topology axiom](topology-axiom.md) specifies finite [intersections](set-intersection.md).
