# Weightlessness

↑ **Parent:** [Apparent weight](apparent-weight.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weightlessness)

Weightlessness is the absence of supporting or constraining [forces](force-split.md) that would give a body an [apparent weight](apparent-weight.md). It does not require zero [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md). A freely falling cabin and a small freely falling object inside share the same acceleration in a uniform field, so neither needs to support the other.

The [circular orbit as continuous free fall](circular-orbit-as-continuous-free-fall.md) gives a sustained example. Real spacecraft have small residual relative accelerations from [tidal forces](tidal-force.md), atmospheric [drag force](drag-physics.md) and onboard disturbances; the ideal common-fall explanation does not assert that every point in an extended spacecraft experiences exactly the same [Newtonian gravitational field](newtonian-gravitational-field.md). [Ramachandran, Numerical and Experimental Investigation of the Effects of Acceleration Disturbances on Microgravity Experiments (2000), abstract: vibration, atmospheric drag and gravity-gradient effects](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20000083212)
