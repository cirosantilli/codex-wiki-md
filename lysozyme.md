# Lysozyme

↑ **Parent:** [Protein](protein-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysozyme)

[Lysozyme](lysozyme.md) is a [protein](protein-split.md) [enzyme](enzyme-split.md) that attacks the [peptidoglycan](peptidoglycan.md) of bacterial [cell walls](cell-wall.md). Its [active site](active-site.md) is a cleft in the folded [protein structure](protein-structure.md), where a suitable [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) can interact with positioned chemical groups. This illustrates why possessing the right [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md) is insufficient if their spatial arrangement is lost. See [Alberts et al.: lysozyme as a worked example of protein binding and enzyme function](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26911/).

A concrete structural example is Cheetham, Artymiuk and Phillips's 1992 study of hen egg-white [lysozyme](lysozyme.md) bound to a short [carbohydrate](carbohydrate.md) that inhibits its action. The bound [carbohydrate](carbohydrate.md) occupies part of the [active site](active-site.md); it is not a reacting [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) in this complex. The study describes specific interactions and changes of shape accompanying binding. Thus a real binding site has chemical interactions and flexibility as well as a geometric outline. See [Cheetham, Artymiuk and Phillips, 1992: structure of a lysozyme complex with bound inhibitor](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1569548/) and [RCSB PDB entry 1HEW: the authors' structure and bound three-unit carbohydrate](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1HEW). The diagrams above illustrate the idea; their twelve-residue strings are not [lysozyme](lysozyme.md) sequences or coordinates.
