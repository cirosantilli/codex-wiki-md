# Composite number

↑ **Parent:** [Number theory](number-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_number)

A [composite number](composite-number.md) is an [integer](integer.md) greater than one that is not a [prime number](prime-number.md). Equivalently, it has a factorization $n=ab$ with [integers](integer.md) $a,b>1$. Thus $4=2\cdot2$, $9=3\cdot3$, and $12=3\cdot4$ are [composite numbers](composite-number.md); one is neither a [composite number](composite-number.md) nor a [prime number](prime-number.md).

Every [composite number](composite-number.md) has a [prime factor](prime-factor.md) at most its [square root](square-root.md). In $91=7\cdot13$, seven is the smaller factor and lies below $\sqrt{91}$; in $49=7^2$, equality occurs. This square-root bound makes [trial division](trial-division.md) finite and lets the [sieve of Eratosthenes](sieve-of-eratosthenes.md) stop marking with [prime numbers](prime-number.md) beyond $\sqrt N$ when listing [prime numbers](prime-number.md) up to $N$.
