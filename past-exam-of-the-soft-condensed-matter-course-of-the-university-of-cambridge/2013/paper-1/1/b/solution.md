<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The tension is supplied by [Stokes drag](../../../../../stokes-s-law.md) on the free bead. With bead [radius](../../../../../radius.md) $b=0.5\,\mu\mathrm m$, the stipulated isolated-sphere approximation gives

$$
\boxed{f=6\pi\eta bU=2.83\,\mathrm{pN}.}
$$

The trap also balances drag on the trapped bead, but that bead's drag is not an additional [force](../../../../../force-split.md) transmitted through the chain. The [polymer contour length](../../../../../polymer-contour-length.md) is $L=Na=6\,\mu\mathrm m$. Use a room-[temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) estimate $T\simeq300\,\mathrm K$, consistent with the water-viscosity approximation imported from part (a); the expressions below retain the dependence on $T$.

For a [theta solvent](../../../../../theta-solvent.md), [Gaussian chain](../../../../../gaussian-chain.md) [elasticity](../../../../../elasticity-physics-split.md) gives $F_{\rm el}=3k_BTR^2/(2Na^2)$ and hence

$$
\boxed{R_\theta=\frac{fNa^2}{3k_BT}
=0.819\left(\frac{300\,\mathrm K}{T}\right)\,\mu\mathrm m.}
$$

This is the mean extension along the [force](../../../../../force-split.md), rather than the unforced [end-to-end distance of a polymer chain](../../../../../end-to-end-distance-of-a-polymer-chain.md) $a\sqrt N=60\,\mathrm{nm}$. At $300\,\mathrm K$, $fa/(k_BT)=0.410$; the finite-link [freely jointed chain](../../../../../ideal-chain.md) result $R=L[\coth(fa/k_BT)-k_BT/(fa)]$ is $0.810\,\mu\mathrm m$, close to the Gaussian estimate.

The [good-solvent force-extension scaling](../../../../../good-solvent-force-extension-scaling.md) changes the [elasticity](../../../../../elasticity-physics-split.md). In the [tensile blob](../../../../../tensile-blob.md) regime, a blob has size $\xi_t=k_BT/f$, contains $g\sim(\xi_t/a)^{5/3}$ segments, and the aligned string of $N/g$ blobs has [length](../../../../../length.md)

$$
\boxed{R_{\rm good}\sim\frac Ng\xi_t
=Na\left(\frac{fa}{k_BT}\right)^{2/3}
\simeq3.31\left(\frac{300\,\mathrm K}{T}\right)^{2/3}\,\mu\mathrm m.}
$$

Only the scaling and order of magnitude of the good-solvent value are determined: solvent-dependent swelling amplitudes are not supplied. The nonlinear [force](../../../../../force-split.md) law is the essential correction to extrapolating a weak-force coil [spring](../../../../../spring.md).[abstract and section I, p. 386: strongly elongated good-solvent chain and restoring force proportional to extension to the power 3/2](../../../../../excluded-volume-effects-and-stretched-polymer-chains-by-philip-alan-pincus.md)

To expose the unrealistic extrapolation, the unforced good-solvent size estimate is $R_0\sim aN^{3/5}=151\,\mathrm{nm}$. Treating this coil as a linear [spring](../../../../../spring.md) with [spring constant](../../../../../spring-constant.md) $3k_BT/R_0^2$ predicts $R\simeq5.17\,\mu\mathrm m$ at $300\,\mathrm K$, although its required condition $fR_0\ll k_BT$ fails by a factor of about $103$. Dropping the order-one factor three, as in the rough estimate $k\sim k_BT/R_0^2$, even gives $R\sim15.5\,\mu\mathrm m>L$. **An extension beyond the [polymer contour length](../../../../../polymer-contour-length.md) is an artefact of extending the coil's [linear response](../../../../../linear-response-theory.md) outside its range.** The [tensile blob](../../../../../tensile-blob.md) estimate avoids that violation but here $\xi_t\simeq1.46\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $g\sim4.4$, so it is only marginally within a many-segment-blob regime. [finite extensibility of a polymer chain](../../../../../finite-extensibility-of-a-polymer-chain.md), chain drag and flow disturbance by both large beads further limit quantitative realism. The specified neglect of chain drag is a model assumption, not evidence that it is physically negligible.
