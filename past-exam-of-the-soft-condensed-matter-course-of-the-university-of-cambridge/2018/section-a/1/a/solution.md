<h1 id="section-a/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume steady fully developed laminar flow of a [Newtonian fluid](../../../../../newtonian-fluid.md) in a long circular tube, with no slip at a rigid wall. The [Hagen-Poiseuille equation](../../../../../hagen-poiseuille-equation.md) gives the volume flow rate

$$
Q=\frac{\pi R^4\Delta p}{8\eta\ell},
$$

where $R$ is the lumen radius, $\ell$ the tube length, $\eta$ the [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) and $\Delta p$ the pressure drop. If $\Delta p$, $\ell$ and $\eta$ remain fixed, then

$$
\frac{Q_{\mathrm{new}}}{Q_{\mathrm{old}}}=\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{new}}}{R_{\mathrm{old}}}\right)^4.
$$

Thus doubling the flow requires

$$
\boxed{R_{\mathrm{new}}=2^{1/4}R_{\mathrm{old}}\simeq1.189R_{\mathrm{old}},\qquad
\frac{\Delta R}{R_{\mathrm{old}}}\simeq18.9\%}.
$$

This estimate neglects pulsation, arterial compliance, entrance losses, the detailed shape of a stenosis and changes in the rest of the circulatory resistance. It treats [blood](../../../../../blood.md) as a homogeneous [Newtonian fluid](../../../../../newtonian-fluid.md) and assumes that enlarging the artery does not change its applied pressure drop. These assumptions supply the intended fourth-power scaling; an actual circulation need not keep that pressure drop fixed.
