<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For an ideally dissociated [electrolyte](../../../../electrolyte.md), the [Debye–Hückel screening length](../../../../debye-huckel-screening-length.md) is

$$
\lambda_D=\left(\frac{\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r k_BT}
{e^2\sum_i n_i z_i^2}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad I=\frac12\sum_i c_i z_i^2.
$$

Here $n_i$ is ionic [number density](../../../../number-density.md), $z_i$ the charge in units of the [elementary charge](../../../../elementary-charge.md), and $c_i$ is molar concentration. Conversion between $I$ and [number density](../../../../number-density.md) cancels in a ratio. At fixed [relative permittivity](../../../../relative-permittivity.md), $\lambda_D\propto\sqrt{T/I}$.

In $0.01\,\mathrm M$ [sodium chloride](../../../../sodium-chloride.md), $c_{\rm Na^+}=c_{\rm Cl^-}=0.01\,\mathrm M$, giving $I_1=0.01\,\mathrm M$. Complete dissociation of $0.1\,\mathrm M$ [aluminium chloride](../../../../aluminium-chloride.md) gives $c_{\rm Al^{3+}}=0.1\,\mathrm M$ and $c_{\rm Cl^-}=0.3\,\mathrm M$, so

$$
I_2=\frac12[0.1(3)^2+0.3(1)^2]=0.60\,\mathrm M=60I_1.
$$

The factor is sixty, not ten: the squared charge and the three counterions per formula unit both matter. Using the given $5\,\mathrm{nm}$ reference length directly, and $T_1\simeq298\,\mathrm K$, $T_2=323\,\mathrm K$,

$$
\boxed{\lambda_{D,2}=5\,\mathrm{nm}\sqrt{\frac{323}{298}\frac{0.01}{0.60}}
\simeq0.67\,\mathrm{nm}.}
$$

The modest [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) increase raises the screening length by only about $4\%$ at fixed concentration; the large increase in [ionic strength](../../../../ionic-strength.md) shortens it by almost an order of magnitude overall.

Under [charge stabilization of colloids](../../../../charge-stabilization-of-colloids.md), the electrostatic double-layer repulsion now has a much shorter range. Attractive [Van der Waals forces](../../../../van-der-waals-force.md) can then dominate at closer separations, reducing the barrier to [particle aggregation](../../../../particle-aggregation.md) in [DLVO theory](../../../../dlvo-theory.md). Thus **an electrostatically stabilized [colloid](../../../../colloid.md) is likely to be destabilized**, with aggregation more likely than in the original dispersion.

This is a conditional physical prediction, not a proof of instability from a length alone. No particle radius, surface potential, attractive interaction strength or steric coating is specified. [Steric stabilization](../../../../steric-stabilization.md) could preserve stability, and trivalent-ion [adsorption](../../../../adsorption.md) can alter the surface charge. Moreover, ideal screening theory is only an estimate at $I\simeq0.6\,\mathrm M$, where ion association, [hydrolysis](../../../../hydrolysis.md) and activities can matter. The numerical result uses the supplied reference length and the stipulated unchanged permittivity, rather than replacing either with an unprovided calibration.
