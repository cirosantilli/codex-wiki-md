↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**Entropic [elasticity](../../../../elasticity-physics-split.md).** A rubber contains flexible [polymer](../../../../polymer-split.md) strands tied into a network by [cross-links](../../../../cross-link.md). Stretching biases the strands toward relatively extended configurations, reducing their configurational [entropy](../../../../entropy-split.md). The restoring [entropic force](../../../../entropic-force.md) follows from the increase in [Helmholtz free energy](../../../../helmholtz-free-energy.md) $F=U-TS$. For a Gaussian strand with $N_s$ statistical steps of length $b$, its deformation-dependent [free energy](../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) is approximately $3k_BTR^2/(2N_sb^2)$. Its stiffness therefore increases with absolute [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), even if the underlying bond stiffness changes little.

In the ideal affine-network model of [rubber elasticity](../../../../rubber-elasticity.md), the [shear modulus](../../../../shear-modulus.md) is

$$
\boxed{G=\nu_s k_BT,}
$$

where $\nu_s$ is the [number density](../../../../number-density.md) of elastically active network strands. Thus heating increases the modulus approximately in proportion to $T$, provided the network density and rubbery state remain approximately unchanged. [Thermal expansion](../../../../thermal-expansion.md), changing relaxation times or damage can modify that simple trend.

Examples are **lightly crosslinked [natural rubber](../../../../natural-rubber.md)**, based on cis-rich [polyisoprene](../../../../polyisoprene.md), and **crosslinked [polydimethylsiloxane](../../../../polydimethylsiloxane.md)**. The crosslinks prevent the long-time macroscopic flow of an unconnected chain solution.

**Density estimate and the unspecified modulus convention.** With room [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) $T\simeq298\,\mathrm K$, the [Boltzmann constant](../../../../boltzmann-constant.md) gives $k_BT\simeq4.11\times10^{-21}\,\mathrm J$. If the stated modulus is $G=2\times10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, then

$$
\boxed{\nu_s=\frac{G}{k_BT}\simeq4.9\times10^{25}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}
\simeq81\,\mathrm{mol\,m^{-3}}.}
$$

The molar value counts moles of active strands. If “modulus” instead means [Young's modulus](../../../../young-s-modulus.md) $E$, a nearly incompressible isotropic rubber has $E\simeq3G$, and the inferred value is

$$
\boxed{\nu_s\simeq\frac{E}{3k_BT}=1.6\times10^{25}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}
\simeq27\,\mathrm{mol\,m^{-3}}.}
$$

Thus the robust order-of-magnitude estimate is $10^{25}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$. The [cross-link density inferred from rubber elasticity](../../../../cross-link-density-inferred-from-rubber-elasticity.md) is usually an effective strand density; the actual junction density is $\nu_j=2\nu_s/f$ for junction functionality $f$ in an ideal network without inactive loops or dangling ends. For four-functional junctions this would be half the strand density. The paper supplies neither modulus type nor junction functionality, so a unique chemical junction count cannot be extracted.
