<h1 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The [Reynolds number](../../../../reynolds-number.md) compares inertial and viscous effects:

$$
\mathrm{Re}=\frac{\rho UL}{\eta},
$$

where $\rho$ is fluid [mass density](../../../../density.md), $U$ a characteristic swimming [speed](../../../../speed.md), $L$ a characteristic body [length](../../../../length.md), and $\eta$ the [dynamic viscosity](../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md). Both swimmers are in the same fluid, so the fluid properties cancel from the ratio:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{bacterium}}}{\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{whale}}}
=\frac{U_bL_b}{U_wL_w}.
$$

For an order-of-magnitude estimate, take $L_b=2\,\mu\mathrm m$, $U_b=20\,\mu\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $L_w=10\,\mathrm m$ and $U_w=5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. With water values $\rho\simeq10^3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and $\eta\simeq10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$, these give

$$
\mathrm{Re}_b\simeq4\times10^{-5},\qquad
\mathrm{Re}_w\simeq5\times10^7,
$$

and hence

$$
\boxed{\frac{\mathrm{Re}_b}{\mathrm{Re}_w}\simeq8\times10^{-13}\sim10^{-12}}.
$$

The dimensions and swimming speeds are estimates, so this ratio is not a universal species-independent constant. Its physical significance is robust: [viscous forces](../../../../viscous-force.md) dominate the bacterium's motion, whereas fluid [inertia](../../../../inertia.md) is crucial for the whale.
