The [Reynolds number](../../../../../reynolds-number.md)

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Re}=\frac{\rho UL}{\eta}=\frac{UL}{\nu}}
$$

compares advective [inertia](../../../../../inertia.md) with viscous [force](../../../../../force-split.md) on a specified [length](../../../../../length.md) scale $L$ and [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) scale $U$. Here $\rho$ is fluid [mass density](../../../../../density.md), $\eta$ is [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) and $\nu=\eta/\rho$ is [kinematic viscosity](../../../../../kinematic-viscosity.md). In the [Navier-Stokes equation](../../../../../navier-stokes-equation.md), advective acceleration scales as $U^2/L$, while the viscous term per unit [mass](../../../../../mass.md) scales as $\nu U/L^2$; their ratio is [Reynolds number](../../../../../reynolds-number.md).

When $\mathrm{Re}\ll1$ and unsteady fluid [inertia](../../../../../inertia.md) is also negligible, the [incompressible flow](../../../../../incompressible-flow.md) equations reduce to [Stokes flow](../../../../../stokes-flow-split.md):

$$
-\nabla p+\eta\nabla^2\mathbf u+\mathbf f=\mathbf0,
\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf u=0.
$$

Thus [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md), applied body [forces](../../../../../force-split.md) and viscous stresses balance at each instant. For a translating rigid [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md), [drag](../../../../../drag-physics.md) is proportional to [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md), $F_D=6\pi\eta aU$, rather than the inertial scale $\rho a^2U^2$. Given the same instantaneous geometry, reversing the imposed boundary velocities reverses the [Stokes flow](../../../../../stokes-flow-split.md); there is no fluid-inertial coasting in this approximation. This statement concerns the fluid limit and does not discard a separately significant particle [inertia](../../../../../inertia.md).

A low steady [Reynolds number](../../../../../reynolds-number.md) alone does not justify dropping rapid unsteady acceleration. For forcing frequency $\omega$, the additional dimensionless ratio is $\rho\omega L^2/\eta$. No organism-specific sizes, velocities or propulsion mechanism are assumed here.
