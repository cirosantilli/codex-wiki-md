<h1 id="section-b/6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

At the [thermodynamic critical point](../../../../../thermodynamic-critical-point.md), the two spinodal branches merge: $f''=0$ and $f^{(3)}=0$. The third [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) is

$$
\frac{f^{(3)}}{k_BT}=-\frac1{N\phi^2}+\frac1{(1-\phi)^2}.
$$

For $0<\phi<1$, its zero gives $1-\phi_c=\sqrt N\,\phi_c$. Substitution in the spinodal relation yields

$$
\boxed{\phi_c=\frac1{1+\sqrt N}\sim N^{-1/2},\qquad
\chi_c=\frac12\left(1+\frac1{\sqrt N}\right)^2
=\frac12+N^{-1/2}+\frac1{2N}.}
$$

An absolute [critical temperature](../../../../../critical-temperature.md) requires an interaction–[temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) relation. Under the usual energetic approximation $\chi=C/T$, define the [theta temperature](../../../../../theta-temperature.md) $\Theta=2C$, at which an infinite chain has $\chi=1/2$. Then

$$
\boxed{T_c=\frac{\Theta}{(1+N^{-1/2})^2}
=\Theta\left[1-2N^{-1/2}+3N^{-1}+O(N^{-3/2})\right].}
$$

Thus $T_c$ tends to a nonzero constant, $T_c\sim N^0$, while $\Theta-T_c\sim N^{-1/2}$. It is the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) deficit, not $T_c$ itself, that scales as $N^{-1/2}$.

More generally, if a smooth $\chi(T)$ obeys $\chi(\Theta)=1/2$ and $\chi'(\Theta)\ne0$, then

$$
T_c-\Theta=\frac{N^{-1/2}}{\chi'(\Theta)}+O(N^{-1}).
$$

This is the [critical point of a Flory–Huggins polymer solution](../../../../../critical-point-of-a-flory-huggins-polymer-solution.md). The same leading chain-length exponent follows for a smooth [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) law, while its coefficient depends on the omitted [solvent](../../../../../solvent.md) thermodynamics. The lattice interpretation connects the chain-length dependence of mixing [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) to the theta condition $\chi=1/2$.[MIT Polymer Physics, lecture 3: mean-field lattice theory](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-063-polymer-physics-spring-2007/resources/lec3_07/).
