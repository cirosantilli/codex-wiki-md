<h1 id="section-b/6/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Interpret the stated transition as an upper critical miscibility [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md): cooling increases the effective $\chi$ through $\chi_c$. Above $T_c$, the homogeneous mixing [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) is convex throughout $0<\phi<1$, with no two-phase common tangent. Below $T_c$, it acquires a region with $f''<0$, bordered by the [spinodal](../../../../../spinodal.md). A lower straight line touches the curve at two binodal compositions $\phi_-$ and $\phi_+$; this [common-tangent construction](../../../../../common-tangent-construction.md) gives the equilibrium polymer-poor and polymer-rich phases.

The precise raw curve need not have two equal minima: subtracting an [affine function](../../../../../affine-function.md) of $\phi$ changes reference chemical potentials without changing [curvature](../../../../../curvature.md) or coexistence compositions. The free-energy panels above subtract the tangent at $\phi_c$ from the upper-temperature curve and the common tangent from the lower-temperature curve. This makes the common tangent and the convex-versus-nonconvex distinction visible.

If the mean initial composition $\phi_0$ lies inside the low-temperature spinodal region, an arbitrarily small separation into equal-volume regions with compositions $\phi_0\pm\delta\phi$ changes the mean free [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) by

$$
\frac{f(\phi_0+\delta\phi)+f(\phi_0-\delta\phi)}2-f(\phi_0)
=\frac12f''(\phi_0)(\delta\phi)^2+O((\delta\phi)^4)<0.
$$

Small fluctuations therefore grow spontaneously: this is [spinodal decomposition](../../../../../spinodal-decomposition.md). Interfacial costs limit very short-wavelength fluctuations, and the polymer-poor and polymer-rich domains subsequently coarsen towards their binodal compositions.

If $\phi_0$ lies just outside a spinodal branch but still inside the binodal, then $f''(\phi_0)>0$. Small fluctuations increase [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md), so the uniform mixture is [metastable](../../../../../metastability.md). Separation requires a sufficiently large nucleus to overcome the interfacial barrier, followed by [nucleation](../../../../../nucleation.md) and growth. There is no guarantee that this happens rapidly. If the initial composition is outside the binodal as well, it remains a stable uniform phase after cooling.

For a mean composition in the coexistence interval, conservation of [polymer](../../../../../polymer-split.md) fixes the volume fraction $\alpha$ of the rich phase by the [lever rule](../../../../../lever-rule.md):

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{\phi_0-\phi_-}{\phi_+-\phi_-},\qquad
1-\alpha=\frac{\phi_+-\phi_0}{\phi_+-\phi_-}.}
$$

The temperatures alone do not determine which mechanism occurs without the initial composition. Numerical free-energy curves additionally require $\chi(T)$; the plotted example uses $\chi(T)=\chi_cT_c/T$ with absolute temperatures $T_c=298.15\,\mathrm K$, $T_1=303.15\,\mathrm K$ and $T_2=293.15\,\mathrm K$.
