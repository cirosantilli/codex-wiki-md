<h1 id="section-b/4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Parallel elements have the same [strain](../../../../../strain.md), while their [stresses](../../../../../stress.md) add. The spring supplies $G_0\epsilon$ and the viscous element supplies $\eta_0\dot\epsilon$, so the [Kelvin-Voigt model](../../../../../kelvin-voigt-model.md) has [constitutive equation](../../../../../constitutive-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{\sigma(t)=G_0\epsilon(t)+\eta_0\dot\epsilon(t).}
$$

For the oscillatory deformation this gives

$$
\sigma(t)=(G_0+i\omega\eta_0)\epsilon_0e^{i\omega t},\qquad
\boxed{G^*(\omega)=G_0+i\omega\eta_0.}
$$

Physical [stress](../../../../../stress.md) and [strain](../../../../../strain.md) are the real parts of these expressions. Thus the [storage modulus](../../../../../storage-modulus.md) is $G_0$, independent of frequency, and the [loss modulus](../../../../../loss-modulus.md) is $\omega\eta_0$. The equation is the quasistatic material law; an inertial equation for an attached mass would require additional mechanical data.
