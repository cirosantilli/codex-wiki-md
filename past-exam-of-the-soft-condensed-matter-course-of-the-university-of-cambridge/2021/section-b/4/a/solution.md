<h1 id="section-b/4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use a [rheometer](../../../../../rheometer.md), for example a [cone-and-plate rheometer](../../../../../cone-and-plate-rheometer.md) or a [parallel-plate rheometer](../../../../../parallel-plate-rheometer.md), to impose a small deformation and measure the required torque, or impose a torque and measure the deformation. The apparatus geometry converts these into [shear stress](../../../../../shear-stress.md) and [strain](../../../../../strain.md). First check that reducing the imposed amplitude does not change the normalized response; this identifies the linear regime and avoids yielding or structural rearrangement.

Three complementary protocols characterize the material's memory. In a [stress relaxation](../../../../../stress-relaxation.md) experiment impose a small step $\epsilon_0H(t)$ and hold the deformation fixed. The output is the [relaxation modulus](../../../../../relaxation-modulus.md) $G(t)=\sigma(t)/\epsilon_0$, revealing how stored [stress](../../../../../stress.md) decays and whether an elastic plateau remains. In a [creep compliance](../../../../../creep-compliance.md) measurement impose a small [stress](../../../../../stress.md) step $\sigma_0H(t)$ and record $J(t)=\epsilon(t)/\sigma_0$. Removing the load also distinguishes recoverable deformation from permanent flow.

In [small-amplitude oscillatory shear](../../../../../small-amplitude-oscillatory-shear.md), impose sinusoidal [strain](../../../../../strain.md) over a range of frequencies and measure both [stress](../../../../../stress.md) amplitude and phase. With $\epsilon=\epsilon_0\cos\omega t$, fit

$$
\sigma(t)=\epsilon_0[G'(\omega)\cos\omega t-G''(\omega)\sin\omega t].
$$

The outputs are the [storage modulus](../../../../../storage-modulus.md) $G'$ and [loss modulus](../../../../../loss-modulus.md) $G''$, or equivalently amplitude ratio and [loss tangent](../../../../../loss-tangent.md) $\tan\delta=G''/G'$. These measure elastic storage and dissipative response, respectively. The step experiments give time-domain information and the oscillatory experiment gives the corresponding frequency-domain response. All comparisons must use the same deformation convention and controlled [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md).
