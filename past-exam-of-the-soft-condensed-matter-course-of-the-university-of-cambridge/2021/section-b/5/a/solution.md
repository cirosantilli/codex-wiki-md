<h1 id="section-b/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [hydrophobic](../../../../../hydrophobicity.md) surface is poorly wetted by [water](../../../../../water.md): on a smooth surface its equilibrium [contact angle](../../../../../contact-angle.md), measured through the liquid, exceeds $90^\circ$. A drop therefore tends to bead up, limiting its contact area. Hydrophobicity does not mean that the surface exerts a mysterious repulsive force on every water [molecule](../../../../../molecule-split.md).

The waxy coating has a largely nonpolar exterior. [Water](../../../../../water.md) [molecules](../../../../../molecule-split.md) form favorable [hydrogen bonds](../../../../../hydrogen-bond.md) with one another, whereas a hydrocarbon-rich wax surface offers few suitable [hydrogen bond donors](../../../../../hydrogen-bond-donor.md) or [hydrogen bond acceptors](../../../../../hydrogen-bond-acceptor.md). Water–surface adhesion is weak relative to water's cohesion. The drop shape minimizes the combined solid–air, solid–water and water–air interfacial [free energies](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md), not merely the liquid's exposed area.

In terms of [Young's equation](../../../../../young-s-equation.md), $\gamma_{SV}-\gamma_{SL}=\gamma_{LV}\cos\theta$. A [contact angle](../../../../../contact-angle.md) greater than $90^\circ$ corresponds to $\gamma_{SL}>\gamma_{SV}$: replacing dry surface by wetted surface has an interfacial cost. The balancing liquid interface then favors a small wetted patch. Microscopic leaf texture can amplify the apparent [hydrophobicity](../../../../../hydrophobicity.md), especially when it traps air under the drop as a [Cassie–Baxter state](../../../../../cassie-baxter-state.md); the chemistry of a smooth wax layer alone does not guarantee an extremely large apparent angle.
