<h1 id="section-b/5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For an ideal smooth homogeneous substrate in [thermodynamic equilibrium](../../../../../thermodynamic-equilibrium.md), [Young's equation](../../../../../young-s-equation.md) is

$$
\boxed{\gamma_{SA}=\gamma_{SL}+\gamma_{LA}\cos\theta,}
$$

where $A$ denotes air, $S$ the solid, and $L$ the chosen liquid. It follows either by balancing tangential interfacial forces at the [contact line](../../../../../contact-line.md) or by varying the wetted area at fixed drop volume.

The dry Teflon–air [surface energy](../../../../../surface-energy.md) is the same in the two measurements, so subtracting the two equations eliminates it:

$$
\gamma_{SW}-\gamma_{SO}
=\gamma_{OA}\cos\theta_O-\gamma_{WA}\cos\theta_W.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\gamma_{SW}-\gamma_{SO}
=23.8\cos25^\circ-72.8\cos102^\circ
\simeq36.7\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}=36.7\,\mathrm{mJ\,m^{-2}}.}
$$

The water–Teflon interface has the higher interfacial [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) per unit area. Neither individual solid–liquid [surface energy](../../../../../surface-energy.md) is determined without the solid–air value; the difference is determined.
