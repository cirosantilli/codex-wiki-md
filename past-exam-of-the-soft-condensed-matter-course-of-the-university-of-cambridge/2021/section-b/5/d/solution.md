<h1 id="section-b/5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[Colloidal](../../../../../colloid.md) particles experience attractive [Van der Waals forces](../../../../../van-der-waals-force.md), arising from correlated molecular charge fluctuations and other dipolar interactions. Integrating many weak molecular attractions over two particles can give a deep near-contact well. For identical spheres of radius $a$ and surface gap $h\ll a$, a positive [Hamaker constant](../../../../../hamaker-constant.md) gives approximately

$$
U_{\mathrm{vdW}}(h)\simeq-\frac{A_Ha}{12h}.
$$

[Brownian motion](../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md) brings particles into encounters. Without a repulsive barrier, they can reach the attractive primary minimum; if the binding [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is much larger than $k_BT$, thermal motion rarely separates them again. Repeated sticking produces aggregates. This is a kinetic stability statement: thermal agitation causes encounters but does not itself supply a stabilizing interparticle repulsion. The molecular cutoff and [hard sphere](../../../../../hard-sphere.md) exclusion prevent the unphysical $h\to0$ divergence of the continuum attraction.
