<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use a [freely rotating chain](../../../../../freely-rotating-chain.md): fixed bond length $b$, fixed angle $\vartheta$ between forward bond vectors, and independently random torsion angles. Then $\langle\hat t_0\cdot\hat t_n\rangle=(\cos\vartheta)^n=e^{-nb/\ell_p}$, giving the [orientational persistence length of a freely rotating chain](../../../../../orientational-persistence-length-of-a-freely-rotating-chain.md)

$$
\boxed{\ell_p=-\frac b{\ln\cos\vartheta}\simeq3.48\,\mathrm{nm}.}
$$

The equivalent long-chain [Kuhn length](../../../../../kuhn-length.md) divided by two, $b(1+\cos\vartheta)/[2(1-\cos\vartheta)]\simeq3.48\,\mathrm{nm}$, is nearly equal here but is a distinct discrete-chain convention. A chemical internal bond angle is supplementary to the forward-vector angle used above.

Synthetic examples are relatively stiff [poly(p-phenylene terephthalamide)](../../../../../poly-p-phenylene-terephthalamide.md) and flexible [polydimethylsiloxane](../../../../../polydimethylsiloxane.md). Light scattering and viscometry established rodlike solution conformations for the former; single-chain mechanical measurements support the latter's flexibility. Persistence is solvent- and model-dependent, so no universal numerical value is inferred for either example.[publisher abstract, dilute-solution light scattering and viscometry](../../../../../properties-of-some-rodlike-polymers-in-solution-by-c-p-wong-and-others.md)[abstract, single-chain atomic-force measurements and flexibility](../../../../../understanding-the-extraordinary-flexibility-of-polydimethylsiloxane-through-single-molecule-mechanics-by-song-lu-and-others.md)
