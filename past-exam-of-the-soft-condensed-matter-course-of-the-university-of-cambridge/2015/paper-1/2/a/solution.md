<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Stokes–Einstein relation](../../../../../stokes-einstein-relation.md) connects thermal [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) with dissipative [mobility](../../../../../order-parameter-mobility.md). For a dilute suspension of isolated rigid [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) of [hydrodynamic radius](../../../../../hydrodynamic-radius.md) $a$ in an unbounded [Newtonian fluid](../../../../../newtonian-fluid.md) of [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) $\eta$,

$$
\boxed{D=\frac{k_BT}{\zeta}=\frac{k_BT}{6\pi\eta a},}
$$

where $\zeta$ is translational [drag coefficient](../../../../../drag-coefficient.md), $D$ [diffusion coefficient](../../../../../diffusion-coefficient.md) and $T$ absolute [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). [Stokes drag](../../../../../stokes-s-law.md) supplies $\zeta=6\pi\eta a$ under no-slip, continuum, low-Reynolds-number conditions. The [Einstein diffusion relation](../../../../../einstein-diffusion-relation.md) $D=k_BT/\zeta$ is more general than that particular spherical [drag](../../../../../drag-physics.md) law.

A short [equilibrium](../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) argument explains the connection. Let a weak external potential $U$ exert [force](../../../../../force-split.md) $\mathbf F=-\nabla U$, so drift [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) is $\mathbf F/\zeta$. The number [flux](../../../../../flux.md) is $\mathbf j=n\mathbf F/\zeta-D\nabla n$. A [Boltzmann distribution](../../../../../boltzmann-distribution.md) $n\propto e^{-U/(k_BT)}$ has $\nabla n=n\mathbf F/(k_BT)$; requiring zero [equilibrium](../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) [flux](../../../../../flux.md) fixes $D=k_BT/\zeta$. [thermal fluctuations](../../../../../thermal-fluctuation.md) and viscous [dissipation](../../../../../dissipation.md) are therefore linked, rather than independent adjustable effects. The original theory also relates [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) to measured random displacements.[Sections 3–5, printed pages 9–18 in the linked translation: equilibrium flux balance, spherical drag and displacement variance](../../../../../on-the-movement-of-small-particles-suspended-in-a-stationary-liquid-demanded-by-the-molecular-kinetic-theory-of-heat-by-albert-einstein.md)

On times long compared with momentum relaxation, an isotropic diffusing [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) has

$$
\langle[\Delta x(t)]^2\rangle=2Dt,\qquad
\langle|\Delta\mathbf r(t)|^2\rangle=6Dt.
$$

[single-particle tracking](../../../../../single-particle-tracking.md) can measure the slope. Alternatively, dilute single-scattering [dynamic light scattering](../../../../../dynamic-light-scattering.md) measures a field correlation $g_1(q,t)=e^{-Dq^2t}$; the corresponding intensity-correlation excess is proportional to $e^{-2Dq^2t}$. The scattering [wavenumber](../../../../../wavenumber.md) and the distinction between field and intensity decay must be retained.

A [Stokes-Einstein experimental consistency test](../../../../../stokes-einstein-experimental-consistency-test.md) independently measures [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and [solvent](../../../../../solvent.md) [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md), uses a narrow distribution of inert [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md), checks decay rate proportional to $q^2$ or displacement variance proportional to time, and extrapolates toward infinite dilution. Subtract drift, account for wall-enhanced [drag](../../../../../drag-physics.md), and verify that aggregation is absent. Tracking additionally needs spatial calibration and correction for finite exposure and [localization precision](../../../../../localization-precision.md). At finite [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md), collective [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) is not automatically the single-particle [self-diffusion](../../../../../self-diffusion.md) in the equation. A [hydrodynamic radius](../../../../../hydrodynamic-radius.md) is not generally identical to a [polymer](../../../../../polymer-split.md) [radius of gyration](../../../../../radius-of-gyration.md); nonspherical particles, slip or viscoelastic [solvents](../../../../../solvent.md) require a different [mobility](../../../../../order-parameter-mobility.md) model.
