The independent fluid-mechanical relation is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Re}=\frac{\rho UL}{\eta}=\frac{UL}{\nu},}
$$

where $\rho$ is [fluid](../../../../../fluid.md) [mass density](../../../../../density.md), $\eta$ [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) and $\nu$ [kinematic viscosity](../../../../../kinematic-viscosity.md). For a generic rigid [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md), the convention must specify whether $L$ is its diameter or radius; their [Reynolds numbers](../../../../../reynolds-number.md) differ by a factor of two. The ratio compares advective [inertia](../../../../../inertia.md) $\rho U^2/L$ with viscous [stress](../../../../../stress.md) gradients $\eta U/L^2$. Small [Reynolds number](../../../../../reynolds-number.md) supports a creeping-flow approximation when unsteady [fluid](../../../../../fluid.md) [inertia](../../../../../inertia.md) is also negligible. No organism-specific size, speed or propulsion calculation is made here.
