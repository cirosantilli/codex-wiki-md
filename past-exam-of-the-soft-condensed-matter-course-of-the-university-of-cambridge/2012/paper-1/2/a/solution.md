A [synthetic fluid membrane](../../../../../synthetic-fluid-membrane.md) is a thin, laterally fluid sheet whose components can rearrange in its plane while the sheet resists changes of [area](../../../../../surface-area.md) and [curvature](../../../../../curvature.md). Synthetic [amphiphiles](../../../../../amphiphile.md) can assemble into two opposed layers with solvent-compatible groups facing outward. Their fluid in-plane response gives no equilibrium static [shear modulus](../../../../../shear-modulus.md), but does not imply zero [membrane bending rigidity](../../../../../membrane-bending-rigidity.md) or zero area-compression modulus. A thin membrane can therefore bend easily while remaining difficult to stretch. These notes cover generic synthetic interfaces.

A convenient continuum description is the [Helfrich energy](../../../../../helfrich-energy.md), augmented by [spontaneous curvature](../../../../../spontaneous-curvature.md) and the Gaussian term:

$$
F=\int\left[\frac\kappa2(2H-C_0)^2+\bar\kappa K+\sigma\right]dA.
$$

Here $H$ and $K$ are [mean curvature](../../../../../mean-curvature.md) and [Gaussian curvature](../../../../../gaussian-curvature.md), $C_0$ is preferred [curvature](../../../../../curvature.md), $\kappa$ and $\bar\kappa$ have [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) units, and $\sigma$ is [membrane tension](../../../../../membrane-tension.md) with units $\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$. Leaflet asymmetry can favor nonzero $C_0$. At fixed topology the integrated Gaussian term is constant for a closed surface by the [Gauss-Bonnet theorem](../../../../../gauss-bonnet-theorem.md). A symmetric tensionless spherical sheet has mean-curvature bending [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $8\pi\kappa$, independent of its [radius](../../../../../radius.md) in this ideal model. Edge [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) can favor closure, while fixed [area](../../../../../surface-area.md) and enclosed [volume](../../../../../volume.md) constrain the resulting shapes.

For a nearly flat sheet, its [small-slope elastic membrane energy](../../../../../small-slope-elastic-membrane-energy.md) becomes

$$
F=\frac12\int[\kappa(\nabla^2h)^2+\sigma|\nabla h|^2]d^2x.
$$

With $h_{\mathbf q}=A^{-1}\int h(\mathbf x)e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x}d^2x$, the [equipartition theorem](../../../../../equipartition-theorem.md) gives the [membrane fluctuation spectrum under tension](../../../../../membrane-fluctuation-spectrum-under-tension.md):

$$
\boxed{\langle|h_{\mathbf q}|^2\rangle=\frac{k_BT}{A(\kappa q^4+\sigma q^2)}.}
$$

Bending suppresses short wavelengths, whereas positive tension suppresses the longest undulations. Their crossover is $q\sim\sqrt{\sigma/\kappa}$. Measuring the spectrum can separate these two contributions, provided finite image resolution, projected versus true [area](../../../../../surface-area.md), and external confinement are accounted for.
