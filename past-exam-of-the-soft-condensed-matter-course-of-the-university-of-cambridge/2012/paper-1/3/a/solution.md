<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The three solvent conditions describe effective interactions between segments of a generic [polymer](../../../../../polymer-split.md). In a [good solvent](../../../../../good-solvent.md), solvent contacts favor a swollen, self-avoiding coil with positive [excluded volume](../../../../../excluded-volume.md). At a [theta solvent](../../../../../theta-solvent.md) condition, leading pair repulsion and attraction cancel; the chain has approximately ideal random-walk statistics. In a [poor solvent](../../../../../poor-solvent.md), effective attraction contracts the chain, eventually producing a dense [polymer globule](../../../../../polymer-globule.md) for a sufficiently long chain.

For $N$ statistical segments of [length](../../../../../length.md) $a$, the [radius of gyration](../../../../../radius-of-gyration.md) has the scaling

$$
\boxed{R_g\sim\begin{cases}
aN^{3/5},&\text{good solvent},\\
aN^{1/2},&\text{theta/ideal solvent},\\
aN^{1/3},&\text{dense poor-solvent globule}.
\end{cases}}
$$

The good-solvent power $3/5$ is the [Flory approximation](../../../../../flory-approximation.md); the asymptotic three-dimensional exponent is closer to $0.588$. In the ideal [freely jointed chain](../../../../../ideal-chain.md), the more precise result is $R_g^2\simeq Na^2/6$, whereas the end-to-end [mean](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) square is $Na^2$. Scaling notation suppresses those geometry-dependent factors. The globule power follows from a [volume](../../../../../volume.md) proportional to $N$, and presumes a dense collapsed state away from the theta crossover. The dilute condition excludes overlap with other chains; it does not eliminate contacts between distant segments of the same chain.
