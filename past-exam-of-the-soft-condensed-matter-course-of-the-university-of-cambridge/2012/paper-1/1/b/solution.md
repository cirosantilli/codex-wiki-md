<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the three-dimensional [worm-like chain](../../../../../worm-like-chain.md), define the bending coefficient $\kappa_b$ by $F_b=(\kappa_b/2)\int|\partial_s\mathbf t|^2ds$. Its [persistence length](../../../../../persistence-length.md) is $l_p=\kappa_b/(k_BT)$. Holding that coefficient and the [polymer contour length](../../../../../polymer-contour-length.md) $L$ fixed gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{l_p(T_2)}{l_p(T_1)}=\frac{T_1}{T_2},\qquad
\frac{\Delta l_p}{l_p(T_1)}=\frac{T_1}{T_2}-1=-\frac{T_2-T_1}{T_2}.}
$$

The [temperatures](../../../../../temperature-split.md) must be absolute. The [end-to-end distance of a worm-like chain](../../../../../end-to-end-distance-of-a-worm-like-chain.md) obeys

$$
\langle R^2\rangle=2Ll_p-2l_p^2(1-e^{-L/l_p}).
$$

For a long flexible chain, $L\gg l_p$, the typical size is $R_{\rm rms}\simeq\sqrt{2Ll_p}$, so **warming contracts the chain**, with $R_{\rm rms}(T_2)/R_{\rm rms}(T_1)\simeq\sqrt{T_1/T_2}$. The finite-length formula also decreases as $l_p$ decreases, but the rod limit $L\ll l_p$ changes very little. A large segment count alone does not guarantee $L\gg l_p$. If the material's bending coefficient itself depends on [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), replace the first ratio by $[\kappa_b(T_2)/\kappa_b(T_1)]T_1/T_2$; the sign then needs that additional material information. This calculation concerns a generic physical filament, with no biomolecular application.
