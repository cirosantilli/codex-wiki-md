<h1 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The relevant comparison is between [gravity](../../../../gravity.md) and [surface tension](../../../../surface-tension-split.md). With the [mass density](../../../../density.md) difference $\Delta\rho\simeq10^3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, the [Bond number](../../../../bond-number.md) and [capillary length](../../../../capillary-length.md) are

$$
\mathrm{Bo}=\frac{\Delta\rho\,g r^2}{\gamma}
=\frac{10^3(9.81)(5\times10^{-4})^2}{0.072}\simeq0.034,
\qquad
\ell_c=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{\Delta\rho\,g}}\simeq2.71\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$

Thus the drop is small compared with the [capillary length](../../../../capillary-length.md); [hydrostatic pressure](../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) variations only weakly perturb its shape. The [Young–Laplace equation](../../../../young-laplace-equation.md) then gives approximately constant interfacial [mean curvature](../../../../mean-curvature.md), and an axisymmetric drop on a uniform flat substrate is a [spherical cap](../../../../spherical-cap.md). The substrate selects the [contact angle](../../../../contact-angle.md), measured through the [water](../../../../water.md).

Write $R$ for the radius of the generating [sphere](../../../../sphere.md), $s$ for the contact-circle radius, and $h$ for the apex height. Elementary [trigonometric identities](../../../../trigonometric-identity.md) give

$$
s=R\sin\theta,\qquad h=R(1-\cos\theta)=s\tan(\theta/2).
$$

Taking the stated droplet radius to mean its radius of [curvature](../../../../curvature.md), $R=r$, gives

$$
\boxed{h=0.5(1-\cos55^\circ)\,\mathrm{mm}=0.213\,\mathrm{mm}.}
$$

There is no diagram or explicit radius convention in the original PDF. If $r$ instead denotes the contact-circle radius, the answer is $h=r\tan27.5^\circ=0.260\,\mathrm{mm}$. If it denotes the radius of an equal-volume free [sphere](../../../../sphere.md), use the [volume of a spherical cap](../../../../volume-of-a-spherical-cap.md), $\pi h^2(3R-h)/3=4\pi r^3/3$, to obtain $h=r[4(1-\cos\theta)/(2+\cos\theta)]^{1/3}=0.436\,\mathrm{mm}$. The [spherical cap](../../../../spherical-cap.md) approximation is justified in all three conventions; a unique numerical height requires specifying which radius is meant.
