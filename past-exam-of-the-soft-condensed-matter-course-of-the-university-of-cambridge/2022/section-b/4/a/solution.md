<h1 id="section-b/4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For an [ideal chain](../../../../../ideal-chain.md), independent [Kuhn length](../../../../../kuhn-length.md) steps give the [random walk](../../../../../random-walk-split.md) relation

$$
\langle R_{\mathrm{sub}}^2\rangle=g b^2,\qquad \boxed{\sigma\sim b\sqrt g,\quad g\sim(\sigma/b)^2,}
$$

where $R_{\mathrm{sub}}$ denotes the end-to-end displacement of a subchain. Equivalently the mass-size [fractal](../../../../../fractal.md) exponent is $2$. Numerical factors depend on the chosen definition of blob diameter.

A subchain smaller than the adsorption height explores three dimensions much as a free [ideal chain](../../../../../ideal-chain.md) does. At scales exceeding $\xi$, [adsorption](../../../../../adsorption.md) restricts its perpendicular extent to order $\xi$, so the coil is anisotropic. Its in-plane projection may still have a [random walk](../../../../../random-walk-split.md) law for an [ideal chain](../../../../../ideal-chain.md), but the isotropic three-dimensional blob construction cannot be extrapolated to $\sigma>\xi$. This is the reason to use [adsorption blobs](../../../../../adsorption-blob.md) of size $\xi$.
