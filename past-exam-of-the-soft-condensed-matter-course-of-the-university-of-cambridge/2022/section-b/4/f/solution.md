<h1 id="section-b/4/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

For the usual interpretation of a real chain in a [good solvent](../../../../../good-solvent.md), the detachment [force](../../../../../force-split.md) is **smaller** in the weak-[adsorption](../../../../../adsorption.md) regime. [Excluded volume](../../../../../excluded-volume.md) swells the chain, increasing the cost of confinement near the substrate. The adsorbed layer becomes thicker and its binding [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) per [monomer](../../../../../monomer.md) decreases. The same contact [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) therefore does not mean the same total binding strength.

The scaling argument can include both adsorption and stretching. Let $\xi\sim bg^\nu$, where $\nu=1/2$ for an [ideal chain](../../../../../ideal-chain.md) and approximately $3/5$ for a [self-avoiding walk](../../../../../self-avoiding-walk.md) in three dimensions. With the same contact-fraction approximation,

$$
\frac{F_{\mathrm{ads}}}{Nk_BT}\sim
\left(\frac b\xi\right)^{1/\nu}-\epsilon\frac b\xi.
$$

Minimization gives $\xi/b\sim\epsilon^{-\nu/(1-\nu)}$ and $\mu/(k_BT)\sim\epsilon^{1/(1-\nu)}$. At detachment, a [tensile blob](../../../../../tensile-blob.md) of size $\xi_t\sim k_BT/f$ costs approximately $k_BT$; its [monomer](../../../../../monomer.md) count is $g_t\sim(\xi_t/b)^{1/\nu}$. Equating the stretching gain per [monomer](../../../../../monomer.md) $k_BT/g_t$ to $\mu$ gives

$$
f\sim\frac{k_BT}{b}\left(\frac{\mu}{k_BT}\right)^\nu
\sim\frac{k_BT}{b}\epsilon^{\nu/(1-\nu)}.
$$

Thus $f_{\mathrm{real}}\sim(k_BT/b)\epsilon^{3/2}$, compared with $f_{\mathrm{ideal}}\sim(k_BT/b)\epsilon$, so their ratio scales as $\epsilon^{1/2}<1$ for weak attraction. These are scaling relations, not exact prefactors. The real-chain adsorption exponents here use the same simple uniform-contact model as the earlier parts; a more detailed surface theory of [critical phenomena](../../../../../critical-phenomenon-split.md) can refine them. The thicker, more weakly bound layer reflects the higher cost of confining a [good solvent](../../../../../good-solvent.md) chain.[Rubinstein and Colby, Polymer Physics, chapter 3, equations (3.59)–(3.66)](https://dasher.wustl.edu/bio5357/readings/rubinstein-chapter3.pdf)

“Real” alone does not specify [solvent](../../../../../solvent.md) quality: a collapsed chain in a poor [solvent](../../../../../solvent.md), strong adsorption, or substantial bending stiffness requires a different comparison. The smaller-force conclusion concerns the flexible [good solvent](../../../../../good-solvent.md) chain implicit in the excluded-volume correction.
