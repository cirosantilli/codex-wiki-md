<h1 id="section-b/5/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For a small opposing [force](../../../../../force-split.md), the relevant parameter is $q=\beta F\delta\ll1$. A [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) gives

$$
e^q-1-q=\frac{q^2}{2}\left(1+\frac q3+\frac{q^2}{12}+\cdots\right),
$$

so

$$
\boxed{j_0=\frac{2D}{\delta^2}\left(1-\frac q3+\frac{q^2}{36}+O(q^3)\right)
\longrightarrow\frac{2D}{\delta^2}.}
$$

In particular the zero-force rate is finite, despite the numerator and denominator of the original expression both vanishing.

For $q\gg1$, the exponential dominates the denominator:

$$
\boxed{j_0\sim D\beta^2F^2e^{-\beta F\delta}
=\frac{D}{\delta^2}q^2e^{-q}.}
$$

Opening a monomer-sized gap is exponentially rare at high opposing [force](../../../../../force-split.md). The algebraic $F^2$ prefactor belongs to this first-passage model and must not be discarded when quoting the leading asymptotic rate.
