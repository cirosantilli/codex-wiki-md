<h1 id="section-b/6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Write the particle [volume fraction](../../../../../volume-fraction.md) as $\eta=(4\pi a^3/3)n_p$. In a stable, repulsion-dominated [colloid](../../../../../colloid.md), low $\eta$ gives a disordered fluid. Increasing $\eta$ makes the interaction between neighboring particles important and can produce an ordered [crystal](../../../../../crystal.md), separated from the fluid by a [phase coexistence curve](../../../../../phase-coexistence-curve.md). Increasing the [Debye–Hückel screening length](../../../../../debye-huckel-screening-length.md) at fixed charge strengthens the repulsion at a given separation and allows freezing at smaller geometrical [volume fraction](../../../../../volume-fraction.md).

The geometric intuition is that particles behave as though they occupy more than their bare diameter: roughly, an effective exclusion diameter $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ extends out to the separation where $U_{\mathrm{el}}$ is of order $k_BT$. A [hard sphere](../../../../../hard-sphere.md) comparison then uses $\eta_{\mathrm{eff}}\sim\eta[d_{\mathrm{eff}}/(2a)]^3$. This explains the trend; it is not an exact freezing criterion.

At short [Debye–Hückel screening length](../../../../../debye-huckel-screening-length.md) and negligible attraction, the diagram approaches [hard sphere](../../../../../hard-sphere.md) fluid–crystal coexistence. For longer-ranged soft repulsion, [body-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../body-centered-cubic-lattice.md) and [face-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../face-centered-cubic-lattice.md) crystals can both be stable, with the former often appearing towards the softer, lower-density freezing boundary. Screened-Coulomb simulations exhibit both these crystal structures.[Kremer, Robbins and Grest, Phase Diagram of Yukawa Systems: Model for Charge-Stabilized Colloids](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.57.2694)

**[Figure 3](#section-b/6/d/image-schematic-phase-diagram-for-a-stable-repulsive-colloid). Schematic phase diagram for a stable repulsive colloid**. Original qualitative fluid–crystal diagram at fixed radius, [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and charge. The two curves bound a fluid–crystal coexistence band. Increased screening length allows freezing at smaller bare particle volume fraction. Crystal-structure boundaries and numerical coexistence positions are deliberately not assigned. The dashed arrow shows adding salt at fixed volume fraction; the second panel shows the direction of the boundary shift when surface charge increases.

No universal quantitative diagram follows from $(\eta,\lambda_D)$ alone: the charge, [Hamaker constant](../../../../../hamaker-constant.md) and surface conditions must also be fixed. If salt suppresses the stabilizing barrier enough for [Van der Waals force](../../../../../van-der-waals-force.md) attraction to dominate, a colloid-poor/colloid-rich separation or aggregation can replace part of this repulsive reference diagram. Dense [colloidal glasses](../../../../../colloidal-glass.md) or [colloidal gels](../../../../../colloidal-gel.md) may also be observed after kinetic arrest; they should not be mistaken for additional [thermodynamic equilibrium](../../../../../thermodynamic-equilibrium.md) [thermodynamic phases](../../../../../thermodynamic-phase.md).
