<h1 id="section-b/6/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

For dissolved, _nonadsorbing_ [polyethylene glycol](../../../../../polyethylene-glycol.md), the usual new interaction is a [depletion force](../../../../../depletion-force.md). Polymer centres are excluded from a layer of thickness of order their [radius of gyration](../../../../../radius-of-gyration.md) $R_g$ around each particle. When the particles approach, their exclusion layers overlap. This increases the volume accessible to polymer and its translational [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md), lowering the colloid pair [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md). Equivalently the surrounding polymer [osmotic pressure](../../../../../osmotic-pressure.md) pushes the particles together.

In the dilute-polymer [Asakura-Oosawa model](../../../../../asakura-oosawa-model.md), let $h=R-2a$ be the surface gap and $R_e=a+R_g$. Then

$$
\boxed{U_{\mathrm{dep}}(R)=-\Pi_p V_{\mathrm{ov}}(R),\qquad
V_{\mathrm{ov}}(R)=\frac\pi{12}(4R_e+R)(2R_e-R)^2}
$$

for $2a\leq R<2R_e$, and $U_{\mathrm{dep}}=0$ for $R\geq2R_e$. Here $\Pi_p\simeq n_p^{(\mathrm{polymer})}k_BT$ is the reservoir polymer [osmotic pressure](../../../../../osmotic-pressure.md). The overlap-volume formula follows by adding two equal [spherical cap](../../../../../spherical-cap.md) volumes with height $R_e-R/2$. Thus the added attraction has range about $2R_g$ and grows with polymer concentration. The total interaction is approximately hard exclusion plus screened electrostatic repulsion plus [Van der Waals force](../../../../../van-der-waals-force.md) and depletion attractions.

Increasing polymer concentration therefore creates a family of [phase diagrams](../../../../../phase-diagram.md), rather than a unique shift determined by polymer size alone. Where the attraction overcomes repulsion, colloid-poor fluid can coexist with a colloid-rich phase, and fluid–crystal coexistence generally broadens. For sufficiently long-ranged attraction, stable fluid–fluid separation can occur. For the stipulated small polymer, the attraction is short-ranged: fluid–fluid separation can be metastable to crystallization, and a rapid attraction increase can instead produce an arrested [colloidal gel](../../../../../colloidal-gel.md). Residual long-ranged repulsion can oppose bulk separation. Simulations and free-volume calculations confirm that longer-ranged electrostatic repulsion can counteract depletion-driven separation.[Fortini, Dijkstra and Tuinier, Phase behaviour of charged colloidal sphere dispersions with added polymer chains](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0509790)

The nonadsorption assumption matters. If the [polyethylene glycol](../../../../../polyethylene-glycol.md) adsorbs to the particles, a dense attached layer can give [steric stabilization](../../../../../steric-stabilization.md), whereas chains attaching to two particles can produce [polymer bridging](../../../../../polymer-bridging.md). The paper does not specify surface affinity or polymer concentration, so depletion is the standard conditional answer, not a prediction of an inevitable particular final phase.
