# Pythagorean inequality for information projection

↑ **Parent:** [Information projection](information-projection.md)

Under the finite-[alphabet](alphabet.md), positive-reference, closed-[convex set](convex-set.md) assumptions above, every feasible law $r\in\mathcal C$ satisfies

$$
\boxed{D(r\Vert p)\geq D(r\Vert q^*)+D(q^*\Vert p).}
$$

Thus a feasible law's excess [relative entropy](kullback-leibler-divergence.md) above the optimum is at least its [relative entropy](kullback-leibler-divergence.md) from the optimizer. The [information projection](information-projection.md) supplies a quantitative comparison with every other feasible law, not merely the location of a minimum.[§12.2, Theorem 12.2, printed p. 133](6-441-information-theory-spring-2016-lecture-notes-by-yury-polyanskiy-and-others/chapter-12-information-projection-and-large-deviation.md)

For affine [expected value](expected-value.md) constraints, the result becomes a [Pythagorean identity for relative entropy](pythagorean-identity-for-relative-entropy.md). In [relative entropy minimization under a mean constraint](relative-entropy-minimization-under-a-mean-constraint.md), the [logarithm](logarithm.md) of $q_i^*/p_i$ is an affine function of the outcome $a_i$. Any feasible $r$ has the same normalization and [mean](expected-value.md) as $q^*$, making the residual in the [three-point identity for relative entropy](three-point-identity-for-relative-entropy.md) zero.[§3, Theorem 3.2 and equation (3.2), printed pp. 442–443](information-theory-and-statistics-a-tutorial-by-imre-csiszar-and-paul-c-shields.md)
