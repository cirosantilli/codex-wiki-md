# Infinite mean first passage of a simple symmetric random walk

↑ **Parent:** [Simple symmetric random walk](simple-symmetric-random-walk.md)

For a [simple symmetric random walk](simple-symmetric-random-walk.md) starting from zero, its first [hitting time](first-passage-time.md) of $1$ is almost surely finite but has infinite [expectation](expected-value.md). Stopping at exit from $[-m,1]$, the [optional stopping theorem](optional-sampling-theorem-for-a-supermartingale.md) for $S_n$ gives upper-exit [probability](probability.md) $m/(m+1)$, while stopping $S_n^2-n$ gives mean exit time $m$. The first [probabilities](probability.md) tend to one, and the exit times are bounded above by the first passage to $1$, proving both conclusions. Finite-time stopping and bounded stopped positions justify passage to the exit time.
