# Locally uniform convergence of holomorphic functions

↑ **Parent:** [Complex analysis](complex-analysis-split.md)

A [locally uniform convergence](locally-uniform-convergence.md) limit of [holomorphic functions](holomorphic-function-split.md) is holomorphic, and its [derivatives](derivative.md) converge locally uniformly as well. The limit is continuous. On each small [triangle](triangle.md) contained in the [domain](domain-mathematical-analysis.md), uniform convergence allows passage to the limit in its [contour integral](contour-integral.md); the integrals vanish by the [Cauchy integral theorem](cauchy-s-integral-theorem.md). The [Morera theorem](morera-s-theorem.md) therefore makes the limit holomorphic. Applying the [Cauchy integral formula](cauchy-integral-formula.md) on circles in a slightly larger compact subset proves locally uniform convergence of every fixed-order [derivative](derivative.md).
