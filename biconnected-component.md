# Biconnected component

↑ **Parent:** [Cut vertex](cut-vertex.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biconnected_component)

A [biconnected component](biconnected-component.md) is a maximal connected [subgraph](subgraph.md) with no [cut vertex](cut-vertex.md) of its own. We use the block convention that also includes each [bridge in a graph](bridge-graph-theory.md) with its two endpoint [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md), and each isolated [vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md), as a [biconnected component](biconnected-component.md). A [biconnected component](biconnected-component.md) with at least three [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md) stays connected after any single [vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) deletion. Distinct [biconnected components](biconnected-component.md) share at most one [vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md); the [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md) belonging to more than one [biconnected component](biconnected-component.md) are exactly the [cut vertices](cut-vertex.md). The shared-$A$ example has two [biconnected components](biconnected-component.md), its two [triangles in a graph](triangle-in-a-graph.md). The joined-$CD$ example has three: $ABC$, the [bridge in a graph](bridge-graph-theory.md) $CD$ with its endpoints, and $DEF$. The [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) partition into these pieces, while the shared [cut vertices](cut-vertex.md) occur in several pieces. [Sedgewick and Wayne, Algorithms, §4.1, biconnected components exercises](https://algs4.cs.princeton.edu/41graph/)
