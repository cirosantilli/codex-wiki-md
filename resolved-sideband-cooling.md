# Resolved sideband cooling

↑ **Parent:** [Trapped-ion quantum control](trapped-ion-quantum-control.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolved_sideband_cooling)

Resolved sideband cooling removes motional quanta by selectively driving a red [motional sideband](motional-sideband.md) and then resetting the internal [quantum state](quantum-state.md) through [spontaneous emission](spontaneous-emission.md) or [optical pumping](optical-pumping.md). Spectral resolution requires the effective cooling-transition width smaller than the motional [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md) in the same units. A narrow [Raman transition](raman-transition.md) can meet that requirement even when the optical transition used for [Doppler cooling](doppler-cooling.md) is broad.

The reset is essential: the coherent red [motional sideband](motional-sideband.md) merely exchanges internal and motional excitation reversibly. To see the distinction, an ideal pulse sends $|g,1\rangle$ to $-i|e,0\rangle$; the same interaction can send it back. An ideal dissipative reset instead takes $|e,0\rangle$ to $|g,0\rangle$, with information and [energy](energy-split.md) carried into the environment. Repeating this cycle accumulates population near the motional [ground state](ground-state.md). Recoil during the reset can add motion, and off-resonant excitation can disturb $|g,0\rangle$; neither is included in the ideal pulse below.
