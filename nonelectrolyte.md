# Nonelectrolyte

↑ **Parent:** [Electrolyte](electrolyte.md)

A [nonelectrolyte](nonelectrolyte.md) supplies neutral [molecules](molecule-split.md) rather than [ions](ion.md) on dissolving in the specified [solvent](solvent.md), as illustrated by [sucrose](sucrose.md) in [water](water.md).

For example, dissolving an idealized $1\,\mathrm{mmol}$ [sucrose](sucrose.md) inventory gives $1\,\mathrm{mmol}$ dissolved [sucrose](sucrose.md) [molecules](molecule-split.md), rather than separate [cations](cation.md) and [anions](anion.md). Dissolving the same [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) of a fully dissociated one-to-one [strong electrolyte](strong-electrolyte.md) instead gives $1\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of each [ion](ion.md). Both can disappear visibly into the [solvent](solvent.md); the resulting [chemical species](chemical-species.md) distinguish them.[Chemistry 2e, §11.2, opening definitions of electrolytes and nonelectrolytes](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/11-2-electrolytes.md)

A [nonelectrolyte](nonelectrolyte.md) is not necessarily insoluble: [sucrose](sucrose.md) provides a soluble counterexample. Nor does the label assert exactly zero measured [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md). [Self-ionization of water](self-ionization-of-water.md), impurities or residual [electrolyte](electrolyte.md) from a probe can supply background [ions](ion.md). Comparing with a [solvent](solvent.md) blank and rinsing between samples, as in the [strong electrolyte](strong-electrolyte.md) experiment, tests whether the dissolved substance itself produces appreciable additional mobile [ions](ion.md). This separates visible [dissolution](dissolution.md) from ion production without treating lamp darkness as an absolute absence of [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md).
