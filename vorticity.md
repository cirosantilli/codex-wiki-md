# Vorticity

↑ **Parent:** [Fluid mechanics](fluid-mechanics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorticity)

Use [vorticity](vorticity.md) to distinguish the local spin of a [fluid element](fluid-element.md) from the motion of its center along a [pathline](pathline.md). Circular [streamlines](streamline.md) can occur both with and without [vorticity](vorticity.md): the two [velocity fields](velocity-field.md) below even have the same [circulation](circulation-physics.md) around the unit [circle](circle.md).

<a id="image-solid-body-rotation-and-a-potential-line-vortex-equal-unit-circle-circulation-different-local-vorticity"></a>
![](vorticity-and-circulation-fields.png)

**[Figure 16](#image-solid-body-rotation-and-a-potential-line-vortex-equal-unit-circle-circulation-different-local-vorticity). Solid-body rotation and a potential line vortex: equal unit-circle circulation, different local vorticity**.

For a continuously differentiable [velocity field](velocity-field.md) $\mathbf u=(u_x,u_y,u_z)$, [vorticity](vorticity.md) is the [curl](curl.md) vector

$$
\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf u=\left(\frac{\partial u_z}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial u_y}{\partial z},\frac{\partial u_x}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial u_z}{\partial x},\frac{\partial u_y}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial u_x}{\partial y}\right).
$$

Each [partial derivative](partial-derivative.md) divides [velocity](velocity.md) by distance, so [vorticity](vorticity.md) has units $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. In a planar [velocity field](velocity-field.md) independent of $z$, only $\omega_z=\partial_xu_y-\partial_yu_x$ survives. Positive $\omega_z$ points along $+\mathbf e_z$: the [right-hand rule](right-hand-rule.md) associates it with counterclockwise spin viewed from positive $z$ toward the plane. [David Tong, Fluid Mechanics, section 2.2](https://davidtong.org/pdfs/teaching/fluid-mechanics/fluids1.pdf)

The local rotational [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) is half the [vorticity](vorticity.md), $\boldsymbol\Omega_{\rm spin}=\boldsymbol\omega/2$. To see the factor, split the [velocity gradient](velocity-gradient.md) $L_{ij}=\partial_ju_i$ into its symmetric [rate-of-strain tensor](strain-rate-tensor.md) $E=(L+L^T)/2$ and antisymmetric [spin tensor](spin-tensor.md) $W=(L-L^T)/2$. A nearby displacement $\delta\mathbf x$ has relative [velocity](velocity.md) $L\delta\mathbf x$; its rotational part satisfies $W\delta\mathbf x=(\boldsymbol\omega/2)\times\delta\mathbf x$. This is a proof outline of the local spin interpretation. The [rate-of-strain tensor](strain-rate-tensor.md) can also change the angle of a particular [material line](material-curve.md), so that line's [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) need not equal $\boldsymbol\omega/2$.

For [solid-body rotation](solid-body-rotation.md), $\mathbf u=(-\Omega y,\Omega x,0)$ gives $\boldsymbol\omega=(0,0,2\Omega)$ and $\boldsymbol\Omega_{\rm spin}=(0,0,\Omega)$. Taking $\Omega=1\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, the [vorticity](vorticity.md) is $2\mathbf e_z\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, although the [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) is only $1\mathbf e_z\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$. For the potential [line vortex](line-vortex.md), $\mathbf u=K\mathbf e_\theta/r$, the planar [curl](curl.md) is $r^{-1}\partial_r(ru_\theta)=r^{-1}\partial_rK=0$ wherever $r>0$. Its circular [pathlines](pathline.md) therefore coexist with zero local spin.

This distinction makes [vorticity](vorticity.md) useful for recognizing [shear flow](shear-flow.md) as well as swirling motion; taking the [curl](curl.md) of the [Euler equations](euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid.md) also removes a [pressure gradient](pressure-gradient.md) and leads to the [vorticity equation](vorticity-equation.md). A historical landmark is Hermann von Helmholtz's 1858 paper on [fluid vortex](vortex-fluid-mechanics.md) motion, an early study devoted to [vortex dynamics](vortex-dynamics.md). [H. Helmholtz, Über Integrale der hydrodynamischen Gleichungen, welche den Wirbelbewegungen entsprechen (1858)](https://zenodo.org/records/1448878)
