# Energy estimate

↑ **Parent:** [Partial differential equation](partial-differential-equation-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_estimate)

An energy estimate tests a differential equation against the solution or one of its derivatives to control a norm by initial data and forcing. Coercive terms represent dissipation, while [Young inequality](young-s-inequality-for-products.md) and the [Gronwall inequality](gronwall-inequality.md) control lower-order terms.

For the difference $w$ of two smooth [heat equation](heat-equation.md) solutions with $D>0$, identical [PDE source term](source-term-in-a-partial-differential-equation.md), [initial conditions](initial-condition.md), and either [periodic boundary conditions](periodic-boundary-conditions.md) or [Dirichlet boundary data](dirichlet-boundary-data.md), [integration by parts](integration-by-parts.md) gives

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\int w^2\,dx=-2D\int w_x^2\,dx\leq0.
$$

The initially zero nonnegative [integral](integral-split.md) stays zero, proving uniqueness. The same computation bounds changes in the [L2 norm](l2-norm.md) when the [initial conditions](initial-condition.md) differ. Specifying data and a solution class makes this argument meaningful.[Stanford Math 220B heat-equation notes, section 2.8.3](https://web.stanford.edu/class/math220b/handouts/heateqn.pdf)
