# Bohr model

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen atom](hydrogen-atom.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr_model)

The Bohr model assumes circular electron orbits with quantized [orbital angular momentum](orbital-angular-momentum.md) $N\hbar$. It reproduces the hydrogen energy levels, but [wavefunctions](wave-function-split.md) in quantum mechanics describe spatial probability distributions rather than definite trajectories. In particular, spherically symmetric [Coulomb bound states](coulomb-bound-state.md) have zero [orbital angular momentum](orbital-angular-momentum.md).

The puzzle is the coexistence of reproducible [spectral lines](spectral-line.md) and stable [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). A classical circular [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) in a [Coulomb potential](coulomb-potential-energy.md) can have any negative [energy](energy-split.md): [Coulomb's law](coulomb-s-law.md) alone selects no special radius or emitted [frequency](frequency.md). Moreover, its [electron](electron.md) has [centripetal acceleration](centripetal-acceleration.md). The [Larmor formula](larmor-formula.md) then predicts positive radiated power, so [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) makes the [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) shrink. Simply drawing concentric circles explains neither stability nor a discrete [spectrum](spectrum-functional-analysis.md).

[Niels Bohr](niels-bohr.md) postulated nonradiating [stationary states](stationary-state.md) and emission with $h\nu=E_i-E_f$. His circular-orbit condition is $L=n\hbar$. These assumptions override classical radiation; the emitted [frequency](frequency.md) need not equal an orbital [frequency](frequency.md).[Part I, pp. 7–8, 15: postulates](on-the-constitution-of-atoms-and-molecules-part-i-by-niels-bohr.md)
