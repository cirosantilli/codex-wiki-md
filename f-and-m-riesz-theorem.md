# F. and M. Riesz theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Hardy space](hardy-space.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._and_M._Riesz_theorem)

If a finite complex [Borel measure](borel-measure.md) $\mu$ on the circle has [Fourier coefficients](fourier-coefficient.md) $\widehat\mu(n)=0$ for all $n<0$, then it is absolutely continuous with respect to arc length. The reflected Fourier convention gives the equivalent theorem with positive coefficients vanishing. The [Poisson integral on the unit disk](poisson-integral-on-the-unit-disk.md) is analytic because of the one-sided Fourier spectrum. Subharmonic comparison for its square root and an $L^2$ maximal estimate produce an integrable radial maximum. This maximum dominates the weak-star boundary measure, forcing [absolute continuity of measures](absolute-continuity-of-measures.md). The same proof on the real line says that a finite measure with [Fourier transform of a finite measure](fourier-transform-of-a-finite-measure.md) supported in $[0,\infty)$ has a Lebesgue density.
