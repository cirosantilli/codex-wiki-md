<h1 id="hardy-s-paradox">Hardy's paradox</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Bell theorem](bell-theorem.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy's_paradox)

A two-setting nonlocality argument in which several zero-probability events forbid a further coincidence in any [deterministic local hidden-variable model](deterministic-local-hidden-variable-model.md), but quantum theory assigns that coincidence positive [probability](probability.md). For binary indicators, suppose $C_+^RC_-^R=0$, $D_+^I=1\Rightarrow C_-^R=1$ and $D_-^I=1\Rightarrow C_+^R=1$. Any assignment with $D_+^ID_-^I=1$ would violate the first relation. A quantum implementation can nevertheless give such a coincidence. Real imperfect experiments require an inequality that accounts for their nonzero error probabilities. [Lucien Hardy](lucien-hardy.md) gave a two-particle inequality-free argument in 1992.[1992 abstract](quantum-mechanics-local-realistic-theories-and-lorentz-invariant-realistic-theories-by-lucien-hardy.md)
