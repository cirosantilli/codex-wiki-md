<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

During [isovolumetric contraction](../../../../isovolumetric-contraction.md), ventricular [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md) lies between the venous and arterial pressures, $p_v<p_{LV}<p_a$. Both valves are closed, so $Q_v=Q_a=0$ and ventricular volume remains at its end-filling value $V^+$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{p_{LV}=E_{LV}(t)(V^+-V_0).}
$$

Here $V^+$ is end-diastolic ventricular volume and $V_0$ is the zero-[pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md)/unstressed volume.

The arterial and venous reservoirs still exchange systemic [blood](../../../../blood.md):

$$
\dot p_a=-\frac{p_a-p_v}{R_cC_a},\qquad\dot p_v=\frac{p_a-p_v}{R_cC_v}.
$$

If contraction is short compared with both $R_cC_a$ and $R_cC_v$, the fractional changes are small and

$$
\boxed{p_a\simeq p_a^+,\qquad p_v\simeq p_v^+,}
$$

where the superscript values refer to the beginning of contraction. Thus [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md) can rise rapidly in the [left ventricle](../../../../left-ventricle.md) through increasing elastance while the other pressures are approximately fixed. This stage ends when $p_{LV}$ reaches $p_a$ and the [aortic valve](../../../../aortic-valve.md) opens.
