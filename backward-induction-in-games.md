# Backward induction in games

↑ **Parent:** [Extensive-form game](extensive-form-game.md)

In a finite [perfect-information game](perfect-information-game.md) without chance moves, solve terminal decision points first. At each earlier decision point, its player chooses an action maximizing its own [payoff](payoff.md) among the already solved continuations. Record a choice in every subgame, including those not reached by the root choice. This constructs a [subgame perfect equilibrium](subgame-perfect-equilibrium.md). Tied choices may yield several equilibrium strategies and several outcomes. The name refers to [Ernst Zermelo](ernst-zermelo.md); the computational procedure is an application of [backward induction](backward-induction.md).
