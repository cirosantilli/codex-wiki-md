# Quadratic-bend turning with finite body mass

↑ **Parent:** [Recoil correction in elongated-body theory](recoil-correction-in-elongated-body-theory.md)

Put $B_j=[m(x)(x-\bar x)^j]_0^L$, $M_j=\int m(x)(x-\bar x)^jdx$, $A=\int\alpha(t)dt$, and $C=B_0(B_2-M_1)-B_1(B_1-M_0)$. For quadratic bending $h_0=-\alpha(t)(x-\bar x)^2$, the linear [recoil correction in elongated-body theory](recoil-correction-in-elongated-body-theory.md) with a stable post-stroke relaxation gives the displayed angle. It follows by setting time [derivatives](derivative.md) to zero in the integrated lateral and [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) equations. The simpler $2UA$ is exact in the zero-body-mass limit, or when $B_1=M_0$, but is not a generic finite-body-mass identity. For $L=1$, $\bar x=1/2$, and $m(x)=m_*x^2$, it becomes $2UA/(1+2M_b/m_*)$. Body deformation inertia affects [derivative](derivative.md) forcing during the stroke, not this final integral relation when the deformation and its time [derivative](derivative.md) return to zero.
