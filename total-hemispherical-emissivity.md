# Total hemispherical emissivity

↑ **Parent:** [Emissivity](emissivity.md)

[Total hemispherical emissivity](total-hemispherical-emissivity.md) integrates over all emitted [wavelengths](wavelength.md) and the outward [hemisphere](hemisphere.md). If $\epsilon_{\lambda,\Omega}$ is [spectral emissivity](spectral-emissivity.md) resolved by direction, and $B_\lambda(T)$ is [blackbody](blackbody.md) [spectral radiance](spectral-radiance.md) from [Planck's law](planck-s-law.md), then

$$
\epsilon(T)=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\!\int_{\Omega_{\rm outward}}
\epsilon_{\lambda,\Omega}(T)B_\lambda(T)\cos\theta\,d\Omega\,d\lambda}{\sigma T^4}.
$$

It is a weighted average, not an unweighted mean of surface properties. Even if the wavelength-resolved material response stays unchanged, the [Planck's law](planck-s-law.md) weighting changes with [temperature](temperature-split.md), so the total ratio can change. Likewise, total [radiometric absorptance](radiometric-absorptance.md) averaged over radiation from hotter surroundings generally differs from this emission-weighted ratio. [Kirchhoff's law of thermal radiation](kirchhoff-s-law-of-thermal-radiation.md) equates matched spectral and directional quantities; it does not equate arbitrary broadband averages.
