# Neutron separation energy

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear separation energy](nuclear-separation-energy.md)

Writing positive [nuclear binding energy](nuclear-binding-energy.md) as $B(Z,N)$ and nuclear [mass](mass.md) as $M(Z,N)$,

$$
S_n(Z,N)=\left[M(Z,N-1)+m_n-M(Z,N)\right]c^2
=B(Z,N)-B(Z,N-1).
$$

The two-[neutron](neutron-split.md) [nuclear separation energy](nuclear-separation-energy.md) is $S_{2n}(Z,N)=B(Z,N)-B(Z,N-2)$. In an independent-particle example, let the last occupied [energy level](energy-level.md) have $\epsilon_-=-8u$ and the next empty level $\epsilon_+=-3u$, for a chosen positive [energy](energy-split.md) unit $u$. With an unchanged core and no residual interactions, removal from the filled level costs $8u$, whereas removing the first particle added above it costs $3u$: the drop $5u$ equals the one-particle gap. These are invented model inputs, not measured nuclear values. [Nuclear pairing](nuclear-pairing.md), rearrangement of the [nuclear mean field](nuclear-mean-field.md), and correlations complicate that identification in real [masses](mass.md); a finite difference of [nuclear binding energies](nuclear-binding-energy.md) is not automatically a bare one-particle gap.
