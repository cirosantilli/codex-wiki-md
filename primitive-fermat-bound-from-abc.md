# Primitive Fermat bound from abc

↑ **Parent:** [abc conjecture](abc-conjecture.md)

For a primitive positive solution of $x^n+y^n=z^n$ with $n\ge4$, the three powers are pairwise [coprime](coprime-integers.md) and their radical is at most $xyz<z^3$. The [abc conjecture](abc-conjecture.md) with $\epsilon=1/6$ gives the displayed inequality, hence $z\le C_{1/6}^2$ uniformly in $n$. The inequality $z^n\le2\max(x,y)^n$ then bounds $n$. Thus there are finitely many primitive triples and exponents. Homogeneity would produce infinitely many unscaled solutions from any one nonzero solution, so a primitive [arithmetic height](height-function.md) bound alone must not be described as finite many arbitrary multiples.
