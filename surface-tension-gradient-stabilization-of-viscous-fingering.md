# Surface-tension-gradient stabilization of viscous fingering

↑ **Parent:** [Planar viscous-fingering dispersion relation](planar-viscous-fingering-dispersion-relation.md)

Suppose the apparent [capillary pressure](capillary-pressure.md) is $p_1-p_2=\gamma(1+\beta x)(\kappa_0+\nabla\cdot\mathbf n)$, with normal towards the displaced fluid and positive apparent tension at the flat front $x=X$. Linearizing the [Darcy law](darcy-law.md) interface problem gives the local growth rate

$$
\sigma(\alpha;X)=\frac{\alpha}{\phi(\mu_1+\mu_2)}[(\mu_2-\mu_1)U-k\gamma\kappa_0\beta-k\gamma(1+\beta X)\alpha^2].
$$

The pressure increase experienced by a forward protrusion is stabilizing when $\kappa_0\beta>0$. It suppresses every positive [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) when $U\le k\gamma\kappa_0\beta/(\mu_2-\mu_1)$. For a fixed spatial gradient, $X=Ut/\phi$ makes the rate time dependent; constant exponential growth is a frozen-position approximation.
