<h1 id="lande-g-factor">Landé g-factor</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Zeeman splitting](zeeman-splitting.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landé_g-factor)

The Landé g-factor is the dimensionless electronic magnetic splitting factor relating a level's [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) to its total [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md). In the approximation where total [orbital angular momentum](orbital-angular-momentum.md) $\mathbf L$ and total [spin](spin.md) $\mathbf S$ couple to $\mathbf J=\mathbf L+\mathbf S$, take the orbital factor as one and the [electron](electron.md) [spin](spin.md) factor as $g_s\simeq2$. For $J>0$,

$$
g_J=1+(g_s-1)\frac{J(J+1)+S(S+1)-L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}.
$$

This follows by projecting $\mathbf L+g_s\mathbf S=\mathbf J+(g_s-1)\mathbf S$ onto $\mathbf J$ and using $2\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf J=\mathbf J^2+\mathbf S^2-\mathbf L^2$. At $S=0$, it gives $g_J=1$; at $L=0$ and $J=S$, it gives $g_J=g_s$. A $J=0$ level instead has zero first-order electronic shift; the formula's zero denominator must not be used. This $g_J$ is distinct from the [statistical weight of an atomic level](statistical-weight-of-an-atomic-level.md) $2J+1$, sometimes also written $g_J$.
