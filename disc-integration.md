# Disc integration

↑ **Parent:** [Solid of revolution](solid-of-revolution.md)

For a [solid of revolution](solid-of-revolution.md) formed by rotating $0\leq y\leq r(x)$ about the horizontal axis, with nonnegative continuous radius $r$ on $[a,b]$, each perpendicular slice is a [disk](disk-mathematics.md) of [area](surface-area.md) $\pi r(x)^2$. Adding the slice volumes by a [definite integral](definite-integral.md) gives $V=\pi\int_a^b r(x)^2dx$. If the rotated region instead has inner and outer radii $0\leq r(x)\leq R(x)$, subtract the missing inner disks: $V=\pi\int_a^b[R(x)^2-r(x)^2]dx$. This is often called the washer method. A complete revolution is already included in the circular cross-sectional area, so no further factor of $2\pi$ belongs in the volume integral.
