# Anomalous Zeeman effect

↑ **Parent:** [Zeeman splitting](zeeman-splitting.md)

The anomalous Zeeman effect is [Zeeman splitting](zeeman-splitting.md) whose pattern is not the simple orbital triplet. [Spin](spin.md) and [spin-orbit interaction](spin-orbit-interaction.md) make the upper and lower [Landé g-factors](lande-g-factor.md) differ. The same weak-field formula still applies when its assumptions hold: “anomalous” does not mean nonlinear in the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md).

For example, take an electronic transition with $L_u=1,S_u=1/2,J_u=1/2$ and $L_l=0,S_l=1/2,J_l=1/2$, neglecting [hyperfine structure](hyperfine-structure.md). With $g_s=2$, the formula above gives $g_u=2/3$ and $g_l=2$. The two allowed $q=0$ transitions have dimensionless shifts $g_um_u-g_lm_l=\pm2/3$, while the $q=\pm1$ transitions give $\pm4/3$. Thus there are four [frequencies](frequency.md), even though each level contains only two [magnetic quantum numbers](magnetic-quantum-number.md). This is an independent worked deduction; it should not be substituted for the unresolved widths in the 1896 experiment.
