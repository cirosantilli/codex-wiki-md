# GW150914

↑ **Parent:** [Gravitational wave](gravitational-wave-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GW150914)

[GW150914](gw150914.md) is the first directly detected [gravitational wave](gravitational-wave-split.md), interpreted as a [binary black hole](binary-black-hole.md) inspiral, merger and ringdown. Its discovery paper reports a peak projected [gravitational wave](gravitational-wave-split.md) strain of approximately $10^{-21}$ and a combined [matched filter](matched-filter.md) [signal-to-noise ratio](signal-to-noise-ratio.md) of 24. The inferred source-frame component [masses](mass.md) were $36^{+5}_{-4}$ and $29^{+4}_{-4}$ [solar masses](solar-mass.md), with 90 percent [credible intervals](credible-interval.md). These are model-dependent estimates from a detector network.[Abstract; II Observation; VI Source discussion, Table I](observation-of-gravitational-waves-from-a-binary-black-hole-merger-by-b-p-abbott-and-others.md)

The interesting measurement is a chain: [curvature](curvature.md) changes relative light travel times, [wave interference](interference-wave-propagation.md) turns those changes into a [photodetector](photodetector.md) signal, calibrated records from separate sites constrain a common source, and [statistical inference](statistical-inference-split.md) tests competing explanations. The following original models explain that chain; the plotted time series are stipulated calculations, never measured [GW150914](gw150914.md) data.
