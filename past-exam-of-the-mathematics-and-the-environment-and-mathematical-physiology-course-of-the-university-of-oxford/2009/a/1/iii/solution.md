<h1 id="a/1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

[Hydrostatic pressure](../../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) at the entrance is $p_w=p_{\rm ref}+\rho_wgh$, with a constant reference [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). Therefore $N=p_i-p_{\rm ref}-\rho_wgh$, and constant overburden [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) gives

$$
\boxed{N_t=-\rho_wg\,h_t=-\rho_wg(M_L-Q/S_L).}
$$

A filling lake raises [water](../../../../../water.md) [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) and lowers [effective pressure](../../../../../effective-pressure-in-subglacial-drainage.md), reducing [material creep](../../../../../creep-deformation.md) closure. A draining lake has the reverse effect. These signs supply the [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) feedback in the coupled drainage model.
