<h1 id="a/3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For [quasistatic bed stability with suspended sediment](../../../../../quasistatic-bed-stability-with-suspended-sediment.md), in the leading approximation, $(hu)_x=0$ and $\eta_x=0$. For spatially varying perturbations at fixed unit [water](../../../../../water.md) [flux](../../../../../flux.md) and fixed surface level, write $u=1+U$, $h=1+H$, $c=1+C$, $s=s_*+S_1$. The constraints give $H=-U$ and $S_1=-H=U$. Linearizing the suspended and bed [equations](../../../../../equation.md) yields

$$
C_x=4U-C,\qquad U_t+3\beta U_x=-4U+C.
$$

For a [Fourier mode](../../../../../fourier-mode.md) $e^{\sigma t+i\kappa x}$, the first relation gives $C=4U/(1+i\kappa)$; substitution into the second gives

$$
\boxed{\sigma=-\frac{4\kappa^2}{1+\kappa^2}-i\kappa\left(3\beta+\frac4{1+\kappa^2}\right).}
$$

Thus every nonzero spatial [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) has negative growth rate, independently of $\beta$. The parameter changes propagation, not damping. The spatially uniform mode has $\sigma=0$ and is neutral, representing a change among uniform reference states compatible with the leading constraints. Hence **there is no growing linear mode in this approximation**; the claim is stability, not strict decay of every homogeneous perturbation. Restoring the small discarded terms or changing [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md) requires a separate calculation.
