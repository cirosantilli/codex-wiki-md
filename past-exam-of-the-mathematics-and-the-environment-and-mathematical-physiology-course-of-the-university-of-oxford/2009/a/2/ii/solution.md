<h1 id="a/2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

To obtain a [power-law river kinematic wave](../../../../../power-law-river-kinematic-wave.md), let $P_b$ be the wetted perimeter and $I$ the small bed slope. A uniform-flow [force](../../../../../force-split.md) balance per unit river [length](../../../../../length.md) is $\rho gAI=\tau_bP_b$. With a [Chézy friction law](../../../../../chezy-friction-law.md) $\tau_b=\rho f u^2$, it gives $u=(gI/f)^{1/2}(A/P_b)^{1/2}$. Thus the [hydraulic radius](../../../../../hydraulic-radius.md) determines the [power](../../../../../power.md) of [area](../../../../../surface-area.md).

For a channel family with $P_b=P_0A^q$, where $q<1$, we have $u=C A^{(1-q)/2}$, with $C=(gI/(fP_0))^{1/2}$. Set $m=(1-q)/2>0$ and $c=(m+1)C$. Then

$$
\boxed{Q=Au=\frac c{m+1}A^{m+1}.}
$$

For example a broad fixed-width rectangular channel has approximately constant wetted perimeter, giving $m=1/2$; a geometrically similar channel family has $P_b\propto A^{1/2}$, giving $m=1/4$. The exponent is therefore a geometry-dependent consequence of the [friction](../../../../../friction.md) balance, not a universal arbitrary value fixed by Chezy's law alone.
