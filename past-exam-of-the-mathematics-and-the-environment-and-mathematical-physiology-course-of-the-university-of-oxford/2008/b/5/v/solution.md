<h1 id="b/5/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

The fast scale resolves the rapid complex buildup that is compressed into a thin [initial layer](../../../../../initial-layer.md) on the [substrate](../../../../../substrate-biochemistry.md)-depletion scale. The outer scale follows depletion over a much longer time and cannot satisfy $c(0)=0$ by itself.

In the overlap $1\ll\tau_f\ll\epsilon^{-1}$, the [inner solution](../../../../../inner-expansion.md) tends to $(s,c)=(1,1/(1+K))$. The [outer solution](../../../../../outer-expansion.md) as $\tau_s\downarrow0$ has the same limit. Subtracting this common part produces a leading [composite approximation](../../../../../additive-composite-expansion.md),

$$
\boxed{s_{\rm comp}=s_{\rm out}(\tau_s),\quad
c_{\rm comp}=\frac{s_{\rm out}(\tau_s)}{s_{\rm out}(\tau_s)+K}-\frac{e^{-(1+K)\tau_s/\epsilon}}{1+K}.}
$$

It satisfies the leading initial values and tends to the outer quasi-steady solution after the [initial layer](../../../../../initial-layer.md). Corrections of order $\epsilon$ describe the small [substrate](../../../../../substrate-biochemistry.md) loss during complex buildup.
