<h1 id="a/3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The depth $h$ is free-surface height $\eta$ minus bed elevation $s$. The first differential equation conserves water volume. Momentum balances local and advective acceleration against the free-surface [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) gradient, mean gravitational bed-slope forcing and the quadratic drag $fu^2/h$.

The [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) $c$ is suspended [sediment](../../../../../sediment.md) mass per water volume. Multiplication of its material rate by depth gives the [sediment](../../../../../sediment.md) change per bed area. Settling at speed $v_s$ removes mass at rate $v_sc$, while [entrainment](../../../../../fluid-entrainment.md) supplies $v_sE(u)$; increasing [equilibrium](../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) $E(u)$ represents stronger erosive capacity at greater speed. The same exchange changes the bed mass per area, whose solid volume fraction is $1-\phi$ and grain density is $\rho_s$. Hence net suspended gain equals $-\rho_s(1-\phi)s_t$: erosion lowers the bed and deposition raises it.

One derives these balances by depth integration with [hydrostatic pressure](../../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) and a sectionally averaged [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md), followed by closures for drag and [sediment](../../../../../sediment.md) [entrainment](../../../../../fluid-entrainment.md)/settling. The model neglects additional bedload transport and uses the dilute-suspension approximation to keep the water-volume balance independent of [sediment](../../../../../sediment.md) volume.
