<h1 id="a/3/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Write $u=1+\widetilde u$, $c=1+\widetilde c$ and let $a_E=E^{*\prime}(1)>0$. To first order $\widetilde s=(1-F^2)\widetilde u$, and the other balances give $\widetilde c_x=a_E\widetilde u-\widetilde c=-\widetilde s_t$. Seek modes $e^{\sigma t+ikx}$. Then

$$
\widetilde c=\frac{a_E}{1+ik}\widetilde u,\qquad
\boxed{\sigma=\frac{a_E}{F^2-1}\frac{ik}{1+ik}=\frac{a_E}{F^2-1}\frac{k^2+ik}{1+k^2}.}
$$

Thus every nonzero [wavenumber](../../../../../wavenumber.md) grows when $F>1$, while it decays when $F<1$. The zero mode is neutral in this reduced model. The exceptional $F=1$ limit makes the algebraic depth-bed relation degenerate and requires neglected effects. If the [entrainment](../../../../../fluid-entrainment.md) [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) vanishes at the reference state, this first-order instability calculation is itself degenerate.

For $F>1$, the [growth rate](../../../../../growth-rate.md) tends to the finite positive limit $a_E/(F^2-1)$ as $|k|\to\infty$. It does not diverge with [wavenumber](../../../../../wavenumber.md), although this reduced theory selects no finite fastest scale and should not be extrapolated beyond its long-wave regime. The [phase speed](../../../../../phase-speed.md) is

$$
\boxed{c_{\rm phase}=-\operatorname{Im}\sigma/k=-\frac{a_E}{(F^2-1)(1+k^2)}<0.}
$$

The unstable bedforms migrate upstream against the water flow and represent [antidunes](../../../../../antidune.md) arising from coupled erosion, deposition and supercritical free-surface flow. This is the [relaxation-controlled antidune instability](../../../../../relaxation-controlled-antidune-instability.md).
