<h1 id="a/1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Set $x=T^4/T_0^4-1$. Since $\sigma\gamma T_0^4=Q/8$, the [steady state](../../../../../steady-state.md) [energy balance](../../../../../energy-balance.md) is $2(1-a_0)=1+x$. Substitution gives

$$
\boxed{\tanh(\mu x)=2x.}
$$

The two sides have slopes $\mu$ and $2$ at the origin. For $x>0$, $\tanh(\mu x)$ is strictly [concave](../../../../../concave-function.md), its initial slope is $\mu$, and it remains below $1$. Consequently no positive root exists for $\mu\leq2$, while for $\mu>2$ there is exactly one positive root $x_+\in(0,1/2)$. Oddness supplies the corresponding negative root. At $\mu=2$ the only root is still zero. Hence **multiple steady temperatures occur precisely when $\mu>\mu_c=2$**.

The largest root corresponds monotonically to the largest positive absolute [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md):

$$
\boxed{T_+=T_0(1+x_+)^{1/4},\qquad a_+=\frac{1-x_+}{2}.}
$$

For large $\mu$, the positive root tends to $1/2$, so $a_+\simeq1/4$ and $T_+/T_0\simeq(3/2)^{1/4}$. The negative branch has larger [albedo](../../../../../albedo.md) and lower [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md).
