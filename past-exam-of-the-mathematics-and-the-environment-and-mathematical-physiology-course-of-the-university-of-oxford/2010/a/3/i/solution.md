<h1 id="a/3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Resolve [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) into components $g\sin\alpha$ down the bed and $-g\cos\alpha$ normal to it. For slow [incompressible flow](../../../../../incompressible-flow.md), [inertia](../../../../../inertia.md) is negligible and [Cauchy momentum equation](../../../../../cauchy-momentum-equation.md) reduces to the divergence of the [stress tensor](../../../../../cauchy-stress-tensor.md) plus the gravitational [force density](../../../../../force-density.md) being zero. Write the dominant shear component as $\tau=\sigma_{xz}$ and the leading normal stresses as $-p$.

The [shallow-ice approximation](../../../../../shallow-ice-approximation.md) assumes a small thickness-to-length ratio. Vertical variation of the shear is retained in the along-bed equation, while longitudinal deviatoric-stress gradients and the shear contribution to the normal equation are higher order. The leading balances are consequently

$$
\boxed{-p_x+\tau_z+\rho g\sin\alpha=0,\qquad -p_z-\rho g\cos\alpha=0.}
$$

The second is a [hydrostatic pressure](../../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) balance; the first balances gravitational driving and the surface-induced [pressure gradient](../../../../../pressure-gradient.md) against vertical shear. This approximation requires shallow geometry and sufficiently slow flow, not a Newtonian [constitutive equation](../../../../../constitutive-equation.md) for [ice](../../../../../ice.md).
