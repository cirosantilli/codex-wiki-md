# Fluorescence

↑ **Parent:** [Physical optics](physical-optics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescence)

[Fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) is [photon emission](photon-emission.md) following [photon absorption](photon-absorption.md) by matter: an [excited state](excited-state.md) releases some of its [energy](energy-split.md) as [light](light.md). A [fluorophore](fluorophore.md) can therefore mark where something is, while its [fluorescence emission spectrum](fluorescence-emission-spectrum.md) and [fluorescence lifetime](fluorescence-lifetime.md) report different aspects of its environment. [Fluorescein](fluorescein.md) is one example; [FRET](forster-resonance-energy-transfer.md) exploits a competing route for excitation [energy](energy-split.md) rather than requiring a second emitted [photon](photon.md).

The usual molecular sequence is excitation, relaxation, then emission, as in the [Jablonski diagram](jablonski-diagram.md). The emitted [photon](photon.md) commonly has lower [energy](energy-split.md) and longer [wavelength](wavelength.md), giving a [Stokes shift](stokes-shift.md); the exceptions are discussed below.[Stokes Shift in Fluorescence Spectroscopy, Figure 4 and relaxation discussion](what-is-the-stokes-shift-by-edinburgh-instruments.md)
