# Gravothermal catastrophe

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar dynamics](stellar-dynamics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravothermal_catastrophe)

A collisional self-gravitating stellar core can lose heat to its halo, contract and become hotter because it has [negative heat capacity of a virialized gravitational system](negative-heat-capacity-of-a-virialized-gravitational-system.md). The increased temperature contrast drives further outward heat flow and further contraction. The classical gravothermal catastrophe is the loss of a finite-density thermodynamic equilibrium at a finite total energy along the confined three-dimensional Newtonian isothermal sequence. It concerns slow [two-body relaxation](two-body-relaxation.md), not simply a collisionless instability. The dimensional form of the gravitational interaction and the thermodynamic ensemble matter: a two-dimensional logarithmic gravitational gas need not have the same finite-energy instability.
