[evaporative cooling](../../../../../evaporative-cooling-atomic-physics.md) removes atoms selectively from the high-energy tail of a trapped gas. The remaining sample has less [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) per atom; [elastic collisions](../../../../../elastic-collision.md) then redistribute [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and restore a thermal distribution at lower [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). Random atom loss would generally remove average [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and would not provide the same cooling mechanism. Repeated [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) selection and rethermalization, rather than one removal event, is the essential cycle.

Using the [evaporation truncation parameter](../../../../../evaporation-truncation-parameter.md) $\eta$, let the escape threshold be $\epsilon_{\rm cut}=\eta k_BT$. As the gas cools, a fixed threshold is populated by fewer atoms, so evaporation stalls. In [forced evaporative cooling](../../../../../forced-evaporative-cooling.md) the threshold is lowered during the sequence. In a magnetic trap, a [radiofrequency evaporation knife](../../../../../radiofrequency-evaporation-knife.md) flips trapped weak-field-seeking atoms into untrapped [spin](../../../../../spin.md) states where the Zeeman splitting matches the [photon](../../../../../photon.md) [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). Energetic atoms reach that resonance surface; sweeping the radiofrequency lowers the [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) knife. In an optical dipole trap, lowering the trap depth offers a different implementation, although it usually also weakens confinement.

A simple [evaporative cooling energy balance](../../../../../evaporative-cooling-energy-balance.md) shows when atom loss is useful. For a classical gas in a three-dimensional harmonic trap of fixed frequencies, equipartition gives $E=3Nk_BT$, counting both kinetic and [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md). Suppose escaping atoms remove average [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $\epsilon_{\rm ev}=(\eta+\kappa)k_BT$, with $\kappa$ a distribution-dependent excess above the threshold. For $dN<0$,

$$
d(3Nk_BT)=\epsilon_{\rm ev}\,dN,\qquad
\alpha\equiv\frac{d\ln T}{d\ln N}=\frac{\eta+\kappa-3}{3}.
$$

Thus preferential removal cools when the removed [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) exceeds $3k_BT$. More stringently, cooling must raise the [phase-space density of an atomic gas](../../../../../phase-space-density-of-an-atomic-gas.md), not merely lower the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md):

$$
\mathcal D=n_{\rm peak}\lambda_T^3
=N\left(\frac{\hbar\bar\omega}{k_BT}\right)^3,\qquad
\lambda_T=\frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi mk_BT}}.
$$

For this [harmonic-trap phase-space density](../../../../../harmonic-trap-phase-space-density.md), with fixed geometric-mean trap [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) $\bar\omega$, $d\ln\mathcal D/d\ln N=1-3\alpha$. Therefore **phase-space density rises during atom loss if $\alpha>1/3$**. The numerical factors here belong to the stated harmonic-trap model, not every evaporative apparatus.

The post-cut distribution is truncated and is not yet a [thermal equilibrium](../../../../../thermal-equilibrium-split.md) distribution.

There must be enough [elastic collisions](../../../../../elastic-collision.md) to repopulate the tail: $\Gamma_{\rm el}\sim n\sigma\bar v$ should exceed damaging loss and heating rates, and the ramp should allow rethermalization. Increasing density can improve [elastic collisions](../../../../../elastic-collision.md) but also enhances inelastic and three-body losses. Very rapid removal can leave a nonequilibrium cloud; very slow removal exposes it to background-gas losses. Lowering an optical trap can reduce density and collision rate, so a falling [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) is not by itself evidence of efficient evaporation.

The dilute-gas condensation experiments made this tradeoff concrete. [Eric A. Cornell](../../../../../eric-a-cornell.md) and [Carl Wieman](../../../../../carl-wieman.md)'s account explains how selective escape and contraction of a trapped cloud raise phase-space density, while the need for rapid two-body equilibration and slow three-body loss restricts usable density. It also describes expansion imaging as an experimental diagnostic.[pp. 876–878, equilibration, selective escape and imaging of expanded clouds](../../../../../nobel-lecture-bose-einstein-condensation-in-a-dilute-gas-the-first-70-years-and-some-recent-experiments-by-eric-a-cornell-and-carl-wieman.md) Useful controls compare different knife ramps and hold times, measure both atom number and expansion [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), and check trap-frequency calibration. Pure adiabatic expansion can cool without increasing phase-space density. **Successful evaporation pays an atom-number cost to increase the remaining gas's phase-space occupancy.**
