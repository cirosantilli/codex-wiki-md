<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [electric dipole transition](../../../../../electric-dipole-transition.md) has amplitude proportional to $\langle f|\mathbf d\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon|i\rangle$. The [electric dipole operator](../../../../../electric-dipole-operator.md) is odd under [parity](../../../../../parity.md), so the initial and final states must have **opposite [parity](../../../../../parity.md)**. As a rank-one spherical [tensor](../../../../../tensor.md) it also requires **$\Delta J=0,\pm1$, excluding $J=0\leftrightarrow0$, and $\Delta m_J=0,\pm1$**. These follow from angular-momentum addition and the [Wigner-Eckart theorem](../../../../../wigner-eckart-theorem.md); vanishing [Clebsch-Gordan coefficients](../../../../../clebsch-gordan-coefficients.md) can impose further restrictions. Relative to the quantization axis, $\pi$ [polarization](../../../../../polarization-waves.md) drives $\Delta m=0$, while $\sigma^\pm$ drive $\Delta m=\pm1$.

For a single active [electron](../../../../../electron.md), [parity](../../../../../parity.md) and the orbital triangle rule combine to give $\Delta l=\pm1$. In [LS coupling](../../../../../ls-coupling.md), the spin-independent electric dipole operator gives $\Delta S=0$ and $\Delta L=0,\pm1$, excluding $L=0\leftrightarrow0$; total [parity](../../../../../parity.md) must still change. Spin-orbit mixing can weakly relax the approximate [spin](../../../../../spin.md) rule. For resolved hyperfine states the corresponding triangle rule is $\Delta F=0,\pm1$, excluding $F=0\leftrightarrow0$, with $\Delta m_F=0,\pm1$. These labels require the appropriate coupling regime. There is no universal E1 rule demanding a particular change in [principal quantum number](../../../../../principal-quantum-number.md). An E1-forbidden line may occur through another multipole or a multiphoton process.
