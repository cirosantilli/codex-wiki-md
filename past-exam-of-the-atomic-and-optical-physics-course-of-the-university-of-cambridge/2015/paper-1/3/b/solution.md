<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [two-photon transition](../../../../../two-photon-transition.md) is a second-order atom-field process. For [two-photon absorption](../../../../../two-photon-absorption.md), [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) conservation requires $E_f-E_g=\hbar(\omega_1+\omega_2)$. A [virtual intermediate state in two-photon absorption](../../../../../virtual-intermediate-state-in-two-photon-absorption.md) need not be populated as a real stationary state. In the electric dipole approximation, the two time orderings give an amplitude proportional to

$$
\mathcal M_{fg}=\sum_n\left[
\frac{\langle f|\mathbf d\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon_2|n\rangle\langle n|\mathbf d\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon_1|g\rangle}{E_g+\hbar\omega_1-E_n}
+\frac{\langle f|\mathbf d\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon_1|n\rangle\langle n|\mathbf d\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon_2|g\rangle}{E_g+\hbar\omega_2-E_n}
\right].
$$

The electric-field amplitudes multiply this expression. Far from intermediate resonance, the denominators are large relative to linewidths and one-photon couplings, keeping real intermediate occupation small. Near an intermediate resonance, sequential absorption, linewidths and saturation require a fuller treatment. For weak unsaturated absorption the rate scales as **$I_1I_2$**, or $I^2$ when both photons come from one field. Absorption followed by [stimulated emission](../../../../../stimulated-emission.md) instead gives a difference-frequency [Raman transition](../../../../../raman-transition.md); it is not [two-photon absorption](../../../../../two-photon-absorption.md) at the sum [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md).

Two electric dipole interactions change parity twice, so initial and final states have the same parity. For a one-electron orbital, the usual changes are $\Delta\ell=0,\pm2$, subject to angular-momentum and polarization constraints. For identical equal-frequency photons, exchange symmetry removes the antisymmetric rank-one tensor: ranks zero and two survive. Thus $J=0\to0$ is possible, whereas $J=0\to1$ is forbidden in this degenerate electric-dipole approximation. It would be too strong to prohibit every $\Delta J=1$ transition between nonzero angular momenta, since a rank-two tensor can connect some such pairs. Spin remains unchanged to leading electric-dipole order.

A central example is hydrogen $1S\to2S$, forbidden as a one-photon [electric dipole transition](../../../../../electric-dipole-transition.md) but accessible with two photons near $243\,\mathrm{nm}$. Absorption of one photon from each of equal-frequency counterpropagating beams makes the first-order [Doppler shift](../../../../../doppler-effect.md) $(\mathbf k_1+\mathbf k_2)\cdot\mathbf v$ vanish and suppresses net [recoil](../../../../../recoil.md). The two same-direction absorption paths can still give a broad Doppler background. A primary demonstration used a frequency-doubled pulsed dye [laser](../../../../../laser-split.md) near this wavelength and compared with a simultaneously recorded Balmer reference.[Phys. Rev. Lett. 34, 307 (1975), abstract: 2430-angstrom source and simultaneous Balmer-beta reference](../../../../../doppler-free-two-photon-spectroscopy-of-hydrogen-by-theodor-w-hansch-and-others.md)

Experimentally, scan the sum [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) and detect the excited state, its subsequent fluorescence or its ionization. Counterpropagating and single-beam spectra distinguish the narrow Doppler-free contribution from its background. Varying intensity tests weak-field scaling and extrapolates [AC Stark shifts](../../../../../ac-stark-shift.md) and [power broadening](../../../../../power-broadening.md); [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) calibration, beam overlap, residual angle and second-order [Doppler shifts](../../../../../doppler-effect.md) still matter. “Doppler free” here cancels the leading [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) term, not every systematic shift.
