<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Two complementary signatures are suppressed low-speed dissipation and [quantized circulation](../../../../../quantized-circulation.md). Condensation alone establishes macroscopic occupation, not all of these flow properties. In a uniform weakly interacting condensate, the [Landau criterion](../../../../../landau-criterion.md) gives

$$
v_L=\inf_{p>0}\frac{E(p)}p=c_s=\sqrt{\mu/m}
$$

for the [Bogoliubov spectrum](../../../../../bogoliubov-quasiparticle-dispersion.md). Creating an excitation in a flow changes [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) by $E(\mathbf p)-\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf p$; a negative value allows [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) transfer. Boundaries and vortex nucleation can make an actual measured threshold lower than this ideal bulk sound speed.

In the [moving-obstacle test of atomic superfluidity](../../../../../moving-obstacle-test-of-atomic-superfluidity.md), a blue-detuned focused [laser](../../../../../laser-split.md) repelled atoms in a sodium condensate and was scanned at different speeds. After a fixed exposure, time-of-flight [absorption imaging](../../../../../absorption-imaging.md) measured the reduction of [condensate fraction](../../../../../condensate-fraction.md) as a heating indicator. Low-speed heating was small, with a pronounced onset near $1.6\,\mathrm{mm/s}$, below the peak-density sound speed near $6.2\,\mathrm{mm/s}$. A stationary-beam control showed no heating; above-transition clouds constrained heating through the noncondensed fraction. Changing scan [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) and amplitude at common obstacle [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) helped separate a flow threshold from a particular trap resonance. Density inhomogeneity, obstacle geometry and vortex nucleation limit identification of the observed onset with a unique homogeneous Landau [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md).[pp. 2502–2504, Figs. 1–3, stationary-beam and above-transition controls](../../../../../evidence-for-a-critical-velocity-in-a-bose-einstein-condensed-gas-by-c-raman-and-others.md)

A second experiment stirred [rubidium-87](../../../../../rubidium-87.md) in a magnetic trap by rotating a small transverse anisotropy generated with a far-detuned attractive [laser](../../../../../laser-split.md). After stirring, the cloud expanded for $27\,\mathrm{ms}$ and [absorption imaging](../../../../../absorption-imaging.md) along the rotation axis revealed vortex cores. Below a threshold no cores appeared; faster rotation produced one or several. Ramping the stirrer down before release reduced additional excitation, and waiting after removing it tested vortex survival. Beam-centre stability and residual trap anisotropy are important controls; an isolated density dip without the rotation and persistence evidence would be less conclusive. This [stirred-condensate vortex experiment](../../../../../stirred-condensate-vortex-experiment.md) is a response to rotation, distinct from merely imaging the appearance of a condensate.[preprint pp. 1–3, rotating dipole anisotropy, time-of-flight imaging and single-vortex lifetime](../../../../../vortex-formation-in-a-stirred-bose-einstein-condensate-by-k-w-madison-and-others.md)

The [superfluid velocity](../../../../../superfluid-velocity.md) is $\mathbf v_s=(\hbar/m)\nabla\theta$. A closed loop avoiding a core must return a single-valued [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md), so

$$
\boxed{\oint\mathbf v_s\cdot d\mathbf l=\frac\hbar m\,2\pi\ell=\ell\frac hm,\qquad\ell\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

A singly quantized vortex therefore has $2\pi$ [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) winding and circulation $h/m$, with depleted density in a core of order the [healing length](../../../../../healing-length.md). This quantized rotational response differs from ordinary rigid-body flow. The experiment supports that interpretation through controlled stirring and vortex formation; imaging cores alone does not independently measure every [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) winding or an absolute zero dissipative rate.
