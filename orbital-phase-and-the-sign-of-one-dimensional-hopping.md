# Orbital phase and the sign of one-dimensional hopping

↑ **Parent:** [Tight binding](tight-binding.md)

In an orthogonal one-orbital nearest-neighbor [tight-binding model](tight-binding.md), the [hopping integral](hopping-integral.md) is $t=\langle\phi_j|H|\phi_{j+1}\rangle$ and the dispersion is the displayed cosine. Using the same orbital-phase convention at every site, equal s-orbital coefficients usually make a bonding state at the zone center, so $t<0$ and $k=0$ is a band minimum. For $p_x$ orbitals along the chain, with every positive lobe pointing right, equal site coefficients bring opposite-sign lobes together between atoms. This is antibonding; alternating coefficients are bonding, giving the usual $t_\sigma>0$ and a band maximum at zero. Changing conventions requires transforming the Bloch basis consistently. The physical statement concerns the curvature and orbital character of a specified Bloch state, not an isolated hopping sign divorced from its basis.
