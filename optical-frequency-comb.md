# Optical frequency comb

↑ **Parent:** [Laser](laser-split.md)

An [optical frequency comb](optical-frequency-comb.md) has evenly spaced optical [spectral lines](spectral-line.md), or teeth. A [mode-locked laser](mode-locked-laser.md) supplies short [optical pulses](optical-pulse.md) at repetition [frequency](frequency.md) $f_r$, the tooth spacing. The [carrier-envelope offset frequency](carrier-envelope-offset-frequency.md) $f_0$ positions the whole ruler:

$$
f_n=n f_r+f_0,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z,\qquad 0\leq f_0<f_r.
$$

Only teeth within the emitted [optical spectrum](optical-spectrum.md) are present; the extrapolated intercept need not be an emitted tooth.[Passion for Precision, p.157: tooth relation](passion-for-precision-by-theodor-w-hansch.md)

The achievement associated with [John L. Hall](john-l-hall.md) and [Theodor W. Hänsch](theodor-w-hansch.md) is a practical bridge between optical [frequencies](frequency.md) and countable electronic [frequencies](frequency.md). Instead of constructing a separate chain of [lasers](laser-split.md) for each optical measurement, a broad comb supplies many linked reference teeth. Stable [lasers](laser-split.md), short [optical pulses](optical-pulse.md) and strong [nonlinear optics](nonlinear-optics.md) in [optical fibers](optical-fiber.md) came together around 1999–2000.[Nobel Lecture: Defining and measuring optical frequencies, pp.1288–1291: chains and comb development](nobel-lecture-defining-and-measuring-optical-frequencies-by-john-l-hall.md)
