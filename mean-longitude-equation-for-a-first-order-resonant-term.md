# Mean-longitude equation for a first-order resonant term

↑ **Parent:** [Lagrange planetary equations](lagrange-planetary-equations.md)

For [disturbing function](disturbing-function.md) $\mathcal R=(GM_2/a_2)f(\alpha)e\cos\phi$, $\alpha=a/a_2$, the leading planar [Lagrange planetary equations](lagrange-planetary-equations.md) give $\dot\lambda=n+n(M_2/M_\star)e\,g(\alpha)\cos\phi$. The derivative with respect to $a$ holds the other [osculating orbital elements](osculating-orbital-element.md) fixed. If one writes $\lambda=n(t)t+\epsilon(t)$, its literal derivative includes $t\dot n$; the physical mean-longitude equation cannot be obtained by silently dropping that term. A consistent accumulated-phase parametrization uses $\lambda=\int_0^t n(s)\,ds+\epsilon_{\rm int}$.
