# Brauer character basis theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Brauer–Nesbitt theorem](brauer-nesbitt-theorem.md)

Over a [splitting field for finite group representations](splitting-field-for-finite-group-representations.md) of characteristic $p$, the irreducible [Brauer characters](brauer-character.md) form a complex basis of the [class functions](class-function.md) on the [p-regular elements](p-regular-element.md). Independence is the character form of the [Brauer–Nesbitt theorem](brauer-nesbitt-theorem.md). To obtain spanning, extend such a class function by zero on the p-singular classes. Ordinary irreducible characters form a basis of all class functions by [character orthogonality](character-orthogonality.md). Restricting them to p-regular elements yields Brauer characters of reductions of an [integral form of a group representation](integral-form-of-a-group-representation.md) in a compatible splitting [p-modular system](p-modular-system.md); if needed, first extend scalars, which does not change the simple-module list under the splitting hypothesis. Each restriction is a nonnegative integral sum of simple Brauer characters by exact-sequence additivity and the [Jordan–Hölder theorem](jordan-holder-theorem.md). Thus these restrictions span, proving the assertion. Consequently the number of simple modules equals the number of p-regular conjugacy classes, and evaluation identifies the complexified [modular representation ring](modular-representation-ring.md) with the product of one copy of $\mathbb C$ for each such class.
