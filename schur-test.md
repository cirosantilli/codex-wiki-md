# Schur test

↑ **Parent:** [Continuous linear operator](continuous-linear-operator.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur_test)

For a matrix with nonnegative entries, row sums at most $R$ and column sums at most $C$ imply an $\ell^2$ operator [norm](norm.md) at most $\sqrt{RC}$. Indeed the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) gives $|\sum_j A_{ij}x_j|^2\le(\sum_jA_{ij})\sum_jA_{ij}|x_j|^2$. Summing over $i$ bounds the result by $RC\sum_j|x_j|^2$. Integral kernels and positive weight functions give analogous forms. The symmetric case has equal row and column bounds.
