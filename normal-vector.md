# Normal vector

↑ **Parent:** [Differential geometry](differential-geometry-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_vector)

A normal vector at a point of an embedded [smooth manifold](smooth-manifold-split.md) is [orthogonal](orthogonal-vectors.md) to every [tangent vector](tangent-vector-split.md) in its [tangent space](tangent-space.md), using the surrounding [dot product](dot-product.md). Its direction describes departure from the local tangent geometry. For a [regular level set](regular-level-set.md) $F(x)=c$ with nonzero [gradient](gradient.md), $\nabla F$ is a [normal vector](normal-vector.md): differentiation of $F$ along any curve on the [level set](level-set.md) gives $\nabla F\cdot r'=0$.

For a [plane curve](plane-curve.md), the normal direction is a [straight line](straight-line.md) perpendicular to its [unit tangent vector](unit-tangent-vector.md); the two [unit normals](unit-normal.md) differ by sign. For a [curve](curve.md) in three-dimensional [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md), the orthogonal directions form a two-dimensional plane. A [principal normal vector](principal-normal-vector.md) selects one of them using [curvature](curvature.md), so it must be distinguished from an arbitrary [normal vector](normal-vector.md).
