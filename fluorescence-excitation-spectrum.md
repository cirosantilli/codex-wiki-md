# Fluorescence excitation spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Fluorescence](fluorescence.md)

A [fluorescence excitation spectrum](fluorescence-excitation-spectrum.md) scans excitation [wavelength](wavelength.md) while detecting [fluorescence](fluorescence.md) in a fixed emission band. A [fluorescence emission spectrum](fluorescence-emission-spectrum.md) does the converse. The inspected spectrofluorometer example uses an excitation [monochromator](monochromator.md) scan with the emission [monochromator](monochromator.md) fixed, and reverses their roles for emission.[What are Absorption, Excitation and Emission Spectra?, Excitation Spectra, PDF pp. 2–3, Figures 3–4; Emission Spectra, p. 4, Figures 5–6](what-are-absorption-excitation-and-emission-spectra-by-edinburgh-instruments.md)

An operational model explains why this need not reproduce absorption. For one dilute, unsaturated emitting species, write $a(\lambda)$ for absorbed fraction, $R_{\rm in}(\lambda)$ for incident [photon](photon.md) rate, $\Phi(\lambda)$ for [fluorescence quantum yield](fluorescence-quantum-yield.md), and $\eta_{\rm band}(\lambda)$ for the fraction of emitted [photons](photon.md) counted in the selected band. The background-subtracted count rate is

$$
C_{\rm ex}(\lambda)=R_{\rm in}(\lambda)a(\lambda)\Phi(\lambda)\eta_{\rm band}(\lambda).
$$

Only after correcting incident [photon](photon.md) rate, and with constant yield and emission-band collection, is this proportional to absorbed fraction. A nonfluorescent absorber contributes to absorption but not this readout. Thick samples, multiple emitting species, and reabsorption of [fluorescence](fluorescence.md) complicate the model.
