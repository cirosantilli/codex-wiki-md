# Electrostatic energy increase on equal-drop coalescence

↑ **Parent:** [Capacitance](capacitance.md)

Two isolated conducting drops of [radius](radius.md) $a$ and equal charge merge at fixed total charge and [volume](volume.md). The new [radius](radius.md) is $2^{1/3}a$, so its [capacitance](capacitance.md) grows by only $2^{1/3}$ while total charge doubles. Relative to the sum of the initially separated self energies, the final [electric field](electric-field-split.md) [energy](energy-split.md) grows by $2^{2/3}$. Decreased surface [energy](energy-split.md) and/or applied mechanical work can supply that increase. Sufficiently large like charges need not coalesce spontaneously; [energy](energy-split.md) conservation is not a guarantee of merging. A maintained external [voltage](voltage.md) would define a different problem.
