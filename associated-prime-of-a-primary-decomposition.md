# Associated prime of a primary decomposition

↑ **Parent:** [Minimal primary decomposition](minimal-primary-decomposition.md)

For a finite [minimal primary decomposition](minimal-primary-decomposition.md) $I=\bigcap_iQ_i$, these are the [prime ideals](prime-ideal.md) $\sqrt{Q_i}$. This terminology applies without a [Noetherian](noetherian-ring.md) hypothesis when the decomposition exists. Its invariance follows from the intrinsic description as those [prime ideals](prime-ideal.md) of the form $\sqrt{(I:x)}$, where $x\notin I$ and $(I:x)$ is a [colon ideal](colon-ideal.md). Indeed, $(I:x)=\bigcap_i(Q_i:x)$, and each factor is either the whole [ring](ring.md) or a [primary ideal](primary-ideal.md) with [radical of an ideal](radical-of-an-ideal.md) $\sqrt{Q_i}$. A finite intersection of [prime ideals](prime-ideal.md) can be prime only if it equals one of them: otherwise multiplying one element outside the intersection from each factor contradicts the [prime ideal](prime-ideal.md) condition. Conversely, irredundancy supplies $x\in\bigcap_{j\ne i}Q_j\setminus Q_i$, giving $\sqrt{(I:x)}=\sqrt{Q_i}$. In a [Noetherian ring](noetherian-ring.md) these agree with the [associated primes of a module](associated-prime-of-a-module.md) $R/I$. For arbitrary [rings](ring.md), the [annihilator](annihilator-ring-theory.md) definition of an [associated prime of a module](associated-prime-of-a-module.md) must not be substituted without a further hypothesis.
