# Gauge theory

↑ **Parent:** [Classical field theory](classical-field-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_theory)

A [classical field theory](classical-field-theory-split.md) is a gauge theory when its description has a local redundancy represented by [gauge transformations](gauge-transformation.md). A [gauge field](gauge-field.md) defines the [covariant derivative](covariant-derivative.md) that compares matter fields at different points, and its [gauge field strength](gauge-field-strength.md) measures the curvature of that comparison. [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism-split.md) is the familiar Abelian example; [Yang-Mills theory](yang-mills-theory.md) generalizes it to a non-Abelian internal group. Both classical equations and their [quantum field theory](quantum-field-theory-split.md) can have this structure. Physically equivalent gauge descriptions must give the same observables, even though the [gauge potential](gauge-field.md) changes.
