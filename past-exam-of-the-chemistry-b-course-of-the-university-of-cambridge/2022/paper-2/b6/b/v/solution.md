<h1 id="b6/b/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

The Co(III) centre in $A^1$ has $t_{2g}^6(e_g^*)^0$: every occupied d subset is orbitally balanced. **There is no first-order metal-centred Jahn–Teller driving force in $A^1$**. This does not require all six experimental [bond lengths](../../../../../../bond-length.md) to be equal, since [pyridine](../../../../../../pyridine.md) and dioxolene are different donors.

The Co(II) centre in $A^2$ has $t_{2g}^5(e_g^*)^2$. Its two $e_g^*$ orbitals each contain one [electron](../../../../../../electron.md), so that subset gives no first-order preference. The $t_{2g}$ hole does give a weaker Jahn–Teller instability. To compare tetragonal distortions, take a positive small splitting parameter $\delta$ and retain each subset's [orbital energy barycentre](../../../../../../orbital-energy-barycentre.md). On axial elongation let $d_{xz},d_{yz}$ shift by $-\delta$ and $d_{xy}$ by $+2\delta$. Five [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) then give

$$
\Delta E_{\rm elong}=4(-\delta)+1(2\delta)=-2\delta.
$$

For equal-magnitude compression the shifts reverse, and filling the lower $d_{xy}$ orbital first gives

$$
\Delta E_{\rm compress}=2(-2\delta)+3(\delta)=-\delta.
$$

The two singly occupied $e_g^*$ orbitals contribute zero net shift in either case. Thus **the elementary tetragonal splitting model favours elongation for high-spin $d^7$**, rather than equal electronic stabilization by the two signs. In a strictly [sigma-only octahedral molecular orbital model](../../../../../../sigma-only-octahedral-molecular-orbital-model.md) $t_{2g}$ is nonbonding, so this weak effect is absent; it arises when the electrostatic and non-sigma interactions neglected in that diagram are restored. [spin-orbit coupling](../../../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md), unequal donors and [chelate](../../../../../../chelate.md) constraints can modify the actual distortion, so the argument does not establish which experimental Co–donor bonds must lengthen.
