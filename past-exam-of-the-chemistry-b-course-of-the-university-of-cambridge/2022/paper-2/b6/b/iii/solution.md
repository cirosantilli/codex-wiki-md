<h1 id="b6/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

[water](../../../../../../water.md) lies below [pyridine](../../../../../../pyridine.md) and [cyanide ligand](../../../../../../cyanide-ligand.md) in the [spectrochemical series](../../../../../../spectrochemical-series.md). At fixed [metal](../../../../../../metal.md) and dioxolene charges, replacement by [water](../../../../../../water.md) decreases $\Delta_o$ and favours the high-spin limits: **Co(II) remains $S=3/2$; Co(III) can become $S=2$ if the splitting falls below the pairing threshold**. Replacement by [cyanide](../../../../../../cyanide.md) increases $\Delta_o$ and favours **Co(III), $S=0$, and Co(II), $S=1/2$**. The two unchanged [semiquinonate ligands](../../../../../../semiquinonate-ligand.md) would then give the Co(II) form a combined independent-spin moment of $3\mu_B$. The ranking predicts the direction of the change; two substituted donors alone do not guarantee a Co(III) [spin crossover](../../../../../../spin-crossover.md), since all six donors determine its splitting. A shift of the valence-tautomer equilibrium is also possible and is separate from this fixed-charge comparison.
