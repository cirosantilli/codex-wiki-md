<h1 id="b6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Six inward-directed donor orbitals form [symmetry-adapted linear combinations](../../../../../symmetry-adapted-linear-combination.md) of $a_{1g}+e_g+t_{1u}$ symmetry. They mix respectively with [metal](../../../../../metal.md) $4s$, $(3d_{z^2},3d_{x^2-y^2})$ and $4p$ orbitals. Thus the sigma framework contains six occupied [bonding molecular orbitals](../../../../../bonding-orbital.md) and six corresponding [antibonding molecular orbitals](../../../../../antibonding-orbital.md). The [metal](../../../../../metal.md) $(d_{xy},d_{xz},d_{yz})$ orbitals have no sigma-compatible partner and remain nonbonding $t_{2g}$ orbitals in this model. The important metal-dominated [octahedral splitting](../../../../../octahedral-splitting.md) is

$$
\boxed{\Delta_o=\varepsilon(e_g^*)-\varepsilon(t_{2g}).}
$$

The $e_g^*$ orbitals point directly toward the donors; the $t_{2g}$ orbitals point between them. The original diagram shows the entire sigma framework, rather than treating the five [metal](../../../../../metal.md) d levels as the whole molecular-orbital diagram.

For the same donor set and approximately the same geometry, **$\Delta_o(\mathrm{Co^{III}})>\Delta_o(\mathrm{Co^{II}})$**. The higher [metal](../../../../../metal.md) charge usually shortens the [metal](../../../../../metal.md)–donor distances and strengthens the interaction that raises $e_g^*$. This comparison holds other chemical variables fixed: changing the ligand at the same time could reverse a comparison between two unrelated complexes.
