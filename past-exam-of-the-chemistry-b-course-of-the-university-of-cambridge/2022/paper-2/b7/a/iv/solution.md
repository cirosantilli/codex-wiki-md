<h1 id="b7/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

With oxide charge $-2$ and four [thiocyanates](../../../../../../thiocyanate.md) each $-1$, $x-2-4=-2$ gives **V(IV), $d^1$**. Counting the terminal oxo as $X_2$ and each [thiocyanate](../../../../../../thiocyanate.md) as X gives

$$
\boxed{N_e=1+4+4(2)=13.}
$$

The expected structure is **[square-pyramidal molecular geometry](../../../../../../square-pyramidal-molecular-geometry.md)**, with the short V=O unit at the apex and the four [thiocyanate](../../../../../../thiocyanate.md) donors in the basal plane. The remaining d [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) can occupy an approximately basal $d_{xy}$ orbital that avoids the strongest donor directions and the oxo pi-antibonding orbitals. This is a high-oxidation-state oxovanadium complex; reaching 18 [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) is not its governing stability criterion. The displayed $\mathrm{SCN}$ composition alone does not establish N versus S linkage, and that choice does not change this count. The stated 13-electron result consistently uses a four-electron $\mathrm{M{=}O}$ convention; counting extra oxide pi-donor interactions as additional ligand [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) requires a different convention throughout.
