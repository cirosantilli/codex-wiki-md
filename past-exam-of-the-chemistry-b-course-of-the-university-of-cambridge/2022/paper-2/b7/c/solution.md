<h1 id="b7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Oxidizing [cobaltocene](../../../../../cobaltocene.md) removes its antibonding nineteenth [electron](../../../../../electron.md) and gives the particularly favourable 18-electron [cobaltocenium](../../../../../cobaltocenium.md) [cation](../../../../../cation.md). [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) can accept the [electrons](../../../../../electron.md), giving **[cobaltocenium](../../../../../cobaltocenium.md) iodide** at the simple [stoichiometry](../../../../../stoichiometry.md)

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm{CoCp_2}+\mathrm I_2\longrightarrow2[\mathrm{CoCp_2}]^+\mathrm I^- .}
$$

Each [cobalt](../../../../../cobalt.md) changes from Co(II), $d^7$, to Co(III), $d^6$; each [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) atom changes from 0 to $-1$. Excess [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) can change the isolated counterion to a polyiodide such as $\mathrm I_3^-$. In contrast, oxidation of 18-electron [ferrocene](../../../../../ferrocene.md) would remove an [electron](../../../../../electron.md) from its occupied closed-shell manifold to form 17-electron ferrocenium. [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) is insufficient to drive that reaction under the conditions implicit here. [ferrocene](../../../../../ferrocene.md) can still be oxidized by suitable stronger oxidants; an [electron](../../../../../electron.md) count is a useful rationale for the comparative redox behaviour, not a universal proof of a numerical electrode potential.
