<h1 id="b8/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The opposite insertion orientation gives a benzylic [metal](../../../../../../metal.md)–[carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) bond, $\mathrm{Co{-}CH(Ph)CH_3}$. C–Si [reductive elimination](../../../../../../reductive-elimination.md) then gives the branched product

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{PhCH(SiR_3)CH_3}.}
$$

A plausible explanation is stabilization of the developing and final benzylic Co–C interaction by the adjacent aromatic pi system, which can outweigh the steric preference for a primary alkyl. That makes an electronically different choice from an ordinary alkyl substituent. This is a mechanistic rationale for the proposed cycle, not proof of its rate-determining step. In the absence of a chiral [catalyst](../../../../../../catalyst.md) or another source of asymmetric induction, the new stereogenic [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) would give a [racemic mixture](../../../../../../racemic-mixture.md).
