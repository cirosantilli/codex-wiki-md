The larger value for the carbonyl bond means that its dissociation costs **$167\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ more** for the supplied comparison. A carbonyl combines a strong [sigma bond](../../../../../sigma-bond.md) with efficient pi overlap between spatially compatible C 2p and O 2p orbitals. This gives a substantial localized multiple-bond contribution.

The short P–O linkage in a [phosphine oxide](../../../../../phosphine-oxide.md) is also strong, but it is more polar, with a substantial $\mathrm{P^+{-}O^-}$ contribution. P 3p and O 2p orbitals have less favourable radial and energy matching than C 2p/O 2p for a simple localized [pi bond](../../../../../pi-bond.md). [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) donation into P–R antibonding orbitals adds stabilization, but it is not identical to the carbonyl [pi bond](../../../../../pi-bond.md). Therefore **the formal double-bond notation does not imply equal bond strength**. A substantial P-3d contribution is not needed to explain the bonding. The stated bond-dissociation values compare complete dissociation processes, so their magnitudes should not be derived solely from one overlap integral or from the two atoms' electronegativity difference.
