<h1 id="b9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Join three trigonal BO$_3$ units corner to corner. The resulting **alternating six-membered B–O ring has one terminal [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) on each boron**:

$$
\boxed{\left[\mathrm{{-}B(O^-){-}O{-}B(O^-){-}O{-}B(O^-){-}O{-}}\right]^{3-}_{\rm cyclic}.}
$$

The superscript records the total charge; the three explicit O$^-$ labels each carry one share of it. Atom counting gives three B, three bridging O and three terminal O. Boron is three-coordinate and approximately trigonal planar. The original structure diagram shows the ring connectivity and terminal atoms.

A planar arrangement permits the perpendicular empty boron p orbitals to accept [pi donation](../../../../../pi-donation.md) from suitable [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) [lone pairs](../../../../../lone-pair.md). The terminal [oxygens](../../../../../oxygen.md) can donate effectively toward one boron, giving their B–O linkages more multiple-bond character and explaining why they are shorter than bridging B–O linkages. A bridging [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) shares its interactions between two boron centres. **An approximately planar ring is therefore expected** from the trigonal units and favourable orbital alignment. This reasoning does not require describing the entire [anion](../../../../../anion.md) as benzene-like with an assumed six-electron [aromatic ring](../../../../../aromatic-ring.md); the [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) [lone pairs](../../../../../lone-pair.md) and terminal donations must actually be counted before making an [aromaticity](../../../../../aromaticity.md) claim.
