<h1 id="b9/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**$\mathrm{OP(OMe)_3}$ also has approximately [tetrahedral molecular geometry](../../../../../../tetrahedral-molecular-geometry.md) at [phosphorus](../../../../../../phosphorus.md)**, now with three P–O–C sigma linkages and a terminal phosphoryl O. Only the latter is being compared with the terminal O of Me$_3$PO; the three P–O–C bonds are chemically distinct.

The electronegative alkoxy substituents withdraw [electron density](../../../../../../electron-density.md) from P and improve its capacity to accept donation from terminal O into the surrounding antibonding framework. The elementary substituent argument therefore predicts **a stronger, stiffer terminal P–O bond in $\mathrm{OP(OMe)_3}$ than in $\mathrm{Me_3PO}$**. A description invoking polar bonding and [negative hyperconjugation](../../../../../../negative-hyperconjugation.md) avoids assigning a literal localized d-orbital double bond solely because the formula is written P=O.

Measure the terminal P–O stretching vibration by infrared or [Raman spectroscopy](../../../../../../raman-spectroscopy.md) under comparable, non-hydrogen-bonding conditions. In an approximate local-mode comparison the [reduced mass](../../../../../../reduced-mass.md) is the same, so a larger terminal stretching wavenumber indicates a larger force constant, $k\propto\widetilde\nu^2$. Mode assignment can be checked by $^{18}$O substitution at the terminal [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) or by a normal-mode calculation; otherwise an alkoxy P–O–C vibration could be compared by mistake. X-ray diffraction can supply a supporting bond-length comparison, but neither [bond length](../../../../../../bond-length.md) nor a single coupled vibrational frequency directly measures an isolated bond-dissociation enthalpy.
