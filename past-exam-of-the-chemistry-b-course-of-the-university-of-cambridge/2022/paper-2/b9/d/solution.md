<h1 id="b9/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Three PO$_4$ tetrahedra share corners in a ring. Each [phosphorus](../../../../../phosphorus.md) has two ring-bridging [oxygens](../../../../../oxygen.md) and two terminal [oxygens](../../../../../oxygen.md), so

$$
\boxed{\left[\mathrm{{-}P(O)(O^-){-}O{-}P(O)(O^-){-}O{-}P(O)(O^-){-}O{-}}\right]^{3-}_{\rm cyclic}}
$$

is one conventional Lewis representation of [trimetaphosphate](../../../../../trimetaphosphate.md). Its three P and nine O atoms consist of three bridging and six terminal O atoms. Within each phosphate unit the two terminal bonds have delocalized character; drawing one P=O and one P–O$^-$ is not a claim that every free [anion](../../../../../anion.md) has permanently different terminal [oxygens](../../../../../oxygen.md).

**A puckered six-membered P–O ring is expected.** The tetrahedral [phosphorus](../../../../../phosphorus.md) geometry and P–O–P torsional preferences allow folding without losing a required continuous perpendicular-p orbital system. This differs from the planar trigonal-boron arrangement. Tetrahedral P alone is not a geometrical proof of nonplanarity, since its two ring bonds could lie in a plane; the argument is that puckering is energetically accessible and relieves conformational strain without sacrificing a strong planar pi-conjugation benefit. The precise chair-like or boat-like distortion depends on environment and counterions.
