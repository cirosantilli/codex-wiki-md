<h1 id="b5/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Association in solution includes replacement of solute–[solvent](../../../../../../solvent.md) contacts, not just formation of the isolated dimer.** [cyclohexane](../../../../../../cyclohexane.md) solvates the [benzene](../../../../../../benzene.md) surface well through dispersion. Much of the London dispersion gained on bringing two [benzene](../../../../../../benzene.md) [molecules](../../../../../../molecule-split.md) together replaces comparable [benzene](../../../../../../benzene.md)–[cyclohexane](../../../../../../cyclohexane.md) contacts, leaving only a modest net gain before the translational and orientational [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) penalty. [cyclohexane](../../../../../../cyclohexane.md) cannot provide the complementary donor/acceptor interactions available when two [waters](../../../../../../water.md) [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md)-bond, so [water](../../../../../../water.md) association gains a more distinctive interaction relative to its separated solvated monomers.

Using the same concentration [standard state](../../../../../../standard-state.md), $K^\circ=K_c c^\circ$ and $\Delta G^\circ_{\rm assoc}=-RT\ln K^\circ$. For illustration at 298 K and $c^\circ=1\ \mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, the ratio of the two supplied [association constants](../../../../../../association-constant.md) corresponds to

$$
\Delta G^\circ_{\rm water}-\Delta G^\circ_{\rm benzene}=-RT\ln(1/0.03)\simeq\boxed{-8.69\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}}.
$$

Thus the [water](../../../../../../water.md)-[molecule](../../../../../../molecule-split.md) pair can associate more favourably in this [solvent](../../../../../../solvent.md) even though the stipulated isolated-pair binding energies are similar. Gas-phase potential energy alone omits both differential [solvation](../../../../../../solvation.md) and the [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) of association.
