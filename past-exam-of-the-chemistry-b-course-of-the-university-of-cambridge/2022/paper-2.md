## B6

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="b6/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#b6)

<h4 id="b6/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b6/a)

Six inward-directed donor orbitals form [symmetry-adapted linear combinations](../../chemistry.md#symmetry-adapted-linear-combination) of $a_{1g}+e_g+t_{1u}$ symmetry. They mix respectively with [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) $4s$, $(3d_{z^2},3d_{x^2-y^2})$ and $4p$ orbitals. Thus the sigma framework contains six occupied [bonding molecular orbitals](../../chemistry.md#bonding-orbital) and six corresponding [antibonding molecular orbitals](../../chemistry.md#antibonding-orbital). The [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) $(d_{xy},d_{xz},d_{yz})$ orbitals have no sigma-compatible partner and remain nonbonding $t_{2g}$ orbitals in this model. The important metal-dominated [octahedral splitting](../../chemistry.md#octahedral-splitting) is

$$
\boxed{\Delta_o=\varepsilon(e_g^*)-\varepsilon(t_{2g}).}
$$

The $e_g^*$ orbitals point directly toward the donors; the $t_{2g}$ orbitals point between them. The original diagram shows the entire sigma framework, rather than treating the five [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) d levels as the whole molecular-orbital diagram.

<a id="b6/a/image-sigma-only-octahedral-orbitals-and-the-donation-backbonding-channels-of-end-on-dinitrogen"></a>
![](../../past-exam-of-the-chemistry-b-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2022/paper-2-orbitals.png)

**[Figure 1](#b6/a/image-sigma-only-octahedral-orbitals-and-the-donation-backbonding-channels-of-end-on-dinitrogen). Sigma-only octahedral orbitals and the donation/backbonding channels of end-on dinitrogen**.

For the same donor set and approximately the same geometry, **$\Delta_o(\mathrm{Co^{III}})>\Delta_o(\mathrm{Co^{II}})$**. The higher [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) charge usually shortens the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–donor distances and strengthens the interaction that raises $e_g^*$. This comparison holds other chemical variables fixed: changing the ligand at the same time could reverse a comparison between two unrelated complexes.

<h3 id="b6/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#b6)

<h4 id="b6/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b6/b)

<h5 id="b6/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b6/b/i)

Neutral [pyridine](../../chemical-substance.md#pyridine) contributes no [formal charge](../../covalent-bond.md#formal-charge). If $n$ of the two [dioxolene ligands](../../chemistry.md#dioxolene-ligand) are dianions, the combined ligand charge is $-2-n$, so the [cobalt](../../chemical-substance.md#cobalt) [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) is $2+n$. The distinct charge assignments are therefore

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{metal}&\text{two dioxolene ligands}\\ \hline
\mathrm{Co^{II}}&L^-+L^-\\
\mathrm{Co^{III}}&L^-+L^{2-}\\
\mathrm{Co^{IV}}&L^{2-}+L^{2-}
\end{array}}
$$

Interchanging the two ligand labels in the middle row does not make another charge combination. Co(IV) belongs in this unrestricted charge-balance list even though the subsequent magnetic analysis excludes it.

<h4 id="b6/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b6/b)

<h5 id="b6/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b6/b/ii)

A Co(III) centre has $d^6$, and charge balance requires one closed-shell [catecholate ligand](../../chemistry.md#catecholate-ligand) and one $S=1/2$ [semiquinonate ligand](../../chemistry.md#semiquinonate-ligand). For low-spin Co(III), $t_{2g}^6(e_g^*)^0$, the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) has $S=0$, giving an [independent-spin magnetic moment](../../electromagnetism.md#independent-spin-magnetic-moment) of

$$
\mu_{\rm eff}/\mu_B=2\sqrt{0+\tfrac12(\tfrac12+1)}=\sqrt3=1.732.
$$

Thus **$A^1$ contains low-spin Co(III), $S_{\rm Co}=0$**, with its observed moment residing on the [free radical](../../chemistry.md#radical-chemistry) ligand. High-spin Co(III), $t_{2g}^4(e_g^*)^2$, would have $S=2$ and give $\sqrt{27}=5.20$ under the stated independent-spin approximation, so it is inconsistent with this measurement.

For Co(II), $d^7$, charge balance instead requires two $S=1/2$ [semiquinonate ligands](../../chemistry.md#semiquinonate-ligand). High-spin Co(II) has $t_{2g}^5(e_g^*)^2$ and $S=3/2$, so

$$
\mu_{\rm eff}/\mu_B=2\sqrt{\tfrac32\tfrac52+2\left(\tfrac12\tfrac32\right)}=\sqrt{21}=4.583.
$$

Therefore **$A^2$ contains high-spin Co(II), $S_{\rm Co}=3/2$**. Low-spin Co(II), $t_{2g}^6(e_g^*)^1$, would give $\sqrt9=3.00$ when the two ligand [spins](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) are included. These four checks distinguish both [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) and [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) [spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin).

The larger [octahedral splitting](../../chemistry.md#octahedral-splitting) at Co(III) exceeds the effective [pairing energy](../../chemistry.md#pairing-energy) and favours a [low-spin complex](../../chemistry.md#low-spin-complex); the smaller [octahedral splitting](../../chemistry.md#octahedral-splitting) at Co(II) favours a [high-spin complex](../../chemistry.md#high-spin-complex) with occupation of both $e_g^*$ orbitals before further pairing. Interconversion also transfers an [electron](../../physics.md#electron) between [cobalt](../../chemical-substance.md#cobalt) and a [dioxolene ligand](../../chemistry.md#dioxolene-ligand), so this is [valence tautomerism](../../chemistry.md#valence-tautomerism), rather than a metal-only [spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) change. Squared moments add here because the supplied model treats the centres as independent; adding the three [spins](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) first would be a different, exchange-coupled model.

<h4 id="b6/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b6/b)

<h5 id="b6/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b6/b/iii)

[water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) lies below [pyridine](../../chemical-substance.md#pyridine) and [cyanide ligand](../../chemistry.md#cyanide-ligand) in the [spectrochemical series](../../chemistry.md#spectrochemical-series). At fixed [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) and dioxolene charges, replacement by [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) decreases $\Delta_o$ and favours the high-spin limits: **Co(II) remains $S=3/2$; Co(III) can become $S=2$ if the splitting falls below the pairing threshold**. Replacement by [cyanide](../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide) increases $\Delta_o$ and favours **Co(III), $S=0$, and Co(II), $S=1/2$**. The two unchanged [semiquinonate ligands](../../chemistry.md#semiquinonate-ligand) would then give the Co(II) form a combined independent-spin moment of $3\mu_B$. The ranking predicts the direction of the change; two substituted donors alone do not guarantee a Co(III) [spin crossover](../../chemistry.md#spin-crossover), since all six donors determine its splitting. A shift of the valence-tautomer equilibrium is also possible and is separate from this fixed-charge comparison.

<h4 id="b6/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b6/b)

<h5 id="b6/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b6/b/iv)

Treat each [semiquinonate ligand](../../chemistry.md#semiquinonate-ligand) as a symmetric O,O [chelate](../../chemistry.md#chelate), with the [free radical](../../chemistry.md#radical-chemistry) delocalized over the dioxolene framework. The ideal octahedral donor-connectivity structures are **one trans form and two mirror-related cis forms**, giving **three [stereoisomers](../../stereochemistry.md#stereoisomer)**. Trans refers to the two [pyridine](../../chemical-substance.md#pyridine) N donors, not to the two O atoms of a [chelate](../../chemistry.md#chelate), which must remain adjacent.

<a id="b6/b/iv/image-the-achiral-trans-arrangement-and-the-two-mirror-related-cis-donor-arrangements"></a>
![](../../past-exam-of-the-chemistry-b-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2022/paper-2-isomers.png)

**[Figure 2](#b6/b/iv/image-the-achiral-trans-arrangement-and-the-two-mirror-related-cis-donor-arrangements). The achiral trans arrangement and the two mirror-related cis donor arrangements**.

In the diagram, coloured curves join the two O donors belonging to one ligand. For an unambiguous coordinate specification, the trans form has Py at $+z,-z$, with [chelate](../../chemistry.md#chelate) pairs $(+x,+y)$ and $(-x,-y)$. A cis pair has Py at $+z,+x$, with [chelates](../../chemistry.md#chelate) $(-z,+y)$ and $(-x,-y)$; reflecting $y\mapsto-y$ gives the other cis form. Proper rotations cannot superpose those two cis arrangements. Ring [conformations](../../stereochemistry.md#conformation) and [pyridine](../../chemical-substance.md#pyridine) rotations are not extra donor-connectivity [isomers](../../chemistry.md#isomer).

<h4 id="b6/b/v">v</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b6/b)

<h5 id="b6/b/v/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#b6/b/v)

The Co(III) centre in $A^1$ has $t_{2g}^6(e_g^*)^0$: every occupied d subset is orbitally balanced. **There is no first-order metal-centred Jahn–Teller driving force in $A^1$**. This does not require all six experimental [bond lengths](../../chemical-bond.md#bond-length) to be equal, since [pyridine](../../chemical-substance.md#pyridine) and dioxolene are different donors.

The Co(II) centre in $A^2$ has $t_{2g}^5(e_g^*)^2$. Its two $e_g^*$ orbitals each contain one [electron](../../physics.md#electron), so that subset gives no first-order preference. The $t_{2g}$ hole does give a weaker Jahn–Teller instability. To compare tetragonal distortions, take a positive small splitting parameter $\delta$ and retain each subset's [orbital energy barycentre](../../chemistry.md#orbital-energy-barycentre). On axial elongation let $d_{xz},d_{yz}$ shift by $-\delta$ and $d_{xy}$ by $+2\delta$. Five [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) then give

$$
\Delta E_{\rm elong}=4(-\delta)+1(2\delta)=-2\delta.
$$

For equal-magnitude compression the shifts reverse, and filling the lower $d_{xy}$ orbital first gives

$$
\Delta E_{\rm compress}=2(-2\delta)+3(\delta)=-\delta.
$$

The two singly occupied $e_g^*$ orbitals contribute zero net shift in either case. Thus **the elementary tetragonal splitting model favours elongation for high-spin $d^7$**, rather than equal electronic stabilization by the two signs. In a strictly [sigma-only octahedral molecular orbital model](../../chemistry.md#sigma-only-octahedral-molecular-orbital-model) $t_{2g}$ is nonbonding, so this weak effect is absent; it arises when the electrostatic and non-sigma interactions neglected in that diagram are restored. [spin-orbit coupling](../../atomic-physics.md#spin-orbit-interaction), unequal donors and [chelate](../../chemistry.md#chelate) constraints can modify the actual distortion, so the argument does not establish which experimental Co–donor bonds must lengthen.

<h3 id="b6/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#b6)

<h4 id="b6/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b6/c)

Four equivalents of base remove the four carboxylic-acid protons from two ligand molecules, giving two dianionic [pyridine-2,5-dicarboxylate](../../chemical-substance.md#pyridine-2-5-dicarboxylate) linkers per Pt/Mn pair. The ring [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) and neighbouring 2-carboxylate supply a five-membered N,O [chelate](../../chemistry.md#chelate). A consistent chain has **Pt(II), N$_2$O$_2$, with [square planar molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#square-planar-molecular-geometry), and Mn(II), O$_6$, with [octahedral molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#octahedral-molecular-geometry)**.

At each Pt, two ligands bind through their ring N and one O of the adjacent 2-carboxylate; their remote 5-carboxylates point toward successive Mn sites. At each Mn, a remote [carboxylate](../../chemical-substance.md#carboxylate) from each of two neighbouring Pt-containing units binds through both O atoms. Two coordinated [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) molecules complete Mn's six-coordinate sphere. The repeating connectivity, with each bracket denoting a single organic linker, is

$$
\cdots\mathrm{Pt}\;\underbrace{\bigl[\kappa^2(N,O)\!:\!L^{2-}\!:\!\kappa^2(O,O)\bigr]}_{\text{one linker}}\;\mathrm{Mn(H_2O)_2}\;\bigl[\kappa^2(O,O)\!:\!L^{2-}\!:\!\kappa^2(N,O)\bigr]\;\mathrm{Pt}\cdots .
$$

Each Pt has a second N,O [chelate](../../chemistry.md#chelate) extending in the opposite chain direction; each Mn similarly receives two [carboxylate](../../chemical-substance.md#carboxylate) [chelates](../../chemistry.md#chelate). The original donor-connectivity diagram makes those counts explicit.

<a id="b6/c/image-a-charge-balanced-pt-mn-chain-model-with-n-o-chelates-at-pt-and-oxygen-donors-at-mn"></a>
![](../../past-exam-of-the-chemistry-b-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2022/paper-2-chain.png)

**[Figure 3](#b6/c/image-a-charge-balanced-pt-mn-chain-model-with-n-o-chelates-at-pt-and-oxygen-donors-at-mn). A charge-balanced Pt–Mn chain model with N,O chelates at Pt and oxygen donors at Mn**.

Pt(II) is $5d^8$ and strongly favours [square planar molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#square-planar-molecular-geometry). Its affinity for the pyridyl N donor and the [chelate effect](../../chemistry.md#chelate-effect) select the mixed N,O pocket. Mn(II) is high-spin $3d^5$, is harder, and favours the [carboxylate](../../chemical-substance.md#carboxylate) and [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) O donors; six-coordinate [octahedral molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#octahedral-molecular-geometry) is natural in [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water). Charge balance per repeat is $+2+2+2(-2)=0$. A suitable repeat formulation is $\mathrm{[PtMn(L)_2(H_2O)_2]_n}$. This is a justified proposed coordination chain, not a unique crystal structure established from [stoichiometry](../../chemical-reaction.md#stoichiometry) alone; [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) content, [carboxylate](../../chemical-substance.md#carboxylate) bridging modes and actual porosity require structural characterization.

## B7

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="b7/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b7/a)

<h5 id="b7/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b7/a/i)

Four [chloride ligands](../../chemistry.md#chloride-ligand) give Ti(IV), $d^0$. Ionic [electron counting](../../chemistry.md#electron-counting) gives

$$
\boxed{N_e=0+4(2)=8.}
$$

**[tetrahedral molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#tetrahedral-molecular-geometry)** separates the four donors without placing them at $90^\circ$. A $d^0$ centre has no occupied [d orbitals](../../chemistry.md#d-orbital) providing a square-planar stabilization; the 18-electron rule is not a requirement for this early-metal compound.

<h4 id="b7/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b7/a)

<h5 id="b7/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b7/a/ii)

Six neutral [ammonia](../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) donors leave Cu(II), $d^9$, so

$$
\boxed{N_e=9+6(2)=21.}
$$

The donor skeleton has **[octahedral molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#octahedral-molecular-geometry) with a pronounced tetragonal Jahn–Teller distortion**, normally four shorter equatorial and two longer axial Cu–N distances. The $t_{2g}^6e_g^3$ occupation is orbitally degenerate in the ideal octahedral limit. An [electron](../../physics.md#electron) count above 18 is not a contradiction: [ammonia](../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) is primarily a sigma donor and the occupied metal-derived antibonding levels make this a weak-field coordination complex rather than a low-valent carbonyl closed shell.

<h4 id="b7/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b7/a)

<h5 id="b7/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b7/a/iii)

Neutral CO ligands require formal Cr($-\mathrm{II}$), hence $d^8$. With five two-electron [carbonyl ligands](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand),

$$
\boxed{N_e=8+5(2)=18.}
$$

A **[trigonal-bipyramidal molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#trigonal-bipyramidal-molecular-geometry)** is expected, analogous in [electron](../../physics.md#electron) count to $\mathrm{Fe(CO)_5}$. Strong [pi-acceptor ligands](../../chemistry.md#pi-acceptor-ligand) stabilize a low [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) through backbonding, and this five-coordinate structure accommodates the closed-shell 18-electron configuration. The negative [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) is a formal assignment; it does not mean that a free [chromium](../../chemical-substance.md#chromium) dianion is trapped inside the molecule.

<h4 id="b7/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b7/a)

<h5 id="b7/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b7/a/iv)

With oxide charge $-2$ and four [thiocyanates](../../chemical-substance.md#thiocyanate) each $-1$, $x-2-4=-2$ gives **V(IV), $d^1$**. Counting the terminal oxo as $X_2$ and each [thiocyanate](../../chemical-substance.md#thiocyanate) as X gives

$$
\boxed{N_e=1+4+4(2)=13.}
$$

The expected structure is **[square-pyramidal molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#square-pyramidal-molecular-geometry)**, with the short V=O unit at the apex and the four [thiocyanate](../../chemical-substance.md#thiocyanate) donors in the basal plane. The remaining d [electron](../../physics.md#electron) can occupy an approximately basal $d_{xy}$ orbital that avoids the strongest donor directions and the oxo pi-antibonding orbitals. This is a high-oxidation-state oxovanadium complex; reaching 18 [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) is not its governing stability criterion. The displayed $\mathrm{SCN}$ composition alone does not establish N versus S linkage, and that choice does not change this count. The stated 13-electron result consistently uses a four-electron $\mathrm{M{=}O}$ convention; counting extra oxide pi-donor interactions as additional ligand [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) requires a different convention throughout.

<h3 id="b7/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b7/b)

In ionic counting, two $\eta^5$ cyclopentadienyl [anions](../../chemistry.md#anion) donate $12$ [electrons](../../physics.md#electron). Therefore **[ferrocene](../../chemistry.md#ferrocene), [cobaltocene](../../chemistry.md#cobaltocene) and [nickelocene](../../chemistry.md#nickelocene) contain 18, 19 and 20 [valence electrons](../../chemical-bond.md#valence-electron)**, respectively: Fe(II) is $d^6$, Co(II) $d^7$ and Ni(II) $d^8$. [ferrocene](../../chemistry.md#ferrocene) fills the bonding and approximately nonbonding levels while leaving the next [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–ring antibonding pair empty. [cobaltocene](../../chemistry.md#cobaltocene) and [nickelocene](../../chemistry.md#nickelocene) add one and two [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) to that antibonding set.

Increasing antibonding occupation lowers the net [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–ring [bond order](../../chemistry.md#bond-order). It therefore explains the simultaneous **increase in M–C distance and decrease in dissociation enthalpy from Fe to Co to Ni**. The trend does not follow the shrinking size of the isolated [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) [ions](../../chemistry.md#ion); the orbital occupation is decisive here. The specified enthalpy separates the entire sandwich into a gaseous [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) dication and two gaseous ring [anions](../../chemistry.md#anion). It measures that complete ionic dissociation process, not ten independent localized M–C bond energies, so dividing it by ten is not a justified bond-energy determination.

<h3 id="b7/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b7/c)

Oxidizing [cobaltocene](../../chemistry.md#cobaltocene) removes its antibonding nineteenth [electron](../../physics.md#electron) and gives the particularly favourable 18-electron [cobaltocenium](../../chemistry.md#cobaltocenium) [cation](../../chemistry.md#cation). [iodine](../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) can accept the [electrons](../../physics.md#electron), giving **[cobaltocenium](../../chemistry.md#cobaltocenium) iodide** at the simple [stoichiometry](../../chemical-reaction.md#stoichiometry)

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm{CoCp_2}+\mathrm I_2\longrightarrow2[\mathrm{CoCp_2}]^+\mathrm I^- .}
$$

Each [cobalt](../../chemical-substance.md#cobalt) changes from Co(II), $d^7$, to Co(III), $d^6$; each [iodine](../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) atom changes from 0 to $-1$. Excess [iodine](../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) can change the isolated counterion to a polyiodide such as $\mathrm I_3^-$. In contrast, oxidation of 18-electron [ferrocene](../../chemistry.md#ferrocene) would remove an [electron](../../physics.md#electron) from its occupied closed-shell manifold to form 17-electron ferrocenium. [iodine](../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) is insufficient to drive that reaction under the conditions implicit here. [ferrocene](../../chemistry.md#ferrocene) can still be oxidized by suitable stronger oxidants; an [electron](../../physics.md#electron) count is a useful rationale for the comparative redox behaviour, not a universal proof of a numerical electrode potential.

<h3 id="b7/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b7/d)

The anionic [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) donor replaces iodide to give

$$
\boxed{A=\mathrm{CpFe(CO)_2(\kappa N\!\text{-}C_4H_4N)},\qquad \text{byproduct}=\mathrm{KI}.}
$$

Here Cp denotes $\eta^5$ cyclopentadienyl and $\kappa N$ specifies attachment through the ring [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) of the pyrrolyl [anion](../../chemistry.md#anion), $\mathrm{C_4H_4N^-}$. Fe(II), $d^6$, receives six [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) from Cp, four from the two CO ligands and two from an N-bound [pyrrolyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#pyrrolyl-ligand): $6+6+4+2=18$.

Two coupled CO stretching modes, approximately symmetric and antisymmetric combinations, account for the two strong carbonyl-region absorptions. The bent two-CO arrangement of this [piano-stool complex](../../chemistry.md#piano-stool-complex) permits both combinations to be infrared active. They are not evidence for two different chemical products.

Heating expels **two CO molecules** and changes the binding of the [pyrrolyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#pyrrolyl-ligand) from $\eta^1$ to $\eta^5$, giving the sandwich **[azaferrocene](../../chemistry.md#azaferrocene)**,

$$
\boxed{B=\mathrm{Fe(\eta^5-C_5H_5)(\eta^5-C_4H_4N)}.}
$$

The four [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) lost with CO are replaced by the four additional [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) donated on changing the [hapticity](../../chemistry.md#hapticity) of the [pyrrolyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#pyrrolyl-ligand). Thus $B$ is also Fe(II), $d^6$, and 18-electron: $6+6+6=18$. Each complex has one time-averaged Cp proton environment, two pairs of protons from the [pyrrolyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#pyrrolyl-ligand), one adjacent to N and one farther from N. Hence **three proton environments, with integrals $5:2:2$**, fit both structures. For [proton nuclear magnetic resonance](../../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#proton-nuclear-magnetic-resonance), three signals means three [chemical shift](../../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#chemical-shift) environments; the individual resonances can still show coupling.

<h4 id="b8/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b8/c)

<h5 id="b8/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b8/c/ii)

[oxidative addition](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidative-addition) of an intact Si–H bond initially places **H and SiR$_3$ cis** in the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) coordination sphere; this is the local arrangement generated by addition of a [sigma bond](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-bond). [hydride](../../chemical-substance.md#hydride) and [alkene](../../chemical-substance.md#alkene) must also attain adjacent sites for intramolecular insertion, and the eventual alkyl and silyl groups must be cis for direct [reductive elimination](../../chemical-reaction.md#reductive-elimination). Subsequent rearrangements should not be confused with the [stereochemistry](../../stereochemistry.md) of the bond-addition event itself.

For an alkyl-substituted terminal [alkene](../../chemical-substance.md#alkene), insertion puts **Co on the terminal [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) and H on the substituted [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon)**, producing $\mathrm{Co{-}CH_2CH_2R}$. It avoids the more crowded secondary [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–alkyl arrangement. Replacement of that Co–C bond by C–Si therefore gives **the linear product $\mathrm{RCH_2CH_2SiR_3}$**, an [Anti-Markovnikov addition](../../chemical-reaction.md#anti-markovnikov-addition) outcome. Hydrometallation is syn at the coordinated [alkene](../../chemical-substance.md#alkene), although this simple terminal substrate generally does not retain two substituents whose relative [stereochemistry](../../stereochemistry.md) could diagnose the complete sequence.

The larger value for the carbonyl bond means that its dissociation costs **$167\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ more** for the supplied comparison. A carbonyl combines a strong [sigma bond](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-bond) with efficient pi overlap between spatially compatible C 2p and O 2p orbitals. This gives a substantial localized multiple-bond contribution.

The short P–O linkage in a [phosphine oxide](../../chemical-substance.md#phosphine-oxide) is also strong, but it is more polar, with a substantial $\mathrm{P^+{-}O^-}$ contribution. P 3p and O 2p orbitals have less favourable radial and energy matching than C 2p/O 2p for a simple localized [pi bond](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond). [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) donation into P–R antibonding orbitals adds stabilization, but it is not identical to the carbonyl [pi bond](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond). The stated bond-dissociation values compare complete dissociation processes, so their magnitudes should not be derived solely from one overlap integral or from the two atoms' electronegativity difference.

Join three trigonal BO$_3$ units corner to corner. The resulting **alternating six-membered B–O ring has one terminal [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) on each boron**:

$$
\boxed{\left[\mathrm{{-}B(O^-){-}O{-}B(O^-){-}O{-}B(O^-){-}O{-}}\right]^{3-}_{\rm cyclic}.}
$$

The superscript records the total charge; the three explicit O$^-$ labels each carry one share of it. Atom counting gives three B, three bridging O and three terminal O. Boron is three-coordinate and approximately trigonal planar. The original structure diagram shows the ring connectivity and terminal atoms.

A planar arrangement permits the perpendicular empty boron p orbitals to accept [pi donation](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-donation) from suitable [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [lone pairs](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair). The terminal [oxygens](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) can donate effectively toward one boron, giving their B–O linkages more multiple-bond character and explaining why they are shorter than bridging B–O linkages. A bridging [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) shares its interactions between two boron centres. **An approximately planar ring is therefore expected** from the trigonal units and favourable orbital alignment. This reasoning does not require describing the entire [anion](../../chemistry.md#anion) as benzene-like with an assumed six-electron [aromatic ring](../../molecule.md#aromatic-ring); the [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [lone pairs](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) and terminal donations must actually be counted before making an [aromaticity](../../molecule.md#aromaticity) claim.

Three PO$_4$ tetrahedra share corners in a ring. Each [phosphorus](../../chemical-substance.md#phosphorus) has two ring-bridging [oxygens](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) and two terminal [oxygens](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen), so

$$
\boxed{\left[\mathrm{{-}P(O)(O^-){-}O{-}P(O)(O^-){-}O{-}P(O)(O^-){-}O{-}}\right]^{3-}_{\rm cyclic}}
$$

is one conventional Lewis representation of [trimetaphosphate](../../chemical-substance.md#trimetaphosphate). Its three P and nine O atoms consist of three bridging and six terminal O atoms. **A puckered six-membered P–O ring is expected.** The tetrahedral [phosphorus](../../chemical-substance.md#phosphorus) geometry and P–O–P torsional preferences allow folding without losing a required continuous perpendicular-p orbital system. This differs from the planar trigonal-boron arrangement. Tetrahedral P alone is not a geometrical proof of nonplanarity, since its two ring bonds could lie in a plane; the argument is that puckering is energetically accessible and relieves conformational strain without sacrificing a strong planar pi-conjugation benefit. The precise chair-like or boat-like distortion depends on environment and counterions.

Next, [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) uses a [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) to remove the alpha proton; the C$_\alpha$–H [electron pair](../../chemical-bond.md#electron-pair) forms the C$_\alpha$=C$_\beta$ [pi bond](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond). This second [transition state](../../chemical-kinetics.md#transition-state) has partly broken C$_\alpha$–H, partly formed water-O–H, and a partly formed C$_\alpha$–C$_\beta$ [pi bond](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond). Positive charge is transferred from the organic [cation](../../chemistry.md#cation) toward the incoming [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water)/[hydronium](../../chemistry.md#hydronium) unit. [hydroxide](../../chemistry.md#hydroxide) from the first step is a spectator in the second.

For **E1cb**, [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) first removes the alpha proton to form a resonance-stabilized [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate) plus [hydronium](../../chemistry.md#hydronium):

$$
\mathrm{CH_3C(O^-){=}CHCH(OH)CH_3\ \leftrightarrow\ CH_3COC^-(H)CH(OH)CH_3}.
$$

The first [transition state](../../chemical-kinetics.md#transition-state) has partly broken C$_\alpha$–H and partly formed O$_{\rm water}$–H, with developing negative charge delocalized over the alpha-carbon/carbonyl-oxygen system and positive charge on the proton-accepting [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) unit. The [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate) then expels [hydroxide](../../chemistry.md#hydroxide): use the carbon-centred contributor to send its alpha-carbon [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) into C$_\alpha$–C$_\beta$, while the C$_\beta$–O pair goes to [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). This second [transition state](../../chemical-kinetics.md#transition-state) has a partly formed [alkene](../../chemical-substance.md#alkene) [pi bond](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond) and partly broken C$_\beta$–O, with negative charge moving from the [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate) framework onto the departing [hydroxide](../../chemistry.md#hydroxide). [hydronium](../../chemistry.md#hydronium) is a spectator in this second step.

For **E2**, [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) removes the alpha proton as [hydroxide](../../chemistry.md#hydroxide) leaves in the same elementary step. Three electron-pair movements are simultaneous: [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) to H$_\alpha$, C$_\alpha$–H to the C$_\alpha$–C$_\beta$ [pi bond](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond), and C$_\beta$–O to O. Its single [transition state](../../chemical-kinetics.md#transition-state) therefore contains **all three changing bonds**: C$_\alpha$–H, water-O–H and C$_\beta$–O, together with the developing [pi bond](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond). The departing O becomes negative, the proton-accepting [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) unit becomes positive, and partial charge at [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) depends on the synchrony of proton loss and leaving-group departure. The reacting [conformation](../../stereochemistry.md#conformation) should align C$_\alpha$–H and C$_\beta$–O approximately in an [antiperiplanar conformation](../../stereochemistry.md#antiperiplanar-conformation) for efficient elimination.

These are formal limiting pathways for the displayed net process, not three claims of equal [reaction rates](../../chemical-kinetics.md#reaction-rate) in neutral [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water). In particular, unassisted loss of OH$^-$ to a secondary [carbocation](../../chemical-substance.md#carbocation) is strongly disfavoured, and [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) is a weak base for making an [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate). The [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate) pathway benefits from resonance stabilization and the final product is a conjugated [enone](../../chemical-substance.md#enone), but actual rate and dominant pathway depend on chemical conditions. The displayed [hydroxide](../../chemistry.md#hydroxide) and [hydronium](../../chemistry.md#hydronium) also rapidly neutralize each other; retaining them separately here tracks which bond cleavage supplied each species. No biological [catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#catalysis) is needed to describe these chemical mechanisms.
