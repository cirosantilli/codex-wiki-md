<h1 id="b4/b/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

**Each isomer has one favourable anomeric interaction.** A has the favourable axial exocyclic [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) in the upper-ring chair; B has it in the lower-ring chair. The NMR-constrained conformations therefore do not provide a doubly anomeric versus non-anomeric comparison.

Both can keep important [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) [substituents](../../../../../../substituent.md) equatorial and each obtains one anomeric stabilization. **Appreciable amounts of both are therefore a reasonable prediction**, rather than a strong preference based solely on counting anomeric interactions. The environments of the two ring [oxygens](../../../../../../oxygen.md) are not identical, so this argument does not determine an exact one-to-one equilibrium ratio; quantitative preference would require further energetic information.
