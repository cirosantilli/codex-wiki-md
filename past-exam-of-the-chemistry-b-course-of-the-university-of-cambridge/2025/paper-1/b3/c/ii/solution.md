<h1 id="b3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Only the low-coupling pair remains, so the detected product is the **trans [diastereomer](../../../../../../diastereomer.md)**, with approximate coupling 4 Hz. The reaction is highly diastereoselective within the spectrum's detection limit. Its possible absolute configurations are $(2R,3R)$ and $(2S,3S)$.

A conventional [proton](../../../../../../proton.md) NMR spectrum in an achiral environment cannot distinguish those [enantiomers](../../../../../../enantiomer.md): they have the same shifts and couplings. Thus a chiral catalyst plus one set of resonances does **not establish an [enantiomeric excess](../../../../../../enantiomeric-excess.md) or an [absolute configuration](../../../../../../absolute-configuration.md)**. [Chiral chromatography](../../../../../../chiral-chromatography.md), an appropriate chiral NMR method, or a calibrated stereochemical comparison would be needed.
