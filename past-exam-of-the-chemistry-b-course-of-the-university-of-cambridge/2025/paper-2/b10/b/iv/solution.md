<h1 id="b10/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**[finasteride](../../../../../../finasteride.md) is reduced at C1, leaving a C2 [enolate](../../../../../../enolate.md) that attacks nicotinamide C4 to make the covalent C–C adduct.** In the inhibitor's unsaturated lactam, the reactive chain is $\mathrm{O{=}C3{-}C2{=}C1}$, with N at steroid position 4. Activate the C3 carbonyl by hydrogen bonding; draw the reduced cofactor's C4–H bond toward inhibitor C1, the C1=C2 pi pair toward C2–C3, and the C3=O pi pair toward [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md). This gives [NADP+](../../../../../../nadp-plus.md) next to the C2-centered resonance form of the inhibitor [enolate](../../../../../../enolate.md).

Instead of completing reduction by protonating C2, move the C2 [lone pair](../../../../../../lone-pair.md) toward the electrophilic C4 of the oxidized nicotinamide ring. Shift the pyridinium pi system toward its positively charged N, and reform the inhibitor carbonyl if using the oxygen-centered [enolate](../../../../../../enolate.md) representation. The result is the **C2(inhibitor)–C4(nicotinamide) bond** and a neutral dihydronicotinamide ring. Numbering the two molecules separately prevents confusing cofactor C4 with the inhibitor's N4. The lactam N–H remains present and can help position the inhibitor through hydrogen bonding; it does not form a covalent bond to the protein.

This is [mechanism-based inhibition](../../../../../../mechanism-based-inhibition.md): enzyme chemistry generates the species that traps its own oxidized cofactor. Isolation of a cofactor–inhibitor adduct after protein denaturation, rather than persistent attachment to a denatured protein, distinguishes it from covalent modification of an amino acid side chain. The original mechanistic study combined radiolabel recovery, purification and spectroscopy to establish the adduct; its slow breakdown also cautions against treating “irreversible” as an absolute statement for every timescale.[1996, pp. 2361–2363, adduct isolation, structural analysis and Fig. 7](../../../../../../mechanism-based-inhibition-of-human-steroid-5-alpha-reductase-by-finasteride-enzyme-catalyzed-formation-of-nadp-dihydrofinasteride-a-potent-bisubstrate-analog-inhibitor-by-herbert-g-bull-and-others.md)
