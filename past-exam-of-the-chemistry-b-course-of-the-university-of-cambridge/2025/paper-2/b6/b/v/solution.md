<h1 id="b6/b/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Both complexes contain [cobalt](../../../../../../cobalt.md)(III), hence a $d^6$ metal ion. The approximately [octahedral molecular geometry](../../../../../../octahedral-molecular-geometry.md), relatively high metal [oxidation state](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md) and five [ammonia](../../../../../../ammonia.md) donors make the [octahedral splitting](../../../../../../octahedral-splitting.md) large enough that pairing is favorable. Their low-spin configuration is

$$
\boxed{t_{2g}^{6}e_g^{0},\qquad S=0.}
$$

All six metal $d$ [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) are paired, and the [ligands in coordination chemistry](../../../../../../ligand-in-coordination-chemistry.md) have closed shells, so neither complex has a permanent spin [magnetic moment](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md): both exhibit [diamagnetism](../../../../../../diamagnetism.md). The difference between N and O attachment is insufficient to change this low-spin assignment.
