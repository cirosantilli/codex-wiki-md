**Primary amines can favor geminal disubstitution through a proton-abstraction route.** The first substitution leaves an exocyclic $\mathrm{PCl(NHR)}$ group. Its remaining N–H [proton](../../../../../../proton.md) provides a pathway unavailable to a fully substituted amino group: base removes H, chloride elimination gives a reactive low-coordinate phosphorus intermediate with increased exocyclic P–N bonding, and a second amine attacks that same phosphorus. [proton](../../../../../../proton.md) transfer then gives $\mathrm{P(NHR)_2}$. Thus the directing effect is not simply the inductive effect of an amino group.

This needs a qualification: “primary amine” alone does not specify a universal product distribution. Substituent, solvent and base concentration affect whether the proton-abstraction route outcompetes direct [nucleophilic substitution](../../../../../../nucleophilic-substitution.md). Kinetic and trapping experiments with aromatic primary amines support the base-catalyzed route to geminal products, while also finding direct substitution pathways under other conditions.[1987, p. 585 abstract, solvent/base dependence and methanol trapping](../../../../../../studies-of-phosphazenes-part-31-by-sivapackia-ganapathiappan-and-setharampattu-s-krishnamurthy.md)
