**Secondary amines normally favor non-geminal disubstitution**, putting the second amino substituent on a different phosphorus. The first installed $\mathrm{NR_2}$ group donates [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) density to the P–N framework and deactivates its own phosphorus toward further attack. It is also bulkier than an initially installed primary-amino group. Crucially, $\mathrm{P{-}NR_2}$ has no N–H [proton](../../../../../../proton.md), so the proton-abstraction/chloride-elimination pathway that can direct a primary amine geminally is unavailable. Direct attack at a less-deactivated $\mathrm{PCl_2}$ site is favored. The resulting non-geminal products can have cis or trans relative orientations around the ring; [non-geminal substitution](../../../../../../non-geminal-substitution.md) alone does not select one of these [stereoisomers](../../../../../../stereoisomer.md). These are useful trends, with known substituent-dependent exceptions in experimentally characterized mixed derivatives.[2018, abstract, pyrrolidine/dimethylamine substitution of differently substituted rings](../../../../../../regiochemical-control-in-the-substitution-reactions-of-cyclotriphosphazene-derivatives-with-secondary-amines-by-serap-besli-and-others.md)
