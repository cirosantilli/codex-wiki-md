<h1 id="b9/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Replace the two chlorides on one ring phosphorus $P_1$ with the two ends of the diiron unit. **$D$ has a spiro $P_1\mathrm{Fe_2}$ triangle**, with an Fe–Fe bond, a $P_1$–Fe bond to each metal and four terminal CO ligands on each Fe. The other two phosphorus atoms retain their $\mathrm{PCl_2}$ groups. Its composition is $\mathrm{N_3P_3Cl_4Fe_2(CO)_8}$. In neutral [electron counting](../../../../../electron-counting.md), each metal has $8_{\mathrm{Fe}}+8_{4\mathrm{CO}}+1_{\mathrm{Fe-P}}+1_{\mathrm{Fe-Fe}}=18$.

**$E$ has a triangular Fe$_3$ cluster, one bridging CO, and an adjacent ring N$\to$Fe bond.** The two original metals remain bonded to $P_1$ and to one another, and each bonds to the added third Fe. Each Fe bears three terminal CO ligands; one additional [bridging carbonyl ligand](../../../../../bridging-carbonyl-ligand.md) spans the original Fe–Fe edge. A [nitrogen](../../../../../nitrogen.md) directly adjacent to $P_1$ donates its [lone pair](../../../../../lone-pair.md) to the third Fe. The formula is therefore $\mathrm{N_3P_3Cl_4Fe_3(CO)_{10}}$, satisfying $8+5-3=10$ retained CO ligands. The original diagram above gives all metal–metal and metal–ring connections.

The counts are an especially useful structural check. Each original Fe has $8+6_{3\mathrm{CO}}+1_{\mu\mathrm{CO}}+1_{\mathrm{P}}+2_{2\mathrm{Fe-Fe}}=18$. The added Fe has $8+6_{3\mathrm{CO}}+2_{\mathrm{N}}+2_{2\mathrm{Fe-Fe}}=18$. Omitting [nitrogen](../../../../../nitrogen.md) coordination leaves the third Fe two [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) short; omitting the bridging CO leaves both original metals one [electron](../../../../../electron.md) short.

In $D$, the two untouched phosphorus sites are equivalent: an $A_2X$ [phosphorus-31 nuclear magnetic resonance](../../../../../phosphorus-31-nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md) system gives their doublet with integral 2, and the unique $P_1$ triplet with integral 1. The two equivalent nuclei do not split each other. In $E$, coordination of one adjacent [nitrogen](../../../../../nitrogen.md) distinguishes the two sides of the ring, producing three inequivalent phosphorus sites. Each couples to the other two and gives a doublet of doublets in the first-order limit; equal couplings or strong coupling could distort this simple pattern.

The high-frequency [infrared spectroscopy](../../../../../infrared-spectroscopy.md) bands belong to terminal carbonyl stretching modes. The extra low-frequency band is diagnostic of a strongly backbonded [bridging carbonyl ligand](../../../../../bridging-carbonyl-ligand.md). The number of IR bands counts allowed, possibly overlapping [normal modes](../../../../../normal-mode.md), not individual CO ligands. Formula, [18-electron rule](../../../../../18-electron-rule.md), NMR equivalence and IR together select this model; the historical experimental work independently established diiron spiro rings and triiron products containing both P–Fe and N–Fe bonds.[1984, pp. 4966–4977, diiron compound 2, triiron compound 3, and conversion with [iron](../../../../../iron.md) carbonyls](../../../../../iron-containing-metallophosphazenes-and-their-clusters-derived-from-chlorophosphazenes-and-organometallic-dianions-by-harry-r-allcock-and-others.md)
