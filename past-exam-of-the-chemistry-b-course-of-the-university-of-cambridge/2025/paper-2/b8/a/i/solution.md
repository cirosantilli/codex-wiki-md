<h1 id="b8/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In $A$, the tridentate ligand contains one anionic [phenoxide ligand](../../../../../../phenoxide-ligand.md), a neutral secondary [amine](../../../../../../amine.md) with its N–H bond intact, and a neutral [phosphine ligand](../../../../../../phosphine-ligand.md). The three [chloride ligands](../../../../../../chloride-ligand.md) contribute another three negative charges. Consequently the neutral complex has **Ti(IV), $d^0$, and 12 valence [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md)**:

$$
\mathrm{OS(Ti)}=+4,\qquad \mathrm{VE}=0+2_{\mathrm O}+2_{\mathrm N}+2_{\mathrm P}+3(2)_{\mathrm{Cl}}=12.
$$

The N–H label is essential: counting this [nitrogen](../../../../../../nitrogen.md) as an anionic [amido ligand](../../../../../../amido-ligand.md) would give the wrong [oxidation state](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md). A six-coordinate early-metal complex need not obey the [18-electron rule](../../../../../../18-electron-rule.md).

In $B$, the substituted [cyclopentadienyl ligand](../../../../../../cyclopentadienyl-ligand.md) is $\eta^5$ and anionic, while the three-carbon bent ligand is an anionic $\eta^3$ [allyl ligand](../../../../../../allyl-ligand.md), a four-electron donor in ionic counting. The pendant [pyridine](../../../../../../pyridine.md) [nitrogen](../../../../../../nitrogen.md) is uncoordinated. Therefore **Mo(II), $d^4$, 18 [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md)** follows from

$$
\mathrm{VE}=4_{d}+6_{\eta^5\mathrm{Cp}}+4_{\eta^3\mathrm{allyl}}+2(2)_{\mathrm{CO}}=18.
$$

The curved delocalization mark across the three-carbon ligand must not be read as a separate alkene or alkyne.
