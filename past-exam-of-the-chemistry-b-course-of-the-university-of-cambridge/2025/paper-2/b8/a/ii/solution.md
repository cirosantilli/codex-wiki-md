<h1 id="b8/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A formal two-electron [reductive elimination](../../../../../../reductive-elimination.md) couples two anionic, mutually cis donors and reduces Ti(IV) to Ti(II). There are two chemically distinct couplings: **Cl–Cl elimination gives $\mathrm{Cl_2}$**, leaving a Ti(II) complex with the intact anionic O,N,P ligand and one chloride; **O–Cl elimination gives an aryl hypochlorite group, $\mathrm{ArOCl}$**, breaking the original Ti–O attachment and leaving a Ti(II) center coordinated by N,P and two chlorides. The latter product can remain tethered through the N,P part of the same ligand; it is not necessarily a free small molecule. The starting facial arrangement supplies cis pairs of both kinds.

**Cl–Cl coupling is the more plausible of these formal alternatives**, because it retains the particularly strong early-metal Ti–O interaction and makes the stronger Cl–Cl rather than relatively weak O–Cl bond. Both alternatives reduce the valence count by two, from 12 to 10, so the [18-electron rule](../../../../../../18-electron-rule.md) does not choose between them. Neither should be asserted to occur readily: reduction of a stable, oxophilic $d^0$ Ti(IV) center is intrinsically unfavorable. Coupling a chloride to the neutral amine or phosphine is not the ordinary elimination of two X-type ligands. [proton](../../../../../../proton.md) transfer between N–H and [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md), if it occurred, would instead be [acid-base chemistry](../../../../../../acid-base-chemistry-split.md), not automatically a two-electron [reductive elimination](../../../../../../reductive-elimination.md).
