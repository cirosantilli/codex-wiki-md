<h1 id="b8/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For an intact complex in the usual fast-conformational-averaging regime, counting distinct [chemical shifts](../../../../../../chemical-shift.md) rather than individual split lines gives **$C:9$, $D:11$, $E:10$ signals**.

In $C$, the uncoordinated pyridinium arm permits the paired positions of a monosubstituted [cyclopentadienyl ligand](../../../../../../cyclopentadienyl-ligand.md) to average: its four ring hydrogens give two sets, $2+2$. The linker $\mathrm{CH_2}$ gives one set, the four inequivalent pyridinium C–H positions give four, its N–H gives one, and the two symmetry-related bound [acetonitrile](../../../../../../acetonitrile.md) methyl groups give one. Hence $2+1+4+1+1=9$. The exchangeable N–H resonance may be broadened or absent experimentally; nine is the structural count before exchange removes it.

In the rigid cis-dicarbonyl chelates $D$ and $E$, the different pyridyl-N and fourth-leg donors destroy the operation that related the two sides of the substituted ring. Each of the four Cp hydrogens is distinct, and the two linker hydrogens are [diastereotopic](../../../../../../diastereotopic.md). Together with four [pyridine](../../../../../../pyridine.md) C–H sites, this gives $4+2+4=10$. The remaining bound [acetonitrile](../../../../../../acetonitrile.md) adds one methyl signal to $D$, giving 11; $E$ has none, giving 10. The two [enantiomers](../../../../../../enantiomer.md) of a racemate have identical spectra in an achiral solvent and do not double these counts. This prediction assumes resolved resonances and no rapid chelate opening that averages inequivalent sites; variable-temperature [proton nuclear magnetic resonance](../../../../../../proton-nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md) would test that assumption.
