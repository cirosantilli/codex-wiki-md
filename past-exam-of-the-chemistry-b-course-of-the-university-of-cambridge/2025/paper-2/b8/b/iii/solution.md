<h1 id="b8/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**The readily displaced [acetonitrile](../../../../../../acetonitrile.md) sites have both been exhausted.** A second chloride would have to displace a strongly bound [carbonyl ligand](../../../../../../carbonyl-ligand.md), open the [chelate ring](../../../../../../chelate-ring.md), or associate to an already 18-electron center to make an unfavorable crowded 20-electron species. The product is neutral, so further chloride uptake also lacks the charge-neutralization advantage of $D\to E$ and would generate an anionic complex if substitution occurred. The [chelate effect](../../../../../../chelate-effect.md) disfavors loss of the tethered [pyridine](../../../../../../pyridine.md), while strong [pi backbonding](../../../../../../pi-backbonding.md) helps retain CO under the stated conditions. These combined kinetic and bonding arguments explain the absence of further reaction. The [18-electron rule](../../../../../../18-electron-rule.md) alone would not forbid replacing CO by chloride, since that replacement is isoelectronic; it therefore cannot be the whole explanation.
