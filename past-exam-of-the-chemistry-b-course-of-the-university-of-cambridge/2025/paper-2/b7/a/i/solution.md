<h1 id="b7/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For [electron counting](../../../../../../electron-counting.md), use the ionic method: subtract the metal [oxidation state](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md) from its group number, then add two [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) for each neutral [phosphine ligand](../../../../../../phosphine-ligand.md) or [carbonyl ligand](../../../../../../carbonyl-ligand.md) and two for each [chloride ligand](../../../../../../chloride-ligand.md).

**$\mathrm{Ni(PPh_3)(CO)_3}$ is Ni(0), $d^{10}$, 18-electron and tetrahedral.** The count is $10+2+3(2)=18$. With only one distinct ligand in an $\mathrm{ML_3L'}$ [tetrahedral molecular geometry](../../../../../../tetrahedral-molecular-geometry.md), there is only one geometrical arrangement.

**$\mathrm{Rh(PPh_3)_2(CO)Cl}$ is Rh(I), $d^8$, 16-electron and square planar, with the two phosphines trans.** Its count is $8+2(2)+2+2=16$. A [square planar molecular geometry](../../../../../../square-planar-molecular-geometry.md) is characteristic of this low-spin $4d^8$ configuration. Opposite [triphenylphosphine](../../../../../../triphenylphosphine.md) groups avoid their largest mutual [steric hindrance](../../../../../../steric-hindrance.md); CO and chloride occupy the other trans pair.

**$\mathrm{Pd[P(^{t}Bu)_2Ph]_2Cl_2}$ is Pd(II), $d^8$, 16-electron and square planar, with the bulky phosphines trans.** Its count is likewise $8+4+4=16$. The two [di-tert-butylphenylphosphine](../../../../../../di-tert-butylphenylphosphine.md) groups are particularly crowded if cis. These predictions concern the favored intact four-coordinate species; ligand dissociation can occur under other conditions.
