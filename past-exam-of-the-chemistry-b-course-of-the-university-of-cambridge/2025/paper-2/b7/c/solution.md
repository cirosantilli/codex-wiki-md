<h1 id="b7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [dissociative substitution](../../../../../dissociative-substitution.md) begins with slow CO loss,

$$
\mathrm{M(CO)_5L}\xrightarrow{k_d}\mathrm{M(CO)_4L}+\mathrm{CO},
$$

followed by capture of the incoming [ligand in coordination chemistry](../../../../../ligand-in-coordination-chemistry.md). A more electron-releasing $L$ increases metal-to-CO [pi backbonding](../../../../../pi-backbonding.md) in the six-coordinate reactant, stabilizing it and strengthening its M–CO attachments. Removing CO sacrifices a useful pi acceptor from that electron-rich center. Thus **the activation free energy for CO dissociation often rises and the substitution slows**. Within an otherwise matched series, $k_d\propto\exp[-\Delta G^\ddagger/(RT)]$ captures this sensitivity. The rate depends on the relative energies of reactant and dissociation [transition state](../../../../../transition-state.md), so the trend is conditional: large changes in [steric hindrance](../../../../../steric-hindrance.md), geometry or mechanism can override the electronic effect.
