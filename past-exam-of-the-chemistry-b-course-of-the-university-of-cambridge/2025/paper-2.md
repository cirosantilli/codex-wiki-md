## B6

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="b6/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#b6)

<h4 id="b6/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b6/a)

The [nitrite](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrite) ion is an [ambidentate ligand](../../chemistry.md#ambidentate-ligand): its [Lewis structures](../../covalent-bond.md#lewis-structure) leave donor [lone pairs](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) on both [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) and [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). In the N-bound mode, $\mathrm{M{-}N(O)_2}$, the [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) makes a [sigma bond](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-bond) to the metal, while a suitable unoccupied [nitrite](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrite) [molecular orbital](../../chemistry.md#molecular-orbital) can accept metal $d$ [electron](../../physics.md#electron) density by [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding). Thus **N-bound nitro is a sigma donor and pi acceptor**. The [nitrite](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrite) group remains bent at [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen); N coordination does not make it a linear nitrosyl.

In the O-bound mode, $\mathrm{M{-}O{-}N{=}O}$, an [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) makes the metal–[oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [sigma bond](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-bond). A second occupied [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) orbital can donate sideways into an available metal orbital: **O-bound nitrito is a sigma and pi donor**, rather than the strong pi acceptor of the N-bound description. [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen)'s greater [electronegativity](../../electronegativity.md) lowers its donor orbital energies, but the occupied [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) orbitals still explain its pi-donor classification. Actual mixing depends on metal [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) and geometry.

A further possibility is [bidentate coordination](../../chemistry.md#bidentate-coordination), $\mathrm{M}(\kappa^2\!\text{-}O,O\text{-}\mathrm{NO_2})$, with both [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) atoms attached to one metal. Each attachment has oxygen-to-metal sigma donation; additional pi donation depends on orbital alignment. [nitrite](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrite) can also be a [bridging ligand](../../chemistry.md#bridging-ligand) between metals. Neither alternative should be confused with [linkage isomerism](../../chemistry.md#linkage-isomerism), which specifically compares attachment through different atoms at the same binding site.

<h4 id="b6/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b6/b)

<h5 id="b6/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b6/b/i)

**$A=[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(OH)}]^{2+}$, $B=[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(ONO)}]^{2+}$, $C=[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(NO_2)}]^{2+}$, and the by-product is $\mathrm{HNO_2}$.** The first transformation is [deprotonation](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#deprotonation) of coordinated [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water), not loss of an [ammonia](../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) ligand. The [hydroxo ligand](../../chemistry.md#hydroxo-ligand) in $A$ is then nitrosated at [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen), retaining its original Co–O attachment in the initially formed O-bound [nitrito ligand](../../chemistry.md#nitrito-ligand). Atom and [electric charge](../../electric-charge.md) balance give

$$
[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(OH)}]^{2+}+\mathrm{N_2O_3}
\longrightarrow[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5(ONO)}]^{2+}+\mathrm{HNO_2}.
$$

The [relative molecular mass](../../chemistry.md#relative-molecular-mass) of [nitrous acid](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#nitrous-acid) is approximately $1+14+2(16)=47$. Heating converts the O-bound [nitrito ligand](../../chemistry.md#nitrito-ligand) to the N-bound [nitro ligand](../../chemistry.md#nitro-ligand); these are [linkage isomers](../../chemistry.md#linkage-isomer), with Co remaining in [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) $+3$ throughout.

<h4 id="b6/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b6/b)

<h5 id="b6/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b6/b/ii)

**The N-bound isomer $C$ is the thermodynamically favored form; heating accelerates access to it.** Formation of $B$ retains the original metal–[oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) attachment and is a plausible [kinetic control](../../chemical-kinetics.md#kinetic-control) outcome. Rearranging that attachment requires overcoming an [activation free energy](../../chemical-kinetics.md#gibbs-energy-of-activation), especially at a substitutionally inert low-spin [cobalt](../../chemical-substance.md#cobalt)(III) center. Raising [temperature](../../temperature.md) speeds the [linkage isomerization](../../chemistry.md#linkage-isomerization), allowing the more stable N-bound [nitro ligand](../../chemistry.md#nitro-ligand) to accumulate. Its stronger [ligand field](../../chemistry.md#ligand-field) and different covalent bonding help rationalize the stability, but a larger field splitting alone is not a complete calculation of the isomerization [Gibbs free energy](../../thermodynamic-potential.md#gibbs-free-energy). The classical crystallographic comparison establishes the different attachments and thermodynamic direction.[1979, pp. 1869–1874, structural comparison and solid-state isomerization](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#nitrito-nitro-linkage-isomerization-in-the-solid-state-2-a-comparative-study-of-the-structures-of-nitrito-and-nitropentaamminecobalt-iii-dichloride-by-ingmar-grenthe-and-ellika-nordin)

<h4 id="b6/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b6/b)

<h5 id="b6/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b6/b/iii)

The relevant [spectrochemical series](../../chemistry.md#spectrochemical-series) order is O-bound nitrito $<$ [ammonia](../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) $<$ N-bound nitro. A [pi-donor ligand](../../chemistry.md#pi-donor-ligand) tends to raise the metal orbitals derived from $t_{2g}$, reducing the [octahedral splitting](../../chemistry.md#octahedral-splitting); a [pi-acceptor ligand](../../chemistry.md#pi-acceptor-ligand) stabilizes those orbitals, increasing the splitting. Since [photon energy](../../quantum-mechanics.md#photon-energy) is $E=hc/\lambda$, the stronger-field complex absorbs at the shorter [wavelength](../../wave-equation.md#wavelength). Thus

$$
\boxed{\lambda_C=458\ \mathrm{nm},\qquad
\lambda_{[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_6}]^{3+}}=474\ \mathrm{nm},\qquad
\lambda_B=486\ \mathrm{nm}.}
$$

The corresponding [spectroscopic wavenumbers](../../wave-equation.md#spectroscopic-wavenumber) are approximately $21834$, $21097$ and $20576\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$. These are the observed band energies, not three exact independent determinations of $\Delta_o$: low-spin $d^6$ transition energies also contain [electron repulsion](../../electric-charge.md#electron-repulsion) contributions, and replacing one ligand lowers the ideal octahedral [symmetry](../../physics.md#symmetry-physics). The relative assignment is nevertheless robust.

<h4 id="b6/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b6/b)

<h5 id="b6/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b6/b/iv)

**$B$ removes more blue-green light and appears orange-red; $C$ removes shorter-wavelength blue light and appears yellow.** The observed [color](../../optics.md#color) is the combined transmitted part of the visible spectrum, approximately complementary to the absorbed band, rather than the color corresponding to the absorption maximum itself. The longer-wavelength band of the O-bound [nitrito ligand](../../chemistry.md#nitrito-ligand) complex therefore shifts the transmitted balance toward orange/red compared with the N-bound [nitro ligand](../../chemistry.md#nitro-ligand) complex. Band width, other absorption bands, [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) and optical path length affect the shade and darkness; a single maximum does not quantitatively determine the perceived [color](../../optics.md#color).

<h4 id="b6/b/v">v</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b6/b)

<h5 id="b6/b/v/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#b6/b/v)

Both complexes contain [cobalt](../../chemical-substance.md#cobalt)(III), hence a $d^6$ metal ion. The approximately [octahedral molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#octahedral-molecular-geometry), relatively high metal [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) and five [ammonia](../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) donors make the [octahedral splitting](../../chemistry.md#octahedral-splitting) large enough that pairing is favorable. Their low-spin configuration is

$$
\boxed{t_{2g}^{6}e_g^{0},\qquad S=0.}
$$

All six metal $d$ [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) are paired, and the [ligands in coordination chemistry](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry) have closed shells, so neither complex has a permanent spin [magnetic moment](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-dipole-moment): both exhibit [diamagnetism](../../electromagnetism.md#diamagnetism). The difference between N and O attachment is insufficient to change this low-spin assignment.

<h4 id="b7/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b7/a)

<h5 id="b7/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b7/a/i)

For [electron counting](../../chemistry.md#electron-counting), use the ionic method: subtract the metal [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) from its group number, then add two [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) for each neutral [phosphine ligand](../../chemistry.md#phosphine-ligand) or [carbonyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand) and two for each [chloride ligand](../../chemistry.md#chloride-ligand).

**$\mathrm{Ni(PPh_3)(CO)_3}$ is Ni(0), $d^{10}$, 18-electron and tetrahedral.** The count is $10+2+3(2)=18$. With only one distinct ligand in an $\mathrm{ML_3L'}$ [tetrahedral molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#tetrahedral-molecular-geometry), there is only one geometrical arrangement.

**$\mathrm{Rh(PPh_3)_2(CO)Cl}$ is Rh(I), $d^8$, 16-electron and square planar, with the two phosphines trans.** Its count is $8+2(2)+2+2=16$. A [square planar molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#square-planar-molecular-geometry) is characteristic of this low-spin $4d^8$ configuration. Opposite [triphenylphosphine](../../chemical-substance.md#triphenylphosphine) groups avoid their largest mutual [steric hindrance](../../stereochemistry.md#steric-hindrance); CO and chloride occupy the other trans pair.

**$\mathrm{Pd[P(^{t}Bu)_2Ph]_2Cl_2}$ is Pd(II), $d^8$, 16-electron and square planar, with the bulky phosphines trans.** Its count is likewise $8+4+4=16$. The two [di-tert-butylphenylphosphine](../../chemical-substance.md#di-tert-butylphenylphosphine) groups are particularly crowded if cis. These predictions concern the favored intact four-coordinate species; ligand dissociation can occur under other conditions.

<h4 id="b7/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b7/a)

<h5 id="b7/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b7/a/ii)

The usual qualitative order of increasing [ligand field](../../chemistry.md#ligand-field) is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Cl^-}<\mathrm{P(^{t}Bu)_2Ph}<\mathrm{PPh_3}<\mathrm{CO}.}
$$

A [chloride ligand](../../chemistry.md#chloride-ligand) is a [sigma-donor ligand](../../chemistry.md#sigma-donor-ligand) and [pi-donor ligand](../../chemistry.md#pi-donor-ligand); its occupied pi orbitals raise the metal $t_{2g}$-derived levels and reduce the splitting. A [carbonyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand) is a particularly effective [pi-acceptor ligand](../../chemistry.md#pi-acceptor-ligand), lowering those levels and producing a large splitting. Both [phosphine ligands](../../chemistry.md#phosphine-ligand) are sigma donors with acceptor orbitals of mainly P–C [antibonding molecular orbital](../../chemistry.md#antibonding-orbital) character. Replacing [phenyl groups](../../chemical-substance.md#phenyl-group) by electron-releasing [tert-butyl groups](../../chemical-substance.md#tert-butyl-group) increases sigma basicity but generally weakens pi acceptance, so the alkyl-rich phosphine is placed below [triphenylphosphine](../../chemical-substance.md#triphenylphosphine) in this qualitative pi-bonding comparison. It would be wrong to equate a stronger sigma donor automatically with a stronger-field ligand, or to invoke empty phosphorus $3d$ orbitals as the essential acceptors.[1992, pp. 4391–4398, sigma basicity, pi acidity and phosphorus orbital participation](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#metal-phosphine-bonding-revisited-sigma-basicity-pi-acidity-and-the-role-of-phosphorus-d-orbitals-in-zerovalent-metal-phosphine-complexes-by-gianfranco-pacchioni-and-paul-s-bagus)

This is a qualitative [spectrochemical series](../../chemistry.md#spectrochemical-series) prediction, not a universal numerical ranking for all metals and geometries. The opposing sigma and pi trends mean that precise comparisons require matched complexes and measured splittings; a [Tolman electronic parameter](../../chemistry.md#tolman-electronic-parameter) measures net effects on CO backbonding and is not itself a ligand-field splitting.

<h3 id="b7/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b7/b)

<h5 id="b7/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b7/b/i)

A [carbonyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand) binds through [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon), whose occupied $5\sigma$ [molecular orbital](../../chemistry.md#molecular-orbital) has a substantial outward-facing [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) lobe. Overlap with an empty metal orbital along the M–C axis gives ligand-to-metal [sigma donation](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-donation). Conversely, an occupied metal $d_\pi$ orbital overlaps sideways with an empty CO $2\pi^*$ [antibonding molecular orbital](../../chemistry.md#antibonding-orbital), giving metal-to-ligand [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding). There are two mutually perpendicular pi channels. The original orbital schematic below shows one of them; $+$ and $-$ denote orbital phase, not [electric charge](../../electric-charge.md).

<a id="b7/b/i/image-carbonyl-sigma-donation-and-one-pi-backbonding-channel"></a>
![](../../past-exam-of-the-chemistry-b-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2025/paper-2-orbitals.png)

**[Figure 1](#b7/b/i/image-carbonyl-sigma-donation-and-one-pi-backbonding-channel). Carbonyl sigma donation and one pi-backbonding channel**.

The interactions are synergic: donation makes [electron](../../physics.md#electron) density available at the metal, and backbonding helps accommodate it while strengthening the metal–[carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) interaction. Occupying CO $\pi^*$ reduces the C–O [bond order](../../chemistry.md#bond-order), lengthens that bond and lowers its [bond force constant](../../molecule.md#bond-force-constant). Therefore stronger [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding) generally lowers the carbonyl stretching [spectroscopic wavenumber](../../wave-equation.md#spectroscopic-wavenumber) even while strengthening the M–C attachment.

<h4 id="b7/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b7/b)

<h5 id="b7/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b7/b/ii)

For the same pair of atoms, the harmonic approximation gives $\widetilde\nu=(2\pi c)^{-1}\sqrt{k/\mu}$, where $k$ is the [bond force constant](../../molecule.md#bond-force-constant) and $\mu$ the [reduced mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#reduced-mass). The large shifts mainly reflect changes in $k$ from CO [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding); the complexes are not distinguished simply by their total number of valence [electrons](../../physics.md#electron).

**$\mathrm{W(CO)_6}$ has stronger net backbonding than $\mathrm{Cr(CO)_6}$.** Both are group-6 M(0), $d^6$, 18-electron complexes. The more spatially extended [tungsten](../../chemical-substance.md#tungsten) $5d$ orbitals provide effective overlap with CO $\pi^*$, consistent with the lower stretching frequency. As a simple same-mode force-constant comparison, $(1977/2000)^2=0.977$, about a 2.3% reduction.

**$\mathrm{Ni(CO)_3(PMe_3)}$ has appreciable but less C–O weakening than those hexacarbonyls.** Ni(0) is $d^{10}$ and the complex is also 18-electron. The more contracted, lower-energy late-3d occupied orbitals give less effective donation into CO $\pi^*$ than the group-6 carbonyls in this comparison. [Trimethylphosphine](../../chemical-substance.md#trimethylphosphine) donates [electron](../../physics.md#electron) density and increases backbonding relative to an otherwise comparable less-donating [nickel](../../chemical-substance.md#nickel) complex; that effect does not force its frequency below chromium's. Comparing different metals requires both orbital energies and overlap.

**$[\mathrm{Cu(CO)_4}]^+$ has very weak backbonding and a frequency above free CO.** Cu(I) is $d^{10}$ and 18-electron here, but positive charge lowers its occupied orbital energies and suppresses backdonation. Sigma donation and the cation's electrostatic polarization can strengthen the internal C–O bond. Consequently the usual free-CO reference, about $2143\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, lies below this cation's band. A blue shift is compatible with [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) coordination; it does not imply absence of a metal–CO bond. Coupled normal modes and measurement conditions limit literal extraction of a local bond force constant from one band.

<h3 id="b7/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b7/c)

A [dissociative substitution](../../chemical-reaction.md#dissociative-substitution) begins with slow CO loss,

$$
\mathrm{M(CO)_5L}\xrightarrow{k_d}\mathrm{M(CO)_4L}+\mathrm{CO},
$$

followed by capture of the incoming [ligand in coordination chemistry](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry). A more electron-releasing $L$ increases metal-to-CO [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding) in the six-coordinate reactant, stabilizing it and strengthening its M–CO attachments. Removing CO sacrifices a useful pi acceptor from that electron-rich center. Thus **the activation free energy for CO dissociation often rises and the substitution slows**. Within an otherwise matched series, $k_d\propto\exp[-\Delta G^\ddagger/(RT)]$ captures this sensitivity. The rate depends on the relative energies of reactant and dissociation [transition state](../../chemical-kinetics.md#transition-state), so the trend is conditional: large changes in [steric hindrance](../../stereochemistry.md#steric-hindrance), geometry or mechanism can override the electronic effect.

<h3 id="b7/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b7/d)

The CO trans to a [phosphine ligand](../../chemistry.md#phosphine-ligand) competes for metal pi donation with a weaker pi acceptor than CO itself, whereas a cis CO has another CO trans to it. The trans-to-phosphine carbonyl can therefore benefit from stronger M–CO [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding); loss of one of the four cis carbonyls is commonly favored. Capture of the incoming phosphine at that vacancy gives **the cis bis(phosphine) product**. The fourfold statistical availability of cis sites reinforces an electronic preference but does not establish it by itself.

This is the carbonyl [cis effect](../../chemical-reaction.md#cis-effect), rather than the square-planar [trans effect](../../chemistry.md#trans-effect) applied without qualification. A fuller explanation compares the five-coordinate dissociation [transition states](../../chemical-kinetics.md#transition-state) and their ligand site preferences. Product geometry alone cannot prove which CO first departed, because the intermediate can rearrange before capture. Isotopically labeled CO exchange is a more discriminating experiment; the classic studies explicitly separate this issue from product stereochemistry.[1976, pp. 3160–3161, isotope exchange, intermediate rearrangement and limitations of inferring dissociation sites from substitution products](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#cis-labilization-of-ligand-dissociation-3-survey-of-group-6-and-7-six-coordinate-carbonyl-compounds-the-site-preference-model-for-ligand-labilization-effects-by-jim-d-atwood-and-theodore-l-brown)

## B8

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="b8/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B8](#b8)

<h4 id="b8/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b8/a)

<h5 id="b8/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b8/a/i)

In $A$, the tridentate ligand contains one anionic [phenoxide ligand](../../chemistry.md#phenoxide-ligand), a neutral secondary [amine](../../chemical-substance.md#amine) with its N–H bond intact, and a neutral [phosphine ligand](../../chemistry.md#phosphine-ligand). The three [chloride ligands](../../chemistry.md#chloride-ligand) contribute another three negative charges. Consequently the neutral complex has **Ti(IV), $d^0$, and 12 valence [electrons](../../physics.md#electron)**:

$$
\mathrm{OS(Ti)}=+4,\qquad \mathrm{VE}=0+2_{\mathrm O}+2_{\mathrm N}+2_{\mathrm P}+3(2)_{\mathrm{Cl}}=12.
$$

The N–H label is essential: counting this [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) as an anionic [amido ligand](../../chemistry.md#amido-ligand) would give the wrong [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state). A six-coordinate early-metal complex need not obey the [18-electron rule](../../chemistry.md#18-electron-rule).

In $B$, the substituted [cyclopentadienyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#cyclopentadienyl-ligand) is $\eta^5$ and anionic, while the three-carbon bent ligand is an anionic $\eta^3$ [allyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#allyl-ligand), a four-electron donor in ionic counting. The pendant [pyridine](../../chemical-substance.md#pyridine) [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) is uncoordinated. Therefore **Mo(II), $d^4$, 18 [electrons](../../physics.md#electron)** follows from

$$
\mathrm{VE}=4_{d}+6_{\eta^5\mathrm{Cp}}+4_{\eta^3\mathrm{allyl}}+2(2)_{\mathrm{CO}}=18.
$$

The curved delocalization mark across the three-carbon ligand must not be read as a separate alkene or alkyne.

<h5 id="b8/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b8/a/ii)

A formal two-electron [reductive elimination](../../chemical-reaction.md#reductive-elimination) couples two anionic, mutually cis donors and reduces Ti(IV) to Ti(II). There are two chemically distinct couplings: **Cl–Cl elimination gives $\mathrm{Cl_2}$**, leaving a Ti(II) complex with the intact anionic O,N,P ligand and one chloride; **O–Cl elimination gives an aryl hypochlorite group, $\mathrm{ArOCl}$**, breaking the original Ti–O attachment and leaving a Ti(II) center coordinated by N,P and two chlorides. The latter product can remain tethered through the N,P part of the same ligand; it is not necessarily a free small molecule. The starting facial arrangement supplies cis pairs of both kinds.

**Cl–Cl coupling is the more plausible of these formal alternatives**, because it retains the particularly strong early-metal Ti–O interaction and makes the stronger Cl–Cl rather than relatively weak O–Cl bond. Both alternatives reduce the valence count by two, from 12 to 10, so the [18-electron rule](../../chemistry.md#18-electron-rule) does not choose between them. Neither should be asserted to occur readily: reduction of a stable, oxophilic $d^0$ Ti(IV) center is intrinsically unfavorable. Coupling a chloride to the neutral amine or phosphine is not the ordinary elimination of two X-type ligands. [proton](../../physics.md#proton) transfer between N–H and [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen), if it occurred, would instead be [acid-base chemistry](../../acid-base-chemistry.md), not automatically a two-electron [reductive elimination](../../chemical-reaction.md#reductive-elimination).

**The readily displaced [acetonitrile](../../chemical-substance.md#acetonitrile) sites have both been exhausted.** A second chloride would have to displace a strongly bound [carbonyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand), open the [chelate ring](../../chemistry.md#chelate-ring), or associate to an already 18-electron center to make an unfavorable crowded 20-electron species. <h5 id="b8/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

For an intact complex in the usual fast-conformational-averaging regime, counting distinct [chemical shifts](../../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#chemical-shift) rather than individual split lines gives **$C:9$, $D:11$, $E:10$ signals**.

In $C$, the uncoordinated pyridinium arm permits the paired positions of a monosubstituted [cyclopentadienyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#cyclopentadienyl-ligand) to average: its four ring hydrogens give two sets, $2+2$. The linker $\mathrm{CH_2}$ gives one set, the four inequivalent pyridinium C–H positions give four, its N–H gives one, and the two symmetry-related bound [acetonitrile](../../chemical-substance.md#acetonitrile) methyl groups give one. Hence $2+1+4+1+1=9$. The exchangeable N–H resonance may be broadened or absent experimentally; nine is the structural count before exchange removes it.

In the rigid cis-dicarbonyl chelates $D$ and $E$, the different pyridyl-N and fourth-leg donors destroy the operation that related the two sides of the substituted ring. Each of the four Cp hydrogens is distinct, and the two linker hydrogens are [diastereotopic](../../stereochemistry.md#diastereotopic). Together with four [pyridine](../../chemical-substance.md#pyridine) C–H sites, this gives $4+2+4=10$. The remaining bound [acetonitrile](../../chemical-substance.md#acetonitrile) adds one methyl signal to $D$, giving 11; $E$ has none, giving 10. The two [enantiomers](../../stereochemistry.md#enantiomer) of a racemate have identical spectra in an achiral solvent and do not double these counts. This prediction assumes resolved resonances and no rapid chelate opening that averages inequivalent sites; variable-temperature [proton nuclear magnetic resonance](../../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#proton-nuclear-magnetic-resonance) would test that assumption.

The six P–N bonds are not well described as three permanently localized single bonds alternating with three ordinary double bonds. The strongly polar P–N framework has contributions from [ionic bonding](../../ionic-bonding.md) and [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) donation into neighboring phosphorus-centered acceptor combinations, including P–Cl $\sigma^*$ orbitals. This [negative hyperconjugation](../../chemical-bond.md#negative-hyperconjugation) supplies additional P–N bonding and equivalent environments around the ring. Approximate planarity is compatible with these interactions, but equal lengths and planarity alone do not establish benzene-like [aromaticity](../../molecule.md#aromaticity). Large phosphorus $3d$ participation is not required.[1997, abstract and bonding analysis, ionic sigma bonding and nitrogen-to-P–X sigma-star donation](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#molecular-structures-and-conformations-of-polyphosphazenes-a-study-based-on-density-functional-calculations-of-oligomers-by-huai-sun)

**Gallium binds to a ring [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen), and the two P–N bonds flanking that [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) lengthen.** Formation of the N$\to$GaCl$_3$ [coordinate covalent bond](../../covalent-bond.md#coordinate-covalent-bond) changes the available [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) [electron](../../physics.md#electron) density and local geometry, weakening its contribution to the adjacent P–N interactions. The other four P–N bonds retain greater multiple-bond character and are correspondingly shorter. Loss of the threefold equivalence and altered local geometry permit the ring to pucker. This is a [Lewis acid-base adduct](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#lewis-acid-base-adduct), not replacement of a P–Cl bond by GaCl$_3$. Single-crystal structures and variable-temperature multinuclear [nuclear magnetic resonance](../../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md) establish this class of nitrogen-bound group-13 adducts.[2011, pp. 8937–8945, adduct structures and variable-temperature NMR](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#group-13-lewis-acid-adducts-of-pcl2n-3-by-zin-min-tun-and-others)

<h4 id="b9/b/i">i</h4>

**Fluoride favors geminal disubstitution:** after the first substitution, the second F replaces the other chloride at that same phosphorus, giving a $\mathrm{PF_2}$ unit and two unchanged $\mathrm{PCl_2}$ units. The first highly electronegative F withdraws [electron](../../physics.md#electron) density inductively, maintaining or increasing the [electrophilicity](../../chemical-reaction.md#electrophilicity) of its phosphorus and activating the neighboring P–Cl bond toward another [nucleophile](../../chemical-reaction.md#nucleophile). The small fluoride ion also has little [steric hindrance](../../stereochemistry.md#steric-hindrance) to attack there. In this context [geminal substitution](../../chemical-reaction.md#geminal-substitution) means both incoming substituents on one phosphorus; it does not describe the relative positions of different ring [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) atoms. Electronic activation at the substituted phosphorus competes with effects elsewhere in the ring, so the argument predicts the preferred pathway rather than an unconditional exclusive product.

<h5 id="b9/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b9/b/ii)

**Primary amines can favor geminal disubstitution through a proton-abstraction route.** The first substitution leaves an exocyclic $\mathrm{PCl(NHR)}$ group. Its remaining N–H [proton](../../physics.md#proton) provides a pathway unavailable to a fully substituted amino group: base removes H, chloride elimination gives a reactive low-coordinate phosphorus intermediate with increased exocyclic P–N bonding, and a second amine attacks that same phosphorus. [proton](../../physics.md#proton) transfer then gives $\mathrm{P(NHR)_2}$. Thus the directing effect is not simply the inductive effect of an amino group.

This needs a qualification: “primary amine” alone does not specify a universal product distribution. Substituent, solvent and base concentration affect whether the proton-abstraction route outcompetes direct [nucleophilic substitution](../../chemical-reaction.md#nucleophilic-substitution). Kinetic and trapping experiments with aromatic primary amines support the base-catalyzed route to geminal products, while also finding direct substitution pathways under other conditions.[1987, p. 585 abstract, solvent/base dependence and methanol trapping](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#studies-of-phosphazenes-part-31-by-sivapackia-ganapathiappan-and-setharampattu-s-krishnamurthy)

**Secondary amines normally favor non-geminal disubstitution**, putting the second amino substituent on a different phosphorus. The first installed $\mathrm{NR_2}$ group donates [electron](../../physics.md#electron) density to the P–N framework and deactivates its own phosphorus toward further attack. It is also bulkier than an initially installed primary-amino group. Crucially, $\mathrm{P{-}NR_2}$ has no N–H [proton](../../physics.md#proton), so the proton-abstraction/chloride-elimination pathway that can direct a primary amine geminally is unavailable. Direct attack at a less-deactivated $\mathrm{PCl_2}$ site is favored. The resulting non-geminal products can have cis or trans relative orientations around the ring; [non-geminal substitution](../../chemical-reaction.md#non-geminal-substitution) alone does not select one of these [stereoisomers](../../stereochemistry.md#stereoisomer). These are useful trends, with known substituent-dependent exceptions in experimentally characterized mixed derivatives.[2018, abstract, pyrrolidine/dimethylamine substitution of differently substituted rings](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#regiochemical-control-in-the-substitution-reactions-of-cyclotriphosphazene-derivatives-with-secondary-amines-by-serap-besli-and-others)

**$B=\mathrm{N_3P_3(NHCy)_6}$**, with two cyclohexylamino substituents on each original phosphorus. All six P–Cl bonds have been replaced; the six N–H bonds of those substituents remain. Each equivalent of [triethylamine](../../chemical-substance.md#triethylamine) traps one equivalent of HCl.

**$C$ contains three four-membered $\mathrm{P^{V}{-}N(Cy){-}P^{III}(Cl){-}N(Cy)}$ spiro rings**, one at each original ring phosphorus. Each PCl$_3$ joins the two geminal NHCy groups: replacement of two of its chlorides forms two new P–N bonds and releases two HCl, consuming both N–H [protons](../../physics.md#proton). The new phosphorus retains one chloride and a [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair), making it three-coordinate P(III); the original six-membered $\mathrm{P_3N_3}$ ring retains P(V). The product formula is $\mathrm{N_3P_3\{(NCy)_2PCl\}_3}$. For each spiro ring, the original phosphorus is bonded to both exocyclic nitrogens, and each of those nitrogens is bonded to the same new PCl center; the braces do not indicate a simple linear substituent.

The three new P–Cl directions permit **an all-cis isomer and a cis,trans isomer**. Relative to the central ring, write these as $(+,+,+)$ and $(+,+,-)$, with cyclic relabeling giving equivalent choices. Reversing all signs does not create a third relative configuration. This describes the configuration pattern; puckering and substituent conformations are additional structural details.

Replace the two chlorides on one ring phosphorus $P_1$ with the two ends of the diiron unit. **$D$ has a spiro $P_1\mathrm{Fe_2}$ triangle**, with an Fe–Fe bond, a $P_1$–Fe bond to each metal and four terminal CO ligands on each Fe. The other two phosphorus atoms retain their $\mathrm{PCl_2}$ groups. Its composition is $\mathrm{N_3P_3Cl_4Fe_2(CO)_8}$. In neutral [electron counting](../../chemistry.md#electron-counting), each metal has $8_{\mathrm{Fe}}+8_{4\mathrm{CO}}+1_{\mathrm{Fe-P}}+1_{\mathrm{Fe-Fe}}=18$.

**$E$ has a triangular Fe$_3$ cluster, one bridging CO, and an adjacent ring N$\to$Fe bond.** The two original metals remain bonded to $P_1$ and to one another, and each bonds to the added third Fe. The added Fe has $8+6_{3\mathrm{CO}}+2_{\mathrm{N}}+2_{2\mathrm{Fe-Fe}}=18$. Omitting [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) coordination leaves the third Fe two [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) short; omitting the bridging CO leaves both original metals one [electron](../../physics.md#electron) short.

The two equivalent nuclei do not split each other. In $E$, coordination of one adjacent [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) distinguishes the two sides of the ring, producing three inequivalent phosphorus sites. Each couples to the other two and gives a doublet of doublets in the first-order limit; equal couplings or strong coupling could distort this simple pattern.

The high-frequency [infrared spectroscopy](../../chemistry.md#infrared-spectroscopy) bands belong to terminal carbonyl stretching modes. The extra low-frequency band is diagnostic of a strongly backbonded [bridging carbonyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#bridging-carbonyl-ligand). Use C1 for the carbonyl [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon), C2 for the adjacent [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon), and C3 for the beta [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) of the reactive [enone](../../chemical-substance.md#enone) fragment $\mathrm{R{-}C1(=O){-}C2H{=}C3H{-}R'}$. **[water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) adds conjugately at C3, giving a beta-hydroxy ketone.** The [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) of neutral [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) attacks C3; move the C2=C3 pi pair toward C1–C2 and the carbonyl pi pair onto [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). This produces the charge-separated [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate)–oxonium intermediate

$$
\mathrm{R{-}C1(O^-){=}C2H{-}C3H(OH_2^+){-}R'}.
$$

A second [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) molecule can relay a [proton](../../physics.md#proton) from the newly attached oxonium [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) to the [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate) system. Using the carbon-centered [resonance structure](../../covalent-bond.md#resonance-structure), reform C1=O and protonate C2 while the donating O–H bond returns its pair to the added [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). Equivalently, [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) protonation first gives an [enol](../../chemical-substance.md#enol), followed by water-mediated [keto-enol tautomerism](../../chemical-reaction.md#keto-enol-tautomerism). Either path yields a neutral beta-hydroxy ketone and regenerates the relay [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water). There is no need to introduce externally supplied H$^+$ or OH$^-$; solvent molecules perform the [proton](../../physics.md#proton) transfers. The uncatalyzed charge separation has a large barrier, so this mechanism describes a slow formal pathway, not a claim of rapid hydration in pure [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water).

At [pH](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) 7, a [glutamate residue](../../protein.md#glutamate-residue) is normally carboxylate. **Its most natural role is general-base activation of the attacking [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water).** Draw the carboxylate [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) toward a [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) H, the [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) O–H pair toward its [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen), and that [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen)'s [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) toward C3 while the conjugated pi system moves toward the carbonyl [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen):

$$
\mathrm{Glu{-}CO_2^-\cdots H{-}OH\cdots C3}
\longrightarrow
\mathrm{Glu{-}CO_2H}+\text{bound beta-hydroxy enolate}.
$$

This can be concerted, avoiding a freely diffusing hydroxide intermediate. The protonated glutamate subsequently returns its [proton](../../physics.md#proton) to the [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate) system, directly or through solvent, regenerating carboxylate. A favorable arrangement is needed; ordinary carboxylate does not quantitatively deprotonate bulk [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) in a prior equilibrium.

The side-chain amide of [glutamine](../../chemical-substance.md#glutamine) is neutral. **Its N–H can donate a [hydrogen bond](../../chemical-bond.md#hydrogen-bond) to the carbonyl [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) and stabilize developing [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate) charge**, while its amide [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) can accept a [hydrogen bond](../../chemical-bond.md#hydrogen-bond) to orient attacking [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water). Draw $\mathrm{Gln{-}CONH_2\cdots O{=}C1{-}C2{=}C3}$, with the dotted interaction from amide N–H to substrate [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). This assists polarization and [transition state stabilization](../../chemical-kinetics.md#transition-state-stabilization); the amide [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) is not an ordinary amine base because its [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) is delocalized into its own carbonyl.

The guanidinium group of [arginine](../../chemical-substance.md#arginine) is positively charged. **It can stabilize developing negative charge by hydrogen bonding and electrostatic attraction**, drawn $\mathrm{Arg{-}guanidinium^+\cdots O^{\delta-}{-}C1}$. Its delocalized cation is not the preferred neutral general base at [pH](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) 7. Productive [catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#catalysis) requires stronger stabilization of the reacting arrangement than of the unreactive ground-state complex.

[Phenylalanine](../../protein.md#phenylalanine) and [leucine](../../chemical-substance.md#leucine) have hydrocarbon side chains and provide no conventional acid/base or hydrogen-bond donor/acceptor group here. They can position the [enone](../../chemical-substance.md#enone), exclude unproductive solvent and select the attacked face through [hydrophobic effect](../../physical-chemistry.md#hydrophobic-effect) and [steric hindrance](../../stereochemistry.md#steric-hindrance). Those binding effects can assist [catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#catalysis) but should not be drawn as an imaginary side-chain [proton](../../physics.md#proton) relay. The original schematic depicts the Glu [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) relay and the Gln/Arg oxyanion-stabilizing contacts.

<h4 id="b10/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b10/a)

<h5 id="b10/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b10/a/iv)

The beta-carbon bond-forming [transition state](../../chemical-kinetics.md#transition-state) retains the same basic connectivity across this [pH](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) range, but the predominant proton-transfer route and enzyme [protonation](../../chemical-reaction.md#protonation) change. Near the acidic end, **protonated glutamate can act as a general acid**, donating a [proton](../../physics.md#proton) or a strong [hydrogen bond](../../chemical-bond.md#hydrogen-bond) to carbonyl [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) and making C3 more [electrophilic](../../chemical-reaction.md#electrophilicity); it is less available as the carboxylate general base that activates [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water). Above its local $pK_a$, **carboxylate-mediated [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) activation is favored**. Acid [catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#catalysis) therefore shifts positive-charge stabilization toward a protonated carbonyl, whereas base-assisted attack reduces positive-charge buildup on incoming [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water). Neither a unique transition-state geometry nor a numerical [pH](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) optimum follows from [pH](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) alone.

Using a free-solution glutamate side-chain $pK_a\simeq4.2$ only as an illustration, the deprotonated fraction is $[1+10^{pK_a-\mathrm{pH}}]^{-1}$: about 0.059 at [pH](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) 3 and 0.998 at [pH](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) 7. An enzyme environment can shift that $pK_a$ substantially. The [glutamine](../../chemical-substance.md#glutamine) amide and the hydrocarbon side chains remain in their ordinary forms from [pH](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) 3 to 10; [arginine](../../chemical-substance.md#arginine) guanidinium, with a typical free side-chain $pK_a$ around 12.5, remains predominantly positive even at [pH](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) 10. Thus the major side-chain switch among those listed is glutamate. At the alkaline end, bulk hydroxide can also contribute to nonenzymatic [conjugate addition](../../chemical-reaction.md#conjugate-addition). A measured pH-rate profile must control substrate stability and enzyme folding before assigning every loss of activity to an active-site titration.

**[catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#catalysis) requires a protonated group, consistent with a general-acid contribution.** Loss of its [proton](../../physics.md#proton) as [pH](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) rises would remove carbonyl activation or the [proton](../../physics.md#proton) needed to finish the conjugate reduction. A [glutamate residue](../../protein.md#glutamate-residue) with a shifted active-site $pK_a$ is a plausible candidate, but a [pH](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) cutoff alone neither identifies the residue nor measures a precise $pK_a$. Irreversible unfolding or changes in substrate binding must also be excluded by reversible [pH](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) jumps, protein-integrity checks and initial-rate measurements.

For the mechanistic notation below, the reduced [cofactor](../../enzyme.md#cofactor-biochemistry) is written [NADPH](../../chemical-substance.md#nadph), the established cofactor of human steroid [5α-reductase](../../enzyme.md#5-alpha-reductase). [NADH](../../chemical-substance.md#nadh) and [NADPH](../../chemical-substance.md#nadph) have the same reactive reduced nicotinamide ring, so the local hydride-transfer arrows are unchanged, although their biochemical specificity is different.[2020, Fig. 3 and catalytic-mechanism discussion: NADPH-dependent reduction and protonated E57](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#structure-of-human-steroid-5-alpha-reductase-2-with-the-anti-androgen-drug-finasteride-by-qingpin-xiao-and-others)

**Y91F implicates the Tyr hydroxyl, while E57Q implicates the Glu carboxylic-acid chemistry.** Replacing [tyrosine](../../chemical-substance.md#tyrosine) by [phenylalanine](../../protein.md#phenylalanine) retains an aromatic side chain but removes its hydroxyl, supporting a role for Tyr91 as a [hydrogen bond donor](../../chemical-bond.md#hydrogen-bond-donor) that polarizes the steroid C3 carbonyl and stabilizes an enolate-like intermediate or [transition state](../../chemical-kinetics.md#transition-state). Its hydroxyl could participate in a [proton](../../physics.md#proton) relay, but inactivity alone does not establish that it supplies the final [proton](../../physics.md#proton).

Replacing a [glutamate residue](../../protein.md#glutamate-residue) by [glutamine](../../chemical-substance.md#glutamine) approximately preserves side-chain size while replacing a titratable carboxylic acid by an amide. Loss of activity, combined with the acidic activity range, supports protonated Glu57 as a carbonyl-activating acid and possible [proton](../../physics.md#proton) donor. The two residues can cooperate to stabilize [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) charge during reduction. The inhibitor-binding structure supports local contacts; it is not a direct picture of a [testosterone](../../chemical-substance.md#testosterone) transition state, and an N–H contact of the aza inhibitor need not occur in [testosterone](../../chemical-substance.md#testosterone).

To distinguish these chemical roles from indirect effects, compare mutant and wild-type abundance, membrane insertion and folding, [cofactor](../../enzyme.md#cofactor-biochemistry) binding, and steroid binding. Measure initial rates with matched enzyme amounts and include no-enzyme and inhibitor-treated wild-type controls. If binding survives but turnover fails, the evidence for a catalytic defect is stronger. The published study combines structure, mutagenesis and modeled [testosterone](../../chemical-substance.md#testosterone) placement, so the proposed residue roles should be distinguished from direct observation of [proton](../../physics.md#proton) trajectories.[2020, Fig. 3a,c–e, structural contacts, docking and mutant activity comparison](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#structure-of-human-steroid-5-alpha-reductase-2-with-the-anti-androgen-drug-finasteride-by-qingpin-xiao-and-others)

<h4 id="b10/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b10/b)

<h5 id="b10/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b10/b/iii)

**Hydride is delivered to steroid C5 and a [proton](../../physics.md#proton) to C4; the C3 ketone is retained.** Use the conjugated fragment $\mathrm{O{=}C3{-}C4{=}C5}$. Carbonyl [hydrogen bonds](../../chemical-bond.md#hydrogen-bond) from Tyr91–OH and protonated Glu57 lower the barrier to [conjugate reduction](../../chemical-reaction.md#conjugate-reduction). Draw a curved arrow from the nicotinamide C4–H bond of [NADPH](../../chemical-substance.md#nadph) to steroid C5, shift the C4=C5 pi pair toward C3–C4, and shift C3=O pi [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) onto [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). The nicotinamide ring rearomatizes to [NADP+](../../chemical-substance.md#nadp-plus); the steroid becomes a bound [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate), or its protonated [enol](../../chemical-substance.md#enol) equivalent. [electron](../../physics.md#electron) shifts across the nicotinamide ring must accompany C–H bond cleavage rather than leaving an unexplained neutral [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) radical.

Next, reform C3=O and protonate steroid C4 from an appropriately positioned acid, directly or through an [enol](../../chemical-substance.md#enol)/proton-relay route. This regenerates the catalytic acid after solvent reprotonation. The enzyme fixes the hydride-accepting face so that the new C5–H has **the 5α configuration**, giving [dihydrotestosterone](../../chemical-substance.md#dihydrotestosterone); delivery to the opposite face would give a different ring-junction configuration. This is not simple 1,2 reduction of the C3 carbonyl to an alcohol. The net local bookkeeping is one hydride plus one [proton](../../physics.md#proton) across C4=C5, with two [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) supplied by [NADPH](../../chemical-substance.md#nadph). The original reactive-fragment diagram shows the enolate-forming arrows and the separate protonation step.

<h4 id="b10/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b10/b)

<h5 id="b10/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b10/b/iv)

**[finasteride](../../chemical-substance.md#finasteride) is reduced at C1, leaving a C2 [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate) that attacks nicotinamide C4 to make the covalent C–C adduct.** In the inhibitor's unsaturated lactam, the reactive chain is $\mathrm{O{=}C3{-}C2{=}C1}$, with N at steroid position 4. Activate the C3 carbonyl by hydrogen bonding; draw the reduced cofactor's C4–H bond toward inhibitor C1, the C1=C2 pi pair toward C2–C3, and the C3=O pi pair toward [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). This gives [NADP+](../../chemical-substance.md#nadp-plus) next to the C2-centered resonance form of the inhibitor [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate).

Instead of completing reduction by protonating C2, move the C2 [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) toward the electrophilic C4 of the oxidized nicotinamide ring. Shift the pyridinium pi system toward its positively charged N, and reform the inhibitor carbonyl if using the oxygen-centered [enolate](../../chemical-substance.md#enolate) representation. The result is the **C2(inhibitor)–C4(nicotinamide) bond** and a neutral dihydronicotinamide ring. Numbering the two molecules separately prevents confusing cofactor C4 with the inhibitor's N4. The lactam N–H remains present and can help position the inhibitor through hydrogen bonding; it does not form a covalent bond to the protein.

This is [mechanism-based inhibition](../../enzyme.md#mechanism-based-inhibition): enzyme chemistry generates the species that traps its own oxidized cofactor. Isolation of a cofactor–inhibitor adduct after protein denaturation, rather than persistent attachment to a denatured protein, distinguishes it from covalent modification of an amino acid side chain. The original mechanistic study combined radiolabel recovery, purification and spectroscopy to establish the adduct; its slow breakdown also cautions against treating “irreversible” as an absolute statement for every timescale.[1996, pp. 2361–2363, adduct isolation, structural analysis and Fig. 7](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#mechanism-based-inhibition-of-human-steroid-5-alpha-reductase-by-finasteride-enzyme-catalyzed-formation-of-nadp-dihydrofinasteride-a-potent-bisubstrate-analog-inhibitor-by-herbert-g-bull-and-others)
