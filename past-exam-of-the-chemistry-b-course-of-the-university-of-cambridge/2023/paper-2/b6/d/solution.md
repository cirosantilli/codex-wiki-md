↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The more radially extended Pt $5d$ orbitals give strong [metal](../../../../../metal.md)–ligand overlap and covalent bonding. This helps compensate the energetic cost of reaching Pt(IV), and the large octahedral [ligand field](../../../../../ligand-field.md) splitting strongly stabilizes its low-spin $d^6$ configuration, with a [crystal field stabilization energy](../../../../../crystal-field-stabilization-energy.md) contribution $-2.4\Delta_o$ before separately counting pairing and the full bond energies. Ni $3d$ bonding is less effective in this respect; Pd $4d$ compounds also generally favour the very stable $d^8$ M(II) arrangement more strongly than Pt does. **The stronger bonding and large ligand-field stabilization available to Pt(IV) make oxidation to $+4$ more favourable relative to its congeners.** This is a comparative trend, not a claim that Ni(IV) or Pd(IV) complexes cannot exist.
