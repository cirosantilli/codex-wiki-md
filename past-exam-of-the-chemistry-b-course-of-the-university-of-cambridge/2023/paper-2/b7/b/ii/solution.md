<h1 id="b7/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The relevant chelate rings contain **four atoms for [dppm](../../../../../../bis-diphenylphosphino-methane.md), five for [dppe](../../../../../../1-2-bis-diphenylphosphino-ethane.md), and six for [dppp](../../../../../../1-3-bis-diphenylphosphino-propane.md)**, counting the [nickel](../../../../../../nickel.md) and both [phosphorus](../../../../../../phosphorus.md) atoms. The one-carbon bridge in [dppm](../../../../../../bis-diphenylphosphino-methane.md) forces a compact ring and the small [bite angle](../../../../../../bite-angle.md) $75.62^\circ$. A two-carbon tether accommodates a five-membered chelate closer to the preferred square-planar cis angle, giving $86.99^\circ$. The more flexible three-carbon tether can open slightly beyond $90^\circ$, giving $91.77^\circ$.

For a simplified symmetric chelate with Ni–P distance $r$ and P–P donor separation $d$,

$$
d=2r\sin(\beta/2).
$$

Increasing the accessible donor separation therefore increases the [bite angle](../../../../../../bite-angle.md) $\beta$. The ligand is a three-dimensional tether, so ring puckering and substituent crowding matter as well as the number of methylene groups. This geometrical explanation does not require different formal [oxidation states](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md) or [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) counts for the three complexes.
