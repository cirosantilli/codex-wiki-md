<h1 id="b9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Prepare a chlorophosphazene ring from [phosphorus pentachloride](../../../../../phosphorus-pentachloride.md) and [ammonium chloride](../../../../../ammonium-chloride.md), isolate the cyclic trimer, open the rings to make the linear chloropolymer, and finally replace P–Cl by P–OEt:

$$
\mathrm{3PCl_5+3NH_4Cl\longrightarrow(NPCl_2)_3+12HCl},
$$



$$
\mathrm{m(NPCl_2)_3\xrightarrow{\text{controlled heating}}[NPCl_2]_{3m}},
$$



$$
\mathrm{[NPCl_2]_n+2nNaOEt\longrightarrow[NP(OEt)_2]_n+2nNaCl}.
$$

The first stage can produce a mixture of cyclic oligomers, so isolation of [hexachlorocyclotriphosphazene](../../../../../hexachlorocyclotriphosphazene.md) is important. Controlled thermal [ring-opening polymerization](../../../../../ring-opening-polymerization.md), conventionally around $250\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ for the classical route, gives soluble linear [polydichlorophosphazene](../../../../../polydichlorophosphazene.md) before excessive cross-linking. Moisture exclusion avoids uncontrolled [hydrolysis](../../../../../hydrolysis.md) of the reactive P–Cl precursor.

The target is [poly\[bis(ethoxy)phosphazene\]](../../../../../poly-bis-ethoxy-phosphazene.md). The final step is [nucleophilic substitution](../../../../../nucleophilic-substitution.md) with an [ethoxide](../../../../../ethoxide.md) salt in a compatible dry medium, replacing two [chlorides](../../../../../chloride.md) per [phosphorus](../../../../../phosphorus.md). **Polymerize the chlorinated ring first, then substitute the linear [polymer](../../../../../polymer-split.md)**: complete ethoxylation of the isolated ring does not by itself produce the requested high [polymer](../../../../../polymer-split.md). The primary macromolecular-substitution work explicitly includes the ethoxy derivative.[Inorganic Chemistry 5, pp. 1709–1715; alkoxide substitution of linear polydichlorophosphazene, including ethoxy derivatives](../../../../../phosphonitrilic-compounds-vi-high-molecular-weight-poly-alkoxy-and-aryloxyphosphazenes-by-harry-r-allcock-and-others.md)
