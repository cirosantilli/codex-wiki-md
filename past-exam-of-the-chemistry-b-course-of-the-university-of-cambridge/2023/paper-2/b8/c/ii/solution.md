<h1 id="b8/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**The terminal nitrosyls are linear in both proposed electron-precise structures; the formal M–M bond orders are V–V double and Fe–Fe single.** Use three-electron terminal linear NO donors, three-electron $\mu_2$-NO bridges, and five-electron neutral Cp donors in covalent counting.

For the [vanadium](../../../../../../vanadium.md) dimer the two [metal](../../../../../../metal.md) fragments together contain

$$
N_{\rm frag}=2(5)_{\rm V}+2(5)_{\rm Cp}+2(3)_{\rm terminal\ NO}+2(3)_{\mu\rm NO}=32.
$$

A [metal](../../../../../../metal.md)–[metal](../../../../../../metal.md) bond of order $b$ adds $b$ to each local count, so $32+2b=36$ gives **$b=2$** and 18 [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) per V. For an ionic cross-check, Cp is $-1$, each linear terminal NO is conventionally NO$^+$, and each bridge is represented as NO$^-$. Thus

$$
2x-2+2-2=0,\qquad\boxed{x_{\rm V}=+1,\quad d^4.}
$$

Each V then has $4+6_{\rm Cp}+2_{\rm terminal\ NO}+4_{\rm two\ bridges}+2_{\rm V=V}=18$ [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md).

The [iron](../../../../../../iron.md) species is **monoanionic**, and $\mathrm L^-$ is a [pyrazolate](../../../../../../pyrazolate.md) bridge binding through its two [nitrogens](../../../../../../nitrogen.md). This is a four-electron donor overall in ionic counting, or three overall in neutral counting. The carbonyl bridge supplies two [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) overall. The covalent fragment total is consequently

$$
N_{\rm frag}=2(8)_{\rm Fe}+4(3)_{\rm terminal\ NO}+2_{\mu\rm CO}+3_{\mu\rm L}+1_{\rm anion}=34.
$$

Therefore $34+2b=36$ gives **$b=1$**. In the formal NO$^+$ description,

$$
2x+4-1=-1,\qquad\boxed{x_{\rm Fe}=-2,\quad d^{10}.}
$$

Each Fe receives $10+4_{\rm two\ NO}+2_{\rm pyrazolate\ N}+1_{\mu\rm CO}+1_{\rm Fe-Fe}=18$ [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md). The negative formal [oxidation state](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md) follows from the chosen nitrosyl assignment and the overall charge; omitting that charge would change the count.

Bent terminal NO ligands would supply fewer neutral-count [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) and demand much higher formal M–M bond orders to attain 18-electron counts. The linear assignments give the ordinary double and single bond solutions. Since NO is a [non-innocent ligand](../../../../../../non-innocent-ligand.md), these [oxidation states](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md) are formal bookkeeping limits, not measured [metal](../../../../../../metal.md) charges; actual nitrosyl bending and M–M bond strengths require structural and spectroscopic evidence.[Coordination Chemistry Reviews 13, pp. 339–406; formal nitrosyl assignments and distinction from actual electron distribution](../../../../../../principles-of-structure-bonding-and-reactivity-for-metal-nitrosyl-complexes-by-john-h-enemark-and-richard-d-feltham.md)
