<h1 id="b8/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**A neutral, simple $\mu_2$-NO contributes three [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) to the two-metal unit overall.** In neutral bookkeeping it is an LX-type bridge: a two-electron donor component plus the odd [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) of neutral NO. For a symmetric bridge, this can be apportioned as $1.5$ [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) to each [metal](../../../../../../metal.md) in a covalent count; it is **not three [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) independently to each [metal](../../../../../../metal.md)**.

An equivalent ionic description represents the bridge as NO$^-$ donating two [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) to each [metal](../../../../../../metal.md), four overall. The extra negative ligand charge must then be included when assigning [metal](../../../../../../metal.md) [oxidation states](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md). Mixing the four-electron ionic donation with a neutral-NO charge assignment would double-count an [electron](../../../../../../electron.md).
