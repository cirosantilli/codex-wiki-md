<h1 id="b8/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [chloride](../../../../../../chloride.md) complex has Pt(II), $d^8$, whereas the neutral bis(phosphine) complex has Pt(0), $d^{10}$ in the neutral-ethene assignment. **The lower-valent, phosphine-supported Pt(0) centre back-donates more strongly into [ethene](../../../../../../ethylene.md) $\pi^*$**, weakening and lengthening its internal C–C bond. This accounts for the longer $1.430\,\text{Å}$ distance compared with $1.375\,\text{Å}$ in the Pt(II) complex.

Both are formally 16-electron complexes: $8+3(2)+2=16$ for the [chloride](../../../../../../chloride.md) species and $10+2(2)+2=16$ for the phosphine species. Thus the difference is not simply a difference in total valence count. It reflects the energies and occupations of the [metal](../../../../../../metal.md)–ligand [molecular orbitals](../../../../../../molecular-orbital.md). The increased reduction of the [ethene](../../../../../../ethylene.md) ligand gives the Pt(0) complex more [metallacyclopropane](../../../../../../metallacyclopropane.md) character; the two descriptions are bonding limits, not distinct products inferred from the distances alone.
