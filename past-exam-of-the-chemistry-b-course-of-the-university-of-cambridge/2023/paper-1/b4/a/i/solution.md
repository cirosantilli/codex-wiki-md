↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**B and C are the two geometrical [ketoxime](../../../../../../ketoxime.md) [isomers](../../../../../../isomer.md)**, formed by replacing the reactive ring C=O with C=N–OH. The separate [spiroacetal](../../../../../../spiroacetal.md) remains intact. Define $K$ as the original [ketone](../../../../../../ketone.md) [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md), $R_a$ as the adjacent ring path towards the [aromatic ring](../../../../../../aromatic-ring.md), and $R_s$ as the adjacent path towards the [spiroacetal](../../../../../../spiroacetal.md)-bearing junction. These are the two [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) [substituents](../../../../../../substituent.md) directly bonded to $K$, not detachable independent fragments.

In **B, N–OH is anti to the $K{-}R_a$ bond** across C=N; in **C, N–OH is anti to $K{-}R_s$**. Those geometries predict exactly the respective [nitrogen](../../../../../../nitrogen.md) insertions in D and E. Both [product](../../../../../../product-chemistry.md)s have formula $\mathrm{C_{18}H_{21}NO_5}$: condensation adds NH2OH to $\mathrm{C_{18}H_{20}O_5}$ and removes H2O. An alternative ring regioisomer or reaction at the protected [ketone](../../../../../../ketone.md) would not represent the pair of [oxime](../../../../../../oxime.md) [geometrical isomers](../../../../../../geometrical-isomer.md).
