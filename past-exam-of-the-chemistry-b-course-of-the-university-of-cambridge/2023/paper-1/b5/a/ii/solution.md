↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The clearest comparison is between the two [amines](../../../../../../amine.md), which have the same [molecular formula](../../../../../../molecular-formula.md) and [molar mass](../../../../../../molar-mass.md). **[isopropylamine](../../../../../../isopropylamine.md) can donate N–H [hydrogen bonds](../../../../../../hydrogen-bond.md) to neighbouring [nitrogen](../../../../../../nitrogen.md) [lone pairs](../../../../../../lone-pair.md); [trimethylamine](../../../../../../trimethylamine.md) has no N–H donor.** The resulting intermolecular association raises the [primary amine](../../../../../../primary-amine.md)'s boiling point, allowing it to remain liquid at room [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), whereas [trimethylamine](../../../../../../trimethylamine.md) is gaseous. Independent phase-change data place their normal boiling points on opposite sides of room [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md).[trimethylamine, CAS 75-50-3, and isopropylamine, CAS 75-31-0, phase-change data](../../../../../../nist-chemistry-webbook-srd-69.md)

[acetone](../../../../../../acetone.md) has a substantial [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) dipole and London dispersion forces sufficient for a room-[temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) liquid, even though it cannot build a strong donor–acceptor [hydrogen bond](../../../../../../hydrogen-bond.md) network by itself. [isobutane](../../../../../../isobutane.md) has mainly London dispersion forces and a compact shape and remains gaseous. [acetamide](../../../../../../acetamide.md) combines [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) acceptors with two N–H donors, allowing an extended, directionally bonded crystal lattice; favourable packing as well as intermolecular association stabilizes its solid state.

These compounds have similar molar masses, so mass alone cannot explain the differences. The [hydrogen-bond acceptor parameter](../../../../../../hydrogen-bond-acceptor-parameter.md) also cannot predict the phase on its own: a pure substance needs complementary donors to exploit strong acceptor sites, and freezing additionally depends on crystal packing and [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md). A stronger acceptor does not automatically imply a higher melting point for arbitrary [molecules](../../../../../../molecule-split.md).
