## B6

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="b6/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#b6)

<h4 id="b6/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b6/a)

The [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) end of a [carbonyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand) has a filled $5\sigma$ [molecular orbital](../../chemistry.md#molecular-orbital) with an outward-facing lobe. Its overlap with a vacant [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) orbital of the same axial symmetry gives CO-to-[metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) [sigma donation](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-donation). Occupied [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) $d_\pi$ orbitals can simultaneously overlap with the empty CO $2\pi^*$ [antibonding molecular orbitals](../../chemistry.md#antibonding-orbital), giving [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)-to-CO [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding). Two perpendicular pi channels are possible; the schematic represents one. Orbital signs indicate phase, not [electric charge](../../electric-charge.md).

<a id="b6/a/image-complementary-donation-and-backbonding-in-carbonyl-and-alkene-coordination"></a>
![](../../past-exam-of-the-chemistry-b-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2024/paper-2-bonding.png)

**[Figure 1](#b6/a/image-complementary-donation-and-backbonding-in-carbonyl-and-alkene-coordination). Complementary donation and backbonding in carbonyl and alkene coordination**.

The two interactions reinforce one another: [sigma donation](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-donation) transfers density toward the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal), and [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding) distributes it into the ligand acceptor orbitals. **Both strengthen M–C attachment, while backbonding weakens C–O.** Occupation of CO $\pi^*$ decreases the internal C–O [bond order](../../chemistry.md#bond-order), generally increasing its [bond length](../../chemical-bond.md#bond-length) and lowering its stretching [spectroscopic wavenumber](../../wave-equation.md#spectroscopic-wavenumber). This is why a strongly backbonded [carbonyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand) can have a strong [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–[carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) bond and a weakened internal [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon)–[oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) bond at the same time.

Place four terminal [trifluorophosphine](../../chemical-substance.md#trifluorophosphine) donors on each [cobalt](../../chemical-substance.md#cobalt) and join the [cobalt](../../chemical-substance.md#cobalt) centers by a single [metal-metal bond](../../chemical-bond.md#metal-metal-bond), as in the first panel. The P atoms bind to [cobalt](../../chemical-substance.md#cobalt); the fluorines remain attached to [phosphorus](../../chemical-substance.md#phosphorus). Each neutral donor supplies two [electrons](../../physics.md#electron). Neutral [electron counting](../../chemistry.md#electron-counting) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{VE(Co)}=9+4(2)+1=18.}
$$

The ionic description is Co(0), $d^9$, with eight [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) from the four donors and one from the Co–Co bond. **A Co–Co single bond supplies the missing [electron](../../physics.md#electron) at each center.** No bridging PF$_3$ is required by this count.

Use an $\eta^5$ [cyclopentadienyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#cyclopentadienyl-ligand) and four terminal [carbonyl ligands](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand) at each [titanium](../../chemical-substance.md#titanium), with a Ti–Ti single [metal-metal bond](../../chemical-bond.md#metal-metal-bond). The middle panel shows this connectivity. Neutral [electron counting](../../chemistry.md#electron-counting) assigns five [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) to each Cp and gives

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{VE(Ti)}=4+5+4(2)+1=18.}
$$

Equivalently, the two Cp anions require an average [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) Ti(I), hence $d^3$; ionic counting gives $3+6+8+1=18$. **The formal Ti–Ti [bond order](../../chemistry.md#bond-order) is one.** The large coordination envelope can make this bond weak and long; reaching 18 [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) is bookkeeping, rather than proof of a short, strong bond. There is no need to invent extra CO bridges or a higher Ti–Ti [bond order](../../chemistry.md#bond-order).

<h4 id="b6/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b6/b)

<h5 id="b6/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b6/b/iii)

A suitable structure is a triangular Ti$_3$ [metal cluster](../../chemistry.md#metal-cluster) with one [1,2-Bis(dimethylphosphino)ethane](../../chemistry.md#1-2-bis-dimethylphosphino-ethane) chelate and one terminal [chloride](../../chemistry.md#chloride) at each [titanium](../../chemical-substance.md#titanium), and one [bridging ligand](../../chemistry.md#bridging-ligand) $\mu$-Cl spanning each edge. Include a direct Ti–Ti single [metal-metal bond](../../chemical-bond.md#metal-metal-bond) along each edge. The last panel distinguishes these direct bonds from the [chloride](../../chemistry.md#chloride) bridges.

The six chlorides have total formal charge $-6$, so equivalent centers are **Ti(II), $d^2$**. At each [titanium](../../chemical-substance.md#titanium) the two [phosphorus](../../chemical-substance.md#phosphorus) donors supply four [electrons](../../physics.md#electron), the terminal [chloride](../../chemistry.md#chloride) supplies two, and the two incident [chloride](../../chemistry.md#chloride) bridges each donate a pair in ionic [electron counting](../../chemistry.md#electron-counting). Two incident Ti–Ti single bonds supply another two [electrons](../../physics.md#electron):

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{VE(Ti)}=2_d+4_{\mathrm{P,P}}+2_{\mathrm{Cl}}+2(2)_{\mu\mathrm{-Cl}}+2_{\mathrm{Ti-Ti}}=14.}
$$

Each $d^2$ center can contribute one [electron](../../physics.md#electron) to each of its two edge bonds, making three bonds from six [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) overall. **This is a 14-[electron](../../physics.md#electron) early-[metal cluster](../../chemistry.md#metal-cluster), not an 18-[electron](../../physics.md#electron) complex.** Forcing the [18-electron rule](../../chemistry.md#18-electron-rule) here would invent excessive bond orders. [chloride](../../chemistry.md#chloride) bridges donate to more than one center, so summing local counts is not the same as counting distinct [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) of the whole cluster.

<h3 id="b6/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#b6)

<h4 id="b6/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b6/c)

The principal electronic variable is the net population of [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–[metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) [bonding molecular orbitals](../../chemistry.md#bonding-orbital) relative to [antibonding molecular orbitals](../../chemistry.md#antibonding-orbital). For otherwise comparable complexes, a larger [bond order](../../chemistry.md#bond-order) generally gives a shorter [metal-metal bond](../../chemical-bond.md#metal-metal-bond); removing an antibonding [electron](../../physics.md#electron) can shorten a bond even though the total [electron](../../physics.md#electron) count falls. Different orbital symmetries also matter: sigma, pi and delta overlaps respond differently to distance and rotation of the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) fragments.

[metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) size, [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) and radial orbital extent set the underlying distance scale. Comparing a first-row [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) with a larger heavier congener does not isolate [bond order](../../chemistry.md#bond-order). Increasing positive charge often contracts the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) orbitals, but can also change [electron](../../physics.md#electron) occupancy, ligand binding and electrostatic interactions. The [ligand field](../../chemistry.md#ligand-field) and donor/acceptor properties of the surrounding [ligands in coordination chemistry](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry) determine which [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) orbitals remain available for direct bonding and how much density instead participates in [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding).

Finally, the geometry can enforce or oppose the preferred separation. [Bridging ligands](../../chemistry.md#bridging-ligand) impose bond-angle and bond-length constraints; bulky substituents produce [steric hindrance](../../stereochemistry.md#steric-hindrance); crystal packing can perturb the observed distance. **A [bond length](../../chemical-bond.md#bond-length) is evidence for bonding only in its electronic and structural context.** In particular, two bridges can hold [metals](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) close without proving a high direct [bond order](../../chemistry.md#bond-order), while a sterically congested formal single bond can be unusually long.

<h3 id="b6/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#b6)

<h4 id="b6/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b6/d)

For the doubly bridged tetracarbonyl, each [iron](../../chemical-substance.md#iron) has one terminal [carbonyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand), two [bridging carbonyl ligands](../../chemistry.md#bridging-carbonyl-ligand) and one [cyclopentadienyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#cyclopentadienyl-ligand). Neutral [electron counting](../../chemistry.md#electron-counting) gives $8+5+2+2=17$ before direct bonding, so a single Fe–Fe bond gives 18 at each [iron](../../chemical-substance.md#iron). For the triply bridged tricarbonyl, each [iron](../../chemical-substance.md#iron) instead has $8+5+3=16$ before direct bonding; formal completion to 18 requires a double Fe–Fe bond. Equivalently, the total fragment counts before [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–[metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) bonding are 34 and 32 respectively.

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Cp_2Fe_2(CO)_2(\mu\! -CO)_2}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{Fe-Fe}}=2.539\ \text{Å},\quad\text{formal single bond},\\
\mathrm{Cp^{*}_2Fe_2(\mu\! -CO)_3}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{Fe-Fe}}=2.265\ \text{Å},\quad\text{formal double bond}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The increased direct [bond order](../../chemistry.md#bond-order) explains the substantial contraction. The [pentamethylcyclopentadienyl ligands](../../chemistry.md#pentamethylcyclopentadienyl-ligand) also alter steric and electronic effects, and bridge geometry changes, so this is not a controlled measurement of [bond order](../../chemistry.md#bond-order) alone. Nevertheless, the markedly shorter distance in the less carbonyl-saturated dimer is consistent with stronger direct Fe–Fe bonding. The isolated triply bridged compound was characterized structurally and magnetically in the primary work.[1985, pp. 4561–4562, isolation, crystal structure and triplet assignment](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#dinuclear-18-electron-species-having-a-triplet-ground-state-isolation-characterization-and-crystal-structure-of-photogenerated-eta-5-c5me5-2fe2-mu-co-3-by-josephine-paw-blaha-and-others)

<h3 id="b6/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#b6)

<h4 id="b6/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#b6/e)

**The dimer has two unpaired [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) and a triplet ground state, $S=1$.** Its approximately threefold-symmetric Fe$_2$(CO)$_3$ core supplies a pair of degenerate frontier [molecular orbitals](../../chemistry.md#molecular-orbital). The last two [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) occupy separate members with parallel [spins](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin), favored by exchange, rather than pairing in one. A useful qualitative picture is a [sigma bond](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-bond) plus two perpendicular, singly occupied pi half-bonds: together these support a formal Fe–Fe double bond while retaining [paramagnetism](../../electromagnetism.md#paramagnetism). The [spin-only magnetic moment](../../electromagnetism.md#spin-only-magnetic-moment) for $S=1$ is $\sqrt8\,\mu_B\simeq2.83\,\mu_B$; real moments can include orbital and [spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin)–orbit effects.

The formal 18-[electron](../../physics.md#electron) count does not require every [molecular orbital](../../chemistry.md#molecular-orbital) to be doubly occupied. It is therefore compatible with an open-shell dimer. The primary structural/magnetic study establishes this unusual [triplet state](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin-one-half-triplet-state); an isolated-atom high-[spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) $d^7$ diagram would not describe the coupled [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–ligand system.[1985, pp. 4561–4562, triplet electronic structure of the dinuclear 18-electron species](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#dinuclear-18-electron-species-having-a-triplet-ground-state-isolation-characterization-and-crystal-structure-of-photogenerated-eta-5-c5me5-2fe2-mu-co-3-by-josephine-paw-blaha-and-others)

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b7/a)

<h5 id="b7/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b7/a/i)

In the ionic assignment, both tetrahedral anions would require Mn(VII), $d^0$. Such a high [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) is strongly oxidizing and is best supported by strong bonding to [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). Short Mn–O bonds combine good [orbital overlap](../../chemistry.md#orbital-overlap) with [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen)'s resistance to further oxidation. This stabilizes [permanganate](../../chemistry.md#permanganate) relative to a hypothetical all-[sulfide](../../chemistry.md#sulfide) analogue.

[sulfide](../../chemistry.md#sulfide) is larger, more polarizable and much easier to oxidize than [oxide](../../chemical-substance.md#oxide). An attempted Mn(VII) [sulfide](../../chemistry.md#sulfide) would therefore combine an exceptionally oxidizing [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) center with reducing ligands: internal [redox](../../chemical-reaction.md#redox) can reduce [manganese](../../chemical-substance.md#manganese) while oxidizing [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur), for example toward S–S-bonded or elemental [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) products. The larger [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) donors also provide less favorable short multiple bonding to the small, highly charged [manganese](../../chemical-substance.md#manganese) center. **The [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) compound supports Mn(VII); the [sulfide](../../chemistry.md#sulfide) combination is [redox](../../chemical-reaction.md#redox)-incompatible.** This predicts instability without requiring a specific unprovided decomposition product.

<h4 id="b7/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b7/a)

<h5 id="b7/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b7/a/ii)

For an octahedral complex, strong axial [sigma donation](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-donation) raises the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–ligand antibonding $e_g$-derived orbitals. A [hydride](../../chemical-substance.md#hydride) is a very strong sigma donor: its occupied $1s$ orbital overlaps effectively along a [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–ligand axis and has no filled perpendicular p orbitals to act as a conventional pi donor. It can therefore produce a large [octahedral splitting](../../chemistry.md#octahedral-splitting) by raising $e_g$ relative to $t_{2g}$.

[Triphenylphosphine](../../chemical-substance.md#triphenylphosphine) also gives [sigma donation](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-donation), but its useful acceptor orbitals additionally receive [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding). This lowers the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) $t_{2g}$-derived levels and enlarges their separation from $e_g$. The acceptors have substantial P–C antibonding character; invoking a dominant [phosphorus](../../chemical-substance.md#phosphorus) $3d$ acceptor is unnecessary. Thus **[hydride](../../chemical-substance.md#hydride) is strong-field principally through sigma bonding, while PPh$_3$ combines [sigma donation](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-donation) with pi acceptance**. The [spectrochemical series](../../chemistry.md#spectrochemical-series) describes the resulting splitting, not just a ranking of ligand basicity.

<h4 id="b7/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b7/a)

<h5 id="b7/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b7/a/iii)

The [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) is Cr(II), $d^4$. A [chloride ligand](../../chemistry.md#chloride-ligand) produces a relatively weak [ligand field](../../chemistry.md#ligand-field), so the octahedral center is a [high-spin complex](../../chemistry.md#high-spin-complex) with $t_{2g}^3e_g^1$, $S=2$. The singly occupied $e_g$ pair is orbitally degenerate: $d_{z^2}$ and $d_{x^2-y^2}$ cannot retain identical occupancies once the distortion selects one. This is a strong setting for the [Jahn-Teller effect](../../chemistry.md#jahn-teller-effect).

On tetragonal elongation, the two axial Cr–Cl distances increase. Repulsion is reduced most for $d_{z^2}$, so it falls relative to $d_{x^2-y^2}$; $d_{xz},d_{yz}$ also fall relative to $d_{xy}$. The single $e_g$ [electron](../../physics.md#electron) occupies the lower $d_{z^2}$-derived level. The electronic stabilization offsets an elastic cost until a distorted equilibrium geometry is reached. **The high-[spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) $d^4$ occupancy drives an elongated, rather than regular, octahedron.** The three lower-set [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) remain one per orbital in this simple diagram; it is primarily the unequal $e_g$ occupancy that produces the strong distortion.

<h4 id="b7/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b7/a)

<h5 id="b7/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b7/a/iv)

**The all-carbonyl [hydride](../../chemical-substance.md#hydride), HCo(CO)$_4$, is more acidic.** Loss of $\mathrm H^+$ gives $[\mathrm{Co(CO)_4}]^-$, whose negative charge is stabilized by [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding) into four [carbonyl ligands](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand). Replacing one CO by [triphenyl phosphite](../../chemical-substance.md#triphenyl-phosphite) reduces the net [electron](../../physics.md#electron)-withdrawing character of the ligand set relative to four carbonyls. The substituted [hydride](../../chemical-substance.md#hydride) is more [electron](../../physics.md#electron) rich and its conjugate-base anion is less stabilized against [protonation](../../chemical-reaction.md#protonation).

$$
\mathrm{HCo(CO)_3L}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm H^++[\mathrm{Co(CO)_3L}]^-,\qquad L=\mathrm{CO}\ \text{or}\ \mathrm{P(OPh)_3}.
$$

Both [conjugate bases](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#conjugate-base) have a conventional 18-[electron](../../physics.md#electron) count, so the difference is not a missing-[electron](../../physics.md#electron) argument. It is the relative stabilization of acid and base by the [ligands in coordination chemistry](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry). This concerns heterolytic [deprotonation](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#deprotonation), not the homolytic Co–H [bond-dissociation energy](../../chemical-bond.md#bond-dissociation-energy).

<h3 id="b7/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b7/b)

For comparable octahedral environments with the same donor set, the expected qualitative order is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Mn^{2+}<Fe^{2+}<Fe^{3+}<Mo^{3+}<Rh^{3+}<Pt^{4+}}.}
$$

Across comparable first-row ions, the increasing effective attraction and changing [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–ligand distances tend to increase [octahedral splitting](../../chemistry.md#octahedral-splitting), giving Mn(II) below Fe(II). Raising the charge on the same [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) gives Fe(III) above Fe(II), because the ligand interaction generally strengthens and [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–ligand distances contract. Moving from $3d$ to more radially extended $4d$ orbitals gives larger [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–ligand overlap, placing Mo(III) above Fe(III); Rh(III) lies further along the same $4d$ series and typically gives the larger splitting. The $5d$ ion Pt(IV) combines the heavier row with high charge and gives the largest splitting here.

**This is a matched-environment trend, not an intrinsic list of constants for bare ions.** Actual $\Delta$ also depends strongly on donor identity, geometry and distance. It would be meaningless to compare an unspecified weak-field tetrahedral compound with a strong-field octahedral compound solely through this ordering.

<h3 id="b7/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b7/c)

Start from an [octahedral molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#octahedral-molecular-geometry) with ligands on $\pm x,\pm y,\pm z$, with $e_g=(d_{z^2},d_{x^2-y^2})$ above $t_{2g}=(d_{xy},d_{xz},d_{yz})$. Remove the $-z$ donor while keeping the other five positions. Loss of axial repulsion lowers $d_{z^2}$ substantially; $d_{x^2-y^2}$ still points directly at four basal donors and remains highest. Loss of an axial donor also lowers the orbitals with $z$ dependence relative to $d_{xy}$. The remaining fourfold axis keeps $d_{xz}$ and $d_{yz}$ degenerate.

The usual qualitative [square-pyramidal molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#square-pyramidal-molecular-geometry) diagram, shown in the last panel above, is therefore

$$
\boxed{d_{x^2-y^2}\ >\ d_{z^2}\ >\ d_{xy}\ >\ d_{xz}=d_{yz}.}
$$

The corresponding $C_{4v}$ labels are $b_1,a_1,b_2,e$. Energies can be re-zeroed to the new orbital barycenter after ligand removal. Symmetry fixes the degeneracy and labels, but does not universally fix the separation of $d_{z^2}$ and $d_{xy}$: a weak or distant axial donor can reverse this middle pair on approach to the square-planar limit. The displayed ordering assumes an appreciable fifth axial interaction.

[iron](../../chemical-substance.md#iron) remains Fe(II), $d^6$, in both states; the change is a [spin crossover](../../chemistry.md#spin-crossover), rather than a change in [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state). $$

This matches the observed moment. At low [temperature](../../temperature.md) a [low-spin complex](../../chemistry.md#low-spin-complex) with $t_{2g}^6e_g^0$ has $S=0$ and shows [diamagnetism](../../electromagnetism.md#diamagnetism). **Cooling switches high-[spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) $d^6$ to low-[spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) $d^6$.** Emptying the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–ligand antibonding $e_g$ orbitals strengthens and shortens the Fe–N bonds, accounting for the contraction by roughly $0.2\ \text{Å}$.

The [octahedral splitting](../../chemistry.md#octahedral-splitting) and [electron pairing energy](../../chemistry.md#electron-pairing-energy) must be sufficiently close for both states to be accessible. The low-[spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) state gains electronic energy, while the high-[spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) state has greater [spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) degeneracy and usually greater vibrational [entropy](../../entropy.md) because of its softer bonds. The resulting [Gibbs free energy](../../thermodynamic-potential.md#gibbs-free-energy) balance favors high [spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) at higher [temperature](../../temperature.md). The two endpoint observations do not by themselves specify [hysteresis](../../critical-phenomenon.md#hysteresis) or transition cooperativity; those require a [temperature](../../temperature.md) series. Coupled structural and magnetic measurements distinguish this explanation from a mere bond-length anomaly.

A plausible explanation is a stronger, less labile Hf–C interaction, reflecting the different bonding of the heavier [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal). Breaking and reorganizing that interaction in [migratory insertion](../../chemical-reaction.md#migratory-insertion) can raise the propagation barrier and reduce the insertion rate. The chain length depends on competing rates, not on propagation speed alone. In a simple stationary competition,

$$
\overline X_n\ \sim\ \frac{k_p[\mathrm{propene}]}{k_{\mathrm{tr,eff}}},
$$

where the denominator includes the operative chain-transfer or chain-stopping rates per active chain. **The Hf catalyst can propagate more slowly yet make longer chains if transfer is suppressed by a still larger factor.** Slower insertion alone would not explain longer [polymers](../../polymer.md). Distinguishing particular transfer pathways would require [polymer](../../polymer.md) end-group analysis and the concentration dependence of molecular mass and [polymerization](../../polymer.md#polymerization) rate.

In [borazine](../../chemical-substance.md#borazine), each B and N is approximately trigonal planar. The alternating ring contains a sigma framework with perpendicular p orbitals; the [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) [lone pairs](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) can donate into the boron acceptor orbitals. Six pi [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) can therefore be distributed around the ring, giving partial B–N multiple-bond character rather than six ordinary isolated single bonds. Equal ring distances are compatible with this symmetric delocalization.

The [electronegativity](../../electronegativity.md) difference makes each B–N bond strongly polar, $\mathrm{B^{\delta+}{-}N^{\delta-}}$. The occupied pi orbitals are weighted toward N, and the empty acceptors toward B. In [benzene](../../chemical-substance.md#benzene), equivalent [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) centers give much more even pi distribution and strong resistance to addition that destroys the aromatic system. **[borazine](../../chemical-substance.md#borazine) resembles [benzene](../../chemical-substance.md#benzene) geometrically but has substantially more polarized, more reactive bonding.** It presents electrophilic boron sites to [nucleophiles](../../chemical-reaction.md#nucleophile) and [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) donors to [Lewis acids](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#lewis-acid). Polar reagents can add across B–N, and [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) can cleave the ring; [benzene](../../chemical-substance.md#benzene) ordinarily favors substitution under suitable conditions rather than facile polar addition or [hydrolysis](../../chemical-reaction.md#hydrolysis). The label “inorganic [benzene](../../chemical-substance.md#benzene)” should therefore not be interpreted as identical aromatic stabilization or reactivity. [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen)-bound Lewis-acid adducts and hydrogen-halide additions demonstrate this contrast experimentally.[2008, p. 136 introduction and pp. 137–142, addition chemistry and nitrogen-bound adducts](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#aluminium-and-gallium-trihalide-adducts-of-2-4-6-triorganylborazines-and-the-synthesis-of-triorganylborazinium-tetrabromoaluminates-by-b-gemund-and-others)

This accounts for B functionalization without needing cleavage of a ring B–N bond or selection of a [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen)-bound product. It does not imply that arbitrary N–H activation is impossible under every condition.

Replacing an electronegative [bromine](../../chemical-substance.md#bromine) substituent by a [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) fragment changes the boron's [electron](../../physics.md#electron) demand. Relative to the B–Br sites, the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)-bound boron is less effective at accepting N-to-B pi donation, so its adjacent B–N bonds are expected to have less multiple-bond character and be longer; donation toward the remaining B–Br sites produces compensating unequal ring bonds. The original threefold equivalence is lost. The detailed lengths depend on the full electronic structure; a localized drawing with three rigid alternating double bonds would conceal the redistribution that the unequal lengths reveal.

In the [chromium](../../chemical-substance.md#chromium) complex, Cr(0) is $d^6$ and receives substantial donation from four [carbonyl ligands](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand). One productive [acid catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#acid-catalysis) route protonates N1, making C6 more electrophilic. [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) then adds at C6 while the C6=N1 pi [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) move onto N1. Subsequent [proton transfers](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#proton-transfer) generate a [carbinolamine](../../chemical-substance.md#carbinolamine) and then a protonated amino leaving group:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{N1{=}C6(NH_2)}&\xrightarrow{\ +H^+\ }\mathrm{N1H^+{=}C6(NH_2)}\\
&\xrightarrow{\ +H_2O\ }\mathrm{N1H{-}C6(OH_2^+)(NH_2)}\\
&\xrightarrow{\ -H^+\ }\mathrm{N1H{-}C6(OH)(NH_2)}\\
&\xrightarrow{\ +H^+\ }\mathrm{N1H{-}C6(OH)(NH_3^+)}\\
&\xrightarrow{\ -NH_3\ }\mathrm{N1H{-}C6({=}OH^+)}\\
&\xrightarrow{\ -H^+\ }\mathrm{N1H{-}C6({=}O)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

These are local bonding fragments: C6 retains its bond to the adjoining ring [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) at every step. In the addition step, an [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) forms C6–O and the C6=N1 [pi bond](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond) opens. During collapse, an [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) reforms C6=O and the C6–N6 bond breaks toward the departing [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen). Solvent mediates each [proton transfer](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#proton-transfer); no direct unsupported transfer across a long distance is required.

**[water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) supplies the new C6 [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen), and [ammonia](../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) is expelled after [protonation](../../chemical-reaction.md#protonation) of the leaving group.** Final [deprotonation](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#deprotonation) gives the [lactam](../../chemical-substance.md#lactam) form of [inosine](../../molecule.md#inosine). In acidic bulk solution the released [ammonia](../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) is largely trapped as [ammonium](../../chemistry.md#ammonium). The catalytic [proton transfers](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#proton-transfer) regenerate acid around the substitution cycle; [ammonium](../../chemistry.md#ammonium) trapping separately affects the net proton balance. This is [hydrolytic deamination](../../chemical-reaction.md#hydrolytic-deamination), not cleavage of the [glycosidic bond](../../covalent-bond.md#glycosidic-bond).

<h3 id="b10/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B10](#b10)

<h4 id="b10/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b10/b)

A [glutamic acid residue](../../protein.md#glutamic-acid-residue) supplies a carboxyl group whose [protonation](../../chemical-reaction.md#protonation) state can change with the [active site](../../enzyme.md#active-site) environment. The following five schematic contributions are possible in a hypothetical [enzyme](../../enzyme.md); they are alternatives and overlapping physical effects, rather than five independently established reactions of this [enzyme](../../enzyme.md). In the diagrams $E$ is the protein tether, and $C6$ is the reacting [purine](../../molecule.md#purine) [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon).

<a id="b10/b/image-five-possible-catalytic-contributions-of-a-glutamate-side-chain-to-hydrolytic-deamination"></a>
![](../../past-exam-of-the-chemistry-b-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2024/paper-2-glutamate.png)

**[Figure 6](#b10/b/image-five-possible-catalytic-contributions-of-a-glutamate-side-chain-to-hydrolytic-deamination). Five possible catalytic contributions of a glutamate side chain to hydrolytic deamination**.

**[general acid catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#general-acid-catalysis):** $\mathrm{E{-}CO_2H\cdots N1\to E{-}CO_2^-+H{-}N1^+}$. Donation of the carboxyl proton activates the amidine-like C6 center for [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) attack. The same acid could instead assist the amino leaving group: $\mathrm{E{-}CO_2H+{-}NH_2\to E{-}CO_2^-+{-}NH_3^+}$. These are two locations for [general acid catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#general-acid-catalysis), rather than two different categories.

**[general base catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#general-base-catalysis):** $\mathrm{E{-}CO_2^-\cdots H{-}OH\to E{-}CO_2H+OH^-}$, coupled to [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) attack on C6. Accepting a proton makes [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) a more effective [nucleophile](../../chemical-reaction.md#nucleophile). This does not require an appreciable pool of free [hydroxide](../../chemistry.md#hydroxide) in bulk solution.

**[covalent catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#covalent-catalysis):** a glutamate [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) can in principle attack activated C6 to form $\mathrm{C6{-}O{-}C(O){-}E}$. After addition/elimination replaces the amino group, [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) must hydrolyze that transient [enzyme](../../enzyme.md)–[substrate](../../enzyme.md#substrate-biochemistry) bond to release product and regenerate $\mathrm{E{-}CO_2H/CO_2^-}$. Such a pathway is a chemically distinct possibility, not established by the given structure.

**[electrostatic catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#electrostatic-catalysis):** the [carboxylate](../../chemical-substance.md#carboxylate) can stabilize a developing positive charge, for example in a protonated [purine](../../molecule.md#purine) or amino leaving group; a protonated carboxyl can also donate a [hydrogen bond](../../chemical-bond.md#hydrogen-bond) to an [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) developing negative charge. This contributes only if the reacting configuration is stabilized relative to the bound starting state. A [carboxylate](../../chemical-substance.md#carboxylate) placed next to an [oxyanion](../../chemistry.md#oxyanion) without compensating geometry would instead be unfavorable.

**[proximity and orientation catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#proximity-and-orientation-catalysis):** donor and acceptor [hydrogen bonds](../../chemical-bond.md#hydrogen-bond) can hold the [purine](../../molecule.md#purine) and attacking [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) in a productive arrangement, schematically $\mathrm{E{-}CO_2H\cdots N1{-}C6\cdots OH_2}$. Binding reduces unproductive orientations and can distort the [substrate](../../enzyme.md#substrate-biochemistry) toward a reactive geometry. This last contribution is sometimes described through [substrate](../../enzyme.md#substrate-biochemistry) binding or strain; a binding interaction is not automatically catalytic unless it improves access to the [transition state](../../chemical-kinetics.md#transition-state).

<h3 id="b10/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B10](#b10)

<h4 id="b10/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b10/c)

The zinc ion is coordinated by protein donors and a [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) placed near the reacting C6 center. **Zinc acts as a [Lewis acid](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#lewis-acid) that activates and positions [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water).** Its positive charge withdraws [electron density](../../chemistry.md#electron-density) from [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen), lowers the effective p$K_a$ of coordinated [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) and facilitates formation of the attacking [hydroxide](../../chemistry.md#hydroxide), potentially with assistance from a nearby base. The same [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–[oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) interaction can stabilize developing negative charge as the addition intermediate forms. Zinc stays Zn(II); this is [metal ion catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#metal-ion-catalysis), not a zinc [redox](../../chemical-reaction.md#redox) cycle.

The structure identifies a plausible reaction geometry, but a static X-ray model does not by itself establish the [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water)'s [protonation](../../chemical-reaction.md#protonation) state, proton-transfer order or the rate-limiting step. Structural work with bound transition-state analogues independently supports a zinc-containing catalytic center.[1991, pp. 1278–1284, catalytic zinc and the bound transition-state analogue](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#atomic-structure-of-adenosine-deaminase-complexed-with-a-transition-state-analog-understanding-catalysis-and-immunodeficiency-mutations-by-david-k-wilson-and-others) An appropriate experimental test would compare native [enzyme](../../enzyme.md), a [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)-depleted preparation and stoichiometrically zinc-reconstituted [enzyme](../../enzyme.md), checking retained fold and active [enzyme](../../enzyme.md) concentration. Loss and recovery of activity with those controls distinguishes a catalytic [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) requirement from nonspecific denaturation.

<h3 id="b10/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B10](#b10)

<h4 id="b10/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b10/d)

<h5 id="b10/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b10/d/i)

**His238 contributes strongly to turnover, with a plausible acid/base or electrostatic role near the attacking [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water).** Replacing its [imidazole](../../chemical-substance.md#imidazole) by the methyl side chain of [alanine](../../chemical-substance.md#alanine) removes both proton-transfer capacity and a positioned polar group, so the large fall in [turnover number](../../mathematical-biology.md#turnover-number) fits impaired chemical catalysis. A candidate role is acceptance of a proton from zinc-bound [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water), or stabilization and subsequent proton shuttling around the attacking [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen).

Little change in the [Michaelis constant](../../mathematical-biology.md#michaelis-constant) suggests that the major kinetic defect is not a gross loss of [substrate](../../enzyme.md#substrate-biochemistry) recognition, but $K_M$ is not generally a binding [dissociation constant](../../chemical-potential.md#dissociation-constant): $K_M=(k_{-1}+k_{\mathrm{cat}})/k_1$ for the elementary scheme. Nor does reduced $k_{\mathrm{cat}}$ uniquely prove that His238 is the initial general base. Purified-protein controls should check fold, zinc occupancy and active concentration. Importantly, primary mouse-[enzyme](../../enzyme.md) experiments found that His238 mutants could still hydrate a [substrate](../../enzyme.md#substrate-biochemistry) analogue, directly illustrating this mechanistic limitation.[1996, abstract and pp. 15019–15028, purine-riboside hydration by mutant enzyme](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#site-directed-mutagenesis-of-histidine-238-in-mouse-adenosine-deaminase-substitution-of-histidine-238-does-not-impede-hydroxylate-formation-by-v-sideraki-and-others)

<h4 id="b10/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b10/d)

<h5 id="b10/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b10/d/ii)

**An ionizable Asp296 carboxyl group is not essential to the measured catalytic pathway at pH 7.** [Asparagine](../../chemical-substance.md#asparagine) retains a similar side-chain size and an amide donor/acceptor pair, while losing the carboxyl group's ordinary acid/base chemistry and negative charge. Preserved kinetics are therefore consistent with a binding or positioning [hydrogen bond](../../chemical-bond.md#hydrogen-bond) that survives the conservative substitution, or with a dispensable/redundant contact.

This does not prove that Asp296 makes no interaction in the native [enzyme](../../enzyme.md), nor that every substitution at that position would be harmless. The displayed Asp296 contact is also distinct from Asp295, which coordinates zinc; their neighboring residue numbers must not be conflated. Other pH values could expose a contribution hidden by the assay condition.

<h4 id="b10/d/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b10/d)

<h5 id="b10/d/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b10/d/iii)

**Glu217 is important for catalysis, most naturally through [proton transfer](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#proton-transfer) at the [purine](../../molecule.md#purine) ring.** Changing a [glutamic acid residue](../../protein.md#glutamic-acid-residue) to [glutamine](../../chemical-substance.md#glutamine) preserves much of the side-chain size and hydrogen-bonding geometry, while removing an ordinary [carboxylic acid](../../chemical-substance.md#carboxylic-acid)/base pair. Its placement near N1 makes acid donation there a plausible way to activate C6 for [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) addition and stabilize the resulting nonaromatic intermediate. The large turnover defect with little change in the [Michaelis constant](../../mathematical-biology.md#michaelis-constant) supports that catalytic interpretation, subject to the same $K_M$ and protein-integrity qualifications as above.

The protonated carboxyl drawn at pH 7 need not behave like free glutamate in [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water): local electrostatics and burial can shift its p$K_a$, and [protonation](../../chemical-reaction.md#protonation) may change during the cycle. The mutation alone cannot choose between an initially protonated Glu217 acid and a Glu217 residue that first accepts and then relays a proton. Primary mutational work supports catalytic roles for active-site [histidine](../../chemical-substance.md#histidine) and glutamate groups while explicitly comparing purified [enzymes](../../enzyme.md) and zinc content.[1993, pp. 5464–5470, kinetic and zinc-content comparisons](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#mutational-analysis-of-active-site-residues-of-human-adenosine-deaminase-by-d-bhaumik-and-others)

<h4 id="b10/d/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b10/d)

<h5 id="b10/d/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b10/d/iv)

**The [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) installed at C6 is derived from solvent [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water).** An attacking zinc-bound [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) or [hydroxide](../../chemistry.md#hydroxide) therefore provides the straightforward explanation of the [oxygen-18](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen-18) label. The result supports hydrolytic addition rather than direct [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) transfer from a pre-existing protein carboxyl group as the sole [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) source.

It does not by itself establish the identity of the base, the timing of [proton transfers](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#proton-transfer), or exclusion of a transient covalent [enzyme](../../enzyme.md) intermediate: [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) could introduce the labeled [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) during breakdown of such an intermediate. Product [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) exchange must also be excluded. Incubate preformed unlabeled [inosine](../../molecule.md#inosine) in labeled [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) with and without [enzyme](../../enzyme.md), and use early-time quenched reactions to compare incorporation with product formation. These controls test whether labeling reports the formation step rather than later exchange.

<h3 id="b10/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B10](#b10)

<h4 id="b10/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#b10/e)

A chemically coherent mechanism combines zinc-activated [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) addition with a [proton relay](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#proton-relay) involving His238 and Glu217. In a simple route consistent with the displayed contacts, neutral His238 accepts a proton from zinc-bound [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) while protonated Glu217 donates to [substrate](../../enzyme.md#substrate-biochemistry) N1. These transfers can be coupled to the bond-forming event rather than requiring accumulation of a free, doubly activated [substrate](../../enzyme.md#substrate-biochemistry). Zinc-bound [hydroxide](../../chemistry.md#hydroxide) attacks C6; the C6=N1 pi [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) move onto N1, yielding the tetrahedral C6 intermediate bearing OH and NH$_2$ while N1 bears H.

<a id="b10/e/image-one-proton-relay-mechanism-consistent-with-water-derived-oxygen-and-the-catalytic-residues"></a>
![](../../past-exam-of-the-chemistry-b-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2024/paper-2-enzyme.png)

**[Figure 7](#b10/e/image-one-proton-relay-mechanism-consistent-with-water-derived-oxygen-and-the-catalytic-residues). One proton-relay mechanism consistent with water-derived oxygen and the catalytic residues**.

Next, the exocyclic amino group receives a proton, through His238, Glu217 and/or intervening [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) as permitted by the changing geometry. The [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) is deprotonated as it forms the C6 carbonyl, and the C6–N6 bond cleaves to release [ammonia](../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia). These transfers can be coupled in the collapse step, avoiding expulsion of $\mathrm{NH_2^-}$. Zinc stabilizes the reacting [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) and the organized intermediate; the ring regains conjugation in the [inosine](../../molecule.md#inosine) product. Product dissociation, fresh [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) binding and proton exchange with solvent reset the catalytic groups.

**The core result is zinc-assisted addition–elimination, with acid/base [proton transfers](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#proton-transfer) and [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) supplying C6 [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen).** The contributions are [metal ion catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#metal-ion-catalysis), [general base catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#general-base-catalysis), [general acid catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#general-acid-catalysis), [electrostatic catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#electrostatic-catalysis) and [proximity and orientation catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#proximity-and-orientation-catalysis). The proposed route does not require [covalent catalysis](../../chemical-kinetics.md#covalent-catalysis) by a protein side chain: a [substrate](../../enzyme.md#substrate-biochemistry)–[water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) C–O bond is not an [enzyme](../../enzyme.md)–[substrate](../../enzyme.md#substrate-biochemistry) covalent linkage. Asp296 can retain a structural hydrogen-bonding role without acting as an indispensable acid/base.

The data establish the importance of His238 and Glu217 but do not uniquely assign the entire [proton relay](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#proton-relay). In particular, an alternative has Glu217 first deprotonate [water](../../chemical-substance.md#water) and then deliver that proton to N1, with His238 assisting [hydroxide](../../chemistry.md#hydroxide) stabilization and later amino-group [protonation](../../chemical-reaction.md#protonation). The primary observation that His238 substitution need not abolish analogue hydration cautions against calling its initial-base role proven.[1996, pp. 15019–15028, retained hydroxylate formation and proposed electrostatic contribution](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#site-directed-mutagenesis-of-histidine-238-in-mouse-adenosine-deaminase-substitution-of-histidine-238-does-not-impede-hydroxylate-formation-by-v-sideraki-and-others) pH-rate profiles, solvent isotope effects and time-resolved intermediate experiments would discriminate these refinements. The diagram is a proposed catalytic route, not a structure directly observed by the crystal experiment.
