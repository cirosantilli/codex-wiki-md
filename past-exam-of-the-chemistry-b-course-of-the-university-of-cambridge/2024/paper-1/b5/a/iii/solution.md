<h1 id="b5/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**C is the best geometrical match for a strongly bound 1:1 complex with E.** Both have an adjacent [amide](../../../../../../amide.md) donor–acceptor edge. Arrange them head-to-tail so that the N–H of E donates to the [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) of C, and the N–H of C donates to the [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) of E. This closes the familiar two-[hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md)-bond cyclic [amide](../../../../../../amide.md) motif, illustrated below.

C combines a strong acceptor with a useful donor in the correct spacing. B has a stronger individual donor, but its [pyridine](../../../../../../pyridine.md) N and OH are separated around the ring and do not reproduce the compact adjacent [amide](../../../../../../amide.md) edge of E. A lacks a matching donor, and D's N–H is weak and has the wrong geometry for the same [amide](../../../../../../amide.md)–[amide](../../../../../../amide.md) motif. **Site strength and spatial complementarity must be considered together**; multiplying two isolated probe constants is not a reliable prediction of the complete association equilibrium. Self-association of C or E can compete and must be included in a quantitative binding analysis.
