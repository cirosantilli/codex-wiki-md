↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Oxazolination occurs para to [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) on the ring that does not bear [bromine](../../../../../../bromine.md).** The product is 2-\[4-(4-bromophenoxy)phenyl\]-[2-Oxazoline](../../../../../../2-oxazoline.md), shown in the final panel above. The brominated ring retains its para [bromine](../../../../../../bromine.md); the other ring receives the new oxazolinyl group at its open para position.

The diaryl [ether](../../../../../../ether.md) [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) activates both rings toward [electrophilic aromatic substitution](../../../../../../electrophilic-aromatic-substitution.md). On the brominated ring, its least crowded para position is blocked, and [bromine](../../../../../../bromine.md) also decreases ring reactivity inductively. The unbrominated ring has an accessible para position, favored over its ortho sites by steric effects. This is substitution of an aromatic [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md), not displacement of the aryl bromide.

The formula provides a useful consistency check: $\mathrm{C_{12}H_9BrO}$ loses one aromatic H and acquires $\mathrm{C_3H_4NO}$, giving **$\mathrm{C_{15}H_{12}BrNO_2}$**. It cannot by itself distinguish positional isomers; the directing and steric arguments select the connectivity.
