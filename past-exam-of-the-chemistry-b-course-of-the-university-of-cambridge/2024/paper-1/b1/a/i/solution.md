<h1 id="b1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An [enolate](../../../../../../enolate.md) is an [ambident nucleophile](../../../../../../ambident-nucleophile.md): the negative charge is concentrated toward [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md), while its occupied conjugated orbital also has substantial reactive amplitude at the alpha [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md). An alkyl [iodide](../../../../../../iodide.md) presents an accessible, polarizable [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) [electrophile](../../../../../../electrophile.md). [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) attack through an [SN2 reaction](../../../../../../sn2-reaction.md) is generally kinetically favorable, while the more strongly charged [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) is often coordinated to the [counterion](../../../../../../counterion.md) or solvated. The [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) reforms as the new C–C bond is made.

$$
\mathrm{R{-}C(O^-){=}CHR'+MeI}\longrightarrow\mathrm{R{-}C(O){-}CH(Me)R'+I^-}.
$$

**[carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) alkylation restores the [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) and makes the new C–C bond.** [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) alkylation instead gives an enol [ether](../../../../../../ether.md). The two outcomes are not resonance contributors of one product, and their ratio is not determined just by where the largest formal negative charge is drawn. [electrophile](../../../../../../electrophile.md), [counterion](../../../../../../counterion.md), solvent and aggregation can change selectivity; hard [silicon](../../../../../../silicon.md) [electrophiles](../../../../../../electrophile.md) and suitable acylating reagents often favor [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) trapping. Product stability alone would not explain an irreversible kinetic choice.
