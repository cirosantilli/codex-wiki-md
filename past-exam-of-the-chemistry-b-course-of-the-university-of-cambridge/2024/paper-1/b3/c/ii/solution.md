↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The axial penalties depend on the ring position. An axial methyl at C2 encounters the axial [hydrogens](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) on C4 and C6. The short C–O links and dioxane geometry make these [1,3-Diaxial interactions](../../../../../../1-3-diaxial-interaction.md) particularly unfavorable. In contrast, an axial substituent at C5 points toward ring oxygens O1 and O3 rather than toward axial C–H groups at those positions. Those [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) sites present much less steric obstruction than axial [hydrogens](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) on [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md).

**Avoiding the expensive axial C2 methyl outweighs the cost of axial tert-butyl at C5.** Transferring cyclohexane tert-butyl preferences unchanged would therefore give the wrong answer. This is primarily a position-dependent steric comparison, not the usual anomeric preference for an axial electronegative substituent at an acetal [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md). Primary [NMR](../../../../../../nuclear-magnetic-resonance-split.md) work on substituted dioxanes documents this axial C5 tert-butyl conformer.[1971, pp. 4772–4777, discussion of cis-2-methyl-5-tert-butyl-1,3-dioxane](../../../../../../carbon-13-magnetic-resonance-conformation-in-some-tert-butyl-1-3-dioxanes-by-alan-j-jones-and-others.md)
