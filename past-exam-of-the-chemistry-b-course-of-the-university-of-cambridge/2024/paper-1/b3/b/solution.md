<h1 id="b3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For both substrates, the [alkene](../../../../../alkene.md) pi bond attacks [bromine](../../../../../bromine.md) and polarizes/breaks Br–Br. A [bromine](../../../../../bromine.md) [lone pair](../../../../../lone-pair.md) completes a three-membered [bromonium ion](../../../../../bromonium-ion.md), and bromide subsequently attacks a [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) from the opposite face, cleaving that C–Br bridge bond. **The two new C–Br bonds are anti.** A free carbocation that rotates before capture is not needed.

In trans-fused $A$, the fixed [conformation](../../../../../conformation.md) makes the alternative anti-opening pathways diastereomeric. A chair-like, trans-diaxial opening is favored over a geometrically strained alternative that requires a twist/boat-like arrangement of the six-membered ring. The [Fürst-Plattner rule](../../../../../furst-plattner-rule.md) describes this conformational preference. **One depicted [enantiomer](../../../../../enantiomer.md) of $A$ therefore gives essentially one dibromide [diastereomer](../../../../../diastereomer.md)**, with the two bromines occupying the trans-diaxial arrangement in its favored chair. If a racemic sample of $A$ were used, its other [enantiomer](../../../../../enantiomer.md) would of course give the mirror product as well.

For cis-fused $B$, the accessible mirror-related folds and the equivalent anti pathways produce mirror-related dibromides with equal rates in an [achiral](../../../../../achirality.md) environment. Adding the two bromines destroys the symmetry that made the starting [configuration](../../../../../mechanical-configuration.md) [achiral](../../../../../achirality.md). **$B$ gives a racemic dibromide pair.** The racemate follows from equivalent [enantiomer](../../../../../enantiomer.md)-forming pathways, not from a loss of anti stereospecificity.
