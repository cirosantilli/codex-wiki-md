<h1 id="b3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The cis fusion admits two chair–chair conformers, related by simultaneous [ring inversion](../../../../../ring-inversion.md). The bridgehead [hydrogens](../../../../../hydrogen.md) remain cis, while their axial/equatorial descriptions exchange. In one fold the [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) next to the fusion occupies the concave, inside position; in the other, the corresponding inside position is occupied by a methylene group. Both chair-only structures are shown in the lower panels of the dioxane diagram.

**The O-inside chair–chair conformer is strongly favored.** In the unfavorable fold, the inward methylene and its C–H bonds create close nonbonded contacts with the other ring, analogous to costly 1,3-diaxial/gauche contacts in cis-[decalin](../../../../../decalin.md). Placing [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) in that location removes the corresponding [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md)-bound [hydrogens](../../../../../hydrogen.md) and reduces the obstruction. The comparison is between the two conformers of the same cis [configuration](../../../../../mechanical-configuration.md); making the fusion trans would be a configurational change, not a chair flip. Nor is it appropriate to flatten both six-membered rings into regular hexagons, which would hide the decisive inside contacts.
