<h1 id="b9/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A ring-retaining bromination replaces the three hydridic B–H positions while preserving N–H. Its net stoichiometry is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{B_3H_3N_3H_3}+3\mathrm{Br_2}\longrightarrow\mathrm{B_3Br_3N_3H_3}+3\mathrm{HBr}.}
$$

Use controlled bromination and conditions allowing elimination of hydrogen bromide from addition intermediates, rather than identifying the first [bromine](../../../../../../bromine.md) adduct as the final ring product. Boron-bound hydrogen has [hydride](../../../../../../hydride.md)-like character because boron is less electronegative than hydrogen; its replacement is consistent with electrophilic attack by [bromine](../../../../../../bromine.md). The primary preparative work reports B-tribromoborazines obtained by bromination of [borazine](../../../../../../borazine.md) and its N-methyl analogue.[1964, pp. 1651–1653, bromination and formation of B-tribromoborazines](../../../../../../b-tribromoborazines-from-the-bromination-of-borazine-and-n-trimethylborazine-by-reed-f-riley-and-carl-j-schack.md)

**The product must retain the unsaturated B$_3$N$_3$ ring.** Merely adding three HBr molecules to [borazine](../../../../../../borazine.md) initially gives a saturated hydrogen-halide addition product, not the required formula. Addition followed by suitable elimination is distinct from simply asserting direct B–H protonolysis by HBr.
