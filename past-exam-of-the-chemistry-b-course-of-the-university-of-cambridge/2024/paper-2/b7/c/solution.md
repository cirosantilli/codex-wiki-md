<h1 id="b7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Start from an [octahedral molecular geometry](../../../../../octahedral-molecular-geometry.md) with ligands on $\pm x,\pm y,\pm z$, with $e_g=(d_{z^2},d_{x^2-y^2})$ above $t_{2g}=(d_{xy},d_{xz},d_{yz})$. Remove the $-z$ donor while keeping the other five positions. Loss of axial repulsion lowers $d_{z^2}$ substantially; $d_{x^2-y^2}$ still points directly at four basal donors and remains highest. Loss of an axial donor also lowers the orbitals with $z$ dependence relative to $d_{xy}$. The remaining fourfold axis keeps $d_{xz}$ and $d_{yz}$ degenerate.

The usual qualitative [square-pyramidal molecular geometry](../../../../../square-pyramidal-molecular-geometry.md) diagram, shown in the last panel above, is therefore

$$
\boxed{d_{x^2-y^2}\ >\ d_{z^2}\ >\ d_{xy}\ >\ d_{xz}=d_{yz}.}
$$

The corresponding $C_{4v}$ labels are $b_1,a_1,b_2,e$. Energies can be re-zeroed to the new orbital barycenter after ligand removal. Symmetry fixes the degeneracy and labels, but does not universally fix the separation of $d_{z^2}$ and $d_{xy}$: a weak or distant axial donor can reverse this middle pair on approach to the square-planar limit. The displayed ordering assumes an appreciable fifth axial interaction.
