<h1 id="b7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For comparable octahedral environments with the same donor set, the expected qualitative order is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Mn^{2+}<Fe^{2+}<Fe^{3+}<Mo^{3+}<Rh^{3+}<Pt^{4+}}.}
$$

Across comparable first-row ions, the increasing effective attraction and changing [metal](../../../../../metal.md)–ligand distances tend to increase [octahedral splitting](../../../../../octahedral-splitting.md), giving Mn(II) below Fe(II). Raising the charge on the same [metal](../../../../../metal.md) gives Fe(III) above Fe(II), because the ligand interaction generally strengthens and [metal](../../../../../metal.md)–ligand distances contract. Moving from $3d$ to more radially extended $4d$ orbitals gives larger [metal](../../../../../metal.md)–ligand overlap, placing Mo(III) above Fe(III); Rh(III) lies further along the same $4d$ series and typically gives the larger splitting. The $5d$ ion Pt(IV) combines the heavier row with high charge and gives the largest splitting here.

**This is a matched-environment trend, not an intrinsic list of constants for bare ions.** Actual $\Delta$ also depends strongly on donor identity, geometry and distance. It would be meaningless to compare an unspecified weak-field tetrahedral compound with a strong-field octahedral compound solely through this ordering.
