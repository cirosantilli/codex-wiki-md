<h1 id="b6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the doubly bridged tetracarbonyl, each [iron](../../../../../iron.md) has one terminal [carbonyl ligand](../../../../../carbonyl-ligand.md), two [bridging carbonyl ligands](../../../../../bridging-carbonyl-ligand.md) and one [cyclopentadienyl ligand](../../../../../cyclopentadienyl-ligand.md). Neutral [electron counting](../../../../../electron-counting.md) gives $8+5+2+2=17$ before direct bonding, so a single Fe–Fe bond gives 18 at each [iron](../../../../../iron.md). For the triply bridged tricarbonyl, each [iron](../../../../../iron.md) instead has $8+5+3=16$ before direct bonding; formal completion to 18 requires a double Fe–Fe bond. Equivalently, the total fragment counts before [metal](../../../../../metal.md)–[metal](../../../../../metal.md) bonding are 34 and 32 respectively.

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Cp_2Fe_2(CO)_2(\mu\! -CO)_2}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{Fe-Fe}}=2.539\ \text{Å},\quad\text{formal single bond},\\
\mathrm{Cp^{*}_2Fe_2(\mu\! -CO)_3}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{Fe-Fe}}=2.265\ \text{Å},\quad\text{formal double bond}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The increased direct [bond order](../../../../../bond-order.md) explains the substantial contraction. The [pentamethylcyclopentadienyl ligands](../../../../../pentamethylcyclopentadienyl-ligand.md) also alter steric and electronic effects, and bridge geometry changes, so this is not a controlled measurement of [bond order](../../../../../bond-order.md) alone. Nevertheless, the markedly shorter distance in the less carbonyl-saturated dimer is consistent with stronger direct Fe–Fe bonding. The isolated triply bridged compound was characterized structurally and magnetically in the primary work.[1985, pp. 4561–4562, isolation, crystal structure and triplet assignment](../../../../../dinuclear-18-electron-species-having-a-triplet-ground-state-isolation-characterization-and-crystal-structure-of-photogenerated-eta-5-c5me5-2fe2-mu-co-3-by-josephine-paw-blaha-and-others.md)
