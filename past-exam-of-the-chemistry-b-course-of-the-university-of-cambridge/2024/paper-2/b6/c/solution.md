<h1 id="b6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The principal electronic variable is the net population of [metal](../../../../../metal.md)–[metal](../../../../../metal.md) [bonding molecular orbitals](../../../../../bonding-orbital.md) relative to [antibonding molecular orbitals](../../../../../antibonding-orbital.md). For otherwise comparable complexes, a larger [bond order](../../../../../bond-order.md) generally gives a shorter [metal-metal bond](../../../../../metal-metal-bond.md); removing an antibonding [electron](../../../../../electron.md) can shorten a bond even though the total [electron](../../../../../electron.md) count falls. Different orbital symmetries also matter: sigma, pi and delta overlaps respond differently to distance and rotation of the [metal](../../../../../metal.md) fragments.

[metal](../../../../../metal.md) size, [oxidation state](../../../../../oxidation-state.md) and radial orbital extent set the underlying distance scale. Comparing a first-row [metal](../../../../../metal.md) with a larger heavier congener does not isolate [bond order](../../../../../bond-order.md). Increasing positive charge often contracts the [metal](../../../../../metal.md) orbitals, but can also change [electron](../../../../../electron.md) occupancy, ligand binding and electrostatic interactions. The [ligand field](../../../../../ligand-field.md) and donor/acceptor properties of the surrounding [ligands in coordination chemistry](../../../../../ligand-in-coordination-chemistry.md) determine which [metal](../../../../../metal.md) orbitals remain available for direct bonding and how much density instead participates in [pi backbonding](../../../../../pi-backbonding.md).

Finally, the geometry can enforce or oppose the preferred separation. [Bridging ligands](../../../../../bridging-ligand.md) impose bond-angle and bond-length constraints; bulky substituents produce [steric hindrance](../../../../../steric-hindrance.md); crystal packing can perturb the observed distance. **A [bond length](../../../../../bond-length.md) is evidence for bonding only in its electronic and structural context.** In particular, two bridges can hold [metals](../../../../../metal.md) close without proving a high direct [bond order](../../../../../bond-order.md), while a sterically congested formal single bond can be unusually long.
