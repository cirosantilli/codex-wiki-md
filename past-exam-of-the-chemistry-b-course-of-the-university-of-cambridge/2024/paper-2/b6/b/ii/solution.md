<h1 id="b6/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use an $\eta^5$ [cyclopentadienyl ligand](../../../../../../cyclopentadienyl-ligand.md) and four terminal [carbonyl ligands](../../../../../../carbonyl-ligand.md) at each [titanium](../../../../../../titanium.md), with a Ti–Ti single [metal-metal bond](../../../../../../metal-metal-bond.md). The middle panel shows this connectivity. Neutral [electron counting](../../../../../../electron-counting.md) assigns five [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) to each Cp and gives

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{VE(Ti)}=4+5+4(2)+1=18.}
$$

Equivalently, the two Cp anions require an average [oxidation state](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md) Ti(I), hence $d^3$; ionic counting gives $3+6+8+1=18$. **The formal Ti–Ti [bond order](../../../../../../bond-order.md) is one.** The large coordination envelope can make this bond weak and long; reaching 18 [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) is bookkeeping, rather than proof of a short, strong bond. There is no need to invent extra CO bridges or a higher Ti–Ti [bond order](../../../../../../bond-order.md).
