<h1 id="b8/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

[iron](../../../../../../iron.md) remains Fe(II), $d^6$, in both states; the change is a [spin crossover](../../../../../../spin-crossover.md), rather than a change in [oxidation state](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md). At the higher [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) the [high-spin complex](../../../../../../high-spin-complex.md) has $t_{2g}^4e_g^2$: four [unpaired electrons](../../../../../../unpaired-electron.md), $S=2$, and

$$
\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sqrt{n(n+2)}\,\mu_B=\sqrt{24}\,\mu_B=4.90\,\mu_B.
$$

This matches the observed moment. At low [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) a [low-spin complex](../../../../../../low-spin-complex.md) with $t_{2g}^6e_g^0$ has $S=0$ and shows [diamagnetism](../../../../../../diamagnetism.md). **Cooling switches high-[spin](../../../../../../spin.md) $d^6$ to low-[spin](../../../../../../spin.md) $d^6$.** Emptying the [metal](../../../../../../metal.md)–ligand antibonding $e_g$ orbitals strengthens and shortens the Fe–N bonds, accounting for the contraction by roughly $0.2\ \text{Å}$.

The [octahedral splitting](../../../../../../octahedral-splitting.md) and [electron pairing energy](../../../../../../electron-pairing-energy.md) must be sufficiently close for both states to be accessible. The low-[spin](../../../../../../spin.md) state gains electronic energy, while the high-[spin](../../../../../../spin.md) state has greater [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) degeneracy and usually greater vibrational [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) because of its softer bonds. The resulting [Gibbs free energy](../../../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md) balance favors high [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) at higher [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). The two endpoint observations do not by themselves specify [hysteresis](../../../../../../hysteresis.md) or transition cooperativity; those require a [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) series. Coupled structural and magnetic measurements distinguish this explanation from a mere bond-length anomaly.
