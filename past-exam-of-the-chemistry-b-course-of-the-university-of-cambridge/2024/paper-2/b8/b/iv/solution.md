<h1 id="b8/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

A plausible explanation is a stronger, less labile Hf–C interaction, reflecting the different bonding of the heavier [metal](../../../../../../metal.md). Breaking and reorganizing that interaction in [migratory insertion](../../../../../../migratory-insertion.md) can raise the propagation barrier and reduce the insertion rate. At the same time, stronger retention of the [metal](../../../../../../metal.md)-bound alkyl can suppress [beta-hydride elimination](../../../../../../beta-hydride-elimination.md) or other [chain transfer in chain polymerization](../../../../../../chain-transfer-in-chain-polymerization.md) processes even more strongly.

The chain length depends on competing rates, not on propagation speed alone. In a simple stationary competition,

$$
\overline X_n\ \sim\ \frac{k_p[\mathrm{propene}]}{k_{\mathrm{tr,eff}}},
$$

where the denominator includes the operative chain-transfer or chain-stopping rates per active chain. **The Hf catalyst can propagate more slowly yet make longer chains if transfer is suppressed by a still larger factor.** Slower insertion alone would not explain longer [polymers](../../../../../../polymer-split.md). Distinguishing particular transfer pathways would require [polymer](../../../../../../polymer-split.md) end-group analysis and the concentration dependence of molecular mass and [polymerization](../../../../../../polymerization.md) rate.
