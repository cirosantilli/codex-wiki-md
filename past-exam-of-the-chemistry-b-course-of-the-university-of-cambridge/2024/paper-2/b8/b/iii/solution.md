<h1 id="b8/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Write $R$ for the current growing chain. [Propene](../../../../../../propene.md) first binds at the available site of $[\mathrm{Cp_2ZrR}]^+$, giving a coordinated [alkene](../../../../../../alkene.md) adjacent to the [metal](../../../../../../metal.md)-bound chain. In the [Cossee-Arlman mechanism](../../../../../../cossee-arlman-mechanism.md), the chain migrates to the substituted [alkene](../../../../../../alkene.md) [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) while the terminal [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) forms the new Zr–C bond:

$$
[\mathrm{Cp_2Zr{-}R}]^++\mathrm{CH_2{=}CHMe}
\rightleftharpoons[\mathrm{Cp_2Zr(R)(\eta^2\! -CH_2{=}CHMe)}]^+
\longrightarrow[\mathrm{Cp_2Zr{-}CH_2{-}CH(Me){-}R}]^+.
$$

The [electron-pair arrows](../../../../../../electron-pair-arrow.md) for insertion take the old Zr–R bond toward the substituted [alkene](../../../../../../alkene.md) [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) and the [alkene](../../../../../../alkene.md) [pi bond](../../../../../../pi-bond.md) toward the new Zr–terminal-[carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) bond. A four-center arrangement permits both bonds to change in one [migratory insertion](../../../../../../migratory-insertion.md); Zr remains Zr(IV). The product again has an accessible site, so coordination and insertion can repeat.

**Predominant 1,2 insertion leaves a primary Zr–CH$_2$ chain end and generates head-to-tail [polypropylene](../../../../../../polypropylene.md).** It avoids the more crowded alternative secondary Zr–CH(Me) end and unfavorable methyl contacts with the [cyclopentadienyl ligands](../../../../../../cyclopentadienyl-ligand.md) in the insertion geometry. This explains [regioselectivity](../../../../../../regioselectivity.md). An unbridged, unsubstituted zirconocene does not by itself establish a fixed [tacticity](../../../../../../tacticity.md); the schematic alignment of methyl groups in a [polymer](../../../../../../polymer-split.md) drawing is not evidence for an isotactic product.
