<h1 id="b8/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The trityl cation acts as an [electrophile](../../../../../../electrophile.md) and removes one [metal](../../../../../../metal.md)-bound methyl by [methyl abstraction](../../../../../../methyl-abstraction.md). The balanced conversion is

$$
\mathrm{Cp_2ZrMe_2}+[\mathrm{Ph_3C}]^+[\mathrm{B(C_6F_5)_4}]^-
\longrightarrow[\mathrm{Cp_2ZrMe}]^+[\mathrm{B(C_6F_5)_4}]^-+\mathrm{Ph_3CMe}.
$$

Thus **$C=[\mathrm{Cp_2ZrMe}]^+[\mathrm{B(C_6F_5)_4}]^-$ and $D=\mathrm{Ph_3CMe}$**, 1,1,1-triphenylethane. Transfer is formally of $\mathrm{Me^-}$ to [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md), not transfer of a [hydride](../../../../../../hydride.md) or a methyl radical. [zirconium](../../../../../../zirconium.md) remains Zr(IV), $d^0$. Ionic [electron counting](../../../../../../electron-counting.md) changes from $12+4=16$ in the dimethyl to $12+2=14$ at the cation, creating an accessible coordination site. The borate is a [weakly coordinating anion](../../../../../../weakly-coordinating-anion.md), although ion pairing can still occur in solution.
