<h1 id="b7/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[triphenylphosphine](../../../../../triphenylphosphine.md) is a good sigma donor and a substantially poorer pi acceptor than CO. Replacing CO by this [phosphine ligand](../../../../../phosphine-ligand.md) increases [electron](../../../../../electron.md) density at [chromium](../../../../../chromium.md) and reduces competition among [carbonyl ligands](../../../../../carbonyl-ligand.md) for [pi backbonding](../../../../../pi-backbonding.md). Backbonding into each remaining CO therefore strengthens, their internal [bond force constants](../../../../../bond-force-constant.md) fall, and **both remaining carbonyl stretching frequencies are expected to shift downward** relative to the parent tricarbonyl.

The two remaining [carbonyl ligands](../../../../../carbonyl-ligand.md) have coupled approximately symmetric and antisymmetric stretches; both are generally infrared active in this geometry. One should not treat the spectral change as simply deleting one of the original two bands. The substituted complex still has $6+6+2(2)+2=18$ valence [electrons](../../../../../electron.md). No numerical frequency shift follows from [electron counting](../../../../../electron-counting.md) alone.
