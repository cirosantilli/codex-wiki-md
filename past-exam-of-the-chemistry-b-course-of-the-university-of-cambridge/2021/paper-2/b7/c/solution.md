<h1 id="b7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The methoxy [substituent](../../../../../substituent.md) donates [electron](../../../../../electron.md) density into the coordinated [arene](../../../../../arene.md) by [chemical resonance](../../../../../resonance-chemistry.md). This increases the [electron](../../../../../electron.md) density available at [chromium](../../../../../chromium.md) for [pi backbonding](../../../../../pi-backbonding.md) to CO, weakening C–O and lowering its stretching frequencies. The [ester](../../../../../ester.md) [substituent](../../../../../substituent.md) withdraws [electron](../../../../../electron.md) density from the [arene](../../../../../arene.md), reducing [pi backbonding](../../../../../pi-backbonding.md) and raising the carbonyl stretching frequencies. For both observed bands, **the methoxy-substituted compound has the lowest carbonyl frequency, the unsubstituted compound is intermediate, and the ester-substituted compound has the highest**.

In the [harmonic oscillator](../../../../../simple-harmonic-motion.md) approximation,

$$
\widetilde\nu=\frac{1}{2\pi c}\sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}},
$$

so the trend reflects an increasing effective C–O [bond force constant](../../../../../bond-force-constant.md) rather than a change in the CO [reduced mass](../../../../../reduced-mass.md). For example, comparison of the higher-frequency bands gives $k_{\rm ester}/k_{\rm methoxy}\approx(1992/1983)^2=1.0091$. This is only an approximate local-bond interpretation because the observed [normal modes](../../../../../normal-mode.md) couple the three CO oscillators. In the idealized local $C_{3v}$ geometry they span $A_1+E$, giving two infrared-active frequencies. A substituted [arene](../../../../../arene.md) can lower the exact [molecular symmetry](../../../../../molecular-symmetry.md) and split the $E$ pair, even if that splitting is unresolved experimentally.
