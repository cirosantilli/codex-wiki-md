↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In a [boroxine](../../../../../boroxine.md), each [boron](../../../../../boron.md) has three [sigma bonds](../../../../../sigma-bond.md) and is approximately trigonal planar, with an $sp^2$ description for its sigma framework and an available perpendicular $2p$ orbital. [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) can be described approximately as $sp^2$: two in-plane hybrids form B–O [sigma bonds](../../../../../sigma-bond.md), one contains an in-plane [lone pair](../../../../../lone-pair.md), and a perpendicular $2p$ [lone pair](../../../../../lone-pair.md) overlaps with neighbouring [boron](../../../../../boron.md) $2p$ orbitals. These three [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) donor pairs supply a six-electron pi system around the alternating ring. The combination of good same-shell overlap and a low-strain six-membered ring favours planarity and partial B–O multiple-bond character. The bonds remain polar; a six-electron diagram alone does not establish benzene-like aromatic stabilization quantitatively.

In a [siloxane](../../../../../siloxane.md), each [silicon](../../../../../silicon.md) instead has four [sigma bonds](../../../../../sigma-bond.md), two to [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) and two to its organic groups, giving an approximately tetrahedral $sp^3$ sigma framework. [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) has two [sigma bonds](../../../../../sigma-bond.md) and two [lone pairs](../../../../../lone-pair.md), so an approximately $sp^3$ localized description is a useful starting point, although actual Si–O–Si angles and lone-pair hybrids vary considerably. There is no comparable ring of vacant [silicon](../../../../../silicon.md) $2p$ orbitals. A description based on strong oxygen-$2p$ to silicon-$3d$ [pi bonds](../../../../../pi-bond.md) is also misleading: those orbitals have poor energy and radial matching. **Strongly polar Si–O sigma bonding, supplemented by [negative hyperconjugation](../../../../../negative-hyperconjugation.md) from [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) [lone pairs](../../../../../lone-pair.md) into appropriate Si–R [antibonding orbitals](../../../../../antibonding-orbital.md), gives a better account**. The ring can therefore pucker rather than requiring continuous planar pi overlap. This follows the broader conclusion that strong main-group bonds and apparently expanded valence do not imply dominant valence $d$-orbital participation.[JACS 112, 1434–1445: ionic contributions and negative hyperconjugation versus d-orbital models](../../../../../chemical-bonding-in-hypervalent-molecules-the-dominance-of-ionic-bonding-and-negative-hyperconjugation-over-d-orbital-participation-by-alan-e-reed-and-paul-von-rague-schleyer.md)
