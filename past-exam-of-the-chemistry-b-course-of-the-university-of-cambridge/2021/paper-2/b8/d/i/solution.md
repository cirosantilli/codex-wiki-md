<h1 id="b8/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

One equivalent of butyllithium removes one proton per hydrocarbon, giving the **hydropentalenyl monoanion $\mathrm{C_8H_7^-}$**. It does not give the doubly deprotonated [pentalenide](../../../../../../pentalenide.md). [salt metathesis](../../../../../../salt-metathesis.md) with half an equivalent of iron(II) chloride therefore gives the sandwich complex

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Fe}(\eta^5\!\text{-}\mathrm{C_8H_7})_2,\qquad d^6+6+6=18.}
$$

Each [coordination ligand](../../../../../../ligand-in-coordination-chemistry.md) binds through its aromatic five-membered portion and retains an uncoordinated [double bond](../../../../../../double-bond.md) in the fused ring. The orientation of this unsymmetrical fused appendage makes the two coordinated [coordination ligand](../../../../../../ligand-in-coordination-chemistry.md) faces distinguishable. Pairing matching and opposite planar configurations gives **a racemic form, $(R_p,R_p)/(S_p,S_p)$, and a meso form, $(R_p,S_p)$**. This describes the two stereoisomeric classes, rather than assigning the two easily interconverting rotations of ordinary [ferrocene](../../../../../../ferrocene.md) as separate configurational isomers. The molecular structure and double-bond disorder of bis(hydropentalenyl)iron have been examined crystallographically.[Acta Crystallographica C 52, 2414–2416: parallel coordinated rings and double-bond isomer disorder](../../../../../../bis-eta-%E2%81%B5-hydropentalenyl-iron-by-elies-molins-and-others.md)
