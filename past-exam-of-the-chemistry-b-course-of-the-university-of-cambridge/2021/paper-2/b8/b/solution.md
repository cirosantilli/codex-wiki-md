↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In a [metallocene](../../../../../metallocene.md), several orbitals with substantial [metal](../../../../../metal.md) $d$ character are [nonbonding molecular orbitals](../../../../../nonbonding-molecular-orbital.md) or only weakly bonding or antibonding with respect to the strong Cp–[metal](../../../../../metal.md) framework. Adding or removing a small number of [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) from them can therefore change [oxidation state](../../../../../oxidation-state.md) or [spin](../../../../../spin.md) without destroying the principal ring–[metal](../../../../../metal.md) bonds. This allows stable compounds on either side of the 18-electron count: for example, the usual neutral counts for [chromocene](../../../../../chromocene.md), [ferrocene](../../../../../ferrocene.md), [cobaltocene](../../../../../cobaltocene.md) and [nickelocene](../../../../../nickelocene.md) are respectively **16, 18, 19 and 20**. The [18-electron rule](../../../../../18-electron-rule.md) remains a useful reference, but occupation of a weakly [antibonding orbital](../../../../../antibonding-orbital.md) is much less disruptive than occupation of a strongly [antibonding orbital](../../../../../antibonding-orbital.md). Redox stability also depends on geometry, [substituents](../../../../../substituent.md) and reaction conditions, so this is a bonding explanation of the observed flexibility rather than a guarantee of equal stability for all counts.
