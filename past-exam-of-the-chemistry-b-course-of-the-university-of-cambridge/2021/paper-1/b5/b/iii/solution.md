<h1 id="b5/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The strongest donor belongs to the acid, $\alpha_1=3.5$, and the strongest acceptor to the [aminopyridine](../../../../../../aminopyridine.md), $\beta_2=8.5$. **The [cocrystal](../../../../../../cocrystal.md) pairs the strongest donor with the strongest acceptor**, while the reciprocal amino-NH···[carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) contact closes a complementary two-contact motif.

Using the simple favorable-contact score $\alpha\beta$, one heterodimer gives

$$
S_{\rm hetero}=\alpha_1\beta_2+\alpha_2\beta_1
=(3.5)(8.5)+(3.0)(5.5)=46.25.
$$

The self-associated comparison, normalized to the same two [molecules](../../../../../../molecule-split.md) and two [hydrogen bonds](../../../../../../hydrogen-bond.md), is half an acid [dimer](../../../../../../dimer.md) plus half an [aminopyridine](../../../../../../aminopyridine.md) [dimer](../../../../../../dimer.md):

$$
S_{\rm self}=\alpha_1\beta_1+\alpha_2\beta_2
=(3.5)(5.5)+(3.0)(8.5)=44.75.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\Delta S=1.50=(\alpha_1-\alpha_2)(\beta_2-\beta_1)>0}.
$$

In a model where bond [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) contains $-\alpha\beta$, this is a favorable energy change. No bonds have been artificially added to one side of the comparison: both alternatives contain two contacts per acid/[aminopyridine](../../../../../../aminopyridine.md) pair.

The calculation rationalizes the hydrogen-bond preference. It is not a complete [crystal](../../../../../../crystal.md) [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md): packing, additional contacts, [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) and possible [solvent](../../../../../../solvent.md) effects also matter. Without a specified calibration, the numerical score should not be promoted to a measured cocrystallization [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md).
