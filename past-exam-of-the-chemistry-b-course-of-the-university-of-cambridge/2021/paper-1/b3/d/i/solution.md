↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Number the [cyclohexane](../../../../../../cyclohexane.md) consecutively, putting the top [methylene group](../../../../../../methylene-group.md) at C1 and the two original [epoxides](../../../../../../epoxide.md) at C2/C3 and C5/C6. Both [epoxide](../../../../../../epoxide.md) [oxygens](../../../../../../oxygen.md) are up.

**A is a diazidocyclohexanediol**, with each [epoxide](../../../../../../epoxide.md) replaced by one azide and one [alcohol](../../../../../../alcohol.md). A standard successive chair-favored opening gives **N3 down at C2 and C5, OH up at C3 and C6**. This is the 1,4-diazido/1,4-diol connectivity drawn in the bottom row of the original map.

For each opening, azide attacks from the face opposite [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md), breaking one C–O bond with inversion at the attacked [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md). [protonation](../../../../../../protonation.md) of the two resulting [alkoxides](../../../../../../alkoxide.md) gives the two OH groups. After the first opening, the preferred [conformation](../../../../../../conformation.md) of the opened portion must be used for the second trans-diaxial opening; simply imposing one unchanged chair on both steps can select the wrong regioisomer.

The elemental change is addition of two $\mathrm{N_3^-}$ groups and two work-up [protons](../../../../../../proton.md) to $\mathrm{C_6H_8O_2}$, giving $\mathrm{C_6H_{10}N_6O_2}$. The formula alone would not distinguish alternative positional isomers; the depicted assignment uses the ordinary successive-opening conformational model.
