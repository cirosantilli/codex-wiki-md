<h1 id="b3/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

[sulfide](../../../../../../sulfide.md) first opens one [epoxide](../../../../../../epoxide.md) by backside attack, giving a carbon-bound [thiolate](../../../../../../thiolate.md) and an [alkoxide](../../../../../../alkoxide.md). Using the same numbering, take this first attack at C2: the C2–S bond is down and C3–O remains up.

The attached [sulfur](../../../../../../sulfur.md) still bears a nucleophilic [lone pair](../../../../../../lone-pair.md) and negative charge. It attacks C5 of the second [epoxide](../../../../../../epoxide.md) **intramolecularly**, breaking C5–O and closing a bridge between C2 and C5. Work-up protonates the [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) anions at C3 and C6. Thus **B is 7-thiabicyclo$[2.2.1]$heptane-2,5-diol**, with both OH groups on the face opposite the [sulfur](../../../../../../sulfur.md) bridge.

Both C–S bonds are formed by inversion at their attacked [epoxide](../../../../../../epoxide.md) [carbons](../../../../../../carbon.md). Because the original [epoxides](../../../../../../epoxide.md) are cis, both new C–S bonds point to the same opposite face and one [sulfur](../../../../../../sulfur.md) [chemical atom](../../../../../../atom-physics.md) can connect them. The 1,4 bridge gives two five-membered sulfur-containing circuits, unlike the more strained closure between nearby 1,3 positions.

The [sulfur](../../../../../../sulfur.md) therefore replaces two separate external [nucleophiles](../../../../../../nucleophile.md): **two [epoxides](../../../../../../epoxide.md) are opened but only one [sulfur](../../../../../../sulfur.md) [chemical atom](../../../../../../atom-physics.md) is incorporated**. Adding that [sulfur](../../../../../../sulfur.md) and two work-up [protons](../../../../../../proton.md) gives $\mathrm{C_6H_{10}O_2S}$, consistent with B.
