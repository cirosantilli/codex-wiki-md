<h1 id="b3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**Both reactions are [E2 eliminations](../../../../../e2-elimination.md) requiring an axial [chloride](../../../../../chloride.md) and an axial beta [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) in an [antiperiplanar conformation](../../../../../antiperiplanar-conformation.md) relative to the C–Cl bond.** The reacting C–H bond supplies the [alkene](../../../../../alkene.md) $\pi$ bond as the C–Cl bond breaks; [hydroxide](../../../../../hydroxide.md) accepts the [proton](../../../../../proton.md).

For the trans-1,2 substrate, the low-energy chair has methyl and [chlorine](../../../../../chlorine.md) both equatorial. [ring inversion](../../../../../ring-inversion.md) is needed to place [chlorine](../../../../../chlorine.md) axial, but this also places methyl axial. The population of the reactive chair is consequently small, explaining the slow reaction. At the methyl-bearing neighboring [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) the axial position is occupied by methyl, not [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md). Only the other beta [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) has the needed anti axial H, so elimination gives **3-methylcyclohexene** rather than the more substituted [alkene](../../../../../alkene.md).

For the cis substrate, a favorable chair has methyl equatorial and [chlorine](../../../../../chlorine.md) axial. It can react without paying the axial-methyl penalty, explaining the faster rate. Both adjacent [carbons](../../../../../carbon.md) now supply an anti axial [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md). Removing the H on the methyl-bearing [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) gives **1-methylcyclohexene, the major product**; removing the other gives **3-methylcyclohexene, the minor product**. The former has the more substituted [double bond](../../../../../double-bond.md).

Thus [alkene](../../../../../alkene.md) stability selects between accessible pathways in the cis case, but it cannot create the missing [antiperiplanar conformation](../../../../../antiperiplanar-conformation.md) H in the trans case.
