## B6

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="b6/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#b6)

<h4 id="b6/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b6/a)

Each [thiocyanate](../../chemical-substance.md#thiocyanate) is a **three-metal bridge**, $\mu_3$-$\kappa N:\kappa S:\kappa S$: its [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) donates to one [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) and its [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) donates to two other [metals](../../chemical-substance.md#metal). Thus the [coordination ligand](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry) is ambidentate, but the structure uses both ends and shares the [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) donor. Each [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) receives two [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) and four [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) donors, giving an approximately octahedral $\mathrm{MN_2S_4}$ [coordination sphere](../../chemistry.md#coordination-sphere). Six donor contacts per [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) are consistent with two [coordination ligands](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry) per [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) because each [coordination ligand](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry) contributes three contacts across the lattice. Sharing these contacts produces a [coordination polymer](../../chemistry.md#coordination-polymer) within each layer; the interlayer [Van der Waals force](../../force.md#van-der-waals-force) does not add coordination bonds. The index $\mu_3$ counts [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) centres, whereas a [hapticity](../../chemistry.md#hapticity) label would count adjacent coordinated [coordination ligand](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry) atoms and would not describe this connectivity correctly.[Structural discussion of the layered first-row metal thiocyanates](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#strengthening-the-magnetic-interactions-in-pseudobinary-first-row-transition-metal-thiocyanates-m-ncs-2-by-euan-n-bassey-and-others)

<h3 id="b6/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#b6)

<h4 id="b6/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b6/b)

Charge balance fixes each [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) as $\mathrm{M^{II}}$. In an octahedral crystal field the lower $t_{2g}$ set is $(d_{xy},d_{xz},d_{yz})$ and the upper $e_g$ set is $(d_{z^2},d_{x^2-y^2})$. The measured [spin-only magnetic moments](../../electromagnetism.md#spin-only-magnetic-moment) select the following occupations:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
&\text{configuration}&n_{\rm unpaired}&S&\mu_{\rm eff}/\mu_B\\\hline
\mathrm{Mn^{II}}&t_{2g}^{3}e_g^{2}&5&5/2&\sqrt{35}=5.92\\
\mathrm{Fe^{II}}&t_{2g}^{4}e_g^{2}&4&2&\sqrt{24}=4.90\\
\mathrm{Cu^{II}}&t_{2g}^{6}e_g^{3}&1&1/2&\sqrt3=1.73
\end{array}
$$

The result follows from $S=n_{\rm unpaired}/2$ and $2\sqrt{S(S+1)}=\sqrt{n_{\rm unpaired}(n_{\rm unpaired}+2)}$. **Manganese(II) and iron(II) are [high-spin complexes](../../chemistry.md#high-spin-complex)**: their octahedral splitting is too small to compensate the additional pairing cost of their low-spin alternatives. Copper(II) is $d^9$, with one unpaired [electron](../../physics.md#electron) regardless of which member of the upper pair is singly occupied. [spin-orbit coupling](../../atomic-physics.md#spin-orbit-interaction) and [exchange interactions](../../electromagnetism.md#exchange-interaction) can produce deviations from the spin-only estimate in real measurements; no such correction is required to explain the supplied values.

<h3 id="b6/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#b6)

<h4 id="b6/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b6/c)

At fixed $+2$ [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state), increasing [effective nuclear charge](../../chemistry.md#effective-nuclear-charge) across the first transition series generally contracts the [ionic radius](../../chemistry.md#ionic-radius). The first expectation is therefore **shorter average [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–[nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) and [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–[sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) distances toward copper**. This is a trend in comparable coordination environments, rather than a requirement that every individual contact shorten monotonically. The occupation of [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–[coordination ligand](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry) [antibonding orbitals](../../chemistry.md#antibonding-orbital), donor identity and lattice distortion all affect the observed distances.

The high-spin $d^5$ and $d^6$ ions both have two [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) in the strongly sigma-antibonding $e_g$ set, so adding the sixth [electron](../../physics.md#electron) to $t_{2g}$ does not itself introduce another sigma-antibonding [electron](../../physics.md#electron). Copper(II), however, has $e_g^3$ occupation and is subject to the [Jahn-Teller effect](../../chemistry.md#jahn-teller-effect). A [tetragonal Jahn-Teller distortion](../../chemistry.md#tetragonal-jahn-teller-distortion) commonly gives four shorter and two longer contacts, so an elongated copper contact can oppose the overall size trend. Here the [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) and [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) donors are already chemically inequivalent; the supplied schematic alone does not determine which trans pair becomes longest. One should compare actual M–N and M–S sets separately, rather than infer a unique axis or a numerical distance from the [magnetic moments](../../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-dipole-moment). The experimentally determined structures illustrate why the donor geometry and individual contacts matter.[Crystallographic characterization and comparison of the layered structures](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#strengthening-the-magnetic-interactions-in-pseudobinary-first-row-transition-metal-thiocyanates-m-ncs-2-by-euan-n-bassey-and-others)

<h3 id="b6/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](#b6)

<h4 id="b6/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b6/d)

For **A, linear [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) coordination with predominantly nitrile-like bonding** is consistent with all three measurements:

$$
\mathrm{M\leftarrow N\equiv C-S^-}.
$$

The short C–N and long C–S distances correspond approximately to a [triple bond](../../covalent-bond.md#triple-bond) and a [single bond](../../covalent-bond.md#single-bond). An approximately $sp$ [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) donor has its donor [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) directed along the N–C axis, explaining the linear M–N–C angle.

For **B, bent [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) coordination with increased $\mathrm{^-N=C=S}$ [chemical resonance](../../covalent-bond.md#resonance-chemistry) character** accounts for the longer C–N and shorter C–S bonds. In this limiting description both internal bonds have substantial double-bond character and [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) is approximately $sp^2$. One [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) can bind a [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) with an M–N–C angle near $120^\circ$; alternatively, donation of both [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) [lone pairs](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) can give a trigonal, nitrogen-bridging unit:

$$
\begin{array}{c}
\mathrm{M}\\[-2pt]
\diagdown\\[-3pt]
\mathrm{N=C=S}\\[-3pt]
\diagup\\[-2pt]
\mathrm{M'}
\end{array}
$$

The nitrogen-bridging drawing is a compatible proposed bonding mode, but the listed internal distances and one angle alone do not prove the number of attached [metals](../../chemical-substance.md#metal). In particular, B cannot be identified simply as terminal [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) coordination: the specified angle explicitly contains an M–N bond. [resonance structures](../../covalent-bond.md#resonance-structure) explain relative [bond orders](../../chemistry.md#bond-order); they do not imply an isolated [coordination ligand](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry) with permanently localized integer bonds.

A distinct possibility is **terminal [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) coordination**, $\mathrm{M\leftarrow S-C\equiv N}$. Unlike the [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) donor in the layer, this [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) need bind only one [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal). The limiting $\mathrm{^-S-C\equiv N}$ [resonance structure](../../covalent-bond.md#resonance-structure) suggests **$r_{\rm CN}\approx1.15\,\mathring{\mathrm A}$ and $r_{\rm CS}\approx1.8\,\mathring{\mathrm A}$**. These are single-/triple-bond-based estimates, not exact predictions for an unspecified [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) complex. The M–S–C linkage should be bent, consistent with [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) lone-pair donation.

## B7

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="b7/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b7/a)

Take the M–C–O direction as $z$. [carbon monoxide](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide) binds through [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) because its occupied $5\sigma$ donor orbital has a substantial carbon-end lobe. End-on overlap with an empty [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) orbital of sigma symmetry gives **CO-to-metal [sigma donation](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-donation)**. At the same time, occupied [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) $d_{xz}$ and $d_{yz}$ orbitals overlap sideways with the two corresponding empty CO $2\pi^*$ orbitals, giving **metal-to-CO [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding)**. Both channels strengthen the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–[carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) bond, while backbonding lowers the internal C–O [bond order](../../chemistry.md#bond-order). The processes are synergic: [sigma donation](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-donation) helps supply [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) [electron](../../physics.md#electron) density for backbonding, while backbonding relieves [electron](../../physics.md#electron) accumulation at the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal).

<a id="b7/a/image-complementary-sigma-donation-and-pi-backbonding-in-a-metal-carbonyl"></a>
![](../../past-exam-of-the-chemistry-b-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2021/paper-2-carbonyl-orbitals.png)

**[Figure 2](#b7/a/image-complementary-sigma-donation-and-pi-backbonding-in-a-metal-carbonyl). Complementary sigma donation and pi backbonding in a metal carbonyl**.

The original orbital sketch shows one of the two perpendicular pi channels. It is the occupied [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) orbital, rather than an empty [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) orbital, that supplies [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) to CO [antibonding orbitals](../../chemistry.md#antibonding-orbital).

<h3 id="b7/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b7/b)

The [arene](../../molecule.md#arene) and three [carbonyl ligands](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand) are neutral, so **[chromium](../../chemical-substance.md#chromium) is in [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) zero**, with a $d^6$ [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) count. The eta-six [arene](../../molecule.md#arene) contributes six [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) and each CO contributes two:

$$
\boxed{6+6+3(2)=18\text{ valence electrons}.}
$$

The [methoxy group](../../chemistry.md#methoxy-group) changes the electronic properties of the [arene](../../molecule.md#arene) without changing this formal [electron](../../physics.md#electron) count. The complex is a [piano-stool complex](../../chemistry.md#piano-stool-complex), with the coordinated [arene](../../molecule.md#arene) as the seat and the three [carbonyl ligands](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand) as the legs.

<h3 id="b7/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b7/c)

The methoxy [substituent](../../stereochemistry.md#substituent) donates [electron](../../physics.md#electron) density into the coordinated [arene](../../molecule.md#arene) by [chemical resonance](../../covalent-bond.md#resonance-chemistry). This increases the [electron](../../physics.md#electron) density available at [chromium](../../chemical-substance.md#chromium) for [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding) to CO, weakening C–O and lowering its stretching frequencies. The [ester](../../chemical-substance.md#ester) [substituent](../../stereochemistry.md#substituent) withdraws [electron](../../physics.md#electron) density from the [arene](../../molecule.md#arene), reducing [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding) and raising the carbonyl stretching frequencies. For both observed bands, **the methoxy-substituted compound has the lowest carbonyl frequency, the unsubstituted compound is intermediate, and the ester-substituted compound has the highest**.

In the [harmonic oscillator](../../classical-mechanics.md#simple-harmonic-motion) approximation,

$$
\widetilde\nu=\frac{1}{2\pi c}\sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}},
$$

so the trend reflects an increasing effective C–O [bond force constant](../../molecule.md#bond-force-constant) rather than a change in the CO [reduced mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#reduced-mass). For example, comparison of the higher-frequency bands gives $k_{\rm ester}/k_{\rm methoxy}\approx(1992/1983)^2=1.0091$. This is only an approximate local-bond interpretation because the observed [normal modes](../../wave-equation.md#normal-mode) couple the three CO oscillators. In the idealized local $C_{3v}$ geometry they span $A_1+E$, giving two infrared-active frequencies. A substituted [arene](../../molecule.md#arene) can lower the exact [molecular symmetry](../../euclidean-geometry.md#molecular-symmetry) and split the $E$ pair, even if that splitting is unresolved experimentally.

<h3 id="b7/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b7/d)

[triphenylphosphine](../../chemical-substance.md#triphenylphosphine) is a good sigma donor and a substantially poorer pi acceptor than CO. Replacing CO by this [phosphine ligand](../../chemistry.md#phosphine-ligand) increases [electron](../../physics.md#electron) density at [chromium](../../chemical-substance.md#chromium) and reduces competition among [carbonyl ligands](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand) for [pi backbonding](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-backbonding). Backbonding into each remaining CO therefore strengthens, their internal [bond force constants](../../molecule.md#bond-force-constant) fall, and **both remaining carbonyl stretching frequencies are expected to shift downward** relative to the parent tricarbonyl.

The two remaining [carbonyl ligands](../../chemistry.md#carbonyl-ligand) have coupled approximately symmetric and antisymmetric stretches; both are generally infrared active in this geometry. One should not treat the spectral change as simply deleting one of the original two bands. The substituted complex still has $6+6+2(2)+2=18$ valence [electrons](../../physics.md#electron). No numerical frequency shift follows from [electron counting](../../chemistry.md#electron-counting) alone.

<h3 id="b7/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](#b7)

<h4 id="b7/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#b7/e)

Photoinduced CO loss gives a 16-electron fragment. In the ester-substituted complex the pendant [ester](../../chemical-substance.md#ester) carbonyl [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) can reach [chromium](../../chemical-substance.md#chromium) and capture that vacant site. A suitable structure is **$\mathrm{Cr}(\eta^6\!\text{-}\mathrm{C_6H_5CO_2Me})(\mathrm{CO})_2(\kappa O\!\text{-ester})$**, retaining the coordinated [arene](../../molecule.md#arene) and adding intramolecular coordination of its [ester](../../chemical-substance.md#ester) [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). The added [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) lone-pair donation restores an 18-electron count. The gaseous by-product is **[carbon monoxide](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide)**.

[heptane](../../chemical-substance.md#heptane) is a poor donor, but [intramolecular ligand trapping](../../chemistry.md#intramolecular-ligand-trapping) avoids needing a donor solvent. Benzene has no pendant donor. Although the [methoxy group](../../chemistry.md#methoxy-group) has [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [lone pairs](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair), its directly attached [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) does not have the same reach and favourable geometry as the projecting [ester](../../chemical-substance.md#ester) carbonyl [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen); possession of a [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) alone is insufficient. For these two substrates, [acetonitrile](../../chemical-substance.md#acetonitrile) can trap the unsaturated fragment through [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) to give $\mathrm{Cr}(\eta^6\!\text{-arene})(\mathrm{CO})_2(\mathrm{NCMe})$. Without a suitable trap, CO recombination can reverse the initial photochemical dissociation.

## B8

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="b8/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B8](#b8)

<h4 id="b8/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b8/a)

Let $z$ be normal to the Cp ring through its centre. The five [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) $p_z$ orbitals form [symmetry-adapted linear combinations](../../chemistry.md#symmetry-adapted-linear-combination) with angular patterns $m=0$, $|m|=1$, and $|m|=2$. The all-in-phase $a_1$ combination has sigma symmetry about this axis and can interact with $d_{z^2}$. The $e_1$ pair matches $(d_{xz},d_{yz})$ by pi symmetry, while the $e_2$ pair matches $(d_{xy},d_{x^2-y^2})$ by delta symmetry. Rotating within either degenerate pair changes the drawing but not the physical interaction.

For the usual eta-five geometry, **the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) $d$ interactions are ranked qualitatively pi \> delta \> sigma**. The $d_{xz},d_{yz}$ lobes overlap effectively with the occupied ring combinations. The delta interaction is weaker and involves the higher, initially unoccupied Cp combinations; the $d_{z^2}$ interaction with the ring is particularly weak because its axial and equatorial parts contribute little net overlap in this geometry. Thus the statement that a [sigma bond](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-bond) is always stronger than a [pi bond](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond) cannot be transferred indiscriminately to this delocalized ring–[metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) overlap problem. [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) $s$ and $p_z$ orbitals also have the symmetry of the $a_1$ [coordination ligand](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry) combination and contribute to overall bonding, so weak $d_{z^2}$ overlap does not mean the entire sigma-symmetry channel is absent.

In ionic [electron counting](../../chemistry.md#electron-counting), Cp is $\mathrm{C_5H_5^-}$ with six pi [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) in its three occupied ring combinations, hence **a six-electron donor**. The same [coordination ligand](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry) is assigned five [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) in neutral counting.

<h3 id="b8/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B8](#b8)

<h4 id="b8/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b8/b)

In a [metallocene](../../chemistry.md#metallocene), several orbitals with substantial [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) $d$ character are [nonbonding molecular orbitals](../../chemistry.md#nonbonding-molecular-orbital) or only weakly bonding or antibonding with respect to the strong Cp–[metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) framework. Adding or removing a small number of [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) from them can therefore change [oxidation state](../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) or [spin](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) without destroying the principal ring–[metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) bonds. This allows stable compounds on either side of the 18-electron count: for example, the usual neutral counts for [chromocene](../../chemistry.md#chromocene), [ferrocene](../../chemistry.md#ferrocene), [cobaltocene](../../chemistry.md#cobaltocene) and [nickelocene](../../chemistry.md#nickelocene) are respectively **16, 18, 19 and 20**. The [18-electron rule](../../chemistry.md#18-electron-rule) remains a useful reference, but occupation of a weakly [antibonding orbital](../../chemistry.md#antibonding-orbital) is much less disruptive than occupation of a strongly [antibonding orbital](../../chemistry.md#antibonding-orbital). Redox stability also depends on geometry, [substituents](../../stereochemistry.md#substituent) and reaction conditions, so this is a bonding explanation of the observed flexibility rather than a guarantee of equal stability for all counts.

<h3 id="b8/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B8](#b8)

<h4 id="b8/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b8/c)

Use neutral [electron counting](../../chemistry.md#electron-counting) consistently: each terminal Cp supplies five [electrons](../../physics.md#electron), while the eight pi [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) of neutral [pentalene](../../chemical-substance.md#pentalene) must be allocated across the shared fused framework rather than counted twice as two independent Cp [coordination ligands](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry). One set of **18-electron-consistent proposals** is:

- **Iron: syn $\mu$-$\eta^5:\eta^5$ [pentalene](../../chemical-substance.md#pentalene) with an Fe–Fe [single bond](../../covalent-bond.md#single-bond).** The two iron–Cp fragments lie on the same face. Assigning four [pentalene](../../chemical-substance.md#pentalene) [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) to each iron gives $8+5+4=17$ before direct bonding; the Fe–Fe bond contributes one [electron](../../physics.md#electron) to each count, giving 18. Its direct bond naturally accounts for the especially short [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal)–[metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) separation. An anti arrangement cannot retain this short same-face bond.
- **Cobalt: $\mu$-$\eta^5:\eta^5$ [pentalene](../../chemical-substance.md#pentalene) without a Co–Co bond.** The count is $9+5+4=18$ at each [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal). Syn and anti arrangements are possible at this formal level because there is no required direct bond. Removal of the bond relative to iron accounts for a larger separation.
- **Nickel: slipped $\mu$-$\eta^3:\eta^3$ coordination to the two outer allylic portions.** The fusion bond remains an uncoordinated [alkene](../../chemical-substance.md#alkene) in this limiting bonding picture, while each outer three-carbon portion contributes three [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) in neutral counting: $10+5+3=18$. Equivalent ionic counting gives a nickel(II) $d^8$ centre, a six-electron Cp and a four-electron [allyl ligand](../../chemistry.md#allyl-ligand). Moving the [metal](../../chemical-substance.md#metal) binding sites toward the outer three-carbon portions increases the separation further. Syn and anti arrangements can again be drawn.

One equivalent of butyllithium removes one proton per hydrocarbon, giving the **hydropentalenyl monoanion $\mathrm{C_8H_7^-}$**. It does not give the doubly deprotonated [pentalenide](../../chemical-substance.md#pentalenide). [salt metathesis](../../chemical-reaction.md#salt-metathesis) with half an equivalent of iron(II) chloride therefore gives the sandwich complex

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Fe}(\eta^5\!\text{-}\mathrm{C_8H_7})_2,\qquad d^6+6+6=18.}
$$

Each [coordination ligand](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry) binds through its aromatic five-membered portion and retains an uncoordinated [double bond](../../covalent-bond.md#double-bond) in the fused ring. The orientation of this unsymmetrical fused appendage makes the two coordinated [coordination ligand](../../chemistry.md#ligand-in-coordination-chemistry) faces distinguishable. Pairing matching and opposite planar configurations gives **a racemic form, $(R_p,R_p)/(S_p,S_p)$, and a meso form, $(R_p,S_p)$**. This describes the two stereoisomeric classes, rather than assigning the two easily interconverting rotations of ordinary [ferrocene](../../chemistry.md#ferrocene) as separate configurational isomers. The molecular structure and double-bond disorder of bis(hydropentalenyl)iron have been examined crystallographically.[Acta Crystallographica C 52, 2414–2416: parallel coordinated rings and double-bond isomer disorder](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#bis-eta-⁵-hydropentalenyl-iron-by-elies-molins-and-others)

Cleavage of the [disulfide](../../chemical-substance.md#disulfide) supplies one [methylthiolate](../../chemistry.md#methylthiolate) per cobalt. A monomer $\mathrm{CpCo(SMe)}$ would have only $7+6+2=15$ [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) by ionic counting. Dimerization with two bridging [thiolates](../../chemistry.md#thiolate) supplies a second [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) interaction at each cobalt, and a direct Co–Co bond completes each count. Thus a suitable structure is **$[\mathrm{CpCo}(\mu\!\text{-}\mathrm{SMe})]_2$ with a Co–Co [single bond](../../covalent-bond.md#single-bond)**, containing an $\mathrm{Co_2S_2}$ diamond:

$$
\mathrm{Co}:\quad d^7+6\ (\mathrm{Cp})+2+2\ (\text{two S donors})+1\ (\mathrm{Co-Co})=18.
$$

Each cobalt is formally $+2$: Cp and [methylthiolate](../../chemistry.md#methylthiolate) each carry one negative charge. The stoichiometry is

$$
2\mathrm{CpCo(CO)_2}+\mathrm{MeSSMe}\longrightarrow[\mathrm{CpCo}(\mu\!\text{-}\mathrm{SMe})]_2+4\mathrm{CO}.
$$

The [molecular formula](../../chemical-substance.md#molecular-formula) $\mathrm{C_{12}H_{16}Co_2S_2}$ reduces to the required empirical formula. The [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur)–[sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) bond has been cleaved, both [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) atoms now bridge the [metals](../../chemical-substance.md#metal), and the loss of two CO molecules per cobalt is balanced. Formally cobalt(I) is oxidized to cobalt(II), while the [disulfide](../../chemical-substance.md#disulfide) is reduced to [thiolate](../../chemistry.md#thiolate).

For the two-atom inorganic backbone unit, $Z_{\rm Si}+Z_{\rm O}=14+8=22$ and $Z_{\rm P}+Z_{\rm N}=15+7=22$. Their valence-electron sums also agree: $4+6=5+5=10$. Since each unit bears the same two organic groups, **$\mathrm{R_2SiO}$ and $\mathrm{R_2PN}$ are [isoelectronic species](../../chemistry.md#isoelectronic-species)**. This electron-count relationship does not require identical bond localization, bond angles or chemical reactivity.

<h3 id="b9/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B9](#b9)

<h4 id="b9/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b9/b)

In a [boroxine](../../chemical-substance.md#boroxine), each [boron](../../chemical-substance.md#boron) has three [sigma bonds](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-bond) and is approximately trigonal planar, with an $sp^2$ description for its sigma framework and an available perpendicular $2p$ orbital. [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) can be described approximately as $sp^2$: two in-plane hybrids form B–O [sigma bonds](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-bond), one contains an in-plane [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair), and a perpendicular $2p$ [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) overlaps with neighbouring [boron](../../chemical-substance.md#boron) $2p$ orbitals. These three [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) donor pairs supply a six-electron pi system around the alternating ring. The combination of good same-shell overlap and a low-strain six-membered ring favours planarity and partial B–O multiple-bond character. The bonds remain polar; a six-electron diagram alone does not establish benzene-like aromatic stabilization quantitatively.

In a [siloxane](../../chemical-substance.md#siloxane), each [silicon](../../chemical-substance.md#silicon) instead has four [sigma bonds](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-bond), two to [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) and two to its organic groups, giving an approximately tetrahedral $sp^3$ sigma framework. [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) has two [sigma bonds](../../covalent-bond.md#sigma-bond) and two [lone pairs](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair), so an approximately $sp^3$ localized description is a useful starting point, although actual Si–O–Si angles and lone-pair hybrids vary considerably. There is no comparable ring of vacant [silicon](../../chemical-substance.md#silicon) $2p$ orbitals. A description based on strong oxygen-$2p$ to silicon-$3d$ [pi bonds](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond) is also misleading: those orbitals have poor energy and radial matching. **Strongly polar Si–O sigma bonding, supplemented by [negative hyperconjugation](../../chemical-bond.md#negative-hyperconjugation) from [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [lone pairs](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair) into appropriate Si–R [antibonding orbitals](../../chemistry.md#antibonding-orbital), gives a better account**. The ring can therefore pucker rather than requiring continuous planar pi overlap. This follows the broader conclusion that strong main-group bonds and apparently expanded valence do not imply dominant valence $d$-orbital participation.[JACS 112, 1434–1445: ionic contributions and negative hyperconjugation versus d-orbital models](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#chemical-bonding-in-hypervalent-molecules-the-dominance-of-ionic-bonding-and-negative-hyperconjugation-over-d-orbital-participation-by-alan-e-reed-and-paul-von-rague-schleyer)

<h3 id="b9/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B9](#b9)

<h4 id="b9/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b9/c)

[boron](../../chemical-substance.md#boron) is sufficiently small for effective oxygen-$2p$/boron-$2p$ overlap, and three-coordinate planar [boron](../../chemical-substance.md#boron) benefits from the pi donation just described. A six-membered [boroxine](../../chemical-substance.md#boroxine) ring provides this overlap without severe angle strain. The larger [aluminium](../../chemical-substance.md#aluminium) centre has poorer oxygen-$2p$/aluminium-$3p$ matching and remains strongly Lewis acidic. It can lower its [electron](../../physics.md#electron) deficiency by accepting additional [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) coordination, making a three-dimensional [aluminoxane](../../chemical-substance.md#aluminoxane) cage favourable. Four-coordinate [aluminium](../../chemical-substance.md#aluminium) does not require a boroxine-like planar pi system.

An appropriate **hexamer model is an alternating Al/O [hexagonal prism](../../euclidean-geometry.md#hexagonal-prism)**, with six [aluminium](../../chemical-substance.md#aluminium) and six [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) vertices. Colour the twelve-vertex prism alternately Al and O: each [aluminium](../../chemical-substance.md#aluminium) then has three [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) neighbours and an outward organic [substituent](../../stereochemistry.md#substituent), making it four-coordinate, while each [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) bridges three [aluminium](../../chemical-substance.md#aluminium) centres. The graph has 18 Al–O edges, hence six Al centres with three Al–O bonds each. Different polyhedral connectivities and sizes can meet similar local coordination requirements, so the hexamer is a proposal rather than a claim that the empirical repeat unit fixes a unique cage.

<h3 id="b9/d">d</h3>

<h4 id="b9/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b9/d)

Ring cleavage of [1,3,5-Trioxane](../../chemical-substance.md#1-3-5-trioxane) regenerates **[formaldehyde](../../chemical-substance.md#formaldehyde)**, whose strong [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon)–[oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [pi bond](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond) stabilizes the monomer; in water much of it is further stabilized as its hydrate. [acetal hydrolysis](../../chemical-reaction.md#acetal-hydrolysis), particularly under acid catalysis, provides a route between the cyclic trimer and these monomeric forms.

The corresponding formal depolymerization of a [siloxane](../../chemical-substance.md#siloxane) would create **$\mathrm{R_2Si=O}$ [silanone](../../chemical-substance.md#silanone) units**. Such ordinary small-substituent [silanones](../../chemical-substance.md#silanone) have much less favourable pi bonding and are very reactive toward forming new Si–O–Si linkages. The connected [siloxane](../../chemical-substance.md#siloxane) is consequently preferred over a persistent aqueous solution of [silanone](../../chemical-substance.md#silanone) monomers. This explains the intended contrast, but aqueous stability is conditional: acid or base can catalyse [siloxane](../../chemical-substance.md#siloxane) cleavage to [silanols](../../chemical-substance.md#silanol), and those [silanols](../../chemical-substance.md#silanol) can condense again. Neither neutral-water inertness of every [siloxane](../../chemical-substance.md#siloxane) nor instantaneous neutral-water dissociation of every trioxane should be inferred.

First count the organic groups and then the unsatisfied [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) valences. **A is the fully condensed [T8 silsesquioxane cage](../../chemical-substance.md#t8-silsesquioxane-cage), $\mathrm{Cy_8Si_8O_{12}}$**, with [silicon](../../chemical-substance.md#silicon) at the eight vertices of a [cube](../../euclidean-geometry.md#cube) and bridging [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) on its twelve edges. Its eight [cyclohexyl groups](../../stereochemistry.md#cyclohexyl-group) supply exactly $\mathrm{C_{48}H_{88}}$.

**B is a fully condensed [T10 silsesquioxane cage](../../chemical-substance.md#t10-silsesquioxane-cage), $\mathrm{Cy_{10}Si_{10}O_{15}}$**, for which a pentagonal-prismatic [silicon](../../chemical-substance.md#silicon) skeleton with an [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) on every edge is a standard compatible structure. Its ten groups give $\mathrm{C_{60}H_{110}}$. The formula establishes full condensation but does not, without more structural data, rule out every other possible ten-vertex cage connectivity.

**C is a corner-deficient [T7 trisilanol cage](../../chemical-substance.md#t7-trisilanol-cage), $\mathrm{Cy_7Si_7O_9(OH)_3}$**. Seven [cyclohexyl groups](../../stereochemistry.md#cyclohexyl-group) account for $\mathrm{C_{42}H_{77}}$, leaving three hydrogen atoms as Si–OH groups. Remove one [silicon](../../chemical-substance.md#silicon) corner of the T8 [cube](../../euclidean-geometry.md#cube) and replace the three severed connections by terminal hydroxyl groups. Nine [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) atoms remain as bridges and three become terminal [silanol](../../chemical-substance.md#silanol) [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) atoms, giving precisely twelve [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) atoms. There are three [silicon](../../chemical-substance.md#silicon) sites bearing OH, three further equivalent fully condensed sites, and one opposite fully condensed site, consistent with **three [silicon](../../chemical-substance.md#silicon) environments in a 3:3:1 site-count ratio**. The less negative resonance is naturally associated with the three silanol-bearing sites; the remaining two resonances belong to the two fully condensed classes. Peak heights alone are not quantitative integrations.

<h5 id="b10/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

Treat the reacting [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) skeletons as an activated glycosyl donor and a 3-substituted [indole](../../molecule.md#indole). The new linkage is **an anomeric-carbon–indole-C2 [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon)–[carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) bond**, not a bond to [indole](../../molecule.md#indole) [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen). A plausible purely chemical pathway is a dissociative [electrophilic aromatic substitution](../../chemical-reaction.md#electrophilic-aromatic-substitution):

- Activation of the anomeric diphosphate [leaving group](../../chemical-reaction.md#leaving-group), for example by acid, permits cleavage of its bond to the [anomeric carbon](../../stereochemistry.md#anomeric-carbon). The ring [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) stabilizes the resulting [glycosyloxocarbenium ion](../../chemical-substance.md#glycosyloxocarbenium-ion), represented by the resonance pair $\mathrm{O_{ring}-C_1^+\leftrightarrow O_{ring}^+=C_1}$. A nearby diphosphate counterion may remain in a [contact ion pair](../../ionic-bonding.md#contact-ion-pair).
- The [indole](../../molecule.md#indole) C2–C3 [pi bond](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond) attacks C1 through C2. This makes the new C1–C2 bond and gives an [arenium ion](../../chemical-reaction.md#arenium-ion), with C2 temporarily tetrahedral and still bearing its original hydrogen. Positive charge can be delocalized over the pyrrolic portion, including an indolium resonance contributor.
- A base removes that C2 hydrogen. The C2–H bond [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) restore the C2–C3 [pi bond](../../covalent-bond.md#pi-bond) and [aromaticity](../../molecule.md#aromaticity), releasing the [C-glycosylation](../../chemical-reaction.md#c-glycosylation) product while retaining N–H.

The drawn donor and product have opposite anomeric faces: the anomeric [leaving group](../../chemical-reaction.md#leaving-group) is on the same face as the hydroxymethyl [substituent](../../stereochemistry.md#substituent), while the new [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) [substituent](../../stereochemistry.md#substituent) is on the opposite face. Thus the target has **net inversion at the [anomeric carbon](../../stereochemistry.md#anomeric-carbon)**. A tight [ion pair](../../ionic-bonding.md#ion-pair) with the departing group shielding its original face can rationalize opposite-face capture in this proposed pathway. A freely solvated planar [oxocarbenium ion](../../chemical-substance.md#oxocarbenium-ion) would not by itself guarantee exclusive inversion; competing faces, [hydrolysis](../../chemical-reaction.md#hydrolysis) and other products remain possible without an experimentally specified chemical promoter. This is a plausible non-enzymatic mechanism, not a claim that the uncatalysed reaction is rapid or clean in neutral water.
