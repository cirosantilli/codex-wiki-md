# Connected subset

↑ **Parent:** [Connected space](connected-space.md)

A [subset](subset.md) $A$ of a [topological space](topological-space.md) $X$ is a [connected subset](connected-subset.md) when $A$, with the [subspace topology](subspace-topology.md) inherited from $X$, is a [connected space](connected-space.md). Thus [connectedness](connected-space.md) is tested inside $A$: a forbidden [separation of a topological space](separation-of-a-topological-space.md) consists of nonempty [subsets](subset.md)

$$
U=A\cap O_1,\qquad V=A\cap O_2,\qquad A=U\cup V,\qquad U\cap V=\varnothing,
$$

where $O_1,O_2$ are [open sets](open-set.md) in $X$. The ambient [open sets](open-set.md) need not be disjoint outside $A$.

For example, in the [real line](real-line.md) with its usual [topology](topology-split.md), $[0,2]$ is a [connected subset](connected-subset.md), whereas $\{0,2\}$ is not: its two [singleton sets](singleton-mathematics.md) are [open](open-set.md) in its [subspace topology](subspace-topology.md). A [subset](subset.md) of a [connected space](connected-space.md) need not be [connected](connected-space.md). Conversely, $[-2,-1]$ is a [connected subset](connected-subset.md) of the disconnected [topological space](topological-space.md) $[-2,-1]\cup[1,2]$. The ambient [topological space](topological-space.md) need not itself be [connected](connected-space.md).

A [finite set](finite-set.md) of distinct points in the [real line](real-line.md) inherits the [discrete topology](discrete-space.md): a sufficiently small [open interval](open-interval.md) around each point isolates that point. Its only nonempty [connected subsets](connected-subset.md) are [singleton sets](singleton-mathematics.md). This depends on the [topology](topology-split.md): a nonempty [finite set](finite-set.md) with the [indiscrete topology](indiscrete-topology.md) is a [connected space](connected-space.md), since its only [open sets](open-set.md) are itself and the [empty set](empty-set.md). Here, as in the definition of [connected space](connected-space.md), the [empty set](empty-set.md) is also [connected](connected-space.md) because it admits no [separation of a topological space](separation-of-a-topological-space.md).[Definition 3.1.1 and Example 3.1.3: connectedness and discrete spaces](point-set-topology-by-romyar-sharifi/chapter-3-connected-and-compact-spaces.md)

The distinction matters when finding [connected components](connected-component.md): we search among [connected subsets](connected-subset.md) of the specified [topological space](topological-space.md), rather than judging whether the surrounding drawing looks like one piece.
