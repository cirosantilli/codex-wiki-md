# Probability theory

↑ **Parent:** [Probability and statistics](probability-and-statistics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_theory)

Probability theory describes uncertainty through a [probability space](probability-space-split.md): possible outcomes form a [sample space](sample-space.md), observable questions are [events](event.md), and a [probability measure](probability-measure.md) assigns them numbers between zero and one. The [Kolmogorov axioms](kolmogorov-axioms.md) make those assignments consistent. They let us combine information about several [events](event.md), update it through [conditional probability](conditional-probability.md), and study [random variables](random-variable-split.md) without listing every possible outcome separately.

For example, roll two dice whose results are [independent random variables](independent-random-variables.md), each with a [discrete uniform distribution](discrete-uniform-distribution.md) on $\{1,\ldots,6\}$. There are 36 equally likely ordered outcomes. Only $(1,6),(2,5),\ldots,(6,1)$ have sum seven, so that [event](event.md) has [probability](probability.md) $6/36=1/6$. The sum itself is not uniformly distributed: sum two has only one outcome. Choosing a [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) for the elementary outcomes is part of the model; equal likelihood does not follow merely from counting the named possibilities.

The subject becomes especially useful when complete outcome lists are enormous or infinite. [Independence](independent-random-variables.md) turns a joint calculation into simpler pieces, [expected value](expected-value-split.md) summarizes a [random variable](random-variable-split.md), and the [law of large numbers](law-of-large-numbers.md) explains how averages can become predictable even when individual observations are uncertain. The [central limit theorem](central-limit-theorem.md) describes the scale and shape of many remaining fluctuations. These are distinct claims: a small [variance](variance-split.md) of an average does not make every observation close to its [expected value](expected-value-split.md).

There is a satisfying unity here: dice, hidden causes, and [Brownian motion](brownian-motion-split.md) all use the same rules for [events](event.md) and [conditional probability](conditional-probability.md). A model still needs justification. Its exact deductions describe what its assumptions imply; comparison with observations belongs to [statistical inference](statistical-inference-split.md).
