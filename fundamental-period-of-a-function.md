# Fundamental period of a function

↑ **Parent:** [Periodic function](periodic-function.md)

The fundamental period is the least positive [period of a function](period-of-a-function.md), when one exists. For $f(x)=\sin(2\pi x)$ it is one; for $f(x)=\sin^2(2\pi x)$ it is $1/2$. A [periodic function](periodic-function.md) need not have a fundamental period: a [constant function](constant-function.md) has every positive period, and [periodic function without a least positive period](periodic-function-without-a-least-positive-period.md) is a [nonconstant](nonconstant-function.md) exception. Every [nonconstant](nonconstant-function.md) [real-valued function](real-valued-function.md) that is [continuous](continuous-function.md) and [periodic](periodic-function.md) on the [real line](real-line.md) does have a least positive period.
