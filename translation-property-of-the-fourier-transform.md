# Translation property of the Fourier transform

↑ **Parent:** [Fourier inversion theorem](fourier-inversion-theorem.md)

For an [integrable function](lebesgue-integrable-function.md) and the angular-frequency convention,

$$
\mathcal F[f(x-a)](k)=e^{-ika}\widehat f(k),
$$

so a [translation](translation-geometry.md) multiplies each [frequency](frequency.md) component by a factor of modulus one. The [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) of every component stays the same, while its [wave phase](phase-waves.md) changes by $-ka$. For example, delaying $\cos(2\pi\nu t)$ by $a$ produces $\cos(2\pi\nu t-2\pi\nu a)$: faster [oscillations](oscillation.md) accumulate a larger [wave phase](phase-waves.md) shift for the same delay. Substituting $y=x-a$ in the defining [Fourier transform](fourier-transform.md) is the entire proof. Conversely, this multiplier in the [Fourier transform](fourier-transform.md) produces a [translation](translation-geometry.md) after [Fourier inversion](fourier-inversion-theorem.md).
