<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Apply the [Rankine-Hugoniot conditions](../../../../rankine-hugoniot-conditions.md) to the conserved variables $(h,hu)$ and flux $(hu,hu^2+h^2/2)$. They give

$$
s(h_1-1)=h_1u_1,\qquad sh_1u_1=h_1u_1^2+\frac{h_1^2-1}{2}.
$$

For a genuine shock, $h_1\ne1$. Eliminating $u_1$ yields $s^2=h_1(h_1+1)/2$. Taking the positive branch requested,

$$
\boxed{s=\sqrt{\frac{h_1(h_1+1)}2},\qquad u_1=(h_1-1)\sqrt{\frac{h_1+1}{2h_1}}}.
$$

When $h_1=1$, the jump conditions instead force $u_1=0$ and describe a constant state; the labelled interface can then move at any speed and is not a shock. The nontrivial formulas describe a [shallow-water shock into stationary unit-depth water](../../../../shallow-water-shock-into-stationary-unit-depth-water.md).
