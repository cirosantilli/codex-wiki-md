# Electric field of a uniformly charged ball

↑ **Parent:** [Gauss's law](gauss-s-law.md)

Distribute a positive [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $Q$ uniformly through a [closed ball](closed-ball.md) of radius $R$ in vacuum, with no external field. Its volume [charge density](charge-density.md) is $3Q/(4\pi R^3)$. For $0<r<R$, the enclosed fraction is $(r/R)^3$. Applying [Gauss's law](gauss-s-law.md) on a concentric [Gaussian surface](gaussian-surface.md) gives

$$
E_r(r)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{Qr}{4\pi\varepsilon_0R^3},&0\leq r\leq R,\\[4pt]\dfrac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2},&r\geq R.\end{cases}
$$

The [electric field](electric-field-split.md) grows linearly inside and decays as an inverse square outside. With $E_R=Q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0R^2)$, its values at $r/R=1/2,1,2$ are $E_R/2,E_R,E_R/4$. The field vanishes at the centre by [spherical symmetry](spherical-symmetry.md) and is continuous at the surface because this ideal volume distribution has no singular surface layer. Its interior behavior differs from a [conductor in electrostatic equilibrium](conductor-in-electrostatic-equilibrium.md), where mobile charge redistributes.
