# Capillary closure of a falling hollow thread

↑ **Parent:** [Capillary tensile force of a hollow slender thread](capillary-tensile-force-of-a-hollow-slender-thread.md)

For steady equal-pressure flow, [mass conservation](mass-conservation.md) gives $Aw=A_0w_0$ and the [kinematic boundary condition](kinematic-boundary-condition.md) gives $(D\sqrt w)_z=\gamma/(2\mu\sqrt w)$, where $D=R_e-R_i$. Therefore finite-height closure occurs exactly when

$$
\frac\gamma{2\mu\sqrt{w_0}}\int_0^{z_*}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{w(z)}}=\sqrt{A_0}-D_0.
$$

For positive [velocity](velocity.md) the [integral](integral.md) is increasing. If it diverges, closure is finite; if its limiting value lies below the right-hand threshold, there is no finite closure. Quadratic freely falling profiles have logarithmically divergent [integrals](integral.md), while sufficiently strongly drawn exponentially accelerating profiles can have finite [integrals](integral.md). Thus nozzle speed alone is insufficient to guarantee closure: the axial tension or downstream condition matters.
