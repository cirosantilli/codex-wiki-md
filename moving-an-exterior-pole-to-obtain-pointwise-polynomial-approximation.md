# Moving an exterior pole to obtain pointwise polynomial approximation

↑ **Parent:** [Polynomial approximation of an exterior pole on a convex compact set](polynomial-approximation-of-an-exterior-pole-on-a-convex-compact-set.md)

Let $z_0$ be a boundary point of a compact convex set and choose exterior points $\zeta_n\to z_0$. Approximate $(z-\zeta_n)^{-1}$ uniformly on the compact set by a polynomial with error at most $1/n$. At each fixed $z\ne z_0$, the reciprocal converges and so does the approximating polynomial. On a boundary accumulating at $z_0$, uniform convergence to the singular reciprocal is impossible because each polynomial is bounded there. This distinguishes [pointwise convergence](pointwise-convergence.md) from [uniform convergence](uniform-convergence.md) without confusing the two norms.
