# Ultraviolet catastrophe

↑ **Parent:** [Rayleigh-Jeans law](rayleigh-jeans-law.md)

The ultraviolet catastrophe is the divergent total thermal radiation energy predicted by classical [equipartition theorem](equipartition-theorem.md) applied to the [electromagnetic mode density in a box](electromagnetic-mode-density-in-a-box.md). Each mode has mean energy $k_BT$, while the number of modes per volume per frequency is $8\pi\nu^2/c^3$. The [Rayleigh-Jeans law](rayleigh-jeans-law.md) therefore gives $u_\nu=8\pi k_BT\nu^2/c^3$, whose integral over all positive frequencies diverges at every $T>0$.

[Planck's law](planck-s-law.md) replaces the classical mode energy by $h\nu/(e^{h\nu/(k_BT)}-1)$. It agrees with equipartition when $h\nu\ll k_BT$ but suppresses high-frequency excitations exponentially. The resulting spectral energy density is integrable and yields the finite [Stefan–Boltzmann law](stefan-boltzmann-law.md). The failure concerns the classical equilibrium distribution of energy among modes, not the existence of electromagnetic wave propagation itself.
