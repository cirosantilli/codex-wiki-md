# Second messenger

↑ **Parent:** [Signal transduction](signal-transduction.md)

A [second messenger](second-messenger.md) is a small intracellular [molecule](molecule.md) or [ion](ion.md) that relays a received signal to target [proteins](protein.md). Its production, release, removal or binding changes its availability after signal reception. For a [cell-surface receptor](cell-surface-receptor.md), distinguish the extracellular [ligand](ligand-biochemistry.md), often called the first messenger, from the intracellular [second messenger](second-messenger.md). An activated relay [protein](protein.md) is not thereby a [second messenger](second-messenger.md): the term identifies small chemical mediators, rather than every downstream component of [signal transduction](signal-transduction.md). [Newton, Bootman and Scott, Second Messengers (2016): definition and classes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27481708/)

Two examples clarify what changes. A [biological cell](biological-cell.md) can make [cAMP](cyclic-adenosine-monophosphate.md) from [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md); alternatively, release of stored [calcium](calcium.md) [ions](ion.md) can raise the free [calcium](calcium.md) [concentration](concentration.md) in its [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md). The latter need not increase the total [calcium](calcium.md) content of the [biological cell](biological-cell.md). Availability to target [proteins](protein.md), not merely the total amount present, carries the message. Removal and sequestration help terminate such signals. [Purves et al., Neuroscience, 2nd edition: second messengers and calcium storage](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK10794/) [Pathways of Intracellular Signal Transduction](the-cell-a-molecular-approach-2nd-edition-by-geoffrey-m-cooper/pathways-of-intracellular-signal-transduction.md)

The experimental motivation is unusually tangible: can a [hormone](hormone.md)'s effect be transferred to machinery that barely responds to the [hormone](hormone.md) itself? In Rall, Sutherland and Berthet's 1957 experiment, washed cat-liver particles were incubated with [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) and [magnesium](magnesium.md) [ions](ion.md), with or without [epinephrine](adrenaline.md) and [glucagon](glucagon.md), two [hormones](hormone.md). After five minutes at $30\,{}^{\circ}\mathrm C$, the mixtures were heated in boiling water for three minutes, chilled, and separated to remove insoluble material. Small samples of the clarified extracts were transferred to a separate soluble dog-liver preparation, again supplied with [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) and [magnesium](magnesium.md) [ions](ion.md). The readout was the change in [glycogen phosphorylase](glycogen-phosphorylase.md) activity over five minutes, not the amount of newly synthesized [protein](protein.md). [Rall, Sutherland and Berthet (1957), Figure 4 and pp. 470–471: preparation and transfer](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9258(18)65045-8)

The [hormone](hormone.md)-conditioned extract caused a larger increase in [glycogen phosphorylase](glycogen-phosphorylase.md) activity. Water, extract made without [hormones](hormone.md), and [hormones](hormone.md) added directly to the soluble preparation produced smaller effects. These comparisons distinguish transferred activity from baseline activity, preparation effects and simple [hormone](hormone.md) carryover. [Rall, Sutherland and Berthet (1957), Figure 4: controls and observations](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9258(18)65045-8)

The inference is a transferable, heat-stable intermediary between the [hormone](hormone.md)-sensitive particles and the soluble machinery. This does not identify the intermediary merely from heat resistance, establish its direct target, or show that every [hormone](hormone.md) uses the same mechanism. Rall and Sutherland's 1958 paper reports the factor's identification as [cAMP](cyclic-adenosine-monophosphate.md) in accompanying chemical work, and follows its production from [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md). It also reports an important limit: washed heart and skeletal-muscle particles produced detectable [cAMP](cyclic-adenosine-monophosphate.md) with [epinephrine](adrenaline.md) under their test conditions, whereas brain particles produced similar amounts with and without it. Tissue and preparation matter. [Rall and Sutherland (1958), introduction and Table IV: cAMP identity and tissue-dependent responses](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9258(19)77422-5)

The useful and beautiful separation is between recognition and execution: an extracellular [ligand](ligand-biochemistry.md) can control intracellular [enzymes](enzyme.md) through a chemically different intermediary. Making many [second messenger](second-messenger.md) [molecules](molecule.md) can support [signal amplification](signal-amplification.md), while different target [proteins](protein.md) give the same mediator different consequences. A [second messenger](second-messenger.md) identifies a mode of relaying information; its presence alone does not specify the size or direction of the response. [Newton, Bootman and Scott (2016): second messengers and their targets](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27481708/)
