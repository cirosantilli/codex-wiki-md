# Solution mass and volume bookkeeping

↑ **Parent:** [Solution (chemistry)](solution-chemistry.md)

The denominator of [molar concentration](molar-concentration.md) is the final [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) of the [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md), including the dissolved [solute](solute.md). It is not the [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) of [solvent](solvent.md) initially poured in. A [volumetric flask](volumetric-flask.md) fixes the final total [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md); weighing the [solute](solute.md) supplies its [mass](mass.md), and a specified [molar mass](molar-mass.md) converts that [mass](mass.md) to [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md).[Chemistry 2e, §3.3, molarity definition and Example 3.16: mass converted to moles and divided by total solution volume](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/3-3-molarity.md)

Consider an original illustrative account, not reported measurements. Suppose $2.922\,\mathrm g$ of pure dry [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md), with stipulated [molar mass](molar-mass.md) $58.44\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$, is completely dissolved and quantitatively transferred into a [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) whose final [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) is $250.0\,\mathrm{mL}$. Assume no losses, no additional [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction-split.md), and complete [dissociation of an ionic compound](dissociation-of-an-ionic-compound.md). Then

$$
n_{\mathrm{NaCl}}=\frac{2.922\,\mathrm g}{58.44\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}}=0.05000\,\mathrm{mol},\qquad
c_{\mathrm{NaCl,analytical}}=\frac{0.05000\,\mathrm{mol}}{0.2500\,\mathrm L}=0.2000\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}.
$$

Here [analytical concentration](analytical-concentration.md) counts the [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md) inventory, not intact dissolved $\mathrm{NaCl}$ [molecules](molecule-split.md). Under these assumptions, the [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) of [sodium](sodium.md) [cations](cation.md) and of [chloride](chloride.md) [anions](anion.md) is separately $0.05000\,\mathrm{mol}$; each has [molar concentration](molar-concentration.md) $0.2000\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. Their combined ionic [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) is $0.1000\,\mathrm{mol}$, but this does not double either the salt inventory or the [mass](mass.md). Each [sodium](sodium.md) [cation](cation.md) still contains a [sodium](sodium.md) [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md), and each [chloride](chloride.md) [anion](anion.md) a [chlorine](chlorine.md) [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md). Counting [chemical elements](chemical-element.md) survives [dissociation of an ionic compound](dissociation-of-an-ionic-compound.md).

The [mass](mass.md) ledger is different from the [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) ledger. If the final [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) had an illustrative net [mass](mass.md) of $252.922\,\mathrm g$, the [water](water.md) contribution would be $252.922-2.922=250.000\,\mathrm g$, assuming nothing entered or escaped except those two components. The ratio $2.922/252.922\approx0.011553$ expresses the solute share of total [mass](mass.md); the separate [mass density](density.md) would be $252.922/250.0=1.011688\,\mathrm{g\,mL^{-1}}$ before rounding. These stipulated quantities are mutually consistent bookkeeping inputs, not a measured [mass density](density.md) for saltwater. Neither establishes that $250.0\,\mathrm{mL}$ of [water](water.md) was added.

More generally, with full transfer and conserved [solute](solute.md), $n=m/M$ and $c=m/(MV)$. No additive [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) or ideal [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) assumption is needed when $V$ is the measured final [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md). If only a fraction $f$ of the weighed [solute](solute.md) reaches the flask, the actual result is $c=fm/(MV)$. This short calculation explains why an undissolved residue or an incomplete rinse creates a systematic deficit even when the final calibration mark is read correctly.
