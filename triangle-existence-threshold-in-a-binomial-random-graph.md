# Triangle-existence threshold in a binomial random graph

↑ **Parent:** [Triangle variance in a binomial random graph](triangle-variance-in-a-binomial-random-graph.md)

If $np\to0$, the [triangle count in a binomial random graph](triangle-count-in-a-binomial-random-graph.md) has [expectation](expected-value.md) at most $(np)^3/6\to0$, so the [Markov inequality](markov-inequality.md) shows there are no [triangles in a graph](triangle-in-a-graph.md) with probability tending to one. If $np\to\infty$, the [triangle variance in a binomial random graph](triangle-variance-in-a-binomial-random-graph.md) divided by the squared mean is $O((np)^{-3})+O((n^2p)^{-1})\to0$. The [second moment method](second-moment-method.md) then gives at least one [triangle in a graph](triangle-in-a-graph.md) with probability tending to one. At the intermediate scale $np\to c\in(0,\infty)$, a nontrivial [Poisson limit for triangles in a binomial random graph](poisson-limit-for-triangles-in-a-binomial-random-graph.md) describes the count.
