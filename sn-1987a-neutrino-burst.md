# SN 1987A neutrino burst

↑ **Parent:** [SN 1987A](sn-1987a.md)

The original [Kamiokande](kamiokande.md) paper reported a low-energy event cluster on 23 February 1987 above surrounding background. [Inverse beta decay](inverse-beta-decay.md), $\bar\nu_e+p\longrightarrow e^++n$, provides an [electron antineutrino](electron-antineutrino.md) channel through [Positron](positron.md) light. [Neutrino](neutrino.md)-[electron](electron.md) [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md) is more directional. The burst supported [core-collapse supernova](core-collapse-supernova.md) [neutrino](neutrino.md) emission, with limited statistics and channel ambiguity.[pp. 1490–1493; Fig. 2, Table I and discussion](observation-of-a-neutrino-burst-from-the-supernova-sn1987a-by-k-hirata-and-others.md)

An original counting illustration shows why timing matters. Suppose an independently measured background is a [Poisson process](poisson-process-split.md) of rate $b$. In a prespecified window $\Delta t$, the probability of at least two background events is

$$
P(N\ge2)=1-e^{-b\Delta t}(1+b\Delta t).
$$

For the synthetic choice $b\Delta t=0.1$, this is about $0.004679$, obtained by subtracting the zero- and one-event probabilities of the [Poisson distribution](poisson-distribution.md). The [Homestake experiment](homestake-experiment.md)'s delayed extraction cannot supply such capture-time clustering. This calculation is not a significance estimate for [SN 1987A](sn-1987a.md): searching many possible windows, varying acceptance and correlated backgrounds change the inference.
