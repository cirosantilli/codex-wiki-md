# Lamb-Dicke regime

↑ **Parent:** [Trapped-ion internal and motional states](trapped-ion-internal-and-motional-states.md)

The Lamb-Dicke regime means the wave-induced [phase](phase-waves.md) change across the occupied motional wavepacket is small. For one [normal mode](normal-mode.md), let $x_0=\sqrt{\hbar/(2m\nu)}$ be the [ground state](ground-state.md)'s position [standard deviation](standard-deviation.md), and write $x=x_0(a+a^\dagger)$ using [creation and annihilation operators](creation-and-annihilation-operators.md). Here $m$ is [mass](mass.md) and $\hbar$ the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md) divided by $2\pi$. The effective [wave vector](wavevector.md) component $k_{\rm eff}$ is the single beam's component for a direct transition, or the difference of two beam components for a [Raman transition](raman-transition.md). With dimensionless $\eta=|k_{\rm eff}|x_0$, the useful condition is $\eta\sqrt{2n+1}\ll1$ over the populated [number states](number-state.md), rather than merely $\eta\ll1$.

Absorb the sign of $k_{\rm eff}$ into the pulse [phase](phase-waves.md). Expanding the coupling factor gives

$$
e^{i\eta(a+a^\dagger)}=1+i\eta(a+a^\dagger)+O(\eta^2).
$$

The constant term preserves $n$; the [annihilation operator](annihilation-operator.md) gives $a|n\rangle=\sqrt n|n-1\rangle$; the [creation operator](creation-operator.md) gives $a^\dagger|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}|n+1\rangle$. The different square roots explain why removing a motional quantum becomes impossible at $n=0$, while adding one remains possible. Smallness of $\eta$ controls this spatial expansion; resolving the resulting transitions is a separate [frequency](frequency.md) requirement.
