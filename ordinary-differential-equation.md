# Ordinary differential equation

↑ **Parent:** [Differential equation](differential-equation-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_differential_equation)

An [ordinary differential equation](ordinary-differential-equation.md) relates an unknown [function](function-split.md) of one independent variable to its ordinary [derivatives](derivative.md). Its order is the highest [derivative](derivative.md) appearing. Thus $y'=-k(y-T_a)$ is first order, while the [harmonic oscillator equation](simple-harmonic-motion.md) $y''+\omega^2y=0$ is second order. A first-order equation in explicit form is

$$
y'(t)=f(t,y(t)).
$$

Here $t$ need not be time, and $y$ can be a [vector](vector.md), giving a system of coupled [ordinary differential equations](ordinary-differential-equation.md). A classical [solution of a differential equation](solution-of-a-differential-equation.md) is a sufficiently [differentiable function](differentiable-function.md) on an [interval](interval-mathematics.md) which satisfies the equation at every point. For the explicit first-order equation, if $f$ is continuous, this [solution of a differential equation](solution-of-a-differential-equation.md) has a continuous [derivative](derivative.md).

For [Newton's law of cooling](newton-s-law-of-cooling.md), the state is a single [temperature](temperature-split.md); for a [second-order differential equation](second-order-differential-equation.md), a first-order system also records its first [derivative](derivative.md). This is why a starting position alone does not specify a [harmonic oscillator](simple-harmonic-motion.md) trajectory: its starting velocity matters too.

The power of an [ordinary differential equation](ordinary-differential-equation.md) is that one rule describes a whole family of histories. An [initial value problem](initial-value-problem.md) chooses one history when uniqueness holds. An [autonomous differential equation](autonomous-system-mathematics.md) has $f$ independent of $t$; its [equilibrium points](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) satisfy $f(y_*)=0$ and give constant [solutions of a differential equation](solution-of-a-differential-equation.md). Equations with explicit time dependence can instead respond to a changing environment.
