# Complete vector field

↑ **Parent:** [Local flow](local-flow.md)

A smooth [vector field](vector-field.md) is complete if its maximal [integral curves of a vector field](integral-curve-of-a-vector-field.md) exist for every real time. Its [local flow](local-flow.md) is then a global one-parameter group of [diffeomorphisms](diffeomorphism.md); uniqueness of [ordinary differential equations](ordinary-differential-equation.md) proves the group law and the inverse-time identity. On a [compact](compact-space.md) manifold without boundary, every smooth vector field is complete: [compactness](compact-space.md) gives a uniform positive local existence interval through every point, so a curve can be successively extended past any proposed finite endpoint. On $\mathbb R$, $x^2\partial_x$ is not complete because its solution $x(t)=x_0/(1-tx_0)$ has finite-time blowup for $x_0>0$.
