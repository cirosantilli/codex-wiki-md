Here $G$ is the complex internal horizontal torque, $P$ the vertically integrated pressure, and $\omega_1,\omega_2$ the approximate [nodal precession](nodal-precession.md) and [apsidal precession](apsidal-precession.md) rates. In the exactly Keplerian case, local plane waves obey $\omega^2=k^2P/(4\Sigma)=k^2c_s^2/4$, so tilt disturbances propagate at $c_s/2$. The secular thin-disk approximation requires wavelengths large relative to the height while the local approximation requires them small relative to the orbital radius.
