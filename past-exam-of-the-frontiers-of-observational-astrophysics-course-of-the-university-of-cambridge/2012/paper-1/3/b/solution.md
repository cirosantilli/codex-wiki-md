<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [radial-velocity method](../../../../../doppler-spectroscopy.md) measures repeated Doppler shifts of stellar [spectral lines](../../../../../spectral-line.md) as a star responds to an orbiting companion. A stable high-resolution spectrograph and [wavelength](../../../../../wavelength.md) reference distinguish stellar motion from instrumental drift; many lines or a spectral template improve precision. A Keplerian fit yields period, eccentricity, phase and velocity semi-amplitude. For a companion [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $m_p$, stellar [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $M_*$ and inclination $i$,

$$
\boxed{K=\left(\frac{2\pi G}{P}\right)^{1/3}
\frac{m_p\sin i}{(M_*+m_p)^{2/3}\sqrt{1-e^2}}.}
$$

With a stellar-mass estimate and $m_p\ll M_*$, this measures approximately $m_p\sin i$, a minimum [mass](../../../../../mass.md) unless the inclination is independently constrained. A nearly face-on orbit can hide a massive companion. The method preferentially detects larger masses, shorter periods and favorable inclinations.

Starspots, activity, pulsations, rotation, blended spectra and sampling aliases can imitate or distort a periodic signal. Line-shape diagnostics, activity indicators, repeated observations and independent constraints help test a planetary explanation. [Photon](../../../../../photon.md) [noise](../../../../../noise-signal-processing.md) and [spectral line](../../../../../spectral-line.md) width constrain precision even for a perfectly calibrated spectrograph. Multiple planets or incomplete orbital coverage require an adequate model and longer monitoring; a periodic centroid shift alone is not a proof of a particular planetary system.
