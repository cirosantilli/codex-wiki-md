↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[thermal bremsstrahlung](../../../../../thermal-bremsstrahlung.md) is continuum radiation from [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) accelerated in ion electric fields. For [Maxwellian](../../../../../local-maxwellian.md) [electrons](../../../../../electron.md),

$$
j_\nu\propto n_e\sum_i Z_i^2n_i\,T_e^{-1/2}e^{-h\nu/(k_BT_e)}g_{\rm ff}.
$$

At high [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) the exponential cutoff constrains $T_e$; X-ray fitting must also model foreground absorption, line emission, detector response and possible multitemperature or nonthermal components. At radio [frequencies](../../../../../frequency.md), $h\nu\ll k_BT_e$, an [optically thin](../../../../../optically-thin-medium.md) free-free spectrum is almost flat, with the weak [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) dependence set mainly by the [Gaunt factor](../../../../../gaunt-factor.md).

For pure ionized hydrogen, radio [free-free opacity](../../../../../free-free-opacity.md) gives

$$
\tau_\nu\propto T_e^{-3/2}\nu^{-2}g_{\rm ff}\,\mathrm{EM},\qquad
\mathrm{EM}=\int n_e^2dl,\qquad
I_\nu=B_\nu(T_e)(1-e^{-\tau_\nu}).
$$

The turnover constrains [emission measure](../../../../../emission-measure.md); a resolved, [optically thick](../../../../../optically-thick-medium.md) source has [brightness temperature](../../../../../brightness-temperature.md) approaching $T_e$. An unresolved source has an unknown beam [filling factor](../../../../../filling-factor-quantum-hall-effect.md), so its observed brightness need not equal its [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). Angular size and an independent distance give a geometric depth $L$; a uniform model then yields **$n_e=\sqrt{\mathrm{EM}/L}$**. Clumping instead measures a density-squared average, not the mean [density](../../../../../density.md).[chapter 4, equations (4.57)–(4.60); emission measure and thick/thin spectra](../../../../../essential-radio-astronomy-by-james-j-condon-and-scott-m-ransom.md)

**A free-free spectrum alone does not uniquely determine [density](../../../../../density.md) and size.** The [density-depth degeneracy of free-free radiation](../../../../../density-depth-degeneracy-of-free-free-radiation.md) is explicit: for a resolved uniform column, $n_e\mapsto qn_e$, $L\mapsto L/q^2$ leaves both emissivity-integrated brightness and [optical depth](../../../../../optical-depth.md) unchanged at fixed [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). With independent SZ information and known $T_e$, the [electron](../../../../../electron.md) column is $N_e=ym_ec^2/(\sigma_Tk_BT_e)$; then

$$
\boxed{n_e=\mathrm{EM}/N_e,\qquad L=N_e^2/\mathrm{EM}}.
$$

These equalities require consistent composition and geometry, and uniform [density](../../../../../density.md) and [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). Relating $L$ to angular extent can give distance; asphericity and the [gas clumping factor](../../../../../gas-clumping-factor.md) are important limitations of this combined cluster method.
