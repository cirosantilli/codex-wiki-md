**Weak transitions in dilute gas.** A [forbidden emission line](../../../../../forbidden-emission-line.md) is a transition absent in the electric-dipole approximation because of its [selection rules](../../../../../selection-rule.md), but permitted through weaker channels such as magnetic-dipole or electric-quadrupole radiation, sometimes aided by state mixing. It is not an impossible transition. Its upper atomic level is relatively long lived, so collisions can deactivate it before a [photon](../../../../../photon.md) is emitted in dense gas. Low-density astrophysical plasmas allow radiative decay to compete successfully.

In a schematic two-level statistical equilibrium,

$$
n_l n_e q_{lu}=n_u(A_{ul}+n_eq_{ul}),\qquad
\epsilon_{ul}=n_uA_{ul}h\nu_{ul}.
$$

The critical density is $n_{\rm crit}=A_{ul}/q_{ul}$ for this simple level system; multiple radiative and collisional channels require summed rates. At $n_e\ll n_{\rm crit}$ each [collisional excitation](../../../../../collisional-excitation.md) is likely to produce radiation, so $\epsilon_{ul}\propto n_l n_e q_{lu}h\nu$. At high density collisional de-excitation suppresses the line relative to this low-density expectation. The rate need not decrease absolutely with increasing density; the suppression is relative to the naive excitation scaling.

**A temperature-sensitive ratio.** For a Maxwell-Boltzmann electron distribution,

$$
q_{lu}(T)\propto T^{-1/2}\Upsilon_{lu}(T)
\exp[-\Delta E_{lu}/(k_BT)],
$$

where $\Upsilon$ is an effective collision strength. Lines from upper levels with different excitation energies therefore have a strongly temperature-dependent ratio. Using one ion reduces sensitivity to abundance and [ionization](../../../../../ionization.md) fraction compared with comparing unrelated species.

A standard example is doubly ionized oxygen: the $4959$ and $5007$ angstrom lines descend from ${}^1D_2$ toward the ${}^3P$ ground term, while the $4363$ angstrom line connects ${}^1S_0$ to ${}^1D_2$. The higher excitation threshold makes the auroral $4363$ line relatively stronger in hotter gas. In the low-density Maxwellian approximation, with slowly varying collision strengths,

$$
\boxed{R_{\rm OIII}=\frac{I_{4959}+I_{5007}}{I_{4363}}
\simeq7.90\exp\left(\frac{32900\ {\rm K}}{T_e}\right),
\qquad T_e\simeq\frac{32900\ {\rm K}}{\ln(R_{\rm OIII}/7.90)}.}
$$

The numerical form is a useful approximation, not a universal exact atomic law; a multilevel atomic calculation with current collision strengths is needed for precise work.[Section 4, equation 18 and the following approximation warning; arXiv:1204.3880v2, PDF pages 5–6](../../../../../resolving-the-electron-temperature-discrepancies-in-h-ii-regions-and-planetary-nebulae-kappa-distributed-electrons-by-david-c-nicholls-and-others.md)

In an [active galactic nucleus](../../../../../active-galactic-nucleus.md) these lines commonly diagnose the relatively dilute narrow-line gas; dense broad-line gas can collisionally quench them. Measure and deblend the [fluxes](../../../../../flux.md), subtract continuum and stellar features, correct differential [extinction](../../../../../extinction.md), and check density independently. The $5007/4959$ ratio mainly tests radiative branching and [calibration](../../../../../calibration-differential-geometry.md) because both lines share one upper level; it is not the required temperature thermometer. Temperature gradients, multiple density components, [shocks](../../../../../shock-wave.md), [ionization](../../../../../ionization.md) structure and departures from the Maxwellian electron distribution can bias a single inferred temperature. Without a measured ratio and density regime, no particular AGN temperature is numerically determined here.
