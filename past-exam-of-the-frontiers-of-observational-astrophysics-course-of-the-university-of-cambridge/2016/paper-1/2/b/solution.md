<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Atmospheric refractive-index fluctuations impose a changing phase screen on the pupil. For a [telescope](../../../../../telescope.md) much larger than the [Fried parameter](../../../../../fried-parameter.md) $r_0$, long-exposure resolution is approximately $0.98\lambda/r_0$, rather than the [diffraction](../../../../../diffraction.md) scale $\lambda/D$. The scaling $r_0\propto\lambda^{6/5}$ makes the [seeing](../../../../../astronomical-seeing.md) width proportional to $\lambda^{-1/5}$ for fixed turbulence. An [atmospheric coherence time](../../../../../atmospheric-coherence-time.md) sets the exposure or correction cadence needed to freeze the phase, while the [isoplanatic angle](../../../../../isoplanatic-angle.md) sets the useful angular separation from a reference source. These are atmospheric quantities, not improvements automatically supplied by a larger [mirror](../../../../../mirror.md).

First reduce avoidable turbulence: choose a good high site, control [mirror](../../../../../mirror.md) and enclosure [temperatures](../../../../../temperature-split.md), ventilate the dome, and observe when the [line of sight](../../../../../line-of-sight.md) crosses less turbulent air. Guiding removes [telescope](../../../../../telescope.md) drift, and a fast tip-tilt correction removes image motion. Slow [active optics](../../../../../active-optics.md) corrects [mirror](../../../../../mirror.md) figure and alignment; it cannot by itself follow all atmospheric phase fluctuations. These measures improve the input wavefront but do not guarantee diffraction-limited performance.

In [adaptive optics](../../../../../adaptive-optics.md), a [wavefront sensor](../../../../../wavefront-sensor.md) measures the reference-star wavefront, a reconstructor estimates its aberration, and a fast control loop commands a [deformable mirror](../../../../../deformable-mirror.md) and tip-tilt element. A Shack-Hartmann sensor obtains local slopes from the displacement of focused lenslet spots. The science beam receives the corresponding optical-path correction. Residual phase [variance](../../../../../variance-split.md) limits the [Strehl ratio](../../../../../strehl-ratio.md) approximately as $S\simeq e^{-\sigma_\phi^2}$ when the residual is small. Enough corrected spatial modes, adequate reference [photons](../../../../../photon.md) and a response faster than atmospheric changes are all needed. [infrared](../../../../../infrared.md) correction is easier because a given path error has smaller phase in radians and the atmospheric coherence length is larger. A faint or distant [guide star](../../../../../guide-star-adaptive-optics.md) leaves larger errors and a core-plus-halo [point spread function](../../../../../point-spread-function-split.md). The NACO manual documents these reference-star and anisoplanatic limitations.[§§3.1–3.2, printed p.13; §4.3, printed p.19: atmospheric coherence, real-time correction and anisoplanatism](../../../../../naco-user-manual-by-european-southern-observatory.md)

A [laser guide star](../../../../../laser-guide-star.md) supplies a wavefront reference in fields lacking a bright suitable star, but a finite-altitude beacon samples a cone rather than the full stellar cylinder. It also does not by itself supply the absolute celestial tip-tilt reference; a natural source is still required for that measurement. Multiple [guide stars](../../../../../guide-star-adaptive-optics.md) and conjugate correction planes can improve wider-field compensation, with greater hardware and reconstruction demands. The [point spread function](../../../../../point-spread-function-split.md) can vary across the field and with time, so reference stars and telemetry are needed to calibrate [astrometry](../../../../../astrometry.md) and [photometry](../../../../../photometry-optics.md); a sharper image alone is not a guarantee of accurate science.[Laser guide stars: artificial reference sources, limited sky coverage and retained natural-star requirements](../../../../../adaptive-optics-by-european-southern-observatory.md)

Short-exposure methods provide another route. Exposures shorter than the [atmospheric coherence time](../../../../../atmospheric-coherence-time.md) freeze [speckle patterns](../../../../../speckle-pattern.md). [speckle interferometry](../../../../../speckle-interferometry.md) uses calibrated Fourier statistics of many frames to recover diffraction-scale information; phase information requires methods such as [bispectrum](../../../../../bispectrum.md) reconstruction, not just averaging the blurred frames. [lucky imaging](../../../../../lucky-imaging.md) selects the best short frames, registers them and combines them. It sacrifices [photons](../../../../../photon.md) through selection and can be read-noise limited for faint targets, so it is most effective with enough reference [photons](../../../../../photon.md). Ordinary shift-and-add mainly corrects motion; it does not remove every high-order phase error. Deconvolution using a measured [point spread function](../../../../../point-spread-function-split.md) can recover supported structure but amplifies noise and cannot reliably invent [spatial frequencies](../../../../../spatial-frequency.md) absent from the observations. **Good-site engineering, fast wavefront correction and short-exposure reconstruction address different limitations and can be combined.**
