<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Type Ia supernovae are [standardizable candles](../../../../../standardizable-candle.md). Repeated multiband [photometry](../../../../../photometry-optics.md) measures peak [apparent magnitude](../../../../../apparent-magnitude.md), light-curve width and colour; spectra identify the supernova class and determine [redshift](../../../../../redshift.md), often from the host [galaxy](../../../../../galaxy-split.md). Nearby objects calibrate the relation between light-curve shape, colour and absolute [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md). Applying those relations produces a corrected [apparent magnitude](../../../../../apparent-magnitude.md) $m_{\rm corr}$, not an assumption that every event has identical [luminosity](../../../../../luminosity.md). The [distance modulus](../../../../../distance-modulus.md) then gives

$$
\mu=m_{\rm corr}-M=5\log_{10}\left(\frac{d_L}{\mathrm{Mpc}}\right)+25.
$$

A common uncertainty in $M$ is degenerate with the absolute distance scale, but does not erase the redshift-dependent shape of the [Hubble diagram](../../../../../hubble-diagram.md).

[luminosity distance](../../../../../luminosity-distance.md) probes the expansion history. In a spatially flat model,

$$
d_L(z)=(1+z)c\int_0^z\frac{dz'}{H(z')},\qquad d_L(z)=\frac c{H_0}\left[z+\frac{1-q_0}{2}z^2+O(z^3)\right],\qquad q_0=-\frac{a\ddot a}{\dot a^2}\bigg|_0.
$$

For fixed low-redshift [calibration](../../../../../calibration-differential-geometry.md), a negative $q_0$ increases the second-order distance relative to a decelerating model. Objects at a given [redshift](../../../../../redshift.md) are therefore fainter. A [Hubble diagram](../../../../../hubble-diagram.md) spanning enough [redshift](../../../../../redshift.md) can distinguish this curvature from the common magnitude intercept. In matter-plus-cosmological-constant models, $q_0=\Omega_m/2-\Omega_\Lambda$; fitting the distances can test whether $q_0<0$. The two original supernova teams found distances supporting an accelerating expansion in that framework.[abstract and cosmological interpretation of standardized supernova luminosity distances](../../../../../observational-evidence-from-supernovae-for-an-accelerating-universe-and-a-cosmological-constant-by-adam-riess-and-others.md)[abstract; cosmological likelihood constraints and tests of systematic uncertainty](../../../../../measurements-of-omega-and-lambda-from-42-high-redshift-supernovae-by-saul-perlmutter-and-others.md)

The comparison requires matched rest-frame passbands and [K corrections](../../../../../k-correction.md), correction for [redshift](../../../../../redshift.md) time dilation in the [light curves](../../../../../light-curve.md), a common [photometric calibration](../../../../../photometric-calibration.md), and explicit uncertainty propagation. Dust [extinction](../../../../../extinction.md) can imitate dimming; multicolour and [infrared](../../../../../infrared.md) observations test reddening and the adopted [extinction](../../../../../extinction.md) law. Evolution in the progenitor population or host-galaxy dependence can alter standardization, so one compares matched populations and fits host-dependent terms where warranted. Flux-limited discovery introduces [Malmquist bias](../../../../../malmquist-bias.md) and other selection effects, which must be modelled through the survey detection efficiency. [peculiar velocities](../../../../../peculiar-velocity.md) matter at low [redshift](../../../../../redshift.md), while [gravitational lensing](../../../../../gravitational-lensing-split.md) and intrinsic scatter broaden the distant sample. Classification and outlier tests reduce contamination by other transient types.

Independent data reductions, nearby anchors, spectroscopic checks and a fit over the full [redshift](../../../../../redshift.md) range distinguish an expansion-history signal from one instrumental offset. Complementary cosmological observations help separate matter [density](../../../../../density.md), curvature and dark-energy parameters. **The acceleration inference comes from the relative distance-redshift relation, conditional on standardization and an expansion model.** Distant supernovae integrate the past expansion history; they do not directly measure the instantaneous second time derivative of today's [scale factor](../../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md) without such modelling.
