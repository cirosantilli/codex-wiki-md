# Real spectral theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Operator theory](linear-operator-theory-split.md)

The real case of the [finite-dimensional spectral theorem](finite-dimensional-spectral-theorem.md) says that a [self-adjoint operator](self-adjoint-operator.md) on a finite-dimensional real [inner product space](inner-product-space.md) has a real [orthonormal eigenbasis](orthonormal-eigenbasis.md). In orthonormal coordinates this is the [spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices](spectral-theorem-for-real-symmetric-matrices.md), $A=Q\Lambda Q^T$. The worked [ellipse](ellipse.md) under [finite-dimensional spectral theorem](finite-dimensional-spectral-theorem.md) shows why both [orthogonal diagonalization](orthogonal-diagonalization-of-a-real-symmetric-matrix.md) and [orthogonality](orthogonal-vectors.md) matter: the new coordinates separate the [quadratic form](quadratic-form.md) into squares without distorting [lengths](length.md) or [angles](angle.md).

A real [normal matrix](normal-matrix.md) need not have real [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md). For example, the quarter-turn $\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ is an [orthogonal matrix](orthogonal-matrix.md), but its [characteristic polynomial](characteristic-polynomial.md) is $z^2+1$ and its [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) are $i,-i$. Over the [complex numbers](complex-number.md) it has an [orthonormal eigenbasis](orthonormal-eigenbasis.md); over the [real numbers](real-number.md) it has no [eigenvector](eigenvector.md). Self-adjointness supplies the reality conclusion in the real spectral theorem.
