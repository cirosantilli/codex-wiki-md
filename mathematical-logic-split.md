# Mathematical logic

↑ **Parent:** [Foundations of mathematics](foundations-of-mathematics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_logic)

Mathematical logic studies [formal languages](formal-language.md), [formal proofs](formal-proof.md), [model theory](model-theory-split.md), [computability theory](computability-theory-split.md), and the [foundations of mathematics](foundations-of-mathematics-split.md). It makes the rules of reasoning explicit enough that reasoning itself becomes an object of [mathematics](mathematics-split.md). [Proof theory](proof-theory.md) studies deductions; [model theory](model-theory-split.md) studies interpretations in which statements hold.

Three questions organize the subject: is an expression a well-formed [first-order formula](first-order-formula.md), is it true in a specified [first-order structure](first-order-structure.md), and does it have a [formal proof](formal-proof.md) from specified [axioms](axiom.md)? These questions are distinct. For example, $\exists n\,(n+n=1)$ is a [first-order sentence](first-order-sentence.md) in an appropriate [first-order language](first-order-language.md); it is false in the [natural numbers](natural-number.md) but true in the [rational numbers](rational-number.md), with $n=1/2$.

The beauty of [mathematical logic](mathematical-logic-split.md) is that the same [logical connectives](logical-connective.md) and rules of [universal quantification](universal-quantification.md) and [existential quantification](existential-quantification.md) organize arguments across [mathematics](mathematics-split.md). [Classical propositional logic](classical-propositional-logic.md) uses two truth values; [Intuitionistic propositional logic](intuitionistic-propositional-logic.md) interprets [logical implication](logical-implication.md) through constructions on proofs, without unrestricted [law of excluded middle](law-of-excluded-middle.md). This proof-based framework is developed in [Frank Pfenning, Natural Deduction, sections 1–3](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~fp/courses/15317-s23/lectures/02-natded.pdf).
