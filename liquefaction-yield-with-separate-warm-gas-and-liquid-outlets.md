# Liquefaction yield with separate warm-gas and liquid outlets

↑ **Parent:** [Liquefaction enthalpy budget](liquefaction-enthalpy-budget.md)

Apply the [steady-flow energy balance](steady-flow-energy-balance.md) to an entire [liquefaction](liquefaction-of-gases.md) apparatus, allowing a warm [gas](gas.md) outlet and a separate cold [liquid](liquid.md) outlet. With incoming [heat](heat-split.md) $q$ per unit feed [mass](mass.md), no shaft [work](work.md), and negligible changes in bulk [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and gravitational [potential energy](potential-energy.md), [conservation of mass](mass-conservation.md) and [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) give $h_{\rm in}+q=yh_l+(1-y)h_g$. Solving this equation gives the displayed liquid [mass fraction](mass-fraction.md).

The two outlet streams need not share a [temperature](temperature-split.md). For example, a [counterflow heat exchanger](counterflow-heat-exchanger.md) can warm returning [gas](gas.md) before it crosses the overall [control volume](control-volume.md) boundary, while collected [liquid](liquid.md) leaves at the cold end. When the outlet temperatures differ, $h_g-h_l$ includes sensible heating as well as phase change; it is not simply a [latent heat](latent-heat.md). A colder two-phase separator nested inside the apparatus has its own boundary states and a different local enthalpy balance.
