# Recoil-free nuclear transition

↑ **Parent:** [Mössbauer effect](mossbauer-effect-split.md)

A [recoil-free nuclear transition](recoil-free-nuclear-transition.md) emits or absorbs a [gamma ray](gamma-ray.md) while leaving the internal vibrational state of the surrounding [solid](solid.md) unchanged. It belongs to the elastic part of the process: no [phonon](phonon.md) is created or destroyed. The [photon](photon.md) still carries [momentum](momentum-split.md) $p=E_\gamma/c$, where $c$ is the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). The emitting [solid](solid.md) takes momentum $-\mathbf p$; the absorbing [solid](solid.md) takes $+\mathbf p$. [Conservation of momentum](momentum-conservation.md) holds for each event.

Why does an isolated [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md) pose a problem? Start with an [excited state](excited-state.md) at rest and let $E_0$ be its transition [energy](energy-split.md), with nuclear [mass](mass.md) $M$ and $E_0\ll Mc^2$. [Conservation of momentum](momentum-conservation.md) gives a recoil momentum of magnitude $p$; its nonrelativistic [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) is therefore

$$
E_R=\frac{p^2}{2M}=\frac{E_\gamma^2}{2Mc^2}\simeq\frac{E_0^2}{2Mc^2}.
$$

By [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md), emission spends part of $E_0$ on [nuclear recoil](nuclear-recoil.md), whereas absorption must supply both $E_0$ and the final [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md). To leading order,

$$
E_{\mathrm{em}}\simeq E_0-E_R,\qquad E_{\mathrm{abs}}\simeq E_0+E_R,\qquad E_{\mathrm{abs}}-E_{\mathrm{em}}\simeq2E_R.
$$

Thus the relevant comparison is $2E_R$ against the overlapping [spectral line](spectral-line.md) widths, rather than $E_R/E_0$ alone. If $2E_R$ greatly exceeds the widths, the isolated-nucleus lines have negligible overlap. Finite [Lorentzian line shape](lorentzian-line-shape.md) tails mean that this is suppression, rather than an absolute prohibition on absorption.

For a bound [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md), the recoil-free channel transfers [momentum](momentum-split.md) to the [center of mass](center-of-mass.md) of the emitting or absorbing [solid](solid.md), with negligible [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) $p^2/(2M_{\mathrm{solid}})$. Other channels exchange [phonons](phonon.md) and carry substantial [energy](energy-split.md) into or out of the internal vibrations. A large $M_{\mathrm{solid}}$ makes the elastic channel's [energy](energy-split.md) cost small; the vibrational [quantum state](quantum-state.md) determines its probability. Merely replacing $M$ by a large [mass](mass.md) in the free-particle formula does not calculate that probability.

Here is a deliberately stipulated teaching system, independent of historical isotope data: $E_0=20\,\mathrm{keV}$, $Mc^2=10^{11}\,\mathrm{eV}$, and a single-line [natural linewidth](natural-linewidth.md) $\Gamma_E=10^{-7}\,\mathrm{eV}$ measured as an [energy](energy-split.md) [full width at half maximum](full-width-at-half-maximum.md). These give $E_R=0.002\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $2E_R/\Gamma_E=40{,}000$. A [solid](solid.md) of $10^{12}$ such nuclear [masses](mass.md) has elastic recoil [energy](energy-split.md) $2\times10^{-15}\,\mathrm{eV}$, far below $\Gamma_E$. In this article $\Gamma_E$ is an [energy](energy-split.md) width; the corresponding lifetime is $\tau=\hbar/\Gamma_E$, with $\hbar$ the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md) divided by $2\pi$.
