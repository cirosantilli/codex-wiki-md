<h1 id="d/d3/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Substitute $t=\log x$, with $dx=e^t\,dt$. The nonnegative integral becomes

$$
\int_0^\infty t e^{-(\alpha-1)t}\,dt=\frac1{(\alpha-1)^2},
$$

by scaling and [integration by parts](../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md). The exponential decays because $\alpha>1$, with zero boundary term at infinity, proving integrability and

$$
\boxed{\int_1^\infty x^{-\alpha}\log x\,dx=(\alpha-1)^{-2}}.
$$
