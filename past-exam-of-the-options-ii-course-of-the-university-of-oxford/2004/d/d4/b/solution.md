**False in both cases.** Without the zero-at-zero condition, $g=0$ and $f\equiv1$ already give an integrable input and a non-integrable composition on $\mathbb R$.

Even imposing $f(0)=0$ does not control how fast $f(t)$ approaches zero. Take $g(x)=(1+x^2)^{-1}$ and the [continuous function](../../../../../continuous-function.md) $f(t)=\sqrt{|t|}$. The input has integral $\pi$, but

$$
(f\circ g)(x)=\frac1{\sqrt{1+x^2}}
$$

has a divergent integral, because its tails are comparable to $1/|x|$. Thus [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) at zero is weaker than the linear growth estimate that would preserve [integrability](../../../../../integrability.md).
