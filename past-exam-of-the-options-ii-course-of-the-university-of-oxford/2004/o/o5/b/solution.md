[Maximum likelihood estimation](../../../../../maximum-likelihood-estimation-split.md) treats the observations as fixed and chooses admissible parameter values maximizing their joint [likelihood function](../../../../../likelihood-function.md), or equivalently its logarithm. Interior score equations identify candidates, but parameter boundaries and global maximality still need checking; an unattained supremum is not an estimate.

The [statistical method of moments](../../../../../method-of-moments-statistics.md) equates enough empirical moments to the corresponding theoretical moments to solve for the unknown parameters. For example, for a one-parameter exponential rate, the relation $\mathbb EX=1/\lambda$ gives the moment estimate $1/\bar X$; maximizing $\lambda^ne^{-\lambda\sum X_i}$ gives the same estimate. Agreement of these two methods in this example does not imply that they agree in every model, nor that either is unbiased.
