<h1 id="b/b2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put $\alpha=\sqrt[3]2$ and $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/3}$. The three roots are $\alpha,\alpha\zeta,\alpha\zeta^2$, so the [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md) is $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\zeta)$. [Eisenstein's criterion](../../../../../eisenstein-criterion.md) at two gives $[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=3$. This intermediate [field](../../../../../field.md) is real, while $\zeta$ is not real, and $\zeta$ satisfies $x^2+x+1=0$. Its degree over the intermediate [field](../../../../../field.md) is therefore exactly two. By the tower law, $\boxed{[K:\mathbb Q]=6}$.
