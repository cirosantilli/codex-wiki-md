<h1 id="c/c2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $p=F_m$ be [prime](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md) with $m\geq1$. The preceding parts give $(3/p)=-1$, so [Euler's criterion](../../../../../../euler-s-criterion.md) implies $3^{(p-1)/2}\equiv-1\pmod p$. The [multiplicative order](../../../../../../multiplicative-order.md) $d$ of three divides $p-1=2^{2^m}$. Every proper divisor of this power of two divides $(p-1)/2$. If $d$ were proper, that half-power would be one, a contradiction. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}_p(3)=p-1,}
$$

which means precisely that three is a [primitive root](../../../../../../primitive-root-modulo-n.md) modulo $p$.
