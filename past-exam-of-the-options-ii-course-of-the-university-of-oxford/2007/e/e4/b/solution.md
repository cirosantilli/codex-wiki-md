<h1 id="e/e4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The surface is a **[Klein bottle](../../../../../klein-bottle.md)**. Here is a cell-count justification that keeps all the arrow directions. Name the corners of the first square $a,b,c,d$, beginning at its upper left and proceeding clockwise; name those of the second $e,f,g,h$ in the same way. The single-arrow gluing identifies $a$ with $e$ and $b$ with $h$. The triple-arrow gluing identifies $d$ with $h$ and $c$ with $g$. The first square's quadruple-arrow gluing identifies $b$ with $d$ and $c$ with $a$. The second square's double-arrow gluing identifies $e,f,g$ with one another. There are exactly two resulting vertex classes:

$$
\{a,c,e,f,g\},\qquad\{b,d,h\}.
$$

There are four edge classes and two faces, so the [Euler characteristic](../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) is $2-4+2=0$. Every edge is paired and the corner links close into circles, giving a [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) surface without boundary; the cross-square identifications make it [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md). It is nonorientable: the quadruple-arrow identification glues two boundary edges of the same square in the same boundary-traversal direction, so no [orientation](../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md) of that face can extend across the gluing. The classification of [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) surfaces now identifies the nonorientable surface of [Euler characteristic](../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) zero as the [Klein bottle](../../../../../klein-bottle.md).

Geometrically, the internal identification in each square produces a [Möbius band](../../../../../mobius-band.md). The remaining single- and triple-arrow edges comprise their two boundary circles, which are glued together. The double of a [Möbius band](../../../../../mobius-band.md) is another description of the same [Klein bottle](../../../../../klein-bottle.md).
