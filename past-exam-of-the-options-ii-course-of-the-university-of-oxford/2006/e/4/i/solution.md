<h1 id="e/4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [quotient map](../../../../../quotient-map.md) $\pi:X\to Y$ is a [surjection](../../../../../surjective-function.md) such that $U\subseteq Y$ is open if and only if $\pi^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X$. Thus the topology on $Y$ is exactly the final topology induced by $\pi$.

For an open continuous [surjection](../../../../../surjective-function.md), [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) gives one implication. For the other, if $\pi^{-1}(U)$ is open then $U=\pi(\pi^{-1}(U))$ is open by openness and [surjectivity](../../../../../surjective-function.md). Hence it is a [quotient map](../../../../../quotient-map.md).

If $\pi:X\to Y$ and $\sigma:Y\to Z$ are [quotient maps](../../../../../quotient-map.md), they are [surjective](../../../../../surjective-function.md) and

$$
(\sigma\circ\pi)^{-1}(U)=\pi^{-1}(\sigma^{-1}(U)).
$$

This set is open exactly when $\sigma^{-1}(U)$ is open, exactly when $U$ is open. Thus **the composition is a [quotient map](../../../../../quotient-map.md)**.

For the factorization, [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) of $g$ implies [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) of $f=g\circ\pi$. Conversely, if $f$ is continuous and $U$ is open in $Z$, then $\pi^{-1}(g^{-1}(U))=f^{-1}(U)$ is open. The [quotient](../../../../../quotient.md) property makes $g^{-1}(U)$ open. This holds for every open $U$, so **$g$ is continuous**.
