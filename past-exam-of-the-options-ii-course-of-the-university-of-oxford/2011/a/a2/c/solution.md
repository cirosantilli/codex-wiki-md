<h1 id="a/a2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The roots are $\pm\sqrt2$ and $\pm\sqrt3$, so the [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md) is $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$. The first adjunction has [degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-field-extension.md) $2$. To see that the second does too, suppose $\sqrt3=u+v\sqrt2$ with $u,v\in\mathbb Q$. Squaring and comparing rational and irrational parts gives $uv=0$ and $u^2+2v^2=3$. If $v=0$, then $u^2=3$; if $u=0$, then $v^2=3/2$. Neither is a rational square, as follows by comparing prime exponents in numerator and denominator. Thus $\sqrt3\notin\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$. The [tower law](../../../../../tower-law.md) now yields

$$
\boxed{[K:\mathbb Q]=4,\qquad K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3).}
$$

A [basis](../../../../../basis.md) is $1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt6$.
