<h1 id="b/b2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Suppose

$$
n=p_1\cdots p_r=q_1\cdots q_s
$$

are two factorizations into primes. Repeatedly apply the cyclic-[subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) construction of part (c), first removing $p_r$, then $p_{r-1}$, and so on. This constructs a [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) for $C_n$ whose [composition factors](../../../../../composition-factor.md) are $C_{p_1},\ldots,C_{p_r}$. The second factorization constructs another [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) with [composition factors](../../../../../composition-factor.md) $C_{q_1},\ldots,C_{q_s}$.

By the [Jordan–Hölder theorem](../../../../../jordan-holder-theorem.md), $r=s$ and these [composition factors](../../../../../composition-factor.md) agree up to [group isomorphism](../../../../../group-isomorphism.md) and permutation. Since isomorphic [finite groups](../../../../../finite-group.md) have the same order, the corresponding primes agree. This proves **uniqueness of [prime factorization](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md) up to permutation**, using existence of prime factorizations but not their uniqueness.
