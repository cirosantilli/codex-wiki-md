A [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) is a finite chain

$$
1=G_0\triangleleft G_1\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft G_r=G
$$

whose inclusions are strict and whose [quotients](../../../../../quotient-group.md) $G_i/G_{i-1}$ are [simple](../../../../../simple-group.md). These [quotients](../../../../../quotient-group.md) are the [composition factors](../../../../../composition-factor.md). The [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) condition is relative to the next member, rather than necessarily to all of $G$.

Induct on the order of a [finite group](../../../../../finite-group.md). The trivial [group](../../../../../group-split.md) has the empty [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md). Otherwise choose a [maximal normal subgroup](../../../../../maximal-normal-subgroup.md) $N\lneq G$, which exists because there are finitely many subgroups. The [subgroup correspondence theorem](../../../../../subgroup-correspondence-theorem.md) identifies [normal subgroups](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $G/N$ with [normal subgroups](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $G$ containing $N$, so maximality makes $G/N$ a [simple group](../../../../../simple-group.md). By induction $N$ has a [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md); appending $G$ gives one for $G$.

For example, the nonabelian [symmetric group](../../../../../symmetric-group.md) $S_3$ has

$$
1\triangleleft A_3\triangleleft S_3,
$$

with [composition factors](../../../../../composition-factor.md) $C_3$ and $C_2$, both [abelian](../../../../../abelian-group.md).
