<h1 id="m/m4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the [characteristic function](../../../../../../characteristic-function-split.md) to establish [convergence in distribution](../../../../../../convergence-in-distribution.md) by the [Lévy continuity theorem](../../../../../../levy-continuity-theorem.md), especially when exponential moments do not exist; a [Cauchy distribution](../../../../../../cauchy-distribution.md), for example, has a [characteristic function](../../../../../../characteristic-function-split.md) but no finite [moment-generating function](../../../../../../moment-generating-function.md) on a neighborhood of zero.

Use the [moment-generating function](../../../../../../moment-generating-function.md) for an exponential-tail estimate: for $t>0$ with $M_X(t)<\infty$, [Markov inequality](../../../../../../markov-s-inequality.md) gives

$$
P(X\geq x)\leq e^{-tx}M_X(t).
$$

Optimizing over $t$ produces a [Chernoff bound](../../../../../../chernoff-bound.md). The modulus-one [characteristic function](../../../../../../characteristic-function-split.md) does not directly give this positive exponential bound.
