<h1 id="c/c2/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Every [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) square remains a square modulo every [prime number](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md), including the zero class at its prime divisors. Conversely, let $a$ be an odd [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) which is not an [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) square. If $a$ is the negative of a square, part (i) supplies an odd $k$ with $(a/k)=-1$. In every other case, the [prime factorization](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md) of $|a|$ has an odd exponent $t$ at some odd prime $q$. Write $a=\pm q^t b$ with $b>0$ odd and $\gcd(q,b)=1$, and apply part (ii).

In either case, factor the resulting $k$ into primes. Since its [Jacobi symbol](../../../../../../jacobi-symbol.md) is a product of [Legendre symbols](../../../../../../legendre-symbol.md) and equals $-1$, at least one prime factor $p$ has $(a/p)=-1$. Thus $a$ is not a square modulo that prime. This proves **an odd [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) is an [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) square exactly when it is a square modulo every prime**. If the phrase [quadratic residue](../../../../../../quadratic-residue-split.md) is restricted to nonzero residue classes, the final statement must instead say “a square modulo every prime”; for example, $9$ reduces to zero modulo $3$.
