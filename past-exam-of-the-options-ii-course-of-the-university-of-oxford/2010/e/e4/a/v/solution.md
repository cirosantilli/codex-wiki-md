<h1 id="e/e4/a/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

For any topological target $Y$, **$f(K)$ is compact**. Pull an open cover of $f(K)$ back by $f$, take a finite subcover of $K$, and map the covering statement forward. Without a separation assumption on $Y$, the image need not be closed, as a constant map into a nonclosed singleton of an indiscrete space shows.

If $Y$ is a [metric space](../../../../../../metric-space.md), $f(K)$ is also closed in $Y$, bounded, [totally bounded](../../../../../../totally-bounded-space.md), complete with its inherited metric, and sequentially compact. Closedness uses the Hausdorff theorem from part (iii); total boundedness follows by taking finite subcovers of the covers by balls of any fixed radius. Compact metric spaces are sequentially compact, and a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) with a convergent subsequence converges to the same limit, proving [completeness](../../../../../../completeness.md). These properties are asserted for the image subset, without requiring the whole ambient [metric space](../../../../../../metric-space.md) to be complete.
