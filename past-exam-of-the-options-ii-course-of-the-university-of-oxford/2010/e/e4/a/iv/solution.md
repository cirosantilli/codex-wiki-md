↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

By the [Heine-Borel theorem](../../../../../../heine-borel-theorem.md), a subset of $\mathbb R$ is compact exactly when it is closed and bounded. Consequently the compact [intervals](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) are

$$
\boxed{[u,v]\quad\text{with finite }u\leq v,}
$$

including singleton [intervals](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md). The empty [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) is compact too, if included in the [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) convention. Nonempty open, proper half-open, and unbounded [intervals](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) are not compact.
