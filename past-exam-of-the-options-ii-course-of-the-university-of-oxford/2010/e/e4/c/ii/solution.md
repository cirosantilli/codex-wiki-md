↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Yes.** [Continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) and nonvanishing on the compact [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) give constants

$$
0<m=\min_{[0,1]}|\alpha|\leq\max_{[0,1]}|\alpha|=M<\infty.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{m\|f-g\|_\infty\leq d_\alpha(f,g)\leq M\|f-g\|_\infty.}
$$

A $d_\alpha$-[Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) is therefore supremum-[norm](../../../../../../norm.md) Cauchy and has a continuous uniform limit $f$ by part (i). The upper bound then makes it converge to $f$ also in $d_\alpha$. Equivalently, multiplication by $\alpha$ is an isometry from this weighted space onto ordinary $C([0,1])$, since division by the continuous nonvanishing $\alpha$ gives its inverse.

This is [completeness of a continuous weighted uniform norm](../../../../../../completeness-of-a-continuous-weighted-uniform-norm.md).
