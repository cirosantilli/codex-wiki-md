↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**Yes.** A [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) in the supremum [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) converges in the given complete space $B([0,1])$ to a bounded function $f$. Supremum-[norm](../../../../../../norm.md) convergence is [uniform convergence](../../../../../../uniform-convergence.md). The uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous: for a point $x_0$ and tolerance $\epsilon$, choose a continuous $f_n$ within $\epsilon/3$ of $f$ uniformly, then use its [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at $x_0$ and the [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md). Hence $f\in C([0,1])$. Thus $C([0,1])$ is a closed, complete subspace of $B([0,1])$.
