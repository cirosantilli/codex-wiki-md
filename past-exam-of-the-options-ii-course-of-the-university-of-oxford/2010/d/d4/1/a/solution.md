<h1 id="d/d4/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For every [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) $j\geq0$, the $j$th derivative with respect to $t$ of $\cos(t\cos x)$ is $(\cos x)^j$ times a sine or cosine with a sign. Its absolute value is at most $1$ on the finite [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) $[0,\pi/2]$. This is an integrable dominating function independent of $t$. Repeated [differentiation under the integral sign](../../../../../../differentiation-under-the-integral-sign.md) is therefore justified for every order, giving **$J_0\in C^\infty(\mathbb R)$**.
