<h1 id="p/p4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume the usual nondegenerate [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) $a<b$. The integral is bilinear and symmetric because the functions and weight are real. It is nonnegative on $(f,f)$. If $f$ is nonzero at a point, [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) makes $|f|$ bounded below by a positive number on a nontrivial relative [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) nearby, and the positive continuous weight is also bounded below there. Hence $\int_a^bf^2w>0$. Thus the formula is positive definite and defines an [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md).

The functions are orthogonal precisely when

$$
\boxed{\langle f,g\rangle=\int_a^bf(x)g(x)w(x)\,dx=0.}
$$

A singleton [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) would make this integral zero for every function, so the condition $a<b$ is necessary.
