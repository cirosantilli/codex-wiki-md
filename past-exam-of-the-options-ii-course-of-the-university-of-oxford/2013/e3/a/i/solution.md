<h1 id="e3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If $A$ is closed and $a_n\in A$ converges to $x\in X$, then $x$ cannot lie in the open complement: an open ball about such an $x$ would eventually contain all $a_n$ while missing $A$. Thus $x\in A$.

Conversely, suppose $A$ contains every limit of a [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) in $A$. If $x\in\overline A$, every ball $B(x,1/n)$ meets $A$; choose $a_n$ in that intersection. Then $a_n\to x$, so $x\in A$. Hence $\overline A=A$, proving that **a subset of a [metric space](../../../../../metric-space.md) is a [closed set](../../../../../closed-set.md) exactly when it is sequentially closed**.
