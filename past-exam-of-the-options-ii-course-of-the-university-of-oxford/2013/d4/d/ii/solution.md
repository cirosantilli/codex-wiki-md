<h1 id="d4/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $N$ be the [vector](../../../../../vector.md) subspace of integrable [functions](../../../../../function-split.md) that vanish [almost everywhere](../../../../../almost-everywhere.md). The [Lebesgue space](../../../../../lp-space.md) is the [quotient vector space](../../../../../quotient-vector-space.md) $L^1(\mathbb R)=\mathcal L^1(\mathbb R)/N$: its elements are equivalence classes of [functions](../../../../../function-split.md) equal [almost everywhere](../../../../../almost-everywhere.md). Addition and scalar multiplication are $[f]+[g]=[f+g]$ and $c[f]=[cf]$.

Set $\|[f]\|_1=\int|f|$. This is well-defined because replacing $f$ by an equal-almost-everywhere representative does not change the [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md). It is nonnegative, homogeneous, and obeys the triangle inequality by $|f+g|\leq|f|+|g|$. Part (i) says its only zero element is the zero class. Hence **it is a [norm](../../../../../norm.md) on $L^1$**.
