<h1 id="d3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $x>0$, the [geometric series](../../../../geometric-series.md) gives

$$
\frac{xe^{-\alpha x}}{1-e^{-x}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}xe^{-(n+\alpha)x}.
$$

Every summand is nonnegative and has [integral](../../../../integral-split.md) $(n+\alpha)^{-2}$ by part (a). This numerical series converges by comparison with $\sum_{n\geq1}n^{-2}$. The series-integration rule proved above therefore yields

$$
\boxed{\int_0^\infty\frac{xe^{-\alpha x}}{1-e^{-x}}\,dx
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac1{(n+\alpha)^2}<\infty.}
$$

The value at the single endpoint zero does not affect this Lebesgue [integral](../../../../integral-split.md).
