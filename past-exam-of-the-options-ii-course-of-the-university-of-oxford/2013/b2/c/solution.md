<h1 id="b2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Two [group actions](../../../../group-action-split.md) on $S_1,S_2$ are equivalent if there is a [bijection](../../../../bijection.md) $\phi:S_1\to S_2$ such that $\phi(gs)=g\phi(s)$ for all $g,s$. Such a bijection is [equivariant](../../../../equivariant-map.md).

The [orbit-stabilizer theorem](../../../../orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md) in this form says that the action on $O_s$ is equivalent to the left [coset](../../../../coset.md) action on $G/G_s$, by $gG_s\mapsto gs$. This map is well-defined and injective because $gs=hs$ exactly when $h^{-1}g\in G_s$, and it is onto by the definition of the [orbit](../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md). It is equivariant by multiplication.

Thus every transitive action is a [coset](../../../../coset.md) action. Two such actions $G/H$ and $G/K$ are equivalent exactly when $H,K$ are conjugate: [stabilizers](../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) at different points of one [orbit](../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) are conjugate, and a conjugacy explicitly supplies a coset-action equivalence. This last classification is what makes counting by [orbit](../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) types possible.
