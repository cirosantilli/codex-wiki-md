<h1 id="e/e4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $X$ is [connected](../../../../../../connected-space.md), its [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) quotient image is [connected](../../../../../../connected-space.md) by part (a). Conversely, suppose $X$ is disconnected. It has a nonempty proper subset $A$ both open and closed. Part (i) makes $A$ and its complement [saturated](../../../../../../saturation-of-a-subset.md), so $q^{-1}(q(A))=A$ and $q^{-1}(q(X\setminus A))=X\setminus A$. The [quotient topology](../../../../../../quotient-topology.md) therefore makes these disjoint nonempty images open, and they cover $X/R$. Thus the quotient is disconnected. This proves **the two spaces are [connected](../../../../../../connected-space.md) simultaneously**.
