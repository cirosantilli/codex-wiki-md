<h1 id="e/e3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Nonnegativity and symmetry follow from $d$, and $d_a(x,y)=0$ with $x\ne y$ would force both $x=a$ and $y=a$, a contradiction. For the [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md), when the intermediate point $z$ is different from both endpoints,

$$
d_a(x,z)+d_a(z,y)=d(x,a)+2d(z,a)+d(a,y)\geq d_a(x,y).
$$

If $z$ equals an endpoint the inequality is immediate, and if $x=y$ its left target is zero. These cover every case, so **$d_a$ is a [metric](../../../../../../metric.md)**.

This is the [star metric centered at a point](../../../../../../star-metric-centered-at-a-point.md).
