<h1 id="b/b2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Choose an inscribed regular [tetrahedron](../../../../../tetrahedron.md) from one of the regular compounds of five tetrahedra in the [dodecahedron](../../../../../dodecahedron.md), and let $H$ be its rotational stabilizer. Its twelve tetrahedral rotations preserve the [dodecahedron](../../../../../dodecahedron.md). Concretely, with $\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, use [dodecahedron](../../../../../dodecahedron.md) vertices $(\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)$ together with cyclic coordinate placements of $(0,\pm\phi^{-1},\pm\phi)$. The [tetrahedron](../../../../../tetrahedron.md) has the four cube vertices whose coordinate product is $+1$. Cyclic coordinate permutations and even numbers of coordinate sign changes preserve both vertex sets, have determinant $+1$, and give twelve distinct rotations. No other rotation can preserve this [tetrahedron](../../../../../tetrahedron.md), because a regular [tetrahedron](../../../../../tetrahedron.md) has only twelve orientation-preserving symmetries.

Their action on its four vertices is faithful and consists exactly of the even permutations: the eight rotations through $\pm120^\circ$ induce three-cycles, the three half-turns induce double transpositions, and there is the identity. Thus **$H\cong A_4$**, independently of part (ii).

Finally a nonidentity element of $H$ has order two or three. In the [dodecahedron](../../../../../dodecahedron.md)'s full rotation group, all fifteen half-turns about opposite-edge axes form one [conjugacy class](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md), and all twenty third-turns about opposite-vertex axes form one [conjugacy class](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md). These geometric conjugacies follow by carrying the corresponding oriented axes to one another by rotations. A [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) containing such an element must contain its whole class, which already has more than twelve elements. Hence no nonidentity [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $G$ is contained in $H$, verifying the required extra property.
