<h1 id="b/b2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $G$ act by left multiplication on its five left cosets of $H$. This gives a [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) $\varphi:G\to S_5$. Its [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) is the [core of a subgroup](../../../../../core-group-theory.md), $\bigcap_{g\in G}gHg^{-1}$: an element fixes every coset precisely when all its conjugates lie in $H$. It is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) contained in $H$, so the hypothesis forces the [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) to be trivial. Thus $G$ embeds in $S_5$.

The image has order $60$ and index two, hence is the [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) of a surjection $S_5\to C_2$. All transpositions are conjugate and generate $S_5$, so under any such surjection they must all have the same nonidentity image. This determines the map uniquely as the sign [homomorphism](../../../../../homomorphism.md). Its [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) is $A_5$, proving $G\cong A_5$.

To prove simplicity directly, the nonidentity [conjugacy classes](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) in $A_5$ have sizes $15,20,12,12$. A double transposition has centralizer of order four in $A_5$; a three-cycle has centralizer of order three; a five-cycle has centralizer of order five, yielding the two classes of size twelve. The class sizes together with the identity account for all sixty elements. A [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) must be a [union](../../../../../set-union.md) of these classes containing the identity, and its order must divide sixty. None of the proper nontrivial sums of these sizes with one is a divisor of sixty. Therefore **$A_5$, and hence $G$, is simple**.
