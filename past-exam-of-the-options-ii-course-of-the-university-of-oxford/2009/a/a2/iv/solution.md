<h1 id="a/a2/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The [multiplicative group](../../../../../multiplicative-group-of-a-field.md) $K^\times$ has order $p^n-1$, so [Lagrange's theorem for finite groups](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) gives $x^{p^n-1}=1$ for $x\ne0$. Including zero, $x^{p^n}=x$ for every $x\in K$. Therefore $\vartheta^n$ is the identity. No positive smaller power is the identity by part (iii), so

$$
\boxed{|\vartheta|=n}.
$$

For $n=1$ this says that Frobenius itself is the identity, as it should be.
