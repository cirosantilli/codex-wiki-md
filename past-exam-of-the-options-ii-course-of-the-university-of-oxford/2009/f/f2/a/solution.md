<h1 id="f/f2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The $i$th [partial derivative](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) is $\partial_if(x_0)=\lim_{t\to0}[f(x_0+te_i)-f(x_0)]/t$. The [directional derivative](../../../../../directional-derivative.md) in direction $v$ is the same limit with $v$ in place of $e_i$, using the specified vector rather than normalizing it. [Differentiability](../../../../../differentiability.md) means that there is a linear map $L:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ with

$$
f(x_0+h)=f(x_0)+Lh+o(\|h\|).
$$

For $v=(v_1,v_2)$ with $v_1\ne0$, the difference quotient at the origin is $t v_2^3/v_1$, tending to zero. If $v_1=0$, the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) is identically zero on the directional line, again giving derivative zero. Thus **every [directional derivative](../../../../../directional-derivative.md) exists and is zero**.

Along the curved path $(x,y)=(t^3,t)$, however, $f(t^3,t)=1$ for $t\ne0$, while $f(0,0)=0$. Hence the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) is **not [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md)** at the origin. [Differentiability](../../../../../differentiability.md) implies [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md), so it is **not differentiable** there either. Direction-by-direction limits alone do not control general approaches to a point.
