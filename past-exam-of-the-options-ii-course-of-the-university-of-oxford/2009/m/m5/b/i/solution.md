<h1 id="m/m5/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Every finite upward path has positive probability, since each upward transition probability is positive. Every state $i\geq1$ has a positive transition probability to zero because $\lambda/(i+1)<1$. From zero one can therefore reach every state, and from any state return to zero and then reach any other. Hence the chain is [irreducible](../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md).

If $\lambda<1$, state zero has a positive self-loop, so its period is one. If $\lambda=1$, that self-loop disappears, but the paths $0\to1\to0$ and $0\to1\to2\to0$ have positive probabilities and lengths two and three. Their greatest common divisor is one. Thus zero has period one in this boundary case too; irreducibility makes the whole chain **[aperiodic](../../../../../../aperiodic-markov-chain.md)**.
