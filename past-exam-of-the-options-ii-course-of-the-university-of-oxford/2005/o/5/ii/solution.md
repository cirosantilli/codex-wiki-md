<h1 id="o/5/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the positive-direction alternative, use the same paired statistic $t_{\rm obs}=2.607/(4.713/\sqrt{15})$, but reject at level $\delta$ only when $t_{\rm obs}>t_{14,1-\delta}$. Its one-sided $p$-value is

$$
\boxed{p_+=1-F_{t_{14}}(t_{\rm obs}).}
$$

Because the observed mean difference is positive and the null Student [probability distribution](../../../../../probability-distribution-split.md) is symmetric, the two-sided $p$-value is twice this upper-tail [probability](../../../../../probability.md), consistent with the supplied $0.05$ and $0.025$.

The two-sided value is the null-model [probability](../../../../../probability.md) of a [test statistic](../../../../../test-statistic.md) at least as far from zero in either direction as the observation. The one-sided value uses only the direction of larger cross-fertilized heights. They are probabilities under the assumed null [sampling distribution](../../../../../sampling-distribution.md), not posterior probabilities that the [null hypothesis](../../../../../null-hypothesis.md) is true.

At the reported precision, the two-sided result is on the conventional 5 percent boundary; rounding can matter for a strict numerical threshold. The one-sided result is significant at5 percent and [supports](../../../../../support.md) a positive mean difference if that directional alternative was chosen in advance. Choosing the direction after observing the sign would not justify using the smaller one-sided [probability](../../../../../probability.md) as a prespecified test. The paired design controls shared pot conditions, while the inference still relies on the [independence](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) and [probability distribution](../../../../../probability-distribution-split.md) assumptions for the differences.
