<h1 id="b/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Map the [integers](../../../../integer-split.md) into a [field](../../../../field.md) by $n\mapsto n1$. Its [kernel](../../../../kernel-algebra.md) is a [prime ideal](../../../../prime-ideal.md) of $\mathbb Z$, since the [image](../../../../image-of-a-function.md) has no [zero divisors](../../../../zero-divisor.md). Thus the [kernel](../../../../kernel-algebra.md) is either zero or $(p)$ for a [prime](../../../../prime-number-split.md) $p$. In the zero case, the map extends injectively to the [rational numbers](../../../../rational-number.md) by taking quotients of nonzero [integers](../../../../integer-split.md). In the other case, its [image](../../../../image-of-a-function.md) is the [field](../../../../field.md) $\mathbb F_p$. This proves the prime-field assertion.

A [finite field](../../../../finite-field.md) cannot contain the infinite rational [field](../../../../field.md), so it has [characteristic](../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) $p$. It is a [finite-dimensional vector space](../../../../finite-dimensional-vector-space.md) over its [prime field](../../../../prime-field.md) $\mathbb F_p$, say of [dimension](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) $n\ge1$, and choosing a [basis](../../../../basis.md) counts its elements as

$$
\boxed{|F|=p^n.}
$$

For existence, put $q=p^n$ and take a [splitting field](../../../../splitting-field.md) of $x^q-x$ over $\mathbb F_p$. Its [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) is $-1$, so it has exactly $q$ distinct [polynomial roots](../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md). The [polynomial roots](../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) are closed under [addition](../../../../addition.md) and [multiplication](../../../../multiplication.md) because the Frobenius identity gives $(u+v)^q=u^q+v^q$ and $(uv)^q=u^qv^q$. They include $0,1$, are closed under [additive inverses](../../../../additive-inverse.md), and for a nonzero [polynomial root](../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) $u$, $(u^{-1})^q=(u^q)^{-1}=u^{-1}$. They therefore form a [field](../../../../field.md) of exactly $q$ elements. Indeed this [field](../../../../field.md) is the [splitting field](../../../../splitting-field.md) because it contains all [polynomial roots](../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) and is generated by them.

In order 4, [characteristic](../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) is 2. For $\omega\ne0,1$, the multiplicative [group](../../../../group-split.md) has order 3, so $\omega^3=1$ and $\omega^2\ne1,\omega$. Factorization of $x^3-1$ gives $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$. The quadratic $x^2+x+1$ has no [polynomial root](../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) in $\mathbb F_2$ and is the [minimal polynomial](../../../../minimal-polynomial.md). Write $s=\omega^2=\omega+1$. The complete tables are

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
+&0&1&\omega&s\\\hline
0&0&1&\omega&s\\
1&1&0&s&\omega\\
\omega&\omega&s&0&1\\
s&s&\omega&1&0
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
\cdot&0&1&\omega&s\\\hline
0&0&0&0&0\\
1&0&1&\omega&s\\
\omega&0&\omega&s&1\\
s&0&s&1&\omega
\end{array}.
$$

For an element $\xi\ne0,1$ in order 8, the [tower law](../../../../tower-law.md) makes $[\mathbb F_2(\xi):\mathbb F_2]$ divide3. It is not1, so its [minimal polynomial](../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) is an irreducible monic cubic. Its [constant term](../../../../constant-term.md) must be1. A cubic $x^3+ax^2+bx+1$ has no [polynomial root](../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) at 1 exactly when $a+b=1$, and absence of a [polynomial root](../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) is equivalent to cubic irreducibility. Consequently the two possibilities are

$$
\boxed{x^3+x+1\quad\text{and}\quad x^3+x^2+1.}
$$

Both occur: together with $x$ and $x+1$ they factor $x^8-x$ over $\mathbb F_2$, whose distinct [polynomial roots](../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) are exactly the eight elements of the [field](../../../../field.md). Each cubic contributes its three [polynomial roots](../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md).
