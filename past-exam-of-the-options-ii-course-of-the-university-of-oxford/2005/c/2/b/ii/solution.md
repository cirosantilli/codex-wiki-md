<h1 id="c/2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Since $n>1$, its [factorial](../../../../../../factorial.md) is even, so $(n!)^2+1$ is odd and its [prime divisor](../../../../../../prime-factor.md) $p$ is odd. Also $p$ cannot divide $n!$, since it would then divide1. Modulo $p$,

$$
(n!)^2\equiv-1.
$$

Raise this equation to the [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) power $(p-1)/2$. [Fermat's little theorem](../../../../../../fermat-little-theorem.md) gives

$$
1\equiv(n!)^{p-1}\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\pmod p.
$$

As $p$ is odd, $1$ and $-1$ are distinct [residues](../../../../../../residue.md). Thus the exponent is even, and

$$
\boxed{p\equiv1\pmod4.}
$$
