<h1 id="c/c2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [quadratic reciprocity](../../../../../quadratic-reciprocity.md) states that for distinct odd [primes](../../../../../prime-number-split.md) $p,q$,

$$
\left(\frac pq\right)\left(\frac qp\right)=(-1)^{(p-1)(q-1)/4}.
$$

With $q=3$, it gives $\left(\frac3p\right)=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\left(\frac p3\right)$. The nonzero square modulo three is one, so $\left(\frac p3\right)=1$ when $p\equiv1\pmod3$, and $-1$ when $p\equiv-1\pmod3$. An [odd prime](../../../../../odd-prime.md) other than three is congruent to $1,5,7$, or $11$ modulo twelve. Evaluating the two signs gives

$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}p\bmod12&1&5&7&11\\\hline
(-1)^{(p-1)/2}&1&1&-1&-1\\
\left(\frac p3\right)&1&-1&1&-1\\
\left(\frac3p\right)&1&-1&-1&1
\end{array}
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac3p\right)=1\text{ for }p\equiv\pm1\pmod{12},\quad -1\text{ for }p\equiv\pm5\pmod{12}.}
$$
