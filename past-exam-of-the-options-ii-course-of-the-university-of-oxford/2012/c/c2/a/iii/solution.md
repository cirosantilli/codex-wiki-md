<h1 id="c/c2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

[Euler's criterion](../../../../../../euler-s-criterion.md) gives

$$
\left(\frac{ab}p\right)\equiv (ab)^{(p-1)/2}
\equiv a^{(p-1)/2}b^{(p-1)/2}
\equiv\left(\frac ap\right)\left(\frac bp\right)\pmod p.
$$

If either factor is divisible by $p$, both asserted values are zero. Otherwise the two values lie in $\{1,-1\}$, whose elements are distinct modulo an [odd prime](../../../../../../odd-prime.md). The [modular congruence](../../../../../../modular-congruence.md) is therefore equality:

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{ab}p\right)=\left(\frac ap\right)\left(\frac bp\right).}
$$

Finally, $a\equiv b\pmod p$ means they give the same [residue class](../../../../../../residue-class.md) and thus the same set of square-congruence solutions. Divisibility by $p$ is also identical. Hence $\left(\frac ap\right)=\left(\frac bp\right)$.
