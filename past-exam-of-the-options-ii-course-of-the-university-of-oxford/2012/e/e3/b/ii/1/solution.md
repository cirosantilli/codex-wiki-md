<h1 id="e/e3/b/ii/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Take $A_1=\mathbb Q^2$. Every [open disk](../../../../../../../open-disk.md) contains infinitely many points with both coordinates rational, so every point of the plane is an [accumulation point](../../../../../../../limit-point.md) of $A_1$. Every disk also contains infinitely many points with an irrational first coordinate, and these lie in the [complement](../../../../../../../complement-of-a-set.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{A_1'=\mathbb R^2=(\mathbb R^2\setminus A_1)'.}
$$

The infinitely-many-points observation ensures that one can avoid the [centre](../../../../../../../center-geometry.md) of the disk itself in both cases.
