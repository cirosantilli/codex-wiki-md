<h1 id="e/e3/b/ii/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Take

$$
A_2=\{(1/n,0):n\geq1\}.
$$

Its only [accumulation point](../../../../../../../limit-point.md) is $(0,0)$, which is absent from $A_2$. Every listed point is isolated, so none belongs to $A_2'$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{A_2'=\{(0,0)\},\qquad A_2'\not\subseteq A_2,\quad A_2\not\subseteq A_2'.}
$$

For completeness, away from the origin a sufficiently small disk meets only finitely many of the listed points and can be shrunk to exclude all but its [centre](../../../../../../../center-geometry.md), proving there are no further [accumulation points](../../../../../../../limit-point.md).
