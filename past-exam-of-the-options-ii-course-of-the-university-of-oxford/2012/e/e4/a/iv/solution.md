<h1 id="e/e4/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Let two distinct quotient points correspond to two [equivalence classes](../../../../../../equivalence-class.md). By [hypothesis](../../../../../../hypothesis.md) those classes lie in disjoint [open](../../../../../../open-set.md) saturated sets $U,V\subseteq X$. Saturation gives $p^{-1}(p(U))=U$, so the quotient definition makes $p(U)$ [open](../../../../../../open-set.md), and likewise $p(V)$.

The image sets contain the two quotient points and are disjoint. Indeed, a class meeting both $U$ and $V$ would be contained in each because both sets are saturated, contradicting their disjointness. Thus **the quotient is [Hausdorff](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md)**.
