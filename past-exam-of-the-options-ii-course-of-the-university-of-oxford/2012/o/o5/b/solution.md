The [Neyman-Pearson lemma](../../../../../neyman-pearson-lemma.md) concerns testing two simple hypotheses with densities $f_0$ and $f_1$ relative to a common measure. A test that rejects when $f_1(x)>k f_0(x)$, accepts when $f_1(x)<k f_0(x)$, and randomizes on equality if necessary to achieve size $\alpha$, is most powerful among all tests with size at most $\alpha$:

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E_0\varphi=\alpha\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathbb E_1\psi\leq\mathbb E_1\varphi\text{ for every test }\psi\text{ with }\mathbb E_0\psi\leq\alpha.}
$$

Here $\varphi$ is the described [likelihood-ratio test](../../../../../likelihood-ratio-test.md) and test functions take values in $[0,1]$. In the [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) normal setting below the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) has [probability](../../../../../probability.md) zero, so randomization is unnecessary.
