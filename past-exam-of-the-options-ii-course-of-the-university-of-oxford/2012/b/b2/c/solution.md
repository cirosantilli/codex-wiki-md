<h1 id="b/b2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $H=Z(x)$. The map $G/H\to C(x)$ sending $gH$ to $gxg^{-1}$ is well defined: multiplying $g$ on the right by an element commuting with $x$ leaves its conjugate unchanged. It is onto by the [orbit](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) definition. If $gxg^{-1}=hxh^{-1}$, then $h^{-1}g$ commutes with $x$, so $gH=hH$, proving [injectivity](../../../../../injective-function.md).

Every [coset](../../../../../coset.md) has $|H|$ elements, so $|C(x)|=|G|/|H|$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{|G|=|Z(x)|\,|C(x)|.}
$$

This is the [orbit-stabilizer theorem](../../../../../orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md) specialized to [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md).
