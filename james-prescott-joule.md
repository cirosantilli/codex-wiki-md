# James Prescott Joule

↑ **Parent:** [List of physicists](list-of-physicists.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Prescott_Joule)

James Prescott Joule was an English [physicist](physicist-split.md) whose private experiments connected [electricity](electricity.md), [heat](heat-split.md) and mechanical [work](work.md). His family ran a brewery in Salford; he developed an experimental laboratory while working in the family business.[Biographical account: brewing family, private laboratory and experimental interests](james-prescott-joule-by-science-museum-group.md) The Royal Society's archive identifies his occupation as [physicist](physicist-split.md) and records his life as 1818–1889.[Person record NA7041: occupation and life dates](james-prescott-joule-by-royal-society.md)

His electrical heating experiments placed metal coils in water and measured the [temperature](temperature-split.md) change. Later, he used the brewery cellar to reduce ambient [temperature](temperature-split.md) fluctuations during friction experiments and took particular care over [thermometer calibration](thermometer-calibration.md).[Electrical heating experiments; brewery-cellar friction measurements and precision thermometers](james-prescott-joule-by-science-museum-group.md) These practical choices address a central difficulty of [calorimetry](calorimetry-split.md): a small [temperature](temperature-split.md) rise can reflect both the intended input of [energy](energy-split.md) and unwanted [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) to the surroundings. A quiet baseline and a known [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) are part of the measurement, not decoration around the formula.

The contrast between [Joule heating](joule-heating.md) and [Joule's paddle-wheel experiment](joule-s-paddle-wheel-experiment.md) makes the connection especially clear. One supplies electrical [work](work.md); the other supplies mechanical [work](work.md). In each case, [calorimetry](calorimetry-split.md) tracks the resulting change of [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md). Comparing quantitatively controlled inputs with thermal changes gives an experimental test of [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md), rather than merely showing that stirring or an [electric current](electric-current.md) can warm water. The paddle-wheel account describes the descending weights, vanes, thermal controls and corrections needed for that comparison.

[Joule's free-expansion experiment](joule-s-free-expansion-experiment.md) asks a different question: how does a gas's [temperature](temperature-split.md) change during expansion without external [work](work.md)? The [Joule-Thomson effect](joule-thomson-effect.md) instead concerns flow through a restriction and a balance involving [enthalpy](enthalpy-split.md). Keeping the two experiments distinct prevents a shared historical name from hiding different system boundaries and different conserved quantities.
