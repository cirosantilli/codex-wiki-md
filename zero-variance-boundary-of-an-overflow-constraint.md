# Zero-variance boundary of an overflow constraint

↑ **Parent:** [Exact Gaussian overflow constraint](exact-gaussian-overflow-constraint.md)

If a random variable has [variance](variance-split.md) zero and mean $m$, it equals $m$ almost surely. For a target probability strictly between zero and one, $\mathbb P(X\geq C)$ meets the target exactly when $m<C$. Substituting zero variance into a positive-variance non-strict quantile constraint incorrectly includes $m=C$, where the overflow probability is one. The distinction comes from the atom at the threshold.
