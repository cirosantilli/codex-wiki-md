# Polar arc length

↑ **Parent:** [Arc length](arc-length.md)

For a piecewise [differentiable](differentiable-function.md) [curve](curve.md) given in [polar coordinates](polar-coordinates.md) by $r=r(\theta)$, differentiate $x=r\cos\theta$ and $y=r\sin\theta$. The cross terms cancel in $dx^2+dy^2$, leaving $ds^2=dr^2+r^2d\theta^2$. Therefore the [arc length](arc-length.md) on an increasing parameter interval is

$$
\ell=\int_{\theta_0}^{\theta_1}\sqrt{r(\theta)^2+r'(\theta)^2}\,d\theta.
$$

This formula remains valid through an isolated zero of the speed when the parametrization is piecewise smooth. A square root of a square must retain its [absolute value](absolute-value.md).
