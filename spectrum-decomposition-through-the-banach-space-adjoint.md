# Spectrum decomposition through the Banach-space adjoint

↑ **Parent:** [Approximate point spectrum](approximate-point-spectrum.md)

For a bounded [linear operator](linear-operator.md) on a complex [Banach space](banach-space-split.md), either a spectral scalar has approximate unit [eigenvectors](eigenvector.md) or it is an [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) of the [Banach-space adjoint](transpose-of-a-bounded-linear-operator.md). Indeed outside the [approximate point spectrum](approximate-point-spectrum.md), $S=T-\lambda I$ is bounded below, hence injective with closed range. If the adjoint has no zero vector in its [kernel](kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) other than zero, the [Hahn-Banach theorem](hahn-banach-theorem.md) makes the range dense: any proper closed range would have a nonzero continuous functional annihilating it. Thus the range is all of the space and the lower bound gives a bounded inverse. Conversely invertibility rules out both alternatives. With complex-linear duals there is no conjugation of $\lambda$ in the displayed formula; Hilbert-adjoint conventions must be distinguished.
