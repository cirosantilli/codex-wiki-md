# Closed-population capture-recapture model

↑ **Parent:** [Capture-recapture model](capture-recapture-model.md)

A [closed-population capture-recapture model](closed-population-capture-recapture-model.md) assumes the same individuals remain throughout the observations: no births, deaths, immigration, or emigration. Its elementary two-sample version additionally requires retained, correctly recognized marks and a second sample chosen by [simple random sampling](simple-random-sampling.md) independently of first-sample membership. Mixing and unchanged capture behavior help make this plausible. Equal individual capture [probabilities](probability.md) alone do not guarantee that every size-$C$ [subset](subset.md) is equally likely; that stronger sampling assumption gives the [hypergeometric distribution](hypergeometric-distribution.md). See [Barker, §§1.1.1 and 1.1.3: closure, marks, and catchability assumptions](https://www.maths.otago.ac.nz/~rbarker/Thesis/thesis.pdf).

Unequal catchability, mark loss, and population change can invalidate this [capture-recapture model](capture-recapture-model.md). For example, if especially catchable individuals appear repeatedly, overlap can be inflated and the [Lincoln-Petersen estimator](lincoln-petersen-estimator.md) can underestimate population [cardinality](cardinality.md). Lost marks can decrease recorded overlap and increase the same [ratio estimator](ratio-estimator.md), all else equal. Two samples alone cannot establish that the sampling assumptions hold.
