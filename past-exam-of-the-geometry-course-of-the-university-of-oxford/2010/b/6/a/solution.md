<h1 id="b/6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

First record the unlettered definitions. A [divisor on an algebraic curve](../../../../../divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) on a [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) [Riemann surface](../../../../../riemann-surfaces-split.md) is a finite formal sum $D=\sum_p n_p[p]$ with $n_p\in\mathbb Z$. Its degree is $\deg D=\sum_p n_p$. A [principal divisor](../../../../../principal-divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) is $(f)=\sum_p\operatorname{ord}_p(f)[p]$ for a nonzero [meromorphic function](../../../../../meromorphic-function.md), with positive order at a zero and negative order at a pole. Two divisors are [linearly equivalent](../../../../../linear-equivalence-of-weil-divisors.md) if their difference is principal. Principal divisors form a subgroup because $(fg)=(f)+(g)$ and $(1/f)=-(f)$.

For the doubly periodic function, put $h=f'/f$. It is periodic with the same [period lattice](../../../../../period-lattice.md), and its [residue](../../../../../residue.md) at $p_i$ equals $n_i$. Opposite sides of the [fundamental parallelogram](../../../../../fundamental-parallelogram-of-a-period-lattice.md) are translates by a period with opposite directions, so their integrals of $h$ cancel. The [residue theorem](../../../../../residue-theorem.md) gives

$$
0=\oint_{\Gamma_a}h(w)\,dw=2\pi i\sum_i n_i.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\sum_i n_i=0.}
$$

In particular an [elliptic function](../../../../../elliptic-function.md) has equally many zeros and poles counted with multiplicity in a period cell.
