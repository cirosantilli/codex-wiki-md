<h1 id="b/6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Set $g(w)=a\wp(w)+b\wp'(w)+c$. The [Weierstrass elliptic function](../../../../../weierstrass-elliptic-function.md) is doubly periodic, with a double pole at the origin modulo $\Lambda$ and no other poles. Its derivative has leading term $-2w^{-3}$. Since $b\ne0$, no lower-order term cancels this, so $g$ has a pole of order exactly three at zero and no others.

The point $\Phi(0+\Lambda)=[0,1,0]$ does not lie on the line, because its line equation evaluates to $b\ne0$. At every other point of the image, lying on the line is equivalent to $g(w)=0$. The three distinct image points therefore yield three distinct zeros modulo $\Lambda$. By the zero-pole count, these are all the zeros and each is simple. Hence its [principal divisor](../../../../../principal-divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) on the torus is

$$
(g)=[w_1+\Lambda]+[w_2+\Lambda]+[w_3+\Lambda]-3[0+\Lambda].
$$

Applying the [elliptic divisor-sum identity](../../../../../elliptic-divisor-sum-identity.md) gives

$$
\boxed{w_1+w_2+w_3\in\Lambda.}
$$

This is the [period lattice](../../../../../period-lattice.md) form of the [chord-and-tangent group law](../../../../../chord-and-tangent-group-law.md) on an elliptic cubic.
