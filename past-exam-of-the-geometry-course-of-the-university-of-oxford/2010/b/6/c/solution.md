<h1 id="b/6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a [divisor on an algebraic curve](../../../../../divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) $D=\sum_jn_j[p_j+\Lambda]$, define

$$
\boxed{\Psi([D])=\left(\sum_jn_j,\ \sum_jn_jp_j+\Lambda\right)\in\mathbb Z\times(\mathbb C/\Lambda).}
$$

Changing the representatives $p_j$ changes the second coordinate only by an element of $\Lambda$. The two preceding contour arguments show that a [principal divisor](../../../../../principal-divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) has degree zero and [period lattice](../../../../../period-lattice.md) sum zero. Thus the formula is unchanged by [linear equivalence of divisors](../../../../../linear-equivalence-of-divisors.md) and is a well-defined [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) on the quotient.

It is nontrivial, since $[0]$ maps to $(1,0+\Lambda)$. In fact it is [surjective](../../../../../surjective-function.md): for any $d\in\mathbb Z$ and $z+\Lambda\in\mathbb C/\Lambda$, the [divisor on an algebraic curve](../../../../../divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) $(d-1)[0]+[z+\Lambda]$ maps to $(d,z+\Lambda)$. Only well-definedness and a nontrivial homomorphism are required here; identifying its entire kernel would be an additional theorem.
