<h1 id="b/5/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The curves have no common component, since their intersection is finite. Let $R=0$ define the irreducible degree-$m$ curve $E$. Choose $p\in E$ outside $C\cap D$, and select $(\lambda,\mu)\ne(0,0)$ with $\lambda P(p)+\mu Q(p)=0$. The polynomial $H=\lambda P+\mu Q$ is not identically zero, since $P,Q$ are not proportional.

The degree-$n$ curve $H=0$ meets $E$ at the prescribed $nm$ base points and also at $p$. If $R$ did not divide $H$, irreducibility of $R$ and the [Bézout theorem](../../../../../bezout-s-theorem.md) would bound the number of distinct intersections by $nm$, contradicting these $nm+1$ points. Thus

$$
H=RS,\qquad\deg S=n-m.
$$

At every remaining common zero of $P,Q$ lying off $E$, $R$ is nonzero and hence $S=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{F=\{S=0\}\text{ contains all remaining }n(n-m)\text{ points},\qquad\deg F\leq n-m.}
$$

This is [residual intersection through a curve pencil](../../../../../residual-intersection-through-a-curve-pencil.md). The existence of a point $p$ outside the finite base locus is automatic on the projective curve $E$.
