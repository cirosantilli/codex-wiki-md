The strong [Bézout theorem](../../../../../bezout-s-theorem.md) says that if two projective plane [algebraic curves](../../../../../algebraic-curve.md) $C,D$ over $\mathbb C$ have degrees $m,n$ and no common irreducible component, their intersection is finite and

$$
\boxed{\sum_{p\in C\cap D}I_p(C,D)=mn.}
$$

Each [intersection multiplicity](../../../../../intersection-multiplicity.md) is a positive integer. Scheme-theoretic multiplicities, rather than just distinct intersection points, are essential: tangencies and singularities can make individual terms larger than one.
