<h1 id="b/5/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Let the six distinct [conic](../../../../../conic-section.md) points be $p_1,\ldots,p_6$, and write $\ell_{ij}$ for their joining line. Form two reducible cubics

$$
C=\ell_{12}\cup\ell_{34}\cup\ell_{56},\qquad D=\ell_{23}\cup\ell_{45}\cup\ell_{61}.
$$

No three distinct points of an irreducible [conic](../../../../../conic-section.md) are collinear, by the [Bézout theorem](../../../../../bezout-s-theorem.md) applied to a line and the [conic](../../../../../conic-section.md). Thus all six side lines are distinct, the cubics have no common component, and six of their intersections are precisely the vertices. The remaining pairings give

$$
q_1=\ell_{12}\cap\ell_{45},\qquad q_2=\ell_{34}\cap\ell_{61},\qquad q_3=\ell_{56}\cap\ell_{23}.
$$

These three points are off the [conic](../../../../../conic-section.md) and distinct. For example, equality of $q_1$ and $q_2$ would put that point on both $\ell_{34}$ and $\ell_{45}$, making it $p_4$ and then placing $p_4$ on $\ell_{12}$, impossible. The other cases are identical. Hence the two cubics meet in nine distinct points.

Apply [residual intersection through a curve pencil](../../../../../residual-intersection-through-a-curve-pencil.md) with $n=3$, $m=2$ and $E$ the [conic](../../../../../conic-section.md): six intersections lie on $E$, so the other three lie on a curve of degree at most one. Therefore **$q_1,q_2,q_3$ are collinear**, proving [Pascal's theorem](../../../../../pascal-s-theorem.md).
