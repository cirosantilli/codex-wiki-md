<h1 id="b/4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

First consider the unlettered [projective frame](../../../../../projective-frame.md) assertion. A list of $n+2$ points in $\mathbb{CP}^n$ is in general position when every $n+1$ of their representative vectors are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md), equivalently no $n+1$ of the points lie in a projective hyperplane. Choose representatives $v_1,\ldots,v_{n+1}$ of the first $n+1$ points as a [basis](../../../../../basis.md). Write $v_{n+2}=\sum c_iv_i$. Each $c_i$ is nonzero, since otherwise $v_{n+2}$ and the other $n$ basis vectors would be dependent. Rescaling the representatives makes $v_{n+2}=v_1+\cdots+v_{n+1}$.

Do the same for the second frame with representatives $w_i$. The [invertible linear map](../../../../../invertible-linear-map.md) $v_i\mapsto w_i$ induces a [projective transformation](../../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md) carrying all $n+2$ points as required. A transformation fixing a normalized frame is represented by a diagonal matrix, since it fixes the first $n+1$ points. Fixing the sum point forces every diagonal entry to coincide, so the matrix is scalar and the [projective transformation](../../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md) is the identity. This proves **existence and uniqueness**.

Return to the four chosen points on the two lines. Since the six points are distinct, at most one is $K\cap L$; put its index third if necessary. No three of $p_1,p_2,q_1,q_2$ are collinear: every triple contains two on one line and a point on the other line away from the intersection. Thus they form a [projective frame](../../../../../projective-frame.md) in $\mathbb{CP}^2$. The four target points also have no collinear triple, as their three-by-three [determinants](../../../../../determinant.md) are nonzero. The frame result gives **a unique [projective transformation](../../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md) with the prescribed four images**.
