<h1 id="a/2/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Choose $f(u)=\sin u$ and $h(u)=\cos u$ for $0\leq u\leq\pi$. Adding the smooth poles at the endpoints gives the unit [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md). Its meridian is parametrized by [arc length](../../../../../arc-length.md), and $-f''/f=1$ away from the poles, hence everywhere by continuity. Thus **the unit [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) has constant [Gaussian curvature](../../../../../gaussian-curvature.md) one**.

There is **no [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) smooth [surface of revolution](../../../../../surface-of-revolution.md) without boundary of constant [Gaussian curvature](../../../../../gaussian-curvature.md) minus one** in this setting. On a [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) such [surface](../../../../../topological-surface-split.md), the distance $f$ from the rotation axis attains a positive maximum. The maximum lies on a regular revolution patch, where the meridian [arc length](../../../../../arc-length.md) coordinate gives $f''\leq0$. Hence $K=-f''/f\geq0$ there, contradicting $K=-1$. Equivalently, constant negative [curvature](../../../../../curvature.md) would require $f''=f>0$, excluding any positive radial maximum. Compact patches with boundary would not be excluded by this argument, but they are not closed surfaces.
