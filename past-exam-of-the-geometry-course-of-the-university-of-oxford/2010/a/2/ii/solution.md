<h1 id="a/2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Work on a regular patch with $f(u)>0$. Differentiating the [surface of revolution](../../../../../surface-of-revolution.md) gives

$$
r_u=(f'\cos v,f'\sin v,h'),\qquad r_v=(-f\sin v,f\cos v,0).
$$

The arc-length condition gives $E=1$, $F=0$, $G=f^2$. Also $\|r_u\times r_v\|=f$, so take

$$
N=(-h'\cos v,-h'\sin v,f').
$$

The second derivatives yield

$$
e=f'h''-f''h',\qquad f_2=0,\qquad g_2=fh'.
$$

Thus the [first fundamental form](../../../../../first-fundamental-form.md) and [second fundamental form](../../../../../second-fundamental-form-split.md) are

$$
\boxed{\mathrm I=du^2+f^2dv^2,\qquad\mathrm{II}=(f'h''-f''h')du^2+fh'\,dv^2.}
$$

The opposite normal reverses the second displayed form. At a smooth pole the revolution coordinates degenerate, so [curvature](../../../../../curvature.md) is understood by a regular local chart or by continuity.
