<h1 id="a/3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For an affinely parametrized [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md) $s\mapsto(x(s),y(s))$, the energy [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md) is $L=(\dot x^2+\dot y^2)/(2y^2)$. The coordinate $x$ is cyclic, so the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) gives $\dot x/y^2=c$, a constant. Parametrize a nonconstant [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md) by hyperbolic [arc length](../../../../../arc-length.md); then

$$
\dot x=cy^2,\qquad\dot x^2+\dot y^2=y^2.
$$

If $c=0$, $x$ is constant: these are vertical lines. If $c\ne0$, on intervals with $\dot y\ne0$,

$$
\frac{dx}{dy}=\frac{\pm cy}{\sqrt{1-c^2y^2}}.
$$

Integration gives $(x-a)^2+y^2=c^{-2}$. The two branches join smoothly at the highest point. Conversely these curves satisfy the [geodesic equations](../../../../../geodesic-equation.md), or are images of vertical lines under half-plane [Möbius transformations](../../../../../mobius-transformation.md) that are [isometries](../../../../../isometry.md). Thus the complete nonconstant [geodesics](../../../../../geodesic.md) are

$$
\boxed{x=\text{constant},\qquad(x-a)^2+y^2=R^2\ (y>0),}
$$

namely vertical lines and upper semicircles orthogonal to the real axis. Their subarcs give [geodesic segments](../../../../../geodesic-segment.md).
