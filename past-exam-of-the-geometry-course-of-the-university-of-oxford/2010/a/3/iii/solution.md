<h1 id="a/3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For distinct points $a,b$ on a [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md), the given disc [isometry](../../../../../isometry.md) $z\mapsto e^{i\theta}(z-a)/(1-\bar az)$ sends $a$ to zero. Choose $\theta$ so that the image of $b$ is the positive number

$$
x=\left|\frac{b-a}{1-\bar ab}\right|\in(0,1).
$$

An orthogonal-circle [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md) through zero is a diameter, so this normalizes the [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md) as required.

There are two [hyperbolic metric](../../../../../hyperbolic-metric.md) normalizations to distinguish. For the metric transferred from the stated half-plane, the radial length is

$$
d_D(0,x)=\int_0^x\frac{2\,dt}{1-t^2}=2\operatorname{artanh}x.
$$

The diameter is minimizing. Indeed, any path $w(t)$ from zero to $x$ has $|\dot w|\geq|d|w|/dt|$, so its length is at least $2\operatorname{artanh}x$, with equality on the radial segment. Therefore the distance consistent with the given half-plane metric is

$$
\boxed{d_D(a,b)=2\operatorname{artanh}\left|\frac{b-a}{1-\bar ab}\right|.}
$$

For the alternative disc metric $d\widetilde s_D^2=|dw|^2/(1-|w|^2)^2$, every length is halved and the same isometries and [geodesics](../../../../../geodesic.md) remain. In that convention,

$$
\boxed{\widetilde d_D(a,b)=\operatorname{artanh}\left|\frac{b-a}{1-\bar ab}\right|.}
$$

**The printed distance corresponds to the latter convention.** It is obtained from one quarter of the given half-plane metric, rather than its direct transfer. The equal-point case gives distance zero in both conventions. This is the [normalization of the Poincare disc metric](../../../../../normalization-of-the-poincare-disc-metric.md).
