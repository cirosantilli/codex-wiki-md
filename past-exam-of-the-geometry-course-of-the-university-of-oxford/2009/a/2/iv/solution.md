<h1 id="a/2/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The total pole order in one period cell is two, so the map has degree two. The [Riemann-Hurwitz formula](../../../../../riemann-hurwitz-formula.md) for [genus](../../../../../genus-of-a-surface.md) one to [genus](../../../../../genus-of-a-surface.md) zero gives $0=2\cdot2-R$, hence $R=4$. Each [local degree](../../../../../local-degree-of-a-continuous-map.md) is at most two, so every ramification contribution is exactly one. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\deg\wp=2,\qquad\text{four ramification points}.}
$$

They are zero and the three nonzero half-period classes. Zero is ramified because its reciprocal has a double zero. At a nonzero half-period, oddness and periodicity of $\wp'$ force $\wp'=0$; the count shows these exhaust the remaining points.
