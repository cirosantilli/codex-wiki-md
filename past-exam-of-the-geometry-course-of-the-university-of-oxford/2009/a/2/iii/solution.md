<h1 id="a/2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The given [Weierstrass elliptic function](../../../../../weierstrass-elliptic-function.md) is even, by replacing the lattice index $\omega$ with $-\omega$. Differentiation gives

$$
\wp'(z)=-2\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda}(z-\omega)^{-3}.
$$

For any $\lambda\in\Lambda$, reindexing the sum shows $\wp'(z+\lambda)=\wp'(z)$. Thus $\wp(z+\lambda)-\wp(z)=c_\lambda$ is constant off the lattice. Replacing $z$ by $-z-\lambda$ and using evenness changes this difference to its negative, so $c_\lambda=-c_\lambda$ and $c_\lambda=0$. Hence **$\wp$ is doubly periodic**.

It therefore descends to a [meromorphic function](../../../../../meromorphic-function.md) on $\mathbb C/\Lambda$, with a double pole at zero modulo the lattice and no others. A [meromorphic function](../../../../../meromorphic-function.md) is a [holomorphic map](../../../../../holomorphic-map.md) to the [Riemann sphere](../../../../../riemann-sphere.md), sending poles to infinity. Locally at zero, $1/\wp(z)=z^2+O(z^4)$ is holomorphic and vanishes there, so the sphere chart at infinity supplies the required extension. The map is nonconstant because it has a pole.
