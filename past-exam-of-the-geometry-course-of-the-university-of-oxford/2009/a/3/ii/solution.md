<h1 id="a/3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Since $\gamma'=r_uu'+r_vv'$, we have $\gamma'\cdot r_u=Eu'+Fv'$. The normal-acceleration characterization of a [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md) gives

$$
\frac d{ds}(Eu'+Fv')=\gamma'\cdot(r_{uu}u'+r_{uv}v').
$$

Expanding and differentiating the metric coefficients yields $r_u\cdot r_{uu}=E_u/2$, $r_v\cdot r_{uu}+r_u\cdot r_{uv}=F_u$, and $r_v\cdot r_{uv}=G_u/2$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac d{ds}(Eu'+Fv')=\tfrac12E_u(u')^2+F_uu'v'+\tfrac12G_u(v')^2.}
$$

Applying the same calculation to $\gamma'\cdot r_v=Fu'+Gv'$ gives

$$
\boxed{\frac d{ds}(Fu'+Gv')=\tfrac12E_v(u')^2+F_vu'v'+\tfrac12G_v(v')^2.}
$$

These are also the [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) of the quadratic metric energy.
