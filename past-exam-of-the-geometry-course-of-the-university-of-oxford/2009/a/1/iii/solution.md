<h1 id="a/1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For the [connected sum of surfaces](../../../../../connected-sum-of-surfaces.md), remove an embedded open disc from each [surface](../../../../../topological-surface-split.md) and identify the resulting boundary circles by a homeomorphism. The resulting closed connected [surface](../../../../../topological-surface-split.md) is denoted $M\#N$.

Removing a disc decreases the [Euler characteristic](../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) by one. Gluing the remaining surfaces along their common boundary subtracts the characteristic of a circle, which is zero. Thus

$$
\boxed{\chi(M\#N)=\chi(M)+\chi(N)-2.}
$$

This can be verified by choosing matching subdivisions of the boundary circles and counting the vertices and edges identified in the gluing.
