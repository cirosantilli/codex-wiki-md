<h1 id="b/6/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Apply the preceding argument to $q+r$ to obtain a nonconstant [meromorphic function](../../../../../meromorphic-function.md) $g$ with simple poles exactly at $q,r$. Both $f,g$, and the constant function one, belong to $L(p+q+r)$.

If $a+bf+cg=0$, examination at $p$, where only $f$ has a pole, forces $b=0$. Examination at $r$, where only $g$ has a pole, forces $c=0$, and then $a=0$. Thus the three functions are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md). Since the [Riemann-Roch theorem](../../../../../riemann-roch-theorem.md) gives $\ell(p+q+r)=3$,

$$
\boxed{\{1,f,g\}\text{ is a basis of }L(p+q+r).}
$$
