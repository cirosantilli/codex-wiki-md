<h1 id="b/5/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The second-derivative matrix is

$$
\begin{pmatrix}6x&3\lambda z&3\lambda y\\3\lambda z&6y&3\lambda x\\3\lambda y&3\lambda x&6z\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its determinant is $54[(4+\lambda^3)xyz-\lambda^2(x^3+y^3+z^3)]$. Hence the [Hessian curve](../../../../../hessian-curve-of-a-plane-curve.md) has equation

$$
\boxed{\lambda^2(x^3+y^3+z^3)-(\lambda^3+4)xyz=0.}
$$

If $\lambda=0$, this is the union $xyz=0$ of three lines and is singular. If $\lambda\ne0$, divide by $\lambda^2$ and write it as a Hesse cubic with parameter $\mu=-(\lambda^3+4)/(3\lambda^2)$. The preceding singularity criterion gives $\mu^3=-1$, equivalently

$$
(\lambda^3+4)^3-27\lambda^6=(\lambda^3+1)(\lambda^3-8)^2=0.
$$

Thus the complete condition is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Hess}(C)\text{ singular}\iff\lambda=0\text{ or }\lambda^3=-1\text{ or }\lambda^3=8.}
$$

The other values beyond the source's first family are zero and the three cube roots of eight.
