<h1 id="b/7/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For a **smooth** complex [projective plane](../../../../../projective-plane.md) curve of degree $d$,

$$
\boxed{g=\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}2.}
$$

Project generically from a point outside the curve to a [projective line](../../../../../projective-line.md). Its degree is $d$, by intersection with a generic line through the center. Ramification occurs when such a line is tangent. In coordinates for projection $[x:y:z]\mapsto[x:z]$, these points satisfy $F=F_y=0$. The polar has degree $d-1$, and Bézout gives total ramification $d(d-1)$; generic projection makes the ramification simple, or the intersection multiplicities can be read as the corresponding ramification orders. The [Riemann-Hurwitz formula](../../../../../riemann-hurwitz-formula.md) yields $2g-2=-2d+d(d-1)$ and hence the formula. The smoothness qualification is essential for [geometric genus](../../../../../geometric-genus.md): singular plane curves have the same arithmetic degree-[genus](../../../../../genus-of-a-surface.md) expression, while the [genus](../../../../../genus-of-a-surface.md) of their normalization can be smaller.
