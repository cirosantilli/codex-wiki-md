<h1 id="b/6/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a plane [affine curve](../../../../../affine-algebraic-curve.md) given by a reduced equation $f(x,y)=0$, a point is singular when **$f=f_x=f_y=0$** there. For a [projective curve](../../../../../projective-curve.md) given by a homogeneous equation $F(x,y,z)=0$, a point is singular when **$F=F_x=F_y=F_z=0$** on a nonzero representative. These conditions are invariant under projective rescaling. In characteristic zero, Euler's identity $dF=xF_x+yF_y+zF_z$ makes the equation $F=0$ follow from vanishing of the partials when $d\ne0$ in the field. Over an arbitrary field one should retain the equation itself, particularly if the characteristic divides the degree.
