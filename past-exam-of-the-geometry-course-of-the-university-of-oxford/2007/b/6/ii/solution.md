<h1 id="b/6/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

[Bézout's theorem](../../../../../bezout-s-theorem.md) says that two [projective plane](../../../../../projective-plane.md) curves over $\mathbb C$ of degrees $d,e$ with no common irreducible component have total [intersection multiplicity](../../../../../intersection-multiplicity.md) $de$, so at most $de$ distinct intersection points. Let the irreducible curve have reduced homogeneous equation $F$ of positive degree $d$. In characteristic zero some [partial derivative](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) is nonzero. For $d>1$ this [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) has degree $d-1$ and cannot share a component with the irreducible degree-$d$ [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $F$. Every [singular point](../../../../../singular-point-of-an-algebraic-variety.md) belongs to their intersection. Bézout bounds their number by $d(d-1)$, proving **there are finitely many [singular points](../../../../../singular-point-of-an-algebraic-variety.md)**. For $d=1$ a partial is a nonzero constant and there are none.
