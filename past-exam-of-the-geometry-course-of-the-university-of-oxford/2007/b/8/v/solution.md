<h1 id="b/8/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

A [divisor on an algebraic curve](../../../../../divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) is a finite integer sum $D=\sum_pn_pp$; its degree is $\sum_pn_p$. For a [meromorphic function](../../../../../meromorphic-function.md) its [principal divisor](../../../../../principal-divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) records its zero and [pole](../../../../../pole.md) orders, and

$$
L(D)=\{0\}\cup\{f\text{ meromorphic}:\operatorname{div}(f)+D\geq0\},\qquad\ell(D)=\dim_{\mathbb C}L(D).
$$

Let $K$ be the divisor of a nonzero meromorphic differential, called a [canonical divisor](../../../../../canonical-divisor.md). The [Riemann-Roch theorem](../../../../../riemann-roch-theorem.md) states

$$
\boxed{\ell(D)-\ell(K-D)=\deg D+1-g.}
$$

Multiplication by that differential identifies $L(K)$ with the space of holomorphic differentials. By the preceding result $L(0)$ consists exactly of constants, so $\ell(0)=1$. Applying the formula at $D=0$ gives $1-\ell(K)=1-g$, hence **$\ell(K)=g$**.
