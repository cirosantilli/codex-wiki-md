<h1 id="b/5/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Choose representative vectors $x_1,\ldots,x_{n+1}$ of the first $n+1$ points; they form a [basis](../../../../../basis.md). Write a representative of the last point as $\sum_i a_ix_i$. General position forces each $a_i\ne0$, since otherwise $n+1$ of the points would lie in a hyperplane. Rescale the [basis](../../../../../basis.md) vectors to $a_ix_i$. The tuple is then projectively equivalent to the standard frame

$$
[e_1],\ldots,[e_{n+1}],[e_1+\cdots+e_{n+1}].
$$

Apply the same normalization to the second tuple and compose the two transformations to obtain existence. An automorphism fixing the coordinate points is represented by a [diagonal matrix](../../../../../diagonal-matrix.md). Fixing their sum forces all its diagonal entries to agree, so its projective action is the identity. This proves **existence and uniqueness**. At $n=1$ it says **the [Möbius group](../../../../../mobius-group.md) acts sharply triply transitively on the [projective line](../../../../../projective-line.md)**: any ordered triple of distinct points can be sent to any other in exactly one way.
