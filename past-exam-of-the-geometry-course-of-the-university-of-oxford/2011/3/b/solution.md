<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Two regular coordinate patches are locally isometric under a coordinate correspondence exactly when it preserves their [first fundamental forms](../../../../first-fundamental-form.md). More explicitly, a [diffeomorphism](../../../../diffeomorphism.md) $\Phi:U\to\widetilde U$ of parameter domains induces a [local isometry](../../../../local-isometry.md) if and only if

$$
\boxed{\Phi^*\widetilde I=I.}
$$

In common coordinates this means equality of the three coefficient functions $E,F,G$. The criterion follows because the [first fundamental form](../../../../first-fundamental-form.md) gives the [inner product](../../../../inner-product-split.md) of every pair of [tangent vectors](../../../../tangent-vector-split.md). Equality therefore preserves lengths of all [curves](../../../../curve.md); conversely a smooth [isometry](../../../../isometry.md) preserves tangent-vector lengths and their [inner products](../../../../inner-product-split.md) by polarization.
