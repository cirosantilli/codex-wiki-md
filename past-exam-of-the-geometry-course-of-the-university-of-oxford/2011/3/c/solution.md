<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Using the two [first fundamental forms](../../../../first-fundamental-form.md) calculated below, choose

$$
\boxed{f(s)=\cosh s.}
$$

The [surface of revolution](../../../../surface-of-revolution.md) is then a [catenoid](../../../../catenoid.md), with metric $\cosh^2s\,(ds^2+dv^2)$. On the [helicoid](../../../../helicoid.md), take $u=\sinh s/a$, assuming $a\ne0$ so that its parametrization is regular. Substitution gives

$$
a^2du^2+(1+a^2u^2)dv^2
=\cosh^2s\,ds^2+\cosh^2s\,dv^2.
$$

Thus the coordinate map

$$
\left(\sinh s\cos v,\sinh s\sin v,v\right)
\longmapsto
\left(\cosh s\cos v,\cosh s\sin v,s\right)
$$

is a [local isometry](../../../../local-isometry.md) on sufficiently small patches. The constant $a$ only changes the radial parametrization of the given [helicoid](../../../../helicoid.md), whose pitch is fixed at one. For $a=0$ the displayed [helicoid](../../../../helicoid.md) parametrization is not a regular [surface](../../../../topological-surface-split.md).
