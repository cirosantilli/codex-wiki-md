<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Injectivity makes the images of any three distinct points distinct. By the ordered-frame result, there is a [projective transformation](../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md) $L$ agreeing with the given map on three chosen points. Composing by $L^{-1}$ reduces to an injective map fixing those points and preserving every [cross-ratio](../../../../cross-ratio.md). Change coordinates so that the three points are $\infty,0,1$.

For any other point with affine coordinate $z$, the convention in the source gives

$$
(\infty\,0:1\,z)=z.
$$

Preservation therefore forces the image of $z$ to have the same coordinate $z$. The normalized map fixes every point and is the identity. Undoing the normalization proves **the original map is exactly $L$**, hence projective. No prior continuity or surjectivity assumption is needed.
