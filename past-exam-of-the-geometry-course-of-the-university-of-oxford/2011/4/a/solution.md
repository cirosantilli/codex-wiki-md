<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose representatives $v_1,\ldots,v_{n+1}$ of the first $n+1$ points. The general-position condition says that these vectors form a [basis](../../../../basis.md) of $\mathbb C^{n+1}$. Write a representative of the last point as $v_{n+2}=\sum_ic_iv_i$. Every $c_i$ is nonzero; otherwise the last point and the other $n$ [basis](../../../../basis.md) points would lie in a [hyperplane](../../../../hyperplane.md). Rescaling $v_i$ by $c_i$ gives representatives with $v_{n+2}=\sum_iv_i$. Normalize representatives $w_i$ of the second frame in the same way.

There is a unique invertible [linear map](../../../../linear-map.md) $L$ with $L(v_i)=w_i$ for $i\leq n+1$. It also maps $v_{n+2}$ to $w_{n+2}$, so its projectivization has the required effect. For uniqueness, the quotient of two candidate [projective transformations](../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md) fixes the normalized frame. A representing matrix must be diagonal in the $v_i$ [basis](../../../../basis.md), with entries $a_i$. Fixing $[\sum_iv_i]$ forces all $a_i$ to be equal. Such a scalar represents the identity [projective transformation](../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md). Thus **the ordered [projective frame](../../../../projective-frame.md) determines the transformation uniquely**.
