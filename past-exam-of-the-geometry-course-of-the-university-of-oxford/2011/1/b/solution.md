<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Form the [dual graph](../../../../dual-graph.md) of the finite [triangulation](../../../../triangulation.md): its vertices are the triangles, and two vertices are joined when their triangles share an edge. Connectedness of the [surface](../../../../topological-surface-split.md) makes this graph connected. Choose a [spanning tree](../../../../spanning-tree.md) and begin with one disjoint triangle. Attach the remaining triangles along the tree edges, one at a time, always joining a new triangle along one boundary edge. At every step the result is a topological disk, so the final union is a polygonal disk.

The edges not used for these attachments still occur twice on the boundary, once for each incident triangle. Identify each pair as in the original [triangulation](../../../../triangulation.md). The result recovers the original [surface](../../../../topological-surface-split.md). This proves **a compact connected closed [surface](../../../../topological-surface-split.md) is a polygon with its boundary edges identified in pairs**. Closedness is the convention here; a boundary would leave some edges unpaired.
