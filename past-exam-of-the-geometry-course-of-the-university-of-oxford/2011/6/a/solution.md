<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [meromorphic function](../../../../meromorphic-function.md) on a compact [Riemann surface](../../../../riemann-surfaces-split.md) has finitely many poles, say $p_j$ of orders $r_j$. For each $p_j$, choose a projective line through it that does not contain $C$. Its [hyperplane divisor](../../../../hyperplane-divisor.md) contains $p_j$ with multiplicity at least one. Include $r_j$ copies of this [divisor](../../../../divisor.md). The sum then dominates the pole [divisor](../../../../divisor.md) of $f$.

Add further arbitrary [hyperplane divisors](../../../../hyperplane-divisor.md) until their number $m$ satisfies $m\geq d$ and $md>2g-2$. Adding effective [divisors](../../../../divisor.md) cannot destroy the pole bound, so for $D=H_1+\cdots+H_m$ we have

$$
(f)+D\geq0,\qquad\boxed{f\in L(D).}
$$

For a constant function, including zero, no initial poles need to be covered; simply add sufficiently many lines.
