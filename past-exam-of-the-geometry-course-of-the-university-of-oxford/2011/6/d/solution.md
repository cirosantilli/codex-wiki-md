<h1 id="6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Write $L_i=a_ix+b_iy+c_iz$ and $S=\prod_iL_i$. The numerator and denominator have the same homogeneous degree, so their ratio is well-defined on projective points away from its poles and extends as a [meromorphic function](../../../../meromorphic-function.md) on $C$. Locally the pole order cannot exceed the zero order of $S|_C$, whose [divisor](../../../../divisor.md) is $D$. Thus $f_Q\in L(D)$.

Equality of two ratios is equivalent to $Q-Q'$ vanishing on $C$. A nonsingular projective plane [curve](../../../../curve.md) is irreducible: distinct positive-degree components would intersect by [Bézout's theorem](../../../../bezout-s-theorem.md), producing a singular point. Its homogeneous vanishing ideal is therefore generated by its irreducible defining [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) $P$. One can also see this using [Bézout's theorem](../../../../bezout-s-theorem.md): a homogeneous [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) not divisible by $P$ has only finitely many intersections with $C$, so cannot vanish on the whole [curve](../../../../curve.md). Consequently

$$
\boxed{f_Q=f_{Q'}\iff Q-Q'=PR,\qquad R\in V_{m-d}.}
$$

Multiplication by the nonzero [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) $P$ is injective. The [linear map](../../../../linear-map.md) $V_m\to L(D)$ therefore has [kernel](../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) $PV_{m-d}$ and image [dimension](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md)

$$
\boxed{\binom{m+2}{2}-\binom{m-d+2}{2}
=md+\frac{3d-d^2}{2}=md+1-g.}
$$

The assumption $m\geq d$ makes both [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md)-space [dimensions](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) applicable directly.
