<h1 id="b/8/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Choose an actual pole $p_i$ of $f$; one exists by nonconstancy and compactness. In a local coordinate $z$ centered there, its [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md) has expansion $f(z)=a_{-1}/z+O(1)$ with $a_{-1}\ne0$. Hence

$$
w(z)=1/f(z)=z/a_{-1}+O(z^2),\qquad w'(0)\ne0.
$$

The [holomorphic inverse function theorem](../../../../../holomorphic-inverse-function-theorem.md) makes **$1/f$ a local coordinate**. Here the source notation $f^{-1}$ denotes the reciprocal, rather than an inverse map of the [meromorphic function](../../../../../meromorphic-function.md).

The chosen pole is among the fixed points, so $\varphi$ maps a sufficiently small neighborhood of it into the coordinate neighborhood. If $f=f\circ\varphi$, then $w=w\circ\varphi$ there. Injectivity of this coordinate forces $\varphi(x)=x$ throughout a nonempty open set. The stated identity principle then gives **$\varphi$ equal to the identity everywhere**.
