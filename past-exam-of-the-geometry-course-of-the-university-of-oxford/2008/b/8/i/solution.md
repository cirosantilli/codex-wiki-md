<h1 id="b/8/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [divisor on an algebraic curve](../../../../../divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) is a finite formal sum $D=\sum n_pp$ with integer coefficients. Its degree is $\deg D=\sum n_p$. The [principal divisor](../../../../../principal-divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) of a nonzero [meromorphic function](../../../../../meromorphic-function.md) is $(f)=\sum\operatorname{ord}_p(f)p$. For a nonconstant $f$, zeros and poles are fibers over zero and infinity of the same map to the [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md), each of total multiplicity equal to its degree; hence $\deg(f)=0$. A nonzero constant has zero divisor.

Write $L(D)=\{f:(f)+D\geq0\}\cup\{0\}$ and $\ell(D)=\dim L(D)$. The [Riemann-Roch theorem](../../../../../riemann-roch-theorem.md) states

$$
\boxed{\ell(D)-\ell(K_C-D)=\deg D+1-g,}
$$

where $K_C$ is a [canonical divisor](../../../../../canonical-divisor.md). Taking $D=p_1+\cdots+p_{g+1}$ gives $\ell(D)=2+\ell(K_C-D)\geq2$. Constants span only one [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md), so there is a nonconstant $f\in L(D)$. It has no poles outside these points and at most a [simple pole](../../../../../simple-pole.md) at each. Thus **the required nonconstant function exists**, with actual poles at some nonempty subset because a [holomorphic function](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on a compact connected curve is constant.
