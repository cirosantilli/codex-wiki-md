<h1 id="a/4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

If two distinct [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md) arcs enclosed a disk, regard its boundary as a curvilinear two-sided polygon, with interior angles $\alpha,\beta$. These are positive; zero angle would make the arcs tangent and local [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md) uniqueness would make them coincide. The [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md)-curvature terms vanish, so Gauss-Bonnet gives

$$
\int_DK\,dA+(\pi-\alpha)+(\pi-\beta)=2\pi,\qquad\int_DK\,dA=\alpha+\beta>0.
$$

This contradicts $K\leq0$. Thus **no such disk-bounding [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md) bigon exists**. The disk hypothesis matters because it fixes the [Euler characteristic](../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) on the right side.
