<h1 id="a/3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For the unit-speed curve, the [normal curvature](../../../../../normal-curvature.md) is $\kappa_n=t'\cdot n$, and the [geodesic curvature](../../../../../geodesic-curvature.md) is $\kappa_g=t'\cdot(n\times t)$. Differentiating $\gamma=r(u,v)$ twice and taking the normal component kills the terms in $r_u,r_v$, giving

$$
\boxed{\kappa_n=L(u')^2+2Mu'v'+N(v')^2.}
$$

An affinely parametrized [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md) has zero tangential acceleration, or equivalently its [covariant derivative](../../../../../covariant-derivative.md) of velocity is zero. For unit speed, $t'\cdot t=0$, and $t,n\times t,n$ form an [orthonormal frame](../../../../../orthonormal-frame-in-spacetime.md). Therefore $t'=\kappa_n n+\kappa_g(n\times t)$, so zero tangential acceleration is exactly **$\kappa_g=0$**.
