# Chain transfer in chain polymerization

↑ **Parent:** [Chain-growth polymerization](chain-growth-polymerization.md)

In [chain transfer in chain polymerization](chain-transfer-in-chain-polymerization.md), a growing chain can lose its [polymerization-active site](polymerization-active-site.md) while another [molecule](molecule.md) acquires reactivity. In a common atom-transfer picture, the old chain becomes inactive and the new carrier can initiate further [chain-growth propagation](chain-growth-propagation.md).[chain transfer, C00963, definition and subsequent-polymerization example; source recommendation p. 1094](iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry.md) The number of reactive carriers can survive even though the identity of the growing chain changes. If the new carrier successfully starts a new chain, $C$ increases; a fixed active-carrier count therefore does not imply a fixed [polymer](polymer.md) [molecule](molecule.md) count. Whether transfer is irreversible, reversible, or involves an already existing chain matters for the final bookkeeping.
