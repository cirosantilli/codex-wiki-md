# Kusch-Foley magnetic moment experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Electron magnetic moment](electron-magnetic-moment.md)

[Polykarp Kusch](polykarp-kusch.md) and [Henry Michael Foley](henry-michael-foley.md) compared [hyperfine structure](hyperfine-structure.md) resonance frequencies for different electronic states of [gallium](gallium.md), [indium](indium.md) and [sodium](sodium.md). Their 1948 paper reports mutually consistent departures from the ratios expected with orbital factor $1$ and spin factor $2$. Comparing several states constrained the alternative explanation of perturbed atomic levels.[Abstract: atomic states, frequency comparisons and interpretation](the-magnetic-moment-of-the-electron-by-polykarp-kusch-and-henry-michael-foley-1948.md)

The [Rabi molecular beam magnetic resonance experiment](rabi-molecular-beam-magnetic-resonance-experiment.md) explains the readout principle: a resonant transition changes an atom's magnetic response, and hence its transmitted beam signal. Kusch describes rapidly interchangeable ovens, several [radio frequency](radio-frequency.md) oscillators and careful field homogenization. Measurements taken during monotonic field drift were reduced to a common field.[pp. 303–304: resonance analysis, ovens, oscillators and field drift](the-magnetic-moment-of-the-electron-by-polykarp-kusch.md)

**[Figure 16](#image-compare-differently-weighted-atomic-magnetic-moments). Compare differently weighted atomic magnetic moments**. Original functional diagram of the [Kusch-Foley magnetic moment experiment](kusch-foley-magnetic-moment-experiment.md) and a separate ideal [LS coupling](ls-coupling.md) calculation. The upper flow identifies the functions of beam preparation, resonance readout and atomic modeling; it is not a surviving apparatus drawing. The lower weights are calculated for $L=1$, $S=1/2$, $g_L=1$ and chosen $g_s=2.0024$, without nuclear [spin](spin.md). The frequencies in the teaching model cannot be substituted directly for measured hyperfine lines.

Real [hyperfine structure](hyperfine-structure.md) lines are not simply bare electronic $g_J$ times field. Nuclear [magnetic moments](magnetic-dipole-moment.md), known interaction constants and nonlinear field dependence enter the inference.[pp. 303–305: hyperfine modeling and atomic-state perturbations](the-magnetic-moment-of-the-electron-by-polykarp-kusch.md) Field homogeneity controls line broadening; alternating species addresses drift. Neither control removes an error in the atomic model. The beauty of the comparison is the separation of two problems: cancelling a shared calibration scale and testing whether several physical systems imply the same [electron spin g-factor](electron-spin-g-factor.md).
