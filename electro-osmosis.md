# Electro-osmosis

↑ **Parent:** [Electric double layer](electric-double-layer.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro-osmosis)

Electro-osmosis is fluid motion driven by an applied [electric field](electric-field-split.md) acting on mobile [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) near a surface. A charged interface attracts a diffuse population of [counterions](counterion.md) in an [electric double layer](electric-double-layer.md). A field component tangent to the interface exerts a force density $\rho_e\mathbf E$ on that mobile charge; momentum transfer to the surrounding [solvent](solvent.md) drives flow opposed by [viscous stress](viscous-stress-tensor.md). In a capillary [electrophoresis](electrophoresis.md) measurement, this flow advects the sample as well as its electrical drift relative to the fluid. Locally, the measured axial [velocity](velocity.md) is $v_{\mathrm{observed}}=u_{\mathrm{eo}}+\mu_{\mathrm{ep}}E$, where $u_{\mathrm{eo}}$ is the electroosmotic fluid velocity and $\mu_{\mathrm{ep}}$ is the [electrophoretic mobility](electrophoretic-mobility.md).
