# One-Mile Telescope

↑ **Parent:** [Aperture synthesis](aperture-synthesis.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-Mile_Telescope)

The One-Mile Telescope used three steerable 60-foot dishes: two fixed, one rail-mounted. The movable–fixed pairs supplied two baselines simultaneously. Twelve-hour tracks at successive rail positions sampled projected spacings. Phase-switching receivers measured in-phase and quadrature correlations; adjustable delay and phase rotation compensated tracking.[Elsmore et al. (1966), sections 1–2, pp. 87–92, Figures 1–2](the-operation-of-the-cambridge-one-mile-diameter-radio-telescope-by-bruce-elsmore-and-others.md)

The readout was correlation versus time. Reference-phase subtraction should leave constant point-source phase; position offsets produce predictable variation. A source near the field's declination supplies a collimation check. Structure, instrumental error and sampling artefacts need separating.[Elsmore et al. (1966), section 4(a), p. 93; Appendix, p. 95](the-operation-of-the-cambridge-one-mile-diameter-radio-telescope-by-bruce-elsmore-and-others.md)
