# Planet-star radius estimate in the Rayleigh-Jeans limit

↑ **Parent:** [Thermal eclipse depth](thermal-eclipse-depth.md)

For [blackbody](blackbody.md) star and planet observed at one common [wavelength](wavelength.md) sufficiently long for the [Rayleigh-Jeans law](rayleigh-jeans-law.md) to hold for both,

$$
f_\lambda=\frac{F_{p,\lambda}}{F_{*,\lambda}}=\left(\frac{R_p}{R_*}\right)^2\frac{T_p}{T_*},\qquad R_p=R_*\sqrt{f_\lambda T_*/T_p}.
$$

This follows by substituting $B_\lambda=2ck_BT/\lambda^4$ in the [thermal eclipse depth](thermal-eclipse-depth.md). A [radiative flux](radiative-flux.md) ratio alone does not determine the [radius](radius.md) without a planetary [brightness temperature](brightness-temperature.md) and a stellar [temperature](temperature.md). Spectral peaks interpreted with [Wien's displacement law](wien-s-displacement-law.md) must refer to $F_\lambda$, not $F_\nu$ or $\lambda F_\lambda$.
