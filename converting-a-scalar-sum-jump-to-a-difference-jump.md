# Converting a scalar sum jump to a difference jump

↑ **Parent:** [Riemann-Hilbert problem](riemann-hilbert-problem.md)

A [Cauchy integral](cauchy-transform.md) solves a prescribed boundary difference, not a prescribed sum. If $W_++W_-=g$ on $(0,c)$, choose $\rho(z)=\sqrt{z(z-c)}\sim z$ at infinity. Its [boundary values](boundary-value.md) satisfy $\rho_-=-\rho_+$, so $V=W/\rho$ has difference $V_+-V_-=g/\rho_+$. Thus $W=\rho[C(g/\rho_+)+H]$, where $C$ is the [Cauchy integral](cauchy-transform.md) with coefficient $1/(2\pi i)$ and $H$ has no jump. Endpoint integrability and normalization at infinity determine permissible regularizations and homogeneous terms. Simply using $C(g)$ for a sum jump is incorrect.
