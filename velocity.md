# Velocity

↑ **Parent:** [Position](position.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocity)

[Velocity](velocity.md) is the [vector](vector.md) giving the rate of change of [position](position.md) with [time](time-in-physics.md), measured in a specified [reference frame](reference-frame.md). For a differentiable path $\mathbf r(t)$, its instantaneous [velocity](velocity.md) and its average [velocity](velocity.md) between two [times](time-in-physics.md) are

$$
\mathbf v(t)=\frac{d\mathbf r}{dt},\qquad \mathbf v_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{\mathbf r(t_2)-\mathbf r(t_1)}{t_2-t_1},\qquad t_2>t_1.
$$

The [displacement](displacement.md) in the numerator retains direction. In [Cartesian coordinates](cartesian-coordinate-system.md), each [vector component](coordinate-linear-algebra.md) is a [derivative](derivative.md), for example $v_x=dx/dt$; its sign tells whether the corresponding coordinate is increasing or decreasing. The [SI](international-system-of-units.md) unit of [velocity](velocity.md) is $\mathrm{m/s}$. [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, University Physics Volume 1, section 3.2: instantaneous velocity and speed](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/3-2-instantaneous-velocity-and-speed)

For a concrete reversal, let $x(t)=(1\,\mathrm m)+(1\,\mathrm{m/s^2})t^2-(2\,\mathrm{m/s})t$ for $0\le t\le2\,\mathrm s$. Its signed [velocity](velocity.md) is $v_x(t)=(2\,\mathrm{m/s^2})t-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$: at $t=0$ it is $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, at $t=1\,\mathrm s$ it is zero, and at $t=2\,\mathrm s$ it is $+2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The opposite endpoint [velocities](velocity.md) describe opposite directions, although the endpoint [speeds](speed.md) agree. The [position](position.md) moves from $1\,\mathrm m$ to $0$ and back to $1\,\mathrm m$.

Recovering [displacement](displacement.md) from a measured [velocity](velocity.md) is an [integration](integral.md) problem: $\mathbf r(t_2)-\mathbf r(t_1)=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\mathbf v(t)\,dt$, with the [integral](integral.md) taken component by component. In the reversal example, $\int_0^{2\,\mathrm s}v_x(t)\,dt=0$: the negative and positive contributions cancel. This distinction is useful in navigation, where the final [position](position.md) depends on direction as well as [speed](speed.md).
