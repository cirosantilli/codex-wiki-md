# Thermal equilibrium

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_equilibrium)

Two internally equilibrated systems are in thermal equilibrium if bringing them into contact that permits only [heat](heat-split.md) exchange causes no net [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md). In ordinary nonrelativistic [thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md) without appreciable gravitational temperature shifts, their [thermodynamic temperatures](thermodynamic-temperature.md) are equal. Merely preventing [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) with insulation does not establish [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md): two insulated bodies can have different [temperatures](temperature-split.md). [Samuel J. Ling, William Moebs and Jeff Sanny, OpenStax University Physics Volume 2, section 1.1](university-physics-volume-2-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others/1-1-temperature-and-thermal-equilibrium.md)

A [thermometer](thermometer.md) illustrates the definition. Its own [temperature](temperature-split.md) settles to the object's [temperature](temperature-split.md) after thermal contact, provided its effect on the object is negligible. The existing [zeroth law of thermodynamics](zeroth-law-of-thermodynamics.md) makes this comparison transitive: two objects separately in [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md) with the same [thermometer](thermometer.md) are in [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md) with each other. This lets one calibrated scale compare different materials.

Equal [temperatures](temperature-split.md) do not imply equal [internal energies](internal-energy-split.md) or equal [heat capacities](heat-capacity.md). Nor does [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md) by itself imply [mechanical equilibrium](mechanical-equilibrium.md) or [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md); these require additional allowed exchanges. There is also a distinction between no net exchange in [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md) and a constant [temperature](temperature-split.md) maintained by equal incoming and outgoing [heat](heat-split.md). The latter can be a [steady state](steady-state.md) with ongoing [entropy production](entropy-production.md). Microscopic exchanges can continue at [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md), but their opposing contributions balance on average.

Historically, OpenStax credits the name “zeroth law” to Ralph Fowler in the 1930s: the already numbered laws needed a logically earlier statement about [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md). [Ling, Moebs and Sanny, section 1.1: naming the zeroth law](university-physics-volume-2-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others/1-1-temperature-and-thermal-equilibrium.md)
