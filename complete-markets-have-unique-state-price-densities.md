# Complete markets have unique state-price densities

↑ **Parent:** [Complete market](complete-market.md)

In a one-period [complete market](complete-market.md) with a strictly positive [numéraire](numeraire.md), at most one positive [state-price density](state-price-density.md) prices every traded asset. Suppose $\rho,\rho'$ do so. For each event $A$, replicate the [contingent claim](contingent-claim.md) $S_1^{(0)}\mathbf1_A$. Linearity of the pricing equations gives

$$
\mathbb E[(\rho-\rho')S_1^{(0)}\mathbf1_A]=0.
$$

Both weighted densities are integrable because they price the [numéraire](numeraire.md). An integrable random variable whose integral over every event is zero vanishes almost surely: take the events where it is positive and negative. Therefore $(\rho-\rho')S_1^{(0)}=0$, and strict positivity of the [numéraire](numeraire.md) gives $\rho=\rho'$. This argument avoids assuming that the unweighted densities themselves are integrable.
