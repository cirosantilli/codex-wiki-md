# Automorphism

↑ **Parent:** [Morphism](morphism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphism)

An [automorphism](automorphism.md) is an [isomorphism](isomorphism.md) $f:X\to X$: a structure-preserving self-map that can be undone by another structure-preserving self-map. Equivalently, it is an invertible [endomorphism](endomorphism.md). Its inverse satisfies

$$
f^{-1}\circ f=f\circ f^{-1}=1_X,
$$

where $1_X$ is the [identity morphism](identity-morphism.md). Both maps must be [morphisms](morphism.md) in the chosen [category](category-split.md). For ordinary [groups](group-split.md), [rings](ring.md), and [vector spaces](vector-space-split.md) over a fixed [field](field.md), a [bijective](bijection.md) [morphism](morphism.md) automatically has a compatible inverse. For [topological spaces](topological-space.md), an [automorphism](automorphism.md) is a [homeomorphism](homeomorphism.md) from the space to itself: a [continuous](continuous-function.md) [bijection](bijection.md) needs a [continuous](continuous-function.md) inverse as well. See [Riehl, Category Theory in Context, Definition 1.1.10 and Example 1.1.11](https://emilyriehl.github.io/files/context.pdf).

For example, $T(x,y)=(x+y,y)$ is an [automorphism](automorphism.md) of the [vector space](vector-space-split.md) $\mathbb R^2$ over the [real numbers](real-number.md), with inverse $T^{-1}(u,v)=(u-v,v)$. Both are [linear maps](linear-map.md). The [linear endomorphism](linear-endomorphism.md) $P(x,y)=(x,0)$ from [endomorphism](endomorphism.md) preserves [addition](addition.md) and [scalar multiplication](scalar-multiplication.md) too, but discards a coordinate and cannot be undone. Preserving the structure and preserving enough information for reversal are separate requirements.

The [automorphisms](automorphism.md) of a fixed object in a [locally small category](locally-small-category.md) form a [group](group-split.md) under [composition in a category](composition-in-a-category.md): the [identity morphism](identity-morphism.md) is the [identity element](identity-element.md), and the [inverse element](inverse-element.md) of a composite reverses the order of its factors. This makes reversible internal changes calculable. For a [group](group-split.md), the resulting [automorphism group](automorphism-group.md) records which relabelings preserve all products; the examples in [group automorphism](group-automorphism.md) show how restrictive that condition can be.
