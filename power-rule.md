# Power rule

↑ **Parent:** [Derivative](derivative.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_rule)

For a real exponent $p$ and $x>0$, write $x^p=\exp(p\ln x)$. The [chain rule](chain-rule.md) and the [derivative](derivative.md) of the [natural logarithm](natural-logarithm.md) give $d(x^p)/dx=px^{p-1}$. For a nonnegative integer exponent this agrees with polynomial differentiation and extends to all real $x$. Reversing differentiation gives $\int x^p\,dx=x^{p+1}/(p+1)+C$ for $p\ne-1$ on a suitable real branch. The exceptional exponent $p=-1$ gives $\ln|x|+C$ on either half-line.
