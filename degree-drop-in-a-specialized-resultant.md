# Degree drop in a specialized resultant

↑ **Parent:** [Eliminating a variable with a resultant](eliminating-a-variable-with-a-resultant.md)

The actual-degree condition in the [resultant](resultant.md) theorem matters when [coefficients](coefficient.md) depend on a parameter. Take

$$
f_t(x)=tx+1,\qquad g_t(x)=tx+2.
$$

The nominal degree-one [Sylvester matrix](sylvester-matrix.md) is $\begin{pmatrix}t&1\\t&2\end{pmatrix}$, whose [determinant](determinant.md) is $t$. Its specialization at $t=0$ therefore vanishes. But the specialized [polynomials](polynomial-split.md) are the nonzero constants $1$ and $2$, which have no [common root](common-root.md) at all.

For $t\ne0$, the [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) are $-1/t$ and $-2/t$; neither has a finite [limit](limit-of-a-function.md) as $t\to0$. Both [leading coefficients](leading-coefficient-of-a-polynomial.md) vanish at the exceptional parameter. The fixed-size [determinant](determinant.md) has retained the original degree format even though the specialized [polynomial degrees](degree-of-a-polynomial.md) have dropped. Recompute using the actual specialized [polynomials](polynomial-split.md) and check the original equations before accepting a zero of a parameter-dependent [resultant](resultant.md) as a solution.
