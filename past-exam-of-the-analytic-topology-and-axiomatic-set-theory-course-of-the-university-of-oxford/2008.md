↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/1/a)

<h6 id="a/1/a/i/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a/1/a/i)

A [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space) is [separable](../topology.md#separable-topological-space) if it has a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [dense subset](../topology.md#dense-set). A [subset](../set.md#subset) $D$ is [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) when its [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology) is the whole [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space), equivalently when every nonempty [open set](../topology.md#open-set) intersects $D$. [Countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) here permits a [finite set](../set.md#finite-set), so finite [topological spaces](../topology.md#topological-space) and the empty [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space) fit the same definition.

<h5 id="a/1/a/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/1/a)

<h6 id="a/1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a/1/a/ii)

A [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space) is [Lindelöf](../topology.md#lindelof-space) if every [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover) has a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) subcover. We also prove the requested equivalence for [metrizable spaces](../mathematics.md#metrizable-space) here, after the two source definition leaves.

Choose a compatible [metric](../topological-analysis.md#metric) $d$. First suppose that $D$ is a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [dense subset](../topology.md#dense-set). The balls $B(d_0,q)$ with centers $d_0\in D$ and positive rational radii $q$ form a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [topological basis](../topology.md#basis-of-a-topology). Indeed, if $x\in U$ and $B(x,\epsilon)\subset U$, choose $d_0\in D$ with $d(x,d_0)<\epsilon/3$ and a rational $q$ between $d(x,d_0)$ and $\epsilon-d(x,d_0)$. The [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) gives $x\in B(d_0,q)\subset U$.

For any [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover), retain the [topological basis](../topology.md#basis-of-a-topology) elements which are contained in some member of the cover, and choose one covering member for each such [topological basis](../topology.md#basis-of-a-topology) element. There are only countably many choices, and the resulting subfamily covers the [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space), since each point has a [topological basis](../topology.md#basis-of-a-topology) [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) inside a covering member. Thus a [separable](../topology.md#separable-topological-space) [metrizable space](../mathematics.md#metrizable-space) is [Lindelöf](../topology.md#lindelof-space).

Conversely, suppose the [metric space](../topological-analysis.md#metric-space) is [Lindelöf](../topology.md#lindelof-space). For each positive [integer](../integer.md) $n$, the balls $B(x,1/n)$ cover it, so select a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) subcover with centers in a [countable set](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) $D_n$. The [union](../set.md#set-union) $D=\bigcup_{n\ge1}D_n$ is [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) and [dense](../topology.md#dense-set): if $B(x,\epsilon)$ is a nonempty [open](../topology.md#open-set) ball, choose $n$ with $1/n<\epsilon$. One of the selected radius-$1/n$ balls contains $x$, so its center lies in $D\cap B(x,\epsilon)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{metrizable: separable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{Lindelöf}.}
$$

The [metric](../topological-analysis.md#metric) hypothesis is used in producing uniform small-ball covers and a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) ball [topological basis](../topology.md#basis-of-a-topology); the equivalence need not hold for general [topological spaces](../topology.md#topological-space).

<h4 id="a/1/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/1/b)

Choose [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) [sets](../set.md) $D_X\subseteq X$ and $D_Y\subseteq Y$. Their [Cartesian product](../set-theory.md#cartesian-product) is [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set). Every nonempty basic [open set](../topology.md#open-set) of the [product topology](../geometry-and-topology.md#product-topology) has the form $U\times V$ with $U,V$ nonempty [open](../topology.md#open-set) in their respective [topological spaces](../topology.md#topological-space). Density gives $d_X\in D_X\cap U$ and $d_Y\in D_Y\cap V$, hence $(d_X,d_Y)\in(D_X\times D_Y)\cap(U\times V)$.

Every nonempty [open set](../topology.md#open-set) in the product contains such a basic [open set](../topology.md#open-set). Thus $D_X\times D_Y$ is [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) in $X\times Y$, proving **[topological separability](../topology.md#separable-topological-space) of the product**. If one factor is empty the product is empty and the conclusion holds directly.

<h4 id="a/1/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/1/c)

Since $D$ is a [discrete subset](../topology.md#discrete-subset) in the [subspace topology](../topology.md#subspace-topology), for each $d\in D$ choose an [open set](../topology.md#open-set) $U_d\subseteq Z$ with $U_d\cap D=\{d\}$. Closedness of $D$ makes $Z\setminus D$ [open](../topology.md#open-set). The family

$$
\{Z\setminus D\}\cup\{U_d:d\in D\}
$$

is therefore an [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover) of $Z$. A [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) subcover exists by the [Lindelöf](../topology.md#lindelof-space) property. Each point $d$ can be covered only by its own $U_d$ among this family: the [complement](../set.md#complement-of-a-set) misses all of $D$, and every other $U_e$ meets $D$ only at $e$. Hence the [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) subcover must include $U_d$ for every $d\in D$. This forces

$$
\boxed{D\text{ is countable}.}
$$

Both closedness and discreteness matter: they provide the [open](../topology.md#open-set) [complement](../set.md#complement-of-a-set) and the single-point traces, respectively.

<h4 id="a/1/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#a/1/d)

The [lower limit topology](../topology.md#lower-limit-topology) on the [real line](../real-analysis.md#real-line) is the [lower limit topology](../topology.md#lower-limit-topology). Every nonempty basic interval $[a,b)$ contains a rational number, so the rational numbers are a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [dense subset](../topology.md#dense-set). By the preceding product argument, $\mathbb Q^2$ is [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) in the square, proving its [topological separability](../topology.md#separable-topological-space).

Consider the uncountable anti-diagonal

$$
D=\{(t,-t):t\in\mathbb R\}.
$$

It is a [discrete subset](../topology.md#discrete-subset): the basic rectangle $[t,t+\epsilon)\times[-t,-t+\epsilon)$ intersects $D$ only at $(t,-t)$, because both coordinates have increased weakly and their sum can remain zero only if neither increases.

It is also [closed](../topology.md#closed-set). At a point $(u,v)$ off the anti-diagonal, $u+v\ne0$. If this sum is positive, every sufficiently small lower-limit rectangle based at $(u,v)$ still has positive coordinate sum. If it is negative, choose $\epsilon>0$ with $2\epsilon<-(u+v)$; then $[u,u+\epsilon)\times[v,v+\epsilon)$ has negative coordinate sum throughout. These [neighborhoods](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) avoid $D$, so its [complement](../set.md#complement-of-a-set) is [open](../topology.md#open-set).

If the square were [Lindelöf](../topology.md#lindelof-space), its [closed discrete subset](../topology.md#closed-discrete-subset) $D$ would be [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) by part (c), contradicting its [bijection](../function.md#bijection) with $\mathbb R$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{the Sorgenfrey plane is separable but not Lindelöf}.}
$$

<h4 id="a/2/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/2/a)

For each point which belongs to at least one $U_t$, its membership-index [set](../set.md) is a nonempty [subset](../set.md#subset) of $[0,1]$, so has an infimum in $[0,1]$. Assigning the value one to the other points therefore defines $f:X\to[0,1]$ everywhere.

For $0<r\le1$,

$$
\{x:f(x)<r\}=\bigcup_{t\in D,\ t<r}U_t.
$$

An index $t<r$ gives $f(x)\le t<r$. Conversely if the infimum is below $r$, some membership index must be below $r$. Thus this strict sublevel [set](../set.md) is [open](../topology.md#open-set).

For $0\le r<1$ we claim

$$
\{x:f(x)>r\}=\bigcup_{t\in D,\ t>r}(X\setminus\overline{U_t}).
$$

If $x\notin\overline{U_t}$ and $t>r$, then $x$ cannot belong to any $U_s$ with $s\le t$, since $U_s\subseteq U_t$ for $s<t$. Hence $f(x)\ge t>r$. Conversely, if $f(x)>r$, density of $D$ supplies $r<t<u<f(x)$ with $t,u\in D$. Since $x\notin U_u$ and $\overline{U_t}\subseteq U_u$, one has $x\notin\overline{U_t}$. This proves the identity, and its right-hand side is [open](../topology.md#open-set).

The remaining sublevel and superlevel [sets](../set.md), for thresholds outside these ranges, are either empty or all of $X$. Preimages of [open](../topology.md#open-set) rays are consequently [open](../topology.md#open-set), and these rays generate the usual real [topology](../topology.md). Therefore **$f$ is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function)**. The [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology) inclusion is what supplies openness of the strict superlevel [sets](../set.md); mere nesting would not suffice.

<h4 id="a/2/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/2/b)

[Urysohn's lemma](../topology.md#urysohn-s-lemma) states that, in a [normal space](../topology.md#normal-space), disjoint [closed](../topology.md#closed-set) [subsets](../set.md#subset) $A,B$ can be separated by a [continuous function](../calculus.md#continuous-function) $u:X\to[0,1]$ with $u=0$ on $A$ and $u=1$ on $B$.

First use the shrinking consequence of the [separation](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-specification) property of a [normal space](../topology.md#normal-space): if a [closed set](../topology.md#closed-set) $F$ lies in an [open set](../topology.md#open-set) $G$, there is an [open](../topology.md#open-set) $W$ with $F\subseteq W\subseteq\overline W\subseteq G$. To prove it, separate the disjoint [closed sets](../topology.md#closed-set) $F$ and $X\setminus G$ by disjoint [open](../topology.md#open-set) [neighborhoods](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) $W,V$. Then $\overline W\subseteq X\setminus V\subseteq G$.

Let $D$ be the dyadic rationals in $[0,1]$. [Set](../set.md) $U_1=X\setminus B$, and shrink around $A$ to choose $U_0$ with $A\subseteq U_0\subseteq\overline{U_0}\subseteq U_1$. At each subsequent dyadic stage, insert between each adjacent pair $s<t$ an [open set](../topology.md#open-set) $U_{(s+t)/2}$ such that

$$
\overline{U_s}\subseteq U_{(s+t)/2}\subseteq\overline{U_{(s+t)/2}}\subseteq U_t.
$$

The shrinking lemma applies because the first [set](../set.md) is [closed](../topology.md#closed-set) and contained in the last [open set](../topology.md#open-set). Induction gives $\overline{U_s}\subseteq U_t$ for all dyadic $s<t$. Part (a) now constructs a [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) $u$. Each point of $A$ lies in $U_0$, so has value zero. Each point of $B$ lies in none of the $U_t$, so has value one. This proves the lemma.

For the embedding, let $I=C(X,[0,1])$, a [set](../set.md) of [continuous functions](../calculus.md#continuous-function), and define the evaluation map

$$
e:X\to\mathbb R^I,\qquad e(x)=(u(x))_{u\in I}.
$$

Every coordinate is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function), so $e$ is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) in the [product topology](../geometry-and-topology.md#product-topology). The [Hausdorff](../topology.md#hausdorff-space) assumption makes singletons [closed](../topology.md#closed-set). For distinct points, [Urysohn's lemma](../topology.md#urysohn-s-lemma) applied to their singletons gives a coordinate with different values, so $e$ is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function).

To check the inverse [topology](../topology.md), take $x\in O$ with $O$ [open](../topology.md#open-set). Apply the lemma to $\{x\}$ and $X\setminus O$, choosing a coordinate $u$ with $u(x)=1$ and $u=0$ outside $O$. Then

$$
x\in u^{-1}((1/2,3/2))\subseteq O.
$$

This is the preimage of an [open](../topology.md#open-set) coordinate cylinder under $e$. Thus every original [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) is detected by coordinate [neighborhoods](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics), and $e^{-1}:e[X]\to X$ is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function). **The evaluation map is a [topological embedding](../topology.md#topological-embedding).**

<h4 id="a/2/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/2/c)

Let $\mathcal B$ be a [topological basis](../topology.md#basis-of-a-topology) consisting of [clopen sets](../topology.md#clopen-set). For $U\in\mathcal B$ its [characteristic function](../characteristic-function.md) $\chi_U:X\to\{0,1\}$ is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function), since the preimages of both singleton values are [open](../topology.md#open-set). Define

$$
e:X\to\{0,1\}^{\mathcal B},\qquad e(x)=(\chi_U(x))_{U\in\mathcal B}.
$$

The coordinate criterion for [product topology](../geometry-and-topology.md#product-topology) makes this map [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function). If $x\ne y$, the $T_0$ property gives an [open set](../topology.md#open-set) containing one and not the other. A [topological basis](../topology.md#basis-of-a-topology) element contained in that [open set](../topology.md#open-set) still distinguishes them, so their corresponding characteristic coordinates differ. Therefore $e$ is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function).

Finally, for each [topological basis](../topology.md#basis-of-a-topology) element $U$,

$$
U=e^{-1}(\{z:z_U=1\}).
$$

The right-hand side is the preimage of an [open](../topology.md#open-set) cylinder in the product of discrete two-point [topological spaces](../topology.md#topological-space). These cylinders recover a [topological basis](../topology.md#basis-of-a-topology) for the [topology](../topology.md) of $X$, proving [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) of the inverse onto the image. Hence

$$
\boxed{X\text{ embeds homeomorphically into }\{0,1\}^{\mathcal B}.}
$$

<h5 id="a/3/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/3/a)

A [Hausdorff compactification](../topology.md#hausdorff-compactification) of $X$ is a pair $(h,Y)$ in which $Y$ is [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) [Hausdorff](../topology.md#hausdorff-space) and $h:X\to Y$ is a [topological embedding](../topology.md#topological-embedding) with [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) image.

For a locally [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) [Hausdorff](../topology.md#hausdorff-space) noncompact [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space), form $X^*=X\cup\{\infty\}$ with one new point. Keep all [open](../topology.md#open-set) [subsets](../set.md#subset) of $X$, and take the [neighborhoods](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) of infinity to contain [sets](../set.md)

$$
\{\infty\}\cup(X\setminus K),\qquad K\subseteq X\text{ compact}.
$$

[Compact subsets](../topology.md#compact-space) of a [Hausdorff space](../topology.md#hausdorff-space) are [closed](../topology.md#closed-set). Finite [unions](../set.md#set-union) of [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) [sets](../set.md) are [compact](../topology.md#compact-space), so finite [intersections](../set.md#set-intersection) of these basic [neighborhoods](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) are again of the same form; their [intersections](../set.md#set-intersection) with ordinary [open sets](../topology.md#open-set) are [open](../topology.md#open-set) in $X$. This specifies a [topology](../topology.md) restricting to the original one on $X$.

It is [compact](../topology.md#compact-space): from an [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover) choose a member containing infinity. Its [complement](../set.md#complement-of-a-set) is contained in some [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) $K$, and finitely many more members cover that [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) remainder. It is [Hausdorff](../topology.md#hausdorff-space): two points of $X$ are separated as before; to separate $x\in X$ from infinity, choose an [open](../topology.md#open-set) [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) $U$ of $x$ lying inside a [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) $K$. The [open set](../topology.md#open-set) $U$ and the infinity [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) $X^*\setminus K$ are disjoint.

The inclusion of $X$ is an embedding. Every basic [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) of infinity meets $X$, because otherwise its [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) [complement](../set.md#complement-of-a-set) $K$ would equal all of $X$, contrary to noncompactness. Thus $X$ is [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) in $X^*$. This proves that **$X^*$ is the [one-point compactification](../topology.md#alexandroff-extension)**.

<h4 id="a/3/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/3/b)

First prove the suggested density fact. If $Z$ is [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) in $Y$, $V$ is [open](../topology.md#open-set) and $y\in V$, then every [open](../topology.md#open-set) [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) $W$ of $y$ has $W\cap V$ nonempty [open](../topology.md#open-set). It meets $Z$, so $W$ meets $V\cap Z$. Hence

$$
V\subseteq\overline{V\cap Z}^{\,Y}.
$$

Identify $X$ with its [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) embedded image in the [hausdorff compactification](../topology.md#hausdorff-compactification). For $x\in X$, local compactness supplies an [open](../topology.md#open-set) [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) $U$ in $X$ and a [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) [set](../set.md) $K\subseteq X$ with $x\in U\subseteq K$. Choose an [open set](../topology.md#open-set) $V$ in $Y$ with $V\cap X=U$. The inclusion into $Y$ is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function), so $K$ is [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) in $Y$ and therefore [closed](../topology.md#closed-set), since $Y$ is [Hausdorff](../topology.md#hausdorff-space). The density fact now yields

$$
x\in V\subseteq\overline{V\cap X}^{\,Y}=\overline U^{\,Y}\subseteq K\subseteq X.
$$

Thus every point of the embedded $X$ has a $Y$-open [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) contained in $X$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{h[X]\text{ is open in }Y.}
$$

<h5 id="a/4/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/4/a)

A [proper filter](../set-theory.md#proper-filter) $\mathcal F$ on $X$ is a nonempty family of [subsets](../set.md#subset), [closed](../topology.md#closed-set) under finite [intersection](../set.md#set-intersection) and passage to [supersets](../set.md#superset), with $\varnothing\notin\mathcal F$. An [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter) is a maximal [proper filter](../set-theory.md#proper-filter), equivalently one which contains, for each $A\subseteq X$, exactly one of $A$ and $X\setminus A$. The [pushforward filter](../set-theory.md#pushforward-filter) is

$$
f_*\mathcal F=\{B\subseteq Y:f^{-1}[B]\in\mathcal F\}.
$$

[Inverse images](../set-theory.md#preimage) preserve [intersections](../set.md#set-intersection) and [complements](../set.md#complement-of-a-set). Thus it is a [proper filter](../set-theory.md#proper-filter), and the either-a-set-or-its-complement criterion shows it is an [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter) whenever $\mathcal F$ is one.

[Convergence of a filter](../set-theory.md#convergence-of-a-filter) $\mathcal F\to x$ means that every [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) of $x$ belongs to $\mathcal F$. A mapping is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) exactly when $\mathcal F\to x$ implies $f_*\mathcal F\to f(x)$ for all [proper filters](../set-theory.md#proper-filter) and points. The forward implication follows from [inverse images](../set-theory.md#preimage) of [neighborhoods](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics). For the converse apply the criterion to the [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) [proper filter](../set-theory.md#proper-filter) at $x$: each [inverse image](../set-theory.md#preimage) of a [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) of $f(x)$ must be a [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) of $x$, which is [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) at $x$.

A [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space) is [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) exactly when every [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter) converges to at least one point; equivalently every [proper filter](../set-theory.md#proper-filter) has a cluster point. For completeness, in a [compact space](../topology.md#compact-space) the [closed sets](../topology.md#closed-set) $\overline A$, $A\in\mathcal U$, have the [finite intersection property](../topology.md#finite-intersection-property), hence a common point $x$. Every [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) $N$ of $x$ belongs to the [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter). Indeed, if $N$ did not, choose an [open](../topology.md#open-set) $V$ with $x\in V\subseteq N$. Then $V$ would not belong either, so $X\setminus V$ would belong. That [set](../set.md) is [closed](../topology.md#closed-set) and excludes $x$, contradicting the choice of $x$ in every [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology). Conversely, if an [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover) has no finite subcover, its [closed](../topology.md#closed-set) [complements](../set.md#complement-of-a-set) generate a [proper filter](../set-theory.md#proper-filter). An [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter) extension cannot converge anywhere, because each point has a covering [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) whose [complement](../set.md#complement-of-a-set) belongs to that [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter). This proves the compactness criterion using the permitted extension principle.

Now suppose $b\in\overline{f[A]}$. The family

$$
\{A\cap f^{-1}[V]:V\text{ is an open neighborhood of }b\}
$$

is a [filter base](../set-theory.md#filter-base) of nonempty [sets](../set.md): [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology) guarantees each [intersection](../set.md#set-intersection) is nonempty, and finite [intersections](../set.md#set-intersection) correspond to [intersections](../set.md#set-intersection) of [neighborhoods](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics). Extend its generated [proper filter](../set-theory.md#proper-filter) to an [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter) $\mathcal H$. It contains $A$ and every $f^{-1}[V]$, so $f_*\mathcal H\to b$.

Conversely, if such an [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter) exists, each [neighborhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) $V$ of $b$ has $f^{-1}[V]\in\mathcal H$. Its [intersection](../set.md#set-intersection) with $A\in\mathcal H$ is nonempty. Thus $V$ meets $f[A]$, proving

$$
\boxed{b\in\overline{f[A]}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists\mathcal H\text{ ultrafilter}:A\in\mathcal H,\ f_*\mathcal H\to b.}
$$

This part includes the unnumbered [proper filter](../set-theory.md#proper-filter) definitions and criteria preceding the printed subparts.

<h4 id="a/4/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#a/4)

<h5 id="a/4/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/4/b)

For every [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter) $\mathcal H$ on $X$, its image is an [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter) on the [compact space](../topology.md#compact-space) $Y$, so it has a limit. That limit is unique because $Y$ is [Hausdorff](../topology.md#hausdorff-space): distinct limits would have disjoint [neighborhoods](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) both belonging to the same [proper filter](../set-theory.md#proper-filter). Therefore $\Lambda$ is well-defined. Since $f[X]$ is [dense](../topology.md#dense-set), every $b\in Y$ lies in $\overline{f[X]}$. Part (a), with $A=X$, supplies an [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter) mapping to $b$, proving [surjectivity](../algebra.md#surjective-function).

Suppose disjoint [subsets](../set.md#subset) have disjoint image [closures](../topology.md#closure-topology). If $\mathcal H\ne\mathcal K$, choose $A\in\mathcal H$ not in $\mathcal K$; the [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter) criterion gives $X\setminus A\in\mathcal K$. Part (a) gives

$$
\Lambda(\mathcal H)\in\overline{f[A]},\qquad
\Lambda(\mathcal K)\in\overline{f[X\setminus A]}.
$$

The two [closures](../topology.md#closure-topology) are disjoint by assumption, so the limits differ. Thus $\Lambda$ is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function).

Conversely, suppose $\Lambda$ is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function) and disjoint $A,B\subseteq X$ had a common point $b$ in their image [closures](../topology.md#closure-topology). Part (a) would supply [ultrafilters](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter) $\mathcal H,\mathcal K$ containing $A,B$ respectively, both with image limit $b$. They must be distinct, since a [proper filter](../set-theory.md#proper-filter) cannot contain the disjoint [sets](../set.md) $A$ and $B$ simultaneously. This contradicts [injectivity](../algebra.md#injective-function). Hence

$$
\boxed{\Lambda\text{ injective}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\overline{f[A]}\cap\overline{f[B]}=\varnothing\text{ whenever }A\cap B=\varnothing.}
$$

<h4 id="a/4/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#a/4)

<h5 id="a/4/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/4/c)

Use the [Stone-Čech extension property](../mathematical-physics.md#stone-cech-extension-property): a discrete [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space) $X$ embeds densely into a [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) [Hausdorff space](../topology.md#hausdorff-space) $\beta X$, and every [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) from $X$ to a [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) [Hausdorff space](../topology.md#hausdorff-space) extends continuously to $\beta X$. Apply it to the [characteristic function](../characteristic-function.md) $\chi_A:X\to\{0,1\}$ of an arbitrary [subset](../set.md#subset) $A$; discreteness makes it [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function). Its extension $\widetilde\chi_A$ has [clopen](../topology.md#clopen-set) fibers, and

$$
\overline A^{\,\beta X}=\widetilde\chi_A^{-1}(1).
$$

Indeed that fiber is [closed](../topology.md#closed-set) and contains $A$, while its openness and density of $X$ show that every point in the fiber is approached by points of $A$.

If $A$ and $B$ are disjoint, the extension of $\chi_A$ is one on $\overline A$ and zero on $\overline B$, so these [closures](../topology.md#closure-topology) are disjoint. The inclusion $X\to\beta X$ therefore satisfies the criterion from part (b), and its ultrafilter-limit map is both [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function) and [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function). Thus

$$
\boxed{\Upsilon(X)\longleftrightarrow\beta X,\qquad
\mathcal H\longmapsto\text{the unique limit of its image filter}.}
$$

This is a [bijection](../function.md#bijection) of underlying [sets](../set.md); identifying the compatible [topology](../topology.md) on the [ultrafilter](../set-theory.md#ultrafilter) [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space) would give the familiar stronger [homeomorphism](../topology.md#homeomorphism) statement, but is not needed for the requested conclusion.

<h5 id="b/5/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/5/i)

In first-order [set](../set.md) theory a [class in set theory](../set-theory.md#class-set-theory) is a definable collection, written schematically $\{x:\theta(x,\vec p)\}$ using a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) and [set](../set.md) parameters. A [class term](../set-theory.md#class-term) is an abbreviation for such a defining condition; it does not add a new kind of object to the [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) of [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory). A [class in set theory](../set-theory.md#class-set-theory) is proper if it is not the collection of elements of any [set](../set.md).

Define the [cumulative hierarchy](../set-theory.md#cumulative-hierarchy) by [transfinite recursion](../ordinal.md#transfinite-recursion):

$$
V_0=\varnothing,\qquad V_{\alpha+1}=\mathcal P(V_\alpha),\qquad
V_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}V_\alpha\quad(\lambda\text{ a nonzero limit ordinal}),
$$

and let $V=\{x:\exists\alpha\text{ ordinal }(x\in V_\alpha)\}$. Without [foundation](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-regularity) this denotes the well-founded part produced by the hierarchy; it must not simply be assumed to contain every ambient [set](../set.md). [Transfinite recursion](../ordinal.md#transfinite-recursion) on [ordinals](../ordinal.md) and the relevant [set](../set.md) constructions remain available in [ZF without foundation](../set-theory.md#zf-without-foundation).

Prove transitivity by [transfinite induction](../ordinal.md#transfinite-induction). The empty stage is transitive. Suppose $T=V_\alpha$ is transitive. Then each member of $T$ is a [subset](../set.md#subset) of $T$, so $T\subseteq\mathcal P(T)$. Also $\mathcal P(T)$ is transitive: if $u\in v\in\mathcal P(T)$, then $u\in T$ and, by transitivity of $T$, $u\subseteq T$, so $u\in\mathcal P(T)$. At a limit stage, a [union](../set.md#set-union) of [transitive sets](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) is transitive. This proves transitivity at every stage.

Finally transitivity of $V_\alpha$ itself implies that every member is a [subset](../set.md#subset) of $V_\alpha$, giving

$$
\boxed{V_\alpha\subseteq\mathcal P(V_\alpha)=V_{\alpha+1}.}
$$

These successive inclusions and the limit definition also imply $V_\alpha\subseteq V_\beta$ whenever $\alpha\le\beta$. The argument has not used [foundation](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-regularity). This leaf includes the unnumbered definitions preceding the source's three claims.

<h4 id="b/5/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/5/ii)

For every $x\in a$, the assumption $a\subseteq V$ supplies an [ordinal](../ordinal.md) stage containing $x$. Let $\alpha_x$ be the least such [ordinal](../ordinal.md). Leastness makes this a uniquely definable assignment, so ambient [replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) gives the [set](../set.md)

$$
S=\{\alpha_x:x\in a\}.
$$

Put $\beta=\sup S$, taking $\beta=0$ if $a$ is empty. Monotonicity of the hierarchy gives $x\in V_{\alpha_x}\subseteq V_\beta$ for every $x\in a$. Hence $a\subseteq V_\beta$, and therefore

$$
\boxed{a\in\mathcal P(V_\beta)=V_{\beta+1}\subseteq V.}
$$

This is a rank-bounding argument: a set-sized collection of elements from the hierarchy has its stages bounded by one [ordinal](../ordinal.md). No simultaneous arbitrary choices are required, since each least stage is uniquely specified.

<h4 id="b/5/iii">iii</h4>

Fix a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\theta(x,y,\vec p)$ with parameters in $V$, and a [set](../set.md) $a\in V$. Suppose $\langle V,\in\rangle$ satisfies that each $x\in a$ has a unique $y$ with $\theta(x,y,\vec p)$. Relativize every unbounded [quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) in $\theta$ to the definable [class in set theory](../set-theory.md#class-set-theory) $V$, obtaining an ambient [first-order formula](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-formula) $\theta^V$.

Transitivity of $V$ ensures that every member of $a$ belongs to $V$. The ambient relation

$$
y\in V\ \wedge\ \theta^V(x,y,\vec p)
$$

therefore defines a unique ambient [set](../set.md) $y$ for every $x\in a$. Apply ambient [replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) to obtain its range [set](../set.md) $b$. By construction every element of $b$ belongs to $V$, so part (ii) gives $b\in V$.

Membership in this [set](../set.md) $b$ is exactly the range relation required by [replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) inside the structure $\langle V,\in\rangle$. Since the [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) and parameters were arbitrary, **every instance of the [replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) scheme holds in the hierarchy [class in set theory](../set-theory.md#class-set-theory)**. The proof uses [formula relativization to a class](../set-theory.md#formula-relativization-to-a-class) and the [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology) property from part (ii), not the unsupported assertion that $V$ is the whole ambient universe.

A bounded [quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) has the form $\exists u\in v$ or $\forall u\in v$, abbreviating $\exists u(u\in v\wedge\cdots)$ or $\forall u(u\in v\to\cdots)$. A $\Sigma_0$ [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula), also called $\Delta_0$, is built from atomic membership and equality [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) using Boolean connectives and only bounded [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier). It has no unbounded [quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier).

For example $z\subseteq y$ abbreviates the bounded [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\forall u\in z\,(u\in y)$. [Quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) are bounded by an existing variable or parameter, not by an unrestricted [class term](../set-theory.md#class-term) such as the [power set](../set.md#power-set) of a parameter unless that [set](../set.md) has separately been supplied as a parameter.

<h5 id="b/6/i/b">b</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/6/i)

<h6 id="b/6/i/b/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/6/i/b)

A $\Sigma_1$ [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) has the form

$$
\boxed{\exists z_1\cdots\exists z_n\,\psi,\qquad\psi\text{ a }\Sigma_0\text{ formula},}
$$

up to the standard logical transformations defining the hierarchy. The initial existential [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) may be unbounded; all [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) inside the matrix are bounded. A finite tuple of witnesses can be encoded by one [set](../set.md) if desired, and [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) with no initial unbounded [quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) are included as the level-zero special case.

The dual $\Pi_1$ [class in set theory](../set-theory.md#class-set-theory) has an initial universal block with a bounded matrix. Negation interchanges these two classes, but does not in general preserve the existential [class in set theory](../set-theory.md#class-set-theory). Part (iii) supplies an explicit reason for the failure in this setting.

<h4 id="b/6/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/6/ii)

Use the strict-order convention appropriate to the membership relation. A bounded [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
TO(x)\ \equiv\;&(\forall u\in x)\,\neg(u\in u)\\
&\wedge(\forall u\in x)(\forall v\in x)(\forall w\in x)
\bigl((u\in v\wedge v\in w)\to u\in w\bigr)\\
&\wedge(\forall u\in x)(\forall v\in x)
\bigl(u=v\vee u\in v\vee v\in u\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

The first clause is irreflexivity, the second transitivity, and the third comparability of distinct elements. Irreflexivity and transitivity exclude $u\in v\in u$, so distinct elements cannot be ordered in both directions. These are exactly the axioms of a [strict total order](../set.md#strict-total-order) on the [set](../set.md) $x$, with $\in$ as its order relation.

Every displayed [quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) is bounded by $x$, so **$TO(x)$ is $\Sigma_0$**. The [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) does not assert that $x$ is transitive as a [set](../set.md), and no such additional assertion is needed to say that the restriction of membership to $x$ is a [total order](../set.md#total-order).

<h4 id="b/6/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/6/iii)

There are two ways for a [set](../set.md) to fail to be the [power set](../set.md#power-set) of $y$: it may contain an object which is not a [subset](../set.md#subset) of $y$, or omit a [subset](../set.md#subset) of $y$. A single existential [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) expressing these alternatives is

$$
\boxed{\phi(x,y)\equiv\exists z\Bigl[
\bigl(z\in x\wedge(\exists u\in z)\,u\notin y\bigr)
\vee\bigl(z\notin x\wedge(\forall u\in z)\,u\in y\bigr)\Bigr].}
$$

Its matrix is bounded, so this is $\Sigma_1$. Its negation is precisely the first-order power-set specification

$$
\forall z\bigl(z\in x\ \longleftrightarrow\ (\forall u\in z)\,u\in y\bigr).
$$

We prove the absoluteness fact needed to exclude a logically equivalent $\Sigma_1$ [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula). If $M\subseteq N$ are [transitive sets](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) and all parameters lie in $M$, bounded [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) have the same truth value in $\langle M,\in\rangle$ and $\langle N,\in\rangle$. Atomic equality and membership agree because both structures use actual membership. Boolean operations preserve agreement. In a bounded [quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) over $v\in M$, transitivity ensures that every element of $v$ already lies in $M$, so the two structures quantify over exactly the same elements of $v$. Induction on [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) proves the claim. It follows that $\Sigma_1$ [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) are upward absolute: witnesses in $M$ remain available in $N$, and their bounded matrix retains its truth value.

Now use a finite counterexample, so no existence of a transitive model of [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) is assumed. Let $M=4=\{0,1,2,3\}$, the [Finite von Neumann ordinal](../set-theory.md#finite-von-neumann-ordinal), and let $N=V_4$. These are [transitive sets](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) with $M\subseteq N$. [Set](../set.md) $y=2$ and $x=3$, both elements of $M$. Inside $M$, the [subsets](../set.md#subset) of $2$ which are available as elements of the domain are exactly $0,1,2$; the remaining domain element $3$ is not a [subset](../set.md#subset) of $2$. Thus $M$ satisfies the displayed power-set specification with $x=3$.

Inside $N$, however, the [set](../set.md) $\{1\}$ is also present and is a [subset](../set.md#subset) of $2$, while $\{1\}\notin3$. Thus the same specification is false in $N$. If $\neg\phi$ were logically equivalent to a $\Sigma_1$ [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula), its truth in $M$ would transfer upward to $N$, a contradiction. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\neg\phi\text{ is not logically equivalent to any }\Sigma_1\text{ formula}.}
$$

This proves the requested unrestricted logical non-equivalence. The finite structures are not being claimed to satisfy all of [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory); logical equivalence must hold in them as well as in any models of a stronger theory.

<h3 id="b/7">7</h3>

<h4 id="b/7/i">i</h4>

<h5 id="b/7/i/solution">Solution</h5>

[König theorem for cardinal numbers](../cardinal-number.md#konig-s-theorem-set-theory) states that if $\kappa_i<\lambda_i$ for each index $i\in I$, then

$$
\boxed{\sum_{i\in I}\kappa_i<\prod_{i\in I}\lambda_i.}
$$

Represent each [cardinal](../cardinal-number.md) by its [initial ordinal](../cardinal-number.md#initial-ordinal), let $A=\{(i,\alpha):i\in I,\alpha<\kappa_i\}$, and let $B=\prod_{i\in I}\lambda_i$.

First there is an [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) $A\to B$: encode $(i,\alpha)$ by the [function](../function.md) which is zero away from $i$ and takes value $\alpha+1$ at $i$. The nonzero coordinate identifies $i$, and its value identifies $\alpha$. It is valid because $\alpha+1\le\kappa_i<\lambda_i$; an index with $\kappa_i=0$ contributes no domain points.

For strictness suppose a surjection $F:A\to B$ existed. At coordinate $i$, the values $F(i,\alpha)(i)$ for $\alpha<\kappa_i$ occupy fewer than $\lambda_i$ possibilities. Choose $b_i$ as the least [ordinal](../ordinal.md) below $\lambda_i$ absent from that [set](../set.md). The resulting $b=(b_i)\in B$ differs from $F(i,\alpha)$ at coordinate $i$ for every $(i,\alpha)\in A$, contradicting [surjectivity](../algebra.md#surjective-function).

An [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) $B\to A$ would produce a surjection $A\to B$ by inverting on its image and assigning the zero [function](../function.md) elsewhere. It is therefore impossible. The [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) in the first direction and impossibility of the reverse give the strict [cardinal](../cardinal-number.md) inequality. This diagonal argument uses the canonical well-orders of the specified [cardinals](../cardinal-number.md) to choose missing coordinate values, rather than an unexplained arbitrary choice at that step.

<h4 id="b/7/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/7/ii)

For an infinite [cardinal](../cardinal-number.md) regarded as an [ordinal](../ordinal.md), its [cofinality](../cardinal-number.md#cofinality) $\theta=\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)$ is the least [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) of a [cofinal subset](../cardinal-number.md#cofinal-subset) of $\kappa$. Equivalently there is a family $(\alpha_i)_{i<\theta}$ of [ordinals](../ordinal.md) below $\kappa$ whose [supremum](../real-analysis.md#supremum) is $\kappa$, and no smaller indexed family has that property. Since an infinite [initial ordinal](../cardinal-number.md#initial-ordinal) is a [limit ordinal](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal), $\theta$ is infinite and in particular nonzero.

Use the diagonal mechanism of [König theorem for cardinal numbers](../cardinal-number.md#konig-s-theorem-set-theory) directly. Suppose $F:\kappa\to{}^{\theta}\kappa$ were [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function). For each $i<\theta$, the [set](../set.md)

$$
E_i=\{F(\alpha)(i):\alpha<\alpha_i\}
$$

has [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) at most $|\alpha_i|<\kappa$. Choose the least $b_i\in\kappa\setminus E_i$. The resulting [function](../function.md) $b:\theta\to\kappa$ is not in the range: for any $\alpha<\kappa$, [cofinality](../cardinal-number.md#cofinality) supplies $i$ with $\alpha<\alpha_i$, and then $b_i\ne F(\alpha)(i)$.

Thus no such surjection exists. Constant [functions](../function.md) provide an [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) $\kappa\to{}^{\theta}\kappa$, and an [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) in the reverse direction would give a surjection by the same inverse-and-default construction as in part (i). Consequently

$$
\boxed{\kappa^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}>\kappa.}
$$

This argument also handles $\kappa=\aleph_0$, when [cofinal](../cardinal-number.md#cofinal-subset) initial segments are finite; it does not incorrectly assume that smaller [cardinals](../cardinal-number.md) themselves must form a [cofinal](../cardinal-number.md#cofinal-subset) sequence in every infinite [cardinal](../cardinal-number.md).

<h4 id="b/7/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/7/iii)

In the usual arithmetic under the [axiom of choice](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-choice) of [cardinal numbers](../cardinal-number.md), take the singular strong-limit [cardinal](../cardinal-number.md)

$$
\kappa=\beth_\omega=\sup_{n<\omega}\beth_n,\qquad
\beth_0=\aleph_0,\quad\beth_{n+1}=2^{\beth_n}.
$$

[Cantor's theorem](../set.md#cantor-s-theorem) makes this sequence strictly increasing. It is [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) and [cofinal](../cardinal-number.md#cofinal-subset) in its [supremum](../real-analysis.md#supremum), so $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)\le\aleph_0$. No finite [subset](../set.md#subset) is [cofinal](../cardinal-number.md#cofinal-subset) in this [limit ordinal](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal), hence $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)=\aleph_0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{2^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}=2^{\aleph_0}=\beth_1<\beth_\omega=\kappa.}
$$

This construction needs no assumption such as the [Continuum hypothesis](../cardinal-number.md#continuum-hypothesis). The conventional use of [cardinals](../cardinal-number.md) here includes well-orderability of these [power sets](../set.md#power-set), supplied by the [axiom of choice](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-choice). It is a separate setting from the explicit failure of well-orderability hypothesized in question 8; one must not impose choice on that later hypothesis.

<h3 id="b/8">8</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/8/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#b/8)

<h5 id="b/8/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/8/i)

For a [set](../set.md) $M$, let $\operatorname{Def}(M)$ consist of the [subsets](../set.md#subset) of $M$ definable over the structure $\langle M,\in\rangle$ by a [first-order formula](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-formula) with finitely many parameters from $M$. It is a [set](../set.md) contained in $\mathcal P(M)$: satisfaction for a [set](../set.md) structure and the [set](../set.md) of [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) codes give a uniform [set](../set.md) construction of these definable [subsets](../set.md#subset). If $M$ is transitive, every $a\in M$ is one such [subset](../set.md#subset), defined over $M$ by $z\in a$, so $M\subseteq\operatorname{Def}(M)$.

Define the [constructible hierarchy](../definable-power-set.md#constructible-hierarchy) by [transfinite recursion](../ordinal.md#transfinite-recursion):

$$
L_0=\varnothing,\qquad L_{\alpha+1}=\operatorname{Def}(L_\alpha),\qquad
L_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}L_\alpha\quad(\lambda\text{ a nonzero limit ordinal}).
$$

Gödel's [constructible universe](../definable-power-set.md#constructible-universe) is the definable [class in set theory](../set-theory.md#class-set-theory)

$$
\boxed{L=\{x:\exists\alpha\text{ ordinal }(x\in L_\alpha)\}.}
$$

At successor stages only [subsets](../set.md#subset) definable over the previous stage are admitted, in contrast to the full power-set construction of the [cumulative hierarchy](../set-theory.md#cumulative-hierarchy). The stages are transitive and increasing, as permitted in the question, and their [union](../set.md#set-union) contains every [ordinal](../ordinal.md). Finite parameters are essential in the definition of $\operatorname{Def}$.

<h4 id="b/8/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#b/8)

<h5 id="b/8/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/8/ii)

Fix $a\in L$. In the ambient universe form the [set](../set.md)

$$
S=\{y\in\mathcal P(a):y\in L\}
$$

by [power set](../set.md#power-set) and [separation](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-specification), using definability of $L$. This is the collection which must serve as the [power set](../set.md#power-set) of $a$ inside $L$; it need not be the entire ambient [power set](../set.md#power-set).

For each $y\in S$, let $\alpha_y$ be the least [ordinal](../ordinal.md) with $y\in L_{\alpha_y}$. **Ambient [replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) is used to collect the [ordinals](../ordinal.md) $\alpha_y$ into a [set](../set.md).** Their [supremum](../real-analysis.md#supremum), enlarged if necessary to include a stage containing $a$, gives an [ordinal](../ordinal.md) $\beta$ such that $a\in L_\beta$ and every $y\in S$ belongs to $L_\beta$.

Because $L_\beta$ is transitive and $a\in L_\beta$, the bounded property $y\subseteq a$ for $y\in L_\beta$ has its actual meaning when evaluated in that structure: every element of $y$ already lies in $L_\beta$, and membership is unchanged. It follows that

$$
S=\{y\in L_\beta:\langle L_\beta,\in\rangle\models y\subseteq a\}.
$$

This is a definable [subset](../set.md#subset) of $L_\beta$ using the parameter $a$, so $S\in\operatorname{Def}(L_\beta)=L_{\beta+1}\subseteq L$. Every member of $L$ which is a [subset](../set.md#subset) of $a$ is in $S$, and every member of $S$ is such a [subset](../set.md#subset). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathcal P^{L}(a)=\mathcal P^{V}(a)\cap L\in L,}
$$

which is exactly the power-set axiom in $\langle L,\in\rangle$. The [replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) step is indispensable to this proof: definability over one stage requires first bounding all the constructible [subsets](../set.md#subset) by one common stage.

<h4 id="b/8/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#b/8)

<h5 id="b/8/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/8/iii)

Every constructible [set](../set.md) is well-orderable: the [constructible hierarchy](../definable-power-set.md#constructible-hierarchy) has a canonical well-order, obtained by ordering the stage of first appearance, [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) codes and the already ordered finite parameter lists. Its restriction to any [set](../set.md) belonging to $L$ is a well-order in $L$ and remains a well-order in the ambient universe. This does not imply that arbitrary ambient [sets](../set.md) are well-orderable.

If $a\in L$ is finite, all its [subsets](../set.md#subset) are finite combinations of its elements and hence constructible. Moreover the [finite set](../set.md#finite-set) of all those [subsets](../set.md#subset) is constructible by the [finite set](../set.md#finite-set) operations available in $L$. Thus the full ambient [power set](../set.md#power-set) $\mathcal P(a)$ belongs to $L$.

Conversely, suppose $a\in L$ is infinite and that its full ambient [power set](../set.md#power-set) belonged to $L$. A well-order of $a$ supplies an [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) $j:\omega\to a$ by taking its first countably many elements. The map

$$
\mathcal P(\omega)\to\mathcal P(a),\qquad u\longmapsto j[u]
$$

is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function). But $\mathcal P(a)\in L$ would make that [set](../set.md) well-orderable in the ambient universe, and pulling its well-order back along this [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) would well-order $\mathcal P(\omega)$. This contradicts the hypothesis. Hence

$$
\boxed{a\in L:\qquad\mathcal P^{V}(a)\in L\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\text{ is finite}.}
$$

The superscript emphasizes that the source's final claim concerns the ambient [power set](../set.md#power-set). The internal [power set](../set.md#power-set) $\mathcal P^L(a)$ exists in $L$ for every $a\in L$ by part (ii), including infinite ones.
