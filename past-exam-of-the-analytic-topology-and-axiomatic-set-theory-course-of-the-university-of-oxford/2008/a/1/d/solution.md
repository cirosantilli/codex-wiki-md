↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [lower limit topology](../../../../../lower-limit-topology.md) on the [real line](../../../../../real-line.md) is the [lower limit topology](../../../../../lower-limit-topology.md). Every nonempty basic interval $[a,b)$ contains a rational number, so the rational numbers are a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [dense subset](../../../../../dense-set.md). By the preceding product argument, $\mathbb Q^2$ is [dense](../../../../../dense-set.md) in the square, proving its [topological separability](../../../../../separable-topological-space.md).

Consider the uncountable anti-diagonal

$$
D=\{(t,-t):t\in\mathbb R\}.
$$

It is a [discrete subset](../../../../../discrete-subset.md): the basic rectangle $[t,t+\epsilon)\times[-t,-t+\epsilon)$ intersects $D$ only at $(t,-t)$, because both coordinates have increased weakly and their sum can remain zero only if neither increases.

It is also [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md). At a point $(u,v)$ off the anti-diagonal, $u+v\ne0$. If this sum is positive, every sufficiently small lower-limit rectangle based at $(u,v)$ still has positive coordinate sum. If it is negative, choose $\epsilon>0$ with $2\epsilon<-(u+v)$; then $[u,u+\epsilon)\times[v,v+\epsilon)$ has negative coordinate sum throughout. These [neighborhoods](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) avoid $D$, so its [complement](../../../../../complement-of-a-set.md) is [open](../../../../../open-set.md).

If the square were [Lindelöf](../../../../../lindelof-space.md), its [closed discrete subset](../../../../../closed-discrete-subset.md) $D$ would be [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) by part (c), contradicting its [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) with $\mathbb R$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{the Sorgenfrey plane is separable but not Lindelöf}.}
$$
