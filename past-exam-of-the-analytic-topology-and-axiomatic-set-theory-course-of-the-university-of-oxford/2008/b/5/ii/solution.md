<h1 id="b/5/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For every $x\in a$, the assumption $a\subseteq V$ supplies an [ordinal](../../../../../ordinal-split.md) stage containing $x$. Let $\alpha_x$ be the least such [ordinal](../../../../../ordinal-split.md). Leastness makes this a uniquely definable assignment, so ambient [replacement](../../../../../axiom-schema-of-replacement.md) gives the [set](../../../../../set-split.md)

$$
S=\{\alpha_x:x\in a\}.
$$

Put $\beta=\sup S$, taking $\beta=0$ if $a$ is empty. Monotonicity of the hierarchy gives $x\in V_{\alpha_x}\subseteq V_\beta$ for every $x\in a$. Hence $a\subseteq V_\beta$, and therefore

$$
\boxed{a\in\mathcal P(V_\beta)=V_{\beta+1}\subseteq V.}
$$

This is a rank-bounding argument: a set-sized collection of elements from the hierarchy has its stages bounded by one [ordinal](../../../../../ordinal-split.md). No simultaneous arbitrary choices are required, since each least stage is uniquely specified.
