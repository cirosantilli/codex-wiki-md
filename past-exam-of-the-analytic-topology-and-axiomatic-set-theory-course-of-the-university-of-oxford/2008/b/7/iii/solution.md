<h1 id="b/7/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In the usual arithmetic under the [axiom of choice](../../../../../axiom-of-choice.md) of [cardinal numbers](../../../../../cardinal-number-split.md), take the singular strong-limit [cardinal](../../../../../cardinal-number-split.md)

$$
\kappa=\beth_\omega=\sup_{n<\omega}\beth_n,\qquad
\beth_0=\aleph_0,\quad\beth_{n+1}=2^{\beth_n}.
$$

[Cantor's theorem](../../../../../cantor-s-theorem.md) makes this sequence strictly increasing. It is [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) and [cofinal](../../../../../cofinal-subset.md) in its [supremum](../../../../../supremum.md), so $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)\le\aleph_0$. No finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md) is [cofinal](../../../../../cofinal-subset.md) in this [limit ordinal](../../../../../limit-ordinal.md), hence $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)=\aleph_0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{2^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}=2^{\aleph_0}=\beth_1<\beth_\omega=\kappa.}
$$

This construction needs no assumption such as the [Continuum hypothesis](../../../../../continuum-hypothesis.md). The conventional use of [cardinals](../../../../../cardinal-number-split.md) here includes well-orderability of these [power sets](../../../../../power-set.md), supplied by the [axiom of choice](../../../../../axiom-of-choice.md). It is a separate setting from the explicit failure of well-orderability hypothesized in question 8; one must not impose choice on that later hypothesis.
