<h1 id="b/8/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Every constructible [set](../../../../../set-split.md) is well-orderable: the [constructible hierarchy](../../../../../constructible-hierarchy.md) has a canonical well-order, obtained by ordering the stage of first appearance, [logical formula](../../../../../logical-formula.md) codes and the already ordered finite parameter lists. Its restriction to any [set](../../../../../set-split.md) belonging to $L$ is a well-order in $L$ and remains a well-order in the ambient universe. This does not imply that arbitrary ambient [sets](../../../../../set-split.md) are well-orderable.

If $a\in L$ is finite, all its [subsets](../../../../../subset.md) are finite combinations of its elements and hence constructible. Moreover the [finite set](../../../../../finite-set.md) of all those [subsets](../../../../../subset.md) is constructible by the [finite set](../../../../../finite-set.md) operations available in $L$. Thus the full ambient [power set](../../../../../power-set.md) $\mathcal P(a)$ belongs to $L$.

Conversely, suppose $a\in L$ is infinite and that its full ambient [power set](../../../../../power-set.md) belonged to $L$. A well-order of $a$ supplies an [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) $j:\omega\to a$ by taking its first countably many elements. The map

$$
\mathcal P(\omega)\to\mathcal P(a),\qquad u\longmapsto j[u]
$$

is [injective](../../../../../injective-function.md). But $\mathcal P(a)\in L$ would make that [set](../../../../../set-split.md) well-orderable in the ambient universe, and pulling its well-order back along this [injection](../../../../../injective-function.md) would well-order $\mathcal P(\omega)$. This contradicts the hypothesis. Hence

$$
\boxed{a\in L:\qquad\mathcal P^{V}(a)\in L\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\text{ is finite}.}
$$

The superscript emphasizes that the source's final claim concerns the ambient [power set](../../../../../power-set.md). The internal [power set](../../../../../power-set.md) $\mathcal P^L(a)$ exists in $L$ for every $a\in L$ by part (ii), including infinite ones.
