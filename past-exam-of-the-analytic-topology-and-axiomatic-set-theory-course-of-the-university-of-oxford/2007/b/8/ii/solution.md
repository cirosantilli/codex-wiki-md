<h1 id="b/8/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For each $x\in a$, let $\beta_x$ be the least [ordinal](../../../../../ordinal-split.md) with $x\in L_{\beta_x}$. It exists because $a\subseteq L$, and it is uniquely definable. [Replacement](../../../../../axiom-schema-of-replacement.md) therefore gives the set $\{\beta_x:x\in a\}$. Choose an [ordinal](../../../../../ordinal-split.md) $\alpha$ at least their [supremum](../../../../../supremum.md). Increasingness of the hierarchy gives

$$
\boxed{a\subseteq L_\alpha.}
$$

If $a$ is empty, $\alpha=0$ works. No assumption that $a$ itself belongs to $L$ is used; it is enough that its elements do.
