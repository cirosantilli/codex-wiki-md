<h1 id="a/1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Fix a horizontal line $L=\mathbb R\times\{c\}$. The restriction of every member of $\mathscr H$ to $L$ is [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) in its usual [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md). Therefore every subbasic [open set](../../../../../open-set.md) intersects $L$ in a usual [open set](../../../../../open-set.md), so the induced [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md) is no finer than the usual one. Conversely the first [coordinate projection](../../../../../projection-map.md) is [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) for the [initial topology](../../../../../initial-topology.md), and its inverse images induce every usual [open set](../../../../../open-set.md) on $L$. The two [topologies](../../../../../topology-split.md) on $L$ are equal. The second projection gives the identical argument for a vertical line.

Write $F=\overline Y^{\,X}$. For every nonzero rational $r$, the [intersection](../../../../../set-intersection.md) of $Y$ with $\{r\}\times\mathbb R$ is [dense](../../../../../dense-set.md) in that vertical line, since the nonzero [rational numbers](../../../../../rational-number.md) are [dense](../../../../../dense-set.md) in the [real line](../../../../../real-line.md). Hence $\{r\}\times\mathbb R\subseteq F$. For each real $c$, it follows that $F\cap(\mathbb R\times\{c\})$ contains $(\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\})\times\{c\}$ and is closed in that line. This [dense subset](../../../../../dense-set.md) forces the whole horizontal line into $F$. Thus $F=X$: **$Y$ is a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [dense subset](../../../../../dense-set.md)**. The two successive uses of line density are necessary; the [initial topology](../../../../../initial-topology.md) is finer than the [Euclidean topology](../../../../../euclidean-topology.md), so Euclidean density alone would not suffice.
