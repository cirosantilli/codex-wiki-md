<h1 id="a/3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a [connected intersection of a closed filter base](../../../../../connected-intersection-of-a-closed-filter-base.md), the filter-base property implies the [finite intersection property](../../../../../finite-intersection-property.md). [Compactness](../../../../../compact-space.md), in its closed-set formulation, therefore gives $K=\bigcap\mathcal F\ne\varnothing$. It is closed in $X$, hence [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md).

Suppose additionally that each base member is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) but $K$ is disconnected. Write $K=A\cup B$ with $A,B$ disjoint nonempty [closed subsets](../../../../../closed-set.md) of $K$. They are [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md), hence closed in the [Hausdorff space](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) $X$. [Normality of a compact Hausdorff space](../../../../../normality-of-a-compact-hausdorff-space.md) gives disjoint [open sets](../../../../../open-set.md) $U,V$ containing $A,B$, respectively. The [compact set](../../../../../compact-space.md) $W=X\setminus(U\cup V)$ misses $K$. For every $w\in W$ some $F_w\in\mathcal F$ misses $w$, so the [open sets](../../../../../open-set.md) $X\setminus F_w$ cover $W$. A finite subcover gives $F_1\cap\cdots\cap F_m\subseteq U\cup V$. Choose $F_0\in\mathcal F$ inside this [finite intersection](../../../../../finite-intersection.md). If $W$ is empty, choose any base member instead.

Since $F_0$ contains $K$, it meets both $U$ and $V$; but it lies in their [disjoint union](../../../../../disjoint-union.md). These two relatively open nonempty pieces disconnect $F_0$, a contradiction. Thus **the [intersection](../../../../../set-intersection.md) is nonempty and [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md)**. [Compactness](../../../../../compact-space.md) is used to pass from an infinite exclusion condition to a single base member.
