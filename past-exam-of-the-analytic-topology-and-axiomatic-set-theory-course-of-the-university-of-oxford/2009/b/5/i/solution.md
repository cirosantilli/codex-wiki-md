<h1 id="b/5/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

König's theorem for [cardinal numbers](../../../../../cardinal-number-split.md) states that, for a set-indexed family with $\kappa_i<\lambda_i$ at every index,

$$
\boxed{\sum_{i\in I}\kappa_i<\prod_{i\in I}\lambda_i.}
$$

Here the sum is the [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md) of a [disjoint union](../../../../../disjoint-union.md) and the product is the [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md) of a [Cartesian product](../../../../../cartesian-product.md). Work in [ZFC](../../../../../zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice.md). Choose disjoint [sets](../../../../../set-split.md) $A_i$ of sizes $\kappa_i$ and [sets](../../../../../set-split.md) $B_i$ of sizes $\lambda_i$. For any [function](../../../../../function-split.md) $f:\bigsqcup_i A_i\to\prod_iB_i$, its $i$th coordinate values on $A_i$ form a [subset](../../../../../subset.md) of $B_i$ of [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md) at most $\kappa_i$. Choose $b_i\in B_i$ outside that [subset](../../../../../subset.md), using [choice](../../../../../axiom-of-choice.md). Then $b=(b_i)_i$ cannot equal $f(a)$ for any $a$: if $a\in A_j$, its $j$th coordinate was specifically excluded. Thus there is no [surjection](../../../../../surjective-function.md) from the [disjoint union](../../../../../disjoint-union.md) onto the product.

[Cardinal comparability](../../../../../cardinal-number-split.md) under [choice](../../../../../axiom-of-choice.md) now implies the displayed strict inequality. Empty index [set](../../../../../set-split.md) gives $0<1$ directly; empty individual $A_i$ cause no difficulty, since the corresponding coordinate excludes an empty image. This is the cardinal diagonal theorem, rather than the finitely branching tree result also bearing König's name.
