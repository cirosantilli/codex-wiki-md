<h1 id="b/5/iii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write again $\kappa=\aleph_\omega$. The [countable](../../../../../../countable-set.md) [cardinal sum](../../../../../../cardinal-sum.md) $\sum_{n<\omega}\aleph_n$ equals $\kappa$: it is at least their supremum, and at most $\aleph_0\cdot\kappa=\kappa$. König's theorem gives

$$
\kappa<\prod_{n<\omega}\aleph_{n+1}.
$$

The latter product injects into $P=\prod_{n<\omega}\aleph_n$ by fixing coordinate zero and putting its $n$th coordinate in coordinate $n+1$. Conversely $P$ injects into the [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) of all [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md) $\omega\to\kappa$. Therefore

$$
\kappa<P\le\kappa^{\aleph_0}\le\kappa^\kappa=2^\kappa=\kappa^+.
$$

No cardinal lies strictly between $\kappa$ and its successor, so

$$
\boxed{\prod_{n<\omega}\aleph_n=\aleph_{\omega+1}.}
$$

The strict lower bound is essential: replacing the product by the supremum of its factors would incorrectly give $\aleph_\omega$.
