<h1 id="b/6/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A sufficient syntactic condition is that $\phi$ be a [sigma-one formula in set theory](../../../../../sigma-one-formula-in-set-theory.md), written $\Sigma_1$: an existential block $\exists z_1\cdots\exists z_m\,\theta$ with $\theta$ bounded, meaning that each of its [quantifiers](../../../../../quantifier.md) is of the form $\forall t\in a$ or $\exists t\in a$. A [bounded formula](../../../../../bounded-formula-in-set-theory.md) is also called $\Delta_0$ and is absolute in both directions between transitive classes.

Prove the latter fact by [structural induction](../../../../../structural-induction.md). Atomic membership and equality have their actual meanings in both classes, and Boolean connectives preserve equivalence of truth. For a [bounded quantifier](../../../../../bounded-formula-in-set-theory.md) with bound $a\in U_1$, transitivity ensures that every element of $a$ belongs to $U_1$; thus its range is exactly the same in both classes, and induction applies to its matrix. This proves the bounded case. If the smaller class satisfies a $\Sigma_1$ formula, take its existential witnesses in $U_1$. They remain available in $U_2$, and bounded absoluteness preserves the matrix. Hence **$\Sigma_1$ formulas are upward absolute**. An arbitrary unbounded [universal quantifier](../../../../../universal-quantification.md) has no such witness-preservation argument.
