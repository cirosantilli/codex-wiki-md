<h3 id="a/1">1</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/1/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a/1/i)

Let $\mathscr H$ be the [set](../set.md) of [separately continuous](../topology.md#separate-continuity) real-valued [functions](../function.md) on the plane. Take as a [subbasis](../topology.md#subbasis-of-a-topology) all [sets](../set.md) $h^{-1}(U)$, where $h\in\mathscr H$ and $U$ is open in the usual [real line](../real-analysis.md#real-line). Unions of finite intersections of these [sets](../set.md) form a [topology](../topology.md), and every $h$ is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) for it by construction. Conversely every [topology](../topology.md) making every $h$ [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) must contain the [subbasis](../topology.md#subbasis-of-a-topology), hence this entire generated [topology](../topology.md). Thus **the required coarsest [topology](../topology.md) exists and is unique**: it is the [initial topology](../topology.md#initial-topology) of the family $\mathscr H$.

The family is a [set](../set.md), being a [subset](../set.md#subset) of $\mathbb R^{\mathbb R^2}$, so this construction has no proper-class issue. The [coordinate projections](../function.md#projection-map) belong to $\mathscr H$; consequently this [topology](../topology.md) contains the ordinary [Euclidean topology](../topological-analysis.md#euclidean-topology). This last observation will also make [convergence](../calculus.md#convergence) in the new [topology](../topology.md) imply ordinary coordinatewise [convergence](../calculus.md#convergence).

<h4 id="a/1/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a/1/ii)

Fix a horizontal line $L=\mathbb R\times\{c\}$. The restriction of every member of $\mathscr H$ to $L$ is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) in its usual [topology](../topology.md). Therefore every subbasic [open set](../topology.md#open-set) intersects $L$ in a usual [open set](../topology.md#open-set), so the induced [topology](../topology.md) is no finer than the usual one. Conversely the first [coordinate projection](../function.md#projection-map) is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) for the [initial topology](../topology.md#initial-topology), and its inverse images induce every usual [open set](../topology.md#open-set) on $L$. The two [topologies](../topology.md) on $L$ are equal. The second projection gives the identical argument for a vertical line.

Write $F=\overline Y^{\,X}$. For every nonzero rational $r$, the [intersection](../set.md#set-intersection) of $Y$ with $\{r\}\times\mathbb R$ is [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) in that vertical line, since the nonzero [rational numbers](../number-theory.md#rational-number) are [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) in the [real line](../real-analysis.md#real-line). Hence $\{r\}\times\mathbb R\subseteq F$. For each real $c$, it follows that $F\cap(\mathbb R\times\{c\})$ contains $(\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\})\times\{c\}$ and is closed in that line. This [dense subset](../topology.md#dense-set) forces the whole horizontal line into $F$. Thus $F=X$: **$Y$ is a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [dense subset](../topology.md#dense-set)**. The two successive uses of line density are necessary; the [initial topology](../topology.md#initial-topology) is finer than the [Euclidean topology](../topological-analysis.md#euclidean-topology), so Euclidean density alone would not suffice.

<h4 id="a/1/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#a/1/iii)

For a fixed nonzero second coordinate $c$, the rational expression defining $h(x,c)$ is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) on the half-line where $xc>0$. On the opposite half-line it is constant. At $x=0$ the ratio tends to $1$, agreeing with the value on the other side. If $c=0$ the entire restriction is constant. By symmetry the restrictions with the first coordinate fixed are [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) too. Hence $h$ is [separately continuous](../topology.md#separate-continuity), and so is $h_t(x,y)=h(x-t,y-t)$ for every real $t$.

On the diagonal $D$, $h_t(t,t)=1$, while $h_t(s,s)=0$ for $s\ne t$. Therefore

$$
D\cap h_t^{-1}((1/2,3/2))=\{(t,t)\}.
$$

Each diagonal [singleton](../set.md#singleton-mathematics) is open in the induced [topology](../topology.md); arbitrary unions show that **$D$ has the [discrete topology](../topology.md#discrete-space)**.

If $X$ were [metrizable](../mathematics.md#metrizable-space), its [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [dense subset](../topology.md#dense-set) from the preceding part would make it [second countable](../topology.md#second-countable-space). Indeed balls with rational radii about [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) points contain a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [basis](../topology.md#basis-of-a-topology) after the usual smaller-ball argument. Every subspace of a [second-countable space](../topology.md#second-countable-space) is [second countable](../topology.md#second-countable-space). A discrete [second-countable space](../topology.md#second-countable-space) is [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set), since each [singleton](../set.md#singleton-mathematics) must itself occur as a [basis](../topology.md#basis-of-a-topology) member. But $D$ is [uncountable](../cardinal-number.md#uncountable-set). This contradiction proves **$X$ is not [metrizable](../mathematics.md#metrizable-space)**.

<h4 id="a/1/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#a/1/iv)

Suppose for contradiction that $p_n=(u_n,v_n)\in Y$ converges to the origin in the [initial topology](../topology.md#initial-topology). The [coordinate projections](../function.md#projection-map) then give $u_n\to0$ and $v_n\to0$ in the usual [real line](../real-analysis.md#real-line). [Rectangular bumps detecting nonconvergence](../topology.md#rectangular-bumps-detecting-nonconvergence), using anisotropic supports, avoids any unsupported regularity assertion about a radial maximum. [Set](../set.md)

$$
\phi(t)=\max(1-|t|,0),\qquad r_n=|u_n|/4,\quad s_n=|v_n|/4,
$$



$$
b_n(x,y)=\phi\!\left(\frac{x-u_n}{r_n}\right)\phi\!\left(\frac{y-v_n}{s_n}\right),\qquad B(x,y)=\sup_n b_n(x,y).
$$

The radii are positive since neither coordinate of $p_n$ vanishes. Each rectangular support misses both coordinate axes, so $B$ vanishes on those axes. For a fixed $y=c\ne0$, all but finitely many vertical support intervals miss $c$, because their centres and radii tend to zero. Hence $B(x,c)$ is a [finite maximum](../set.md#maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order) of [continuous functions](../calculus.md#continuous-function) of $x$, and is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function). At $c=0$ it is identically zero. The same argument with the coordinates exchanged proves [separate continuity](../topology.md#separate-continuity) everywhere. Thus $B$ is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) for the [initial topology](../topology.md#initial-topology).

However $B(p_n)=1$ for every $n$, whereas $B(0,0)=0$. This contradicts preservation of [convergent sequences](../sequence-and-series.md#convergent-sequence) by a [continuous function](../calculus.md#continuous-function). Therefore **no [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) from $Y$ converges to the origin**.

The radial hint in the scan is not [separately continuous](../topology.md#separate-continuity) for every candidate [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence): for $p_n=(2^{-n},2^{-2n})$, its value at the origin is zero, while its value at $(2^{-n},0)$ is at least $1-2(2^{-2n})/\sqrt{2^{-2n}+2^{-4n}}$, which tends to one. The rectangular construction supplies the needed valid proof independently of that hint.

<h4 id="a/1/v">v</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/v/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#a/1/v)

If the origin had a [countable local basis](../topology.md#first-countable-space) $U_1,U_2,\ldots$, replace it by the decreasing [local basis](../topology.md#first-countable-space) $V_n=\bigcap_{j\le n}U_j$. Each $V_n$ meets the [dense set](../topology.md#dense-set) $Y$, so choose $p_n\in V_n\cap Y$. Given any [neighbourhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) $U$ of the origin, some $U_j$ is contained in $U$; for every $n\ge j$ we then have $p_n\in V_n\subseteq U_j\subseteq U$. Thus $p_n$ converges to the origin, contradicting the preceding part. Consequently **[first countability](../topology.md#first-countable-space) fails already at the origin**, and $X$ is not [first countable](../topology.md#first-countable-space).

<h3 id="a/2">2</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/2/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a/2/i)

[Urysohn's lemma](../topology.md#urysohn-s-lemma) states that if $X$ is normal and $A,B$ are disjoint [closed subsets](../topology.md#closed-set), there is a [continuous function](../calculus.md#continuous-function) $u:X\to[0,1]$ with $u|_A=0$ and $u|_B=1$. The values on the rest of $X$ remain in that interval. Interchanging $A,B$ or composing with an affine [function](../function.md) gives any two prescribed real endpoint values. This is **functional separation of [closed sets](../topology.md#closed-set)**, stronger than merely choosing disjoint open [neighbourhoods](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics).

<h4 id="a/2/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a/2/ii)

A [C-embedded subset](../topology.md#c-embedded-subset) $Y\subseteq X$ is one when every [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) [real-valued function](../function.md#real-valued-function) on $Y$ extends continuously to $X$. It is a [Cb-embedded subset](../topology.md#cb-embedded-subset) when this is required for every bounded [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) [real-valued function](../function.md#real-valued-function), with a bounded extension. These definitions concern the [subspace topology](../topology.md#subspace-topology) on $Y$.

Here is the bounded Tietze extension construction from [Urysohn's lemma](../topology.md#urysohn-s-lemma). Let $g:Y\to\mathbb R$ be [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) with $|g|\le M$. The case $M=0$ has the zero extension. For $M>0$, the [sets](../set.md) $A=\{g\le-M/3\}$ and $B=\{g\ge M/3\}$ are disjoint and closed in $X$, since $Y$ is closed. [Urysohn's lemma](../topology.md#urysohn-s-lemma) gives a [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) $u_0:X\to[-M/3,M/3]$ taking the endpoint values on $A,B$. On $Y$,

$$
|g-u_0|\le2M/3.
$$

Indeed on $A$ or $B$ this follows from the prescribed endpoint; on their complement both $|g|$ and $|u_0|$ are at most $M/3$.

Repeat on the residual. With $M_n=M(2/3)^n$, choose [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) $u_n$ satisfying $\|u_n\|_\infty\le M_n/3$ and reducing the residual on $Y$ to at most $M_{n+1}$. The [series](../sequence-and-series.md#series-mathematics) $F=\sum_{n\ge0}u_n$ converges uniformly by the geometric majorant. Its sum is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function), has $|F|\le\sum M_n/3=M$, and restricts to $g$, since the residual tends uniformly to zero. Thus **every bounded [continuous function](../calculus.md#continuous-function) extends, with its bound preserved**, proving $C^b$-embeddedness without assuming the extension theorem being proved.

<h4 id="a/2/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#a/2/iii)

For a [strict-range modification of a continuous extension](../topology.md#strict-range-modification-of-a-continuous-extension), the [closed sets](../topology.md#closed-set) $Y$ and $Z=\{x:|f(x)|\ge1\}$ are disjoint. Choose by [Urysohn's lemma](../topology.md#urysohn-s-lemma) a [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) $u:X\to[0,1]$ with $u|_Y=0$ and $u|_Z=1$, and define

$$
\boxed{\widetilde f=(1-u)f.}
$$

This [function](../function.md) is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) and agrees with $f$ on $Y$. On $Z$ it vanishes. Off $Z$ we have $|f|<1$ and $0\le1-u\le1$, so $|\widetilde f|\le|f|<1$. Hence its range lies strictly inside $(-1,1)$ on all of $X$. The construction also covers empty $Y$ or empty $Z$ by a constant separating [function](../function.md) where appropriate.

<h4 id="a/2/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#a/2/iv)

Let $h:Y\to\mathbb R$ be [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function), without a boundedness assumption. The [homeomorphism](../topology.md#homeomorphism) $\theta(t)=t/(1+|t|)$ maps the [real line](../real-analysis.md#real-line) onto $(-1,1)$, with inverse $\theta^{-1}(s)=s/(1-|s|)$. The [bounded function](../function.md#bounded-function) $g=\theta\circ h$ extends by the preceding bounded construction to a [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) real-valued $G$ on $X$. Since $|G|<1$ on $Y$, the strict-range modification gives a [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) $\widetilde G$ with range in $(-1,1)$ and the same restriction to $Y$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{H(x)=\frac{\widetilde G(x)}{1-|\widetilde G(x)|}}
$$

is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) everywhere: its denominator is pointwise positive. On $Y$ it equals $\theta^{-1}(\theta(h))=h$. Thus **$Y$ is $C$-embedded**. It is important to modify the extension before applying the inverse: an extension allowed to attain $1$ or $-1$ outside $Y$ would make this formula undefined there.

<h4 id="a/2/v">v</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/v/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#a/2/v)

Take **the [natural numbers](../arithmetic.md#natural-number) as the [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) [discrete subspace](../topology.md#discrete-space) of their [Stone-Čech compactification](../mathematical-physics.md#stone-cech-compactification) $\beta\mathbb N$**. The ambient [compact Hausdorff space](../topology.md#compact-hausdorff-space) is normal, and the [Stone-Čech extension property](../mathematical-physics.md#stone-cech-extension-property) makes this subspace $C^b$-embedded. It is not $C$-embedded: the [continuous function](../calculus.md#continuous-function) $n\mapsto n$ is unbounded, whereas every [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) [real-valued function](../function.md#real-valued-function) on a [compact space](../topology.md#compact-space) is bounded. The subspace is not closed, so this does not contradict the preceding closed-subspace theorem.

<h3 id="a/3">3</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/3/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a/3/i)

A [filter base](../set-theory.md#filter-base) on a [set](../set.md) $X$ is a nonempty family $\mathcal F$ of nonempty [subsets](../set.md#subset) such that for every $F,G\in\mathcal F$ there is $H\in\mathcal F$ with $H\subseteq F\cap G$. Repeated application gives a member contained in any specified [finite intersection](../set.md#finite-intersection). In particular every [finite intersection](../set.md#finite-intersection) is nonempty, so the family has the [finite intersection property](../topology.md#finite-intersection-property). Its upward closure $\{A\subseteq X:\exists F\in\mathcal F, F\subseteq A\}$ is the corresponding [proper filter](../set-theory.md#filter-set-theory).

<h4 id="a/3/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a/3/ii)

For a [connected intersection of a closed filter base](../topology.md#connected-intersection-of-a-closed-filter-base), the filter-base property implies the [finite intersection property](../topology.md#finite-intersection-property). [Compactness](../topology.md#compact-space), in its closed-set formulation, therefore gives $K=\bigcap\mathcal F\ne\varnothing$. It is closed in $X$, hence [compact](../topology.md#compact-space).

Suppose additionally that each base member is [connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) but $K$ is disconnected. Write $K=A\cup B$ with $A,B$ disjoint nonempty [closed subsets](../topology.md#closed-set) of $K$. They are [compact](../topology.md#compact-space), hence closed in the [Hausdorff space](../topology.md#hausdorff-space) $X$. [Normality of a compact Hausdorff space](../topology.md#normality-of-a-compact-hausdorff-space) gives disjoint [open sets](../topology.md#open-set) $U,V$ containing $A,B$, respectively. The [compact set](../topology.md#compact-space) $W=X\setminus(U\cup V)$ misses $K$. For every $w\in W$ some $F_w\in\mathcal F$ misses $w$, so the [open sets](../topology.md#open-set) $X\setminus F_w$ cover $W$. A finite subcover gives $F_1\cap\cdots\cap F_m\subseteq U\cup V$. Choose $F_0\in\mathcal F$ inside this [finite intersection](../set.md#finite-intersection). If $W$ is empty, choose any base member instead.

Since $F_0$ contains $K$, it meets both $U$ and $V$; but it lies in their [disjoint union](../set-theory.md#disjoint-union). These two relatively open nonempty pieces disconnect $F_0$, a contradiction. Thus **the [intersection](../set.md#set-intersection) is nonempty and [connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space)**. [Compactness](../topology.md#compact-space) is used to pass from an infinite exclusion condition to a single base member.

<h4 id="a/3/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#a/3/iii)

A [Hausdorff compactification](../topology.md#hausdorff-compactification) of $X$ is an [embedding](../topology.md#topological-embedding) $h:X\to Y$ into a [compact Hausdorff space](../topology.md#compact-hausdorff-space) with [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) image. The complement $Y\setminus h[X]$ is the remainder; a one-point or two-point compactification has a remainder of that [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality).

For one point, take the [unit circle](../complex-analysis.md#complex-unit-circle) and

$$
h_1(x)=(\cos(2\arctan x),\sin(2\arctan x)).
$$

The angle runs over $(-\pi,\pi)$, so this is a [homeomorphism](../topology.md#homeomorphism) onto the [circle](../topology.md#circle) with $(-1,0)$ removed. That image is [dense](../topology.md#dense-set), and the [circle](../topology.md#circle) is [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) Hausdorff. For two points, take $Y=[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ and $h_2(x)=\arctan x$. This is a [homeomorphism](../topology.md#homeomorphism) onto its [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) open interval, leaving exactly the two endpoints. Therefore **both required compactifications exist**; in the first the positive and negative ends meet, whereas in the second they remain distinct.

<h4 id="a/3/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#a/3/iv)

Write $T_n=h(\{x\in\mathbb R:|x|>n\})$, making explicit that these are [subsets](../set.md#subset) of $Y$. If $p\notin h[\mathbb R]$ and $U$ is an open [neighbourhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) of $p$, then $h([-n,n])$ is [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) and hence closed in $Y$. The [open set](../topology.md#open-set) $U\setminus h([-n,n])$ still contains $p$ and meets the [dense](../topology.md#dense-set) image of $h$. Such an [intersection](../set.md#set-intersection) point belongs to $T_n$, so $p\in\overline{T_n}$ for every $n$.

Conversely, if $p=h(x)$ choose an integer $n>|x|$. Since $h$ is an [embedding](../topology.md#topological-embedding), there is an open [neighbourhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) $U$ of $p$ in $Y$ whose [intersection](../set.md#set-intersection) with $h[\mathbb R]$ is $h((-n,n))$. It misses $T_n$, so $p\notin\overline{T_n}$. Hence

$$
\boxed{Y\setminus h[\mathbb R]=\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\overline{T_n}^{\,Y}.}
$$

For the [two ends of a real-line compactification](../topology.md#two-ends-of-a-real-line-compactification), let $A_n^+=\overline{h((n,\infty))}$ and $A_n^-=\overline{h((-\infty,-n))}$. Each is nonempty [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) and [connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space), being the [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology) of a [connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) image. The two nested families therefore have nonempty [connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) intersections $C^+$ and $C^-$. Since [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology) commutes with finite unions, $\overline{T_n}=A_n^+\cup A_n^-$. Moreover

$$
\bigcap_n(A_n^+\cup A_n^-)=C^+\cup C^-.
$$

For if a point fails both intersections, it fails one positive member and one negative member, hence both members at the larger index by nesting.

If the remainder were a finite three-point [set](../set.md), its Hausdorff [subspace topology](../topology.md#subspace-topology) would be discrete. Each nonempty [connected subset](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-subset) $C^\pm$ would then be a [singleton](../set.md#singleton-mathematics), so their [union](../set.md#set-union) would have at most two points. This contradiction proves **there is no three-point [Hausdorff compactification](../topology.md#hausdorff-compactification) of the [real line](../real-analysis.md#real-line)**. The argument does not assert that every possible remainder is finite.

<h3 id="b/4">4</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/4/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#b/4)

<h5 id="b/4/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/4/i)

The [cumulative hierarchy](../set-theory.md#cumulative-hierarchy) is defined by [transfinite recursion](../ordinal.md#transfinite-recursion) on the [ordinals](../ordinal.md):

$$
\boxed{V_0=\varnothing,\qquad V_{\alpha+1}=\mathcal P(V_\alpha),\qquad V_\lambda=\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}V_\beta\quad(\lambda\text{ a nonzero limit}).}
$$

The recursion theorem of [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) supplies each [set](../set.md) stage. The whole universe is $V=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}}V_\alpha$, a proper-class [union](../set.md#set-union). Equivalently, $x\in V_\alpha$ exactly when the rank of $x$ is less than $\alpha$, where $\operatorname{rank}(x)=\sup\{\operatorname{rank}(z)+1:z\in x\}$.

<h4 id="b/4/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#b/4)

<h5 id="b/4/ii/a">a</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/4/ii)

<h6 id="b/4/ii/a/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/4/ii/a)

A [set](../set.md) $T$ is transitive if every element of an element of $T$ is itself in $T$, equivalently each element of $T$ is a [subset](../set.md#subset) of $T$. Proceed by [transfinite induction](../ordinal.md#transfinite-induction). The [empty set](../set.md#empty-set) is transitive. If $T$ is transitive and $u\in v\in\mathcal P(T)$, then $v\subseteq T$ gives $u\in T$, and transitivity of $T$ gives $u\subseteq T$. Thus $u\in\mathcal P(T)$, proving the successor stage transitive. A [union](../set.md#set-union) of [transitive sets](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) is transitive: if $u\in v\in\bigcup_iT_i$, choose $i$ with $v\in T_i$ and infer $u\in T_i$. This proves the limit step and hence **every $V_\alpha$ is transitive**.

<h5 id="b/4/ii/b">b</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/4/ii)

<h6 id="b/4/ii/b/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/4/ii/b)

For $x\in V_\alpha$, transitivity of $V_\alpha$ gives $x\subseteq V_\alpha$. Therefore $x\in\mathcal P(V_\alpha)=V_{\alpha+1}$, proving

$$
\boxed{V_\alpha\subseteq V_{\alpha+1}.}
$$

Together with the [union](../set.md#set-union) definition at limits, [transfinite induction](../ordinal.md#transfinite-induction) now gives $V_\alpha\subseteq V_\beta$ whenever $\alpha\le\beta$. No rank theorem is needed for this monotonicity argument.

<h5 id="b/4/ii/c">c</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/4/ii)

<h6 id="b/4/ii/c/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/4/ii/c)

Induct on $\alpha$. For every $\beta<\alpha$, the induction hypothesis says $\beta\in V_{\beta+1}$, and monotonicity gives $V_{\beta+1}\subseteq V_\alpha$. Thus every element of the [ordinal](../ordinal.md) $\alpha$ lies in $V_\alpha$, so $\alpha\subseteq V_\alpha$. Taking its [power set](../set.md#power-set) gives

$$
\boxed{\alpha\in\mathcal P(V_\alpha)=V_{\alpha+1}.}
$$

For $\alpha=0$ the inclusion is vacuous and $\varnothing\in\mathcal P(\varnothing)$ supplies the base case. This argument uses [ordinal](../ordinal.md) membership as strict order and the previously proved hierarchy monotonicity.

<h4 id="b/4/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#b/4)

<h5 id="b/4/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/4/iii)

Take a nonzero [limit ordinal](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal) $\lambda$ and $x\in V_\lambda$. Choose $\beta<\lambda$ with $x\in V_\beta$. Transitivity gives $x\subseteq V_\beta$. Hence every actual [subset](../set.md#subset) of $x$ belongs to $\mathcal P(V_\beta)=V_{\beta+1}$, and the actual [set](../set.md) $\mathcal P(x)$ belongs to $V_{\beta+2}$. Since $\lambda$ is a limit, $\beta+2<\lambda$, so $\mathcal P(x)\in V_\lambda$. Thus the internal [power set](../set.md#power-set) contains every actual [subset](../set.md#subset) of $x$, and the [power-set axiom](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-power-set) holds in $V_\lambda$.

Here the absoluteness fact used is: every [bounded formula](../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory) is absolute between [transitive sets](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) or classes with the actual membership relation. In particular $z\subseteq x$, expressed by $\forall t\in z\,(t\in x)$, has the same meaning internally and externally. The argument supplies the actual [power set](../set.md#power-set) as an internal witness. If zero is included in one's convention for [limit ordinals](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal), its empty stage has no nonempty first-order domain; the assertion is understood for nonzero limits.

If also $\lambda>\omega$, the preceding ordinal-membership result gives $\omega\in V_{\omega+1}\subseteq V_\lambda$. The actual [set](../set.md) $\omega$ contains the [empty set](../set.md#empty-set) and is closed under $z\mapsto z\cup\{z\}$. These bounded membership and successor conditions are absolute in the transitive stage, so $\omega$ is an internal inductive [set](../set.md). Therefore **[Infinity](../mathematics.md#infinity) holds as well**. In contrast $V_\omega$ has only hereditarily finite [sets](../set.md) and cannot use $\omega$ as an element.

<h4 id="b/4/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#b/4)

<h5 id="b/4/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b/4/iv)

To prove [replacement failure at V omega plus omega](../set-theory.md#replacement-failure-at-v-omega-plus-omega), put $M=V_{\omega+\omega}$. The [set](../set.md) $\omega$ belongs to $M$, and for every $n\in\omega$ the [ordinal](../ordinal.md) $\omega+n$ also belongs to $M$. The relation saying that $y$ is obtained by taking $n$ successive [ordinal](../ordinal.md) successors of $\omega$ is definable, with parameter $\omega$, and is single valued in $M$.

For clarity this does not assume [Replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) inside $M$. For each finite $n$ its finite computation [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) exists in $M$: its entries have rank below $\omega+n+1$, and the finite coding overhead still has rank below $\omega+\omega$. The [bounded formula](../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory) verifying this finite successor chain is absolute between $M$ and the universe. The ordinary finite induction identifying its unique last entry uses the actual finite [ordinals](../ordinal.md), which are the same in $M$. This justifies the internal definable [function](../function.md) $n\mapsto\omega+n$ without invoking the axiom being tested.

If [Replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) held there, its range $B=\{\omega+n:n\in\omega\}$ would be an element of $M$. But [ordinal](../ordinal.md) rank equals the [ordinal](../ordinal.md), so

$$
\operatorname{rank}(B)=\sup_{n<\omega}(\omega+n+1)=\omega+\omega.
$$

Every element of $V_{\omega+\omega}$ has strictly smaller rank. This contradiction proves **[Replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) fails in $V_{\omega+\omega}$**. Each individual output is present; the missing object is the [set](../set.md) collecting all outputs.

<h3 id="b/5">5</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/5/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/5/i)

König's theorem for [cardinal numbers](../cardinal-number.md) states that, for a set-indexed family with $\kappa_i<\lambda_i$ at every index,

$$
\boxed{\sum_{i\in I}\kappa_i<\prod_{i\in I}\lambda_i.}
$$

Here the sum is the [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) of a [disjoint union](../set-theory.md#disjoint-union) and the product is the [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) of a [Cartesian product](../set-theory.md#cartesian-product). Work in [ZFC](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice). Choose disjoint [sets](../set.md) $A_i$ of sizes $\kappa_i$ and [sets](../set.md) $B_i$ of sizes $\lambda_i$. For any [function](../function.md) $f:\bigsqcup_i A_i\to\prod_iB_i$, its $i$th coordinate values on $A_i$ form a [subset](../set.md#subset) of $B_i$ of [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) at most $\kappa_i$. Choose $b_i\in B_i$ outside that [subset](../set.md#subset), using [choice](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-choice). Then $b=(b_i)_i$ cannot equal $f(a)$ for any $a$: if $a\in A_j$, its $j$th coordinate was specifically excluded. Thus there is no [surjection](../algebra.md#surjective-function) from the [disjoint union](../set-theory.md#disjoint-union) onto the product.

[Cardinal comparability](../cardinal-number.md) under [choice](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-choice) now implies the displayed strict inequality. Empty index [set](../set.md) gives $0<1$ directly; empty individual $A_i$ cause no difficulty, since the corresponding coordinate excludes an empty image. This is the cardinal diagonal theorem, rather than the finitely branching tree result also bearing König's name.

<h4 id="b/5/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/5/ii)

The [generalized continuum hypothesis](../cardinal-number.md#generalized-continuum-hypothesis) is

$$
\boxed{2^\kappa=\kappa^+\quad\text{for every infinite cardinal }\kappa.}
$$

Equivalently, for every [ordinal](../ordinal.md) $\alpha$, $2^{\aleph_\alpha}=\aleph_{\alpha+1}$. Here $2^\kappa$ is the [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) of the [power set](../set.md#power-set) of a [set](../set.md) of [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $\kappa$, and $\kappa^+$ is the least strictly larger cardinal. The hypothesis says that no intermediate cardinal lies between an [infinite set](../set.md#infinite-set) and its [power set](../set.md#power-set).

<h4 id="b/5/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/iii/a">a</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/5/iii)

<h6 id="b/5/iii/a/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/5/iii/a)

For [GCH arithmetic at aleph omega](../cardinal-number.md#gch-arithmetic-at-aleph-omega), let $\kappa=\aleph_\omega$. Use monotonicity of [cardinal exponentiation](../cardinal-number.md#cardinal-exponentiation), the law $(a^b)^c=a^{bc}$, and $\kappa\cdot\kappa=\kappa$ for infinite cardinals in [ZFC](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice). Since $2\le\kappa\le2^\kappa$,

$$
2^\kappa\le\kappa^\kappa\le(2^\kappa)^\kappa=2^{\kappa\cdot\kappa}=2^\kappa.
$$

Thus $\kappa^\kappa=2^\kappa$, and the [generalized continuum hypothesis](../cardinal-number.md#generalized-continuum-hypothesis) yields

$$
\boxed{\aleph_\omega^{\aleph_\omega}=\aleph_{\omega+1}.}
$$

The lower bound embeds binary-valued [functions](../function.md) into $\kappa$-valued ones; the upper bound codes each value by a [subset](../set.md#subset) and combines the resulting pairs of indices. In particular the equality before applying the hypothesis is a [ZFC](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice) equality.

<h5 id="b/5/iii/b">b</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/5/iii)

<h6 id="b/5/iii/b/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/5/iii/b)

Write again $\kappa=\aleph_\omega$. The [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [cardinal sum](../cardinal-number.md#cardinal-sum) $\sum_{n<\omega}\aleph_n$ equals $\kappa$: it is at least their supremum, and at most $\aleph_0\cdot\kappa=\kappa$. König's theorem gives

$$
\kappa<\prod_{n<\omega}\aleph_{n+1}.
$$

The latter product injects into $P=\prod_{n<\omega}\aleph_n$ by fixing coordinate zero and putting its $n$th coordinate in coordinate $n+1$. Conversely $P$ injects into the [set](../set.md) of all [functions](../function.md) $\omega\to\kappa$. Therefore

$$
\kappa<P\le\kappa^{\aleph_0}\le\kappa^\kappa=2^\kappa=\kappa^+.
$$

No cardinal lies strictly between $\kappa$ and its successor, so

$$
\boxed{\prod_{n<\omega}\aleph_n=\aleph_{\omega+1}.}
$$

The strict lower bound is essential: replacing the product by the supremum of its factors would incorrectly give $\aleph_\omega$.

<h5 id="b/5/iii/c">c</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/5/iii)

<h6 id="b/5/iii/c/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/5/iii/c)

The product from the preceding part injects into $\kappa^{\aleph_0}$, where $\kappa=\aleph_\omega$, because each coordinate value is an [ordinal](../ordinal.md) below $\kappa$. Combining its strict König lower bound with the exponentiation upper bound gives

$$
\kappa<\kappa^{\aleph_0}\le\kappa^\kappa=\kappa^+.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{\aleph_\omega^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_{\omega+1}.}
$$

Only the instance $2^{\aleph_\omega}=\aleph_{\omega+1}$ of the [generalized continuum hypothesis](../cardinal-number.md#generalized-continuum-hypothesis) was needed in these three computations, though the question assumes the full hypothesis. No assumption that [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) exponentiation leaves this [singular cardinal](../cardinal-number.md#singular-cardinal) unchanged is valid: König's theorem rules that out.

<h3 id="b/6">6</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/6/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/6/i)

Let $U_1\subseteq U_2$ be transitive classes, both interpreted with actual membership. A formula $\phi(\bar x)$ is upward absolute from $U_1$ to $U_2$ if for every tuple of parameters $\bar a$ from $U_1$,

$$
\boxed{(U_1,\in)\models\phi(\bar a)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad(U_2,\in)\models\phi(\bar a).}
$$

For proper classes this means the usual relativization of the fixed formula's [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier). Full absoluteness requires the implication in both directions. The qualification on parameters matters: the smaller class must contain every object named in the comparison.

<h4 id="b/6/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/6/ii)

A sufficient syntactic condition is that $\phi$ be a [sigma-one formula in set theory](../set-theory.md#sigma-one-formula-in-set-theory), written $\Sigma_1$: an existential block $\exists z_1\cdots\exists z_m\,\theta$ with $\theta$ bounded, meaning that each of its [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) is of the form $\forall t\in a$ or $\exists t\in a$. A [bounded formula](../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory) is also called $\Delta_0$ and is absolute in both directions between transitive classes.

Prove the latter fact by [structural induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#structural-induction). Atomic membership and equality have their actual meanings in both classes, and Boolean connectives preserve equivalence of truth. For a [bounded quantifier](../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory) with bound $a\in U_1$, transitivity ensures that every element of $a$ belongs to $U_1$; thus its range is exactly the same in both classes, and induction applies to its matrix. This proves the bounded case. If the smaller class satisfies a $\Sigma_1$ formula, take its existential witnesses in $U_1$. They remain available in $U_2$, and bounded absoluteness preserves the matrix. Hence **$\Sigma_1$ formulas are upward absolute**. An arbitrary unbounded [universal quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#universal-quantification) has no such witness-preservation argument.

<h4 id="b/6/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/iii/a">a</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/6/iii)

<h6 id="b/6/iii/a/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/6/iii/a)

Use the [bounded formula](../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory)

$$
\boxed{\forall z\in x\ (z\in y).}
$$

It expresses $x\subseteq y$ with no unbounded [quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier). For parameters $x,y\in U_1$, transitivity puts every member of $x$ in $U_1$ already, and membership in $y$ has the same meaning in both classes. Thus the formula is $\Delta_0$ and is absolute in both directions, in particular upward absolute.

<h5 id="b/6/iii/b">b</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/6/iii)

<h6 id="b/6/iii/b/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/6/iii/b)

Equality with the [union](../set.md#set-union) is expressed by

$$
\boxed{\bigl(\forall z\in x\ \exists u\in y\ z\in u\bigr)\ \land\ \bigl(\forall u\in y\ \forall z\in u\ z\in x\bigr).}
$$

The first conjunct says that every member of $x$ lies in some member of $y$; the second gives the reverse inclusion. Every [quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) is bounded by a parameter or by an element of a previously bounded parameter. Therefore bounded absoluteness applies, proving full absoluteness and hence upward absoluteness of $x=\bigcup y$. This only compares the relation for existing parameters; it does not require either class to contain the [union](../set.md#set-union) of every one of its [sets](../set.md).

<h5 id="b/6/iii/c">c</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/6/iii)

<h6 id="b/6/iii/c/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/6/iii/c)

A bounded expression for the successor relation is

$$
\boxed{y\in x\ \land\ (\forall z\in y\ z\in x)\ \land\ (\forall z\in x\ (z\in y\lor z=y)).}
$$

The first two clauses put $y$ and all its elements into $x$, while the last forbids any additional member of $x$. [Extensionality](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-extensionality) identifies this exactly with $x=y\cup\{y\}$. Since the formula is $\Delta_0$, it is absolute in both directions between the transitive classes and therefore upward absolute. This bounded description also justifies the successor computations used earlier in the rank-stage [Replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) counterexample.

<h4 id="b/6/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b/6/iv)

To prove that [power-set equality is not upward absolute](../set-theory.md#power-set-equality-is-not-upward-absolute), use the standard [constructible universe theorem](../definable-power-set.md#constructible-universe-theorem): $L$ is a transitive [inner model](../set-theory.md#inner-model) of [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) containing every [ordinal](../ordinal.md), and [set](../set.md) rank is absolute between $L$ and $V$. Since $V\ne L$, some rank stage contains a nonconstructible [set](../set.md). Let $\alpha$ be the least [ordinal](../ordinal.md) with $V_\alpha\nsubseteq L$. It is not zero; it cannot be a limit, since a [union](../set.md#set-union) of earlier stages contained in $L$ is still contained in $L$. Thus $\alpha=\beta+1$ and $V_\beta\subseteq L$.

We need not merely this inclusion, but that $y=V_\beta$ itself belongs to $L$. Inside $L$, [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) constructs its own rank stage $(V_\beta)^L$, which is a [set](../set.md) of $L$. By transitivity and [rank absoluteness](../set-theory.md#set-theoretic-absoluteness),

$$
(V_\beta)^L=V_\beta\cap L=V_\beta.
$$

Consequently $y\in L$. Let $x=(\mathcal P(y))^L$, the [power set](../set.md#power-set) computed in $L$. Then $x\in L$ and $L\models x=\mathcal P(y)$.

By minimality there is $z\in V_{\beta+1}\setminus L$. Externally $z\subseteq V_\beta=y$, but $z\notin x$, because every member of the [set](../set.md) $x\in L$ is in the [transitive class](../set-theory.md#transitive-class) $L$. Hence $V\not\models x=\mathcal P(y)$. Taking $U_1=L$ and $U_2=V$ proves **[power-set equality is not upward absolute](../set-theory.md#power-set-equality-is-not-upward-absolute), even between transitive models of [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory)**.

There is no contradiction with absoluteness of $z\subseteq y$: that bounded relation agrees whenever both parameters are present in the smaller class. [Power-set equality](../set-theory.md#power-set-equality-is-not-upward-absolute) quantifies over all [subsets](../set.md#subset), including a new $z$ absent from the smaller class. The step proving $V_\beta\in L$ is essential; inclusion in a class alone would not imply membership in it.
