# Radial reduction and growth at infinity

↑ **Parent:** [Radial Laplacian](radial-laplacian.md)

For $r>0$, the substitution $y=ru$ removes the first derivative in the three-dimensional [radial Laplacian](radial-laplacian.md), since $u''+2u'/r=y''/r$. Bounded $u$ at zero implies $y(0)=0$. Bounded $u$ at infinity means $y=O(r)$; it does not require bounded $y$. For example, constant forcing $g=1$ has the bounded particular solution $u=-1$, whose reduced function is $y=-r$. A [Dirichlet half-line Green function for d2 minus 1](dirichlet-half-line-green-function-for-d2-minus-1.md) can still represent this solution when its integral converges, but the resulting reduced function has the growth demanded by the forcing.
