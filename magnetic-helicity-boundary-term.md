# Magnetic helicity boundary term

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic helicity](magnetic-helicity.md)

For regular globally defined electromagnetic potentials, use $\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi-\partial_t\mathbf A$, $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$ and the homogeneous [Maxwell equations](maxwell-equations-split.md) to prove the displayed identity. With vanishing spatial surface flux, its spacetime integral is minus one half the change of [magnetic helicity](magnetic-helicity.md). Zero integral therefore needs matching temporal helicity boundaries, not merely localized [electric fields](electric-field-split.md) and [magnetic fields](magnetic-field-split.md). The Gaussian potential $a(t)e^{-r^2}(-y,x,\lambda)$ has helicity $2\lambda(\pi/2)^{3/2}a(t)^2$ and supplies an explicit spatially localized counterexample when $a$ changes from zero to one.
