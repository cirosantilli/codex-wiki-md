# Cubic-time resistive damping in a differentially rotating cell

↑ **Parent:** [Phase mixing in magnetic flux expulsion](phase-mixing-in-magnetic-flux-expulsion.md)

In a smooth interior region with locally constant shear $\Omega'$, a wound azimuthal [Fourier mode](fourier-mode.md) has radial [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) $k_s\simeq-m\Omega't$. Integrating its resistive damping rate $\eta k_s^2$ gives the [exponential decay](exponential-decay.md) in the title. The characteristic time is $\tau_c=[3/(\eta m^2\Omega'^2)]^{1/3}$. For $m=1$ and $\Omega'=-\Omega_0/d$, $\Omega_0\tau_c=(3\mathrm{Rm})^{1/3}$, where $\mathrm{Rm}=\Omega_0d^2/\eta$. This is a high-[magnetic Reynolds number](magnetic-reynolds-number.md) interior transient on a time long compared with the rotation time but short compared with $d^2/\eta$. Physical [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) components may have additional algebraic factors from taking [derivatives](derivative.md) of the wound [magnetic vector potential](magnetic-vector-potential.md). A maintained exterior field, an axial core and a circulation-edge [boundary layer](boundary-layer.md) prevent interpreting this estimate as an exact, uniform, infinite-time solution.
