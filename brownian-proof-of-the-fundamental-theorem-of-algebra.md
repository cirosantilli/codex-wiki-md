# Brownian proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Fundamental theorem of algebra](fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md)

If a nonconstant complex polynomial $P$ had no zeros, its reciprocal $g=1/P$ would be an entire [holomorphic function](holomorphic-function.md), bounded and tending to zero at infinity. Its real and imaginary parts are [harmonic functions](harmonic-function.md). Thus $g(z+B_t)$, for [planar Brownian motion](planar-brownian-motion.md) started at zero, is a bounded complex [martingale](martingale-split.md) with expectation $g(z)$. For fixed $R$, the Gaussian density bounds $\mathbb P(|z+B_t|\leq R)$ by $R^2/(2t)$. Outside a sufficiently large disk, $|g|\leq\varepsilon$. Hence $|g(z)|\leq\varepsilon+\|g\|_\infty R^2/(2t)$. Let $t\to\infty$ and then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ to obtain $g(z)=0$, a contradiction. This proves the [fundamental theorem of algebra](fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md) without the [maximum modulus principle](maximum-modulus-principle.md).
