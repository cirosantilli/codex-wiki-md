# Uniform convergence product rule for bounded functions

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform convergence](uniform-convergence.md)

If [sequences of functions](sequence-of-functions.md) $f_n\to f$ and $g_n\to g$ have [uniform convergence](uniform-convergence.md) on the same [domain](domain-mathematical-analysis.md) and each term is a [bounded function](bounded-function.md), their products converge uniformly to $fg$. The two families are [uniformly bounded families of functions](uniformly-bounded-family-of-functions.md): one late term and the [Uniform Cauchy criterion](uniform-cauchy-criterion.md) bound the entire tail, while finitely many early terms have a finite common bound. Write $f_ng_n-fg=f_n(g_n-g)+g(f_n-f)$ and take the [supremum norm](supremum-norm.md) to obtain the displayed estimate. The boundedness hypothesis cannot be dropped: $f_n(x)=x$ and $g_n(x)=1/n$ converge uniformly on $\mathbb R$, whereas $x/n$ fails to converge uniformly to zero.
