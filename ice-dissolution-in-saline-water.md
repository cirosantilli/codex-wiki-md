# Ice dissolution in saline water

↑ **Parent:** [Ice ablation](ice-ablation.md)

Salt lowers the freezing temperature of water. Consequently [ice](ice.md) can ablate in saline water below the pure-water [melting](melting.md) temperature if the ocean is warmer than its own [liquidus](liquidus.md). Solute diffusion towards the interface sustains the freezing-point depression. In the [diffusive glacier-ablation interface law](diffusive-glacier-ablation-interface-law.md), small compositional [Péclet number](peclet-number.md) makes interfacial salinity close to ocean salinity and gives $P\simeq[T_\infty-T_m+\Gamma(C_\infty-C_s)]/[LD/(c_p\kappa)+\Gamma(C_\infty-C_s)]$ near the no-ablation threshold. This is diffusion-assisted dissolution rather than a supply of heat above the pure-water melting point; latent heat must still be supplied to complete the phase change.
