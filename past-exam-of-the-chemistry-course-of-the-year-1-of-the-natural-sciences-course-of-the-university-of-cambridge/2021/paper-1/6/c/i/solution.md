**[Graphite](../../../../../../graphite.md):** each [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) forms three strong in-plane [sigma bonds](../../../../../../sigma-bond.md) in an sp$^2$ honeycomb layer. The remaining p orbitals form delocalized pi bands, allowing electrical conduction especially along the layers. Much weaker interlayer attraction permits sliding, explaining softness without implying weak in-plane C-C bonds.

**Carborundum is [silicon](../../../../../../silicon.md) carbide, SiC.** A suitable structure is a three-dimensional, tetrahedrally coordinated covalent network: each Si has four C neighbors and each C four Si neighbors. Strong directional bonding in all directions resists slip and gives hardness. Its filled bonding states are separated from empty states by a substantial [band gap](../../../../../../band-gap.md), giving low conductivity when pure and undoped.

“Non-conductor” is the intended contrast with [graphite](../../../../../../graphite.md); SiC is more precisely a [wide-bandgap semiconductor](../../../../../../wide-bandgap-semiconductor.md), whose conductivity can change with doping and temperature.
