↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**Group 6:** cohesion increases from [chromium](../../../../../chromium.md) to [molybdenum](../../../../../molybdenum.md) to [tungsten](../../../../../tungsten.md). partially occupied d bands make a major contribution to metallic cohesion. Relative to localized 3d orbitals in Cr, more extended 4d/5d orbitals support stronger overlap and broader bonding bands. Strong 5d bonding and the compact dimensions associated with lanthanide/relativistic effects help make W especially cohesive.

**Group 11:** cohesion increases from [silver](../../../../../silver.md) to [copper](../../../../../copper.md) to [gold](../../../../../gold.md). the filled d shell changes the balance from Group 6. Going from Cu to larger Ag weakens overlap and lowers cohesion. [Gold](../../../../../gold.md) reverses that simple size trend: relativistic 6s stabilization/contraction, changes in the 5d orbitals and stronger s-d participation enhance bonding. A size-only argument cannot explain Au exceeding both Ag and Cu.

**Group 15, As \> Sb \> Bi:** bonding in these elemental solids involves directional p-orbital interactions. Increasing size and longer separations weaken overlap down the group, reducing [enthalpy of atomization](../../../../../enthalpy-of-atomization.md). Changes in detailed solid structure and relativistic effects refine the numbers, but the underlying weakening of p-derived bonding explains the trend.

[Enthalpy of atomization](../../../../../enthalpy-of-atomization.md) measures removal from the entire standard-state solid to gaseous ground-state atoms; it is not simply the dissociation energy of one selected diatomic bond.
