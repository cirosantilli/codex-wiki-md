↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Both Be$_2$'s occupied bonding and antibonding 2s levels cancel in the simplest diagram. [Beryllium](../../../../../../beryllium.md)'s relatively accessible empty 2p orbitals allow mixing and correlated configurations to change that cancellation and produce a **weak bond**. An s-only single-configuration picture is therefore insufficient; spectroscopy and accurate correlated calculations establish its binding.[Beryllium dimer caught in the act of bonding by Jeremy M. Merritt and colleagues](../../../../../../beryllium-dimer-caught-in-the-act-of-bonding-by-jeremy-m-merritt-and-colleagues.md)

For He$_2$, $(\sigma_{1s})^2(\sigma_{1s}^*)^2$ similarly gives zero elementary [covalent bond](../../../../../../covalent-bond-split.md) order, but the empty-shell excitation gap is much larger and there is no comparable low-energy 2s/2p near-degeneracy. Thus it has **no ordinary stable [covalent bond](../../../../../../covalent-bond-split.md)**.

The literal claim that He$_2$ does not exist needs qualification: an extraordinarily weak, cold van der Waals [helium dimer](../../../../../../helium-dimer.md) has been observed. That does not contradict the zero covalent-bond-order diagram.[The weakest bond: experimental observation of helium dimer by Fei Luo and colleagues](../../../../../../the-weakest-bond-experimental-observation-of-helium-dimer-by-fei-luo-and-colleagues.md)

The [beryllium dimer](../../../../../../beryllium-dimer.md) and [helium dimer](../../../../../../helium-dimer.md) illustrate two physically different ways that the elementary bond-order picture can miss weak binding.
