↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

All comparisons assume the same temperature and comparable reagent thermodynamic activities in [methanol](../../../../../../methanol.md).

- **Iodomethane:** faster, because iodide is a better [leaving group](../../../../../../leaving-group.md) and the C-I bond is more readily broken than C-Cl.
- **1-Chloropropane:** slower than methyl chloride for [SN2](../../../../../../sn2-reaction.md), because its ethyl substituent crowds backside approach and raises the substitution barrier. It is still a primary substrate, so [SN2](../../../../../../sn2-reaction.md) remains plausible.
- **MeSNa:** generally faster substitution with methanethiolate than methoxide in this [protic solvent](../../../../../../protic-solvent.md). Sulfur is more polarizable and its larger anion is less strongly hydrogen-bond-solvated, making it a better [nucleophile](../../../../../../nucleophile.md) here. This must not be inferred simply from basicity: methanethiolate is the weaker base.
