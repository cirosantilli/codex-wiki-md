↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The configurations are $\mathrm{C}:1s^22s^22p^2$ and $\mathrm{N}:1s^22s^22p^3$. A simple complete-core-screening estimate subtracts the two $1s$ [electrons](../../../../../electron.md), giving **$Z_{\rm eff}\approx4$ for [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) and $5$ for [nitrogen](../../../../../nitrogen.md)**. This neglects screening by the other valence [electrons](../../../../../electron.md), and is an explicitly rough estimate.

A more systematic estimate using [Slater's rules](../../../../../slater-s-rules.md) includes $0.85$ for each $1s$ [electron](../../../../../electron.md) and $0.35$ for each other [electron](../../../../../electron.md) in the $2s,2p$ group:

$$
Z_{\rm eff}(\mathrm C)=6-[2(0.85)+3(0.35)]=3.25,\qquad Z_{\rm eff}(\mathrm N)=7-[2(0.85)+4(0.35)]=3.90.
$$

Both models predict the important trend: [nitrogen](../../../../../nitrogen.md)'s valence shell experiences a greater [effective nuclear charge](../../../../../effective-nuclear-charge.md). There is no unique numerical [effective nuclear charge](../../../../../effective-nuclear-charge.md) without specifying an orbital and a screening prescription; the one-electron formula in (a) does not by itself define neutral-atom orbital energies.[1930, printed pp. 58–59: screening prescription and grouping of ns and np electrons](../../../../../atomic-shielding-constants-by-john-c-slater.md)
