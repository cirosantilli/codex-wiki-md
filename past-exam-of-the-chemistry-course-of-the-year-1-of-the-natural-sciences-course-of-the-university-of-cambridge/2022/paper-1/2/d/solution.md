<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Take the bond axis as $z$. The heavy-atom $2s$ and $2p_z$ [atomic orbitals](../../../../../atomic-orbital.md) can mix with [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) $1s$ to make three sigma-symmetry [molecular orbitals](../../../../../molecular-orbital.md). In the qualitative valence ordering, these are a low predominantly heavy-atom $2s$ level, a bonding $\sigma$ level and a higher $\sigma^*$ level. The perpendicular $2p_x,2p_y$ orbitals form a degenerate approximately nonbonding $\pi$ pair because [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) has no valence orbitals of the matching sideways symmetry. The filled heavy-atom $1s$ core is omitted from the diagram.

Counting valence [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) gives

$$
\mathrm{CH}:\quad(\sigma_s)^2(\sigma_b)^2(\pi_x,\pi_y)^1,\qquad
\mathrm{NH}:\quad(\sigma_s)^2(\sigma_b)^2(\pi_x)^1(\pi_y)^1.
$$

Thus **both exhibit [paramagnetism](../../../../../paramagnetism.md): CH has one unpaired [electron](../../../../../electron.md) and NH has two in its ground state.** For NH, [Hund's rules](../../../../../hund-s-rules.md) favour separate parallel-spin occupation of the two degenerate $\pi$ orbitals. The corresponding observed ground terms are $X\,{}^2\Pi$ and $X\,{}^3\Sigma^-$; the total [bond order](../../../../../bond-order.md) is approximately one in this simple sigma-bond/nonbonding-pi description.[Methylidyne, CAS 3315-37-5, and Imidogen, CAS 13774-92-0: diatomic constants, ground-state rows](../../../../../nist-chemistry-webbook-srd-69.md)

The nitrogen-derived orbital energies lie lower than the corresponding carbon-derived ones on an approximate common energy scale, while the isolated [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) $1s$ reference is unchanged. The bonding orbitals consequently have different polarizations and mixing coefficients; drawing identical energies for C and N would miss the screening trend. The figure specifies ordering and occupancy, not measured gaps or a numerical prediction from (a).
