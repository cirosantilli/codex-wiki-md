↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The $2s$ [atomic orbital](../../../../../atomic-orbital.md) is spherically symmetric and has one [radial node](../../../../../radial-node.md). A real $2p$ [atomic orbital](../../../../../atomic-orbital.md) has two lobes of opposite [quantum phase](../../../../../quantum-phase.md), one [atomic-orbital nodal plane](../../../../../atomic-orbital-nodal-plane.md) through the nucleus and no [radial node](../../../../../radial-node.md). The three spatial orientations $2p_x,2p_y,2p_z$ remain degenerate in a spherically symmetric atom when external fields and fine structure are neglected.

In a many-electron atom, $2s$ penetrates the screened core more effectively than $2p$, experiences a stronger attraction and is lower in energy: **$\varepsilon_{2s}<\varepsilon_{2p}$**. The [hydrogen-like ion](../../../../../hydrogen-like-ion.md) degeneracy therefore does not survive screening. Both valence orbital types are generally more tightly bound and spatially contracted in [nitrogen](../../../../../nitrogen.md) than in [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md), because of the larger [effective nuclear charge](../../../../../effective-nuclear-charge.md). These are central-field orbital statements; the detailed atomic multiplet structure also involves [electron](../../../../../electron.md) repulsion and [Hund's rules](../../../../../hund-s-rules.md).
