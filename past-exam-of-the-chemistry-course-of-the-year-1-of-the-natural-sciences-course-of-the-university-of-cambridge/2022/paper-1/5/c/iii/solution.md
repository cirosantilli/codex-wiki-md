<h1 id="5/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Integrating the [Van't Hoff equation](../../../../../../van-t-hoff-equation.md) with approximately constant [reaction enthalpy](../../../../../../reaction-enthalpy.md) gives

$$
\ln\!\left(\frac{K_{318}}{K_{298}}\right)=-\frac{\Delta_rH^\circ}{R}\left(\frac1{318}-\frac1{298}\right).
$$

Using the full calculated $K_{298}=2.4596538\ldots$ yields

$$
\boxed{\Delta_rH^\circ=-8.3145\frac{\ln(2.31/2.4596538\ldots)}{1/318-1/298}=-2.47\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.}
$$

The negative sign agrees with the decrease of $K$ on heating. This is the two-temperature estimate from the supplied equilibrium data. It is not exactly the $-2.23\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ implied by treating equal prefactors and a fixed activation-energy difference as exact throughout the interval: that stricter model predicts $K_{318}=2.3243$ rather than $2.31$. The data and idealizations are therefore only approximately mutually consistent; no rounding of intermediate calculations can make both numerical [enthalpy](../../../../../../enthalpy-split.md) estimates identical.
