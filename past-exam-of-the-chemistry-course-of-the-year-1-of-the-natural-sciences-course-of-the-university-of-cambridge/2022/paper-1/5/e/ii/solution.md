<h1 id="5/e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Subtract the new equilibrium [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) and divide by the initial displacement:

$$
\frac{[\mathrm{trans}](t)-[\mathrm{trans}]_{\rm eq2}}{[\mathrm{trans}]_{\rm eq1}-[\mathrm{trans}]_{\rm eq2}}
=e^{-(k'_1+k'_{-1})t}.
$$

At the halfway [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) this ratio is $1/2$, so

$$
\boxed{t_{1/2}=\frac{\ln2}{k'_1+k'_{-1}}=465\,\mathrm s\simeq7.75\,\mathrm{min}.}
$$

This is the half-time of the displacement from equilibrium. It is not the [time](../../../../../../time-in-physics.md) for the absolute trans [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) to fall to half its initial value; that value lies far below the attainable new equilibrium plateau. The corresponding [chemical relaxation time](../../../../../../chemical-relaxation-time.md) is $671$ s.
