<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Eliminate cis using $[\mathrm{cis}]=C_{\rm tot}-[\mathrm{trans}]$:

$$
\frac{\mathrm d[\mathrm{trans}]}{\mathrm dt}
=k'_1C_{\rm tot}-(k'_1+k'_{-1})[\mathrm{trans}].
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{c_1=k'_1=1.04\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}},\qquad c_2=k'_1+k'_{-1}=1.4902\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}.}
$$

Both directions restore the perturbed distribution: an excess of trans both increases its reverse consumption and reduces cis available for forward formation. This explains why the [chemical relaxation time](../../../../../chemical-relaxation-time.md) is $1/(k'_1+k'_{-1})$, rather than the reciprocal of only one [rate constant](../../../../../rate-constant.md).
