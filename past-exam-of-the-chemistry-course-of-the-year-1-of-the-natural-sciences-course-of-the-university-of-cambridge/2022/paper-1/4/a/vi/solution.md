<h1 id="4/a/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

Combining $\Delta_rG^\circ=\Delta_rH^\circ-T\Delta_rS^\circ$ with the equilibrium relation gives

$$
\boxed{\ln K=-\frac{\Delta_rH^\circ}{R}\frac1T+\frac{\Delta_rS^\circ}{R}.}
$$

If [reaction enthalpy](../../../../../../reaction-enthalpy.md) and [reaction entropy](../../../../../../reaction-entropy.md) are approximately constant over the measurement interval, a plot of $y=\ln K$ against $x=1/T$ is straight, with **slope $-\Delta_rH^\circ/R$ and intercept $\Delta_rS^\circ/R$**. An exothermic reaction therefore has a positive slope. In the more general local form, the [Gibbs–Helmholtz equation](../../../../../../gibbs-helmholtz-equation.md) gives

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\ln K}{\mathrm d(1/T)}=-\frac{\Delta_rH^\circ(T)}R,
$$

which is the [Van't Hoff equation](../../../../../../van-t-hoff-equation.md) without assuming a constant [enthalpy](../../../../../../enthalpy-split.md).

Use a dimensionless [equilibrium constant](../../../../../../equilibrium-constant.md) built from [thermodynamic activities](../../../../../../thermodynamic-activity.md). Measure sufficiently separated temperatures after verifying true equilibrium, and avoid phase changes or a changing chemical reaction. Temperature-dependent [heat capacities](../../../../../../heat-capacity.md) cause curvature: the fitted straight-line slope then estimates an interval-dependent [enthalpy](../../../../../../enthalpy-split.md). Small errors in the equilibrium measurements can produce large slope errors over a narrow $1/T$ range. The [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) intercept is particularly sensitive because $1/T=0$ requires distant extrapolation, and slope/intercept errors are correlated. A numerical line through a few points is not, by itself, evidence that all these assumptions hold.
