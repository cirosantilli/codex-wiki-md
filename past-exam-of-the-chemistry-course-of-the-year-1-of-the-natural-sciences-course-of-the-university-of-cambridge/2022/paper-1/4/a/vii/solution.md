<h1 id="4/a/vii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vii](../vii.md)

The [exponential function](../../../../../../exponential-function.md) makes modest [Gibbs free energy](../../../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md) differences correspond to extreme equilibrium ratios. At 298 K, $RT\simeq2.48\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$; for example,

$$
\Delta_rG^\circ=+100\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\Rightarrow K\simeq3.0\times10^{-18},\qquad
\Delta_rG^\circ=-100\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\Rightarrow K\simeq3.4\times10^{17}.
$$

The minor equilibrium component may therefore lie below detection, or a species' required [thermodynamic activity](../../../../../../thermodynamic-activity.md) may be experimentally inaccessible. Apparently complete conversion supplies a bound on $K$, rather than an accurate finite value. Additionally, some reactions do not reach [chemical equilibrium](../../../../../../chemical-equilibrium.md) within a practicable observation [time](../../../../../../time-in-physics.md) because of kinetic barriers. The thermodynamic identity still holds even when direct room-temperature determination of both sides of the equilibrium ratio fails.
