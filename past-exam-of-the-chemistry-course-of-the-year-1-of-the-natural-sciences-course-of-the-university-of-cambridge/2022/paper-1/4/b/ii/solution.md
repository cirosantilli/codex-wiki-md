<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At coexistence of the two pure solid forms, their [chemical potentials](../../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) are equal and $\Delta_rG^\circ=0$. Treating the tabulated [enthalpy](../../../../../../enthalpy-split.md) and [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) differences as constant gives

$$
\boxed{T_{\rm tr}=\frac{\Delta_rH^\circ}{\Delta_rS^\circ}=\frac{500}{1.0}=500\,\mathrm{K}\simeq227\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}.}
$$

Above this [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) the model favours the higher-entropy yellow form; below it the red form is favoured. This is a prediction from the given thermodynamic data, not a guarantee of an immediately visible colour change: kinetic trapping, particle-size effects and temperature-dependent [heat](../../../../../../heat-split.md) capacities are not included. A ratio of masses of two coexisting pure solids is not the [equilibrium constant](../../../../../../equilibrium-constant.md), since their bulk solid activities are each unity.
