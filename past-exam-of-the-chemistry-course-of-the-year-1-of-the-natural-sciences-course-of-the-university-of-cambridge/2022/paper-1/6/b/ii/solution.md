<h1 id="6/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At low [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) the four terminal [methyl groups](../../../../../../methyl-group.md) contain 12 equivalent protons and the two bridging methyls contain six, giving the **$2:1$ pair of singlets**. On warming, terminal and bridging positions interchange rapidly on the [NMR timescale](../../../../../../nmr-timescale.md). A [methyl group](../../../../../../methyl-group.md) then samples both environments during the observation, producing a single population-weighted resonance, $\delta_{\rm av}=(2\delta_{\rm term}+\delta_{\rm bridge})/3$. This [Chemical exchange in NMR](../../../../../../chemical-exchange-in-nmr.md) is not merely faster rotation of each methyl about its own Al–C direction, which cannot turn a terminal site into a bridge.

The [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) independence argues against a rate-limiting collision between two intact dimers. An intramolecular bridge-opening and reclosing rearrangement can exchange which methyls are bridges; a unimolecular dissociation followed by rapid reassociation is another mechanism whose observed rate could be [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) independent. **The spectra establish rapid site exchange, but not a unique elementary exchange pathway.** A variable-temperature line-shape analysis or exchange experiment would be needed to distinguish detailed mechanisms. Primary organoaluminium studies describe the same low-temperature resolution and warm-temperature averaging, while considering dimer dissociation.[1968, printed p. 137, introduction: separate methyl environments and temperature-induced averaging](../../../../../../organoaluminium-compounds-xiii-association-through-aryl-and-phenylethynyl-bridges-by-e-a-jeffery-and-others.md)
