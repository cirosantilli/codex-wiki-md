<h1 id="6/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The four terminal [methyl groups](../../../../../../methyl-group.md) form ordinary Al–C [sigma bonds](../../../../../../sigma-bond.md), with formal [bond order](../../../../../../bond-order.md) one. Each of the two bridging methyl carbons connects the two [aluminium](../../../../../../aluminium.md) centres through a [three-centre two-electron bond](../../../../../../three-centre-two-electron-bond.md). One occupied bonding combination extends over Al–C–Al; the other combinations are unoccupied in the simple electron-deficient bridge model. A single shared pair therefore supplies one bond in total across the two contacts, giving **formal Al–C bridge [bond order](../../../../../../bond-order.md) $1/2$ per contact**, compared with one for terminal Al–C.

Each [aluminium](../../../../../../aluminium.md) has approximately tetrahedral, $sp^3$-like orbital directions. A terminal methyl [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) uses $sp^3$-like orbitals for its three C–H bonds and its Al–C bond. A bridging methyl [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) still uses three directed orbitals for C–H bonding, while its remaining approximately $sp^3$ hybrid overlaps with orbitals on both [aluminium](../../../../../../aluminium.md) atoms. This shared bridge avoids placing five independent ordinary two-electron bonds around [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md). The hybrid labels are idealized and the bridge geometry is distorted; the four occupied C–H/bridge orbital directions are not equivalent to five localized [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) bonds.

The total valence count provides a useful check: $2(3)+6(4)+18(1)=48$ electrons. Eighteen C–H bonds use 36, four terminal Al–C bonds use eight, and two three-centre bridges use four. A description assigning a full two-electron bond to each of the four bridging contacts would require too many electrons.
