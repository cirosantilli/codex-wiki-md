<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The simplest assignment is **two phenylethynyl bridges and four equivalent terminal [methyl groups](../../../../../methyl-group.md)**, represented as $\mathrm{Al_2Me_4(\mu-C\equiv CPh)_2}$. All 12 methyl protons then occupy the same class of environment, accounting for the single nonaromatic singlet without requiring methyl bridge/terminal averaging. The phenyl protons account for the aromatic-region signals; the metal-bound acetylide has no terminal acetylenic [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md).

An [alkynyl bridge](../../../../../alkynyl-bridge.md) has electronic possibilities unavailable to a saturated methyl: in addition to sigma attachment from its [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) end to one [Lewis acid](../../../../../lewis-acid.md) centre, its occupied C≡C pi system can donate to the other [aluminium](../../../../../aluminium.md). A delocalized sigma/pi description stabilizes the phenylethynyl-bridged arrangement while keeping the [methyl groups](../../../../../methyl-group.md) terminal. One must not draw an unchanged localized triple bond plus two independent full ordinary Al–C bonds at the same [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) and count all of them as separate two-electron bonds. The exact distribution of sigma and pi donation requires structural or orbital evidence; this NMR observation alone does not supply a numerical bridge [bond order](../../../../../bond-order.md).

The absence of [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) or [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) dependence is consistent with this preferred arrangement throughout the measured range. It is not proof that the molecule has no motion: exchange between symmetry-equivalent terminal positions, or exchange remaining fast throughout that range, could be spectroscopically invisible. Phenylethynyl bridging in this particular dimethylaluminium dimer has independent primary NMR precedent.[1968, printed p. 137, summary: phenylethynyl groups bridge dimethyl(phenylethynyl)aluminium dimers](../../../../../organoaluminium-compounds-xiii-association-through-aryl-and-phenylethynyl-bridges-by-e-a-jeffery-and-others.md)
