<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**The central carbon in [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) has two bonding directions and no lone pair, whereas the central oxygen in [ozone](../../../../../ozone.md) has two bonding directions and one lone pair.** In the [VSEPR model](../../../../../valence-shell-electron-pair-repulsion-theory.md), carbon dioxide therefore has a linear arrangement; ozone has an approximately trigonal-planar electron-domain arrangement but a bent atomic skeleton. Multiple bonding occupies one direction for this geometrical count. The extra central-oxygen lone pair, rather than a different number of atomic nuclei, creates the difference.
