↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**[Carbon dioxide](../../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) is linear, with two equivalent C–O bonds of formal [bond order](../../../../../../bond-order.md) two; its [Homo](../../../../../../homo.md) is predominantly oxygen nonbonding and its [LUMO](../../../../../../lowest-unoccupied-molecular-orbital.md) is C–O $\pi^*$ antibonding.** The carbon uses two opposite $sp$ hybrids for its [sigma bonds](../../../../../../sigma-bond.md) and two perpendicular p [atomic orbitals](../../../../../../atomic-orbital.md) for [pi bonding](../../../../../../pi-bond.md). A localized $\mathrm{O=C=O}$ picture assigns each terminal oxygen an $sp^2$ sigma/lone-pair framework and one p orbital for its local pi bond.

In a symmetry-adapted description, each of the two perpendicular sets of oxygen and carbon p functions forms bonding, oxygen-centred nonbonding and antibonding combinations. The occupied nonbonding pair is labelled $1\pi_g$ and the lowest empty pi pair $2\pi_u^*$. The latter has appreciable amplitude at carbon, explaining its electrophilic reactivity. Hybridization of a terminal atom with only one bonded neighbour is a choice of localized basis, not an independently measurable bond angle; an alternative oxygen $sp$ plus p basis can represent the same delocalized system.

There are 16 [valence electrons](../../../../../../valence-electron.md): four in the two sigma bonds, four in the two net pi bonds, and eight in the remaining oxygen lone-pair combinations. These local electron counts and the delocalized orbital description are alternative bookkeeping schemes, not additional electrons.
