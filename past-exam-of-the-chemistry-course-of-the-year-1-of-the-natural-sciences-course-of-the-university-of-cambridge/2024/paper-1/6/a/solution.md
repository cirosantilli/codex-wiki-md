<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**The central carbon is approximately $sp$ hybridized, with two opposite sigma bonds and no carbon lone pair; the electron-rich donor ends are sulfur and nitrogen.** The 16 valence electrons allow the principal [resonance structures](../../../../../resonance-structure.md)

$$
\mathrm{^-S-C\equiv N\ \leftrightarrow\ S=C=N^-}.
$$

Each has octets and a linear S–C–N skeleton. Carbon's two perpendicular p orbitals support the pi interactions. The first contributor emphasizes the C≡N bond and sulfur-centred negative charge; the second distributes pi bonding over S=C=N and puts negative charge on N. The real anion has partial multiple-bond character and delocalized charge rather than alternating between these drawings.

Nitrogen's greater [Allen electronegativity](../../../../../allen-electronegativity.md) favours electron accumulation there, while sulfur supports a diffuse, polarizable electron-rich region. Thus **both terminal atoms are important electron-rich sites relative to central carbon**. “Greatest electron density” should not be interpreted as a literal comparison of all core electrons on different elements; the chemically relevant comparison is valence charge, donor density or electrostatic potential. The exact partition of negative charge between S and N is not uniquely determined by electronegativities or Lewis structures alone.
