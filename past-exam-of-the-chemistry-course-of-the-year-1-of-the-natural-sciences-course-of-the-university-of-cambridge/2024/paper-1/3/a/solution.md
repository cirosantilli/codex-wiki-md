↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**The most acidic sites are: acetic-acid O–H, $\mathrm pK_a\approx4.8$; phenol O–H, $\approx10$; ethanal alpha-CH$_3$, $\approx17$–$20$; propyne terminal ≡C–H, $\approx25$.** These are ordinary approximate organic-chemistry estimates; exact acidities depend on solvent and reference convention.

[Acetic acid](../../../../../acetic-acid.md) gives a carboxylate in which negative charge is delocalized over two oxygens through equivalent [resonance structures](../../../../../resonance-structure.md). [Phenol](../../../../../phenol.md) gives a phenoxide with delocalization into the aromatic system, but its important contributors are less equivalent and do not stabilize the charge as effectively as a carboxylate.

For ethanal, removal of a methyl hydrogen gives the [enolate](../../../../../enolate.md) $\mathrm{^-CH_2-CH=O\leftrightarrow CH_2=CH-O^-}$. The aldehydic hydrogen is not the preferred acidic site: removing it does not give this alpha-carbon/oxygen delocalization. For [propyne](../../../../../propyne.md), loss of the terminal hydrogen gives an acetylide with a lone pair in an $sp$ orbital. Its greater s character stabilizes negative charge relative to an ordinary $sp^2$ or $sp^3$ carbanion; the methyl hydrogens are much less acidic.
