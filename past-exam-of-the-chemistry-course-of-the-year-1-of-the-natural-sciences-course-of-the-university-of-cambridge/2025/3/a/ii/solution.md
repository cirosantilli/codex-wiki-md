<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**The primary chloride undergoes an [SN2 reaction](../../../../../sn2-reaction.md); the triphenylmethyl chloride undergoes an [SN1 reaction](../../../../../sn1-reaction.md).** The primary site is accessible to back-side attack, whereas three phenyl groups make back-side attack at the triphenylmethyl site extremely hindered and stabilize its [carbocation](../../../../../carbocation.md) through [resonance](../../../../../resonance.md).

For the [SN2 reaction](../../../../../sn2-reaction.md), a suitable [transition state](../../../../../transition-state.md) is

$$
\left[\mathrm{N\equiv C\;\cdots\;CH_2(CH_2)_3CH_3\;\cdots\;Cl}\right]^{\ddagger-},
\qquad \boxed{v=k_2[\mathrm{CH_3(CH_2)_4Cl}][\mathrm{CN^-}]}.
$$

The entering C and leaving Cl lie on opposite sides of the reacting carbon, with partial C–C and C–Cl bonds. The total charge is $-1$. This [transition state](../../../../../transition-state.md) is not a discrete [reaction intermediate](../../../../../reaction-intermediate.md).

For the [SN1 reaction](../../../../../sn1-reaction.md), the approximately planar $\mathrm{Ph_3C^+}$ [carbocation](../../../../../carbocation.md) is a genuine [reaction intermediate](../../../../../reaction-intermediate.md). Assuming its formation by C–Cl [heterolysis](../../../../../heterolysis.md) is rate determining and trapping is rapid,

$$
\boxed{v=k_1[\mathrm{Ph_3CCl}]}.
$$

Thus the limiting [rate law](../../../../../rate-law.md) is independent of [ethanol](../../../../../ethanol.md) [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md). The successive attack and [proton transfer](../../../../../proton-transfer.md) occur after the slow ionization; a cation-stabilizing environment and efficient trapping underpin this idealized [SN1](../../../../../sn1-reaction.md) description.
