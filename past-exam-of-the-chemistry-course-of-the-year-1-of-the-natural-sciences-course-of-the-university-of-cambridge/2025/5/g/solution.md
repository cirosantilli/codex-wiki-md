<h1 id="5/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

**The remaining fraction is $\boxed{0.120\approx12.0\%}$.** Using the tabulated $300\,\mathrm K$ value rather than replacing it by the fitted value, the constant ozone [concentration](../../../../concentration.md) makes nitrogen dioxide loss a [pseudo-first-order reaction](../../../../pseudo-first-order-reaction.md):

$$
\frac{d[\mathrm{NO_2}]}{dt}=-k_{\rm obs}[\mathrm{NO_2}],\qquad
k_{\rm obs}=2k_1[\mathrm{O_3}]
=2(23\times10^6)(1.6\times10^{-12})=7.36\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}.
$$

Integrating with initial concentration $[\mathrm{NO_2}]_0$ and $t=8(3600)=28800\,\mathrm s$ gives

$$
\frac{[\mathrm{NO_2}](t)}{[\mathrm{NO_2}]_0}
=e^{-k_{\rm obs}t}=e^{-2.11968}=0.12007.
$$

This uses the nitrate-radical [quasi-steady-state approximation](../../../../quasi-steady-state-approximation.md) over the interval, constant [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and ozone [concentration](../../../../concentration.md), and no extra nitrogen dioxide sources or sinks. The stated $\mathrm{cm^3}$ and $\mathrm{mol\,cm^{-3}}$ units cancel directly; no factor of $1000$ is needed.
