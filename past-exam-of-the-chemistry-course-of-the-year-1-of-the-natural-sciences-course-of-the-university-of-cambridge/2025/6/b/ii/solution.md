**A $2.24\,\mathrm{eV}$ [band gap](../../../../../band-gap.md) is consistent with a [semiconductor](../../../../../semiconductor-split.md).** In pure [gallium phosphide](../../../../../gallium-phosphide.md), the [valence band](../../../../../valence-band.md) is essentially full and the [conduction band](../../../../../conduction-band.md) essentially empty at low [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md); thermal or optical excitation can produce mobile [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) and [electron holes](../../../../../electron-hole.md).

At room [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), $k_BT\approx0.026\,\mathrm{eV}$ is much smaller than the [band gap](../../../../../band-gap.md), so intrinsic excitation is strongly suppressed. Pure material consequently has a low intrinsic carrier density, while [semiconductor doping](../../../../../semiconductor-doping.md) can markedly increase its [electrical conductivity](../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md).
