↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**Sulfur substituting for phosphorus acts as a [donor impurity](../../../../../donor-impurity.md), giving [n-type semiconductor](../../../../../n-type-semiconductor.md) behaviour and usually increasing [electrical conductivity](../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md).** A P site normally supplies five [valence electrons](../../../../../valence-electron.md); S supplies six. After forming the surrounding bonds, the extra [electron](../../../../../electron.md) can occupy a donor level below the [conduction band](../../../../../conduction-band.md) and be thermally ionized into that band. The [donor impurity](../../../../../donor-impurity.md) then carries positive charge.

This assignment is supported experimentally: infrared transmission measurements on S-doped [gallium phosphide](../../../../../gallium-phosphide.md) resolved bound-donor transitions and determined a sulfur donor optical ionization energy of about $0.104\,\mathrm{eV}$, far smaller than the host [band gap](../../../../../band-gap.md).[1969, abstract: sulfur donor transitions and optical ionization energy](../../../../../optical-absorption-due-to-excitation-of-electrons-bound-to-si-and-s-in-gap-by-a-a-onton.md)

The effect depends on substitution on P sites, donor ionization, compensation by acceptors and [charge carrier mobility](../../../../../charge-carrier-mobility.md). A dilute active donor population increases the electron term in $\sigma=e(n\mu_n+p\mu_p)$; not every dopant must donate a mobile electron at room temperature. Sulfur's larger [Allen electronegativity](../../../../../allen-electronegativity.md) does not overturn this electron-counting argument or make a P-site sulfur atom an acceptor.
