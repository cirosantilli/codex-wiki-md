<h1 id="6/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**[Allen electronegativity](../../../../../allen-electronegativity.md) is proportional to the average binding energy of the free ground-state atom's [valence electrons](../../../../../valence-electron.md).** For an s,p main-group atom, use positive, spectroscopically inferred one-electron binding energies $\varepsilon_s,\varepsilon_p$ and their occupancies $n_s,n_p$:

$$
\chi_{\rm Allen}=C\,\frac{n_s\varepsilon_s+n_p\varepsilon_p}{n_s+n_p}.
$$

Here $C>0$ fixes the conventional scale; the one-electron energies are obtained from appropriate atomic ionization/term energies, not merely from the first ionization energy. For group 15 the weighted average is $(2\varepsilon_s+3\varepsilon_p)/5$.[1989, definition and spectroscopic construction](../../../../../electronegativity-is-the-average-one-electron-energy-of-the-valence-shell-electrons-in-ground-state-free-atoms-by-leland-c-allen.md)

The approximate connection to [effective nuclear charge](../../../../../effective-nuclear-charge.md) is $\varepsilon_{n\ell}\sim R_\infty[Z_{\rm eff}(n\ell)/n_{\rm eff}]^2$. At a fixed shell, stronger effective nuclear attraction generally raises the valence binding energies and therefore [Allen electronegativity](../../../../../allen-electronegativity.md). Down a group, increasing shell size and changes in penetration offset that attraction. **There is no universal relation $\chi_{\rm Allen}\propto Z_{\rm eff}$ across different shells**, nor can one substitute a core-electron $Z_{\rm eff}$ for the valence average.
