<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Two [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) can occupy a fully bonding orbital delocalized equally over all three [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) atoms.** Three $1s$ [atomic orbitals](../../../../../../atomic-orbital.md) on an equilateral triangle form one totally symmetric $a_1'$ combination and a degenerate pair $e'$ of higher orbitals. In an orthonormal equal-interaction model with diagonal energy $\alpha$ and off-diagonal $\beta<0$,

$$
E(a_1')=\alpha+2\beta,\qquad E(e')=\alpha-\beta.
$$

The lower orbital is proportional to $1s_A+1s_B+1s_C$, with constructive overlap along every edge. Its two [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) give a [three-centre two-electron bond](../../../../../../three-centre-two-electron-bond.md) and equivalent interactions between every pair of nuclei. At the equilibrium distance, this bonding competes with nuclear [Coulomb force](../../../../../../coulomb-force.md) to favour the symmetric triangular arrangement. The occupied shell is nondegenerate and closed, so there is no first-order Jahn-Teller incentive to distinguish the three edges. A straight-line or localized single-bond description would miss that equally shared bonding pattern.
