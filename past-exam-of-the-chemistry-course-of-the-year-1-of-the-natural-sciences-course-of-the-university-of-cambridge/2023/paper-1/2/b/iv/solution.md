↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**Li$_3$ can retain a bound triangular framework, but an equilateral triangle with three [valence electrons](../../../../../../valence-electron.md) is Jahn-Teller unstable.** The six [core electrons](../../../../../../core-electron.md) are effectively spectators. In the simplest triangular valence model, two [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) occupy the fully bonding level and one occupies the degenerate upper pair. Making one edge different splits that pair; the single electron occupies the lower component, giving an electronic stabilization that can outweigh the cost of a small distortion. An isosceles triangle is sufficient to lift the degeneracy, so three different edge lengths are not required.

The two 2.8-Å contacts are close to, but slightly longer than, the 2.7-Å dimer bond, while the 3.5-Å edge is much weaker. Delocalization and partial antibonding occupation explain why these cannot be treated as three independent full-strength Li–Li single bonds. [lithium](../../../../../../lithium.md)'s nearby valence $2p$ functions provide additional polarization and mixing channels absent from a [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) $1s$-only description, allowing a more flexible multicentre electronic structure. The existence of a bound minimum, unlike ground-state H$_3$, is an energetic fact rather than a consequence of three-electron counting alone. Calculated Li$_3$ surfaces show bound states, a [conical intersection](../../../../../../conical-intersection.md) and Jahn-Teller coupling.[Abstract and potential-energy-surface analysis](../../../../../../a-diabatic-representation-of-the-two-lowest-electronic-states-of-li3-by-elham-nour-ghassemi-and-others.md)
