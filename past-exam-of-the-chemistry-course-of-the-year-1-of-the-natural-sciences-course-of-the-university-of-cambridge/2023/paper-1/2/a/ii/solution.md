<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**The product-forming pairing is $n(\mathrm{EtO^-})\to\pi^*(\mathrm{C=O})$.** Orient an occupied [ethoxide](../../../../../../ethoxide.md) [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md) lobe toward the large [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) lobe of the [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) acceptor with matching phase. An oblique approach permits constructive overlap while the [pi bond](../../../../../../pi-bond.md) weakens; this is the usual [Bürgi-Dunitz trajectory](../../../../../../burgi-dunitz-trajectory.md) to acyl [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md). The high-energy occupied orbital on the anion makes [ethoxide](../../../../../../ethoxide.md) the stronger electron-pair donor than the neutral [ester](../../../../../../ester.md) [oxygen](../../../../../../oxygen.md).

The reverse frontier pairing, $n(\mathrm{ester\ O})\to\mathrm{empty\ Li}$, gives oxygen-to-lithium coordination. It is a plausible stabilizing contact and can coexist with the first interaction, rather than constituting an alternative way to replace the [ester](../../../../../../ester.md) alkoxy group. In the elementary frontier picture, the anionic donor-to-carbonyl interaction is the important pairing for the new [covalent bond](../../../../../../covalent-bond-split.md) and is favoured by its donor strength and appropriate accepting coefficient at [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md). The Li contact can polarize the [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) and organize the reacting pair. Precise energy-gap rankings require a specified solvated [lithium](../../../../../../lithium.md) species; the sketches alone do not justify pretending that [lithium](../../../../../../lithium.md) coordination cannot occur.
