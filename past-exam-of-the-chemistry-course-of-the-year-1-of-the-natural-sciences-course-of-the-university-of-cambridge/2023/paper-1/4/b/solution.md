<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The two remaining [partial pressures](../../../../../partial-pressure.md) sum to $200-160=40$ bar. Their ratio therefore gives $p_{\mathrm{N_2}}=10$ bar and $p_{\mathrm{NH_3}}=30$ bar. With the ideal-gas activity approximation $a_i=p_i/p^\circ$ and $p^\circ=1$ bar,

$$
K_{800}=\frac{(30/1)^2}{(10/1)(160/1)^3}
=\boxed{2.1973\times10^{-5}}.
$$

The [equilibrium constant](../../../../../equilibrium-constant.md) is dimensionless: pressures enter as ratios to the standard [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). Blindly substituting pressures in pascals into an unnormalized expression would give a different, incorrect thermodynamic $K$.

The standard reaction [enthalpy](../../../../../enthalpy-split.md) is $2(-45.90)=-91.80\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ per mole of reaction as written, because the elements are in their reference states. Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ln\frac{K_{298}}{K_{800}}
&=-\frac{-91800}{8.3145}\left(\frac1{298}-\frac1{800}\right),\\
K_{298}&=\boxed{2.7466\times10^5}\simeq2.75\times10^5.
\end{aligned}
$$

This calculation uses the supplied constant-enthalpy simplification. At 200 bar, an accurate thermodynamic treatment would use [chemical fugacities](../../../../../chemical-fugacity.md) rather than ideal [partial pressures](../../../../../partial-pressure.md); no [chemical fugacity](../../../../../chemical-fugacity.md) coefficients are supplied. The [iron](../../../../../iron.md) catalyst changes the approach to equilibrium, not its thermodynamic value.
