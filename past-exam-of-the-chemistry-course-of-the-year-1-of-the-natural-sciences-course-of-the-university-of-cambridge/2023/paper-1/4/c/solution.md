<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Using the [equilibrium constant](../../../../../equilibrium-constant.md) calculated without premature rounding,

$$
\Delta_rG^\circ_{298}=-RT\ln K_{298}
=\boxed{-31.03\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}}.
$$

Then $\Delta_rG^\circ=\Delta_rH^\circ-T\Delta_rS^\circ$ gives

$$
\Delta_rS^\circ_{298}
=\frac{-91800-(-31029.18)}{298}
=\boxed{-203.93\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}}.
$$

The sign agrees with the reduction in gas stoichiometric amount. The standard reaction [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) is not the absolute [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) of ammonia: its stoichiometric coefficient is two. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
2S_m^\circ(\mathrm{NH_3})-191.61-3(130.68)&=-203.93,\\
\boxed{S_m^\circ(\mathrm{NH_3},298\,\mathrm K)}&=\boxed{189.86\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is an absolute [standard molar entropy](../../../../../standard-molar-entropy.md) inferred within the paper's thermodynamic approximations, not a claim that elemental gases have zero absolute [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md). Only their standard enthalpies of formation are zero in this calculation.
