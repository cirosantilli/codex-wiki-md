<h1 id="5/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [Arrhenius equation](../../../../../../arrhenius-equation.md) is

$$
\boxed{k(T)=A\exp\left(-\frac{E_a}{RT}\right)}.
$$

The exponential gives the strongly temperature-dependent energetic factor. For a simple bimolecular collision picture, $A$ collects the encounter frequency per reactant [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) and the fraction of encounters with a suitable orientation and other dynamical requirements. Thus a collision is not automatically a successful reaction even when its energy is sufficient.

$A$ has the same units as $k$, which depend on the [rate law](../../../../../../rate-law.md); it is not always a frequency in $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. More generally it is an effective prefactor containing statistical and dynamical contributions rather than a literal universal collision count. Treating it as [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) independent is the approximation requested here.
