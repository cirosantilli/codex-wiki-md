<h1 id="5/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**The reverse elementary [rate constant](../../../../../../rate-constant.md) is extraordinarily small at 298 K.** Its reaction would require [hydrogen chloride](../../../../../../hydrogen-chloride.md) and a [chlorine](../../../../../../chlorine.md) [free radical](../../../../../../radical-chemistry.md) to produce a [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) [free radical](../../../../../../radical-chemistry.md) and [chlorine](../../../../../../chlorine.md), reversing a highly exothermic propagation step. The value found above makes this channel negligible for the intended reacting mixture compared with forward propagation.

A large [equilibrium constant](../../../../../../equilibrium-constant.md) alone does not permit a reverse reaction to be ignored under every conceivable composition: if forward reactants were exhausted, [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) ratios would matter. The simplification applies during the forward chain-reaction regime specified, rather than constituting a universal statement about all late-time or imposed reverse conditions.
