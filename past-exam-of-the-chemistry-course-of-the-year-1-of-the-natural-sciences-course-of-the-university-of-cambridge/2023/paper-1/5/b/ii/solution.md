<h1 id="5/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For forward and reverse elementary reactions at equilibrium, the two rates are equal. Both directions have two reactant particles and use the same [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) convention, so the conversion factors cancel and $K_{\rm eq}=k_3/k_{-3}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{k_{-3}=\frac{1.933\times10^{-14}}{7.244\times10^{33}}
=2.668\times10^{-48}\,\mathrm{dm^3\,molecule^{-1}\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

For molar rather than molecular number [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md), the bimolecular [rate constant](../../../../../../rate-constant.md) is multiplied by the [Avogadro constant](../../../../../../avogadro-constant.md):

$$
\boxed{k_{-3}=2.668\times10^{-48}(6.022\times10^{23})
=1.607\times10^{-24}\,\mathrm{dm^3\,mol^{-1}\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

Only one factor is needed: converting the two [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) factors contributes $N_A^2$, while converting the production rate from [molecules](../../../../../../molecule-split.md) to moles removes one factor.
