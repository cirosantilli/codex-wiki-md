**The large drop from [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) to [silicon](../../../../../silicon.md) is the main trend; the smaller reversals at Ge and Pb reflect changes in shielding and valence-electron binding.** [Allen electronegativity](../../../../../allen-electronegativity.md) is based on the mean [binding energy](../../../../../binding-energy.md) of [valence electrons](../../../../../valence-electron.md) in free ground-state atoms, rather than an arbitrary charge-transfer count. Moving to larger principal shells generally makes the [valence electrons](../../../../../valence-electron.md) more diffuse and more shielded, reducing their attraction to the nucleus. [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md)'s compact second-shell valence orbitals therefore give particularly high [electronegativity](../../../../../electronegativity-split.md).

The intervening filled 3d shell shields imperfectly, increasing effective nuclear attraction in [germanium](../../../../../germanium.md) relative to a simple smooth extrapolation from [silicon](../../../../../silicon.md). This helps explain Ge being slightly above Si rather than below it. Increased size then lowers Sn relative to Ge. The filled inner d and f shells and relativistic stabilization of [lead](../../../../../lead.md)'s 6s [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) modify the next step; contraction and stronger binding of the 6s contribution help Pb lie slightly above Sn. The small reversals should not obscure the much larger C-to-Si change. The spectroscopic definition includes both occupied s and p valence contributions, so the relativistic s effect can influence the Allen average even though not every electron participates equally in every bond.[Definition of the valence-shell spectroscopic average](../../../../../electronegativity-is-the-average-one-electron-energy-of-the-valence-shell-electrons-in-ground-state-free-atoms-by-leland-c-allen.md)
