# Optical gain

↑ **Parent:** [Laser](laser-split.md)

[Optical gain](optical-gain.md) is amplification of [light](light.md) by a medium. Here the small-signal intensity gain coefficient $g$ has units $\mathrm{m^{-1}}$: for fixed populations, a weak beam obeys

$$
\frac{dI}{dz}=gI,
\qquad I(z)=I(0)e^{gz},
\qquad g=\sigma(N_2-N_1)
$$

in the nondegenerate resonant two-level model, with common transition cross-section $\sigma$. Thus $g$ includes the competition between [stimulated emission](stimulated-emission.md) and [photon absorption](photon-absorption.md) on that transition. Separate passive intensity loss $\alpha$ gives $I(z)=I(0)e^{(g-\alpha)z}$. Both coefficients describe [light intensity](light-intensity.md), not [wave amplitude](wave-amplitude.md); the corresponding field-amplitude exponent is half as large. [Franz Kärtner, MIT 6.974, §§6.7–6.8: amplification and stimulated-transition cross-section](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-974-fundamentals-of-photonics-quantum-electronics-spring-2006/2641db6431ea70989c29e8512d5d91a7_chapter6.pdf).

For $g=0.30\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, $\alpha=0.10\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ and $z=0.50\,\mathrm m$, the single-pass intensity multiplier is $e^{0.10}\approx1.10517$. This is amplification during one passage, before mirror losses. [Population inversion](population-inversion.md) can therefore yield positive [optical gain](optical-gain.md) while the complete oscillator remains below [laser threshold](laser-threshold.md).
