# Hertz spark transmission and detection experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation-split.md)

In his 1888 reflection experiments, [Heinrich Hertz](heinrich-hertz.md) excited a straight conductor with a central [spark gap](spark-gap.md) driven by an [induction coil](induction-coil.md). The receiver was a wire circle of $35\,\mathrm{cm}$ [radius](radius.md) with an adjustable small [spark gap](spark-gap.md); tiny sparks were observed in darkness. There was no connecting transmission wire. [Hertz, Electric Waves (1893 translation), VII, pp. 108–109; VIII, pp. 125–127](electric-waves-being-researches-on-the-propagation-of-electric-action-with-finite-velocity-through-space-by-heinrich-hertz.md)

He removed hanging metal fixtures, installed a $4\,\mathrm m$ by $2\,\mathrm m$ zinc reflecting sheet, and moved and rotated the receiver. Spark strength alternated with position and gap orientation, supporting [specular reflection](specular-reflection.md) and [wave interference](interference-wave-propagation.md). He checked observations from farther away because the observer's body perturbed the response. Changing oscillator dimensions changed the spatial pattern. [Hertz, Electric Waves, VIII, pp. 125–127 and 135–136: preparations and controls](electric-waves-being-researches-on-the-propagation-of-electric-action-with-finite-velocity-through-space-by-heinrich-hertz.md)

These tests supported propagating [electromagnetic waves](electromagnetic-wave-split.md), rather than mere detection of nearby induction. They did not establish unlimited reception distance. His wire-versus-air [wave interference](interference-wave-propagation.md) experiment addressed finite [velocity](velocity.md); he later judged its quantitative velocity comparison doubtful because of room effects. [Hertz, Electric Waves, VII, pp. 107–108; Introduction, pp. 9–12: retrospective limitations](electric-waves-being-researches-on-the-propagation-of-electric-action-with-finite-velocity-through-space-by-heinrich-hertz.md)

The physical inference combines transmission with a spatially structured response to a reflected disturbance. In an ideal monochromatic incident-and-reflected model, [standing wave](standing-wave.md) nodes are separated by $\lambda/2$; combining a reliable [wavelength](wavelength.md) with independently known [frequency](frequency.md) gives [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) $v=\nu\lambda$. A spark is a threshold event, however: its presence or length is not a calibrated [wave intensity](intensity-physics.md) or a direct arrival-time measurement. The actual loop also responds to its orientation and finite size, so the simple standing-wave model is an explanation of the test's logic, rather than a reconstruction of Hertz's measured points. [Poynting theorem](poynting-theorem.md) explains how source [energy](energy-split.md) can reach and drive the receiver's discharge.
