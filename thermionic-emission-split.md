# Thermionic emission

↑ **Parent:** [Electron](electron.md)

Release of [electrons](electron.md) from a heated material; one source of [electrons](electron.md) in an [X-ray tube](x-ray-tube.md).

Heating a [metal](metal.md) gives some [electrons](electron.md) enough [energy](energy-split.md) to escape its surface. A heated [diode cathode](diode-cathode.md) can therefore supply an [electric current](electric-current.md) across a [vacuum](vacuum.md) gap to a collecting [diode anode](diode-anode.md). This makes [temperature](temperature-split.md) a control for the supply of [electrons](electron.md). Conversely, measuring that supply at several [temperatures](temperature-split.md) lets us estimate the surface [work function](work-function.md): the [energy](energy-split.md) barrier measured from the [Fermi level](fermi-level.md) to the nearby [vacuum](vacuum.md) level. A [Richardson plot](richardson-plot.md) turns the strong [exponential function](exponential-function.md) dependence into a slope.

Three questions must be kept separate: how many [electrons](electron.md) cross the surface, how many traverse the gap, and how many the collecting [diode anode](diode-anode.md) records. The [Richardson-Dushman law](richardson-dushman-law.md) answers the first under specified surface assumptions. [Space charge](space-charge.md) can obstruct the second, while incomplete collection can alter the third.
