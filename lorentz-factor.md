# Lorentz factor

↑ **Parent:** [Special relativity](special-relativity-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_factor)

For a relative [speed](speed.md) $0\leq v<c$, the [Lorentz factor](lorentz-factor.md) is

$$
\gamma=\frac1{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}.
$$

At $v=0.6c$, $\gamma=1/\sqrt{1-0.36}=1/0.8=1.25$. Thus a moving clock accumulates $0.8$ seconds of [proper time](proper-time.md) per laboratory second, and a parallel moving rod has $0.8$ times its rest length. The [Lorentz factor](lorentz-factor.md) approaches one at small [speed](speed.md) and grows without bound as $v$ approaches the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md); a massive object's [rest frame](rest-frame.md) never requires a boost at $v=c$.
