# Energy release from an antiplane square-root coefficient

↑ **Parent:** [Energy release rate](energy-release-rate.md)

Use antiplane energy $\frac\mu2\int|\nabla u|^2$ and normalize the tip singularity as $u=k\sqrt r\sin(\theta/2)$ plus a less singular term. The [energy release rate](energy-release-rate.md) is the small-circle flux $\int[\mathcal W n_x-\mu u_r u_x],r,d\theta$. Here $\mathcal W=\mu k^2/(8r)$, $u_r=k\sin(\theta/2)/(2\sqrt r)$ and $u_x=-k\sin(\theta/2)/(2\sqrt r)$. Integrating over $(-\pi,\pi)$ gives the displayed coefficient. The full three-dimensional antiplane stress has squared [Frobenius norm](frobenius-norm.md) $2\mu^2|\nabla u|^2$, so its leading norm ahead of the tip is $\mu|k|/\sqrt{2r}$. These constants depend on the normalization of the [stress intensity factor](stress-intensity-factor.md).
