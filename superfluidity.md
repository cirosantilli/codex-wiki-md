# Superfluidity

↑ **Parent:** [Statistical physics](statistical-physics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluidity)

[Superfluidity](superfluidity.md) is a quantum-fluid response with [phase stiffness](phase-stiffness.md) and flow without viscous dissipation in an appropriate low-speed regime. A [superfluid](superfluid.md) supports a phase-gradient current; defects and excitations can limit that flow. [Bose-Einstein condensation](bose-einstein-condensation.md) concerns macroscopic one-particle occupation and is a distinct criterion: the Gaussian [phase stiffness](phase-stiffness.md) theory can have divergent absolute-phase fluctuations while retaining a cost for [gradients](gradient.md).

Why does cooling a [liquid](liquid.md) make it flow more freely through a tiny gap? [Helium-4](helium-4.md) supplies an experimental entrance to this question: the [helium slit-flow experiment](helium-slit-flow-experiment.md) compares the motion of liquid levels on opposite sides of the transition into [superfluidity](superfluidity.md). A flow measurement probes resistance to motion; a measurement of [Bose-Einstein condensation](bose-einstein-condensation.md) probes occupation of a [quantum state](quantum-state.md). The [two-fluid model of superfluidity](two-fluid-model-of-superfluidity.md) and the [Landau criterion](landau-criterion.md) connect these questions to [entropy](entropy-split.md) transport and to the [energy](energy-split.md) required to create an excitation.
