# Isotopic abundance

↑ **Parent:** [Isotope](isotope.md)

An [isotopic abundance](isotopic-abundance.md) is an atom fraction within a specified population of one [chemical element](chemical-element.md).[isotopic abundance](compendium-of-chemical-terminology-gold-book-second-edition/isotopic-abundance.md) If $n_i$ counts its [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) of [isotope](isotope.md) $i$, use

$$
f_i=\frac{n_i}{\sum_j n_j},\qquad f_i\geq0,\qquad\sum_i f_i=1.
$$

The denominator includes every [isotope](isotope.md) of that [chemical element](chemical-element.md) included in the stated population, not every [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) of every [chemical element](chemical-element.md) in the sample. Thus an [oxygen](oxygen.md) atom fraction among all [chemical elements](chemical-element.md) is a different quantity.

For an illustrative neutral [neon](neon.md) population, take the displayed central [isotopic abundances](isotopic-abundance.md) $f_{20}=0.9048$, $f_{21}=0.0027$, and $f_{22}=0.0925$ from the reference table, so $0.9048+0.0027+0.0925=1$. Its mean [atomic mass](atomic-mass.md) is the [weighted arithmetic mean](weighted-arithmetic-mean.md)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar m&=\sum_i f_i m_i\\
&=\bigl(0.9048\times19.9924401762+0.0027\times20.993846685+0.0925\times21.991385114\bigr)\,\mathrm u\\
&=20.18004638052026\,\mathrm u\approx20.1800\,\mathrm u.
\end{aligned}
$$

These are rounded reference inputs with uncertainties, not exact universal [isotopic abundances](isotopic-abundance.md); the long decimal shows their arithmetic result, not measurement precision.[Atomic Weights and Isotopic Compositions for Neon](atomic-weights-and-isotopic-compositions-for-neon.md)

The population convention matters. If instead $w_i$ is the fraction of total neutral-atom [mass](mass.md) supplied by [isotope](isotope.md) $i$, then $w_i=f_i m_i/\bar m$ and

$$
\bar m=\left(\sum_i\frac{w_i}{m_i}\right)^{-1}.
$$

This is a [weighted harmonic mean](weighted-harmonic-mean.md) in the [mass](mass.md) fractions. Using $\sum_i w_i m_i$ with those [mass](mass.md) fractions would answer a different question. The conversion follows directly by counting [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) and adding their [masses](mass.md).
