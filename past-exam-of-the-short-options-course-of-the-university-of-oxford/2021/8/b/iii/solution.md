<h1 id="8/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

When both speeds are much smaller than $c$, expand the denominator of the [velocity-addition formula](../../../../../velocity-addition-formula.md):

$$
v''=(v+v')\left[1-\frac{vv'}{c^2}+O\!\left(\frac{v^2v'^2}{c^4}\right)\right],\qquad\boxed{v''\simeq v+v'.}
$$

This is the [Galilean transformation](../../../../../galilean-transformation.md) limit. For $0\le\beta=v/c<1$ and $0\le\beta'=v'/c<1$,

$$
1-\frac{v''}c=\frac{(1-\beta)(1-\beta')}{1+\beta\beta'}>0.
$$

If both $v,v'\to c$ from below, the right side tends to zero, so

$$
\boxed{v''\to c\text{ from below}.}
$$

No sum of these subluminal speeds exceeds the [speed of light](../../../../../speed-of-light.md).
