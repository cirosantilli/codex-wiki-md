<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use a triangle with [graph vertices](../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) $v,a,b$ and [edge](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) weights

$$
w(va)=2,\qquad w(ab)=3,\qquad w(vb)=4.
$$

Its unique [minimum spanning tree](../../../../minimum-spanning-tree.md) is $\{va,ab\}$, of cost $5$, because the other two spanning-tree costs are $6,7$. In this [tree](../../../../tree-graph-theory-split.md) the $v$-to-$b$ [graph path](../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) has length $2+3=5$, whereas the [edge](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $vb$ gives length $4$ in the original [graph](../../../../graph-split.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{T_{\mathrm{MST}}=\{va,ab\}\ne T_{\mathrm{shortest}}=\{va,vb\}.}
$$

Since the [minimum spanning tree](../../../../minimum-spanning-tree.md) is unique, none is a [shortest-path tree](../../../../shortest-path-tree.md) rooted at $v$.
