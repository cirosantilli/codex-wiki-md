<h1 id="9/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The per-capita net growth rate is $r/(1+bN^2)-\delta$. Thus **$r$ is the low-density per-capita birth or recruitment rate**, **$\delta$ is the constant per-capita death or removal rate**, and **$b$ controls density-dependent suppression of recruitment**. Both $r$ and $\delta$ have units of inverse time; $b$ has units of inverse population squared. The population scale $b^{-1/2}$ halves the recruitment rate from its low-density value. Larger $b$ makes suppression important at smaller population sizes. These are interpretations of this phenomenological [population dynamics](../../../../../population-dynamics.md) model, rather than independently specified biological mechanisms.
