<h1 id="9/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Linearizing at the positive [steady state](../../../../../steady-state.md) gives $n'=f'(N^*)n$, so a small displacement decays as $n(t)=n(0)e^{-t/\tau}$. The [recovery time of a stable equilibrium](../../../../../recovery-time-of-a-stable-equilibrium.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\tau=-\frac1{f'(N^*)}=\frac r{2\delta(r-\delta)}.}
$$

As $\delta\uparrow r$ with $r,b$ fixed, $N^*\to0$ and $\tau\to\infty$. Thus the equilibrium population approaches extinction and recovers increasingly slowly after small perturbations. This is [critical slowing down](../../../../../critical-slowing-down.md). Exactly at the threshold the leading equation is $N'\sim-rbN^3$, which gives algebraic decay $N(t)\sim(2rb t)^{-1/2}$ instead of exponential recovery.
