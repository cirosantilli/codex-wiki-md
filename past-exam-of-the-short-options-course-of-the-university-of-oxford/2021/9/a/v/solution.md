<h1 id="9/a/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

The nonnegative [bifurcation diagram](../../../../../bifurcation-diagram.md) has the branch $N=0$ for every $r>0$, stable for $r<\delta$ and unstable for $r>\delta$. At $r=\delta$ the stable positive branch emerges:

$$
\boxed{N^*(r)=\sqrt{\frac{r-\delta}{b\delta}},\qquad r>\delta.}
$$

The figure in (iv) uses solid curves for stable [steady states](../../../../../steady-state.md) and a dashed curve for the unstable zero branch. At the meeting point the zero [steady state](../../../../../steady-state.md) is nonhyperbolic and attracts from the nonnegative side. If the equation is formally extended to negative $N$, there is also the symmetric branch $-N^*$, giving a [supercritical pitchfork bifurcation](../../../../../supercritical-pitchfork-bifurcation.md); its negative half is omitted in the biological diagram. This qualification avoids calling the single nonnegative square-root branch a full pitchfork by itself.
