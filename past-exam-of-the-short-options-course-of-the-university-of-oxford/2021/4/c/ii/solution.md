<h1 id="4/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose $DF_x$ were invertible at some $x\in\Omega$. The [inverse function theorem](../../../../../inverse-function-theorem.md) supplies a neighborhood $V$ of $x$ for which $F(V)$ is a nonempty open subset of $\mathbb R^2$. But $F(V)\subset M$, and a one-dimensional [embedded submanifold](../../../../../embedded-submanifold.md) of $\mathbb R^2$ has empty interior. To see this last assertion, a [slice chart for an embedded submanifold](../../../../../slice-chart-for-an-embedded-submanifold.md) takes $M$ locally into a coordinate line; a coordinate line cannot contain an open plane neighborhood, and a chart preserves openness. This is a contradiction.

Thus $DF_x$ is singular everywhere. Part (c)(i) strengthens singularity to $DF_x=0$ everywhere. By (b) and [connectedness](../../../../../connected-space.md) of $\Omega$,

$$
\boxed{F\text{ is constant}.}
$$

The special form of the [Jacobian matrix](../../../../../jacobian-matrix.md) is essential: an arbitrary differentiable map can have nonconstant image in a curve with derivative of rank one.
