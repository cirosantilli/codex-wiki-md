**No.** On $\Omega=\mathbb R^2$ let

$$
\boxed{F(s,t)=(s^3,t).}
$$

It is a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) onto $\mathbb R^2$, with inverse $(u,v)\mapsto(\sqrt[3]u,v)$. Nevertheless $DF_{(s,t)}=\operatorname{diag}(3s^2,1)$ is singular whenever $s=0$. Set-theoretic invertibility, even together with a continuous inverse, does not force invertibility of the [total derivative](../../../../../total-derivative.md). If the inverse were assumed differentiable, the [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md) applied to $F^{-1}\circ F$ would force both derivatives to be invertible; that stronger assumption is absent here.
