<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $|G|=p^2$. The allowed [center of a group](../../../../center-of-a-group.md) theorem gives $|Z(G)|>1$. By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) its order is $p$ or $p^2$. In the first case $G/Z(G)$ has order $p$ and is a [cyclic group](../../../../cyclic-group.md). A [cyclic quotient by the center](../../../../cyclic-quotient-by-the-center.md) forces $G$ to be [Abelian](../../../../abelian-group.md): if $G/Z(G)=\langle gZ(G)\rangle$, write arbitrary elements as $g^az,g^bw$ with $z,w\in Z(G)$, and commute them directly. Hence in every case

$$
\boxed{|G|=p^2\ \Longrightarrow\ G\text{ is abelian}.}
$$

For two examples at prime-cube order, take the [dihedral group](../../../../dihedral-group.md) of a square, $\langle r,s\mid r^4=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle$, and the [quaternion group](../../../../quaternion-group.md) $Q_8=\{\pm1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}$. Both have order $8=2^3$. They are nonabelian because $sr\ne rs$ and $ij=k\ne-k=ji$, respectively. They are distinct examples even up to [group isomorphism](../../../../group-isomorphism.md): the [dihedral group](../../../../dihedral-group.md) has five elements of order two and the [quaternion group](../../../../quaternion-group.md) has only one. The request asks for two examples; it does not require the prime to be arbitrary.
