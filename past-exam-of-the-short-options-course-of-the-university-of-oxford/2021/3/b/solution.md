<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Conjugate by a [projective transformation](../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md) sending $P$ to $[1:0]$ and $Q$ to $[0:1]$. A matrix representing the conjugated transformation must have zero diagonal, since its first column represents $[0:1]$ and its second represents $[1:0]$. Therefore it has the form

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\b&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad ab\ne0.
$$

Its square is $abI$, a nonzero scalar matrix, which induces the identity on the [projective line](../../../../projective-line.md). Conjugating back proves

$$
\boxed{\tau^2=\operatorname{id}.}
$$

For the requested explicit [involution](../../../../involution.md), take

$$
\boxed{\tau([x:y])=[y:x].}
$$

More generally $[x:y]\mapsto[ay:bx]$ works whenever $ab\ne0$.
