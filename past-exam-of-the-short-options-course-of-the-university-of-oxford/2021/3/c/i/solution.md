<h1 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The four points form a [projective frame](../../../../../projective-frame.md): no three are collinear. By the allowed uniqueness theorem and [cross-ratio](../../../../../cross-ratio.md) invariance, use

$$
P_0=[1:0:0],\quad P_1=[0:1:0],\quad P_2=[0:0:1],\quad P_3=[1:1:1].
$$

Intersecting the indicated [projective lines](../../../../../projective-line.md) gives

$$
Q_0=[1:0:1],\qquad Q_1=[1:1:0],\qquad Q_2=[0:1:1].
$$

A point of $Q_0Q_2$ has coordinates $[a:b:a+b]$. Intersecting with $P_0P_1$, whose equation is $z=0$, gives $Q_3=[1:-1:0]$. On that line use its first two [homogeneous coordinates](../../../../../homogeneous-coordinate.md). The [cross-ratio](../../../../../cross-ratio.md) from (a)(ii) is

$$
\boxed{(P_0P_1:Q_3Q_1)=\frac{(-1)(-1)}{(1)(-1)}=-1.}
$$

The [characteristic of a field](../../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) being zero ensures that $Q_3\ne Q_1$. The construction is the diagonal-point property of a [complete quadrangle](../../../../../complete-quadrangle.md), producing [harmonic conjugate points](../../../../../harmonic-conjugate-points.md).
