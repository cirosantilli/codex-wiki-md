<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [Sylow theorems](../../../../../sylow-theorems.md) also give $n_5\mid14$ and $n_5\equiv1\pmod5$. Of $1,2,7,14$, only $1$ is congruent to one modulo five. Thus $H$ is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $G$ as well.

By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), $H\cap K=\{1\}$, since its [group order](../../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) divides both $5$ and $7$. For $h\in H$ and $k\in K$, the [group commutator](../../../../../group-commutator.md) $hkh^{-1}k^{-1}$ lies in $H$ by the [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) property of $H$, and in $K$ by the [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) property of $K$. It is therefore the [identity element](../../../../../identity-element.md), so all elements of $H$ commute with all elements of $K$. This is the fact that [normal subgroups of coprime order commute](../../../../../normal-subgroups-of-coprime-order-commute.md).

The [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $HK$ consequently has $|H||K|=35$ elements: the expression $hk$ is unique because $H\cap K=\{1\}$. Since $H$ and $K$ have [prime](../../../../../prime-number-split.md) orders, they are [cyclic groups](../../../../../cyclic-group.md). Write $H=\langle h\rangle$ and $K=\langle k\rangle$. The [product of commuting elements of coprime order](../../../../../product-of-commuting-elements-of-coprime-order.md) has order $35$, so $hk$ generates $HK$. Finally, $HK$ is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) because both its factors are [normal subgroups](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md); equivalently, it has [subgroup index](../../../../../index-of-a-subgroup.md) two. Therefore

$$
\boxed{HK\cong C_{35},\qquad HK\triangleleft G.}
$$
