<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Apply [projective joins and meets by cross products](../../../../../projective-joins-and-meets-by-cross-products.md) to each corresponding pair. The [cross products](../../../../../cross-product.md) of their representatives are proportional to $(0,1,0)$, $(0,0,1)$, and $(0,1,-1)$, respectively. Hence

$$
\boxed{PP':y=0,\qquad QQ':z=0,\qquad RR':y-z=0.}
$$

Their common point is

$$
\boxed{O=[1:0:0].}
$$

It is a valid [projective point](../../../../../projective-point.md), because its representative is nonzero. The three equations force $y=z=0$, so this common [projective point](../../../../../projective-point.md) is unique. Thus the two [projective triangles](../../../../../projective-triangle.md) are in [perspectivity from a point](../../../../../perspectivity-from-a-point.md) $O$.
