The [real projective plane](../../../../../real-projective-plane.md) is the set of one-dimensional [linear subspaces](../../../../../vector-subspace.md) of $\mathbb R^3$. Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb{RP}^2=(\mathbb R^3\setminus\{0\})/\sim,\qquad v\sim\lambda v\ (\lambda\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}).}
$$

A [projective point](../../../../../projective-point.md) is written in [homogeneous coordinates](../../../../../homogeneous-coordinate.md) $[x:y:z]$. A [projective line](../../../../../projective-line.md) is the set of [projective points](../../../../../projective-point.md) whose representatives belong to one fixed two-dimensional [linear subspace](../../../../../vector-subspace.md) of $\mathbb R^3$. Such a [linear subspace](../../../../../vector-subspace.md) is the [kernel of a linear map](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) of a nonzero [linear functional](../../../../../linear-functional.md), so its equation has the form $\alpha x+\beta y+\gamma z=0$ with $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\ne0$.

Two distinct [projective points](../../../../../projective-point.md) have [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md) representatives $p,q$. Their [linear span](../../../../../linear-span.md) has dimension two and hence defines a [projective line](../../../../../projective-line.md) containing both. Conversely, any two-dimensional [linear subspace](../../../../../vector-subspace.md) containing both representatives contains their entire [linear span](../../../../../linear-span.md) and therefore equals it. Thus **exactly one [projective line](../../../../../projective-line.md) passes through two distinct [projective points](../../../../../projective-point.md)**.
