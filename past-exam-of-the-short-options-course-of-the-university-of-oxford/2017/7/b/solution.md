<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use a triangle with vertices $v,a,b$ and weights $w(va)=w(vb)=2$, $w(ab)=-1$. The unique shortest $va$-path is $v,b,a$, of weight one, while the unique shortest $vb$-path is $v,a,b$, also of weight one. A shortest-path tree would therefore need all three edges of the triangle. This is impossible for a tree.

Thus **negative edge weights can prevent a [shortest-path tree](../../../../shortest-path-tree.md)**, even though the shortest simple path to each individual vertex exists. This is the example in [signed edge weights can prevent a shortest-path tree](../../../../signed-edge-weights-can-prevent-a-shortest-path-tree.md).
