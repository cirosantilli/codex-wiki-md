<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Since $A$ is invertible, the [quadratic form gradient](../../../../../quadratic-form-gradient.md) vanishes exactly at the origin. At every nonzero point of $C_\alpha$, the scalar derivative is surjective, so the [regular level set theorem](../../../../../regular-level-set-theorem.md) gives a smooth submanifold of dimension $n-1$.

If $\alpha\ne0$, the origin is absent. Therefore **the largest relatively open smooth subset is all of $C_\alpha$**.

If $\alpha=0$, the nontriviality assumption forces the form to be an [indefinite quadratic form](../../../../../indefinite-quadratic-form.md): an invertible definite form would have zero set consisting only of the origin. Every nonzero point is regular. To show that the origin really fails to be a manifold point, use [Sylvester's law of inertia](../../../../../sylvester-s-law-of-inertia.md) to reduce to $|u|^2-|w|^2=0$. With positive and negative coordinate vectors $e_i,f_j$, the null vectors $e_i+f_j$ and $e_i-f_j$ span $\mathbb R^n$. Each line in one of these directions lies in the cone.

If a relatively open manifold neighborhood of the origin existed, its [tangent space](../../../../../tangent-space.md) would contain all these line directions, so would have dimension $n$. Such an embedded manifold would be open in $\mathbb R^n$, impossible for the zero set of a nonzero quadratic form. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\text{smooth locus}=\begin{cases}C_\alpha,&\alpha\ne0,\\C_0\setminus\{0\},&\alpha=0.\end{cases}}
$$

This gives the [smooth locus of a nondegenerate real quadratic level set](../../../../../smooth-locus-of-a-nondegenerate-real-quadratic-level-set.md), including maximality at the singular vertex.
