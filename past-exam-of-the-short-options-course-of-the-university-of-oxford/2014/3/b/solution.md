<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [projective quadric](../../../../projective-quadric.md) is the zero locus of a nonzero homogeneous [quadratic polynomial](../../../../quadratic-polynomial.md) $q(v)=v^TBv$, with $B$ symmetric over a [field](../../../../field.md) of [characteristic](../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) different from two. A [projective point](../../../../projective-point.md) on it is singular when all first [partial derivatives](../../../../partial-derivative.md) vanish. Since $\nabla q(v)=2Bv$, the quadric is nonsingular exactly when $B$ is invertible.

Over the [complex numbers](../../../../complex-number.md), a nonzero [quadratic form](../../../../quadratic-form-split.md) in three variables can be diagonalized by an invertible [change of basis](../../../../change-of-basis.md). Its rank classifies a [projective conic](../../../../projective-conic.md):

- Rank three gives a nonsingular conic.
- Rank two gives **two distinct [projective lines](../../../../projective-line.md)**, since the reduced form $X^2+Y^2$ factors as $(X+iY)(X-iY)$. Their intersection is the singular point.
- Rank one gives **a double [projective line](../../../../projective-line.md)**, defined by a squared linear form. The gradient vanishes along that line.

The zero polynomial is excluded from the definition of a conic. Thus these last two cases are precisely the singular conics.
