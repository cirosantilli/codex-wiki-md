<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The required [general linear position](../../../../general-linear-position.md) condition is that every $n+1$ of the $n+2$ representing vectors are [linearly independent](../../../../linear-independence.md). Such an ordered configuration is a [projective frame](../../../../projective-frame.md).

Choose representatives $p_0,\ldots,p_n$ of the first $n+1$ [projective points](../../../../projective-point.md); they form a [basis](../../../../basis.md). Write a representative of the last point as $p_{n+1}=\sum_i a_ip_i$. Every $a_i$ is nonzero: otherwise the last point together with the other $n$ basis points would fail the independence condition. Rescale the basis representatives to $\widetilde p_i=a_ip_i$, so that $p_{n+1}=\sum_i\widetilde p_i$.

Do the same for the target configuration, obtaining representatives $\widetilde q_i$ with $q_{n+1}=\sum_i\widetilde q_i$. The unique [linear map](../../../../linear-map.md) sending the basis $\widetilde p_i$ to $\widetilde q_i$ is invertible and also sends $p_{n+1}$ to $q_{n+1}$. Its induced [projective transformation](../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md) sends every point to its prescribed partner. Thus **any two ordered [projective frames](../../../../projective-frame.md) are projectively equivalent**.
