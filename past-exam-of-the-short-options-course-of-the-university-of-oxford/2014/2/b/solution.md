<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $1\triangleleft N\triangleleft G$ be the given length-two [composition series](../../../../composition-series.md). Thus $N$ and $G/N$ are [simple groups](../../../../simple-group.md). Let $M$ be any proper nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $G$, such as the penultimate term of another [composition series](../../../../composition-series.md).

If $M=N$, its two factors are already the given ones. Suppose $M\ne N$. Since $M\cap N$ is normal in the simple [group](../../../../group-split.md) $N$, it is either $1$ or $N$. The latter would mean $N\subseteq M$, but then $M/N$ would be a proper nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $G/N$, impossible. Thus $M\cap N=1$.

Also $M$ cannot be contained in $N$, since it would then be a nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $N$ distinct from $N$. The product $MN$ is a [normal subgroup](../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $G$, strictly containing $N$. Simplicity of $G/N$ therefore forces $MN=G$. The [second isomorphism theorem for groups](../../../../second-isomorphism-theorem-for-groups.md) gives

$$
\boxed{M\cong G/N,\qquad G/M\cong N.}
$$

In particular $M$ is simple. Every other [composition series](../../../../composition-series.md) has such an $M$ as its last proper term, and no nontrivial refinement inside a simple $M$ is possible. Hence **every [composition series](../../../../composition-series.md) has length two**.

The general **[Jordan–Hölder theorem](../../../../jordan-holder-theorem.md)** states that any two [composition series](../../../../composition-series.md) of a finite [group](../../../../group-split.md) have the same length and the same [composition factors](../../../../composition-factor.md) up to [group isomorphism](../../../../group-isomorphism.md) and permutation. The argument above proves both assertions in this length-two case: another series either uses $N$ itself or has factors isomorphic to the original pair in the opposite order. No general uniqueness theorem was used to prove this special case.
