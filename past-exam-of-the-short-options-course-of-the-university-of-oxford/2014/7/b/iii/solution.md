<h1 id="7/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Differentiate each segment and express the [four-velocity](../../../../../four-velocity.md) using a continuous [rapidity](../../../../../rapidity.md):

$$
U(s)=c(\cosh\theta(s),\sinh\theta(s),0,0),\qquad
\theta(s)=\begin{cases}
\omega s,&0\leq s<T,\\
\omega(2T-s),&T\leq s<3T,\\
\omega(s-4T),&3T\leq s\leq4T.
\end{cases}
$$

At $T$ both rapidities are $\omega T$; at $3T$ both are $-\omega T$. Hence **the [four-velocity](../../../../../four-velocity.md) is continuous at both junctions**. This expression also gives $g(U,U)=c^2$ everywhere, so the parameter remains [proper time](../../../../../proper-time.md) on the later segments.

Away from the junctions,

$$
A(s)=c\theta'(s)(\sinh\theta(s),\cosh\theta(s),0,0),
$$

with $\theta'$ successively $+\omega,-\omega,+\omega$. The rapidity is continuous but its slope reverses. Thus **the [four-acceleration](../../../../../four-acceleration.md) has a finite jump to its negative at each junction**, while its magnitude stays $c\omega$ on either side. There is no velocity jump; the idealized path is continuously differentiable but not twice differentiable at the switching events.
