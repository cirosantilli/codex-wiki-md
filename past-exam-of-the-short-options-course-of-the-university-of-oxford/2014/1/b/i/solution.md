<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Writing $a=21r$, the second condition becomes $r\equiv4\pmod5$. Writing $b=15s$, its second condition becomes $s\equiv6\pmod7$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{a=84+105m,\qquad b=90+105n,\qquad m,n\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}}
$$

gives an infinite set of positive [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) solutions. Conversely these are the positive solutions in the indicated [congruence classes](../../../../../congruence-class.md), by the [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md).
