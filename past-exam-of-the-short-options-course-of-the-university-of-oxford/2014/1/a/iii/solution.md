<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

[Gauss's lemma](../../../../../gauss-s-lemma-riemannian-geometry.md) states that, for $p\nmid a$, if $N$ of the least positive residues of $a,2a,\ldots,((p-1)/2)a$ exceed $p/2$, then $(a/p)=(-1)^N$.

For $a=2$, the residues are simply $2,4,\ldots,p-1$, with no reduction needed. Write $m=(p-1)/2$. A term $2j$ exceeds $p/2$ exactly when $j>p/4$, so

$$
N=\frac{p-1}2-\left\lfloor\frac p4\right\rfloor.
$$

If $p$ is congruent to $1,3,5,7$ modulo eight, the parity of this count is respectively even, odd, odd, even. [Gauss's lemma](../../../../../gauss-s-lemma-riemannian-geometry.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac2p\right)=\begin{cases}1,&p\equiv1,7\pmod8,\\-1,&p\equiv3,5\pmod8.\end{cases}}
$$

This proves the [second supplementary law for quadratic reciprocity](../../../../../second-supplementary-law-for-quadratic-reciprocity.md), rather than merely applying it.
