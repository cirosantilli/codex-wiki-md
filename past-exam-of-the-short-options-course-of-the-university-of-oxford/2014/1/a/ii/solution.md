<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

[Euler's criterion](../../../../../euler-s-criterion.md) states, for an [odd prime](../../../../../odd-prime.md) $p$,

$$
a^{(p-1)/2}\equiv\left(\frac ap\right)\pmod p.
$$

Apply it to $a=-1$. The [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) $(-1)^{(p-1)/2}$ is $1$ when $p\equiv1\pmod4$ and $-1$ when $p\equiv3\pmod4$. Since those signs are distinct modulo an [odd prime](../../../../../odd-prime.md), the [modular congruence](../../../../../modular-congruence.md) determines the actual [Legendre symbol](../../../../../legendre-symbol.md):

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{-1}p\right)=\begin{cases}1,&p\equiv1\pmod4,\\-1,&p\equiv3\pmod4.\end{cases}}
$$

This is the [first supplementary law for quadratic reciprocity](../../../../../first-supplementary-law-for-quadratic-reciprocity.md).
