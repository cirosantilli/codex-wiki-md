<h1 id="1/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Because $p$ is odd, $x$ must be odd. Every odd square is $1$ modulo eight. If $y$ is even, $2y^2$ is zero modulo eight; if $y$ is odd, it is two modulo eight. Thus

$$
\boxed{p\equiv1\text{ or }3\pmod8.}
$$

More precisely, the first case has even $y$ and the second has odd $y$. These are necessary conditions on the [congruence class](../../../../../congruence-class.md), as requested; the elementary calculation alone does not prove their sufficiency.

Equivalently, reduction modulo $p$ makes $-2$ a [quadratic residue](../../../../../quadratic-residue-split.md): $p$ cannot divide $y$, since that would also force $p\mid x$ and hence $p^2\mid p$. The two supplementary laws and [multiplicativity of the Legendre symbol](../../../../../multiplicativity-of-the-legendre-symbol.md) give the same two allowed classes.
