<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At a constrained [critical point](../../../../critical-point.md), the directional [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) of $f$ vanishes along every admissible tangent velocity:

$$
Df_a(v)=0\qquad(v\in T_aM).
$$

For a regular zero locus, $T_aM=\ker Dg_a$. A [linear functional](../../../../linear-functional.md) vanishing on this kernel lies in the [row space](../../../../row-space.md) of $Dg_a$. Equivalently there are [Lagrange multipliers](../../../../lagrange-multiplier.md) $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k$ such that

$$
\boxed{\nabla f(a)=Dg_a^T\lambda
=\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_j\nabla g_j(a),\qquad g(a)=0.}
$$

Geometrically, $\nabla f$ is orthogonal to the [tangent space](../../../../tangent-space.md), so it lies in its [orthogonal complement](../../../../orthogonal-complement.md), spanned by the independent constraint [gradients](../../../../gradient.md). Thus the level set of $f$ has no first-order change in a tangent direction. The equations provide candidates for constrained extrema; they must still be classified and compared, and boundary or singular cases must be treated separately. The condition is necessary for a regular constrained extremum, not a guarantee of a maximum.
