<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

When an endpoint's horizontal coordinate is fixed but its height is free, the [natural boundary conditions for a free endpoint](../../../../natural-boundary-conditions-for-a-free-endpoint.md) require $F_{y'}=0$ there. In this problem,

$$
\boxed{\left.\frac{y'}{y\sqrt{1+y'^2}}\right|_{\text{free endpoint}}=0,
\qquad\text{equivalently }y'=0.}
$$

Every admissible [extremal](../../../../extremal.md) is an upper [circular arc](../../../../circular-arc.md) with centre on $y=0$, by part (b). Its tangent is horizontal precisely above its centre. Thus imposing the [natural boundary conditions for a free endpoint](../../../../natural-boundary-conditions-for-a-free-endpoint.md) at $x=1$ forces $h=1$. The fixed height at $x=-1$ gives $R^2=4+4c^2$, so

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\sqrt{4(1+c^2)-(x-1)^2},\qquad -1\le x\le1,\quad c>0.}
$$

Its free endpoint height is $y(1)=2\sqrt{1+c^2}$. Positivity on the whole [interval](../../../../interval-mathematics.md) follows because the radicand is at least $4c^2$.

There can be no solution satisfying the [natural boundary conditions for a free endpoint](../../../../natural-boundary-conditions-for-a-free-endpoint.md) at both ends: an upper [circular arc](../../../../circular-arc.md) has only one point with a horizontal tangent, whereas the two endpoints have distinct horizontal coordinates. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{There is no admissible extremal with both endpoint heights free.}}
$$
