A [matching in a graph](../../../../../matching-graph-theory-split.md) is a set of [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) no two of which share a [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md). A cover here means a [vertex cover](../../../../../vertex-cover.md): a set $C$ of [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) such that every [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) has at least one endpoint in $C$. It is not an [edge cover](../../../../../edge-cover.md).

[Kőnig's theorem for bipartite matching](../../../../../konig-s-theorem-graph-theory.md) states that, in any finite [bipartite graph](../../../../../bipartite-graph-split.md), the maximum size of a [matching in a graph](../../../../../matching-graph-theory-split.md) equals the minimum size of a [vertex cover](../../../../../vertex-cover.md):

$$
\boxed{\nu(G)=\tau(G).}
$$

Here $\nu$ is the [matching number](../../../../../matching-number.md) and $\tau$ is the [vertex cover number](../../../../../vertex-cover-number.md).
