<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The [cross-ratio permutation orbit](../../../../cross-ratio-permutation-orbit.md) is generated by the two operations $\lambda\mapsto1/\lambda$ and $\lambda\mapsto1-\lambda$ from part (d). The full list is

$$
\boxed{\lambda,\quad\frac1\lambda,\quad1-\lambda,\quad
\frac1{1-\lambda},\quad\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda},\quad
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}.}
$$

These are all the possibilities: the four permutations generated by interchanging both pairs and by exchanging the two pairs leave the [cross-ratio](../../../../cross-ratio.md) unchanged. They form the [Klein four-group](../../../../klein-four-group.md) inside the [symmetric group](../../../../symmetric-group.md) on four symbols, leaving at most $24/4=6$ different values; the displayed operations attain all six rational expressions.

There are fewer than six values exactly when a nonidentity operation in this six-element action fixes $\lambda$: if two operations give the same value, composing one with the inverse of the other gives such a fixed point. For real $\lambda\ne0,1$, the fixed-point equations are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda=1/\lambda&\Longrightarrow\lambda=-1,\\
\lambda=1-\lambda&\Longrightarrow\lambda=\tfrac12,\\
\lambda=\lambda/(\lambda-1)&\Longrightarrow\lambda=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The other two equations reduce to $\lambda^2-\lambda+1=0$, which has no real root. Hence

$$
\boxed{\lambda\in\{-1,\tfrac12,2\}\text{ are exactly the real exceptional values}.}
$$

Each exceptional value has the same three-element [orbit](../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) $\{-1,\tfrac12,2\}$. Every other permitted real value gives six different values.

For the geometric interpretation, put $P_0=[1:0]=\infty$ and $P_i=[x_i:1]$ for $i>0$. Then

$$
\lambda=\frac{x_1-x_3}{x_1-x_2}.
$$

The three exceptional cases become

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda=-1&\Longleftrightarrow x_1=\tfrac12(x_2+x_3),\\
\lambda=\tfrac12&\Longleftrightarrow x_3=\tfrac12(x_1+x_2),\\
\lambda=2&\Longleftrightarrow x_2=\tfrac12(x_1+x_3).
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus **the three finite points are collinear and equally spaced, with one their midpoint**, even when the $x_i$ are viewed as points of the [complex plane](../../../../complex-plane.md). Equivalently they form an [arithmetic progression](../../../../arithmetic-progression.md) in some order. These are the [harmonic conjugate points](../../../../harmonic-conjugate-points.md) configurations with the fourth point equal to the [point at infinity of the complex projective line](../../../../point-at-infinity-of-the-complex-projective-line.md).
