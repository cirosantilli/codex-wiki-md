<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Choose three distinct [projective points](../../../../projective-point.md) $A,B,C$. Their images under the [injective](../../../../injective-function.md) map $\tau$ are distinct. Sharp triple transitivity gives a [projective transformation](../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md) $S$ agreeing with $\tau$ on these three points. The map $\sigma=S^{-1}\circ\tau$ fixes $A,B,C$ and preserves their [cross-ratios](../../../../cross-ratio.md).

For any other [projective point](../../../../projective-point.md) $P$, invariance gives

$$
(AB:C\,\sigma(P))=(AB:CP).
$$

Let $T$ normalize $(A,B,C)$ to $(\infty,0,1)$. By part (b), the left side is $T(\sigma(P))$ and the right side is $T(P)$. Since $T$ is a [bijection](../../../../bijection.md), $\sigma(P)=P$. It fixes the original three points too, so $\sigma$ is the [identity map](../../../../identity-function.md) everywhere. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\tau=S\text{ is a projective transformation}.}
$$

No continuity or prior [surjectivity](../../../../surjective-function.md) assumption was needed. This is the rigidity assertion that [cross-ratio preserving maps are projective](../../../../cross-ratio-preserving-maps-are-projective.md).
