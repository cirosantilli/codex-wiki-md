<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the [cross-ratio](../../../../cross-ratio.md) convention compatible with the permutation identities in this paper. Represent a [projective point](../../../../projective-point.md) $P_i\in\mathbb{CP}^1$ by a nonzero vector $p_i\in\mathbb C^2$, and write $[p_i,p_j]=\det(p_i,p_j)$. Define

$$
\boxed{(P_0P_1:P_2P_3)=\frac{[p_0,p_2][p_1,p_3]}{[p_0,p_3][p_1,p_2]}.}
$$

The denominator is nonzero because the four [projective points](../../../../projective-point.md) are distinct. Rescaling any one representative multiplies numerator and denominator by the same nonzero [scalar](../../../../scalar.md), so the definition is independent of the chosen [homogeneous coordinates](../../../../homogeneous-coordinate.md). In finite affine coordinates it reads

$$
(z_0z_1:z_2z_3)=\frac{(z_0-z_2)(z_1-z_3)}{(z_0-z_3)(z_1-z_2)},
$$

with the [point at infinity of the complex projective line](../../../../point-at-infinity-of-the-complex-projective-line.md) handled by the homogeneous formula.

A [projective transformation](../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md) is represented by an [invertible matrix](../../../../invertible-matrix.md) $A\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb C)$. Since $[Ap_i,Ap_j]=\det(A)[p_i,p_j]$, the two factors of $\det(A)$ in the numerator cancel the two in the denominator. Therefore **every [projective transformation](../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md) preserves this [cross-ratio](../../../../cross-ratio.md)**. This also shows that the formula descends from matrices to the [projective linear group](../../../../projective-linear-group.md), whose representatives differ by a nonzero common [scalar](../../../../scalar.md).
