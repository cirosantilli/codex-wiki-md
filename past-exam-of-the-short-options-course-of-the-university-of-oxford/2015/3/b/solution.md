<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The needed general-position theorem is **sharp triple transitivity of the [projective linear group](../../../../projective-linear-group.md) on the [projective line](../../../../projective-line.md)**: for any two ordered triples of distinct [projective points](../../../../projective-point.md), exactly one [projective transformation](../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md) takes the first triple to the second.

In particular, let $T_P$ take $(P_0,P_1,P_2)$ to $(\infty,0,1)$. In these coordinates,

$$
(\infty\,0:1\,z)=z,
$$

so [cross-ratio](../../../../cross-ratio.md) invariance gives $T_P(P_3)=\lambda$, where $\lambda=(P_0P_1:P_2P_3)$. Distinctness implies $\lambda\ne0,1$ and $\lambda$ is finite. The entire ordered quadruple is thus normalized to

$$
\boxed{(\infty,0,1,\lambda).}
$$

For a second quadruple $(Q_0,Q_1,Q_2,Q_3)$ with the same [cross-ratio](../../../../cross-ratio.md), its normalizing map $T_Q$ yields the same quadruple. Consequently

$$
\boxed{T_Q^{-1}\circ T_P(P_i)=Q_i\quad(0\leq i\leq3).}
$$

This proves the equivalence. Uniqueness on the first three points also shows that the projectivity carrying one ordered quadruple to the other is unique. The [cross-ratio normalization of four projective points](../../../../cross-ratio-normalization-of-four-projective-points.md) therefore gives a complete invariant for this ordered classification.
