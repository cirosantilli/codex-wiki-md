<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The given [unitriangular group of degree three over F3](../../../../unitriangular-group-of-degree-three-over-f3.md) has $27$ elements, just as $G$ does, so counting alone cannot distinguish them. Instead compare [element orders](../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md).

Write an element as $I+U$, with $U$ strictly upper triangular of size $3$. Then $U^3=0$. Over the [finite field](../../../../finite-field.md) $\mathbb F_3$, the [binomial theorem for commuting matrices](../../../../binomial-theorem-for-commuting-matrices.md) gives

$$
(I+U)^3=I+3U+3U^2+U^3=I.
$$

Every nonidentity element of $H$ consequently has [element order](../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $3$. In contrast, $x\in G$ has [element order](../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $9$, by part (b). An [isomorphism](../../../../isomorphism.md) preserves [element order](../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md), so

$$
\boxed{H\not\cong G.}
$$

Equivalently, the [exponent of a finite group](../../../../exponent-of-a-finite-group.md) is $3$ for $H$ and $9$ for $G$.
