<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [cyclic subgroup](../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) $\langle x^3\rangle$ is normal: [conjugation](../../../../conjugation.md) by $y$ sends $x^3$ to $x^{12}=x^3$, and [conjugation](../../../../conjugation.md) by $x$ fixes it. Modulo this [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md), the relation $yxy^{-1}=x^4$ becomes $yxy^{-1}=x$, so the [quotient group](../../../../quotient-group.md) is [Abelian](../../../../abelian-group.md). Thus the [commutator subgroup](../../../../commutator-subgroup.md) $G'$ is contained in $\langle x^3\rangle$.

Conversely the displayed relation gives the [commutator](../../../../commutator.md) $yxy^{-1}x^{-1}=x^3$, so $\langle x^3\rangle\subseteq G'$. Hence

$$
G'=\langle x^3\rangle\cong C_3,\qquad G''=\{e\}.
$$

Since $x$ has [element order](../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $9$, $x^3\ne e$, so the first [commutator subgroup](../../../../commutator-subgroup.md) is nontrivial. Therefore

$$
\boxed{G\text{ is solvable, with derived length }2.}
$$

Here [derived length](../../../../derived-length.md) counts the number of steps needed for the [derived series](../../../../derived-series.md) to reach the trivial [group](../../../../group-split.md).
