A [projective conic](../../../../projective-conic.md) in $\mathbb P^2(\mathbb C)$ is the zero locus of a nonzero homogeneous quadratic [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md), up to nonzero scaling of that [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md). Under an invertible linear change of homogeneous coordinates, its [symmetric matrix](../../../../symmetric-matrix.md) has rank $3$, $2$, or $1$. Over $\mathbb C$, the corresponding forms are a nonsingular conic such as $XZ-Y^2=0$, two distinct lines such as $XY=0$, or a double line $X^2=0$. In the last case the repeated factor records multiplicity, even though the underlying point set is one line.

A [projective line](../../../../projective-line.md) is the projectivization of a two-dimensional [linear subspace](../../../../vector-subspace.md) of $\mathbb C^3$, equivalently the zero locus of one nonzero linear form. For two such subspaces $U,W$, the [dimension formula for a sum of subspaces](../../../../dimension-formula-for-a-sum-of-subspaces.md) gives $\dim(U\cap W)\geq2+2-3=1$. A nonzero vector in the intersection therefore supplies a common projective point. Distinct lines have exactly one such point.
