<h1 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The identity $T=S^{-1}(ST)$ shows that $S,ST$ generate the same [group](../../../../../group-split.md). Direct multiplication gives

$$
S^2=-I,\qquad ST=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad(ST)^3=-I.
$$

Consequently their images $\bar S,\overline{ST}$ in the [quotient group](../../../../../quotient-group.md) $PSL(2,\mathbb Z)$ satisfy $\bar S^2=1$ and $\overline{ST}^{\,3}=1$. The [universal property of a group presentation](../../../../../universal-property-of-a-group-presentation.md) gives a [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) taking $u\mapsto\bar S$, $v\mapsto\overline{ST}$ from the stated presentation. It is [surjective](../../../../../surjective-function.md) because these images generate the target. Injectivity is not needed here.
