<h1 id="5/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At zero, the [Fourier transform](../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) is $\hat f(0)=\int f=1$, so $\lambda f(0)=1$. In particular $\lambda\ne0$ and $f(0)=1/\lambda$. The [Fourier inversion theorem](../../../../../fourier-inversion-theorem.md) at zero also gives

$$
f(0)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\hat f(s)\,ds=\frac\lambda{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}f(s)\,ds=\frac\lambda{2\pi}.
$$

Equating the two values yields $\boxed{\lambda^2=2\pi}$. Since the explicit normalized [Gaussian function](../../../../../gaussian-function.md) in part (i) has $f(0)>0$, here $\lambda=\sqrt{2\pi}$.
