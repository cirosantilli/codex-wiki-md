<h1 id="9/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The terms $r_iN_i$ give intrinsic per-capita population growth. The terms $-r_iN_i^2/K_i$ model intraspecific crowding in a [logistic equation](../../../../../logistic-differential-equation.md), while $-r_ib_{ij}N_iN_j/K_i$ model interspecific competition. Here $K_i$ is the single-species [carrying capacity](../../../../../carrying-capacity.md), and $b_{ij}$ measures the competitive effect of one unit of species $j$ relative to one unit of species $i$ in that species' crowding term. The last term $-\delta N_2$ is per-capita removal of grey squirrels: constant effort, rather than a constant number removed per unit time.

The intrinsic rates $r_1,r_2$ and removal rate $\delta$ have units of inverse time. The capacities $K_1,K_2$ have the same density units as the respective $N_i$; with both densities expressed in the same units, $b_{12},b_{21}$ are dimensionless. The two equations form a [Competitive Lotka-Volterra model](../../../../../competitive-lotka-volterra-model.md) with density regulation and harvesting.
