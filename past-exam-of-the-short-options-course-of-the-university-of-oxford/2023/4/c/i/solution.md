<h1 id="4/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [matrix exponential](../../../../../matrix-exponential.md) is smooth, and part (a) identifies its derivative at zero as the identity. The [inverse function theorem](../../../../../inverse-function-theorem.md) therefore gives a local [diffeomorphism](../../../../../diffeomorphism.md) from an open neighborhood $U_0$ of zero onto a neighborhood of $I$. Choose a sufficiently small [operator norm](../../../../../operator-norm.md) ball $U\subset U_0$ and let $V=\exp U$. This is again a [diffeomorphism](../../../../../diffeomorphism.md) onto an open set, since it is a restriction of the original one.

The ball $U$ is preserved by $A\mapsto A^T$ and $A\mapsto-A$. Since $(\exp A)^T=\exp(A^T)$ and $(\exp A)^{-1}=\exp(-A)$, $V$ is preserved by adjoint and inversion. For its local inverse $L$, injectivity also gives

$$
\boxed{L(Q^T)=L(Q)^T,\qquad L(Q^{-1})=-L(Q)}\qquad(Q\in V).
$$
