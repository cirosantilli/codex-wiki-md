<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The multiplicative [group](../../../../../group-split.md) $\mathbb F_p^\times$ is a [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) of order $p-1$, so choose a [primitive root](../../../../../primitive-root-modulo-n.md) $g$. Every nonzero residue is uniquely $g^j$, with $j$ taken modulo $p-1$. The condition becomes $(p-1)\mid kj$. Writing $d=\gcd(k,p-1)$, its solutions are $j=0,(p-1)/d,\ldots,(d-1)(p-1)/d$. Zero is not a solution. Therefore the number is

$$
\boxed{\gcd(k,p-1)}.
$$

This includes $p=2$. The [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) argument gives the exact count, whereas the bound of $k$ roots for a nonzero degree-$k$ [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) over a [field](../../../../../field.md) would only give an upper bound.
