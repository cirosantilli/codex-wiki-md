# Frobenius theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Integrable distribution](integrable-distribution.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_theorem_(differential_topology))

If $D=\bigcap_{i=1}^q\ker\alpha^i$ has constant rank, the Frobenius theorem says that $D$ is [integrable](integrable-distribution.md) exactly when its annihilator is closed under the [exterior derivative](exterior-derivative.md): equivalently,

$$
d\alpha^i=\sum_j\beta^i{}_j\wedge\alpha^j.
$$

This is also equivalent to involutivity of $D$.

For a nowhere-zero 1-form $\alpha$, the hyperplane distribution $\ker\alpha$ is tangent to hypersurfaces exactly when $\alpha\wedge d\alpha=0$. In [general relativity](general-relativity-split.md) this characterizes hypersurface-orthogonal vector fields and implies vanishing twist.
