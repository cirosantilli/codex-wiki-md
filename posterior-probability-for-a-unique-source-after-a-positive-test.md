# Posterior probability for a unique source after a positive test

↑ **Parent:** [Bayes' theorem](bayes-theorem.md)

Suppose exactly one of $N$ equally likely candidates is the source of an observation. A test is always positive for that source and is positive with probability $p$ for any nonsource candidate. For one randomly selected candidate, [Bayes' theorem](bayes-theorem.md) gives $P(G\mid+)=(1/N)/[(1/N)+p(1-1/N)]$, proving the displayed result. Even a small [false positive rate](false-positive-rate.md) can leave a substantial chance of a different source when there are many alternatives. The model concerns one selected candidate with the stated prior; searching many candidates and selecting a match requires accounting for that selection procedure.
