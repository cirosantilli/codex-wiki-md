# Zero-natural-length spring

↑ **Parent:** [Hooke's law](hooke-s-law.md)

An ideal spring with stiffness $k>0$ and zero natural length has extension equal to the distance between its endpoints. Its [elastic potential energy](elastic-energy.md) is exactly $k|\mathbf r_2-\mathbf r_1|^2/2$, and its force on endpoint 2 is $-k(\mathbf r_2-\mathbf r_1)$. Thus its Cartesian stiffness is isotropic and constant. Coupled systems of such springs have exactly linear displacement equations after a constant force, such as gravity, is absorbed into the [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) position. This special model differs from an ordinary finite-natural-length spring, whose transverse small-motion stiffness depends on its tension and equilibrium length.
