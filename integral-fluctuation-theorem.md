# Integral fluctuation theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Finite trajectory entropy production](finite-trajectory-entropy-production.md)

Normalization of the reverse law gives an exact finite identity:

$$
\left\langle e^{-\Sigma_F}\right\rangle_F
=\sum_\gamma P_F[\gamma]\frac{P_R[\Theta\gamma]}{P_F[\gamma]}=1.
$$

The sum notation describes discrete paths; the corresponding path [integral](integral.md) has the same change-of-variable argument. [Jensen's inequality](jensen-s-inequality.md) for the [exponential function](exponential-function.md) then gives $e^{-\langle\Sigma_F\rangle_F}\le1$, hence $\langle\Sigma_F\rangle_F\ge0$. Equivalently this mean is the [Kullback-Leibler divergence](kullback-leibler-divergence.md) between the forward path law and the reverse path law pulled back by $\Theta$. Equality requires those two laws to agree. [Seifert, 2005, Eqs. (17)–(18): integral relation and its mean inequality](entropy-production-along-a-stochastic-trajectory-and-an-integral-fluctuation-theorem-by-udo-seifert.md)

Rare negative [entropy production](entropy-production.md) contributes disproportionately to this [expectation](expected-value.md) through $e^{-\Sigma_F}$. Missing such events in a finite sample can spoil an estimate of the identity. Matching [probability support](support-of-a-probability-distribution.md) is also essential: reverse paths absent from the forward law cannot be recovered by cancelling forward weights. This [Second law of thermodynamics](second-law-of-thermodynamics.md) statement concerns an [ensemble average](expected-value.md); an individual negative value is compatible with it.
