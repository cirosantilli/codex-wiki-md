# Irreducible representation

↑ **Parent:** [Representation theory](representation-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_representation)

An irreducible [group representation](group-representation.md) is nonzero and has no nonzero proper [invariant subspace](invariant-subspace.md). These are the pieces that cannot be decomposed further by taking a smaller symmetry-preserved [vector space](vector-space-split.md).

The real two-dimensional rotation through $120$ degrees illustrates the role of the coefficient [field](field.md). A one-dimensional real [invariant subspace](invariant-subspace.md) would supply a real [eigenvector](eigenvector.md), but the characteristic polynomial $\lambda^2+\lambda+1$ has no real root. The [representation](group-representation.md) is therefore irreducible over the [real numbers](real-number.md). Over the [complex numbers](complex-number.md), that polynomial has distinct [roots of unity](root-of-unity.md) $e^{2\pi i/3}$ and $e^{-2\pi i/3}$, giving two invariant [eigenlines](eigenline.md). Irreducibility over the [real numbers](real-number.md) does not imply [absolute irreducibility](absolute-irreducibility-of-a-group-representation.md).
