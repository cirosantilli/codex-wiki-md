# Heat-capacity difference and mechanical stability

↑ **Parent:** [Difference between constant-pressure and constant-volume heat capacities](difference-between-constant-pressure-and-constant-volume-heat-capacities.md)

For a fixed-composition [simple compressible system](simple-compressible-system.md), a [Maxwell relation](maxwell-relations.md) gives $C_p-C_V=T(\partial p/\partial T)_V(\partial V/\partial T)_p$. At fixed [volume](volume.md), the differential [equation of state](equation-of-state.md) gives $(\partial p/\partial T)_V=\alpha_V/\kappa_T$, proving the displayed result. Positive absolute [temperature](temperature-split.md) and positive [isothermal compressibility](isothermal-compressibility.md) imply a nonnegative difference. It vanishes when [thermal expansion](thermal-expansion.md) does, so strict inequality requires a nonzero expansion coefficient. This stability argument includes nonideal internal-energy changes, unlike a purely ideal-gas expansion-work explanation.
