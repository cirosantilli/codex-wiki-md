# Rectangular-barrier tunnelling probability

↑ **Parent:** [Rectangular potential barrier](rectangular-potential-barrier.md)

For a [rectangular potential barrier](rectangular-potential-barrier.md) of height $V_0$, width $a$, and equal constant potentials outside, a particle with $0<E<V_0$ has exterior [wave number](wavenumber.md) $k=\sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$ and interior decay constant $\kappa=\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar$. Write the three [wavefunctions](wave-function-split.md) as $e^{ikx}+re^{-ikx}$, $Ae^{\kappa x}+Be^{-\kappa x}$, and $te^{ikx}$. Continuity of the [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) and its derivative at both interfaces determines the [transmission probability](transmission-probability.md) $T=|t|^2$ in the title. Equal exterior velocities are what permit the last equality.

For $\kappa a\gg1$, $\sinh^2(\kappa a)\simeq e^{2\kappa a}/4$, so

$$
T\simeq\frac{16E(V_0-E)}{V_0^2}e^{-2\kappa a}.
$$

Keeping only $e^{-2\kappa a}$ estimates the exponentially dominant [quantum tunnelling](quantum-tunnelling.md) suppression; the interface prefactor can still change a numerical answer by a factor of order one.
