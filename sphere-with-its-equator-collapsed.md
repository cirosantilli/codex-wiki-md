# Sphere with its equator collapsed

↑ **Parent:** [Quotient topological space](quotient-topological-space.md)

Collapse the equator of a [sphere](sphere.md) $S^2$ to a single [equivalence class](equivalence-class.md) and leave every other point in its own class. The two closed hemispheres are [disks](disk-mathematics.md) sharing that equator. A disk with its boundary collapsed is a sphere, so the two hemisphere quotients are spheres sharing just one point: their [wedge sum](wedge-sum.md). An explicit map sends height $t\in[0,1]$ and longitude $\theta$ on the upper hemisphere to $(1-\cos\pi t,\sin\pi t\cos\theta,\sin\pi t\sin\theta)$, and sends the lower hemisphere to the sphere centred at $(-1,0,0)$ by reversing the first coordinate. The equator goes to the common origin and the poles go to $(\pm2,0,0)$. This map is continuous and has precisely the collapsed equator as its only nonsingleton fibre. Its induced map is a [homeomorphism](homeomorphism.md) by the [compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem](compact-to-hausdorff-continuous-bijection-theorem.md).
