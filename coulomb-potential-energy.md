# Coulomb potential energy

↑ **Parent:** [Coulomb's law](coulomb-s-law.md)

The [Coulomb potential energy](coulomb-potential-energy.md) is the interaction [potential energy](potential-energy.md) of two [point charges](point-charge.md) in [vacuum](vacuum.md). With separation $r>0$, [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) $q_1,q_2$, $k=1/(4\pi\varepsilon_0)$ and zero [potential energy](potential-energy.md) at infinite separation,

$$
\boxed{U(r)=\frac{kq_1q_2}{r}.}
$$

This is mutual interaction [energy](energy-split.md); it does not include the divergent self-energies of ideal [point charges](point-charge.md). Unlike [electric potential](electric-potential.md), which has units of [volts](volt.md), $U$ has [SI](international-system-of-units.md) units of joules. With charge 1 fixed and positive radial direction away from it, the [conservative force](conservative-force.md) in [Coulomb's law](coulomb-s-law.md) gives

$$
U(r)-U(\infty)=-\int_\infty^r\frac{kq_1q_2}{s^2}\,ds=\frac{kq_1q_2}{r},\qquad
F_r=-\frac{dU}{dr}=\frac{kq_1q_2}{r^2}.
$$

Thus the $1/r$ [potential energy](potential-energy.md) and the [inverse-square law](inverse-square-law.md) for [force](force-split.md) are two descriptions of the same interaction. [OpenStax, University Physics II, §7.1: electric potential energy](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/7-1-electric-potential-energy).

For $q_1=+2\,\mathrm{nC}$, $q_2=-3\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $r=0.20\,\mathrm m$, using the stipulated approximation $k=8.99\times10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$,

$$
U=\frac{(8.99\times10^9)(2\times10^{-9})(-3\times10^{-9})}{0.20}\,\mathrm J=-2.697\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm J.
$$

At $r=0.40\,\mathrm m$, $U=-1.3485\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm J$. Separating the pair from $0.20$ to $0.40\,\mathrm m$ therefore requires external [work](work.md) $\Delta U=+1.3485\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm J$ if the initial and final [kinetic energies](kinetic-energy.md) agree; the [electric force](electric-force.md) does $-1.3485\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm J$ of [work](work.md). Separating them all the way to infinity requires $+2.697\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm J$. The negative initial [potential energy](potential-energy.md) means that the pair lies below the chosen separated reference; it does not mean a negative [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md).

Like-signed [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) have positive $U$, which decreases as they separate, so their [electric force](electric-force.md) is repulsive. Opposite-signed [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) have negative $U$, which decreases as they approach, so their [electric force](electric-force.md) is attractive. This attractive interaction supplies the [Coulomb potential](coulomb-potential-energy.md) used in the [hydrogen atom](hydrogen-atom.md); determining its stationary quantum states also requires [quantum mechanics](quantum-mechanics-split.md). [Feynman lectures III.19, §19–1: the Schrödinger equation for hydrogen](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/III_19.html#Ch19-S1). Dividing $U$ by a nonzero test [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $q_2$ gives the source's [electric potential](electric-potential.md) $\Phi_1=kq_1/r$. More generally, the [electrostatic potential energy of point charges](electrostatic-potential-energy-of-point-charges.md) sums each unordered charge pair once. This [energy](energy-split.md) description is useful because [work](work.md) between two separations follows from endpoint values, without integrating a complicated trajectory.
