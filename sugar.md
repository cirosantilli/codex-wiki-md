# Sugar

↑ **Parent:** [Carbohydrate](carbohydrate.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar)

In chemical usage, [sugars](sugar.md) commonly means small [carbohydrates](carbohydrate.md), including single-unit [monosaccharides](monosaccharide.md) and two-unit [disaccharides](disaccharide.md). [Glucose](glucose.md), [ribose](ribose.md), and [deoxyribose](deoxyribose.md) are [monosaccharides](monosaccharide.md). [Cellulose](cellulose.md) is instead a [polysaccharide](polysaccharide.md) assembled from many [glucose](glucose.md) units. The distinction counts chemically linked units; it does not classify every [carbohydrate](carbohydrate.md) by sweetness or biological role. See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 3.2: carbohydrates](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/3-2-carbohydrates).

A useful first description of a [monosaccharide](monosaccharide.md) asks how many [carbon](carbon.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) it contains and what its open-chain structure is. [Ribose](ribose.md) is an [aldopentose](aldopentose.md), a five-[carbon](carbon.md) [aldehyde](aldehyde.md) [sugar](sugar.md); [glucose](glucose.md) is an [aldohexose](aldohexose.md), with six [carbon](carbon.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). A [Fischer projection](fischer-projection.md) then records the [stereochemistry](stereochemistry-split.md), information missing from the [molecular formula](molecular-formula.md). See [McMurry, Organic Chemistry, 25.1: carbohydrate classification](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/25-1-classification-of-carbohydrates).
