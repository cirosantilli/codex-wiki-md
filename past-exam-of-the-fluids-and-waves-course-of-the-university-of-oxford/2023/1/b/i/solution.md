<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the [spherical symmetry](../../../../../spherical-symmetry.md) of the expansion, with fluid at rest at infinity and no superposed imposed flow. The radial [Laplace equation](../../../../../laplace-equation.md) gives

$$
\partial_r(r^2\phi_r)=0,
\qquad \phi_r=\frac{A(t)}{r^2},
\qquad \phi=G(t)-\frac{A(t)}r.
$$

The moving sphere is a material boundary: its [kinematic boundary condition](../../../../../kinematic-boundary-condition.md) is $u_r(R,t)=\dot R$. Consequently $A(t)=R^2\dot R$, and the [spherically symmetric incompressible radial flow](../../../../../spherically-symmetric-incompressible-radial-flow.md) has

$$
\boxed{\phi(r,t)=G(t)-\frac{R^2\dot R}{r},\qquad
\mathbf u=\frac{R^2\dot R}{r^2}\,\widehat{\mathbf r}}.
$$

Equivalently, its [volumetric flow rate](../../../../../volumetric-flow-rate.md) through any concentric sphere is $4\pi r^2u_r=4\pi R^2\dot R$, the rate of change of the enclosed [volume](../../../../../volume.md).
