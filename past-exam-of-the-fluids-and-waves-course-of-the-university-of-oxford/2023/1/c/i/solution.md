<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

With zero internal [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) and negligible [surface tension](../../../../../surface-tension-split.md), [pressure continuity](../../../../../pressure-continuity.md) sets the liquid-side boundary value to $p(R,t)=0$. Evaluating the [pressure field of a pulsating sphere](../../../../../pressure-field-of-a-pulsating-sphere.md) there gives

$$
0=\frac1R(R^2\ddot R+2R\dot R^2)-\frac12\dot R^2+\frac{p_\infty}\rho.
$$

Thus the [Rayleigh equation for an inviscid spherical bubble](../../../../../rayleigh-equation-for-an-inviscid-spherical-bubble.md), together with its [initial conditions](../../../../../initial-condition.md), is

$$
\boxed{R\ddot R+\frac32\dot R^2=-\frac{p_\infty}\rho,
\qquad R(0)=a,\quad\dot R(0)=0}.
$$

For $p_\infty>0$, initially $\ddot R(0)=-p_\infty/(\rho a)<0$, so the physically relevant branch describes [Rayleigh collapse of a spherical cavity](../../../../../rayleigh-collapse-of-a-spherical-cavity.md).
