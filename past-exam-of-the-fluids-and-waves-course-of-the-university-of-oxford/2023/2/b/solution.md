<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow](../../../../complex-potential-of-a-two-dimensional-potential-flow.md) gives

$$
q(z):=W'(z)
=-\frac{me^{i\alpha}}{(z-b)^2}
+\frac{ma^2e^{-i\alpha}}{(bz-a^2)^2}.
$$

At a [stagnation point](../../../../stagnation-point.md), $q=0$. For $m\ne0$, taking the two square-root alternatives gives

$$
bz-a^2=\sigma a e^{-i\alpha}(z-b),\qquad \sigma\in\{+1,-1\}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{z_\sigma
=a\,\frac{a-\sigma b e^{-i\alpha}}{b-\sigma a e^{-i\alpha}},
\qquad \sigma=\pm1}.
$$

The denominators are nonzero because $b>a$. Moreover

$$
|a-\sigma b e^{-i\alpha}|^2
=a^2+b^2-2\sigma ab\cos\alpha
=|b-\sigma a e^{-i\alpha}|^2,
$$

so both [stagnation points](../../../../stagnation-point.md) satisfy $|z_\sigma|=a$. They are distinct: equality would require $2e^{-i\alpha}(a^2-b^2)=0$. Neither is a singularity of $q$. Conversely, the numerator of $q$ is a quadratic polynomial with nonzero leading coefficient, so these are all its finite zeros. Thus there are exactly two boundary [stagnation points](../../../../stagnation-point.md) and none in the open fluid region. If $m=0$, the flow instead vanishes everywhere, which is the degenerate exception to the assertion.
