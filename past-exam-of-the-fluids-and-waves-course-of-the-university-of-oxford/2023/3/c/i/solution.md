<h1 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In the deep-water limit $kh\gg1$, $\tanh(kh)\sim1$. Put $\beta=B/\rho>0$. The squared [phase speed](../../../../../phase-speed.md) of the [flexural-gravity wave](../../../../../flexural-gravity-wave.md) is then

$$
c_p^2\sim\frac gk+\beta k^3.
$$

It diverges as $k\to0^+$ and as $k\to\infty$ in this limiting model. Its unique stationary point satisfies

$$
-\frac g{k^2}+3\beta k^2=0,
\qquad \boxed{k_* =\left(\frac{\rho g}{3B}\right)^{1/4}}.
$$

The second derivative $2g/k^3+6\beta k$ is positive. Using $g/k_*=3\beta k_*^3$ gives the [minimum phase speed of a flexural-gravity wave](../../../../../minimum-phase-speed-of-a-flexural-gravity-wave.md):

$$
c_{\min}^2=4\beta k_*^3
=\frac4{3^{3/4}}\beta^{1/4}g^{3/4},
\qquad
\boxed{c_{\min}=\frac2{3^{3/8}}
\left(\frac{Bg^3}\rho\right)^{1/8}}.
$$

This is the deep-water minimum: applying it to a finite depth requires $k_*h\gg1$. Otherwise the $\tanh(kh)$ factor must be retained in the minimization. Within the deep-water approximation, [phase speed](../../../../../phase-speed.md) and [group velocity](../../../../../group-velocity.md) coincide at $k_*$, although the wave family remains dispersive.
