<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $z=x+iy$,

$$
\operatorname{Im}g(z)=y\left(1-\frac1{x^2+y^2}\right).
$$

Both factors are positive in the fluid region, so its image is contained in the [upper half-plane](../../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md). The boundary correspondence is also explicit: on the upper unit [semicircle](../../../../../semicircle.md), $z=e^{i\theta}$ gives $g(z)=2\cos\theta\in[-2,2]$, while the real rays $x>1$ and $x<-1$ map onto the rays $\zeta>2$ and $\zeta<-2$.

To prove the image is the whole [upper half-plane](../../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md), fix any $\operatorname{Im}\zeta>0$. The equation $g(z)=\zeta$ is

$$
z^2-\zeta z+1=0.
$$

Its roots have product one. Neither root can have unit modulus, since that would make $\zeta=z+1/z$ real. Exactly one root therefore has modulus greater than one. For that root the formula for $\operatorname{Im}g$ forces $y>0$, so it belongs to the fluid region. Moreover, if $g(z_1)=g(z_2)$, then

$$
(z_1-z_2)\left(1-\frac1{z_1z_2}\right)=0.
$$

Two distinct roots in the fluid region would require $z_1z_2=1$, impossible when both moduli exceed one. Thus the [Joukowski map of the upper exterior of a unit disk](../../../../../joukowski-map-of-the-upper-exterior-of-a-unit-disk.md) is a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) from the fluid region onto the [upper half-plane](../../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md).
