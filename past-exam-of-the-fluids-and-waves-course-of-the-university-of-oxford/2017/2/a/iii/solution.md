<h1 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Define the [line vortex](../../../../../line-vortex.md) strength $\kappa$ by $\mathbf u=\kappa\mathbf e_\theta/(2\pi r)$, with $\kappa>0$ meaning counterclockwise motion. On an enclosing [circle](../../../../../circle.md) of radius $R$,

$$
\Gamma=\int_0^{2\pi}\frac\kappa{2\pi R}R\,d\theta=\kappa,\qquad M=0,
$$

because the [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) is tangent to the [circle](../../../../../circle.md). For any simple positively oriented enclosing curve, the same results follow by [Stokes theorem](../../../../../stokes-theorem.md) and the [divergence theorem](../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) on the region between that curve and a small inner [circle](../../../../../circle.md). Both [curl](../../../../../curl.md) and [divergence](../../../../../divergence.md) vanish throughout that intervening [annulus](../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md). Consequently

$$
\boxed{\Gamma=\kappa,\qquad M=0.}
$$

Equivalently, $\Gamma=(\kappa/(2\pi))\oint d\theta$ and the total angle change is $2\pi$. If strength is instead denoted by $K$ in $u_\theta=K/r$, the answer is $\Gamma=2\pi K$. An enclosing disk through the singular origin cannot be used to infer zero [circulation](../../../../../circulation-physics.md) from zero local [vorticity](../../../../../vorticity-split.md).
