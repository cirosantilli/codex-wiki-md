<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

On the translating [circular cylinder](../../../../circular-cylinder.md) $r=a$, the preceding [velocity potential](../../../../velocity-potential.md) and [streamfunction](../../../../stream-function.md) give

$$
\phi=-Ua\cos\theta,\qquad d\psi=Ua\cos\theta\,d\theta.
$$

Using the [boundary integral for kinetic energy of planar potential flow](../../../../boundary-integral-for-kinetic-energy-of-planar-potential-flow.md),

$$
T=-\frac\rho2\int_0^{2\pi}(-U^2a^2\cos^2\theta)\,d\theta
=\frac{\rho\pi a^2U^2}{2}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{T=\frac12MU^2,\qquad M=\rho\pi a^2.}
$$

As a direct check, $|\mathbf u|^2=U^2a^4/r^4$, and integration over $r>a$ gives the same result.

The fluid [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) has the same dependence on body speed as the [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) of an extra mass $M$ carried by the body. Thus $M$ is its [added mass](../../../../added-mass.md), here equal to the mass of displaced fluid per unit cylinder length. For a cylinder of physical mass per length $m$, the combined [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is $\tfrac12(m+M)U^2$. Accelerating the body requires accelerating the surrounding [fluid](../../../../fluid.md), producing the inertial reaction $-M\dot U$ along the direction of motion. This is the [added mass of a circular cylinder](../../../../added-mass-of-a-circular-cylinder.md).
