# Trapped-ion quantum control

↑ **Parent:** [Ion trap](ion-trap.md)

Trapped-ion quantum control prepares an [ion](ion.md)'s internal [quantum state](quantum-state.md), couples it to quantized motion with [laser](laser-split.md) pulses, and measures the result through state-dependent [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md). Its appeal is access to both a controllable [two-level system](two-level-system.md) and a [quantum harmonic oscillator](quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md) in one apparatus.

Confinement, preparation, control and readout solve different problems. The [Paul trap](paul-trap.md)'s RF [electric field](electric-field-split.md) confines the [ion](ion.md) through its [RF-averaged trapping potential](rf-averaged-trapping-potential.md); it still produces [Paul trap micromotion](paul-trap-micromotion.md). [Doppler cooling](doppler-cooling.md) and [resolved sideband cooling](resolved-sideband-cooling.md) reduce motional excitation. [Optical pumping](optical-pumping.md) selects an internal [quantum state](quantum-state.md). A coherent pulse then changes amplitudes, while [electron shelving](electron-shelving.md) converts an internal population into a detectable stream of [photons](photon.md). A bright/dark count by itself does not reveal a [quantum superposition](quantum-superposition.md)'s relative [phase](phase-waves.md); an additional controlled rotation before detection can make that [phase](phase-waves.md) affect the measured population.
