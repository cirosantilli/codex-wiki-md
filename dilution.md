# Dilution

↑ **Parent:** [Solution (chemistry)](solution-chemistry.md)

[Dilution](dilution.md) lowers a [solute](solute.md)'s [concentration](concentration.md) by adding [solvent](solvent.md). For conserved [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md), [molar concentration](molar-concentration.md) satisfies

$$
n=c_1V_1=c_2V_2,\qquad c_2=c_1\frac{V_1}{V_2}.
$$

Both $V_1$ and $V_2$ are [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) [volumes](volume.md); $V_2$ is the final total, not the added [solvent](solvent.md).[Chemistry 2e, §3.3, Dilution of Solutions: conserved moles and dilution equation](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/3-3-molarity.md)

The proof is simply the definition $n=cV$ applied before and after. Its useful assumptions are full transfer of the selected portion, no loss or gain of the counted [solute](solute.md), and complete mixing. An ideal [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) approximation is not required for this inventory equation. If [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md) redistributes dissolved [chemical species](chemical-species.md), use a conserved [analytical concentration](analytical-concentration.md) or [chemical element](chemical-element.md) inventory rather than assuming each individual species is conserved. This conservation statement does not by itself preserve the [pH](ph.md): a [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md) may change as the [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) is diluted.
