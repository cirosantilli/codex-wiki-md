# Solid angle

↑ **Parent:** [Sphere](sphere.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_angle)

A [solid angle](solid-angle.md) measures how much of the surrounding directions a region occupies, as seen from a specified point $O$. Draw a [sphere](sphere.md) of [radius](radius.md) $R$ centered at $O$ and mark where those directions meet its surface. If the marked patch has [surface area](surface-area.md) $A_R$, then

$$
\boxed{\Omega=\frac{A_R}{R^2}.}
$$

A [Euclidean similarity](euclidean-similarity.md) scales both $A_R$ and $R^2$ by the same factor, so the chosen [radius](radius.md) does not affect the answer. On the unit [two-sphere](two-sphere.md), the numerical value is simply the patch's [surface area](surface-area.md). Unlike an [angle](angle.md) drawn in one plane, a [solid angle](solid-angle.md) measures coverage in two angular directions.

For example, the full [sphere](sphere.md) has [surface area](surface-area.md) $4\pi R^2$ and hence [solid angle](solid-angle.md) $4\pi$ [steradians](steradian.md). A [hemisphere](hemisphere.md) occupies $2\pi$ [steradians](steradian.md). Three mutually perpendicular coordinate planes divide the directions into eight congruent pieces, so each octant occupies $4\pi/8=\pi/2$ [steradians](steradian.md). A [solid angle](solid-angle.md) of one [steradian](steradian.md) therefore covers $1/(4\pi)\approx7.96\%$ of all directions, rather than one full turn.

This makes a geometric question answer a collection question. For an ideal [point emitter](point-emitter.md) with [isotropy](isotropy.md), equal [solid angles](solid-angle.md) carry equal [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md). A detector accepting directions of total [solid angle](solid-angle.md) $\Omega$ intercepts the fraction $\Omega/(4\pi)$ of that emitted [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md), before losses. With direction-dependent [radiant intensity](radiant-intensity.md) $I$, the accepted [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) is instead $\int I\,d\Omega$. The [solid angle of a disk](solid-angle-of-a-disk.md) below gives an exact example where the familiar [inverse-square law](inverse-square-law.md) is only a distant-source approximation.
