<h1 id="section-b/23/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $q_i=z_i-z_i^{(0)}$ denote a small longitudinal displacement from equilibrium, and let every atomic mass be $m$. The constant equilibrium lengths disappear from the second derivatives. The [force constant matrix](../../../../../../force-constant-matrix.md) is

$$
K=\begin{pmatrix}
k&-k&0&0\\
-k&k+\gamma&-\gamma&0\\
0&-\gamma&k+\gamma&-k\\
0&0&-k&k
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

**The mass-weighted dynamical matrix is $D=K/m$**, with equations $\ddot{\mathbf q}=-D\mathbf q$. If the phrase dynamical matrix denotes the unweighted Hessian, that object is $K$ instead; the normal-mode equation is $K\mathbf v=m\omega^2\mathbf v$ in either notation. Every row sums to zero, as required for a freely translating chain.
