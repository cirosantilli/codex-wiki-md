<h1 id="section-b/22/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**The measured decay is approximately consistent with the predicted $198\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$ loss rate.** Following rapid singlet-to-triplet population transfer, the unquenched [phosphorescence](../../../../../../phosphorescence.md) intensity obeys

$$
I_P(t)=I_P(0)e^{-(k_P+k_D)t},\qquad \ln I_P=\ln I_P(0)-198t.
$$

A straight-line fit of $\ln I_P$ to the four times, expressed in seconds, gives

$$
\boxed{k_{\rm obs}=194.55\ \mathrm{s^{-1}},\qquad \tau_{\rm obs}=5.14\ \mathrm{ms},\qquad I_P(0)\simeq99.2.}
$$

This differs from $198\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$ by about $1.7\%$. For example, using the first point and the predicted rate gives subsequent intensities about $67.3,55.2,45.3$, close to the supplied $66.3,56.2,45.3$. The data support the expected millisecond [exponential decay](../../../../../../exponential-decay.md) to this precision. With no measurement uncertainties supplied, a formal statistical acceptance or rejection is not justified.
