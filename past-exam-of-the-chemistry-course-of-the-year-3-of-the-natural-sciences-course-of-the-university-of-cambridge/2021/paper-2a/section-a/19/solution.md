<h1 id="section-a/19/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [19](../19.md)

**Each site has a pi-electron population $4/3$, and each adjacent pi bond order is $2/3$.** The two carbon sites supply one [pi electron](../../../../../pi-electron.md) each and the nitrogen lone pair supplies two, giving four electrons in two occupied [molecular orbitals](../../../../../molecular-orbital.md). Orthogonality to the given [Homo](../../../../../homo.md) and [LUMO](../../../../../lowest-unoccupied-molecular-orbital.md) fixes the remaining occupied normalized orbital, up to overall sign, as

$$
\psi_1=\frac1{\sqrt6}(\phi_1+2\phi_2+\phi_3).
$$

For doubly occupied orbitals, the [Hückel orbital charge density](../../../../../huckel-orbital-charge-density.md) and [Hückel pi bond order](../../../../../huckel-pi-bond-order.md) follow from

$$
P_{ij}=2\sum_{a\in\mathrm{occupied}}c_{ia}c_{ja},\qquad
P=\begin{pmatrix}
4/3&2/3&-2/3\\
2/3&4/3&2/3\\
-2/3&2/3&4/3
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The diagonal sums to four electrons. The negative $P_{13}$ is an off-diagonal density-matrix element between nonadjacent sites, not an additional directly connected chemical bond.

Relative to neutral pi contributions $(1,2,1)$, the pi-system charges are $(-e/3,+2e/3,-e/3)$: electron density has moved from nitrogen toward the two carbon sites. Thus the physical [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) vector points **from the midpoint of the carbon pair toward nitrogen** along the molecular symmetry bisector. The conventional chemical dipole arrow, with a crossed positive tail, points oppositely: from nitrogen toward the carbon pair. This is only the pi contribution, not a prediction of the full sigma-plus-pi molecular dipole.
