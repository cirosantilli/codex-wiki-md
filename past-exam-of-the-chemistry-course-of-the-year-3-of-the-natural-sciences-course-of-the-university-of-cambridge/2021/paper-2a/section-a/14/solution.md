<h1 id="section-a/14/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [14](../14.md)

**The Stokes vibrational band has O, Q and S branches**, with $\Delta N=-2,0,+2$, respectively. Here $N$ denotes end-over-end [rotational angular momentum](../../../../../rotational-angular-momentum.md); treating electronic fine structure as unresolved gives the usual elementary rotor calculation. The absolute incident [wavenumber](../../../../../wavenumber.md) is

$$
\widetilde\nu_L=\frac{10^7}{1064}=9398.496\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1},
$$

and energy conservation for [Stokes Raman scattering](../../../../../stokes-raman-scattering.md) gives

$$
\widetilde\nu_s=\widetilde\nu_L-1580-B\{N'(N'+1)-N(N+1)\}.
$$

For the actual $^{16}\mathrm O_2$ ground state, only odd $N$ have nonzero nuclear-spin statistical weight. Thus the lowest initial levels are $N=3$ for O and $N=1$ for Q and S. Ignoring the small change in $B$ upon vibration, the lines are:

| Branch | Lowest transition | Rotational energy gained, in $B$ | Absolute scattered wavenumber, $\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ | O | $3\to1$ | $-10B$ | **7832.5** | Q | $1\to1$ | $0$ | **7818.5** | S | $1\to3$ | $10B$ | **7804.5** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

The odd-$N$ restriction follows from the zero nuclear spin and exchange symmetry of $^{16}\mathrm O_2$; the electronic spin splits each such $N$ into several $J$ levels without changing this simplified branch-centre calculation.[Section 3, manuscript page 4: only odd lower-state N has nonzero statistical weight for oxygen-16 and oxygen-18](../../../../../o2-a-band-line-parameters-to-support-atmospheric-remote-sensing-part-ii-by-david-a-long-and-others.md)

If the phrase simplest approximation is instead taken to discard nuclear-spin statistics as well, a fictitious unrestricted rotor gives O $2\to0$ at $7826.9\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and S $0\to2$ at $7810.1\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, with Q still at $7818.5\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. Those even-$N$ lines are not physical ground-state $^{16}\mathrm O_2$ lines.
