<h1 id="section-a/15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**The terms are $^3\Sigma_g^-$, $^1\Delta_g$ and $^1\Sigma_g^+$.** Closed [molecular orbital](../../../../../../molecular-orbital.md) shells contribute no net [spin angular momentum](../../../../../../spin.md) or axial orbital projection, so only the two equivalent $\pi_g$ electrons need be coupled. Their individual projections are $\lambda=\pm1$.

Putting both electrons in the same projection gives $|\Lambda|=2$ and forces opposite spins by the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md), yielding $^1\Delta_g$. With one electron in each projection, the spin-adapted symmetric and antisymmetric spatial combinations give a singlet $\Sigma$ and a triplet $\Sigma$. Their reflection parities are $+$ and $-$, respectively. Both open-shell electrons are gerade, so their product is gerade. The dimensions $3+2+1=6$ exhaust the $\binom42=6$ microstates. By [Hund's rules](../../../../../../hund-s-rules.md), the triplet is the lowest term, and $^1\Delta_g$ is the lower singlet.
