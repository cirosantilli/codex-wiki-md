<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use [Wade's rules](../../../../../polyhedral-skeletal-electron-pair-theory.md) with the metal included as a skeletal vertex. Each BH vertex supplies two skeletal [electrons](../../../../../electron.md), each CH supplies three, and the two additional [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) atoms supply two more. The $\mathrm{Ru(CO)_3}$ fragment supplies $8+3(2)-12=2$ skeletal [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) after the metal's non-skeletal orbitals have been accounted for. Thus

$$
N_{\mathrm{skeletal}}=3(2)+2(3)+2+2=16,\qquad \mathrm{SEP}=8=6+2.
$$

The six-vertex cluster is therefore **nido, with a pentagonal-pyramidal skeleton**: Ru is the apex, bonded to the five vertices of the planar $\mathrm{B_3C_2}$ base, and the three [carbonyl ligands](../../../../../carbonyl-ligand.md) project away from that base. This is the six-vertex fragment obtained by removing an equatorial vertex from a seven-vertex [pentagonal bipyramid](../../../../../pentagonal-bipyramid.md). The five ring atoms carry their terminal B–H or C–H bonds; the two additional [hydrogens](../../../../../hydrogen.md) belong to the hydrogen-rich open-face bonding framework. The skeletal [electron](../../../../../electron.md) count does not, by itself, locate every [bridging hydride](../../../../../bridging-hydride.md), so a unique detailed [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) arrangement should not be inferred from it.
