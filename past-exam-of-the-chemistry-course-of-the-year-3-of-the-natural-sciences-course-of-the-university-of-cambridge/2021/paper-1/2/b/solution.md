<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

There are **two carbon-placement isomers**, represented by the cyclic base sequences $\mathrm{CCBBB}$ and $\mathrm{CBCBB}$. The [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) vertices are adjacent in the first and separated by one [boron](../../../../../boron.md) vertex in the second. Rotation or reversal of a sequence does not produce another constitutional isomer.

Each arrangement has a [reflection symmetry](../../../../../reflection-symmetry.md) that exchanges a pair of [boron](../../../../../boron.md) sites while leaving the third distinct. Consequently each can give **two $^{11}\mathrm B$ environments, with site populations $2:1$**, in a [proton-decoupled NMR](../../../../../proton-decoupled-nmr.md) spectrum. This assumes that the [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) and [carbonyl](../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) arrangements preserve, or rapidly average to, the corresponding skeletal symmetry. Two peaks alone neither choose between the two skeletons nor establish a unique [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) arrangement; accidental overlap or [fluxionality](../../../../../fluxionality.md) can also reduce the number of observed signals.
