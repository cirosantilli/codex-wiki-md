↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The usual qualitative prediction is the **carbon-separated $\mathrm{CBCBB}$ isomer**. It has four B–C ring edges and one B–B edge, whereas the adjacent-carbon isomer has two B–C, two B–B and one C–C edge. Separating the more electron-rich [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) vertices distributes skeletal [electron](../../../../../electron.md) density more evenly and avoids concentrating both [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) vertices on one edge, a useful [carborane](../../../../../carborane.md) stability heuristic.

This is a thermodynamic suggestion, not a consequence of the peak count. [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) placement, metal–ring interactions and entropy can affect the ordering. Establishing the preferred isomer experimentally would require reversible interconversion and an equilibrium composition, rather than simply identifying the product formed fastest.
