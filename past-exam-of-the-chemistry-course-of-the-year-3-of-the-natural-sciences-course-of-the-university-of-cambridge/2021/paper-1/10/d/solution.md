<h1 id="10/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [bipyrimidine](../../../../../bipyrimidine.md) ligand has two distinct N,N-chelating pockets. It can use one pocket as a terminal [bidentate ligand](../../../../../bidentate-ligand.md), or use both to bridge two Eu centres. Take all three nitrates per Eu to be bidentate O,O donors.

A consistent structure for B is **$\mathrm{Eu(NO_3)_3(bpm)_2}$**, with two terminal, singly chelating bpm ligands. Six nitrate [oxygens](../../../../../oxygen.md) plus four bpm [nitrogens](../../../../../nitrogen.md) give **[coordination number](../../../../../coordination-number.md) ten**. The unused pocket of each terminal bpm points away from that Eu centre.

For C, use a dimer **$\mathrm{Eu_2(NO_3)_6(bpm)_3}$**. Place one bpm between the metals, chelating each Eu through a different N,N pocket. Add one terminal bidentate bpm to each Eu and three bidentate nitrates to each Eu. Each metal again has $2+2+6=10$ donor atoms, while the overall Eu:bpm ratio is $2:3$. In this model all four [nitrogens](../../../../../nitrogen.md) of the bridging bpm are used, but only two of each terminal bpm are used.

Both structures have their accessible coordination sites occupied by multidentate ligands, and the [chelate effect](../../../../../chelate-effect.md) favours retention of those donors over adding or exchanging a solvent molecule. This accounts for the absence of coordinated solvent while allowing a higher [coordination number](../../../../../coordination-number.md) than the [thiocyanate](../../../../../thiocyanate.md) example. Stoichiometry alone does not exclude polymeric alternatives or different nitrate binding modes; the monomer/dimer pair is an explicit internally consistent prediction.
