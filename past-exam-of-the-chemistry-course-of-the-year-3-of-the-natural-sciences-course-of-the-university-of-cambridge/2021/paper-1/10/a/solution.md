<h1 id="10/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Strong ligand-to-metal [pi donation](../../../../../pi-donation.md) is favoured by **an occupied ligand [p orbital](../../../../../p-orbital.md) and a suitable empty metal [d orbital](../../../../../d-orbital.md)** of matching symmetry, with reasonably similar energies and good spatial overlap. Small donor atoms such as O or N can supply short bonds and efficient overlap; oxo, imido and nitrido ligands are familiar examples. Early [transition metals](../../../../../transition-metal.md) in high [oxidation states](../../../../../oxidation-state.md), often $d^0$, are particularly favourable because the metal acceptor orbitals are vacant and relatively low in energy. More spatially extended $4d$ and $5d$ orbitals can strengthen the overlap.

Metal-to-ligand [pi backbonding](../../../../../pi-backbonding.md) is the complementary case: electron-rich, low-oxidation-state metals donate occupied [d orbitals](../../../../../d-orbital.md) into low-lying ligand [antibonding orbitals](../../../../../antibonding-orbital.md), as for CO. In both directions, filling a metal–ligand pi-antibonding orbital offsets the multiple-bond stabilization. The relevant prediction is orbital occupancy and overlap, rather than the presence of a formal double-bond line in a drawing.
