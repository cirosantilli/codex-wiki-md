<h1 id="10/c/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

An N-bound [thiocyanate](../../../../../../thiocyanate.md) need not make an exactly linear Eu–N–C linkage. Eu(III) bonding is predominantly electrostatic, with weak directional covalent contributions from the shielded $4f$ orbitals. There is consequently less energetic reward for enforcing a precisely collinear metal–donor arrangement than in a strongly directional transition-metal [pi bond](../../../../../../pi-bond.md). In the crowded eight-coordinate sphere, bending the external NCS groups can reduce ligand–ligand repulsion while retaining favourable Eu–N distances. **Angles of $150^\circ$–$160^\circ$ are compatible with bent N coordination**; they do not require changing the linkage to sulfur. This concerns the Eu–N–C angle, not bending the internal N–C–S skeleton.
