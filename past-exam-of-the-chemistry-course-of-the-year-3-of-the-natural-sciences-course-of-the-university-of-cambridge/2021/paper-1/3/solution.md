<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

**A [triangular prism](../../../../triangular-prism.md)** has exactly the required nine metal–metal edges: six edges around two triangular faces and three edges joining corresponding vertices of those faces. It naturally offers two symmetry-distinct edge families, of sizes six and three.

With twelve $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ ligands, neutral $\mathrm{Re_6Cl_{12}}$ has total rhenium oxidation number $+12$, hence six formally Re(II), $d^5$ centres. After treating the Re–Cl interactions as ligand bonds, the available metal $d$ [electrons](../../../../electron.md) are

$$
6(7-2)=30\ \mathrm{electrons}=15\ \mathrm{bonding\ pairs}.
$$

Two localized [bond order](../../../../bond-order.md) assignments use those fifteen pairs:

$$
\begin{array}{c|cc|c}
&\text{six triangular edges}&\text{three prism edges}&\text{total bond order}\\
\mathrm{I}&1&3&6+9=15\\
\mathrm{II}&2&1&12+3=15
\end{array}
$$

In scheme I the three inter-triangle edges should be shorter; in scheme II the six [triangle](../../../../triangle.md) edges should be shorter. Both give each Re five [electrons](../../../../electron.md)' worth of metal–metal bond-order contributions: two singles plus a triple, or two doubles plus a single. **The [electron](../../../../electron.md) count permits both schemes**; identifying which family is shorter distinguishes them at this level. Actual cluster [molecular orbitals](../../../../molecular-orbital.md) are delocalized, so these integer [bond orders](../../../../bond-order.md) summarize bonding rather than asserting isolated textbook multiple bonds on every edge.
