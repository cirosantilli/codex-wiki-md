The [isolobal analogy](../../../../isolobal-analogy.md) compares fragments through their [frontier molecular orbitals](../../../../frontier-molecular-orbital.md): their number, approximate energies, shapes, symmetries and [electron](../../../../electron.md) occupancies should correspond. It does not require the fragments to have the same total number of [electrons](../../../../electron.md) or the same constituent elements. If the orbitals available for forming new bonds correspond, analogous ways of joining the fragments become plausible.

For example, **$\mathrm{CH_3^{\bullet}}$ and $\mathrm{Mn(CO)_5^{\bullet}}$ are isolobal**. The [methyl radical](../../../../methyl-radical.md) has one singly occupied approximately outward-pointing orbital; removing one [carbonyl ligand](../../../../carbonyl-ligand.md) from an [octahedral molecular geometry](../../../../octahedral-molecular-geometry.md) leaves an analogous frontier orbital on the 17-electron $\mathrm{Mn(CO)_5}$ fragment. Joining two such fragments pairs the two [electrons](../../../../electron.md): the organic example is the C–C bond of [ethane](../../../../ethane.md), and the organometallic example is the Mn–Mn bond of $\mathrm{Mn_2(CO)_{10}}$. The analogy predicts a bonding pattern, rather than equality of bond energies or reaction rates.[1982, discussion of the methyl and metal-carbonyl fragment analogy](../../../../building-bridges-between-inorganic-and-organic-chemistry-nobel-lecture-by-roald-hoffmann.md)
