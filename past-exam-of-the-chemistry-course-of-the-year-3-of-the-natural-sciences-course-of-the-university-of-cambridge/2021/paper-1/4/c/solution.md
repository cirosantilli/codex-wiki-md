<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A natural feed can contain [thorium](../../../../../thorium.md) and [uranium](../../../../../uranium.md), as well as other [lanthanides](../../../../../lanthanide.md). Th(IV) hydrolyses readily and can accompany the cerium precipitate; [uranium](../../../../../uranium.md) speciation depends on its [oxidation state](../../../../../oxidation-state.md) and the ligands present. Raising [pH](../../../../../ph.md) too far also precipitates other rare-earth hydroxides. Even before their bulk [precipitation](../../../../../precipitation-chemistry.md) thresholds, adsorption and [coprecipitation](../../../../../coprecipitation.md) on a high-area hydrated cerium oxide can contaminate the solid. Local high-pH zones during base addition and incomplete Ce(III) oxidation make the separation less reproducible.

Thus **a cerium-rich precipitate need not be pure cerium**. A sensible test measures the Ce [oxidation state](../../../../../oxidation-state.md), analyses both filtrate and washed solid, and includes thorium-containing and thorium-free mixtures; otherwise low dissolved cerium could be mistaken for selective purification.
