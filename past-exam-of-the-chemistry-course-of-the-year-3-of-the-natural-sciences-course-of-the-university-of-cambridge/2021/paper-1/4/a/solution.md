<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The key is coupling [redox equilibrium](../../../../../redox-equilibrium.md) to [hydrolysis](../../../../../hydrolysis.md) and [precipitation](../../../../../precipitation-chemistry.md). Ce(IV) has a much greater tendency than Ce(III) to form a hydrated [cerium dioxide](../../../../../cerium-dioxide.md) solid. Raising [pH](../../../../../ph.md) therefore preferentially stabilizes the oxidized form. In the presence of dissolved [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) a representative net reaction is

$$
4\mathrm{Ce^{3+}}+\mathrm{O_2}+6\mathrm{H_2O}
\rightleftharpoons4\mathrm{CeO_2(s)}+12\mathrm{H^+}.
$$

Removing $\mathrm{H^+}$ drives this reaction towards Ce(IV). Writing the solid as hydrated oxide changes its water content, not this redox argument.

The [Nernst equation](../../../../../nernst-equation.md) makes the [pH](../../../../../ph.md) effect explicit. At $25^\circ\mathrm C$, the reduction $\mathrm{CeO_2+4H^++e^-\to Ce^{3+}+2H_2O}$ has a [pH](../../../../../ph.md) slope approximately $-0.236\ \mathrm{V}$ per [pH](../../../../../ph.md) unit at fixed Ce(III) activity. The [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md)/water reduction has slope about $-0.059\ \mathrm{V}$ per [pH](../../../../../ph.md) unit. Raising [pH](../../../../../ph.md) thus makes oxidation of Ce(III) by [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) increasingly favourable within the relevant phase-stability region. Conversely strong acid favours soluble Ce(III) relative to the oxide and makes Ce(IV) a stronger [oxidizing agent](../../../../../oxidizing-agent.md). **[pH](../../../../../ph.md) changes the redox driving force; an [electron](../../../../../electron.md) acceptor or donor is still required**, and slow [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) evolution or oxidation kinetics can prevent immediate equilibration.
