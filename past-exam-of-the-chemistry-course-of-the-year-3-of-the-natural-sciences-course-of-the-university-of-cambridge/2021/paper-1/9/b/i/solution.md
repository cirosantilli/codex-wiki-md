<h1 id="9/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Neutral counting assigns five [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) to [cyclopentadienyl ligand](../../../../../../cyclopentadienyl-ligand.md) and two to CO. Thus each $\mathrm{FeCpCO}$ fragment has $8+5+2=15$ [valence electrons](../../../../../../valence-electron.md) and contributes three skeletal [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md); each $\mathrm{CoCpCO}$ fragment has sixteen and contributes four:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm A:\quad&4(15)-12(4)=12\ \mathrm{skeletal\ electrons}=6\ \mathrm{pairs},\\
\mathrm B:\quad&3(16)-12(3)=12\ \mathrm{skeletal\ electrons}=6\ \mathrm{pairs}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For A the six-pair four-vertex count is the nido count derived from a five-vertex [trigonal bipyramid](../../../../../../trigonal-bipyramid.md). Removing an axial vertex leaves a **[tetrahedron](../../../../../../tetrahedron.md)**: the three equatorial vertices plus the remaining axial vertex. For B the corresponding arachno fragment is obtained by removing both axial vertices from that same parent, leaving a **three-metal [triangle](../../../../../../triangle.md)**. Small clusters require specifying the parent and removed vertices; mechanically calling every nido fragment a square pyramid is wrong.

The Fe tetramer is indeed known to have a tetrahedral $\mathrm{Fe_4}$ core, with four face-capping CO groups. The [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) atoms of those [carbonyls](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) and the iron atoms form interpenetrating tetrahedra, giving a cubane-like Fe–C connectivity. Bridging rather than terminal CO changes the distribution of local donation, not the total [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) count above.[1972, pp.3383–3388, tetrahedral iron core and face-capping [carbonyls](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md)](../../../../../../structural-characterization-of-the-cyclopentadienyliron-carbonyl-tetramer-fe4-eta5-c5h5-4-co-4-a-cubane-type-structure-containing-a-completely-bonding-tetrametal-cluster-unit-by-melvin-a-neuman-and-others.md)
