<h1 id="section-b/23/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose x along O–O. The valence orbital order appropriate to O$_2$ is approximately

$$
2\sigma_g(2s)<2\sigma_u^*(2s)<3\sigma_g(2p_x)<1\pi_u(2p_y,2p_z)<1\pi_g^*(2p_y,2p_z)<3\sigma_u^*(2p_x).
$$

Its twelve valence [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) occupy

$$
\boxed{(2\sigma_g)^2(2\sigma_u^*)^2(3\sigma_g)^2(1\pi_u)^4(1\pi_{g,y}^*)^1(1\pi_{g,z}^*)^1.}
$$

The two degenerate pi-antibonding orbitals are singly occupied with parallel spins according to [Hund's rules](../../../../../../hund-s-rules.md). Thus **$S=1$, [multiplicity](../../../../../../multiplicity-mathematics.md) $2S+1=3$**, with three possible $M_S$ values. A triplet is a many-electron spin classification, not three [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) in one orbital. The resulting ground term is ${}^3\Sigma_g^-$. Exchange stabilization favours this triplet over the singlets formed within the same frontier-orbital manifold.
