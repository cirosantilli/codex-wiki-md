↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In the Condon approximation the intensity from $v''=0$ to $v'$ contains the [Franck-Condon factor](../../../../../../../franck-condon-factor.md)

$$
|\langle\chi^A_{v'}|\chi^X_0\rangle|^2.
$$

The lower ground-state vibrational [wavefunction](../../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) is localized near its equilibrium bond length. If the two equilibrium lengths are similar, it overlaps strongly with the upper $v'=0$ [wavefunction](../../../../../../../wave-function-split.md), but its overlap with highly excited upper vibrational states is usually small or suffers oscillatory cancellation. If the equilibrium lengths differ enough that a vertical excitation reaches the outer portion of the upper well, high-$v'$ transitions can be strong while the $0\to0$ overlap is small. Thus **the two ends of a long progression are not normally both intense**. This is an overlap argument, not a ban on either set of transitions; anharmonicity, a varying electronic transition moment and unusual [wavefunctions](../../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) can modify the intensity envelope.
