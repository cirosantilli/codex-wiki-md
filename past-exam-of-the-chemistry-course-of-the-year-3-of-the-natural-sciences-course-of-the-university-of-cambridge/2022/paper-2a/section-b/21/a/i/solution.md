<h1 id="section-b/21/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The coarse spacing is about $2\widetilde B$, so $\widetilde B\simeq0.25\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$. The two groups near 1.5 and $2.0\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ therefore have **$J=2\to3$ and $J=3\to4$**, respectively. For positive $\widetilde D_{JK}$, increasing $K^2$ moves a component to lower [wavenumber](../../../../../../../wavenumber.md). The assignments are

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c|c}
J\to J+1&K&\widetilde\nu\ /\mathrm{cm^{-1}}\\ \hline
2\to3&0&1.50130\\
&1&1.50128\\
&2&1.50122\\ \hline
3\to4&0&2.00171\\
&1&2.00168\\
&2&2.00160\\
&3&2.00147
\end{array}}
$$

Within one group, successive separations scale as $(K+1)^2-K^2=1,3,5,\ldots$. The observed gaps follow that pattern to their printed precision. There are $J+1$ distinct $|K|$ components because $|K|\leq J$ in the lower state; the $+K$ and $-K$ members remain coincident without an applied field. A high-resolution feature here is therefore not simply assigned to an arbitrary magnetic sublevel.
