<h1 id="section-b/21/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The subbranch spacing gives $\widetilde A-\widetilde B=4.9\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, hence **$\widetilde A\simeq5.15\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$**. Rotation about the C–I [symmetry](../../../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) axis has moment

$$
I_a=\frac{h}{8\pi^2c\widetilde A}\simeq5.44\times10^{-47}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2}.
$$

C and I lie on this axis, so only the three [hydrogens](../../../../../../../hydrogen.md) contribute to $I_a$. If $\theta$ is the I–C–H angle, their perpendicular distances are $r_{\rm CH}\sin\theta$. Taking integer [hydrogen](../../../../../../../hydrogen.md) mass gives

$$
I_a=3m_Hr_{\rm CH}^2\sin^2\theta,\qquad
\sin^2\theta\simeq\frac{5.44\times10^{-47}}{3(1.66054\times10^{-27})(1.1\times10^{-10})^2}\simeq0.902.
$$

The molecular arrangement selects the obtuse solution, so **$\theta\simeq108^\circ$**. The supplementary acute angle describes the opposite axial direction, not the bond angle to iodine. This is a rigid-geometry estimate based on an effective [rotational constant](../../../../../../../rotational-constant.md) and the stipulated bond length, rather than a precision equilibrium-structure measurement.
