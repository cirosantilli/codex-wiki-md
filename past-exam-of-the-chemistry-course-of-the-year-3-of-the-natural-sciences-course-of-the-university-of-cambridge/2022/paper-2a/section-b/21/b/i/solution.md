<h1 id="section-b/21/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A regularly repeated set of sharp features is characteristic of the unresolved **Q subbranches of a [perpendicular rovibrational band](../../../../../../../perpendicular-rovibrational-band.md)**. The transition dipole is perpendicular to the [symmetry](../../../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) axis, giving $\Delta K=\pm1$; for the [Q branch](../../../../../../../q-branch.md) $\Delta J=0$. Holding the [rotational constants](../../../../../../../rotational-constant.md) fixed between vibrational states cancels the $BJ(J+1)$ term, leaving

$$
\widetilde\nu_Q=\widetilde\nu_v+(\widetilde A-\widetilde B)\big[(K\pm1)^2-K^2\big]
=\widetilde\nu_v+(\widetilde A-\widetilde B)(\pm2K+1).
$$

Many J transitions therefore coincide within each K-changing Q subbranch in this approximation. The different K subbranches are separated by

$$
\boxed{\Delta\widetilde\nu_Q=2(\widetilde A-\widetilde B).}
$$

The P and [R branches](../../../../../../../r-branch.md) have $\Delta J=-1$ and $+1$, respectively. Their extra J-dependent terms spread many transitions across a broad background at modest resolution. A parallel band, with $\Delta K=0$, would not generate this repeated Q-subbranch spacing under the stated assumptions.
