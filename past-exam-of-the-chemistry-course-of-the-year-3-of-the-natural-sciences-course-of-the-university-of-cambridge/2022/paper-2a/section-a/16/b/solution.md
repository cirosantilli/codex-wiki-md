<h1 id="section-a/16/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Rearrangement gives the [Stern-Volmer equation](../../../../../../stern-volmer-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{\frac{\phi_0}{\phi_Q}=1+k_Q\tau_0[Q].}
$$

Thus a straight-line plot against $[Q]$ has unit intercept and slope $K_{\rm SV}=k_Q\tau_0$. **Determine $k_Q=K_{\rm SV}/\tau_0$ using an independently measured unquenched lifetime**, or use known $k_F$ in the restricted model. Yield measurements alone do not separate two unknown [rate constants](../../../../../../rate-constant.md). Equivalently the slope of $1/\phi_Q$ is $k_Q/k_F$.

Use the absorbed intensity rather than incident intensity to normalize samples, and correct solvent/background signals, quencher [absorption](../../../../../../absorption-electromagnetic-radiation.md) and [fluorescence](../../../../../../fluorescence-split.md) reabsorption. Under [dynamic fluorescence quenching](../../../../../../dynamic-fluorescence-quenching.md) the lifetime ratio should follow the same straight line as the yield ratio. A reduced intensity without the corresponding lifetime change suggests [static fluorescence quenching](../../../../../../static-fluorescence-quenching.md) or an optical artefact; curvature can also indicate multiple populations or more complicated kinetics.
