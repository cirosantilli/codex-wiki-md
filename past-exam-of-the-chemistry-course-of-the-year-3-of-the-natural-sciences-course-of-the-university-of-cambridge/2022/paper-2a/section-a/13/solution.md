<h1 id="section-a/13/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [13](../13.md)

Using mass $7\,\mathrm u$ for the dominant [lithium](../../../../../lithium.md) isotope gives a transition [wavenumber](../../../../../wavenumber.md) $\widetilde\nu_0=14925\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$. Inserting the thermal speed into the stated [Doppler broadening](../../../../../doppler-broadening.md) expression gives

$$
\Delta\widetilde\nu_D={\widetilde\nu_0\over c}\sqrt{\frac{2k_BT\ln2}{m}}
\simeq0.128\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}.
$$

The supplied formula is the Gaussian [half width at half maximum](../../../../../half-width-at-half-maximum.md); the corresponding full width is about $0.256\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$. Meanwhile exponential population decay with lifetime $\tau$ produces a [natural linewidth](../../../../../natural-linewidth.md) expressed as a full width

$$
\delta\widetilde\nu_{\rm nat}=\frac{1}{2\pi c\tau}\simeq1.97\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}.
$$

Here $c$ must be in $\mathrm{cm\,s^{-1}}$ in the second expression. Equivalently the fine-structure separation is about $9.0$ GHz, the Doppler half width about $3.83$ GHz, and the [natural linewidth](../../../../../natural-linewidth.md) expressed as a full width about $5.89$ MHz. The [natural linewidth](../../../../../natural-linewidth.md) is therefore negligible compared with thermal broadening; low density also makes collision broadening less important.

**The doublet is resolvable in principle, but only marginally against the Doppler width.** The separation exceeds one Doppler full width, so two components can survive, although their profiles overlap strongly. The $J=3/2$ and $J=1/2$ upper levels have different [statistical weights](../../../../../statistical-weight.md), so the weaker member need not make an equally conspicuous maximum. A [spectral resolving power](../../../../../spectral-resolving-power.md) $R\gtrsim\widetilde\nu_0/0.3\simeq5\times10^4$ is the basic instrumental requirement; a width well below $0.1\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ and adequate signal would make the test more convincing. An arbitrarily fine spectrometer cannot remove the physical Doppler overlap. This conclusion uses the stipulated thermal, optically thin picture; additional photospheric velocity fields or optical-depth effects would require more information.
