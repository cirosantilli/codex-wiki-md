<h1 id="section-a/17/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Writing $A(\mathbf r)=\phi_{1sA}(\mathbf r)$ and $B(\mathbf r)=\phi_{1sB}(\mathbf r)$, the normalized [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) is

$$
\boxed{\Psi_{\rm UHF}={1\over\sqrt2}\left[A(\mathbf r_1)\alpha(\omega_1)B(\mathbf r_2)\beta(\omega_2)-B(\mathbf r_1)\beta(\omega_1)A(\mathbf r_2)\alpha(\omega_2)\right].}
$$

Its two [spin orbitals](../../../../../../spin-orbital.md) are orthogonal because $\langle\alpha|\beta\rangle=0$, even if the spatial atomic functions have nonzero overlap. Exchanging both the spatial and spin coordinates of the two [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) reverses the sign, as required for fermionic antisymmetry.
