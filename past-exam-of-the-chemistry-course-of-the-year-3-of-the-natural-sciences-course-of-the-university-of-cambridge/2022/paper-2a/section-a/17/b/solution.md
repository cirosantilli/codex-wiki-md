<h1 id="section-a/17/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At this separation the [orbital overlap](../../../../../../orbital-overlap.md) and interatomic [two-electron exchange integrals](../../../../../../two-electron-exchange-integral.md) are negligible. Each localized [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) then contributes essentially the isolated [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) energy, while the interatomic electrostatic terms cancel to leading order for neutral fragments. Including nuclear repulsion in the molecular energy gives

$$
\boxed{E_{\rm UHF}\simeq2(-\tfrac12 E_h)=-1E_h\simeq-27.2\ \mathrm{eV}.}
$$

The closed-shell [RHF](../../../../../../restricted-hartree-fock-method.md) [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) instead doubly occupies $g\simeq(A+B)/\sqrt2$. It retains ionic configurations with both [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) on the same atom, even at dissociation. Using [atomic units](../../../../../../atomic-units.md) and the [Hartree energy](../../../../../../hartree-energy.md) in the stated unrelaxed hydrogenic 1s basis, the same-atom [two-electron Coulomb integral](../../../../../../two-electron-coulomb-integral.md) is $J_{AA}=J_{BB}=5E_h/8$. Consequently at infinite separation

$$
E_{\rm RHF}\simeq-1+\tfrac14(J_{AA}+J_{BB})=-\frac{11}{16}E_h\simeq-18.7\ \mathrm{eV},
$$

where energies and integrals inside the first expression are in [atomic units](../../../../../../atomic-units.md). It is much too high compared with two neutral atoms. Retaining the leading intersite Coulomb term at $R=10\ \text{Å}=18.90a_0$ gives $E_{\rm RHF}\simeq-11/16-1/(2R)\simeq-0.714E_h$, still about $0.286E_h$ above [UHF](../../../../../../unrestricted-hartree-fock-method.md). The numerical [RHF](../../../../../../restricted-hartree-fock-method.md) estimates specify the supplied fixed 1s basis; a larger optimized orbital space changes them. If nuclear repulsion is excluded by an electronic-energy convention, subtract the same $1/R$ from both quoted total molecular energies. The restricted/unrestricted comparison and their dissociation behaviour are unchanged.[1949, pp. 386–393, minimal atomic-1s molecular-orbital treatment](../../../../../../xxxiv-notes-on-the-molecular-orbital-treatment-of-the-hydrogen-molecule-by-charles-alfred-coulson-and-inga-fischer-hjalmars.md)
