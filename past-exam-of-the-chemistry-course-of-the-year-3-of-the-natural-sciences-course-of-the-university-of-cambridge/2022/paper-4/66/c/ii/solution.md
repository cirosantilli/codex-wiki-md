<h1 id="66/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The three regions represent competing pathways whose leaving-group sensitivities differ.

- **Region 1, $\beta_{\rm lg}=-1.33$:** good leaving groups permit [E1cB mechanism](../../../../../../e1cb-mechanism.md). OH$^-$ first deprotonates the para phenol. The phenoxide lone pair then pushes through the ring, with loss of ArO$^-$ from the acyl group, giving a [quinoid ketene](../../../../../../quinoid-ketene.md), schematically $\mathrm{O=C_6H_4=C=O}$. Water/[hydroxide](../../../../../../hydroxide.md) traps the ketene and proton transfers restore the aromatic hydroxybenzoate. Rate-limiting fragmentation has extensive C–OAr cleavage, hence strong dependence on leaving-group basicity.
- **Region 2, $\beta_{\rm lg}=-0.35$:** as ArO$^-$ becomes a worse leaving group, elimination slows steeply and ordinary [hydroxide](../../../../../../hydroxide.md) addition to neutral C becomes competitive/dominant. The tetrahedral acyl-substitution pathway is much less sensitive to leaving-group p$K_a$.
- **Region 3, $\beta_{\rm lg}=-0.20$:** [hydroxide](../../../../../../hydroxide.md) attacks the deprotonated phenoxide [ester](../../../../../../ester.md) C$^-$. Its low electrophilicity and anion-anion interaction make addition slow. The leaving group is little involved in that addition transition state. Combined with prior [deprotonation](../../../../../../deprotonation.md), this is the third-order term.

The bend in the upper plot is a crossover of mechanisms, not a discontinuous change in the identity of the leaving group. The lower plot is the separate hydroxide-on-phenoxide pathway.
