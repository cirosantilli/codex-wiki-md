<h1 id="66/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The kink indicates that **two pathways with different leaving-group sensitivities compete**: one dominates for good leaving groups and another for poorer ones. A single straight-line free-energy relationship is insufficient. The additional hydroxide-order information can be accommodated by rapid phenol [deprotonation](../../../../../deprotonation.md),

$$
\mathrm{C+OH^-\rightleftharpoons C^-+H_2O},\qquad
[\mathrm{C^-}]=K_{\rm OH}[\mathrm C][\mathrm{OH^-}].
$$

Two rates are then possible. A route with one [hydroxide](../../../../../hydroxide.md) equivalent in its overall kinetic dependence contributes $k_2[\mathrm C][\mathrm{OH^-}]$. Attack of a **further [hydroxide](../../../../../hydroxide.md)** on phenoxide [ester](../../../../../ester.md) contributes

$$
k_{\rm add}[\mathrm{C^-}][\mathrm{OH^-}]
=k_{\rm add}K_{\rm OH}[\mathrm C][\mathrm{OH^-}]^2.
$$

Thus $k_3=k_{\rm add}K_{\rm OH}$. The third-order term need not involve a simultaneous collision of three molecules: sequential acid-base equilibration followed by bimolecular attack produces it.
