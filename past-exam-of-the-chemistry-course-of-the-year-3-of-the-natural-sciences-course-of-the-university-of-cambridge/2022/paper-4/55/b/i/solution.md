<h1 id="55/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write Im for an imidazolyl group. The acid protonates one imidazole nitrogen, and its carboxylate attacks the central [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) of [carbonyldiimidazole](../../../../../../carbonyldiimidazole.md). Collapse of the [tetrahedral intermediate](../../../../../../tetrahedral-intermediate.md) expels imidazole and gives the mixed anhydride $\mathrm{RCO-O-CO-Im}$. Acyl transfer to imidazole, with decarboxylation, produces the activated acyl imidazole $\mathrm{RCO-Im}$, CO$_2$ and imidazole.

The amine attacks the **RCO [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md)** of that acyl imidazole. Its [tetrahedral intermediate](../../../../../../tetrahedral-intermediate.md) collapses with loss of imidazole; proton transfer gives $\mathrm{RCONHR'}$. The [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) originally belonging to CDI leaves as CO$_2$, while the product amide [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) is the original acid [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md). Imidazole serves both as a leaving group and a proton-transfer base.
