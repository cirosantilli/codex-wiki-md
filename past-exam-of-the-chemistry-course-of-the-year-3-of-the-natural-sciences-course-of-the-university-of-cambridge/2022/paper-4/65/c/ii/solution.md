<h1 id="65/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Potassium is electropositive and transfers [electron density](../../../../../../electron-density.md) toward the metal. The resulting surface dipole generally lowers the metal [work function](../../../../../../work-function.md) at such submonolayer coverages, reducing the tunnelling barrier and its inverse decay length $q\propto\sqrt{\Phi_{\rm effective}}$.

At the same specified $5\,\mathrm{\mathring A}$ separation, a smaller $q$ gives a larger $\exp(-2qs)$, so the expected tunnelling current is **greater than $1\,\mathrm{nA}$** in the barrier-dominated comparison. No numerical value follows without the modified [work function](../../../../../../work-function.md) and electronic [density of states](../../../../../../density-of-states.md). A real STM image also depends on the local [density of states](../../../../../../density-of-states.md) and adsorbate corrugation; equal geometric separation does not imply equal electronic barrier height.
