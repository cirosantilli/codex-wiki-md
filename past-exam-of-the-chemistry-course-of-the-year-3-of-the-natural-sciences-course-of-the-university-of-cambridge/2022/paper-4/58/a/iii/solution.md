<h1 id="58/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For a strong $h$ term, maxima lie where $S(h)\cos(2\pi hx)=+1$. At either possible set of such maxima, $\cos(4\pi hx)=+1$: doubling a phase of either $0$ or $\pi$ gives zero modulo $2\pi$. A strong $2h$ term reinforces those positive maxima when $S(2h)=+1$. The [direct-methods pair relationship](../../../../../../direct-methods-pair-relationship.md) is therefore

$$
S(h)^2S(2h)\simeq+1,\qquad\hbox{hence }S(2h)\simeq+1.
$$

A negative strong second harmonic would oppose the maxima favored by the first and tend to create implausible negative or flattened density elsewhere. The relationship is a strong-reflection inference, not a sign identity for arbitrary weak coefficients.
