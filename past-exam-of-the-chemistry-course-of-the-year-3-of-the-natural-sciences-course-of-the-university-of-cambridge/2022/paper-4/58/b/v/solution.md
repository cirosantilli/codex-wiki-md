Set $\mathbf k=\mathbf h$ in the [direct-methods triplet relationship](../../../../../../direct-methods-triplet-relationship.md). It becomes $S(\mathbf h)^2S(2\mathbf h)\simeq+1$, and since the square of a nonzero sign is one, $S(2\mathbf h)\simeq+1$. This recovers the [direct-methods pair relationship](../../../../../../direct-methods-pair-relationship.md), with the same strong-amplitude qualifications.
