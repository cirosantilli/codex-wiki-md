<h1 id="51/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

All three marked points are at strongly oxidizing potentials, where X is depleted at the electrode: $c_X(0)\simeq0$. Assuming only X initially in bulk, $c_{X^+}(\infty)=0$ and $c_X(\infty)=c_b$. Oxidation produces X$^+$ at the interface.

- **A:** the largest steady limiting current corresponds to the fastest rotation and thinnest [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) boundary layer. X rises steeply from zero to $c_b$; X$^+$ falls steeply from its surface value to zero.
- **B:** slower rotation gives a thicker layer and shallower gradients, hence a smaller limiting current.
- **C:** the nonrotating electrode gives the peaked, subsequently decaying current. Its [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) layer continues to broaden with time, approximately as $\sqrt{Dt}$, giving the broadest and shallowest profiles at the indicated late-scan point.

For equal [diffusion coefficients](../../../../../diffusion-coefficient.md), the two concentration profiles are complementary, $c_X+c_{X^+}=c_b$. More generally equality of their interfacial molar fluxes requires $D_Xc_X'(0)=-D_{X^+}c_{X^+}'(0)$, so the product surface concentration need not equal $c_b$. A and B are approximately steady hydrodynamic profiles; C is a time-dependent [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) profile.
