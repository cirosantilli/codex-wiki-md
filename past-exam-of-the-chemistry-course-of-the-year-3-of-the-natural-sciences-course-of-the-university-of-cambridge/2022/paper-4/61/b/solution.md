<h1 id="61/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Far from a neutral bound system, the slowest-decaying electron-removal amplitude is controlled by the first ionization energy $I=E_{N-1}-E_N>0$. Its asymptotic [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) satisfies a free-particle-like equation at negative energy $-I$, giving a decay exponent $\kappa=\sqrt{2m_eI}/\hbar$. Density is quadratic in the amplitude, so

$$
\boxed{a=2\kappa=\frac{2\sqrt{2m_eI}}{\hbar}}
$$

or $a=2\sqrt{2I}$ in [atomic units](../../../../../atomic-units.md). Power-law radial factors and angular dependence do not alter this leading exponential coefficient. It is the ionization energy, not the electron affinity, that controls this tail.
