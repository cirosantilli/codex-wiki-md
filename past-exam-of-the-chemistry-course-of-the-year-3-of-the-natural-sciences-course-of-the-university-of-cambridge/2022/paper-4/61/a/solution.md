<h1 id="61/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For fixed electron-electron interaction and electron number, a nondegenerate ground-state [electron density](../../../../../electron-density.md) determines the external potential up to an additive constant, and hence determines the Hamiltonian and ground-state observables.

Suppose two potentials $v,v'$ differing by more than a constant have different [ground states](../../../../../ground-state.md) $\Psi,\Psi'$ but the same density $\rho$. Strict [Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle](../../../../../rayleigh-ritz-variational-principle.md) inequalities give

$$
E< E'+\int[v(\mathbf r)-v'(\mathbf r)]\rho(\mathbf r)\,d^3r,
$$



$$
E'< E+\int[v'(\mathbf r)-v(\mathbf r)]\rho(\mathbf r)\,d^3r.
$$

Adding produces the contradiction $E+E'<E+E'$. This proves the first [Hohenberg-Kohn theorem](../../../../../hohenberg-kohn-theorem.md) under the stated nondegeneracy assumptions. A constant potential shift changes energy by $N$ times that constant without changing the density.[Density-potential uniqueness argument](../../../../../inhomogeneous-electron-gas-by-pierre-hohenberg-and-walter-kohn.md)
