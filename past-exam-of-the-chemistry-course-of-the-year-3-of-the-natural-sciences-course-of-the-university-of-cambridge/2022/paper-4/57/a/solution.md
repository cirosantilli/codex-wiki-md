<h1 id="57/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The useful feature is the selenium atom's wavelength-dependent [anomalous X-ray scattering](../../../../../anomalous-x-ray-scattering.md). Close to its absorption edge the atomic scattering factor is complex:

$$
f_{\rm Se}=f_0+f'(\lambda)+if''(\lambda),\qquad
F(\mathbf h,\lambda)=\sum_j f_j(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\mathbf h\cdot\mathbf r_j}.
$$

Replacing a sulfur-containing side chain by its selenium analogue introduces relatively strong, spatially localized anomalous scatterers. This is the diffraction-physics reason for the label; no biological preparation procedure is needed for the calculation.

The Se substructure can be located from intensity differences or [Patterson maps](../../../../../patterson-map.md). Measurements at several wavelengths change its known complex contribution while leaving most other atoms' scattering nearly unchanged. Interference between that contribution and the unknown remainder constrains the otherwise missing [structure factor](../../../../../structure-factor.md) phases. Both wavelength-dependent dispersive differences and differences between [Friedel pairs](../../../../../friedel-pair.md) contribute to [multiwavelength anomalous diffraction](../../../../../multiwavelength-anomalous-diffraction.md). Several wavelength constraints resolve phase ambiguities that remain with one intensity difference. The resulting phased Fourier synthesis yields the electron-density map.
