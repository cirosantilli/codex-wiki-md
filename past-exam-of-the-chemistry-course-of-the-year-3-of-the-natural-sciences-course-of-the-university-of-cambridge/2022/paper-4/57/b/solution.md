<h1 id="57/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Patterson maps](../../../../../patterson-map.md) give interatomic displacement vectors rather than absolute positions. The three strong half-cell vectors are consistent with a face-centered four-point Se substructure. One permissible coordinate set is

$$
(0,0,0),\quad(0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12),\quad
(\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12),\quad(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,0).
$$

Each nonzero vector occurs four times among the twelve ordered Se-Se pairs, explaining equal weights. These positions form a valid four-site orbit of the specified [space group](../../../../../space-group.md) in a suitable origin convention.

A common translation of all four sites is invisible to a [Patterson map](../../../../../patterson-map.md); absolute coordinates are therefore not uniquely fixed by these three peaks. Subsequent phases are stated in the origin convention above. The remaining atoms need not have face-centered translational symmetry merely because the dominant Se substructure does.
