↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The crystalline expression diverges at **$\phi=\phi_{\rm cp}\simeq0.907$**, where the cage free area vanishes. This is physically different from the spurious $\phi=0.5$ pole produced by extrapolating the low-density fluid approximation. A real compression path cannot be inferred by following that fluid pole and then abruptly switching curves. At low density it follows a fluid equation of state, while at sufficiently high density an ordered arrangement can leave more accessible rattling configurations than a disordered crowded arrangement. This is [entropy-driven hard-sphere crystallization](../../../../../entropy-driven-hard-sphere-crystallization.md), here in two dimensions: allowed hard-core configurations have the same interaction [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), so [configurational entropy](../../../../../configurational-entropy.md) drives the phase competition. An equilibrium first-order coexistence interval would have common [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) and [chemical potential](../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md), giving a constant-pressure segment as [mean](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) density changes. The two asymptotic expressions alone neither locate that interval nor determine the detailed two-dimensional melting sequence. They require a consistent free-energy comparison and trustworthy approximations in the transition region.
