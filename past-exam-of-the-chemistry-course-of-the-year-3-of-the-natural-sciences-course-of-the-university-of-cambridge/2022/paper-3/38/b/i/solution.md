A [Fischer carbene complex](../../../../../../fischer-carbene-complex.md) typically has a low-oxidation-state, relatively electron-rich middle/late transition metal with strong pi-acceptor ligands such as CO. The [carbene](../../../../../../carbene.md) is treated as a neutral two-electron donor; metal-to-carbene back-donation contributes to its bond, but its [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) remains electrophilic. An OR or NR$_2$ substituent stabilizes that [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) by heteroatom donation. Here the metal is Cr(0), $d^6$, in an 18-electron pentacarbonyl complex.

A [Schrock carbene complex](../../../../../../schrock-carbene-complex.md) usually contains an early metal in a high [oxidation state](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md), often $d^0$, with electron-donating ligands rather than a shell of pi-accepting [carbonyls](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md). Its alkylidene has substantial dianionic [character](../../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) in ionic counting, and its [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) is correspondingly nucleophilic. Alkyl substituents are common; heteroatom stabilization of an electrophilic [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) is not the defining feature. These are bonding/electronic limiting descriptions rather than a rule based only on the drawn metal-carbon double bond.
