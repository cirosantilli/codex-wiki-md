↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $L$ for the imidazolylidene [N-heterocyclic carbene](../../../../../../n-heterocyclic-carbene.md) bearing the two mesityl groups shown. The proposed active unit is **$\mathrm{LNi(H)(SiPh_3)}$**, with H and SiPh$_3$ both bonded to nickel, rather than a nickel-free hydrosilane or a bare NHC-Ni fragment.

Displacement of coordinated styrene and formal [oxidative addition](../../../../../../oxidative-addition.md) of Si-H to Ni(0) give a low-coordinate nickel(II) hydride-silyl unit; substrate binding fills a vacant site. The productive cycle is [alkene](../../../../../../alkene.md) coordination, [migratory insertion](../../../../../../migratory-insertion.md) into Ni-H, then [beta-hydride elimination](../../../../../../beta-hydride-elimination.md) from the other beta [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md), releasing the shifted [alkene](../../../../../../alkene.md) and regenerating Ni-H.

The strongly donating NHC stabilizes a reactive low-coordinate metal and assists Si-H activation. Its steric bulk discourages unproductive saturated bis-alkene species and promotes product departure, but excessive crowding can hinder substrate binding/insertion. The [silyl ligand](../../../../../../silyl-ligand.md) also tunes the metal electronically: changing its aryl substituents changes donation and the barriers for hydride transfer/elimination. Product release and reversible insertion determine whether further double-bond migration occurs. Bulky ligands can suppress competing alkyl-silyl [reductive elimination](../../../../../../reductive-elimination.md), which would instead consume substrate by hydrosilylation.

The hydride-silyl formulation and insertion/elimination interpretation are the experimentally proposed mechanism; the individual catalyst resting states need not all be uniquely fixed by the activation recipe.[Modular nickel-silane catalytic system for the isomerization of alkenes by Kiana E. Kawamura and colleagues](../../../../../../modular-nickel-silane-catalytic-system-for-the-isomerization-of-alkenes-by-kiana-e-kawamura-and-colleagues.md)
