↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For one specified channel, the [photolysis rate coefficient](../../../../../photolysis-rate-coefficient.md) is

$$
J(z)=\int F_\lambda(z)\,\sigma_\lambda(T)\,\Phi_\lambda(T)\,d\lambda.
$$

It depends on spectral [actinic flux](../../../../../actinic-flux.md), absorption [cross section](../../../../../cross-section-physics.md), and channel [quantum yield](../../../../../quantum-yield.md). Solar zenith angle, season, overhead absorbers, clouds and [aerosol](../../../../../aerosol.md) scattering affect the photon field; branching and collisional quenching affect the chemical yield. Absorption alone does not guarantee the desired dissociation.

**Curve A is [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) [photodissociation](../../../../../photodissociation.md); curve B is [ozone](../../../../../ozone.md) [photodissociation](../../../../../photodissociation.md).** Molecular [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) needs relatively short-wavelength ultraviolet photons, which are strongly screened at lower altitudes, so its rate increases steeply with height. [Ozone](../../../../../ozone.md) can dissociate using longer wavelengths that penetrate much further, producing the much larger, less height-sensitive rate shown by B. The plotted quantity is a first-order coefficient with units $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, rather than a bimolecular [rate constant](../../../../../rate-constant.md).
