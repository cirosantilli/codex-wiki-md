↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [spin isomers of hydrogen](../../../../../../spin-isomers-of-hydrogen.md) illustrate how [nuclear spin statistics](../../../../../../nuclear-spin-statistics.md) constrain the product of electronic, vibrational, rotational and nuclear-spin exchange symmetries. In the ground electronic and vibrational states of these homonuclear [molecules](../../../../../../molecule-split.md), the first two factors are symmetric. Exchanging the nuclei reverses the internuclear direction, so a rotational state has parity $(-1)^J$.

For $^1\mathrm H_2$, the protons are spin-one-half [fermions](../../../../../../fermion.md) and the total nuclear-exchange wavefunction must be antisymmetric. The antisymmetric [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) singlet pairs with **[even](../../../../../../even-function.md) $J$ (para-hydrogen, one [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) state)**; the symmetric [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) triplet pairs with **[odd](../../../../../../odd-function.md) $J$ (ortho-hydrogen, three [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) states)**. For $^2\mathrm H_2$, spin-one deuterons are [bosons](../../../../../../boson.md), so the total must be symmetric. Nuclear spins $I=0,2$ supply six symmetric states paired with **[even](../../../../../../even-function.md) $J$ (ortho-deuterium)**; $I=1$ supplies three antisymmetric states paired with **[odd](../../../../../../odd-function.md) $J$ (para-deuterium)**. The high-temperature ortho/para statistical ratios are $3:1$ for [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) and $2:1$ for deuterium, while low-temperature equilibrium populations also depend on rotational energies and whether [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) conversion can occur.
