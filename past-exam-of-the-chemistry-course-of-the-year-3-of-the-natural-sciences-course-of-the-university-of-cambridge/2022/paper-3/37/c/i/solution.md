<h1 id="37/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

These are [inner-sphere electron transfer](../../../../../../inner-sphere-electron-transfer.md) pathways distinguished by where Cr(II) attacks the coordinated thiocyanate. Cr(II) is substitution-labile and can replace an aqua ligand to form a bridged Co(III)/Cr(II) precursor.

- **Remote attack at nitrogen:** form $\mathrm{Co^{III}{-}S{-}C{-}N{-}Cr^{II}}$. [Electron transfer](../../../../../../electron-transfer.md) through the bridge gives Co(II)/Cr(III). Cleavage on the now labile Co(II) side leaves the N-bound product $\mathrm{[Cr(NCS)(OH_2)_5]^{2+}}$. This is the faster $2.0\times10^5\,\mathrm{M^{-1}s^{-1}}$ channel.
- **Adjacent attack at sulfur:** Cr binds the same sulfur that is attached to cobalt, giving a shared-S bridge, $\mathrm{Co^{III}{-}S(CN){-}Cr^{II}}$. [Electron transfer](../../../../../../electron-transfer.md) and Co-S cleavage leave $\mathrm{[Cr(SCN)(OH_2)_5]^{2+}}$, the $0.8\times10^5$ channel.

In both cases the product Co(II) rapidly loses its ammines to give the hexaaqua [ion](../../../../../../ion.md), whereas substitution-inert Cr(III) retains the bridge-end information. The two chromium linkage isomers are therefore a record of two precursor geometries, rather than evidence that one outer-sphere electron-transfer event somehow selects two arbitrary new ligands afterwards.
