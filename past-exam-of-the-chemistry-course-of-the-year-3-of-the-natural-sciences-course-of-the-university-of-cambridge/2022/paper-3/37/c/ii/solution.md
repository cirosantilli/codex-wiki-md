<h1 id="37/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The free end of N-bound $\mathrm{Co{-}NCS}$ is sulfur. Cr(II) binds there, giving the remote bridge $\mathrm{Co^{III}{-}N{-}C{-}S{-}Cr^{II}}$. [Electron transfer](../../../../../../electron-transfer.md) and cleavage of the Co-N bond leave S-bound Cr(III), $\mathrm{[Cr(SCN)(OH_2)_5]^{2+}}$; labile Co(II) becomes hexaaquacobalt(II).

The much smaller [rate constant](../../../../../../rate-constant.md) shows that the efficiency of bridge formation/[electron](../../../../../../electron.md) transmission is strongly sensitive to linkage orientation. Efficient donation and coupling in a Co-S-bound precursor need not be reproduced when cobalt is N-bound. Its reduction is still consistent with [inner-sphere electron transfer](../../../../../../inner-sphere-electron-transfer.md); a slow rate alone is not a criterion for outer-sphere transfer.
