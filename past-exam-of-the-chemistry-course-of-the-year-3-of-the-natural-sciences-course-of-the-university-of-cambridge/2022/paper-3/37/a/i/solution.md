<h1 id="37/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Ni(II) is $d^8$. A [square-planar complex](../../../../../../square-planar-complex.md) can keep its strongly antibonding in-plane orbital empty and has a paired, $S=0$ configuration. A tetrahedral $d^8$ arrangement has two unpaired [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md), $S=1$.

The important change here is the substituent **on the imine nitrogen**: Me, Et, then tert-butyl. The phenolic ortho-tert-butyl substituent is common to all three ligands. The small N-Me groups permit coplanar N$_2$O$_2$ coordination in $\mathrm{Ni(L_a)_2}$. N-tert-butyl crowds that arrangement and favors twisting the two chelate planes toward a distorted tetrahedron in $\mathrm{Ni(L_c)_2}$. N-Et gives a closer energetic competition, so $\mathrm{Ni(L_b)_2}$ accesses both structures. The observed equilibrium reflects ligand-field stabilization, pairing, steric strain and temperature-dependent [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md); it is not a change in nickel [oxidation state](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md).
