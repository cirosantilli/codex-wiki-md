↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The [Woodward-Hoffmann rules](../../../../../../woodward-hoffmann-rules.md) for a $4n+2$ [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) [electrocyclic reaction](../../../../../../electrocyclic-reaction.md) permit **thermal disrotation and photochemical conrotation**. Here six [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) [mean](../../../../../../expected-value-split.md) $n=1$. For the ground-state six-electron chain, opposite terminal rotations match the terminal phases of its highest occupied orbital as the closing sigma bond forms. A one-electron excitation changes the relevant occupancy and reverses the allowed rotational mode. Thus A opens conrotatorily under light to the specified B, while B closes disrotatorily on heating. Reversing the rotational mode changes the adjacent methyl relationship from cis to trans, exactly as in C.
