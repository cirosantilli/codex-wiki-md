<h1 id="50/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The difficulty is competing chemistry and isolation selectivity. Alkaline treatment can promote [dehydrohalogenation](../../../../../dehydrohalogenation.md) at chlorinated backbone units while hydrolysing the acetate side groups. Loss of HCl produces unsaturated segments, potentially followed by further degradation or cross-linking; hydroxyl formation also changes solvent compatibility and intermolecular association. The isolated material can therefore differ from the ideal undegraded C in part (e). [Competing dehydrochlorination during copolymer saponification](../../../../../competing-dehydrochlorination-during-copolymer-saponification.md) explains why selective conditions are needed.

This is not an absolute prohibition on hydroxyl-containing [chloride](../../../../../chloride.md) copolymers. Analytical work on these copolymers used staged alcoholysis/[saponification](../../../../../saponification.md) and [polymer](../../../../../polymer-split.md) precipitation to restrain dehydrochlorination. The sensible conclusion is that clean, exhaustive conversion and isolation are difficult under unselective conditions, rather than that no suitable treatment can ever preserve [chloride](../../../../../chloride.md) units.[Abstract, pp. 619–623: staged treatment and precipitation used to restrain competing dehydrochlorination](../../../../../determination-of-vinyl-acetate-in-vinyl-chloride-acetate-copolymers-by-takanori-shimazaki-and-others.md)
