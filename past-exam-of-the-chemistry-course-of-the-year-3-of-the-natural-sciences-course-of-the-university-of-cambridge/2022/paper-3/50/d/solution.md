<h1 id="50/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Taking the stated melting point as the reference for a crystallizable A, introducing acetate-bearing units disrupts regular sequences and crystal packing. The expected result is **a lower melting point for B**, often with broader or less complete crystallization. The ideal [copolymer melting-point depression](../../../../../copolymer-melting-point-depression.md) estimate is

$$
\frac1{T_m}-\frac1{T_m^0}\simeq-\frac{R}{\Delta h_f}\ln x_{\rm crystallizable}>0
$$

when unlike units are excluded from the crystal. The larger vinyl ester in the drawing is [vinyl pentanoate](../../../../../vinyl-pentanoate.md): its acyl substituent has a four-carbon alkyl chain. At comparable incorporation it should generally disrupt regular packing more and favour a still lower melting range or suppressed crystallinity, although the exact trend depends on composition and side-group interactions. The formula concerns melting of crystallites, not the glass transition; a universal numerical melting point cannot be inferred for an unspecified real PVC copolymer.
