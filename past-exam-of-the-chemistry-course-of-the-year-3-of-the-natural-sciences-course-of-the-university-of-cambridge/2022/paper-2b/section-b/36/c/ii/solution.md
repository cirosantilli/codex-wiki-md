<h1 id="section-b/36/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For two [electrons](../../../../../../../electron.md), $n+1+D<4$ whenever $0<n<1$: both sides of $D<3-n$ are positive and squaring reduces the inequality to $n<1$. Since $\beta<0$, the trapezium then has a higher total electronic [energy](../../../../../../../energy-split.md) than the square. **The two-electron model favours the square.** For four [electrons](../../../../../../../electron.md), $D>2$, so $2\beta D<4\beta$: **the four-electron model favours the distortion toward a trapezium.** The lower split middle orbital is occupied while the upper one is empty.

The distinction is especially clear near the square. Write $\delta=1-n>0$; then $D=2+\delta^2/4+O(\delta^4)$ and

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_2^{\rm trap}-E_2^{\rm sq}&=\beta[-\delta+\delta^2/4+O(\delta^4)]>0,\\
E_4^{\rm trap}-E_4^{\rm sq}&=\tfrac12\beta\delta^2+O(\delta^4)<0.
\end{aligned}
$$

In particular the four-electron lowering in this specified one-edge model is quadratic near the square; a first-order lowering of the entire occupied sum should not be asserted merely from the middle-level splitting. This [Hückel square-to-trapezium distortion](../../../../../../../huckel-square-to-trapezium-distortion.md) is an electronic comparison under the stated fixed-$\alpha$, fixed-short-edge-$\beta$ assumptions. Nuclear repulsion, other bonding contributions and the structural cost of distortion are omitted, so an actual equilibrium geometry or optimum $n$ is not determined by this calculation alone.
