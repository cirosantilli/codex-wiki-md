<h1 id="section-b/36/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Treat the site [atomic orbitals](../../../../../../../atomic-orbital.md) $s_1,\ldots,s_4$ as orthonormal, as in the [Hückel method](../../../../../../../huckel-method.md). A normalized set of [symmetry-adapted linear combinations](../../../../../../../symmetry-adapted-linear-combination.md) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{A_{1g}}&=\tfrac12(s_1+s_2+s_3+s_4),\\
\Phi_{B_{1g}}&=\tfrac12(s_1-s_2+s_3-s_4),\\
\Phi_{E_u,x}&=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(s_3-s_1),\\
\Phi_{E_u,y}&=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(s_2-s_4).
\end{aligned}}
$$

The first combination is unchanged by every operation; the alternating combination has the transformation signs of $x^2-y^2$ for the printed axes. The last two transform together like $(x,y)$. The squared coefficients sum to one in each case, and their pairwise inner products vanish. Any orthogonal rotation of the two $E_u$ combinations is an equally valid [basis](../../../../../../../basis.md) for that degenerate subspace.
