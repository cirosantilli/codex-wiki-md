<h1 id="section-b/36/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The drawn $x,y$ axes pass through opposite sites, rather than along the square edges. In the [D4h molecular point group](../../../../../../../d4h-molecular-point-group.md) class order $(E,2C_4,C_2,2C_2',2C_2'',i,2S_4,\sigma_h,2\sigma_v,2\sigma_d)$, an [s orbital](../../../../../../../s-orbital.md) contributes $+1$ whenever its centre is fixed and zero otherwise. Thus

$$
\chi_\Gamma=(4,0,0,2,0,0,0,4,2,0).
$$

For example, a transverse twofold rotation about the printed $x$ axis fixes sites 1 and 3, while its diagonal counterpart exchanges all four sites. Reflection in the square plane fixes every site. Taking [character inner products](../../../../../../../character-inner-product.md), with class sizes $(1,2,1,2,2,1,2,1,2,2)$, gives

$$
\boxed{\Gamma=A_{1g}\oplus B_{1g}\oplus E_u.}
$$

These have dimensions $1+1+2=4$. The $B_{1g}$ label is tied to the printed diagonal-axis convention; rotating the transverse coordinate labels by $45^\circ$ would interchange $B_{1g}$ and $B_{2g}$.
