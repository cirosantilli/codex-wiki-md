<h1 id="section-b/36/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The long edge is $3$–$4$. In the orthonormal pair [basis](../../../../../../../basis.md) $(a,b)$, its weaker resonance integral $n\beta$ changes the second diagonal element. The two short side edges contribute an inter-pair [matrix element](../../../../../../../matrix-element.md) $\beta$, giving

$$
H_{A_1}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha+\beta&\beta\\\beta&\alpha+n\beta\end{pmatrix}.
$$

With $\lambda=(E-\alpha)/\beta$, its secular equation is

$$
(1-\lambda)(n-\lambda)-1=0,
\quad\lambda^2-(n+1)\lambda+(n-1)=0.
$$

The [eigenvalues](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are therefore

$$
\boxed{E_{A_1}=\alpha+\frac\beta2\left[n+1\pm\sqrt{n^2-2n+5}\right].}
$$

At $n=1$ they become $\alpha+2\beta$ and $\alpha$, agreeing with the square limit. [Symmetry](../../../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) permits mixing of the two $A_1$ pair functions; treating each pair as an isolated bond would miss the off-diagonal term.
