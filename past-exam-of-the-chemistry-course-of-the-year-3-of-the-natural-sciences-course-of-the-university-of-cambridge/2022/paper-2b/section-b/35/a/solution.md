<h1 id="section-b/35/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The operator $\hat J_z$ describes the $z$ component of [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md), whereas $\hat J^2$ describes its total squared magnitude. For a [spherical harmonic](../../../../../../spherical-harmonic.md) $Y_{JM}$ the corresponding [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are

$$
\boxed{\hat J_zY_{JM}=M\hbar Y_{JM},\qquad
\hat J^2Y_{JM}=J(J+1)\hbar^2Y_{JM}.}
$$

Thus the magnitude is $\hbar\sqrt{J(J+1)}$, not $J\hbar$. For ordinary orbital rotation $J=0,1,2,\ldots$ and $M=-J,-J+1,\ldots,J$. The simultaneous values are possible because these two operators commute.
