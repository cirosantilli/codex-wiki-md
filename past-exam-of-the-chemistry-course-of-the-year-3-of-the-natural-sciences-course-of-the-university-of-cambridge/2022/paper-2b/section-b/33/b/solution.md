<h1 id="section-b/33/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Expand the trial [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) in a complete [orthonormal set](../../../../../../orthonormal-set.md) of exact [eigenfunctions](../../../../../../eigenfunction.md), $\psi=\sum_n c_n\phi_n$, with $\hat H\phi_n=E_n\phi_n$ and $E_n\ge E_0$. The coefficients may depend on any chosen variational parameters. [Orthogonality](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) removes the cross terms in both integrals, giving

$$
\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=\sum_n|c_n|^2,\qquad
\langle\psi|\hat H|\psi\rangle=\sum_n E_n|c_n|^2.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathcal E[\psi]-E_0=\frac{\sum_n(E_n-E_0)|c_n|^2}{\sum_n|c_n|^2}\ge0.}
$$

This proves the [quantum variational principle](../../../../../../quantum-variational-principle.md). The denominator is positive because the trial function is nonzero. Equality requires every occupied exact state to have $E_n=E_0$, allowing for [ground-state degeneracy](../../../../../../ground-state-degeneracy.md). With a continuous part of the spectrum, the corresponding nonnegative spectral integral gives the same conclusion.
