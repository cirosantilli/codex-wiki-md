<h1 id="section-b/33/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $g=e^{-q^2/2}$ and $f=2q^2-a$. Product differentiation for the reduced [quantum harmonic oscillator](../../../../../../../quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md) gives

$$
\hat H(fg)=\left[-\tfrac12 f''+qf'+\tfrac12 f\right]g
=\left(5q^2-2-\frac a2\right)g.
$$

For an [eigenfunction](../../../../../../../eigenfunction.md), this must equal $E(2q^2-a)g$ at every $q$. Matching the $q^2$ coefficients gives $E=5/2$, and matching constants then gives $5a/2=2+a/2$. Hence **$a=1$ and $E=5/2$**. This is the even $n=2$ oscillator state. Its normalized [wavefunction](../../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) is $\phi_2=(2q^2-1)e^{-q^2/2}/(\sqrt2\,\pi^{1/4})$.
