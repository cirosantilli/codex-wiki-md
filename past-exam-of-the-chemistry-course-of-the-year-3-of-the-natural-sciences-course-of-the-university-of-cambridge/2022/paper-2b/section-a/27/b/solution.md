<h1 id="section-a/27/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For [chromium](../../../../../../chromium.md) the configuration is $[\mathrm{Ar}]3d^54s^1$. The half-filled $3d$ subshell has $S_d=5/2$ and $L_d=0$; aligning the $4s$ spin gives $S=3$, while $L=0$. Therefore the ground-state [term symbol](../../../../../../term-symbol.md) is **$\mathrm{Cr}:{}^7S_3$**. For [cobalt](../../../../../../cobalt.md), $[\mathrm{Ar}]3d^74s^2$ has a closed $4s^2$ pair. The $d^7$ subshell has the same possible $L,S$ values as three $d$ holes; maximizing spin and then orbital [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) gives $S=3/2$, $L=3$. Because it is more than half full, $J=3+3/2=9/2$, giving **$\mathrm{Co}:{}^4F_{9/2}$**. For [copper](../../../../../../copper.md), $[\mathrm{Ar}]3d^{10}4s^1$ has a closed $d$ subshell, leaving $L=0$, $S=1/2$ and **$\mathrm{Cu}:{}^2S_{1/2}$**. These are neutral-atom terms, rather than the terms of the common metal ions. The tabulated zero-energy levels independently confirm all three assignments.[Cr I table, ground row 3d5(6S)4s 7S3](../../../../../../energy-levels-of-neutral-chromium-in-the-nist-handbook.md)[Co I table, ground row 3d7 4s2 4F9/2](../../../../../../handbook-of-basic-atomic-spectroscopic-data-by-jean-e-sansonetti-and-william-c-martin/energy-levels-of-neutral-cobalt-co-i-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology.md)[Cu I table, ground row 3d10(1S)4s 2S1/2](../../../../../../handbook-of-basic-atomic-spectroscopic-data-by-jean-e-sansonetti-and-william-c-martin/energy-levels-of-neutral-copper-cu-i-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology.md)
