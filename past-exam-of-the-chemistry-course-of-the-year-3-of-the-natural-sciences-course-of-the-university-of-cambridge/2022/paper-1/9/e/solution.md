<h1 id="9/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The wording places both dicarbollide cages on the same [iron](../../../../../iron.md), leaving the second [iron](../../../../../iron.md) outside the cages. The corresponding proposal is **$[L_2\mathrm{Fe\rightarrow Fe(CO)_4}]^{2-}$**, with the first [iron](../../../../../iron.md) sandwiched between the two open $\eta^5$ faces and the second carrying four carbonyls. It is not the symmetric arrangement with one cage on each [iron](../../../../../iron.md), which would contradict the specified single cage-interacting metal site.

The donor fragment $[\mathrm{Fe^{II}}L_2]^{2-}$ has $d^6+2(6)=18$ electrons; the external neutral $\mathrm{Fe(CO)_4}$ fragment has $8+4(2)=16$. Metal-to-metal donation therefore provides a natural 18-electron acceptor adduct. In covalent-single-bond oxidation-state bookkeeping, the analogous assignments Fe(III)/Fe(−I) give $d^5+12+1=18$ and $d^9+8+1=18$. In either account the whole species has two negative charges. The outside [iron](../../../../../iron.md) can have an approximately trigonal-bipyramidal five-coordinate arrangement when the Fe–Fe direction and its four CO ligands are counted.

The important structural distinction is **a bis(dicarbollide) [iron](../../../../../iron.md) donor joined to an exo tetracarbonyl [iron](../../../../../iron.md) acceptor**. Formula and [electron counting](../../../../../electron-counting.md) alone do not uniquely fix all [carbonyl](../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) angles or prove a particular crystalline conformation.
