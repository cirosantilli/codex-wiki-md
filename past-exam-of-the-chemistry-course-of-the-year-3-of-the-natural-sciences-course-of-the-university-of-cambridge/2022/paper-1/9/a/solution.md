<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [isolobal analogy](../../../../../isolobal-analogy.md) compares fragment frontier orbitals, not atomic sizes or total [electron](../../../../../electron.md) counts. The neutral BH fragment has four [valence electrons](../../../../../valence-electron.md); two make the external B–H bond, leaving **two skeletal electrons in three frontier orbitals** directed towards possible cluster neighbours. For the neutral $\mathrm{CpCo}$ fragment, $\mathrm{Cp=C_5H_5}$ contributes five electrons and [cobalt](../../../../../cobalt.md) nine, giving 14. Removing the 12 electrons assigned to filled, predominantly non-skeletal fragment orbitals again leaves **two electrons in three cluster-directed frontier orbitals**.

The corresponding frontier sets have comparable numbers, spatial symmetries and occupations, allowing replacement of a BH vertex by CpCo in a suitable cluster-electron count. This is why the metal fragment can cap an open [carborane](../../../../../carborane.md) face without adding an arbitrary new set of skeletal electrons. Exact orbital energies, ligand preferences and bond lengths need not be identical.[1982, isolobal fragment correspondence and its use across organic/inorganic bonding](../../../../../building-bridges-between-inorganic-and-organic-chemistry-nobel-lecture-by-roald-hoffmann.md)
