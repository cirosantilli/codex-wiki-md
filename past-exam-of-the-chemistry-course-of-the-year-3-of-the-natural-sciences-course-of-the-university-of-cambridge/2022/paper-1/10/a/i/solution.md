Ultrashort metal–metal contacts are favoured when the valence configuration fills strongly metal–metal-bonding orbitals while leaving their antibonding partners empty. Early or middle [transition metals](../../../../../../transition-metal.md) in low [oxidation states](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md) can retain enough d electrons for multiple bonding without the unavoidable antibonding occupation of a more filled d shell. Good radial and angular overlap, a compatible metal–metal axis, and low competing ligand-field stabilization all matter; a short contact should be interpreted together with an orbital/electron-counting model, not automatically called a high-order bond.

Ligands should support a geometry in which the relevant d orbitals can overlap and protect the low-valent pair from oligomerization or oxidative attack. Bulky aryl-substituted bridging amidinates can provide this protection while maintaining an appropriate small coordination number. Strongly coordinating axial ligands or inappropriate pi interactions can compete with metal–metal bonding, especially its weaker delta components. For quadruple or quintuple bonds, retaining two appropriately oriented delta-capable orbitals is particularly important; a geometry optimized only for metal–ligand bonding can remove that advantage.

Ligand bridges need not literally squeeze two otherwise unbound metals together. Comparing bond lengths across [oxidation states](../../../../../../oxidation-state.md) and across related ligands helps distinguish electronic multiple bonding from enforced steric compression. The lantern-type dichromium experiment in (ii) is informative because reduction changes the [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) occupation within essentially the same three-bridge supporting framework.[2008, printed pp. 7250–7253: mixed-valent precursor, reduced anion, orbital analysis and discussion of ligand constraints](../../../../../../remarkably-short-metal-metal-bonds-a-lantern-type-quintuply-bonded-dichromium-i-complex-by-yi-chou-tsai-and-others.md)
