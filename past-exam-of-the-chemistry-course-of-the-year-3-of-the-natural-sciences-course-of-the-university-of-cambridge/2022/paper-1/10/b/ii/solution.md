<h1 id="10/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The **large positive sum of ionization energies and large negative [hydration enthalpy](../../../../../../hydration-enthalpy.md) dominate**, with a smaller but significant positive atomization term. Ionization depends on effective nuclear attraction, orbital energies and the stability of successive gas-ion [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) configurations; removing the third [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) may require disruption of a particularly stable divalent f configuration. Atomization depends on the metal's cohesive bonding and valence participation, and need not vary perfectly smoothly.

Hydration becomes more favourable for higher charge density. All the compared ions have charge $+3$, so smaller effective ionic radius generally gives more negative [hydration enthalpy](../../../../../../hydration-enthalpy.md). Incomplete shielding by the 4f shell produces the [lanthanide contraction](../../../../../../lanthanide-contraction.md) and strengthens ion–water attraction along the series. A Born-type electrostatic estimate has a stabilization scale proportional to $-z^2/r$, but hydration-shell structure, water polarization and [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) dependence prevent treating that crude scale as an exact [enthalpy](../../../../../../enthalpy-split.md) formula. The hydration trend is smoother than the sharp configuration-dependent ionization anomalies. The measured formation [enthalpy](../../../../../../enthalpy-split.md) reflects cancellation of large terms, so comparison of just one term can give a misleading prediction.
