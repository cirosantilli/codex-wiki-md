# Electrical synapse

↑ **Parent:** [Synapse](synapse.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_synapse)

An [electrical synapse](electrical-synapse.md) passes ionic [electric current](electric-current.md) directly between [neurons](neuron.md) through [gap junctions](gap-junction.md), whose paired channels connect the two [cytoplasms](cytoplasm.md). It does not require [synaptic vesicle](synaptic-vesicle.md) release and postsynaptic [neurotransmitter](neurotransmitter.md) binding. Transmission across the junction is very fast; the receiving [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)'s [capacitance](capacitance.md) and other conductances still determine its voltage response. Electrical coupling can coordinate the timing of activity across [neurons](neuron.md). See [Purves and colleagues: electrical synapses and gap junctions](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK11164/).

For a passive junction with constant conductance $g_j$, the inward [electric current](electric-current.md) into cell 2 is

$$
I_{2\leftarrow1}=g_j(V_1-V_2),\qquad I_{1\leftarrow2}=-I_{2\leftarrow1},
$$

where each $V_k$ is the cell's [membrane potential](membrane-potential.md), measured against a common extracellular reference. Taking $g_j=2\,\mathrm{nS}$, $V_1=-55\,\mathrm{mV}$ and $V_2=-65\,\mathrm{mV}$ gives $20\,\mathrm{pA}$ into cell 2 and the equal opposite [electric current](electric-current.md) into cell 1. Exchanging the voltages reverses the [electric current](electric-current.md). Both [depolarization](depolarization.md) and [hyperpolarization](hyperpolarization.md) can therefore be transmitted; an [electrical synapse](electrical-synapse.md) is not inherently an excitatory connection.

Many [electrical synapses](electrical-synapse.md) conduct in both directions, but this does not guarantee equal voltage responses: the cells can have different effective [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) and [capacitance](capacitance.md). Some junctions rectify, meaning they conduct preferentially in one direction, so the constant-$g_j$ formula is insufficient. A concrete molecular example is the original study [Shui and colleagues, 2020: Molecular basis of junctional current rectification at an electrical synapse](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7455501/). Bidirectionality is thus a common property with important qualifications, rather than part of the definition.
