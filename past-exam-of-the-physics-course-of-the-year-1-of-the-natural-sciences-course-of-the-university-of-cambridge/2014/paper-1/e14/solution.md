<h1 id="e14/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E14](../e14.md)

An inverse-square field spreads through area proportional to squared distance. More generally the [flux](../../../../flux.md) contribution of a point source is proportional to the [solid angle](../../../../solid-angle.md) subtended by a surface element; the oriented [solid angles](../../../../solid-angle.md) sum to $4\pi$ for an enclosed source and zero for an exterior one. [Superposition](../../../../superposition-principle.md) extends this to a [mass distribution](../../../../mass-distribution.md).

[Newton's law of universal gravitation](../../../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation-split.md) gives $\mathbf g=-GM\hat{\mathbf r}/r^2$ for a [point mass](../../../../point-mass.md). A surrounding sphere therefore has [flux](../../../../flux.md) $-4\pi GM$, so

$$
\boxed{C=4\pi G,\qquad \oint\mathbf g\cdot d\mathbf S=-4\pi GM_{\rm enc}.}
$$

For the tunnel, approximate its water as an infinite uniform cylinder with $R=3.5\,\mathrm m$ and [density](../../../../density.md) $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. The bob is at radial distance $r=R+1.5\,\mathrm m=5.0\,\mathrm m$. A coaxial [Gaussian surface](../../../../gaussian-surface.md) gives

$$
2\pi rL_cg_r=-4\pi G(\pi R^2\rho L_c),\qquad
|g_r|=\frac{2\pi G\rho R^2}{r}.
$$

For the [plumb-line deflection from cylindrical gravity](../../../../plumb-line-deflection-from-cylindrical-gravity.md), horizontal equilibrium gives $d/L\simeq|g_r|/g$. Draining removes the attraction toward the tunnel, so the bob shifts away by

$$
\boxed{d\simeq\frac{2\pi G\rho R^2L}{rg}=5.23\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm m=0.523\,\mathrm{\mu m}.}
$$

The unchanged rock and ordinary [gravitational field](../../../../gravitational-field.md) cancel in the before/after comparison; end effects and mechanical disturbances are neglected.

For the [tidal water loading of a spring balance](../../../../tidal-water-loading-of-a-spring-balance.md), approximate the extra water at high tide as an infinite plane slab of thickness $h=8\,\mathrm m$, with the suspended [mass](../../../../mass.md) above it. The [gravitational field of an infinite plane sheet](../../../../gravitational-field-of-an-infinite-plane-sheet.md), derived with a pillbox [Gaussian surface](../../../../gaussian-surface.md), gives added downward field $\Delta g=2\pi G\rho h$. With initial [spring](../../../../spring.md) extension $x_0=mg/k=1\,\mathrm m$,

$$
\boxed{\Delta x=\frac m k\Delta g=x_0\frac{2\pi G\rho h}{g}
=3.42\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm m=0.342\,\mathrm{\mu m}.}
$$

**The extension increases at high tide.** These estimates use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ and the stated water-density approximation; salinity, finite estuary geometry and terrestrial tidal deformation alter a real measurement.
