<h1 id="b8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B8](../b8.md)

In standard configuration the frames are inertial, their corresponding axes are parallel, S$'$ moves at $v$ along the positive $x$ axis of S, and their origins coincide at $t=t'=0$. Here $(t,x,y,z)$ and $(t',x',y',z')$ label the same event, $c$ is the [speed of light](../../../../speed-of-light.md) and $\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$.

A clock fixed in S$'$ has $\Delta x'=0$. The inverse time transformation gives

$$
\boxed{\Delta t=\gamma\Delta t'=\gamma\Delta\tau,}
$$

so the moving clock accumulates less [proper time](../../../../proper-time.md) between its two ticks than the elapsed S-coordinate time.

Without [time dilation](../../../../time-dilation.md) the flight time from the given altitude would be $66.7\,\mathrm{\mu s}$, about $30.3$ rest-frame [mean lifetimes](../../../../mean-lifetime-physics.md), giving survival fraction only $e^{-30.3}\simeq6.8\times10^{-14}$ even at [speed](../../../../speed.md) $c$. Relativistic [muons](../../../../muon.md) can instead reach the ground with appreciable survival. Momentum-selected decay measurements test this clock-rate dependence experimentally.[Physical Review 59, 223 (1941), primary abstract: different momentum groups have different observed decay rates consistent with relativistic moving clocks](../../../../variation-of-the-rate-of-decay-of-mesotrons-with-momentum-by-bruno-rossi-and-david-b-hall.md)

A [mean-lifetime survival energy estimate](../../../../mean-lifetime-survival-energy-estimate.md) requires a mean flight time no greater than the dilated [mean lifetime](../../../../mean-lifetime-physics.md):

$$
\gamma\tau_0\gtrsim\frac Hc,\qquad
\boxed{\gamma\gtrsim30.3,\qquad E=\gamma m_\mu c^2\gtrsim3.21\,\mathrm{GeV}.}
$$

This corresponds to [survival probability](../../../../survival-probability.md) at least about $e^{-1}$. **There is no sharp minimum [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) for an individual random decay.** For a required probability $p$, use $\exp[-H/(\gamma v\tau_0)]\ge p$. [Energy](../../../../energy-split.md) loss in the atmosphere is neglected in this estimate. In the [muon](../../../../muon.md)'s frame the atmospheric path is length-contracted to $H/\gamma\simeq660\,\mathrm m$; the ground reaches it in about $\tau_0$, giving the same [survival probability](../../../../survival-probability.md).

For the two-body decay, use the [rest frame](../../../../rest-frame.md) of A. [Momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) conservation makes B and the [photon](../../../../photon.md) oppositely directed with equal [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) magnitude $p$. [Energy](../../../../energy-split.md) conservation and the [energy–momentum relation](../../../../energy-momentum-relation.md) give

$$
m_Ac^2=E_B+pc,\qquad E_B^2=p^2c^2+m_B^2c^4.
$$

Substitution and cancellation of $E_B^2$ give

$$
\boxed{E_B=\frac{(m_A^2+m_B^2)c^2}{2m_A},\qquad
E_\gamma=\frac{(m_A^2-m_B^2)c^2}{2m_A}.}
$$

A nonzero emitted [photon](../../../../photon.md) requires $m_A>m_B$.
