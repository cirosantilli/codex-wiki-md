<h1 id="e15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A field assigns a physical quantity to each point. [Gravitational field](../../../../../gravitational-field.md) is [force](../../../../../force-split.md) per unit test [mass](../../../../../mass.md); [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is [force](../../../../../force-split.md) per unit test charge. In a conservative, time-independent field,

$$
\boxed{\mathbf F=-\nabla U,\qquad \Delta U=-\int\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r.}
$$

Write $U=m\Phi$ for gravitational potential $\Phi$, or $U=q\phi$ for [electric potential](../../../../../electric-potential.md) $\phi$, giving $\mathbf g=-\nabla\Phi$ and $\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi$. A [point mass](../../../../../point-mass.md) has $U=-GMm/r$ with zero at infinity, while two point charges have $U=Qq/(4\pi\epsilon_0r)$. Only potential-energy differences matter; a constant can be added freely. The [mechanical energy](../../../../../mechanical-energy.md) $K+U$ is conserved if no nonconservative [forces](../../../../../force-split.md) do work. A magnetic [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md) $q\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ does no work on a point charge and generally cannot be represented by a position-only scalar [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md).
