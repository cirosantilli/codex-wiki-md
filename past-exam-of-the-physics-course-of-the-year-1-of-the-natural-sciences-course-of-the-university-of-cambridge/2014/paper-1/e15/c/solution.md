<h1 id="e15/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For steady currents in vacuum, the [Biot-Savart law](../../../../../biot-savart-law.md) sums the contributions of current elements:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B(\mathbf r)=\frac{\mu_0I}{4\pi}\int
\frac{d\boldsymbol\ell'\times(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|^3}.}
$$

It is useful for known wire geometries; a circular loop of radius $a$ gives $B=\mu_0I/(2a)$ at its centre.

[Ampère's circuital law](../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) in [magnetostatics](../../../../../magnetostatics.md) is

$$
\boxed{\oint\mathbf B\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=\mu_0I_{\rm enc}.}
$$

With sufficient symmetry it finds fields directly: a long straight wire gives $B=\mu_0I/(2\pi r)$, while a long [solenoid](../../../../../solenoid.md) has interior $B\simeq\mu_0nI$ and a small exterior field away from its ends. Without symmetry, a circulation value alone does not determine the local field. For time-dependent [electric fields](../../../../../electric-field-split.md), use the [Ampère-Maxwell equation](../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) with the displacement-current term $\mu_0\epsilon_0\,d\Phi_E/dt$ as well as conduction current.
