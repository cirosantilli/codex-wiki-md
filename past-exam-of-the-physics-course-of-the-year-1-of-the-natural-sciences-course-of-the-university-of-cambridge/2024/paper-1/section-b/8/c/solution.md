<h1 id="section-b/8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In the small-amplitude approximation, $\dot x$ is the speed along the track, and $y=Bx^2$ supplies the [gravitational potential](../../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md). The conserved energy is

$$
E=\frac M2\left(1+\frac{r_2^2}{2r_1^2}\right)\dot x^2+MgBx^2.
$$

Differentiating and extending through turning points by continuity gives

$$
\left(1+\frac{r_2^2}{2r_1^2}\right)\ddot x+2gBx=0.
$$

This is [simple harmonic motion](../../../../../../simple-harmonic-motion.md) with $\omega_0^2=4gB/\{(r_2/r_1)^2+2\}$. Hence

$$
\boxed{f_0=\frac{\sqrt{gB}}{\pi\sqrt{(r_2/r_1)^2+2}}=0.235\,\mathrm{Hz}.}
$$

The [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) is lower than for a sliding point [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) because energy is also stored in rotation.
