<h1 id="section-b/6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The inclined parcel has [tension](../../../../../../tension.md) $T$ up the slope, weight $mg$ vertically downward, [normal reaction](../../../../../../normal-force.md) $N=mg\cos\theta$ and [friction](../../../../../../friction.md) $\mu mg\cos\theta$ down the slope. The hanging [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) has weight downward and [tension](../../../../../../tension.md) upward. The ideal string makes the two [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) magnitudes equal.

The [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) slope is **$a=2.4/1.5=1.60\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$**, up the track for the parcel and downward for the hanging [mass](../../../../../../mass.md). The [free-body diagrams](../../../../../../free-body-diagram.md) show gravity itself as one vector; its slope-parallel and normal components are used in the equations rather than counted as additional [forces](../../../../../../force-split.md).
