<h1 id="section-b/6/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

For actual complete bounce distances $D_1=4\,\mathrm m$ and $D_{n+1}=rD_n$, the [geometric series](../../../../../../geometric-series.md) gives

$$
\boxed{D_{\rm total}=\frac{4}{1-r}\,\mathrm m,\qquad0\leq r<1.}
$$

A natural intended estimate compares the observed first round trip with the loss-free round trip from the initially compressed position,

$$
D_{\rm ideal}=2(H_s+x)=5.13764\,\mathrm m.
$$

If the reduction factor is taken to be $r=D_1/D_{\rm ideal}=0.77857$, the answer is **$D_{\rm total}\simeq18.1\,\mathrm m$**. The first term is the actual 4 m, not the hypothetical loss-free distance.

This numerical inference needs the stated comparison assumption. Strictly, knowing the first actual bounce distance and saying subsequent distances share a ratio does not independently specify that ratio. A change in [spring](../../../../../../spring.md) compression also means that an energy-loss fraction is not automatically the same as a vertical-distance ratio.
