<h1 id="section-b/7/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Set the launch event to $(t,x)=(0,0)$ and measure $x$ toward Earth in light-years and $t$ in years, so light has coordinate slope magnitude one. Earth's [worldline](../../../../../../world-line.md) is $x=2$, and the lander's is $x=(35/37)t$. The outgoing signal is $x=t$ and reaches Earth at $B=(2,2)$. Earth's reply is sent at $C=(25/12,2)$.

The return signal has $x=2-(t-25/12)$. Its intersection with the lander gives $D=(1813/864,1715/864)$. Both signal lines are null; the lander's [worldline](../../../../../../world-line.md) lies inside the [light cone](../../../../../../light-cone.md). The drawing exaggerates the small gap near Earth in an inset so that B, C and D remain distinguishable.
