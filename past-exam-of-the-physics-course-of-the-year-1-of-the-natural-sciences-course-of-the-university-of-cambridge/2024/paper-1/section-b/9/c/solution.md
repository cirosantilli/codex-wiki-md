<h1 id="section-b/9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Substitution into the [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) gives $E_n=V_0+n^2\pi^2\hbar^2/(2mL^2)$. For the stated [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) well,

$$
\boxed{E_2=37.6\,\mathrm{eV}=6.02\times10^{-18}\,\mathrm J.}
$$

The transition emits the energy difference, $\Delta E=E_2-E_1=3E_1=28.2\,\mathrm{eV}$, so

$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{hc}{\Delta E}=44.0\,\mathrm{nm}.}
$$

Using the full excited-state energy as the [photon](../../../../../../photon.md) energy would neglect the nonzero ground-state energy.
