<h1 id="section-b/9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

$V(x)$ is the particle's [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) as a function of position, and $E$ is its total energy [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) in this [time-independent Schrödinger equation](../../../../../../time-independent-schrodinger-equation.md). Solving it with the boundary conditions determines the allowed [stationary states](../../../../../../stationary-state.md) and energy levels.

The [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) is a [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) amplitude: the [Born rule](../../../../../../born-rule.md) gives position [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) [density](../../../../../../density.md) $|\psi(x)|^2$, normalized to unit [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md) for one particle. $\psi$ itself need not be real or positive and is not a classical matter-density profile. The full stationary-state time dependence is $\Psi(x,t)=\psi(x)e^{-iEt/\hbar}$, leaving $|\Psi|^2$ time independent.
