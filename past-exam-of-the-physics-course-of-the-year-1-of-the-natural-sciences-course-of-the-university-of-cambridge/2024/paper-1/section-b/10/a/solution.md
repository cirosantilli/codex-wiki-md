↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In [magnetostatics](../../../../../../magnetostatics.md), [Ampère's circuital law](../../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) is

$$
\oint_C\mathbf B\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=\mu_0I_{\rm enclosed}.
$$

It relates magnetic circulation around a closed curve to the steady current through a spanning surface. It is especially useful when [symmetry](../../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) makes the tangential field constant on a chosen path: an infinite straight wire gives $B=\mu_0I/(2\pi r)$, while an ideal long [solenoid](../../../../../../solenoid.md) gives approximately $B=\mu_0nI$ inside.

The [Biot-Savart law](../../../../../../biot-savart-law.md) gives the field contribution from the actual current geometry:

$$
\mathbf B(\mathbf r)=\frac{\mu_0I}{4\pi}\int\frac{d\boldsymbol\ell'\times(\mathbf r-\mathbf r')}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|^3}.
$$

The [cross product](../../../../../../cross-product.md) determines the direction by the [right-hand rule](../../../../../../right-hand-rule.md). Integrating around a circular loop of radius $a$ gives $B=\mu_0I/(2a)$ at its centre. A finite wire segment can also be integrated directly, even though it lacks enough [symmetry](../../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) for an Ampère-loop shortcut. The full steady-current circuit includes a return path; a segment calculation specifies that segment's contribution.
