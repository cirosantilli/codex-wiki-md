<h1 id="section-a/5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

At closest approach the [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) is tangential. The conserved specific [orbital energy](../../../../../orbital-energy.md) and [specific angular momentum](../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md) are

$$
\varepsilon=\frac{v_p^2}{2}-\frac{GM}{r_p}=-2.1972\times10^8\,\mathrm{m^2s^{-2}},\qquad\ell=r_pv_p.
$$

The negative energy gives an [elliptic orbit](../../../../../elliptic-orbit.md). Its [semi-major axis](../../../../../semi-major-axis.md) is

$$
a=-\frac{GM}{2\varepsilon}=3.0376\times10^{11}\,\mathrm m.
$$

Since $r_p+r_a=2a$,

$$
\boxed{r_a=2a-r_p=5.58\times10^{11}\,\mathrm m.}
$$

This treats the star as fixed, appropriate to $m\ll M$. Equivalently, equate the energy at the two turning points using $v_a=\ell/r_a$. The planet [mass](../../../../../mass.md) cancels; a circular-orbit relation would be inappropriate here.
