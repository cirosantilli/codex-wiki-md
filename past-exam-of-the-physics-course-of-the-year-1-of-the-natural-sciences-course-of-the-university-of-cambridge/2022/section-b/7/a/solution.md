<h1 id="section-b/7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [mass](../../../../../mass.md) and surface [area](../../../../../surface-area.md) of the spherical droplet are $m=(4\pi/3)\rho r^3$ and $A=4\pi r^2$. Constant surface [mass accretion rate](../../../../../mass-accretion-rate.md) $\beta$ means

$$
\dot m=4\pi r^2\beta=4\pi\rho r^2\dot r.
$$

Therefore $\dot r=\beta/\rho=\mu$, and the initial radius gives

$$
\boxed{r(t)=r_0+\mu t,\qquad\mu=\frac\beta\rho.}
$$

Here $[\beta]=\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$, $[\rho]=\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, and $[\mu]=\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. This [surface-fed growth of an accreting sphere](../../../../../surface-fed-growth-of-an-accreting-sphere.md) law assumes constant droplet [mass density](../../../../../density.md).
