<h1 id="section-b/9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [scanning tunneling microscope](../../../../../scanning-tunneling-microscope.md) brings a tip and surface, both [electrical conductors](../../../../../electrical-conductor.md), close enough to leave only a small [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md) gap. An applied [electric potential difference](../../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) makes occupied [electron](../../../../../electron.md) states on one side able to feed unoccupied states on the other. Although the gap is a classically forbidden [potential barrier](../../../../../potential-barrier.md), [quantum tunnelling](../../../../../quantum-tunnelling.md) through its finite width produces an [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md).

For an approximate [rectangular potential barrier](../../../../../rectangular-potential-barrier.md) of gap width $d$, the [transmission probability](../../../../../transmission-probability.md), and thus the [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) at fixed small bias, varies approximately as $e^{-2\kappa d}$. This strong distance dependence makes the device sensitive to changes in separation on the scale of a [chemical atom](../../../../../atom-physics.md). [Feedback control](../../../../../closed-loop-control.md) moves the tip vertically to keep the [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) constant during a scan; the required height changes form an image of the surface, with contrast also depending on its electronic states. The finite gap permits transmitted [probability current](../../../../../probability-current.md), whereas the semi-infinite step in part (b) does not.
