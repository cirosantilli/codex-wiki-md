<h1 id="section-b/9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Classically, a particle with $0<E<V_0$ is completely reflected and never enters the raised region. In [quantum mechanics](../../../../../quantum-mechanics-split.md) the [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) penetrates the classically forbidden side as an [evanescent wave](../../../../../evanescent-wave.md). Define the [tunnelling decay constant](../../../../../tunnelling-decay-constant.md)

$$
\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}}\hbar.
$$

Boundedness at positive infinity excludes $e^{+\kappa x}$, so

$$
\psi(x)=\begin{cases}Ae^{ikx}+Be^{-ikx},&x\le0,\\Ce^{-\kappa x},&x>0.\end{cases}
$$

Matching the [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) and its [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) gives

$$
\frac BA=\frac{k-i\kappa}{k+i\kappa},\qquad\frac CA=\frac{2k}{k+i\kappa}.
$$

The reflected [probability amplitude](../../../../../probability-amplitude.md) has unit [modulus](../../../../../modulus.md), hence [quantum reflection probability](../../../../../quantum-reflection-probability.md) one. The decaying tail carries zero [probability current](../../../../../probability-current.md): this semi-infinite [potential step](../../../../../step-potential.md) has no transmitted beam at infinity, unlike a finite [potential barrier](../../../../../potential-barrier.md). The sketch shows an exact matched example rather than attempting to assign a single real value to a complex [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md).

<a id="section-b/9/b/image-real-and-imaginary-components-of-a-matched-wavefunction-at-a-potential-step-with-exponential-decay-of-the-probability-density"></a>
![](../../../../../past-exam-of-the-physics-course-of-the-year-1-of-the-natural-sciences-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2022-q9-step-penetration.png)

**[Figure 3](#section-b/9/b/image-real-and-imaginary-components-of-a-matched-wavefunction-at-a-potential-step-with-exponential-decay-of-the-probability-density). Real and imaginary components of a matched wavefunction at a potential step, with exponential decay of the probability density**.

Because the [probability density](../../../../../probability-density.md) decays as $|C|^2e^{-2\kappa x}$, the requested $1/e$ penetration distance is

$$
\boxed{d=\frac1{2\kappa}=\frac{\hbar}{2\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}}.}
$$

For a [proton](../../../../../proton.md), take $m_p=1.67262\times10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $V_0-E=10\,\mathrm{MeV}=1.60218\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm J$ and $\hbar=1.05457\times10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}$. Then

$$
\boxed{d=7.20\times10^{-16}\,\mathrm m=0.720\,\mathrm{fm}.}
$$

The [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) amplitude decay length $1/\kappa=1.44\,\mathrm{fm}$ is twice this value. The nonrelativistic equation supplied in the paper is appropriate for this estimate since $10\,\mathrm{MeV}$ is small compared with the [proton](../../../../../proton.md) [rest energy](../../../../../rest-energy.md), approximately $938\,\mathrm{MeV}$. If the original PDF's unbarred $h$ is instead read literally as the [Planck constant](../../../../../planck-constant.md), the displayed differential equation gives $d_{\rm literal}=2\pi d=4.53\,\mathrm{fm}$. This is a valid calculation for that literal coefficient, but $0.720\,\mathrm{fm}$ is the physical estimate using the standard [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md).
