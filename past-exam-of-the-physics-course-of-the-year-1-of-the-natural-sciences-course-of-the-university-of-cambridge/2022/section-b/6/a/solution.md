<h1 id="section-b/6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose three mutually perpendicular axes through the same point, and label coordinates $x,y,z$. The [moments of inertia](../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) of any [rigid body](../../../../../rigid-body-dynamics-split.md) are

$$
I_x=\int(y^2+z^2)\,dm,\qquad I_y=\int(z^2+x^2)\,dm,\qquad I_z=\int(x^2+y^2)\,dm.
$$

Subtracting gives

$$
I_x+I_y-I_z=2\int z^2\,dm\ge0.
$$

For a thin [planar lamina](../../../../../planar-lamina.md) lying in $z=0$, the right-hand side vanishes, proving the [perpendicular axis theorem](../../../../../perpendicular-axis-theorem.md):

$$
\boxed{I_z=I_x+I_y.}
$$

The two in-plane axes and the perpendicular axis must intersect at one point; this need not be the [centre of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md). For an extended three-dimensional [mass distribution](../../../../../mass-distribution.md), the equality generally fails. The general [moment-of-inertia triangle inequalities](../../../../../moment-of-inertia-triangle-inequalities.md) are

$$
\boxed{I_x+I_y\ge I_z,\quad I_y+I_z\ge I_x,\quad I_z+I_x\ge I_y,}
$$

with equality in the first precisely when all [mass](../../../../../mass.md) lies in the plane perpendicular to its $z$ axis, and cyclically for the others. None of these identities requires [principal axes](../../../../../principal-axis.md).
