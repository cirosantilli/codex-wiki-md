<h1 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The [relativistic kinetic energy](../../../../relativistic-kinetic-energy.md) is the total [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) minus the [rest energy](../../../../rest-energy.md):

$$
\boxed{K=(\gamma-1)Mc^2,\qquad\gamma=\frac1{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}.}
$$

Here $\gamma$ is the [Lorentz factor](../../../../lorentz-factor.md) and $c$ the [speed of light](../../../../speed-of-light.md). The [binomial series](../../../../binomial-series.md), with $b=v/c$, gives

$$
(1-b^2)^{-1/2}=1+\frac12b^2+\frac38b^4+O(b^6),\qquad K=\frac12Mv^2+\frac38M\frac{v^4}{c^2}+O\!\left(M\frac{v^6}{c^4}\right).
$$

For $v\ll c$, the leading term is the classical [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) $Mv^2/2$. The ratio of the fourth-order term to that leading term is $3v^2/(4c^2)$. Consequently

$$
\frac34\frac{v^2}{c^2}=0.01\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\boxed{\frac vc=\sqrt{\frac{0.04}{3}}=0.11547\simeq11.5\%.}
$$

The condition concerns one term of the [binomial series](../../../../binomial-series.md); it is not the condition that the entire relativistic correction be exactly $1\%$.
