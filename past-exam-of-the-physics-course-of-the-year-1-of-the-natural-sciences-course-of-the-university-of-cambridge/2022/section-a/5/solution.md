<h1 id="section-a/5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Write $a=0.020\,\mathrm m$ for the side length. In an [equilateral triangle](../../../../equilateral-triangle.md), the distance from centre to a vertex is $a/\sqrt3$; it is two thirds of the altitude $\sqrt3a/2$. By the [superposition principle](../../../../superposition-principle.md), the [electric potential](../../../../electric-potential.md) at the centre due to the three fixed [electrons](../../../../electron.md) is

$$
\Phi(0)=-\frac{3e}{4\pi\varepsilon_0(a/\sqrt3)}.
$$

Here $e>0$ is the [elementary charge](../../../../elementary-charge.md) and $\varepsilon_0$ the [vacuum permittivity](../../../../vacuum-permittivity.md). The approaching [electron](../../../../electron.md) has [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md) $-e$, so its [potential energy](../../../../potential-energy.md), measured relative to infinity, is positive:

$$
U(0)=(-e)\Phi(0)=\frac{3\sqrt3e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0a}.
$$

The fixed sources do not move, and radiation is excluded. Hence [conservation of mechanical energy](../../../../conservation-of-mechanical-energy.md) gives

$$
\frac12m_ev_\infty^2=U(0),\qquad\boxed{v_\infty=\sqrt{\frac{6\sqrt3e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0m_ea}}.}
$$

Using $e=1.60218\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$, $m_e=9.10938\times10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$ for the [electron mass](../../../../electron-mass.md), and $(4\pi\varepsilon_0)^{-1}=8.98755\times10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2\,C^{-2}}$ gives

$$
U(0)=5.99\times10^{-26}\,\mathrm J=3.74\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{eV},\qquad\boxed{v_\infty\simeq3.63\times10^2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

This [speed](../../../../speed.md) is small enough to justify classical [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md). There is an idealization in the word “stops”: along the line normal to the triangle through its centre, this threshold [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) makes the [electron](../../../../electron.md) approach the central [equilibrium](../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) asymptotically. A smooth, conservative trajectory cannot reach an [equilibrium](../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) with exactly zero [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) in finite time unless it was already there. The energy threshold, and therefore the requested initial [speed](../../../../speed.md), is nevertheless unambiguous.
