<h1 id="section-e/15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [electric dipole](../../../../../electric-dipole.md) is a localized pair of opposite [electric charges](../../../../../electric-charge-split.md), or more generally a neutral [electric charge](../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) distribution with nonzero first electric moment. For charges $-q$ and $+q$ separated by [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) $\mathbf d$ directed from negative to positive, the [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf p=q\mathbf d,\qquad [p]=\mathrm{C\,m}.}
$$

A neutral [molecule](../../../../../molecule-split.md) can have a permanent [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) when its positive and negative charge centres do not coincide. An applied [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) can also produce an induced [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) by distorting an initially nonpolar [electric charge](../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) distribution.

At distance $r\gg|\mathbf d|$ in [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md), expand the difference of the two point-charge potentials to first order in the separation. The leading [electric potential](../../../../../electric-potential.md) is

$$
\Phi(\mathbf r)=\frac{\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r}}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}.
$$

The monopole term cancels because the net [electric charge](../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) is zero. Taking $\mathbf E=-\nabla\Phi$ gives the [electric dipole field](../../../../../electric-dipole-field.md)

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E(\mathbf r)=\frac{3(\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})\widehat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf p}
{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^3}.}
$$

On the dipole axis its magnitude is $2p/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r^3)$ and its direction is along $\mathbf p$; in the equatorial plane the magnitude is $p/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r^3)$ and the field points opposite to $\mathbf p$. The $r^{-3}$ decay is faster than the $r^{-2}$ field of an isolated [point charge](../../../../../point-charge.md).

In a uniform external [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md), the opposite forces on the two charges cancel in their resultant but form a [couple](../../../../../couple-mechanics.md). Its [torque](../../../../../torque.md) is

$$
\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf p\times\mathbf E,\qquad
|\boldsymbol\tau|=pE\sin\theta.
$$

For a fixed permanent [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md), the interaction [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) is

$$
\boxed{U=-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E=-pE\cos\theta.}
$$

Differentiating with respect to orientation recovers the torque. Parallel alignment minimizes $U$ and is stable against a small rotation; antiparallel alignment maximizes $U$ and is unstable. Rotating a fixed dipole slowly from parallel to antiparallel requires external [work](../../../../../work.md) $2pE$.

A nonuniform electrostatic [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) can also exert a net [force](../../../../../force-split.md). Expanding the two charge forces gives $\mathbf F\simeq(\mathbf p\cdot\nabla)\mathbf E$, which equals $\nabla(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E)$ for a fixed dipole in a curl-free electrostatic field. Thus a suitably aligned dipole is attracted toward stronger field. An induced [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) requires care in computing energy: when $\mathbf p=\alpha\mathbf E$ for constant isotropic [electric polarizability](../../../../../electric-polarizability.md), building the dipole costs energy and the effective interaction is $U=-\alpha E^2/2$, rather than substituting directly into the fixed-dipole formula. These field, torque and energy effects explain molecular orientation and [electric polarization](../../../../../polarization-density.md).
