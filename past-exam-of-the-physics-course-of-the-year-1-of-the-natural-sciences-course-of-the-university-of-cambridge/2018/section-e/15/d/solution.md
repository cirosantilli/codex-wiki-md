<h1 id="section-e/15/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For a light [test particle](../../../../../test-particle.md) of [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $m$ outside a spherical source of [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $M$, [Newton's law of universal gravitation](../../../../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation-split.md) gives $\mathbf F=-GMm\widehat{\mathbf r}/r^2$. It is a [central force](../../../../../central-force.md), so its [torque](../../../../../torque.md) about the source vanishes and [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) $\mathbf L=m\mathbf r\times\mathbf v$ is conserved. The motion therefore lies in a fixed plane when $L\ne0$. Its [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../gravitational-energy.md) is $-GMm/r$, and [conservation of mechanical energy](../../../../../conservation-of-mechanical-energy.md) gives

$$
\epsilon=\frac{v^2}{2}-\frac{GM}{r}=\text{constant},\qquad
h=\frac Lm=|\mathbf r\times\mathbf v|=\text{constant}.
$$

Here $\epsilon$ is [specific orbital energy](../../../../../specific-orbital-energy.md) and $h$ is [specific angular momentum](../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md). A [Kepler orbit](../../../../../kepler-orbit.md) has polar equation

$$
\boxed{r(\theta)=\frac{p}{1+e\cos\theta},\qquad
p=\frac{h^2}{GM},\qquad
 e^2=1+\frac{2\epsilon h^2}{(GM)^2}.}
$$

The angle is measured from periapsis. The [conic section](../../../../../conic-section.md) is an [ellipse](../../../../../ellipse.md) for $\epsilon<0$ and $0\le e<1$, a [parabola](../../../../../parabola.md) for $\epsilon=0$ and $e=1$, and a [hyperbola](../../../../../hyperbola.md) for $\epsilon>0$ and $e>1$. The circle is the $e=0$ elliptical case. The special case $h=0$ is radial motion and must be handled as a degenerate limit. Bound ellipses have the source at one focus, which is [Kepler's first law](../../../../../kepler-s-first-law.md).

For a bound orbit of [semi-major axis](../../../../../semi-major-axis.md) $a$, $\epsilon=-GM/(2a)$. Combining this with the energy equation gives the [vis-viva equation](../../../../../vis-viva-equation.md):

$$
\boxed{v^2=GM\left(\frac2r-\frac1a\right).}
$$

The particle therefore moves faster near periapsis and slower near apoapsis. Conservation of [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) also gives constant [areal velocity](../../../../../areal-velocity.md), $dA/dt=h/2$, expressing [Kepler's second law](../../../../../kepler-s-second-law.md). Using the ellipse's area and $h^2=GMa(1-e^2)$ gives the [orbital period](../../../../../orbital-period.md)

$$
\boxed{T^2=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{GM},}
$$

which is [Kepler's third law](../../../../../kepler-s-third-law.md).

A [circular orbit](../../../../../circular-orbit.md) follows from [centripetal acceleration](../../../../../centripetal-acceleration.md) $v_c^2/r=GM/r^2$, giving $v_c=\sqrt{GM/r}$ and $T=2\pi\sqrt{r^3/(GM)}$. The minimum [escape velocity](../../../../../escape-velocity.md) from radius $r$ follows from the marginally unbound condition $\epsilon=0$:

$$
\boxed{v_c=\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}},\qquad v_{\rm esc}=\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}=\sqrt2\,v_c.}
$$

These ideal orbits neglect [drag force](../../../../../drag-physics.md), other gravitating bodies and changes in the central source. For two comparable [masses](../../../../../mass.md), the relative [two-body problem](../../../../../two-body-problem.md) uses $G(M+m)$ instead of $GM$. An orbiting object is continuously in [free fall](../../../../../free-fall.md); the inward gravitational [force](../../../../../force-split.md) changes its direction even when its [speed](../../../../../speed.md) is constant. These inverse-square results assume the physical variation of gravity with radius, rather than extending a near-surface constant-$g$ approximation to an entire orbit.
