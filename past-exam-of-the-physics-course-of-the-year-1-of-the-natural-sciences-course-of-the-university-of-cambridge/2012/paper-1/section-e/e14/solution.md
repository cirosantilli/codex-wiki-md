<h1 id="section-e/e14/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E14](../e14.md)

For steady currents in vacuum, [Ampère's law](../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) is $\oint\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf l=\mu_0I_{\rm enclosed}$. A rectangular loop with one long side inside an ideal long [solenoid](../../../../../solenoid.md) and the other outside gives $B\ell=\mu_0(n\ell)I$, because the exterior field is negligible and the perpendicular sides contribute zero. Thus **$B=\mu_0nI$**, approximately uniform away from the ends.

Write the [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [electric charge](../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) as $q_e=-e$, with $e>0$. The [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md) is $\mathbf F=-e\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$; it does no work. For perpendicular motion, $mv^2/r_L=evB$ gives

$$
\boxed{\omega_c=\frac{eB}{m},\qquad r_L=\frac{mv}{eB}.}
$$

Here $\omega_c$ is the positive [angular speed](../../../../../angular-speed.md); the rotation sense is determined by the negative [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [electric charge](../../../../../electric-charge-split.md).

The parallel [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) is unaffected, while the perpendicular component rotates. Hence the path is a [helix](../../../../../helix.md) with $v_\parallel=v\cos\theta$ and $r_L=mv\sin\theta/(eB)$. For [on-axis entry into a solenoidal tube](../../../../../on-axis-entry-into-a-solenoidal-tube.md), the transverse circle passes through that axis: its [guiding centre](../../../../../guiding-center.md) is displaced by $r_L$, and its maximum distance from the tube axis is **$2r_L$**, not $r_L$.

<a id="section-e/e14/image-end-on-electron-orbit-the-circle-passes-through-the-tube-axis-and-reaches-twice-its-larmor-radius-from-it"></a>
![](../../../../../past-exam-of-the-physics-course-of-the-year-1-of-the-natural-sciences-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2012/paper-1-ia2012-electron-orbit.png)

**[Figure 5](#section-e/e14/image-end-on-electron-orbit-the-circle-passes-through-the-tube-axis-and-reaches-twice-its-larmor-radius-from-it). End-on electron orbit: the circle passes through the tube axis and reaches twice its Larmor radius from it**.

Using the nonrelativistic approximation appropriate to $1\,\mathrm{keV}$,

$$
v=\sqrt{\frac{2E_0}{m}}=1.876\times10^7\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\qquad
\boxed{t=\frac{l}{v\cos3^\circ}=4.27\,\mathrm{ns}.}
$$

The [cyclotron period](../../../../../cyclotron-period.md) is $2\pi/\omega_c=0.893\,\mathrm{ns}$, so the flight lasts $4.783$ revolutions. The [electron](../../../../../electron.md) therefore crosses the axis again **four times** before leaving. At $3^\circ$, $2r_L=0.279\,\mathrm{mm}<a$. Wall clearance requires $2mv\sin\theta/(eB)\le a$, giving

$$
\boxed{\theta_m=\sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{eBa}{2mv}\right)=5.38^\circ.}
$$

The transit is longer than a full orbit, so the maximum transverse excursion really is sampled before exit.

For the [solenoidal time-of-flight energy window](../../../../../solenoidal-time-of-flight-energy-window.md), the prescribed time fixes $v_\parallel=l/t_0=v_0=\sqrt{2E_0/m}$, while $0\le v_\perp\le eBa/(2m)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{E_{\min}=E_0=1.000\,\mathrm{keV},\qquad
E_{\max}=E_0+\frac{e^2B^2a^2}{8m}=1.0088\,\mathrm{keV}.}
$$

These are total [kinetic energies](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md). The upper value is the ideal grazing limit; if touching the wall counts as rejection, it is a [supremum](../../../../../supremum.md) approached from below.
