<h1 id="section-e/e15/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For two isolated [point masses](../../../../../../point-mass.md), introduce total [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) $M_T=M+m$, [reduced mass](../../../../../../reduced-mass.md) $\mu=Mm/M_T$ and relative separation $r$. The relative [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) is $\ddot{\mathbf r}=-GM_T\mathbf r/r^3$. Conserved [specific angular momentum](../../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md) $h=|\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r}|$ fixes a plane and a constant areal [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) $h/2$. The [specific orbital energy](../../../../../../specific-orbital-energy.md) is

$$
\varepsilon=\frac{\dot r^2}{2}+\frac{h^2}{2r^2}-\frac{GM_T}{r}.
$$

For $h\ne0$, the trajectory is a conic:

$$
\boxed{r(\varphi)=\frac{h^2/(GM_T)}{1+e\cos(\varphi-\varphi_0)},\qquad
e^2=1+\frac{2\varepsilon h^2}{G^2M_T^2}.}
$$

Negative [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) gives an [ellipse](../../../../../../ellipse.md), including the circular limit $e=0$; zero [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) gives a [parabola](../../../../../../parabola.md); positive [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) gives a [hyperbola](../../../../../../hyperbola.md). Bound [ellipses](../../../../../../ellipse.md) obey $\varepsilon=-GM_T/(2a_o)$ and

$$
T^2=\frac{4\pi^2a_o^3}{GM_T}.
$$

Thus [Kepler's laws of planetary motion](../../../../../../kepler-s-laws-of-planetary-motion.md) follow from [angular momentum conservation](../../../../../../conservation-of-angular-momentum.md) and the [inverse-square force](../../../../../../inverse-square-force.md). For a [circular orbit](../../../../../../circular-orbit.md) of [radius](../../../../../../radius.md) $r$, $v_c=\sqrt{GM_T/r}$, while escape from that [radius](../../../../../../radius.md) requires $v_{\rm esc}=\sqrt{2GM_T/r}$. **[Energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) determines whether the orbit is bound; [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) also determines its shape.** These ideal results neglect extended-body perturbations, drag and relativistic corrections.
