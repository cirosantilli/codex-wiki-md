<h1 id="section-e/e15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [electric dipole](../../../../../../electric-dipole.md) consists of opposite charges separated by a small displacement, with [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) $\mathbf p=q\mathbf d$ directed from negative to positive. At distances large compared with its size,

$$
\Phi=\frac{\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r}}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2},\qquad
\mathbf E=\frac{3(\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})\widehat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf p}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3}.
$$

The net charge term vanishes; the field decays faster than a monopole field. In an imposed slowly varying [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md),

$$
\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf p\times\mathbf E,\qquad U=-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E,\qquad
\mathbf F=\nabla(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E)
$$

for a permanent fixed dipole. A uniform field produces a [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) but no net [force](../../../../../../force-split.md). For a linear induced dipole $\mathbf p=\alpha\mathbf E$, the interaction [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) is instead $-\alpha E^2/2$, because building the dipole also costs [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md).

A small planar [current loop](../../../../../../current-loop.md) has [magnetic dipole moment](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) $\boldsymbol\mu=IA\widehat{\mathbf n}$, with the normal fixed by the [right-hand rule](../../../../../../right-hand-rule.md). Outside the source,

$$
\mathbf B=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r^3}[3(\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})\widehat{\mathbf r}-\boldsymbol\mu],\qquad
\boldsymbol\tau=\boldsymbol\mu\times\mathbf B,\qquad U=-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B.
$$

[Magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) lines form closed loops; a current-loop dipole does not require [magnetic monopoles](../../../../../../magnetic-monopole.md). **Dipole moments describe the leading directional field of a neutral localized source**, and their [torques](../../../../../../torque.md) explain alignment in external fields.
