<h1 id="section-a/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For a [diffraction grating](../../../../diffraction-grating.md) with $N$ illuminated lines in order $p$, its ideal [spectral resolving power](../../../../spectral-resolving-power.md) is

$$
\mathcal R=\frac\lambda{\Delta\lambda}=pN.
$$

The two [spectral lines](../../../../spectral-line.md) have separation $\Delta\lambda=3.4\,\mathrm{nm}$ and mean [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) $\overline\lambda=395.1\,\mathrm{nm}$. In first order, $p=1$, so the [Rayleigh criterion](../../../../rayleigh-criterion.md) requires

$$
N\gtrsim\frac{395.1}{3.4}=116.2.
$$

With line density $500\,\mathrm{mm^{-1}}$, the ideal continuous estimate for the illuminated width is

$$
W\simeq\frac{116.2}{500\,\mathrm{mm^{-1}}}=\boxed{0.2324\,\mathrm{mm}\simeq0.23\,\mathrm{mm}.}
$$

Rounding the required number of illuminated grating lines upward gives **$117$ lines**, conventionally an illuminated width $W\simeq117/(500\,\mathrm{mm^{-1}})=\boxed{0.234\,\mathrm{mm}}$. Using the longer [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) rather than the mean also gives the same integer requirement. This is the ideal limit set by the [resolving power of a diffraction grating](../../../../resolving-power-of-a-diffraction-grating.md); additional broadening from the illumination or observing apparatus could require a larger width.
