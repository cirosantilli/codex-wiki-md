<h1 id="section-b/8/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The starting radius is $r_0=\sqrt2\,c$. In uniform [circular motion](../../../../../circular-motion.md), the required [centripetal acceleration](../../../../../centripetal-acceleration.md) has magnitude $\omega^2r_0=kr_0/m$. The initial [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) must be tangent to the starting radius, with the orientation chosen anticlockwise:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf v(0)=\omega\widehat{\mathbf z}\times(c,c,0)=(-\omega c,\omega c).}
$$

The resulting trajectory is

$$
x=c(\cos\omega t-\sin\omega t),\qquad y=c(\cos\omega t+\sin\omega t).
$$

It has constant $x^2+y^2=2c^2$, so

$$
\boxed{U(t)=kc^2\quad\text{for all }t.}
$$

This is the horizontal line in the right panel of the figure. The [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) also equals $kc^2$, giving total [mechanical energy](../../../../../mechanical-energy.md) $2kc^2$, twice that for release from rest at the same point.
