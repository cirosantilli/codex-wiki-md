<h1 id="section-b/8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put $\delta=\phi_2-\phi_1$. If both coordinate [amplitudes](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) are nonzero, eliminate the common [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) to obtain

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{x}{x_0}\right)^2+\left(\frac{y}{y_0}\right)^2-2\frac{xy}{x_0y_0}\cos\delta=\sin^2\delta.}
$$

For $\sin\delta\ne0$, this is an [ellipse](../../../../../ellipse.md) centred at the origin. The following special cases exhaust the possible nontrivial shapes:

- $\delta=0$ or $\pi$ gives straight-line [simple harmonic motion](../../../../../simple-harmonic-motion.md), including an axis-aligned line if one coordinate [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) vanishes.
- $\delta=\pm\pi/2$ gives an axis-aligned [ellipse](../../../../../ellipse.md); if additionally $x_0=y_0$, it is a [circle](../../../../../circle.md) traversed in one of the two orientations.
- Other [phase differences](../../../../../phase-difference.md) give tilted [ellipses](../../../../../ellipse.md).

The degenerate zero-[amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) solution is the stationary origin. There are no general multi-loop patterns here because the two [angular frequencies](../../../../../angular-frequency.md) are equal. Another useful description is $\mathbf r=\mathbf a\cos\omega t+\mathbf b\sin\omega t$, the image of a [circle](../../../../../circle.md) under a [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md).
