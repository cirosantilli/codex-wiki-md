<h1 id="section-b/10/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For steady [electric currents](../../../../../electric-current.md) in [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md), [Ampère's circuital law](../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) is

$$
\boxed{\oint_C\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf l=\mu_0 I_{\rm enclosed}.}
$$

Choose an oriented closed contour and a spanning surface; the positive enclosed [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) crosses that surface according to the [right-hand rule](../../../../../right-hand-rule.md) for the contour. First use the source [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) to establish the direction of $\mathbf B$ and which positions have the same magnitude. Then choose the contour so its useful segments are parallel to $\mathbf B$ with constant magnitude, and the remaining segments are perpendicular to $\mathbf B$ or have vanishing field contribution. The [line integral](../../../../../line-integral.md) reduces to the unknown magnitude times a known length or sum of lengths.

For example, cylindrical [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) around an infinite wire allows a circular contour, giving $B(2\pi r)=\mu_0I$. A uniform [current sheet](../../../../../current-sheet.md) instead calls for a rectangle straddling it. The law is always valid in this magnetostatic setting, but by itself does not determine the local [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) from an arbitrary enclosed current without these extra [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) arguments. Here $\mu_0$ is the [vacuum permeability](../../../../../vacuum-permeability.md).
