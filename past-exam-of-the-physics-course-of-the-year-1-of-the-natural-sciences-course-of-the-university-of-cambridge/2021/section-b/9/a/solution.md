<h1 id="section-b/9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the original diagram's positive distances: $u$ from the object to the vertex, $d$ from the vertex to the image, and $R$ from the vertex toward the centre of curvature on the outgoing side. Let a paraxial [light ray](../../../../../light-ray.md) strike at height $Y$. Its incoming inclination is $\alpha\simeq Y/u$ above the [optical axis](../../../../../optical-axis.md), its outgoing inclination is $\beta\simeq Y/d$ below it, and the inward surface normal is inclined by $\theta\simeq Y/R$ below it. Thus the incidence and refraction angles relative to that normal are respectively $\alpha+\theta$ and $\theta-\beta$.

The local tangent-plane form of [Snell's law](../../../../../snell-s-law.md), using the [small-angle approximation](../../../../../small-angle-approximation.md), gives

$$
n_1(\alpha+\theta)=n_2(\theta-\beta),\qquad n_1\alpha+n_2\beta=(n_2-n_1)\theta.
$$

Substitute the three height-to-distance ratios and cancel $Y$:

$$
\boxed{\frac{n_1}{u}+\frac{n_2}{d}=\frac{n_2-n_1}{R}.}
$$

This [refraction at a spherical surface](../../../../../refraction-at-a-spherical-surface.md) result keeps only first-order ray angles. The surface sag shifts the height's axial reference only at higher order. Distances for other object, image or curvature orientations must be signed consistently; the displayed positive-distance form follows the particular orientation in the paper.
