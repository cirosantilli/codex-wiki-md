<h1 id="section-e/e14/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E14](../e14.md)

The closed surface integral sums the outward electric [flux](../../../../../flux.md): $\mathbf E$ is the [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md), $d\mathbf S$ its outward vector [area](../../../../../surface-area.md), $Q$ the total enclosed charge and $\epsilon_0$ the [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md) permittivity. [Gauss's law](../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) relates that [flux](../../../../../flux.md) to enclosed charge for any closed surface. High [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) can make the field's magnitude constant on a suitable [Gaussian surface](../../../../../gaussian-surface.md) and fix its direction; without [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md), [flux](../../../../../flux.md) alone does not determine the local field.

For an isolated conducting [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md), charges reside at its surface and the exterior field is radial. Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E(r)=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2}\widehat{\mathbf r}\quad(r>a),\qquad
V(a)=\int_a^\infty E(r)\,dr=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0a},\qquad C=4\pi\epsilon_0a.}
$$

The conductor has zero interior field. Integrating exterior field [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) gives

$$
U=\int_a^\infty\frac12\epsilon_0\left(\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2}\right)^2 4\pi r^2dr
=\boxed{\frac{Q^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0a}}.
$$

Let the initial charge per drop be $q=4\pi\epsilon_0aV_1$. After disconnecting the charging supply, charge is conserved. [Volume](../../../../../volume.md) conservation gives final [radius](../../../../../radius.md) $A=2^{1/3}a$, total charge $2q$ and

$$
\boxed{V_f=\frac{2q}{4\pi\epsilon_0A}=2^{2/3}V_1.}
$$

Initial mutual [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is negligible at the stipulated large separation. Hence

$$
U_i=\frac{q^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0a},\qquad
U_f=\frac{4q^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0\,2^{1/3}a}=2^{2/3}U_i,\qquad
\boxed{U_i/U_f=2^{-2/3}.}
$$

**The [electrostatic energy increase on equal-drop coalescence](../../../../../electrostatic-energy-increase-on-equal-drop-coalescence.md) is positive.** It can be supplied by the decrease in surface [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) during coalescence and/or by external mechanical work bringing like charges together. Remaining [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) can become motion and heat. If the charge is too large, surface-energy release alone need not suffice to make merging spontaneous; conservation does not assert that it will. Keeping the drops connected to a [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) supply would instead be a different charge-balance problem.
