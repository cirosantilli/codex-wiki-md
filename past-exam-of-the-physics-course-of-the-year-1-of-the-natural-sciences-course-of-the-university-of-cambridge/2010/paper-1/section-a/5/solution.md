<h1 id="section-a/5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

For a [proper lifetime inferred from a delayed decay flash](../../../../../proper-lifetime-inferred-from-a-delayed-decay-flash.md), the measured interval ends when the light returns to the detector, not when the [muon](../../../../../muon.md) decays. Put passage at $(t,x)=(0,0)$ in the detector frame. If decay occurs at $t_d$, its distance is $vt_d$, and the backwards [photon](../../../../../photon.md) takes another $vt_d/c$ to arrive. Therefore

$$
t_{\rm record}=t_d(1+v/c),\qquad t_d=\frac{10\,\mathrm{ns}}{1+1/2}=\frac{20}{3}\,\mathrm{ns}.
$$

The [muon](../../../../../muon.md)'s proper elapsed time is

$$
\boxed{\Delta\tau=t_d\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}
=10\,\mathrm{ns}\sqrt{\frac{1-v/c}{1+v/c}}
=\frac{10}{\sqrt3}\,\mathrm{ns}\approx5.77\,\mathrm{ns}.}
$$

Applying [time dilation](../../../../../time-dilation.md) directly to the 10 ns reception interval would transform the wrong pair of events.
