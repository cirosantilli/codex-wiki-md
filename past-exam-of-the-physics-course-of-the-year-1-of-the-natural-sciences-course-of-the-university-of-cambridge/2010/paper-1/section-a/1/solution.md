<h1 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

For [two equal lenses with a virtual intermediate object](../../../../../two-equal-lenses-with-a-virtual-intermediate-object.md), use positive distances for real objects on a lens's incident side and [real images](../../../../../real-image.md) on its outgoing side. The first lens gives $1/v_1=1/f-1/u_1=1/30-1/60$, hence $v_1=60\,\mathrm{cm}$. At the second lens these rays are already converging toward a point $50\,\mathrm{cm}$ to its right: its object distance is therefore $u_2=-50\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
\frac1{v_2}=\frac1{30}-\frac1{-50}=\frac4{75}\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}},\qquad
\boxed{v_2=18.75\,\mathrm{cm}\text{ to the right of lens 2}.}
$$

Thus **the final image is real**, $28.75\,\mathrm{cm}$ to the right of lens 1. Its [transverse magnification](../../../../../transverse-magnification.md) is $(-60/60)(-18.75/[-50])=-3/8$: it is inverted. The intermediate focus is not a real object placed before lens 2.
