<h1 id="section-b/b9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B9](../b9.md)

For [collinear inelastic capture followed by two photons](../../../../../collinear-inelastic-capture-followed-by-two-photons.md), each particle has $\mathbf p=\gamma m\mathbf u$ and $E=\gamma mc^2$, with $\gamma=(1-u^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$. An isolated collision conserves the vector sum of [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) and total [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), including [rest energy](../../../../../rest-energy.md), internal excitation and radiation. It need not conserve the sum of individual [rest masses](../../../../../invariant-mass.md). At low [speed](../../../../../speed.md),

$$
E=mc^2+\tfrac12mu^2+O(mu^4/c^2),
$$

recovering the classical [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) after subtracting $mc^2$.

Here the incoming [Lorentz factors](../../../../../lorentz-factor.md) are $5/3$ and $5/4$, so

$$
E_1=E_2=5mc^2,\qquad p_{1x}=4mc,\quad p_{2x}=-3mc.
$$

The composite therefore has

$$
\boxed{v=\frac{pc^2}{E}=\frac c{10}\text{ in the }+x\text{ direction},\qquad
M=\frac{\sqrt{E^2-p^2c^2}}{c^2}=\sqrt{99}\,m=3\sqrt{11}\,m.}
$$

Its [rest mass](../../../../../invariant-mass.md) exceeds $7m$ because relative [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) has become internal [rest energy](../../../../../rest-energy.md).

For the subsequent collinear [photon](../../../../../photon.md) pair, $E_++E_-=10mc^2$ and $(E_+-E_-)/c=mc$. Thus $E_+=11mc^2/2$, $E_-=9mc^2/2$, and

$$
\boxed{\nu_+/\nu_-=11/9.}
$$

The positive-$x$ [photon](../../../../../photon.md) is the higher-frequency one.
