<h1 id="section-a/a4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A4](../a4.md)

For an ideal air-gap [parallel-plate capacitor](../../../../../parallel-plate-capacitor.md), neglecting fringing, the [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) between oppositely charged plates is $E=\sigma/\epsilon_0=Q/(\epsilon_0 A)$. [Dielectric breakdown](../../../../../dielectric-breakdown.md) therefore limits the magnitude of the [electric charge](../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) on either plate to

$$
\boxed{Q_{\max}=\epsilon_0 A E_{\rm bd}=(8.854\times10^{-12})(0.03)^2(5\times10^6)\simeq3.98\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm C=39.8\ \mathrm{nC}.}
$$

The two plates carry $+Q$ and $-Q$; the quoted stored [electric charge](../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) is the magnitude on one plate, rather than the net [electric charge](../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) of the whole [capacitor](../../../../../capacitor.md). The gap does not enter this ideal bound: $C=\epsilon_0 A/d$ and $V_{\max}=E_{\rm bd}d$ make $CV_{\max}$ independent of $d$. Edge enhancement of the [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) can lower the practical limit.
