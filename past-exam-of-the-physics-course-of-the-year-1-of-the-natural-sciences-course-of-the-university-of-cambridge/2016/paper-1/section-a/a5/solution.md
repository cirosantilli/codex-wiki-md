<h1 id="section-a/a5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A5](../a5.md)

Far from its ends, approximate the body by an infinite uniform [cylinder](../../../../../cylinder-geometry.md). By cylindrical [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md), the [Newtonian gravitational field](../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-field.md) is radial. Applying [Gauss's law for gravity](../../../../../gauss-s-law-for-gravity.md) to a coaxial cylindrical surface of radius $s$ and length $\ell$ outside the material gives

$$
g(s)\,2\pi s\ell=4\pi G\rho\pi R^2\ell,\qquad g(s)=\frac{2\pi G\rho R^2}{s}.
$$

At the surface, $R=50\ \mathrm m$, so

$$
\boxed{g(R)\simeq2\pi G\rho R\simeq4.19\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}.}
$$

The [gravitational acceleration](../../../../../gravitational-acceleration.md) points inward toward the axis. Its small magnitude reflects both the small size and the modest [density](../../../../../density.md). The infinite-[cylinder](../../../../../cylinder-geometry.md) approximation uses the specified distance from both ends; a spherical approximation would discard the relevant geometry. No rotational contribution is included, since no rotation rate is supplied.
