<h1 id="section-b/b8/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At first contact the globally highest trajectory just touches the ceiling at its apex. Its flight time to that point is $t_*=r\omega\cos\theta_*/g$. The horizontal displacement from the wheel axis is

$$
x_{\rm hit}=-r\cos\theta_*+r\omega\sin\theta_*\frac{r\omega\cos\theta_*}{g}=\cos\theta_*\left(-r+\frac{r^2\omega^2}{g}\sin\theta_*\right)=0.
$$

Hence **the first point struck is directly above the rotation axis**, independently of $h$. The release starts to the left of the axis, but the drop's horizontal travel exactly cancels that offset at the optimum apex. The first-contact [angular velocity](../../../../../../angular-velocity.md) satisfies $r^2\omega_*^2=g(h+\sqrt{h^2-r^2})$.
