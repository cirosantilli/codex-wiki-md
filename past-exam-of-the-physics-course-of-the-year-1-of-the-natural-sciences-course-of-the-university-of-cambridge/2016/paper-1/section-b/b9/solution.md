<h1 id="section-b/b9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B9](../b9.md)

The [Michelson-Morley experiment](../../../../../michelson-morley-experiment.md) splits coherent [light](../../../../../light.md) into two perpendicular arms, reflects the beams and recombines them to observe [interference fringes](../../../../../interference-fringe.md). Rotating the apparatus interchanges each arm's orientation relative to a proposed stationary [luminiferous aether](../../../../../luminiferous-aether.md). The historical experiment used multiple reflections to increase the effective optical path and a rotatable support to compare orientations.[1887, printed pp. 333–341: optical arrangement, rotation and fringe-shift comparison](../../../../../on-the-relative-motion-of-the-earth-and-the-luminiferous-ether-by-albert-abraham-michelson-and-edward-w-morley.md)

**[Figure 2](#section-b/b9/image-original-schematic-of-perpendicular-round-trip-optical-paths-in-a-michelson-interferometer). Original schematic of perpendicular round-trip optical paths in a Michelson interferometer**.

For equal one-way effective arm lengths $\ell$, a classical stationary-aether model gives

$$
t_\parallel=\frac\ell{c-v}+\frac\ell{c+v}\simeq\frac{2\ell}{c}\left(1+\frac{v^2}{c^2}\right),\qquad t_\perp=\frac{2\ell}{\sqrt{c^2-v^2}}\simeq\frac{2\ell}{c}\left(1+\frac{v^2}{2c^2}\right).
$$

A right-angle rotation should reverse their difference, producing a [fringe shift](../../../../../fringe-shift.md) of approximately $2\ell v^2/(\lambda c^2)$. The observed orientation-dependent shift was much smaller than the stationary-aether prediction. In [special relativity](../../../../../special-relativity-split.md), the apparatus rest frame has the same vacuum [speed of light](../../../../../speed-of-light.md) along both arms, so no such orientation-dependent shift is expected. The result supports [special relativity](../../../../../special-relativity-split.md) but does not, by itself, uniquely establish every postulate or exclude every modified aether theory.

For the rocket, take its tail at $x'=0$ and nose at $x'=L_0$, with coincident frame origins when $t=t'=0$. Denote emission, reflection and return by $A,B,C$. In the rocket's [inertial frame](../../../../../inertial-frame.md), their coordinates $(t',x')$ are

$$
\boxed{A:(0,L_0),\quad B:(L_0/c,0),\quad C:(2L_0/c,L_0).}
$$

The inverse [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) is $x=\Gamma(x'+ut')$, $t=\Gamma(t'+ux'/c^2)$, where $\Gamma=(1-u^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$. Thus the same [spacetime events](../../../../../event-relativity.md), in coordinates $(t,x)$, are

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
A&:\left(\Gamma uL_0/c^2,\ \Gamma L_0\right),\\
B&:\left(\Gamma L_0/c,\ \Gamma uL_0/c\right),\\
C&:\left(\Gamma(2L_0/c+uL_0/c^2),\ \Gamma(L_0+2uL_0/c)\right).
\end{aligned}}
$$

There is no requirement that emission have $t=0$ in this chosen laboratory coordinate system.

The nose-to-tail travel time is $\Delta t_{AB}=\Gamma L_0(1-u/c)/c$. If $L$ is the laboratory length measured at one laboratory time, a left-moving pulse and the right-moving tail close their initial separation at speed $c+u$. Therefore

$$
L=(c+u)\Delta t_{AB}=\Gamma L_0(1-u^2/c^2),\qquad \boxed{L=L_0/\Gamma.}
$$

This derives [length contraction](../../../../../length-contraction.md) from the pulse's travel. Comparing the positions of the two endpoints at different laboratory times would not measure the rocket's laboratory length.
