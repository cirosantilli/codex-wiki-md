<h1 id="section-e/e15/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

An [electric dipole](../../../../../../electric-dipole.md) consists of opposite [electric charges](../../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) separated by a short displacement. Its [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) is $\mathbf p=q\mathbf d$, directed from negative to positive [electric charge](../../../../../../electric-charge-split.md). At distances large compared with the separation, its leading [electric potential](../../../../../../electric-potential.md) and [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) in vacuum are

$$
\boxed{V(\mathbf r)=\frac{\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r}}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2},\qquad \mathbf E(\mathbf r)=\frac{3(\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})\widehat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf p}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3}.}
$$

The absence of net [electric charge](../../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) removes the monopole term; the dipole [electric potential](../../../../../../electric-potential.md) therefore falls as $r^{-2}$ rather than $r^{-1}$. On its axis the [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is parallel to $\mathbf p$ with magnitude $2p/(4\pi\epsilon_0r^3)$; on the equatorial plane it points oppositely with half that magnitude.

In a uniform applied [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md), the two [forces](../../../../../../force-split.md) cancel as a net translation but form a couple. The [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) and [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) are

$$
\boxed{\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf p\times\mathbf E,\qquad U=-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E.}
$$

Alignment parallel to the [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is stable. A nonuniform [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) can also exert a net [force](../../../../../../force-split.md), $\mathbf F=\nabla(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E)$ for a fixed permanent [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md). An induced [electric dipole](../../../../../../electric-dipole.md) instead depends on the applied [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md), so its [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) must account for the work needed to create it.
