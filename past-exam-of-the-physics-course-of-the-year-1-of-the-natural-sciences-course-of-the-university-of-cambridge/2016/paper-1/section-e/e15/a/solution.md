<h1 id="section-e/e15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In a fixed point-mass [Newtonian gravitational field](../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-field.md), a test [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) $m$ experiences

$$
\mathbf F=-\frac{GMm}{r^2}\widehat{\mathbf r}.
$$

Because this is a [central force](../../../../../../central-force.md), its [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) about the attracting centre vanishes and [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) is conserved. The orbit lies in a fixed plane and sweeps out equal areas in equal times. Since the [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) is also conservative, the specific orbital [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) is constant:

$$
\varepsilon=\frac12\dot r^2+\frac{j^2}{2r^2}-\frac{GM}{r},\qquad j=r^2\dot\varphi.
$$

The last two terms form an [effective potential](../../../../../../effective-potential.md). They show how the angular-momentum barrier competes with attraction and determines the turning radii. For nonradial motion the orbit is a [conic section](../../../../../../conic-section.md):

$$
\boxed{r=\frac{p}{1+e\cos(\varphi-\varphi_0)},\qquad p=\frac{j^2}{GM},\qquad e^2=1+\frac{2\varepsilon j^2}{G^2M^2}.}
$$

Negative [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) gives an [ellipse](../../../../../../ellipse.md), with $e<1$; zero [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) gives a [parabola](../../../../../../parabola.md); positive [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) gives a [hyperbola](../../../../../../hyperbola.md). The circular limit has $e=0$, $v_c=\sqrt{GM/r}$ and $\varepsilon=-GM/(2r)$. For a bound [ellipse](../../../../../../ellipse.md) of semimajor axis $a$, $\varepsilon=-GM/(2a)$ and the [orbital period](../../../../../../orbital-period.md) is $T=2\pi\sqrt{a^3/(GM)}$. The local [escape speed](../../../../../../escape-velocity.md) is $\sqrt{2GM/r}$. These are the point-mass, test-particle limits; extended mass distributions or noncentral perturbations change the orbit laws.
