**Experimental evidence**. [Tests of special relativity](../../../../tests-of-special-relativity.md) check linked predictions about light propagation, clocks and [four-momentum](../../../../four-momentum.md). Several independent kinds of experiment support those predictions.


- In the [Michelson-Morley experiment](../../../../michelson-morley-experiment.md), light follows perpendicular paths in an [interferometer](../../../../interferometer.md). Rotating the apparatus would change their round-trip travel-time difference in a classical stationary-aether model. The expected orientation-dependent [interference fringe](../../../../interference-fringe.md) shift was absent at the predicted scale, consistent with isotropy of the round-trip [speed of light](../../../../speed-of-light.md) in [special relativity](../../../../special-relativity-split.md). This experiment alone does not distinguish special relativity from every alternative theory.[1887 report, apparatus and predicted versus observed orientation-dependent fringe shifts](../../../../on-the-relative-motion-of-the-earth-and-the-luminiferous-ether-by-albert-abraham-michelson-and-edward-w-morley.md)
- The [Fizeau experiment](../../../../fizeau-experiment.md) compares light propagation with and against flowing [water](../../../../water.md) using an [interference fringe](../../../../interference-fringe.md) displacement. Neglecting [optical dispersion](../../../../dispersion-optics.md), the [velocity-addition formula](../../../../velocity-addition-formula.md) predicts


$$
v_{\rm lab}=\frac{c/n+u}{1+u/(nc)}
\simeq\frac cn+u\left(1-\frac1{n^2}\right).
$$

The observed partial-drag coefficient agrees with this first-order prediction rather than simple Galilean addition of $u$. It tests a different consequence of the [Lorentz transformation](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) from a particle-lifetime measurement.[1851 report, counter-propagating beams, reversing water flow and resulting fringe displacements](../../../../the-hypotheses-relating-to-the-luminous-aether-by-hippolyte-fizeau.md)
- A controlled [muon storage-ring time-dilation experiment](../../../../muon-storage-ring-time-dilation-experiment.md) measured circulating [muons](../../../../muon.md) at $\gamma=29.33$. Its measured [mean lifetimes](../../../../mean-lifetime-physics.md) were about $64.4\,\mathrm{\mu s}$, compared with about $2.2\,\mathrm{\mu s}$ at rest, consistent with $\tau_{\rm lab}=\gamma\tau_0$. This tests [time dilation](../../../../time-dilation.md) with accelerator-selected particles.[1977 abstract, Lorentz factor and positive- and negative-muon lifetimes](../../../../measurements-of-relativistic-time-dilatation-for-positive-and-negative-muons-in-a-circular-orbit-by-j-bailey-and-others.md)

**Atmospheric muons and the two frame descriptions**. Atmospheric [cosmic rays](../../../../cosmic-ray.md) create unstable particles whose decay chains produce [muons](../../../../muon.md). A stopped-muon experiment measures the time between a stopping signal and the subsequent decay electron, obtaining the proper [mean lifetime](../../../../mean-lifetime-physics.md) $\tau_0\simeq2.2\,\mathrm{\mu s}$. The associated light-travel distance is only $c\tau_0\simeq660\,\mathrm m$, yet substantial numbers of energetic atmospheric [muons](../../../../muon.md) reach detectors after travelling several kilometres.

For an ideal selected cohort produced height $h$ above a detector and moving downward at constant [speed](../../../../speed.md) $v$, the flight time in the terrestrial frame is $h/v$. [Time dilation](../../../../time-dilation.md) predicts a laboratory [mean lifetime](../../../../mean-lifetime-physics.md) $\gamma\tau_0$, hence survival fraction

$$
\boxed{\frac{N(h\to0)}{N(h)}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{h}{\gamma v\tau_0}\right),\qquad
\gamma=\frac1{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}.}
$$

Without [time dilation](../../../../time-dilation.md), the predicted exponent would be $-h/(v\tau_0)$ and the surviving population much smaller. Altitude comparisons constitute a [cosmic-ray muon time-dilation experiment](../../../../cosmic-ray-muon-time-dilation-experiment.md): select comparable [momenta](../../../../momentum-split.md) or [energies](../../../../energy-split.md) and account for detector acceptance, atmospheric energy loss and backgrounds before interpreting the count ratio.

For illustration, not as a claimed measurement, take $h=10\,\mathrm{km}$ and $v=0.998c$. Then $\gamma=15.82$, the laboratory flight time is $33.4\,\mathrm{\mu s}$, and the predicted surviving fraction is about $0.38$. Omitting [time dilation](../../../../time-dilation.md) would give only about $2.5\times10^{-7}$ on the same idealized constant-speed model.

In the [muon](../../../../muon.md) [rest frame](../../../../rest-frame.md), its decay still follows $e^{-\Delta\tau/\tau_0}$. The atmosphere and ground move upward. Their separation, measured simultaneously in that frame, is shortened by [length contraction](../../../../length-contraction.md):

$$
h'=\frac h\gamma,\qquad
\Delta\tau=\frac{h'}v=\frac{h}{\gamma v}.
$$

The same survival fraction follows. The terrestrial description uses a longer-lived moving particle; the muon-frame description uses a shorter atmospheric flight path. They describe the same production and detection events. This consistency illustrates both [time dilation](../../../../time-dilation.md) and [length contraction](../../../../length-contraction.md), while the survival measurement is not two independent experimental tests merely because it can be described in two frames.

**Energy-momentum invariant**. For a particle with rest [mass](../../../../mass.md) $m$,

$$
\boxed{E^2-p^2c^2=m^2c^4.}
$$

Since $E=mc^2+K$, substitution and cancellation of $m^2c^4$ give

$$
(mc^2+K)^2-p^2c^2=m^2c^4,
\qquad
\boxed{K^2+2mKc^2=p^2c^2.}
$$

This [relativistic energy-momentum relation](../../../../energy-momentum-relation.md) reduces to $K\simeq p^2/(2m)$ when $K\ll mc^2$.

**Laboratory beam energies**. If both final [muons](../../../../muon.md) are at rest in the laboratory, their total [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) is zero and their total [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is $2m_\mu c^2$. [Four-momentum conservation](../../../../four-momentum-conservation.md) therefore requires equal and opposite incoming [momenta](../../../../momentum-split.md). Because the [electron](../../../../electron.md) and [Positron](../../../../positron.md) have equal rest [masses](../../../../mass.md), they then have equal total [energies](../../../../energy-split.md):

$$
E_-=E_+=m_\mu c^2.
$$

Subtract each particle's own rest [energy](../../../../energy-split.md):

$$
\boxed{K_-=K_+=(m_\mu-m_e)c^2.}
$$

Using the usual handbook rest energies gives approximately $105.1\,\mathrm{MeV}$ for each beam. These are the ideal kinematic threshold values. Just above threshold, the pair has small, equal and opposite [momenta](../../../../momentum-split.md) in this laboratory frame.

**Positron momentum**. Apply the [relativistic energy-momentum relation](../../../../energy-momentum-relation.md) to $E_+=m_\mu c^2$ and use its stipulated negative-$x$ direction:

$$
p_x^2c^2=m_\mu^2c^4-m_e^2c^4,
\qquad
\boxed{p_x=-c\sqrt{m_\mu^2-m_e^2}.}
$$

The [electron](../../../../electron.md) has the opposite momentum, $p_{e,x}=+p_0$ with $p_0=c\sqrt{m_\mu^2-m_e^2}$.

**Boost to the electron rest frame**. The electron's [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) is $v=p_{e,x}c^2/E_-=p_0/m_\mu>0$. Thus $S'$ moves in the positive-$x$ direction, and its [Lorentz factor](../../../../lorentz-factor.md) satisfies

$$
\gamma=\frac{E_-}{m_ec^2}=\frac{m_\mu}{m_e}.
$$

The positron has the same laboratory total [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) as the electron, so $vE_+/c^2=vm_\mu=p_0$. The supplied [Lorentz transformation](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) then gives

$$
p'_x=\gamma\left(p_x-\frac{vE_+}{c^2}\right)
=\gamma(-p_0-p_0),
\qquad
\boxed{p'_x=-2\gamma c\sqrt{m_\mu^2-m_e^2}.}
$$

As a sign check, the same boost gives $p'_{e,x}=\gamma(p_0-vE_-/c^2)=0$, as required for the [electron](../../../../electron.md) [rest frame](../../../../rest-frame.md).
