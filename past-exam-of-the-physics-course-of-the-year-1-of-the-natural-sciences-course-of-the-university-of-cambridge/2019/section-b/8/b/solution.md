<h1 id="section-b/8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [probability current](../../../../../probability-current.md) of the incident wave is $j_{\rm in}=\hbar k_1|A|^2/m$, and that of the reflected wave is $j_{\rm ref}=-\hbar k_1|B|^2/m$. The [quantum reflection probability](../../../../../quantum-reflection-probability.md) is the positive ratio of reflected to incident flux magnitudes:

$$
\boxed{R=\frac{|j_{\rm ref}|}{j_{\rm in}}=\frac{|B|^2}{|A|^2}
=\left(\frac{k_1-k_2}{k_1+k_2}\right)^2.}
$$

For comparison, the transmitted [probability current](../../../../../probability-current.md) is $j_{\rm tr}=\hbar k_2|C|^2/m$, so the [transmission probability](../../../../../transmission-probability.md) is

$$
T=\frac{k_2}{k_1}\left|\frac CA\right|^2
=\frac{4k_1k_2}{(k_1+k_2)^2}.
$$

These satisfy $R+T=1$, as required by conservation of [probability current](../../../../../probability-current.md) for a real, nonabsorbing [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md). In particular, $|C/A|^2$ alone is not the transmitted flux fraction when $k_2\ne k_1$.
