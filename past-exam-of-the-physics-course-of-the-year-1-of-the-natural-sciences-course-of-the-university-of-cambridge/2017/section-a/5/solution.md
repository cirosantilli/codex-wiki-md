<h1 id="section-a/5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Use distinct symbols for the dimensions of [mass](../../../../mass.md), length, time, temperature and amount of substance. The [molar gas constant](../../../../molar-gas-constant.md) has dimensions $\mathsf M\mathsf L^2\mathsf T^{-2}\mathsf\Theta^{-1}\mathsf N^{-1}$, while the given $M$ is a [molar mass](../../../../molar-mass.md) with dimensions $\mathsf M\mathsf N^{-1}$. Matching dimensions in the proposed power law gives

$$
\alpha+\gamma=0,\qquad 2\alpha+\delta=0,\qquad -2\alpha=-1,\qquad -\alpha+\beta=0,
$$

with the amount-of-substance equation repeating $\alpha+\gamma=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac12,\quad\beta=\frac12,\quad\gamma=-\frac12,\quad\delta=-1,\qquad f\propto\frac1L\sqrt{\frac{RT}{M}}.}
$$

[Dimensional analysis](../../../../dimensional-analysis.md) fixes these exponents but not a dimensionless multiplier. For example, an open-tube mode number and the gas's heat-capacity ratio enter the actual [resonance](../../../../resonance.md) [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) without changing the dimensions.
