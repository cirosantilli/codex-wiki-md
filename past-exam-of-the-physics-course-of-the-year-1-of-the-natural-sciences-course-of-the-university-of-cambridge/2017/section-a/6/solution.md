<h1 id="section-a/6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

In the $e^{i\omega t}$ [phasor](../../../../phasor.md) convention, the total series [RL circuit](../../../../rl-circuit.md) impedance is $R+i\omega L$. The resistor voltage follows the current, so its [transfer function](../../../../transfer-function.md) relative to the applied voltage is

$$
\frac{\widetilde V_R}{\widetilde V_{\mathrm{in}}}=\frac R{R+i\omega L}.
$$

At the stated condition $R=\omega L$,

$$
\boxed{\frac{\widetilde V_R}{\widetilde V_{\mathrm{in}}}=\frac{1-i}{2}=\frac{e^{-i\pi/4}}{\sqrt2}.}
$$

Thus the resistor-voltage [amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md) is $\boxed{V_{R,0}=V_{\mathrm{in},0}/\sqrt2}$ and it **lags by $45^\circ$**. For a cosine input it is $V_R(t)=V_{\mathrm{in},0}\cos(\omega t-\pi/4)/\sqrt2$.
