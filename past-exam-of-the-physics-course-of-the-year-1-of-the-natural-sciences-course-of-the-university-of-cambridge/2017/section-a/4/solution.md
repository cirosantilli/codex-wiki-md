<h1 id="section-a/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For uniform Earth density, the [shell theorem](../../../../spherical-shell-theorem.md) says that only [mass](../../../../mass.md) within radius $r$ contributes to the [gravitational field](../../../../gravitational-field.md) there. Since enclosed [mass](../../../../mass.md) is proportional to $r^3$,

$$
g(r)=\frac{GM(r)}{r^2}=g(R)\frac rR.
$$

At depth $h$, $g_h/g_0=1-h/R$. A [simple pendulum](../../../../simple-pendulum-split.md) has small-[amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md) period $P=2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$, so the [pendulum clock below a uniform-density surface](../../../../pendulum-clock-below-a-uniform-density-surface.md) runs at the fraction $P_0/P_h=\sqrt{1-h/R}$ of its surface-calibrated rate. After a surface-clock hour, its lag is

$$
\boxed{\Delta t=3600\left[1-\sqrt{1-\frac{1000}{6.371\times10^6}}\right]\,\mathrm s\simeq0.283\,\mathrm s.}
$$

The mine clock is slower because gravity is weaker under the stated uniform-density assumption. The expansion $\Delta t\simeq3600h/(2R)$ gives the same result. This is a change in the mechanical pendulum period, not a relativistic clock effect; a realistic Earth density profile would require a different interior-gravity calculation.
