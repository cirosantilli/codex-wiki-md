<h1 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Take a representative tennis-ball [mass](../../../../mass.md) of $m\simeq0.06\,\mathrm{kg}$. The [speed](../../../../speed.md) is nonrelativistic, so the [de Broglie wavelength](../../../../de-broglie-wavelength.md) is

$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac hp\simeq\frac h{mv}=\frac{6.626\times10^{-34}}{0.06\times10}\simeq1.1\times10^{-33}\,\mathrm m.}
$$

This is an estimate because the [mass](../../../../mass.md) was not specified. The [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) is vastly smaller than any practicable aperture or obstacle separation: a [diffraction](../../../../diffraction.md) angle of order $\lambda/d$ is unresolvably small for laboratory-scale $d$. Moreover, interactions with air, thermal radiation and the apparatus destroy observable coherence between macroscopically distinct paths. [Quantum mechanics](../../../../quantum-mechanics-split.md) still applies; the small [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) and rapid [decoherence](../../../../quantum-decoherence.md) explain why ordinary experiments reveal classical trajectories rather than a measurable [interference](../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) pattern.
