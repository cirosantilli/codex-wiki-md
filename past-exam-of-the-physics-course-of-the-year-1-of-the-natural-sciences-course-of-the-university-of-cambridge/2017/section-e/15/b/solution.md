<h1 id="section-e/15/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A position-dependent [force](../../../../../force-split.md) is a [conservative force](../../../../../conservative-force.md) when its [work](../../../../../work.md) depends only on endpoints. Equivalently, its [work](../../../../../work.md) around any closed allowed path vanishes, and one can define a [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) $U$ satisfying

$$
\boxed{\mathbf F=-\boldsymbol\nabla U,\qquad W_{A\to B}=U(A)-U(B).}
$$

The potential is defined up to an additive constant. A vanishing [curl](../../../../../curl.md), $\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf F=0$, is a local test; it guarantees a global single-valued potential on a [simply connected](../../../../../simply-connected-space.md) region with suitable regularity. Holes or excluded singularities require checking the global path condition.

The [work-energy theorem](../../../../../work-energy-theorem.md) gives $dK/dt=\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf v$. For a static potential this implies $d(K+U)/dt=0$. If $U$ depends explicitly on time, then instead $d(K+U)/dt=\partial U/\partial t$ under that [force](../../../../../force-split.md) alone. Friction and other nonconservative forces add their own power and can convert [mechanical energy](../../../../../mechanical-energy.md) into heat.

Examples include the [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../gravitational-energy.md) $-GMm/r$ outside a spherical [mass](../../../../../mass.md) and the electrostatic [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $U=qV$, where $\mathbf E=-\boldsymbol\nabla V$. For a point source charge $Q$, $U=qQ/(4\pi\epsilon_0r)$, giving attraction or repulsion through the sign of $qQ$. A spring has $U=kx^2/2$ and [force](../../../../../force-split.md) $-kx$. An equilibrium has $\boldsymbol\nabla U=0$; a strict local minimum is stable against small displacements when the quadratic curvature is positive. Expanding about that minimum explains the harmonic restoring [force](../../../../../force-split.md) used in many small-oscillation models.

Magnetic [force](../../../../../force-split.md) on a moving point charge does no [work](../../../../../work.md), but it is [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md)-dependent and is not generally a position-only [force](../../../../../force-split.md) $-\boldsymbol\nabla U$. Likewise a time-varying [magnetic flux](../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) induces a circulating [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) with $\oint\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf l=-d\Phi_B/dt$, so that [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) cannot globally be represented solely by an electrostatic scalar potential. These distinctions prevent treating every field [force](../../../../../force-split.md) as conservative simply because an [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) description exists for the complete system.
