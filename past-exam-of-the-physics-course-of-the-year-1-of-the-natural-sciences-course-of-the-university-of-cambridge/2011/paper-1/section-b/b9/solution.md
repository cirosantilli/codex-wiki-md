<h1 id="section-b/b9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B9](../b9.md)

For the stated standard configuration, the [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) is

$$
\boxed{t'=\gamma(t-vx/c^2),\quad x'=\gamma(x-vt),\quad y'=y,\quad z'=z,\quad
\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}.}
$$

Differentiate along the moving object's worldline and divide $dx'$ by $dt'$ to obtain the [velocity-addition formula](../../../../../velocity-addition-formula.md):

$$
\boxed{u_x'=\frac{u_x-v}{1-u_xv/c^2}.}
$$

For a receding source, two emissions separated by proper time $1/f'$ occur $\gamma/f'$ apart in S. The second signal travels an additional distance $v\gamma/f'$, so its reception interval is $\gamma(1+v/c)/f'$. Hence the longitudinal [relativistic Doppler effect](../../../../../relativistic-doppler-effect.md) is

$$
\boxed{f=\frac{f'}{\gamma(1+v/c)}=f'\sqrt{\frac{1-v/c}{1+v/c}}.}
$$

The inverse velocity transformation for the forward throw gives

$$
\boxed{u=\frac{c/2+c/7}{1+1/14}=\frac35c.}
$$

The ball approaches the stationary receiver, so its forward light is blueshifted. The received wavelength is $400\sqrt{(1-3/5)/(1+3/5)}=\boxed{200\,\mathrm{nm}}$, in the [ultraviolet](../../../../../ultraviolet.md). **The stated monochromatic emission is outside the visible band.** Other reflected or emitted frequencies have not been specified.
