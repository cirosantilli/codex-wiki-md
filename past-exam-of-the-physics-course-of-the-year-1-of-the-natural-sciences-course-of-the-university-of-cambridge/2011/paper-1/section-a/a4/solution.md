<h1 id="section-a/a4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A4](../a4.md)

The PDF's apex angle is $30^\circ$, so each contacted face makes $\alpha=15^\circ$ with the vertical. Let N be the inward [normal force](../../../../../normal-force.md) per face. Its vertical component is downward, $-N\sin\alpha$; limiting upward [static friction](../../../../../static-friction.md) contributes $\mu N\cos\alpha$. Symmetric contacts cancel horizontal forces and torque. Incipient lift consequently requires

$$
\boxed{N_{\min}=\frac{mg}{2(\mu\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha)}\simeq3.08\,\mathrm N\quad\text{per face}.}
$$

This is the usual **normal gripping force** convention, using $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. Friction can lift the prism only if $\mu>\tan\alpha$, which is satisfied here.

If “force applied” means the magnitude of the entire finger-contact resultant, including its tangential component, the [normal versus resultant force in a frictional pinch](../../../../../normal-versus-resultant-force-in-a-frictional-pinch.md) gives

$$
\boxed{F_{\min}=N_{\min}\sqrt{1+\mu^2}=3.31\,\mathrm N\quad\text{per face}.}
$$

The optimal resultant lies at the limiting [friction angle](../../../../../friction-angle.md) $\arctan\mu$ from the inward normal. Stating which force is being measured avoids confusing these two legitimate quantities.
