<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

In the initial [centre-of-mass frame](../../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md), equal fragments acquire opposite velocities $\pm w$ by [conservation of momentum](../../../../../momentum-conservation.md). The released energy is

$$
\Delta K=2\left[\frac12\frac m2w^2\right]=\frac12mw^2.
$$

The [equal-mass fragmentation energy budget](../../../../../equal-mass-fragmentation-energy-budget.md) needs $w=500\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ merely to match the other ship, costing $1.25\,\mathrm{GJ}$. To close an existing gap requires a little more than this. Using the full reserve gives

$$
\boxed{w_{\max}=\sqrt{\frac{2(2\times10^9)}{10^4}}=632\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\qquad
v_{\rm front}=2.632\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

**The reserve is sufficient to overtake.** The rear half moves at $1.368\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, preserving momentum. The kinetic-energy increase is the same in any inertial frame because total momentum is unchanged. Subsequent capture or braking would require additional interaction details; no target mass is needed for the interception test.
