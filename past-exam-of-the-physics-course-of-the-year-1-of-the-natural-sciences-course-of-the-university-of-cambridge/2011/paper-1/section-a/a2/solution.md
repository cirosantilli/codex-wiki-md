<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

By symmetry, the two grounded plates take equal induced surface charge and the [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) has magnitude $\sigma/(2\epsilon_0)$ between either plate and the sheet. The [grounded-plate potential of a central charged sheet](../../../../../grounded-plate-potential-of-a-central-charged-sheet.md) is therefore

$$
V_{\rm sheet}=\frac{\sigma d}{4\epsilon_0}\simeq282\,\mathrm V.
$$

An [electron](../../../../../electron.md) released at zero potential gains [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) $eV_{\rm sheet}$. Thus

$$
\boxed{v=\sqrt{\frac{e\sigma d}{2\epsilon_0m_e}}\simeq9.97\times10^6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The planar infinite-sheet approximation neglects edge effects. Here $e>0$ denotes charge magnitude; the electron's negative charge makes it accelerate toward the positive sheet. Relativistic corrections are below0.1%. Numerical constants use rounded CODATA values, not a claim about the historical handbook: $\epsilon_0=8.85419\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F\,m^{-1}}$, $e=1.602176634\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ and $m_e=9.10938\times10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$.
