<h1 id="section-a/a1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A1](../a1.md)

For the [gravitational-electrostatic balance of equal charged spheres](../../../../../gravitational-electrostatic-balance-of-equal-charged-spheres.md), the [shell theorem](../../../../../spherical-shell-theorem.md) and [Coulomb's law](../../../../../coulomb-s-law.md) give $F_g=GM^2/r^2$ and $F_e=q^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0r^2)$ for well-separated spherical particles. Their separation cancels at balance. Since $M=4\pi\rho a^3/3$,

$$
\boxed{a=\left(\frac{9q^2}{64\pi^3\epsilon_0G\rho^2}\right)^{1/6}
=\left[\frac{3|q|}{4\pi\rho\sqrt{4\pi\epsilon_0G}}\right]^{1/3}.}
$$

The two charges have the same sign, so electrostatic repulsion opposes gravitational attraction. A zero charge gives no nontrivial force balance. Spherical symmetry of the external charge distribution, or the leading far-field monopole approximation, is understood.
