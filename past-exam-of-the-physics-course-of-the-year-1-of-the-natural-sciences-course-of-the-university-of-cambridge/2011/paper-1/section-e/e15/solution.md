<h1 id="section-e/e15/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E15](../e15.md)

Treat Earth as a stationary spherical source, with $\mu=GM_E$, and neglect drag and other bodies. The conserved quantities are total mechanical energy and the [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) vector, implying a fixed orbital plane and constant areal velocity. The Kepler problem additionally conserves the [Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector](../../../../../laplace-runge-lenz-vector.md). The orbiting object's linear momentum is not constant because gravity exerts a force.

For a circular orbit, $mv_c^2/r_0=\mu m/r_0^2$, so $K=\mu m/(2r_0)=-U/2$. Hence

$$
\boxed{E_0=K+U=-\frac{\mu m}{2r_0}.}
$$

At the closest approach of a noncircular orbit, the velocity is tangential and $v_0=k\sqrt{\mu/r_0}$. Its energy and specific angular momentum are

$$
\boxed{E=\frac{\mu m}{2r_0}(k^2-2)=E_0(2-k^2),\qquad
h=r_0v_0=k\sqrt{\mu r_0}.}
$$

The [pericentre-speed qualification of Kepler orbit classes](../../../../../pericentre-speed-qualification-of-kepler-orbit-classes.md) requires $k\ge1$ when $r_0$ is genuinely the closest approach. For an ellipse, conservation of h gives $v_m=v_0r_0/r_m$. Equating energies at the two turning points and writing $x=r_m/r_0$ gives

$$
k^2-2=\frac{k^2}{x^2}-\frac2x,\qquad
(x-1)[(k^2-2)x+k^2]=0.
$$

The distinct apocentre root is therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{r_m}{r_0}=\frac{k^2}{2-k^2},\qquad 1<k<\sqrt2.}
$$

For an unbound orbit, $v^2(r)=2E/m+2\mu/r$ decreases toward infinity. The [asymptotic minimum speed of an unbound Kepler orbit](../../../../../asymptotic-minimum-speed-of-an-unbound-kepler-orbit.md) is

$$
\boxed{v_\infty=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_0}(k^2-2)}.}
$$

It is the limiting infimum, reached only asymptotically: positive for a hyperbola and zero for a parabola. The turning-point interpretation of $r_0$ is essential; if $0<k<1$, the same instantaneous energy describes a bound orbit whose $r_0$ is the apocentre instead.
