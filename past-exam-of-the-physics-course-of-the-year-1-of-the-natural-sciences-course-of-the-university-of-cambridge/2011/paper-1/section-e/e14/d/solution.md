<h1 id="section-e/e14/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A [conservative force](../../../../../../conservative-force.md) has work depending only on the endpoints, so it can be written

$$
\boxed{\mathbf F=-\nabla U,\qquad W_{A\to B}=U(A)-U(B),\qquad
\oint\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=0.}
$$

The arbitrary additive constant in [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) has no effect on force. A smooth curl-free force is conservative on a simply connected domain; a hole or excluded singularity requires checking global circulation as well.

Uniform gravity has $U=mgz$, central gravity has $U=-GMm/r$ with zero at infinity, and a linear spring has $U=kx^2/2$ relative to equilibrium. If the potential is time independent and no other force does work, $K+U$ is conserved by the [work-energy theorem](../../../../../../work-energy-theorem.md). A time-dependent potential or nonconservative work can change mechanical energy; having an instantaneous gradient force alone is not sufficient for energy conservation.
