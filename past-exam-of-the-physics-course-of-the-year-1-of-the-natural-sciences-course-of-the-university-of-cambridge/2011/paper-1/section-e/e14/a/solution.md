<h1 id="section-e/e14/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Gauss's law](../../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) states

$$
\boxed{\oint_{\partial V}\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf A=\frac{Q_{\rm enc}}{\epsilon_0},\qquad
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\rho_e/\epsilon_0.}
$$

The surface is closed and oriented outward, and $Q_{\rm enc}$ includes all enclosed charge. The integral law follows from the local form through the [divergence theorem](../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md). It is universally valid in classical electromagnetism, but calculating the field directly from the flux requires enough symmetry.

For spherical charge symmetry, $E(r)=Q(<r)/(4\pi\epsilon_0r^2)$. A uniform infinite line gives $E=\lambda/(2\pi\epsilon_0r)$ from a coaxial cylinder. An infinite sheet gives $E=\sigma/(2\epsilon_0)$ on either side from a pillbox, with the direction set by charge sign. The normal field jump is $\sigma/\epsilon_0$; the electrostatic field inside a conductor is zero. Zero enclosed charge implies zero net flux, not generally zero field.
