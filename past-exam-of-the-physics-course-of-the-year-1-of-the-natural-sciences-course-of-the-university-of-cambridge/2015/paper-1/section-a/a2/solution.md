<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

For a [circular orbit](../../../../../circular-orbit.md), $v^2/r=GM/r^2$. The relation for [circular orbital speed from surface gravity](../../../../../circular-orbital-speed-from-surface-gravity.md) eliminates the planet's [gravitational parameter](../../../../../standard-gravitational-parameter.md): $GM=gR^2$. The orbital radius is $r=R+h=7.4\times10^6$ m, so

$$
\boxed{v=\sqrt{\frac{gR^2}{R+h}}=\sqrt{\frac{10(6.4\times10^6)^2}{7.4\times10^6}}\simeq7.44\times10^3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

This is about **7.4 km/s**, under the circular-orbit and spherical-Earth approximation. Using surface $g$ unchanged at orbital height would overestimate the [centripetal acceleration](../../../../../centripetal-acceleration.md); its role here is to determine $GM$.
