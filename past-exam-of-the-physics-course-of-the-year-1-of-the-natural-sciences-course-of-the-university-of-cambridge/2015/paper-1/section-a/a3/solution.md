<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

In the stipulated constant-speed model, the Earth-frame [speed](../../../../../speed.md) is $v=D/t=3.844\times10^8/(76\times3600)\simeq1.405\times10^3$ m/s. The Earth and Moon are stationary endpoints in this model, so their Earth-frame separation $D$ is the [proper length](../../../../../proper-length.md). The spacecraft-frame separation, measured simultaneously in that frame, is $D'=D/\gamma=D\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$. Thus

$$
D-D'=\frac{D(v/c)^2}{1+\sqrt{1-(v/c)^2}}\simeq\frac{Dv^2}{2c^2}\simeq4.22\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm m.
$$

**The spacecraft-frame [distance](../../../../../distance.md) is shorter by about 4.2 mm.** This compares simultaneous endpoint measurements in two frames, not the coordinate difference between a launch event and an arrival event. The [small-speed length contraction estimate](../../../../../small-speed-length-contraction-estimate.md) also avoids numerical cancellation when evaluating the tiny contraction.
