<h1 id="section-e/e15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An inverse-square point-source field has constant flux through enclosing spheres because their [area](../../../../../../surface-area.md) grows as $r^2$. In [electrostatics](../../../../../../electrostatics-split.md), [Gauss's law](../../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) is $\oint_S\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf S=Q_{\rm enc}/\varepsilon_0$, or $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\rho_e/\varepsilon_0$. For a spherical charge distribution, [rotational symmetry](../../../../../../rotational-symmetry.md) makes the exterior field radial and constant in magnitude on a concentric sphere, giving $\mathbf E=Q\hat r/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2)$. Symmetry is needed to take the field outside the [surface integral](../../../../../../surface-integral.md); [Gauss's law](../../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) alone does not make an arbitrary charge distribution spherical.

For [gravity](../../../../../../gravity.md) the corresponding relations are $\oint_S\mathbf g\cdot d\mathbf S=-4\pi GM_{\rm enc}$ and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf g=-4\pi G\rho_m$. A spherical [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) has exterior field $\mathbf g=-GM\hat r/r^2$. Inside a uniform sphere, $M_{\rm enc}(r)=4\pi\rho_mr^3/3$, so $\boxed{\mathbf g=-(4\pi G\rho_m/3)r\hat r}$. Inside an empty spherical shell the enclosed [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) is zero and symmetry gives zero field. These examples distinguish an inverse-square exterior field from a field inside distributed matter.

The law also treats other symmetries, but their field-distance dependence differs: an infinitely long [line charge](../../../../../../line-charge.md) has cylindrical rather than spherical spreading and a $1/r$ field. Flux conservation and geometry must both be used, rather than assuming every [Gaussian surface](../../../../../../gaussian-surface.md) leads to an inverse-square answer.
