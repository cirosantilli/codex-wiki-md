<h1 id="section-e/e15/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

In SI units, with microscopic [charge density](../../../../../../charge-density.md) $\rho_e$ and [current density](../../../../../../current-density.md) $\mathbf J$, [Maxwell's equations](../../../../../../maxwell-equations-split.md) can be written

$$
\boxed{\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\frac{\rho_e}{\varepsilon_0},\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,\quad\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t},\quad\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf J+\mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t}.}
$$

The first relates [electric flux](../../../../../../electric-flux.md) to charge. The second states that the net [magnetic flux](../../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) through a closed surface is zero. [Faraday's law](../../../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) relates electric [circulation](../../../../../../circulation-physics.md) to changing [magnetic flux](../../../../../../magnetic-flux.md). The [Ampère-Maxwell equation](../../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) includes both conduction current and [displacement current](../../../../../../displacement-current.md); the latter is essential to time-dependent fields and local [charge conservation](../../../../../../charge-conservation.md).

For fixed contours and spanning surfaces, the [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md) [curl](../../../../../../curl.md) equations are $\oint\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf l=-d\Phi_B/dt$ and $\oint\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf l=\mu_0I_{\rm enc}+\mu_0\varepsilon_0d\Phi_E/dt$. For a moving material contour the first must include the motional [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) term; its total emf is $\oint(\mathbf E+\mathbf v_{\rm loop}\times\mathbf B)\cdot d\mathbf l$. This prevents confusing a changing circuit [area](../../../../../../surface-area.md) with a partial time [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) of a static [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md).

Taking the [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md) of the [Ampère-Maxwell equation](../../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) and using electric [Gauss's law](../../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) gives $\partial_t\rho_e+\nabla\cdot\mathbf J=0$. In a source-free [vacuum](../../../../../../vacuum.md), combining the two [curl](../../../../../../curl.md) equations gives $\nabla^2\mathbf E-\mu_0\varepsilon_0\partial_t^2\mathbf E=0$, and similarly for $\mathbf B$, predicting [electromagnetic waves](../../../../../../electromagnetic-wave-split.md) of [speed](../../../../../../speed.md) $c=1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}$. The [Lorentz force](../../../../../../lorentz-force.md) law then specifies how these fields act on a charge. Macroscopic matter descriptions may instead use $\mathbf D$ and $\mathbf H$ with free sources and constitutive relations; those should not be silently mixed with the microscopic-source equations above.
