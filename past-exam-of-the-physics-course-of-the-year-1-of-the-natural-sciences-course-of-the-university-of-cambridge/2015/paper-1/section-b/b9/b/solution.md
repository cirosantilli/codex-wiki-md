<h1 id="section-b/b9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Boost along $+x$ at $0.6c$ into the [centre-of-momentum frame](../../../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md). The composite is stationary. The incoming particle has $p'_x=1.25(2.4-0.6\times3)mc=0.75mc$, while the target has $p'_x=-0.75mc$. After decay the two [photons](../../../../../../photon.md) have energies $Mc^2/2=1.6mc^2$ and opposite momenta.

Equal laboratory [photon](../../../../../../photon.md) energies require the rest-frame [photons](../../../../../../photon.md) to have zero [momentum](../../../../../../momentum-split.md) component along the boost: $E_{\rm lab}=\gamma(E'+Vp'_x)$ must be equal for two opposite [photons](../../../../../../photon.md). Their directions are therefore **$+y$ and $-y$ in the centre-of-momentum diagram**, perpendicular to the incoming collision axis. Boosting those transverse [photons](../../../../../../photon.md) gives exactly the laboratory momenta in part (a). A stationary composite has no direction-of-travel arrow in this frame.
