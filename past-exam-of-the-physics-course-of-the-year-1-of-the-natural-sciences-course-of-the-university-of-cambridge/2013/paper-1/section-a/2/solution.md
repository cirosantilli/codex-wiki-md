<h1 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

For equal mean [mass densities](../../../../../density.md), $M_a/M_E=(r/R_E)^3$. Thus the asteroid's surface gravitational [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) is $g_a=g_Er/R_E$ and its [escape speed](../../../../../escape-velocity.md) is

$$
v_{\rm esc}^2=\frac{2GM_a}{r}=\frac{2g_Er^2}{R_E},\qquad
\boxed{r_{\max}=v_J\sqrt{\frac{R_E}{2g_E}}.}
$$

An explicit estimate of jumping ability is an [Earth](../../../../../earth.md) jump height $H=0.50\,\mathrm m$, giving $v_J=\sqrt{2g_EH}\simeq3.1\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{r_{\max}=\sqrt{R_EH}\simeq1.8\,\mathrm{km}.}
$$

This is an order-of-magnitude estimate, scaling with assumed take-off [speed](../../../../../speed.md); no unique human jump [speed](../../../../../speed.md) is supplied. A spherical nonrotating asteroid and negligible atmosphere are assumed.
