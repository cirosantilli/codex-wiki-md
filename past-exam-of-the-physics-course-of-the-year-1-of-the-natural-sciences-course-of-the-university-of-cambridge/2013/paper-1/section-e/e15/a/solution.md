<h1 id="section-e/e15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [gravitational field](../../../../../../gravitational-field.md) is [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) per unit test [mass](../../../../../../mass.md), $\mathbf g=\mathbf F/m$. [Newton's law of universal gravitation](../../../../../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation-split.md) gives the field of a [point mass](../../../../../../point-mass.md) M:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf g=-\frac{GM}{r^2}\widehat{\mathbf r},\qquad
\Phi=-\frac{GM}{r},\qquad\mathbf g=-\nabla\Phi.}
$$

The potential is [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) per unit [mass](../../../../../../mass.md), with zero at infinity for an isolated [mass](../../../../../../mass.md). Fields and potentials superpose. The potential difference fixes the work per unit [mass](../../../../../../mass.md), and a conservative [gravitational field](../../../../../../gravitational-field.md) has zero circulation around a closed path.

For a continuous [mass distribution](../../../../../../mass-distribution.md), the gravitational Gauss law is $\oint\mathbf g\cdot d\mathbf S=-4\pi GM_{\rm enc}$. A spherical body therefore has the same exterior field as a [point mass](../../../../../../point-mass.md) at its centre. Inside a uniform sphere of [density](../../../../../../density.md) $\rho$,

$$
\mathbf g=-\frac{4\pi G\rho}{3}\mathbf r.
$$

The field is continuous at its surface and vanishes at its centre. An empty spherical shell has zero interior field but a nonzero constant interior potential. Near a small region of [Earth](../../../../../../earth.md)'s surface one may approximate $\mathbf g$ as uniform and the potential change as $g\Delta h$. Farther away its spatial [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) produces tidal relative [accelerations](../../../../../../acceleration.md). Escape to infinity from radius R requires [speed](../../../../../../speed.md) $\sqrt{2GM/R}$ when rotation and other bodies are neglected.
