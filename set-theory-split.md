# Set theory

↑ **Parent:** [Foundations of mathematics](foundations-of-mathematics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_theory)

Set theory studies [sets](set-split.md), their [set elements](element-of-a-set.md), and the constructions and sizes possible for them. It supplies a shared vocabulary for [mathematics](mathematics-split.md): a [function](function-split.md) connects two [sets](set-split.md), a [binary relation](binary-relation.md) specifies which pairs of objects are related, and a [probability space](probability-space-split.md) starts with a [set](set-split.md) of possible outcomes. Giving these objects a common language lets a construction from one subject be used in another without repeating its underlying bookkeeping.

A particularly beautiful question is how to compare sizes without first assigning a number. Two [sets](set-split.md) have the same [cardinality](cardinality.md) when a [bijection](bijection.md) pairs every member of one with exactly one member of the other. For finite [sets](set-split.md), this agrees with ordinary counting. For infinite [sets](set-split.md), it produces surprises: $n\mapsto2n$ bijects the [natural numbers](natural-number.md) with the nonnegative [even integers](even-number.md), and the [Cantor pairing function](cantor-pairing-function.md) bijects the [Cartesian product](cartesian-product.md) $\mathbb N^2$ with $\mathbb N$. A proper [subset](subset.md) can have the same [cardinality](cardinality.md) as its containing [set](set-split.md).

This does not make all infinite [sets](set-split.md) equally large. [Cantor's diagonal argument](cantor-s-diagonal-argument.md) shows that all infinite [binary sequences](bitstream.md) cannot be listed by the [natural numbers](natural-number.md). [Cantor's theorem](cantor-s-theorem.md) gives the wider principle: the [power set](power-set.md) of any [set](set-split.md) has strictly greater [cardinality](cardinality.md). The contrast between an explicit enumeration and an impossibility argument makes the meaning of infinity much sharper than a picture of something merely continuing forever.

Set formation itself requires care. [Russell's paradox](russell-s-paradox.md) shows why not every condition defines a [set](set-split.md) of all objects satisfying it. [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory.md) instead uses [axioms](axiom.md) specifying permitted constructions, including [set comprehension](restricted-set-comprehension.md) within a previously given [set](set-split.md). The restrictions are useful: they let us form the collections ordinary arguments need while blocking the unrestricted construction that produces the paradox.
