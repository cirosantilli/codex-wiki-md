# Martingale proof of the integral representation of a Lipschitz function

↑ **Parent:** [Dyadic slope martingale](dyadic-slope-martingale.md)

For an $L$-[Lipschitz function](lipschitz-continuity.md) $f$ on $[0,1]$, its slopes on the [dyadic partition](dyadic-partition.md), evaluated at a [uniform distribution](continuous-uniform-distribution.md) input, form a bounded [martingale](martingale-split.md) for the [dyadic filtration](dyadic-filtration.md). The [uniformly integrable martingale convergence theorem](uniformly-integrable-martingale-convergence-theorem.md) yields a bounded terminal [random variable](random-variable-split.md) $g(X)$, with $g$ chosen as a [Borel measurable function](borel-measurable-function.md) by the [Doob-Dynkin lemma](doob-dynkin-lemma.md). The terminal [conditional expectation](conditional-expectation.md) identity says that each slope is the average of $g$ over the corresponding [dyadic interval](dyadic-interval.md). Integrating over adjacent [dyadic intervals](dyadic-interval.md) and taking a [telescoping sum](telescoping-sum.md) proves the displayed representation first at dyadic endpoints. The [Lipschitz condition](lipschitz-continuity.md) for $f$ and the bound $|g|\leq L$ extend it to every endpoint by [continuity](continuous-function.md). This proves [absolute continuity of a function](absolutely-continuous-function.md) without presupposing a [derivative](derivative.md).
