# Net ionic equation

↑ **Parent:** [Precipitation (chemistry)](precipitation-chemistry.md)

A [net ionic equation](net-ionic-equation.md) retains the [chemical species](chemical-species.md) changed by a [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md) and cancels [spectator ions](spectator-ion.md) present unchanged on both sides. Dissolved [strong electrolytes](strong-electrolyte.md) are represented by their separated [ions](ion.md), while a [solid](solid.md) product retains its [chemical formula](chemical-formula.md). Cancellation preserves both elemental inventories and [electric charge conservation](charge-conservation.md).[Chemistry 2e, §4.2, Precipitation Reactions and Solubility Rules: molecular and net ionic equations for silver chloride](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others.md)

For the [calcium carbonate](calcium-carbonate.md) example, the complete ionic account is

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}(aq)+2\mathrm{Cl}^-(aq)+2\mathrm{Na}^+(aq)+\mathrm{CO}_3^{2-}(aq)\\
&\qquad\longrightarrow\mathrm{CaCO}_3(s)+2\mathrm{Na}^+(aq)+2\mathrm{Cl}^-(aq).
\end{aligned}
$$

Canceling the [sodium](sodium.md) [cations](cation.md) and [chloride](chloride.md) [anions](anion.md) yields the [net ionic equation](net-ionic-equation.md) above. Neither cancellation nor the conventional [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md) formula implies that the remaining dissolved [ions](ion.md) have become paired [molecules](molecule-split.md).

**[Figure 1](#image-precipitation-species-and-phase-balance). Precipitation transfers selected ions into a solid while conserving inventories**. Original one-to-one model: four A [cations](cation.md) and four B [anions](anion.md) initially dissolved; three of each enter AB [solid](solid.md), leaving one of each dissolved. Four X [anions](anion.md) and four Y [cations](cation.md) are [spectator ions](spectator-ion.md) throughout. Counts are illustrative, not measured, and do not represent the [calcium carbonate](calcium-carbonate.md) experiment or determine a [solubility product](solubility-product.md).

For this schematic, let $\xi$ be the [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) of AB formed. With no competing [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction-split.md) or losses,

$$
n_{\mathrm A,aq}=n_{\mathrm A,0}-\xi,\qquad n_{\mathrm B,aq}=n_{\mathrm B,0}-\xi,\qquad n_{\mathrm{AB},s}=\xi,
\qquad 0\le\xi\le\min(n_{\mathrm A,0},n_{\mathrm B,0}).
$$

Adding each dissolved inventory to its contribution in the [solid](solid.md) recovers its initial total. Each dissolved pair has net [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) zero, so transferring a pair into neutral AB also preserves [electric charge conservation](charge-conservation.md). This is a short [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md) argument; choosing the actual $\xi$ additionally needs [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md) or [chemical kinetics](chemical-kinetics-split.md) information.
