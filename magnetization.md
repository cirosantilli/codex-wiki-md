# Magnetization

↑ **Parent:** [Bound charge and bound current](bound-charge-and-bound-current.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetization)

[Magnetization](magnetization.md) is [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) per unit [volume](volume.md), described by a [vector field](vector-field.md) $\mathbf M(\mathbf r)$. Average the microscopic [magnetic dipole moments](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) in a small region of [volume](volume.md) $\Delta V$ around the [point](point-geometry.md) $\mathbf r$:

$$
\mathbf M(\mathbf r)\simeq\frac{1}{\Delta V}\sum_{i\in\Delta V}\mathbf m_i,
\qquad
\mathbf m_{\rm total}=\int_V\mathbf M\,dV.
$$

The averaging region should contain many microscopic constituents while being small compared with the distance over which the macroscopic [magnetization](magnetization.md) varies. With [number density](number-density.md) $n$ of identical constituents, this becomes $\mathbf M=n\langle\mathbf m\rangle$. [Magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) has [SI](international-system-of-units.md) units $\mathrm{A\,m^2}$, so [magnetization](magnetization.md) has units $\mathrm{A/m}$: [amperes](ampere.md) per metre. The average is a [vector](vector.md) sum, not a sum of magnitudes.[Classical Electromagnetism: An intermediate level course by Richard Fitzpatrick; Magnetization, equations (856)–(857) and the surrounding averaging definition](classical-electromagnetism-an-intermediate-level-course-by-richard-fitzpatrick/magnetization.md)

Alignment can change [magnetization](magnetization.md) without changing the magnitude of any individual [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md). In a constructed counting example, eight equal [magnetic dipole moments](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) of magnitude $m_0$ occupy the same [volume](volume.md) $\Delta V$. Six along $+z$ and two along $-z$ give $\mathbf M=4m_0\widehat{\mathbf z}/\Delta V$; turning the two opposing [magnetic dipole moments](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) to $+z$ doubles [magnetization](magnetization.md) to $8m_0\widehat{\mathbf z}/\Delta V$. Both arrangements have the same sum of magnitudes, $8m_0$. Changing the individual [magnetic dipole moments](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) provides another possible response; their alignment and their magnitudes are distinct ingredients. Microscopic [spin](spin.md) contributes [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) without being a literal rotating charged ball.

Original eight-[magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) counting model, with equal arrow lengths and equal averaging [volumes](volume.md). Reorienting two arrows doubles the net [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) without enlarging any individual [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md). The small count illustrates the sum; a macroscopic averaging region contains many more constituents.

Distinguish [magnetization](magnetization.md) $\mathbf M$, [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) $\mathbf B$, and [magnetic field intensity](magnetic-field-intensity.md) $\mathbf H$. In [SI](international-system-of-units.md),

$$
\mathbf B=\mu_0(\mathbf H+\mathbf M),\qquad
[\mathbf M]=[\mathbf H]=\mathrm{A/m},\qquad [\mathbf B]=\mathrm T.
$$

Here $\mu_0$ is the [vacuum permeability](vacuum-permeability.md), and $\mathrm T$ denotes the [tesla](tesla-unit.md). [Magnetization](magnetization.md) describes matter's [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) density; [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) enters the [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md); [magnetic field intensity](magnetic-field-intensity.md) separates the free [electric current](electric-current.md) contribution in the macroscopic equations. In particular, zero applied [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) does not generally imply $\mathbf H=0$ inside a finite magnet: its [demagnetizing field](demagnetizing-field.md) depends on shape.[Classical Electromagnetism: An intermediate level course by Richard Fitzpatrick; Magnetization, equations (860)–(864): current decomposition and definition of H](classical-electromagnetism-an-intermediate-level-course-by-richard-fitzpatrick/magnetization.md)

For stationary [electric polarization](polarization-density.md), [magnetization](magnetization.md) produces [bound current](bound-current.md) in the [volume](volume.md) and on its bounding [surface](topological-surface-split.md):

$$
\mathbf J_{\rm b}=\nabla\times\mathbf M,
\qquad
\mathbf K_{\rm b}=\mathbf M\times\mathbf n.
$$

The [curl](curl.md) gives a [current density](current-density.md) in $\mathrm{A/m^2}$; $\mathbf K_{\rm b}$ is a [surface current density](surface-current-density.md) in $\mathrm{A/m}$. The [unit normal](unit-normal.md) $\mathbf n$ points outward from the magnetized material into a region with zero [magnetization](magnetization.md). Across an interface between two magnetized media, the net bound [current sheet](current-sheet.md) is

$$
\mathbf K_{\rm interface}=(\mathbf M_1-\mathbf M_2)\times\mathbf n,
$$

where the [unit normal](unit-normal.md) points from medium 1 to medium 2. Both faces contribute: medium 2 has the opposite outward [normal vector](normal-vector.md). This fixes the [cross product](cross-product.md) sign. If [electric polarization](polarization-density.md) changes with time, add its contribution $\partial_t\mathbf P$ to the [bound current](bound-current.md).[Classical Electromagnetism by Richard Fitzpatrick; Permanent Ferromagnets, equation (716): volume and outward-surface magnetization currents](classical-electromagnetism-by-richard-fitzpatrick/permanent-ferromagnets.md)

A short proof outline explains why both contributions are needed. Write $\mathbf M_{\rm ext}=\mathbf M\,\chi_V$, where $\chi_V$ is the [indicator function](indicator-function.md) of the material's [volume](volume.md). In the sense of [distributions](distribution-mathematical-analysis.md), its [gradient](gradient.md) is $\nabla\chi_V=-\mathbf n\delta_S$, with $\delta_S$ the [surface delta distribution](surface-delta-distribution.md). The [product rule](product-rule.md) for the [curl](curl.md) gives

$$
\nabla\times(\chi_V\mathbf M)
=\chi_V\nabla\times\mathbf M+(\mathbf M\times\mathbf n)\delta_S.
$$

Thus a constant interior [magnetization](magnetization.md) can have zero interior [curl](curl.md) and still produce a nonzero boundary [electric current](electric-current.md). Equivalently, neighboring ideal microscopic [current loops](current-loop.md) cancel along shared edges while uncompensated edges remain on the outside. This [bound current](bound-current.md) representation predicts the same macroscopic [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) as the [magnetization](magnetization.md) distribution; it does not require a wire or free charge transport around the magnet. Its usefulness is that the familiar [Biot-Savart law](biot-savart-law.md) can now calculate the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) from a specified [magnetization](magnetization.md).

A time-dependent average [magnetization](magnetization.md) need not vary smoothly under a smooth drive. The [Barkhausen effect](barkhausen-effect.md) resolves collective jumps through their induced [electromotive force](electromotive-force.md); [Barkhausen pulse area](barkhausen-pulse-area.md) explains how that derivative readout relates to the underlying change.
