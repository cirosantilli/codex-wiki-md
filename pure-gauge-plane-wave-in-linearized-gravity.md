# Pure-gauge plane wave in linearized gravity

↑ **Parent:** [Residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity](residual-gauge-symmetry-of-linearized-gravity.md)

Under the convention $\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\xi_\nu+\partial_\nu\xi_\mu$, choose $\xi^\mu=b^\mu\sin(k\cdot x)$ with constant $b^\mu$ and null $k^\mu$. The [trace-reversed metric perturbation](trace-reversed-metric-perturbation.md) has amplitude

$$
\epsilon_{\rm gauge}^{\mu\nu}=k^\mu b^\nu+k^\nu b^\mu-\eta^{\mu\nu}(k\cdot b).
$$

It satisfies $k_\mu\epsilon_{\rm gauge}^{\mu\nu}=0$ and $\Box\xi^\mu=0$, so it is an allowed [residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity](residual-gauge-symmetry-of-linearized-gravity.md). Its [linearized Riemann curvature operator](linearized-riemann-curvature-operator.md) is zero: the four wavevector factors in each second-derivative term cancel in the antisymmetric curvature combinations. Nonzero transverse-traceless curvature distinguishes the two physical [gravitational wave polarizations](gravitational-wave-polarization.md) from these coordinate modes.[Section 5.3.1, equations 5.232–5.234: residual plane-wave gauge transformation and transverse-traceless reduction](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gr/grhtml/S5.html)
