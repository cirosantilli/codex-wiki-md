# Logarithmic parameter integral with Cauchy weight

↑ **Parent:** [Differentiation under the integral sign](differentiation-under-the-integral-sign.md)

For real $\alpha$, the displayed [improper integral](improper-integral.md) converges and is an even [function](function-split.md) of $\alpha$. For $\alpha>0$, [differentiation under the integral sign](differentiation-under-the-integral-sign.md) and [partial fraction decomposition](partial-fraction-decomposition.md) give

$$
E'(\alpha)=\int_0^\infty\frac{2\alpha x^2}{(1+\alpha^2x^2)(1+x^2)}\,dx=\frac\pi{1+\alpha}.
$$

For $\alpha\ne1$, use $x^2/[(1+\alpha^2x^2)(1+x^2)]=[1/(1+x^2)-1/(1+\alpha^2x^2)]/(\alpha^2-1)$. Integrate the resulting rational functions and extend to $\alpha=1$ by continuity. Integrating in $\alpha$ and using $E(0)=0$ gives $E(\alpha)=\pi\ln(1+\alpha)$ for $\alpha\ge0$; evenness gives the formula for negative parameters. Thus $E'(\alpha)=\pi\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha)/(1+|\alpha|)$ away from zero. At zero the one-sided [derivatives](derivative.md) are opposite, so $E$ is continuous but not differentiable. Substituting $\alpha=0$ into the pointwise differentiated integrand would therefore be an invalid interchange.
