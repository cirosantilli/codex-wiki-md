# Rate constant

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical kinetics](chemical-kinetics.md)

A [rate constant](rate-constant.md) is the coefficient in a specified [rate law](rate-law.md), held fixed under specified conditions. It can describe an empirical overall [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md), as well as an [elementary reaction](elementary-reaction.md). Fixed [temperature](temperature.md) matters: changing [temperature](temperature.md) can change $k$, even for the same [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md). See [IUPAC Gold Book: order of reaction and rate constants](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/O04322) and [IUPAC Gold Book: rate coefficient](https://old.goldbook.iupac.org/html/R/R05137.html).

For $v=k[\mathrm A]^n$, dimensional consistency requires

$$
\operatorname{units}(k)=(\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}})^{1-n}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}.
$$

A [zero-order reaction](zero-order-reaction.md) therefore uses $\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$; a [first-order reaction](first-order-reaction.md) uses $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$; a [second-order reaction](second-order-reaction.md) uses $\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$. Comparing bare numerical values of $k$ across different [orders of reaction](order-of-reaction.md) is meaningless without their units and [concentrations](concentration.md). Even for the same [order of reaction](order-of-reaction.md), state whether $k$ multiplies the normalized [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) $v$ or a particular [reactant](reactant.md) disappearance rate: a stoichiometric coefficient can change that convention.
