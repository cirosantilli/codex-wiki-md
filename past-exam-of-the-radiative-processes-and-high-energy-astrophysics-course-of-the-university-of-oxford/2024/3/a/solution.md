<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Thermal bremsstrahlung](../../../../thermal-bremsstrahlung.md) is radiation from the acceleration of [electrons](../../../../electron.md) in the [Coulomb force](../../../../coulomb-force.md) of ions. At fixed [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), the encounter rate per [electron](../../../../electron.md) is proportional to ion [number density](../../../../number-density.md) $n_i$, and the number of radiating [electrons](../../../../electron.md) per unit [volume](../../../../volume.md) is $n_e$. Therefore $j_\nu\propto n_en_i$ in a dilute, [optically thin](../../../../optically-thin-medium.md) [plasma](../../../../plasma-physics.md). This is a binary-encounter rate, not a one-particle emission process.

For a fixed encounter geometry, the ionic [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) and hence the [electron](../../../../electron.md) acceleration are proportional to ionic charge $Ze$. The [Larmor formula](../../../../larmor-formula.md) is quadratic in acceleration, giving $j_\nu\propto Z^2$ at fixed species [number densities](../../../../number-density.md). The dimensionless [Gaunt factor](../../../../gaunt-factor.md) $g(\nu,T)$ corrects the simple classical result for the detailed quantum scattering and radiation calculation, after averaging over the thermal [electron](../../../../electron.md) distribution. It depends on [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) and [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and, in a more complete notation, ionic charge.
