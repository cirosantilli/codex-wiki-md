<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Under an additional thermalization assumption, an opaque [star](../../../../star.md) emits approximately [black-body radiation](../../../../black-body-radiation.md) characterized by the [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) near the layer from which thermal [photons](../../../../photon.md) escape. [Planck's law](../../../../planck-s-law.md) gives a [Rayleigh-Jeans law](../../../../rayleigh-jeans-law.md) low-[frequency](../../../../frequency.md) tail $B_\nu\propto T\nu^2$ and a [Wien approximation](../../../../wien-approximation.md) high-[frequency](../../../../frequency.md) tail $B_\nu\propto\nu^3e^{-h\nu/(k_BT)}$.

Using the gas-dominated [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) profile in [d](../d.md) and the local escape criterion in [c](../c.md), the model's putative emission [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) for the [Sun](../../../../sun.md)'s parameters would be

$$
T_{\rm esc}\simeq T_c\frac{\Delta R_{\rm esc}}R
\simeq16.1\,\mathrm K.
$$

Using radial $\tau=1$ instead gives $22.8\,\mathrm K$. The [frequency form of Wien's displacement law](../../../../frequency-form-of-wien-s-displacement-law.md) for the maximum of $B_\nu$ is $h\nu_{\max}/(k_BT)=2.82144$, so these two prescriptions predict

$$
\boxed{\nu_{\max}\simeq9.5\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{Hz}
\quad\hbox{or}\quad1.34\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{Hz},}
$$

respectively, in the far infrared/submillimetre region. These are conditional estimates for this model, not observed solar peaks. A realistic solar [effective temperature](../../../../effective-temperature.md) near $5.8\times10^3\,\mathrm K$ gives $\nu_{\max}\simeq3.4\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}$ in $B_\nu$. The maximum of $B_\lambda$ occurs near $0.50\,\mathrm{\mu m}$; it is not obtained by simply taking $c/\nu_{\max}$, because spectral densities transform with a [Jacobian determinant](../../../../jacobian-determinant.md).

The model is unsuitable for the solar [photosphere](../../../../photosphere.md): fully ionized [hydrogen](../../../../hydrogen.md) at $16$–$23\,\mathrm K$ is inconsistent with thermal ionization, and the assumed opacity and near-surface [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) gradient do not resemble the [Sun](../../../../sun.md)'s. Even its central [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) is substantially below the actual solar value, about $1.6\times10^7\,\mathrm K$.

There is a further logical limitation: the question retains **only [Thomson scattering](../../../../thomson-scattering.md)**. In its coherent, cold-[electron](../../../../electron.md) limit, this redistributes [photon](../../../../photon.md) directions without generating a [Planck photon distribution](../../../../planck-photon-distribution.md). For a concrete counterexample, monochromatic injected radiation remains monochromatic after any number of such elastic scatterings; large [optical depth](../../../../optical-depth.md) cannot turn it into [black-body radiation](../../../../black-body-radiation.md). True absorption and emission, or a specified pre-existing thermal radiation field with appropriate energy exchange, are missing. Thus an approximately [blackbody](../../../../blackbody.md) emergent spectrum is the usual additional physical assumption, while the stated scattering-only model does not uniquely predict an emergent spectrum or its peak.
