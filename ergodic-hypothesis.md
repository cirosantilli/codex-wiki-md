# Ergodic hypothesis

↑ **Parent:** [Statistical physics](statistical-physics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic_hypothesis)

The [ergodic hypothesis](ergodic-hypothesis.md) identifies long-time averages of equilibrium observables along typical trajectories with averages over the invariant equilibrium ensemble at the same conserved quantities. In the microcanonical formulation, the ensemble is normalized phase-space measure on the energy surface. In a measure-preserving description, [ergodicity](ergodicity.md) means invariant measurable sets have measure zero or one; the [Birkhoff ergodic theorem](birkhoff-ergodic-theorem.md) then equates time averages and ensemble expectations almost everywhere for integrable observables. This explains why statistical-mechanical ensembles can predict long-time measurements. A particular mechanical model still requires its own ergodicity assumptions; integrable idealized systems need not be ergodic.
