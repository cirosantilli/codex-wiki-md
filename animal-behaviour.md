## Outcome devaluation

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Changing the current value of an outcome after instrumental training, then testing the associated action. Testing without delivering that outcome helps distinguish immediate use of changed outcome value from new direct action reinforcement. Sensitivity and insensitivity provide operational tests for goal-directed and habitual control under stated conditions.[Abstract, experiments 1–2; pp. 181–189: interval training, devaluation, extinction test and lesion controls](neuroscience-bibliography.md#lesions-of-dorsolateral-striatum-preserve-outcome-expectancy-but-disrupt-habit-formation-in-instrumental-learning-by-henry-h-yin-and-others)

### Slips-of-action test

↑ **Parent:** [Outcome devaluation](#outcome-devaluation)

A speeded test requiring responses associated with still-valued outcomes and withholding responses associated with devalued outcomes. Response errors can reveal deficient use of outcome knowledge, but also require controls for memory retention, comprehension and inhibitory control.[American Journal of Psychiatry 168, 718–726; Method, instructed outcome devaluation and slips-of-action test](psychology-bibliography.md#disruption-in-the-balance-between-goal-directed-behavior-and-habit-learning-in-obsessive-compulsive-disorder-by-claire-m-gillan-and-others)

## Scavenging

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Consumption of remains of dead organisms. It can remove, disarticulate and transport material before burial.

## Locomotion

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Movement of an organism through its environment, including walking, swimming and [saltation](#saltation).

### Saltation

↑ **Parent:** [Locomotion](#locomotion)

[locomotion](#locomotion) by jumps. Skeletal geometry can constrain jumping capability but does not establish the exclusive behaviour of an extinct organism.

## State-dependent behaviour

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Behaviour whose expression depends on properties such as condition, age, reproductive state or current social position.

## Social decision rule

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

A mapping from perceived social information and internal state to action. A stable mapping can generate flexible actions when its inputs change.

## Sleep

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

A regulated behavioural and physiological state identified through converging criteria including reversibility, responsiveness and homeostatic regulation. Immobility alone does not establish [sleep](#sleep).

### Synaptic homeostasis hypothesis

↑ **Parent:** [Sleep](#sleep)

The proposal that waking-associated strengthening is balanced by sleep-associated renormalization of overall strength of [synapses](neuron.md#synapse). Selective weakening could improve capacity and relative signal contrast while preserving important differences. It is compatible with some strengthening or reactivation, and is not the claim that sleep indiscriminately erases learning.

### Polysomnography

↑ **Parent:** [Sleep](#sleep)

Combined recording used to classify sleep and waking, commonly including [electroencephalography](neurology.md#electroencephalography), eye-movement signals and muscle activity. Classification of a sleep stage is not itself a measure of the content of a memory.

### Rapid eye movement sleep

↑ **Parent:** [Sleep](#sleep)

A sleep state identified by rapid eye movements and characteristic electrophysiological and muscle-tone patterns. Its contribution to a particular learned task must be tested rather than inferred from the existence of dreams.

### Non-rapid eye movement sleep

↑ **Parent:** [Sleep](#sleep)

A group of sleep stages distinct from [rapid eye movement sleep](#rapid-eye-movement-sleep). Deep stages feature prominent [sleep slow oscillations](#sleep-slow-oscillation); stage assignment uses [polysomnography](#polysomnography).

#### Sleep spindle

↑ **Parent:** [Non-rapid eye movement sleep](#non-rapid-eye-movement-sleep)

A transient thalamocortical oscillatory event, often around 10–16 Hz in humans, with frequency boundaries depending on the measurement convention. Its timing relative to other events can matter beyond its total count.

#### Sleep slow oscillation

↑ **Parent:** [Non-rapid eye movement sleep](#non-rapid-eye-movement-sleep)

A slow alternation in cortical population activity, conventionally below about one cycle per second. Its phase can organize other sleep events; a scalp electrical rhythm alone does not reveal a particular represented episode.

## Foraging

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Searching for, acquiring and handling food. Tests distinguish resource-specific actions from general movement or appetite.

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

## Sickness behaviour

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Coordinated changes in activity, appetite and social or other motivations associated with immune activation. Context dependence and experimental controls distinguish it from simple incapacity.

## Self-medication in animals

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Use of substances or actions that reduce harm from [parasites](ecology.md#parasite) or [disease](biology.md#disease). Tests require a health benefit, a relevant behavioural response and controls against ordinary nutritional preference.

### Transgenerational medication

↑ **Parent:** [Self-medication in animals](#self-medication-in-animals)

Parental use or selection of substances that reduce [disease](biology.md#disease) in offspring. A benefit to offspring does not imply treatment or cure of the parent.

## Behavioural immune system

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Sensory and behavioural processes reducing exposure to [pathogens](biology.md#pathogen), such as avoidance of contamination. This broader defence usage should be distinguished from cellular and molecular [immunity](biology.md#immunity-medical).

## Hygienic behaviour

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Removal or avoidance of contaminated material or infected individuals, potentially reducing exposure of an individual or group to [pathogens](biology.md#pathogen). [disease](biology.md#disease) reduction must be measured rather than inferred from cleaning alone.

## Animal communication

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Transmission of information between animals through signals or cues, assessed through effects on receivers.

### Species recognition

↑ **Parent:** [Animal communication](#animal-communication)

Discrimination among potential social or mating partners according to species-associated information. Recognition errors and discrimination costs affect its [evolution](evolution.md).

### Signal eavesdropping

↑ **Parent:** [Animal communication](#animal-communication)

Use of a signal by an unintended receiver, such as a [predator](ecology.md#predator) detecting a mating call. This can impose selection opposed to intended-receiver attraction.

### Biological cue

↑ **Parent:** [Animal communication](#animal-communication)

An informative feature not necessarily evolved for the receiver’s response. Distinguishing cues from signals requires an evolutionary argument, not just evidence of information transfer.

### Animal signal

↑ **Parent:** [Animal communication](#animal-communication)

A feature or action shaped by selection for its effects on a receiver, with receiver responses also subject to selection. A merely informative incidental feature can instead be a [biological cue](#biological-cue).

#### Signal honesty

↑ **Parent:** [Animal signal](#animal-signal)

A relation between a signal and a property relevant to its receiver. Honesty can follow aligned interests, physical constraints or differential costs and benefits; high production cost alone does not establish it.

##### Costly signalling

↑ **Parent:** [Signal honesty](#signal-honesty)

Signalling in which costs contribute to the evolutionary incentives governing expression. Reliability depends on differences in net payoffs among signaller types, not merely on a signal being expensive.

##### Index signal

↑ **Parent:** [Signal honesty](#signal-honesty)

A signal whose relation to the advertised property is constrained by its production mechanism. The receiver’s use and the relevant physical constraint should both be tested.

#### Multimodal signalling

↑ **Parent:** [Animal signal](#animal-signal)

Communication using more than one sensory modality. Components can supply redundant information, different information or context-dependent detection benefits.

#### Signal modality

↑ **Parent:** [Animal signal](#animal-signal)

The sensory channel through which a signal acts, such as acoustic, visual, chemical, mechanical or electrical stimulation.

### Aposematism

↑ **Parent:** [Animal communication](#animal-communication)

Use of a warning signal associated with defence. A conspicuous colour pattern alone does not establish either effective defence or [predator](ecology.md#predator) avoidance.

## Parental care

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Behaviour by a parent affecting offspring survival or development, including provisioning, defence and control of the developmental environment. Its costs and benefits need separate measurement.

### Biparental care

↑ **Parent:** [Parental care](#parental-care)

Care supplied by both parents. It can create shared offspring benefits alongside conflict over which parent bears the costs.

#### Parental negotiation

↑ **Parent:** [Biparental care](#biparental-care)

Conditional adjustment of care in response to a partner, offspring or the environment. A matching response can reflect information about brood demand rather than absence of conflict.

##### Partial compensation in parental care

↑ **Parent:** [Parental negotiation](#parental-negotiation)

An increase in care following a partner’s reduction that replaces less than the entire lost contribution. The predicted response depends on information, costs and the time available to adjust.

### Parental investment

↑ **Parent:** [Parental care](#parental-care)

An allocation to current offspring that carries an opportunity cost for other reproductive opportunities. Time spent near offspring is not automatically an investment of the same value in both sexes.

#### Reproductive allocation

↑ **Parent:** [Parental investment](#parental-investment)

#### Paternity certainty

↑ **Parent:** [Parental investment](#parental-investment)

Information concerning the probability that offspring are genetically sired by a male. Its effect on care depends on detection, future mating opportunities and effects on offspring, not only average extra-pair paternity.

## Socially transferred material

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Biological material passed from one individual's body to another and capable of influencing the receiver. Simply carrying external food is not sufficient for the narrower framework; donor-processed or donor-produced components matter.

## Maternal provisioning

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Maternal behaviour supplying offspring with resources and potentially bioactive transferred material. Resource quantity and material composition can affect offspring differently.

### Brood ball

↑ **Parent:** [Maternal provisioning](#maternal-provisioning)

A provisioned dung mass surrounding the developmental site of an individual dung-beetle offspring. Its food quantity and maternal contributions can be manipulated separately.

#### Faecal pedestal

↑ **Parent:** [Brood ball](#brood-ball)

Maternal faecal material associated with the egg chamber in certain dung beetles. Ingested material can provide an indirect route of maternal microbial transfer.

## Mirror self-recognition

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Use of mirror information in recognizing one's own body. A particular test outcome does not establish all forms of self-representation or theory of mind.

### Mirror-mark test

↑ **Parent:** [Mirror self-recognition](#mirror-self-recognition)

An assay asking whether an animal uses reflected information to investigate an otherwise unseen mark on its own body. Visible and sham marks, absence of the mirror and independent detection or action controls constrain the interpretation.

## Metacognition

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacognition)

Monitoring or control of one's own [cognition](psychology.md#cognition). [Metacognitive monitoring](#metacognitive-monitoring) estimates aspects of current or future performance, while [metacognitive control](#metacognitive-control) uses such estimates to guide behaviour. [Metamemory](#metamemory) applies these functions to remembering; [uncertainty responses](#uncertainty-response) provide a way to study some of them without verbal reports. Successful behaviour alone need not establish [introspection](psychology.md#introspection) or subjective awareness.

### Subjective confidence

↑ **Parent:** [Metacognition](#metacognition)

An individual's expressed certainty in a judgment. Its relation to objective correctness is measured separately from the certainty itself. [Metacognitive calibration](#metacognitive-calibration) concerns numerical agreement with outcomes; [metacognitive sensitivity](#metacognitive-sensitivity) concerns discrimination between better and worse performance.

#### Second-order inference about decisions

↑ **Parent:** [Subjective confidence](#subjective-confidence)

A computation that evaluates an action using partially shared evidence about the action-producing system. First-order decisions and confidence need not have identical internal samples; calling the computation second order does not imply conscious verbal reasoning.[2017, model architecture](psychology-bibliography.md#self-evaluation-of-decision-making-a-general-bayesian-framework-for-metacognitive-computation-by-stephen-m-fleming-and-nathaniel-d-daw)

#### Self-consistency theory of confidence

↑ **Parent:** [Subjective confidence](#subjective-confidence)

Confidence inferred from agreement among sampled representations supporting a response. Internally consistent evidence can systematically favour a wrong answer, separating confidence from correctness.[2012, consistency, accessibility and reliability–validity discrepancy](psychology-bibliography.md#the-self-consistency-model-of-subjective-confidence-by-asher-koriat)

#### Metacognitive sensitivity

↑ **Parent:** [Subjective confidence](#subjective-confidence)

The ability of confidence to distinguish a person's correct from incorrect responses, or to rank items by later success. It is distinct from [metacognitive calibration](#metacognitive-calibration). A [type 2 receiver operating characteristic](#type-2-receiver-operating-characteristic) measures discrimination in a binary-correctness task; other metamemory designs may need other measures.

##### Meta-d-prime

↑ **Parent:** [Metacognitive sensitivity](#metacognitive-sensitivity)

The first-order [sensitivity index](neuroscience.md#sensitivity-index) that, in a fitted [signal detection theory](neuroscience.md#signal-detection-theory) model, would generate the observed confidence discrimination. Model assumptions and sampling uncertainty matter; raw confidence–accuracy association alone does not estimate it.[2012, definition and method](psychology-bibliography.md#a-signal-detection-theoretic-approach-for-estimating-metacognitive-sensitivity-from-confidence-ratings-by-brian-maniscalco-and-hakwan-lau)

###### Metacognitive efficiency

↑ **Parent:** [Meta-d-prime](#meta-d-prime)

A model-based comparison of [meta-d-prime](#meta-d-prime) with first-order discrimination, often their ratio. Interpretation becomes unstable when the denominator is close to zero. Efficiency, [metacognitive calibration](#metacognitive-calibration) and overall confidence are different quantities.

##### A strictly increasing confidence transformation preserves metacognitive resolution

↑ **Parent:** [Metacognitive sensitivity](#metacognitive-sensitivity)

If confidence $C$ is replaced by a strictly increasing function $g(C)$, the attainable threshold partitions have the same ordering and ties. Consequently the [type 2 receiver operating characteristic](#type-2-receiver-operating-characteristic) and its [area under the ROC curve](statistical-learning.md#area-under-the-roc-curve) are unchanged. Equivalently, $P(C_{\rm correct}>C_{\rm error})+\tfrac12P(C_{\rm correct}=C_{\rm error})$ is invariant. Calibration need not be preserved: adding a positive constant to probabilities within their allowed range changes their absolute values while preserving ranks. A merely nondecreasing transformation can merge distinct reports and requires additional care.

##### Type 2 receiver operating characteristic

↑ **Parent:** [Metacognitive sensitivity](#metacognitive-sensitivity)

A [receiver operating characteristic](statistical-learning.md#receiver-operating-characteristic) treating high confidence as a prediction that a first-order answer was correct. Varying confidence threshold $t$ traces the false-positive and true-positive probabilities in the displayed expression. Raw discrimination can depend on first-order task performance; it is not automatically a performance-independent measure of monitoring quality.

#### Metacognitive calibration

↑ **Parent:** [Subjective confidence](#subjective-confidence)

For correctness [indicator function](measure-theory.md#indicator-function) $Y$ and stated probability $C$, ideal calibration means that answers assigned confidence $c$ are correct with conditional probability $c$. Overall overconfidence is $\mathbb E[C-Y]>0$; a zero overall difference does not establish calibration at every confidence level. Estimated calibration depends on sample size and how reports are grouped.

#### Retrospective confidence

↑ **Parent:** [Subjective confidence](#subjective-confidence)

Confidence evaluated after making a response. It may use evidence, further processing and information about the action that were not all available at the moment of the response.

##### Confidence as posterior correctness probability

↑ **Parent:** [Retrospective confidence](#retrospective-confidence)

A normative [Bayesian inference](bayesian-statistics.md) model of [retrospective confidence](#retrospective-confidence). For two exhaustive alternatives with equal [prior probabilities](bayesian-statistics.md#prior-probability), let $L=\log[p(E\mid H_1)/p(E\mid H_0)]$ be the [log-likelihood ratio](statistical-hypothesis-test.md#log-likelihood-ratio). [Bayes' theorem](probability-theory.md#bayes-theorem) gives posterior odds $e^L$. Choosing the alternative with the larger posterior yields $P(\text{correct}\mid E)=(1+e^{-|L|})^{-1}$. Unequal priors add prior log odds; choosing a different action changes which posterior probability is appropriate. This calculation does not establish that an observer knows the true likelihoods.

### Metamemory

↑ **Parent:** [Metacognition](#metacognition)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamemory)

[Metacognitive monitoring](#metacognitive-monitoring) and [metacognitive control](#metacognitive-control) applied to [memory encoding](neuroscience.md#memory-encoding), [memory retention](neuroscience.md#memory-retention) and [memory retrieval](neuroscience.md#memory-retrieval). Different judgments predict different outcomes; a prediction of future recall need not agree with confidence in an answer currently retrieved.

#### Cue-utilization approach to metamemory

↑ **Parent:** [Metamemory](#metamemory)

A theory in which [metamemory](#metamemory) judgments infer likely performance from available cues, rather than necessarily inspecting a memory trace directly. Cue use and cue validity need not coincide.[1997, cue-utilization proposal](psychology-bibliography.md#monitoring-one-s-own-knowledge-during-study-by-asher-koriat)

##### Mnemonic cue

↑ **Parent:** [Cue-utilization approach to metamemory](#cue-utilization-approach-to-metamemory)

An aspect of the learner's current processing experience, such as retrieval ease, used in judging memory.

##### Extrinsic metamemory cue

↑ **Parent:** [Cue-utilization approach to metamemory](#cue-utilization-approach-to-metamemory)

A property of the learning conditions, such as study time or repetitions, used in judging memory.

##### Intrinsic metamemory cue

↑ **Parent:** [Cue-utilization approach to metamemory](#cue-utilization-approach-to-metamemory)

A property of the material, such as the difficulty or relatedness of paired words, used in judging memory.

#### Feeling of knowing

↑ **Parent:** [Metamemory](#metamemory)

An estimate that currently unrecalled information could be recognized or recovered later. It differs from confidence in an answer already produced and from a prediction made while initially studying it.

#### Judgment of learning

↑ **Parent:** [Metamemory](#metamemory)

A prediction, made during or after study, of whether material will be remembered on a later test. Specify the delay, the test and whether the target remains visible; these determine what information the learner can use.

##### Delayed judgment-of-learning effect

↑ **Parent:** [Judgment of learning](#judgment-of-learning)

Improved itemwise predictive accuracy of [judgments of learning](#judgment-of-learning) made after a delay under suitable conditions. The available cue matters: delaying a cue-alone judgment can be useful even when delaying a cue–target judgment gives little benefit.[1992, abstract](psychology-bibliography.md#importance-of-the-kind-of-cue-for-judgments-of-learning-and-the-delayed-jol-effect-by-john-dunlosky-and-thomas-o-nelson)

### Metacognitive control

↑ **Parent:** [Metacognition](#metacognition)

Regulation of [cognition](psychology.md#cognition) using information about one's own processing: allocating further study, seeking information, answering or declining a test. A change in a monitoring report does not by itself demonstrate a beneficial change in control.

#### Uncertainty response

↑ **Parent:** [Metacognitive control](#metacognitive-control)

An action permitting a subject to decline a difficult test, seek information or select an explicit uncertainty option. Reward, delay and task contingencies influence the action; an uncertainty response is an operational behaviour rather than a direct observation of subjective awareness.

##### Optimal confidence threshold for an uncertainty response

↑ **Parent:** [Uncertainty response](#uncertainty-response)

Under linear [expected utility](utility-function.md#expected-utility), let a correct answer yield $r$, an error yield $-w$, and declining yield $s$, with $r+w>0$. Answering has expected payoff $pr-(1-p)w$, so it is preferred exactly when $p>(s+w)/(r+w)$. Equality gives indifference. Different utility, time costs or beliefs change this criterion; behaviour across differently rewarded tasks cannot be compared as if the threshold were fixed.

### Metacognitive monitoring

↑ **Parent:** [Metacognition](#metacognition)

Estimating aspects of one's own [cognition](psychology.md#cognition), such as whether an answer is correct or whether material can later be recalled. [Metacognitive monitoring](#metacognitive-monitoring) should be distinguished from [metacognitive control](#metacognitive-control), which changes behaviour in response to such estimates.

#### Meta-awareness

↑ **Parent:** [Metacognitive monitoring](#metacognitive-monitoring)

Awareness of one's current mental state, such as noticing that attention has drifted. Off-task thought and awareness of that thought can be measured separately.[2007, distinction between aware and unaware mind wandering](psychology-bibliography.md#the-lights-are-on-but-no-one-s-home-by-jonathan-smallwood-and-others)

## Food caching

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Storage of food for future use, which creates opportunities for both memory and competition over stored resources.

## Anticipatory looking

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Looking towards a predicted future action or event before it occurs. Gaze measures require prespecified regions, timing and exclusions.

## Mental representation

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

An internal state or process used in representing information about the world, self or others. Behavioural tests should distinguish its proposed content from simpler predictive rules.

### Mental imagery

↑ **Parent:** [Mental representation](#mental-representation)

Perception-like experience without a corresponding current sensory input. Its emotional effects, voluntary control and relation to [autobiographical memory](neuroscience.md#autobiographical-memory) are separable research questions.

### Self-representation

↑ **Parent:** [Mental representation](#mental-representation)

Representation of aspects of one's own body, knowledge or actions. These components need not entail an autobiographical or linguistically expressed concept of self.

#### Self-awareness

↑ **Parent:** [Self-representation](#self-representation)

Awareness involving oneself as an object or subject of representation. Its proposed forms must be operationally distinguished; [bodily self-recognition](#bodily-self-recognition) does not alone establish reflective or autobiographical awareness.

#### Body schema

↑ **Parent:** [Self-representation](#self-representation)

An action-related representation of bodily configuration and capacities. Its functional use need not entail a verbal or autobiographical concept of self.

#### Bodily self-recognition

↑ **Parent:** [Self-representation](#self-representation)

Identification of one’s own body or body-related sensory information. It is narrower than reflective knowledge of one’s identity and does not alone demonstrate [theory of mind](#theory-of-mind).

## Theory of mind

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Attribution or representation of mental states in oneself or others to explain or predict behaviour. Component abilities need not form an all-or-none package.

### EmpaToM

↑ **Parent:** [Theory of mind](#theory-of-mind)

A task framework using video narratives to manipulate emotionality and mental-state reasoning separately. [Affect](psychology.md#affect) and [compassion](psychology.md#compassion) ratings, reasoning accuracy and confidence can be analysed as distinct outcomes.

### Goal attribution

↑ **Parent:** [Theory of mind](#theory-of-mind)

Interpreting another agent's action in relation to an outcome it is pursuing. It can be narrower than representing the agent's beliefs or intentions in a human-like way.

### False-belief understanding

↑ **Parent:** [Theory of mind](#theory-of-mind)

Representing an agent's belief as differing from reality and predicting action accordingly. False belief, ignorance and a correct remembered location require different controls.

#### Emergence and expression accounts of false-belief development

↑ **Parent:** [False-belief understanding](#false-belief-understanding)

An emergence account assigns a capacity such as [language](README.md#language) or executive control a role in constructing understanding of [belief](psychology.md#belief) over development. An expression account assigns it a role in accessing and displaying already available understanding. The same observed [correlation](variance.md#pearson-correlation-coefficient) is compatible with both. Immediate task-demand manipulations primarily probe expression; lasting transfer from a controlled intervention can constrain emergence, provided strategy and practice explanations are addressed.

#### False-belief task

↑ **Parent:** [False-belief understanding](#false-belief-understanding)

A task testing how an agent will act or respond when its representation differs from the actual state of affairs. Unexpected-transfer tasks contrast the location an agent witnessed with a subsequent displacement it did not witness; comprehension controls and response demands affect interpretation.[Cognition 13, 103–128; unexpected-transfer stories](psychology-bibliography.md#beliefs-about-beliefs-by-heinz-wimmer-and-josef-perner)

##### Performance factors in false-belief tasks

↑ **Parent:** [False-belief task](#false-belief-task)

[Language](README.md#language) interpretation, [memory](neuroscience.md#memory-in-animals), [inhibitory control](psychology.md#inhibitory-control), response generation and other demands affecting the observed answer. Lowering concurrent demands while preserving the mistaken-belief contrast can reveal successful performance earlier than a standard task. The modified procedure still needs controls against cueing and simplified nonmental strategies.[PNAS 113, 13360–13365; Experiments 1–4 and picture-book apparatus](psychology-bibliography.md#two-and-a-half-year-olds-succeed-at-a-traditional-false-belief-task-with-reduced-processing-demands-by-peipei-setoh-and-others)

### Knowledge attribution

↑ **Parent:** [Theory of mind](#theory-of-mind)

Tracking another individual's informational access or knowledge. Success does not automatically imply understanding false belief.

### Visual perspective taking

↑ **Parent:** [Theory of mind](#theory-of-mind)

Using differences in visual access between individuals. Knowing what another can see differs from representing exactly how an object appears to them.

## Judgement bias

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

A change in responses to ambiguous stimuli relative to trained reference stimuli. Motivation, general activity and perceptual discrimination require control.

## Anthropomorphism

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Attribution of human-like properties to non-human animals. Its evidential risk depends on whether the attributed mechanism is tested rather than assumed.

## Animal consciousness

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

The study of conscious experience in non-human animals. Behavioural and neural evidence can constrain hypotheses without directly providing another individual's standpoint.

## Learning

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Change in performance or behaviour through experience, distinguished from immediate sensory responses, fatigue and maturation.

### Sensorimotor learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

[Learning](#learning) relationships between sensory events and one’s own actions. Correlated observation and execution can shape a mapping without a special-purpose innate matching mechanism.

#### Countermirror training

↑ **Parent:** [Sensorimotor learning](#sensorimotor-learning)

Training in which one observed action is consistently paired with a different executed action. Subsequent changes in matching-related responses test plasticity of the sensorimotor mapping.

#### Visuomotor association

↑ **Parent:** [Sensorimotor learning](#sensorimotor-learning)

A learned relationship between a visual event and a motor representation. A matching relationship and a deliberately mismatching relationship can both be learned.

### Item-specific learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

Acquiring information about particular encountered items. It can coexist with abstraction, but improvement on practised items does not establish generalisation to new combinations.

### Sequence learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

Acquisition of ordered relations among events or actions. A sequence-learning deficit can reflect problems with perception, motor execution, memory or [learning](#learning) itself; control tasks determine which interpretation is warranted.

### Motor learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

Experience-dependent changes supporting acquisition or improvement of movement skills. Baseline execution, rate of improvement, retention and transfer should be distinguished when testing a [learning](#learning) impairment.

#### Oral praxis

↑ **Parent:** [Motor learning](#motor-learning)

Planning and executing learned mouth and facial movements. [Speech](README.md#speech) and nonspeech versions can constrain whether an impairment concerns articulatory sequencing, broader motor planning or additional [language](README.md#language) processes.

### Mere-exposure effect

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

An increase in preference associated with prior exposure. Preference and explicit [recognition memory](neuroscience.md#recognition-memory) can dissociate. This does not by itself determine whether evaluation of the stimulus's significance occurred.

### Preparedness in psychology

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

A proposed biological bias that makes some associations easier to acquire than others under comparable experience. Prepared [learning](#learning) does not mean that the resulting response is already present before [learning](#learning), nor that it is unchangeable.

### Observational learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

[Learning](#learning) through observation of another organism rather than only through direct exposure to the relevant consequences. [Observational conditioning](#observational-conditioning) is one form.

### Associative learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

[Learning](#learning) relations among events, actions or outcomes. [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning) and [instrumental conditioning](#instrumental-conditioning) provide complementary preparations for testing what information was acquired and how it controls responding.

### Reversal learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

[Learning](#learning) after a previously established stimulus–outcome or action–outcome relation is reversed. Acquisition, retention and perseveration controls help distinguish flexibility from general [learning](#learning) or performance deficits.

### Perceptual learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

Experience-dependent improvement in a perceptual task. Training can alter sensory representations, [attention](psychology.md#attention), decisions or motor responses. Transfer, retention and matched training controls help identify what changed.

### Inhibitory learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

[Learning](#learning) that suppresses or competes with an established response or expectation without necessarily erasing the original association. An exposure-based treatment can therefore need practice across contexts and a plan for retrieving the new [learning](#learning) after fear returns.[2014, learning and retrieval framework](psychology-bibliography.md#maximizing-exposure-therapy-an-inhibitory-learning-approach-by-michelle-g-craske-and-others)

### Habituation

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

A reduction in a response following repeated stimulation, distinguished from sensory adaptation or motor fatigue. A decrease in [anxiety](psychology.md#anxiety) during exposure can occur, but neither defines all therapeutic [learning](#learning) nor guarantees its transfer to another context.

#### Between-session habituation

↑ **Parent:** [Habituation](#habituation)

Reduction in responding from one session to another. Comparisons should account for changes in task difficulty and context rather than interpreting lower distress during an easier task as equivalent [learning](#learning).

#### Within-session habituation

↑ **Parent:** [Habituation](#habituation)

Reduction in responding during a session. Its measurement depends on the response and time window; a distress trajectory is not automatically an index of lasting clinical change.

### Domain-general learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

A [learning](#learning) capacity applicable across more than one content domain. Similar performance with speech and tone sequences argues against exclusive linguistic applicability of the measured process, without showing that every representation or neural implementation is identical.[Cognition 70, 27–52; nonlinguistic tone-stream segmentation by infants and adults](psychology-bibliography.md#statistical-learning-of-tone-sequences-by-human-infants-and-adults-by-jenny-r-saffran-and-others)

### Distributional learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

[Learning](#learning) from patterns of occurrence, covariation or sequential dependence in experience. A behavioural result can constrain which regularities were learned without uniquely identifying whether the mechanism used associations, stored chunks or [hypothesis](README.md#hypothesis) testing. Distributional information can be exploited by a general algorithm operating over specialised representations.

### Language acquisition

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

Development of the representations and processes used to understand and produce a [language](README.md#language). Evidence about recognising a sound pattern, [learning](#learning) its meaning and generalising grammatical structure addresses different components of the problem.

#### Oral-language proficiency

↑ **Parent:** [Language acquisition](#language-acquisition)

Knowledge and skill used to understand and produce spoken language. Performance in one language or dialect should not be treated as a complete measure of a child's language competence across contexts.

#### Oral-language intervention

↑ **Parent:** [Language acquisition](#language-acquisition)

Teaching intended to improve spoken-language knowledge or its use. A programme can affect [reading comprehension](psychology.md#reading-comprehension), but its package effect does not isolate a single [language](README.md#language) component.[Psychological Science 21, 1106–1116; text/oral/combined interventions, untreated comparison and eleven-month follow-up](psychology-bibliography.md#ameliorating-children-s-reading-comprehension-difficulties-a-randomized-controlled-trial-by-paula-j-clarke-and-others)

#### Age of acquisition

↑ **Parent:** [Language acquisition](#language-acquisition)

The age at which [learning](#learning) of a [language](README.md#language) begins, distinguished from current proficiency and duration of use. Delayed access to any first [language](README.md#language) can affect later [language processing](neuroscience.md#language-processing) and is not equivalent to [deafness](neuroscience.md#deafness).

#### Metalinguistic awareness

↑ **Parent:** [Language acquisition](#language-acquisition)

Ability to attend to and reason about properties of [language](README.md#language) itself. Improvement in this relatively language-proximal ability is a different [hypothesis](README.md#hypothesis) from broad transfer to every [executive function](psychology.md#executive-function).

#### Bilingualism

↑ **Parent:** [Language acquisition](#language-acquisition)

Knowledge and use of two [languages](README.md#language), varying in proficiency, acquisition age and social setting. Treating it as a single binary exposure can obscure which experiences a cognitive [hypothesis](README.md#hypothesis) actually concerns.

##### Bimodal bilingualism

↑ **Parent:** [Bilingualism](#bilingualism)

Use of a signed and a spoken [language](README.md#language). Within-person comparisons can reduce some between-group [confounding](causal-inference.md#confounding) while leaving differences in sensory input, movement and [language](README.md#language) structure.

##### Bilingual advantage

↑ **Parent:** [Bilingualism](#bilingualism)

A proposed benefit associated with bilingual experience for a stated outcome. [Language](README.md#language) proficiency, [metalinguistic awareness](#metalinguistic-awareness), domain-general task performance and later clinical symptoms are different outcomes and require separate evidence.

#### Developmental language disorder

↑ **Parent:** [Language acquisition](#language-acquisition)

Persistent developmental difficulty with [language](README.md#language) associated with functional impact. It is not interchangeable with motor [speech](README.md#speech) apraxia or [stuttering](neuroscience.md#stuttering), and a rare single-family genetic finding does not establish a universal cause.

#### Infant-directed speech

↑ **Parent:** [Language acquisition](#language-acquisition)

Speech addressed to an [infant](organism.md#infant), often differing in [prosody](README.md#prosody) from adult-directed speech. A listening preference is an experimentally measurable tendency whose magnitude can depend on the stimuli, the [infant](organism.md#infant)’s experience and the measurement method; it is not a universal individual trait.[Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science 3, 24–52; Participants, preregistered analyses and Discussion](psychology-bibliography.md#quantifying-sources-of-variability-in-infancy-research-using-the-infant-directed-speech-preference-by-the-manybabies-consortium)

#### Linguistic nativism

↑ **Parent:** [Language acquisition](#language-acquisition)

The position that some language-relevant structure or [learning](#learning) constraints are present prior to the relevant linguistic experience. It is not equivalent to denying all [learning](#learning); the specificity and content of the proposed initial endowment require evidence.

#### Word learning

↑ **Parent:** [Language acquisition](#language-acquisition)

##### Cross-situational word learning

↑ **Parent:** [Word learning](#word-learning)

[Learning](#learning) word–referent mappings by integrating information across individually ambiguous encounters. Recurrent co-occurrence can favour a mapping even when no single encounter uniquely identifies it. A target-looking preference need not distinguish parallel associative accumulation from testing and revising a candidate [hypothesis](README.md#hypothesis).[Cognition 106, 1558–1568; ambiguous word/picture pairings and target-versus-distractor looking](psychology-bibliography.md#infants-rapidly-learn-word-referent-mappings-via-cross-situational-statistics-by-linda-b-smith-and-chen-yu)

#### Statistical language learning

↑ **Parent:** [Language acquisition](#language-acquisition)

Use of distributional information in acquiring speech-sound distinctions, recurring units, dependencies or word–referent correspondences. These levels require different statistics, and success at one does not show that the complete natural-language acquisition problem has been solved.

##### Nonadjacent dependency learning

↑ **Parent:** [Statistical language learning](#statistical-language-learning)

[Learning](#learning) a relation between elements separated by intervening material. In an artificial $aXb$ system, increasing variability in $X$ can help expose the stable $a$–$b$ relation. Such a relation need not be hierarchical [syntax](README.md#syntax).[Psychological Science 13, 431–436; three-element artificial-language strings, middle-element variability and nonadjacent dependencies](psychology-bibliography.md#variability-and-detection-of-invariant-structure-by-rebecca-l-gomez)

#### Speech segmentation

↑ **Parent:** [Language acquisition](#language-acquisition)

Identifying candidate units in continuous speech. Sequential predictability, [prosody](README.md#prosody) and other cues may support different boundaries; discrimination of a familiarised unit does not alone demonstrate that it has been assigned a meaning.

#### Phonetic category learning

↑ **Parent:** [Language acquisition](#language-acquisition)

Experience-dependent changes in grouping or discriminating speech sounds. Comparing exposure distributions while holding the token range fixed can test a contribution from distributional information.[Cognition 82, B101–B111; unimodal/bimodal speech-sound exposure and discrimination](psychology-bibliography.md#infant-sensitivity-to-distributional-information-can-affect-phonetic-discrimination-by-jessica-maye-and-others)

### Operant conditioning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

[Learning](#learning) involving a relation between an action and its consequences. In a mobile contingency task, an [infant](organism.md#infant)’s kicking causes movement of a mobile; later kicking can be used as a retention measure with appropriate baseline and noncontingent controls.

#### Punishment

↑ **Parent:** [Operant conditioning](#operant-conditioning)

A consequence that decreases the future occurrence of a response. It is distinguished from [negative reinforcement](#negative-reinforcement), which increases a response by removing or preventing an aversive event.

#### Contingency management

↑ **Parent:** [Operant conditioning](#operant-conditioning)

Arranging consequences contingent on clearly specified behaviour to change its future occurrence. The target, measurement, timing and alternative responses determine what is reinforced; [reinforcement](evolution.md#reinforcement) is not identical to [punishment](#punishment).

#### Functional analysis of behavior

↑ **Parent:** [Operant conditioning](#operant-conditioning)

Analysis of antecedents and consequences that maintain a specified behaviour. A useful formulation predicts what changes when a relevant contingency is manipulated, rather than merely giving the behaviour a new label.

#### Avoidance learning

↑ **Parent:** [Operant conditioning](#operant-conditioning)

[Learning](#learning) actions that prevent or escape an anticipated aversive outcome. In [anxiety](psychology.md#anxiety), immediate relief and the absence of disconfirming experience can maintain avoidance even when the feared prediction is inaccurate.

##### Avoidance behavior

↑ **Parent:** [Avoidance learning](#avoidance-learning)

An action reducing contact with an anticipated aversive situation or experience. It can provide immediate relief while limiting corrective [learning](#learning), but [avoidance](#avoidance-behavior) of genuine danger is not thereby pathological.

##### Safety behavior

↑ **Parent:** [Avoidance learning](#avoidance-learning)

A precaution intended to prevent a feared outcome or conceal [anxiety](psychology.md#anxiety). It can maintain fear when safety is attributed to the precaution, when it blocks informative experience, or when it produces misleading symptoms. Whether a behavior has this function depends on context; objectively useful precautions are not inherently pathological.

###### Judicious safety-behavior use

↑ **Parent:** [Safety behavior](#safety-behavior)

Use of a precaution in a way intended to support engagement while preserving useful [learning](#learning). Its value cannot be settled from its label; prediction testing, attribution of safety, replacement precautions and longer-term transfer should be measured. An undergraduate contamination experiment showed why reduction in [symptoms](psychology.md#symptom) need not be logically impossible in the presence of a [safety behavior](#safety-behavior).[Journal of Behavior Therapy and Experimental Psychiatry 42, 397–404; 80 undergraduates, exposure with or without hygienic wipes, two-session analogue](psychology-bibliography.md#reducing-contamination-by-exposure-plus-safety-behaviour-by-stanley-rachman-and-others)

###### Reassurance seeking

↑ **Parent:** [Safety behavior](#safety-behavior)

Seeking another person's confirmation of safety or certainty. In an [anxiety](psychology.md#anxiety)-maintaining cycle, immediate relief can reinforce repeated requests without changing the underlying interpretation. This function should be distinguished from appropriate information seeking or clinical assessment.

#### Reward learning

↑ **Parent:** [Operant conditioning](#operant-conditioning)

Learning from rewarding outcomes to guide subsequent choices. A reduced change in response bias can reflect altered outcome sensitivity, integration over trials, exploration or performance; it does not uniquely diagnose reduced experienced pleasure.

##### Reward

↑ **Parent:** [Reward learning](#reward-learning)

An outcome that is valued or promotes approach under specified conditions. Its hedonic impact, learned predictive value and ability to motivate action can dissociate; no single behavioural measurement identifies all three.

#### Learned helplessness

↑ **Parent:** [Operant conditioning](#operant-conditioning)

A behavioural phenomenon in which prior exposure to uncontrollable aversive events impairs subsequent escape or coping. The original explanation invoked learning that action and outcome are independent; later accounts emphasize learning control over stress responses. The phenomenon is an animal model relevant to some symptoms, not a complete diagnosis of human depression.

#### Action-outcome contingency

↑ **Parent:** [Operant conditioning](#operant-conditioning)

For an action $A$ and an outcome $O$, a descriptive contingency is $\Delta P=P(O\mid A)-P(O\mid\neg A)$. If both probabilities are high, outcomes may be frequent while control is absent. If both are low, outcomes may be rare while control is still absent. For causal control, actions must be experimentally varied or common causes adequately controlled; observational conditional probabilities alone can be confounded.

#### Negative reinforcement

↑ **Parent:** [Operant conditioning](#operant-conditioning)

An increase in a response because it terminates or avoids an aversive outcome. It is not punishment: reinforcement describes the increase in responding, whereas punishment describes a decrease.

#### Mobile conjugate reinforcement paradigm

↑ **Parent:** [Operant conditioning](#operant-conditioning)

A ribbon links an [infant](organism.md#infant)’s ankle to a mobile so kicking produces visible movement. Baseline kicking and a later nonreinforced test permit assessment of a learned contingency. A reminder moving the familiar mobile can reactivate later performance; the crucial control asks whether reminder exposure alone teaches the response.[Science 208, 1159–1161; mobile conditioning, reminder and retention experiments](psychology-bibliography.md#reactivation-of-infant-memory-by-carolyn-rovee-collier-and-others)

### Transfer of learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

An effect of [learning](#learning) or training on performance beyond the practised activity. The similarity of the new task, its stimuli, procedures and representations determines how strong a generalisation claim is warranted.

#### Far transfer

↑ **Parent:** [Transfer of learning](#transfer-of-learning)

Improvement in substantially different tasks or domains, such as academic attainment after isolated [working memory](neuroscience.md#working-memory) exercises. [Randomised](causal-inference.md#randomization) comparisons with credible [active controls](design-of-experiments.md#active-control) and prespecified outcome measures make the claim more informative.

#### Near transfer

↑ **Parent:** [Transfer of learning](#transfer-of-learning)

Improvement in an untrained activity closely resembling the trained tasks. It can demonstrate more than memorisation of particular items without establishing improvement in distant educational outcomes.

### Classical conditioning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

Learning a relation between a cue and an outcome. Prediction-error models explain why a new cue may acquire little value when an established cue already predicts the outcome, the phenomenon of blocking.

#### Observational conditioning

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

Acquisition of a response through observing another individual's reaction to a stimulus. The recipient's later behavior can be tested without the demonstrator present, distinguishing [learning](#learning) from an immediate reaction to the demonstrator.

#### Stimulus generalisation

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

Transfer of a learned response to stimuli differing from the original cue. Similarity and context determine what is being generalised; a response to a reminder does not require literal identity with the original stimulus.

#### SOP model

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

A model associated with [Allan R. Wagner](psychologist.md#allan-r-wagner) in which stimulus elements occupy an inactive state $I$, primary activation $A_1$, or secondary activation $A_2$. External presentation recruits inactive elements into $A_1$; activity then passes through $A_2$ to inactivity. Learned associates can recruit elements into $A_2$. These are functional states, not established neuronal or [receptor](protein.md#receptor-biochemistry) identities.

##### SOP element-state conservation

↑ **Parent:** [SOP model](#sop-model)

Represent a three-state [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md) by occupancy fractions $\boldsymbol p=(p_I,p_1,p_2)$ in $I,A_1,A_2$. A nonnegative transfer $f_{ij}\leq p_i$ subtracts the same amount from donor $i$ that it adds to recipient $j$. The total therefore remains $p_I+p_1+p_2=1$. If all simultaneous outflows from a state sum to at most its old occupancy, each occupancy remains nonnegative. This elementary conservation argument makes a proposed numerical realization checkable; it does not identify the states with biological compartments.

##### Affective extension of SOP

↑ **Parent:** [SOP model](#sop-model)

A development of the model by [Allan R. Wagner](psychologist.md#allan-r-wagner) and [Susan E. Brandon](psychologist.md#susan-e-brandon) distinguishing sensory-specific and emotive components of outcome representations, with potentially different activation time courses and response consequences. It allows one cue to influence multiple response systems differently without requiring contradictory values in one undifferentiated representation.

##### Modified SOP model

↑ **Parent:** [SOP model](#sop-model)

An extension allowing association changes involving associatively activated, physically absent cues. In the common symmetric formulation, matched $A_1/A_1$ and $A_2/A_2$ states support excitation, while mismatched $A_1/A_2$ and $A_2/A_1$ states support inhibition. The extra rules allow forms of [retrospective revaluation](#retrospective-revaluation); neural reactivation alone does not verify their signs.

#### Retrospective revaluation

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

A change in responding to an absent target cue after new experience with its previously associated cues. It constrains accounts that permit changes only when the target is physically present.

#### Sensory preconditioning

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

Training a relation between initially neutral cues before giving one cue biological significance. Responding to the other cue can then depend on inference through the learned structure rather than direct cue–outcome reinforcement.

#### Blocking in classical conditioning

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

Reduced [learning](#learning) about an added cue when another cue already predicts the outcome. Control conditions must match exposure and outcome history; blocking in [learning](#learning) is distinct from blocking in experimental design.

#### Conditioned inhibition

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

[Learning](#learning) through which a cue signals an outcome’s absence or reduction relative to expectation. Summation and retardation tests can help distinguish inhibition from simple low excitatory strength.

#### Latent inhibition

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

Retarded conditioning to a previously nonreinforced cue relative to an appropriately matched novel cue. Changes in [attention](psychology.md#attention), retrieval and context can contribute, so it is not uniquely diagnostic of one mechanism.

#### Conditioned response

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

A response expressed to a cue after [learning](#learning). It need not be a literal copy of the response directly elicited by the outcome.

#### Unconditioned stimulus

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

An outcome that can elicit a response without the particular cue–outcome [learning](#learning) under study. The term does not imply that every property of the response is immune to previous experience.

#### Conditioned stimulus

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

A cue whose relation to another event is learned in a conditioning procedure.

#### Fear conditioning

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

[Learning](#learning) through which a cue associated with an aversive event later evokes measured defensive responses. The learned response, the aversive event and subjective experience must be distinguished.

##### Fear-potentiated startle

↑ **Parent:** [Fear conditioning](#fear-conditioning)

Enhancement of the [acoustic startle reflex](neuroscience.md#acoustic-startle-reflex) in the presence of a cue associated with threat. It operationalizes a defensive response rather than directly reading subjective [fear](psychology.md#fear).

#### Conditioned fear

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

A defensive response learned through a relation between a cue and an aversive outcome. Laboratory cue responses and the complex [fears](psychology.md#fear) involved in a clinical [anxiety disorder](psychiatry.md#anxiety-disorder) require an explicit translational bridge.

#### Extinction learning

↑ **Parent:** [Classical conditioning](#classical-conditioning)

[Learning](#learning) during presentations of a previously predictive cue without the expected outcome. Reduced responding need not mean that the original association has been erased. Context, delay and subsequent experience can alter which association is retrieved.

##### Extinction recall

↑ **Parent:** [Extinction learning](#extinction-learning)

Retrieval of [learning](#learning) established during [extinction](#extinction-learning), often tested after a delay. Successful immediate [extinction](#extinction-learning) need not guarantee later retrieval under different temporal or contextual conditions.[Biological Psychiatry 66, 1075–1082; 16 PTSD/15 trauma-exposed controls, two-day conditioning, skin conductance and 3-T fMRI](psychology-bibliography.md#neurobiological-basis-of-failure-to-recall-extinction-memory-in-posttraumatic-stress-disorder-by-mohammed-r-milad-and-others)

##### Renewal after extinction

↑ **Parent:** [Extinction learning](#extinction-learning)

Recovery of a [conditioned response](#conditioned-response) when the context differs from the context of extinction. It shows that performance after extinction can depend on retrieval conditions.

##### Developmental fear-extinction experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Extinction learning](#extinction-learning)

Pattwell and colleagues compared conditioned responding and [extinction](#extinction-learning) across [human](organism.md#human) developmental groups, with parallel mouse investigations. Skin-conductance responses indexed [human](organism.md#human) [fear](psychology.md#fear) [learning](#learning). The developmental laboratory pattern motivates clinical hypotheses but does not directly estimate the efficacy of [exposure therapy](psychology.md#exposure-therapy) for [adolescents](organism.md#adolescent).[PNAS 109, 16318–16323; human conditioned-fear/extinction experiment and parallel mouse work](psychology-bibliography.md#altered-fear-learning-across-development-in-both-mouse-and-human-by-siobhan-s-pattwell-and-others)

##### Return of fear

↑ **Parent:** [Extinction learning](#extinction-learning)

Reappearance of [fear](psychology.md#fear) after it has decreased. It can reflect a change in retrieval conditions without implying that no useful [learning](#learning) occurred during treatment.

###### Fear renewal

↑ **Parent:** [Return of fear](#return-of-fear)

Return of conditioned responding when testing occurs in a context different from the [extinction](#extinction-learning) context. It motivates tests of generalisation beyond the treatment setting.

### Instrumental conditioning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

Learning about the consequences of an action, such as lever pressing that produces an outcome. Tests of contingency and [outcome devaluation](#outcome-devaluation) help distinguish action–outcome control from habit-like responding.

#### Habit

↑ **Parent:** [Instrumental conditioning](#instrumental-conditioning)

A learned tendency to respond that can persist despite a change in the current value of the outcome. Devaluation insensitivity is informative only under conditions in which the outcome change is understood and performance is otherwise possible. It need not imply exceptionally rapid [habit](#habit) acquisition.[American Journal of Psychiatry 168, 718–726; Method, instructed outcome devaluation and slips-of-action test](psychology-bibliography.md#disruption-in-the-balance-between-goal-directed-behavior-and-habit-learning-in-obsessive-compulsive-disorder-by-claire-m-gillan-and-others)

#### Goal-directed action

↑ **Parent:** [Instrumental conditioning](#instrumental-conditioning)

Action sensitive to both the relationship between an action and its outcome and the outcome's current value. [Outcome devaluation](#outcome-devaluation) and contingency manipulation provide operational tests, subject to task comprehension and performance limitations.

### Odor-taste conditioning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

A change in an odor's taste-like qualities or associations after pairing it with a taste. Pre/post odor-only judgments can test transfer beyond the original mixture, while controls are needed for expectation and use of response scales. Such transfer supports learned cross-sensory effects but does not by itself prove a distinct developmental [synesthesia](neuroscience.md#synesthesia) mechanism.[Experiment 1, sucrose- and acid-paired odors](psychology-bibliography.md#the-acquisition-of-taste-properties-by-odors-by-richard-j-stevenson-and-others)

### Predator learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

Changes in predatory responses through experience, including association of [prey](ecology.md#prey) signals with unprofitability. Experiments must distinguish [learning](#learning) from immediate avoidance and from loss of appetite.

### Social learning

↑ **Parent:** [Learning](#learning)

Learning influenced by other individuals or their products. Its mechanism can range from attention to a location to acquisition of more detailed behavioural information.

#### Emulation in social learning

↑ **Parent:** [Social learning](#social-learning)

Reproducing an outcome or environmental result without necessarily copying the observed means. It must be distinguished from [imitation](#imitation) when an observer reaches the same goal by another action.

#### Imitation

↑ **Parent:** [Social learning](#social-learning)

Reproducing an observed action or its organisation. Model-specific matching must be distinguished from increased general activity, emulation of an outcome or a response elicited by many interesting stimuli.

##### Rational imitation

↑ **Parent:** [Imitation](#imitation)

Selective reproduction of a demonstrated means in relation to contextual constraints and inferred goals. A behavioural difference can also admit attentional or perceptual explanations; selective copying alone is not a unique signature of explicit [belief](psychology.md#belief) reasoning.

##### Automatic imitation

↑ **Parent:** [Imitation](#imitation)

A compatibility effect in which observed task-irrelevant actions influence action execution. It is not necessarily deliberate copying, wholly uncontrolled behaviour or a pure measure of [empathy](psychology.md#empathy).

###### Imitative compatibility

↑ **Parent:** [Automatic imitation](#automatic-imitation)

Whether an observed action matches the action a participant must perform, distinguished experimentally from spatial stimulus–response compatibility.

##### Motor correspondence problem

↑ **Parent:** [Imitation](#imitation)

The problem of mapping another person’s perceptible action onto the observer’s own motor commands, despite differences in viewpoint and access to bodily feedback.

##### Neonatal imitation

↑ **Parent:** [Imitation](#imitation)

Model-specific action matching during the neonatal period. Early positive reports and subsequent disputed [replication](README.md#scientific-replication) and reanalysis make this a useful case for distinguishing whether an action occurs, whether it is selectively elicited by its matching model, and whether it supports a broad theory of [imitation](#imitation).[Science 198, 75–78; modelled facial/manual actions and coded infant responses](psychology-bibliography.md#imitation-of-facial-and-manual-gestures-by-human-neonates-by-andrew-n-meltzoff-and-m-keith-moore)

##### Deferred imitation

↑ **Parent:** [Imitation](#imitation)

Reproduction of an observed action after a delay without continuous access to its demonstration. It supplies a nonverbal [memory](neuroscience.md#memory-in-animals) measure, with baseline and control conditions needed to exclude actions likely to occur without the prior demonstration.

## Cultural inheritance

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Transmission of acquired information or behavioural patterns between individuals or generations through social processes. It need not follow genetic parentage.

## Habitat preference

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Differential use or selection of habitats, which can affect exposure to resources, mates and selection.

### Host fidelity

↑ **Parent:** [Habitat preference](#habitat-preference)

Repeated use of a host type across life stages or generations. Host-associated mating can translate host fidelity into partial reproductive isolation.

## Ethology

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Biological study of [animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md), combining observation, experiments and evolutionary explanation.

<h3 id="tinbergen-s-four-questions">Tinbergen's four questions</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Ethology](#ethology)

Complementary questions concerning mechanism, development, adaptive function and evolutionary history.

## Dominance hierarchy

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

A pattern of asymmetric competitive outcomes among group members. Rank can constrain access and participation without implying efficient allocation or identical interests.

### Winner-loser effect

↑ **Parent:** [Dominance hierarchy](#dominance-hierarchy)

Dependence of subsequent contest behaviour or outcomes on previous wins or losses. Repeated outcomes require controls for pre-existing ability and opponent differences.

## Division of labour

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Distribution of different tasks among members of a group. Specialization, individual efficiency and collective productivity are separate quantities requiring separate measurements.

### Age polyethism

↑ **Parent:** [Division of labour](#division-of-labour)

Age-associated changes in the tasks performed by social-group members. Social and physiological regulation can make the sequence plastic rather than strictly chronological.

## Behavioural response threshold

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

The stimulus level at which an individual becomes likely to perform an action. Differences in thresholds can generate task allocation when actions change the stimuli confronting other group members.

## Social role

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

A recurring contribution or position occupied by a group member in a particular social setting. A stable role can arise through intrinsic differences, state, partner responses or their interaction.

### Social niche specialization

↑ **Parent:** [Social role](#social-role)

Persistent differentiation of social roles through interactions between an individual’s behaviour and the responses of its partners. Such differentiation need not be genetically fixed.

## Animal personality

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Repeatable differences in behaviour among individuals, assessed after accounting for relevant contexts and states. Repeatability does not by itself establish heritability or an adaptive function.

### Behavioural repeatability

↑ **Parent:** [Animal personality](#animal-personality)

The fraction of appropriately modelled phenotypic variance associated with among-individual differences across repeated observations. Estimates depend on the timescale, measured setting and covariates.

### Behavioural syndrome

↑ **Parent:** [Animal personality](#animal-personality)

An association among behavioural tendencies across different contexts, distinct from repeatability of one measured behaviour.

## Sensory exploitation

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Use of an existing sensory response to obtain a benefit, including responses maintained by functions outside the exploiting interaction.

## Behavioural manipulation

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Alteration of another organism’s behaviour in a way potentially favouring the manipulator. Demonstrating an adaptive manipulation requires distinguishing its fitness consequences from pathology, host defence and other effects.

## Deceptive signalling

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Signalling that exploits a receiver response in circumstances where that response does not provide its normally associated benefit, while benefiting the signaller. Conscious intent is not required by the evolutionary definition.

### Aggressive mimicry

↑ **Parent:** [Deceptive signalling](#deceptive-signalling)

Mimicry by an exploiter that attracts or otherwise misdirects a potential victim, for example by resembling a resource or reproductive opportunity.

## Coordinated behaviour

↑ **Parent:** [Animal behaviour](animal-behaviour.md)

Adjustment of the timing, location or type of actions in response to other individuals. Coordination can supply immediate benefits without requiring evolutionary [altruism](ecology.md#altruism).

### Quorum response

↑ **Parent:** [Coordinated behaviour](#coordinated-behaviour)

A nonlinear increase in commitment to an action as the number of other individuals performing it increases. The effective threshold can depend on group size, competing actions and environmental risk.

### Animal leadership

↑ **Parent:** [Coordinated behaviour](#coordinated-behaviour)

Consistent influence on the initiation or direction of group action. Leadership may arise from differences in motivation and information together with followers’ responses.
