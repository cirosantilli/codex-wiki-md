# Black hole

↑ **Parent:** [General relativity](general-relativity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole)

In an asymptotically predictable spacetime, a black hole is the region that cannot send a causal signal to future null infinity. Its future boundary is the event horizon.

## Primordial black hole

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)

A [black hole](black-hole.md) formed in the early universe rather than as the endpoint of a star. It is not subject to the ordinary stellar-remnant [baryon](physics.md#baryon) budget in the same way as a [black hole](black-hole.md) formed from a baryonic progenitor. Formation scenarios and observational constraints depend strongly on its [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass).

## Kerr-Newman metric

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerr–Newman_metric)

The stationary axisymmetric Einstein–Maxwell family of [asymptotically flat spacetimes](general-relativity.md#asymptotically-flat-spacetime) extends the [Kerr metric](general-relativity.md#kerr-metric) by including [electric charge](electric-charge.md). Its horizon polynomial is the displayed $\Delta$; for $M^2>Q^2+a^2$ the roots are $r_\pm=M\pm\sqrt{M^2-Q^2-a^2}$. The outer [Killing horizon](general-relativity.md#killing-horizon) has area $4\pi(r_+^2+a^2)$, angular velocity $a/(r_+^2+a^2)$ and electric potential $Qr_+/(r_+^2+a^2)$.

### First law for the Kerr-Newman family

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr-Newman metric](#kerr-newman-metric)

Differentiate $M=(r_+^2+a^2+Q^2)/(2r_+)$, $J=Ma$ and $A=4\pi(r_+^2+a^2)$. Eliminating $dr_+$ and $da$ gives the displayed relation, with $\Omega_H=a/(r_+^2+a^2)$ and $\Phi_H=Qr_+/(r_+^2+a^2)$. Matching [Hawking temperature](#hawking-temperature) to this [First law of black-hole mechanics](#first-law-of-black-hole-mechanics) fixes the [Bekenstein-Hawking entropy](#bekenstein-hawking-entropy) as $A/4$.

### Kerr-Newman horizon temperature

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr-Newman metric](#kerr-newman-metric)

The outer-horizon [surface gravity](general-relativity.md#surface-gravity) is $(r_+-r_-)/(2(r_+^2+a^2))$. [Hawking radiation](#hawking-radiation) or nondegenerate [Euclidean black-hole regularity condition](#euclidean-black-hole-regularity-condition) gives $T_H=\kappa/(2\pi)$ in units $G=\hbar=c=k_B=1$. The zero-temperature extremal value is the nonextremal limit; conical regularity alone does not fix a Euclidean period at a degenerate horizon.

## Oppenheimer-Snyder model

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer–Snyder_model)

The [Oppenheimer-Snyder model](#oppenheimer-snyder-model) describes a homogeneous ball of [pressureless matter](cosmology.md#pressureless-matter) collapsing from rest, with a closed [FLRW metric](cosmology.md#friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric) inside and a [Schwarzschild metric](schwarzschild-spacetime.md) outside. In geometric units its interior is $ds^2=-d\tau^2+a^2(d\chi^2+\sin^2\chi\,d\Omega^2)$, with $a=a_{\max}(1+\cos\eta)/2$ and $\tau=a_{\max}(\eta+\sin\eta)/2$. The boundary is a fixed comoving angle. [Density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) conservation gives $\rho a^3$ constant, and the [Friedmann equation](cosmology.md#friedmann-equations) at the initial turning point gives $8\pi\rho_0a_{\max}^2/3=1$.

### Oppenheimer-Snyder cloud

↑ **Parent:** [Oppenheimer-Snyder model](#oppenheimer-snyder-model)

The comoving dust ball $0\le\chi\le\chi_0<\pi/2$ in the [Oppenheimer-Snyder model](#oppenheimer-snyder-model) is an Oppenheimer-Snyder cloud. Its [pressureless matter](cosmology.md#pressureless-matter) has uniform [density](fluid-mechanics.md#density) on each constant-[proper time](special-relativity.md#proper-time) slice and reaches a simultaneous final singularity in the ideal homogeneous model.

### Event horizon inside an Oppenheimer-Snyder cloud

↑ **Parent:** [Oppenheimer-Snyder model](#oppenheimer-snyder-model)

Radial outgoing [null geodesics](special-relativity.md#null-geodesic) in the interior of [Oppenheimer-Snyder collapse](#oppenheimer-snyder-model) obey $d\chi/d\eta=1$. Trace the ray crossing the boundary at $(\eta_H,\chi_0)$ backward: it starts at the centre at $\eta=\eta_H-\chi_0$. This ray is the [event horizon](#event-horizon); it can exist inside the cloud before the surface crosses $R=2M$. An [event horizon](#event-horizon) is determined by global escape to [future null infinity](general-relativity.md#future-null-infinity), rather than by a local stopwatch signal.

### Proper-time horizon crossing in homogeneous dust collapse

↑ **Parent:** [Oppenheimer-Snyder model](#oppenheimer-snyder-model)

A boundary clock in [Oppenheimer-Snyder collapse](#oppenheimer-snyder-model) reads $\tau=a_{\max}(\eta+\sin\eta)/2$. Its [event horizon](#event-horizon) crossing occurs at $R=2M$, giving the displayed angle, and the final [curvature singularity](general-relativity.md#curvature-singularity) occurs at $\eta=\pi$. The remaining [proper time](special-relativity.md#proper-time) is $a_{\max}(\pi-\eta_H-\sin\eta_H)/2$. For $R_0\gg2M$ this approaches $4M/3$, but it is not exactly that value for finite release radius.

### Homogeneous dust-ball matching

↑ **Parent:** [Oppenheimer-Snyder model](#oppenheimer-snyder-model)

At the boundary of an [Oppenheimer-Snyder cloud](#oppenheimer-snyder-cloud), its [areal radius](general-relativity.md#areal-radius) is $R=a\sin\chi_0$. Matching to a [Schwarzschild metric](schwarzschild-spacetime.md) gives $M=4\pi\rho R^3/3=a_{\max}\sin^3\chi_0/2$. Thus $\sin^2\chi_0=2M/R_0$ and $a_{\max}=\sqrt{R_0^3/(2M)}$. The boundary is a radial [timelike geodesic](general-relativity.md#timelike-geodesic) with specific [Killing energy](killing-vector-field.md#killing-energy) $\sqrt{1-2M/R_0}=\cos\chi_0$. Its radial equation is $\dot R^2=2M/R-2M/R_0$. The induced metric and the angular [extrinsic curvature](smooth-manifold.md#extrinsic-curvature) agree on both sides, so no surface layer is required.

## Schwarzschild-Tangherlini metric

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)

For $D\geq4$, the static spherical vacuum metric $ds^2=-fdt^2+f^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega_{D-2}^2$ has an [event horizon](#event-horizon) at $r_h=\mu^{1/(D-3)}$. With time normalized at infinity its [ADM mass](general-relativity.md#arnowitt-deser-misner-mass) is $M=(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}\mu/(16\pi G_D)$, where $\Omega_{D-2}$ is the unit-sphere area. For $D=5$ and $\mu=2m$, $r_h=\sqrt{2m}$ and $M=3\pi m/(4G_5)$. The spherical orbits are three-spheres, even though the radial-time [Penrose diagram](#penrose-diagram) has the same causal pattern as its four-dimensional counterpart.

### Kruskal extension of the five-dimensional Schwarzschild-Tangherlini metric

↑ **Parent:** [Schwarzschild-Tangherlini metric](#schwarzschild-tangherlini-metric)

The [tortoise coordinate](#tortoise-coordinate) is $r_*=r+(r_h/2)\log|(r-r_h)/(r+r_h)|$. Set $u=t-r_*$ and $v=t+r_*$, then $U=-e^{-u/r_h}$ and $V=e^{v/r_h}$ in the right exterior. The extended radial metric is $-r_h^2(r+r_h)^2e^{-2r/r_h}dU\,dV/r^2$, regular at $r=r_h$. The horizons are $UV=0$, while $r=0$ gives $UV=1$ and is a spacelike curvature singularity. Compactification by $\arctan U,\arctan V$ displays two exteriors, a future [black hole](black-hole.md) and a past [white hole](#white-hole).

## Penrose inequality

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)

The [Penrose inequality](#penrose-inequality) compares asymptotic energy and an appropriate horizon or enclosing area in [geometrized units](general-relativity.md#geometrized-units). Its physical motivation uses censorship, positive radiated energy, settling to a [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole), and [Hawking's area theorem](#hawking-s-area-theorem). The area variable requires care: the area of an arbitrary [apparent horizon](general-relativity.md#apparent-horizon) on non-time-symmetric data does not give a universally valid inequality. The [Riemannian Penrose inequality](#riemannian-penrose-inequality) uses the relevant outermost [minimal surface](second-fundamental-form.md#minimal-surface) under nonnegative [scalar curvature](second-fundamental-form.md#scalar-curvature).

### Riemannian Penrose inequality

↑ **Parent:** [Penrose inequality](#penrose-inequality)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_Penrose_inequality)

For complete asymptotically flat three-dimensional Riemannian initial data with nonnegative [scalar curvature](second-fundamental-form.md#scalar-curvature) and an outermost compact [minimal surface](second-fundamental-form.md#minimal-surface) boundary of area $A$, the [Riemannian Penrose inequality](#riemannian-penrose-inequality) gives the displayed bound. Equality is realized by the spatial exterior of [Schwarzschild spacetime](schwarzschild-spacetime.md). Time-symmetric gravitational initial data have this form when the [Einstein field equations](einstein-field-equations.md) and energy hypothesis give the required nonnegative curvature. This theorem does not justify substituting arbitrary non-time-symmetric [apparent horizon](general-relativity.md#apparent-horizon) area into the same formula.

### Apparent-horizon area comparison

↑ **Parent:** [Penrose inequality](#penrose-inequality)

The heuristic derivation of the [Penrose inequality](#penrose-inequality) from [Hawking's area theorem](#hawking-s-area-theorem) needs an initial area comparison, in addition to enclosure of the [apparent horizon](general-relativity.md#apparent-horizon) by the [event horizon](#event-horizon). Set inclusion alone is not an area inequality. An [outer area-minimizing surface](second-fundamental-form.md#outer-area-minimizing-surface) or appropriate enclosing-area hypotheses supply the needed comparison in formulations where it is valid; arbitrary slice-dependent apparent-horizon area need not do so.

## Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)

The four-dimensional charged spherically symmetric electrovacuum solution has radial function $f=1-2M/r+Q^2/r^2$. For $0<|Q|<M$, its outer and inner horizon radii are $r_\pm=M\pm\sqrt{M^2-Q^2}$. A complete two-ended spatial bridge has a [maximal Cauchy development](general-relativity.md#maximal-cauchy-development) ending at smoothly extendible inner [Cauchy horizons](general-relativity.md#cauchy-horizon) in the exact solution.

### Reissner-Nordstrom Hawking temperature

↑ **Parent:** [Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime](#reissner-nordstrom-spacetime)

For $f=(r-r_+)(r-r_-)/r^2$, near-horizon Euclidean geometry has polar form $d\rho^2+[f'(r_+)/2]^2\rho^2d\tau^2$. Smoothness gives inverse [temperature](temperature.md) $4\pi/f'(r_+)$ and the displayed formula. This assumes a nondegenerate [Killing horizon](general-relativity.md#killing-horizon) and time normalized at infinity. Its nonextremal limit is zero at $M=|Q|$.

#### Charge-preserving Reissner-Nordstrom evaporation endpoint

↑ **Parent:** [Reissner-Nordstrom Hawking temperature](#reissner-nordstrom-hawking-temperature)

In the ideal thermal-cutoff model where charged particles of [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) $m$ are emitted only for $T>m$, a sufficiently large initial [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) keeps charge fixed throughout evaporation exactly when $m$ is at least the [fixed-charge Reissner-Nordstrom temperature maximum](#fixed-charge-reissner-nordstrom-temperature-maximum). Neutral radiation then approaches an extremal charged remnant of area $4\pi Q^2$. This conclusion does not include nonthermal electric-field discharge, nor establish finite-time attainment.

#### Fixed-charge Reissner-Nordstrom temperature maximum

↑ **Parent:** [Reissner-Nordstrom Hawking temperature](#reissner-nordstrom-hawking-temperature)

Set $y=r_+/|Q|\geq1$. Then $M/|Q|=(y+y^{-1})/2$ and $|Q|T=(y^{-1}-y^{-3})/(4\pi)$. Differentiation gives a unique maximum at $y=\sqrt3$, proving the displayed [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) and [temperature](temperature.md). The curve is zero at extremality and asymptotic to $1/(8\pi M)$ at large [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass).

### Extremal Reissner-Nordstrom causal crossing

↑ **Parent:** [Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime](#reissner-nordstrom-spacetime)

A neutral radial timelike geodesic of an [extremal black hole](#extremal-black-hole) has conserved specific energy $E=(1-M/r)^2\dot t$, giving the displayed radial equation. An infalling geodesic reaches $r=M$ with finite nonzero $\dot r=-E$ and finite proper time. In ingoing coordinates, outgoing radial null curves have $dr/dv=(1-M/r)^2/2$; those starting inside approach $r=M$ only at infinite $v$ and cannot return to the same exterior future null infinity. This distinguishes causal access from length on a static slice, and supplies an operational [event horizon](#event-horizon) test even without strictly trapped round spheres.

### Extremal Reissner-Nordstrom isotropic coordinates

↑ **Parent:** [Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime](#reissner-nordstrom-spacetime)

For $M=|Q|>0$ and the exterior areal radius $r>M$, integrate $d\rho/\rho=dr/(r-M)$ and choose $\rho=r-M$. The [Reissner-Nordstrom metric](#reissner-nordstrom-spacetime) becomes the displayed form. Constant-time proper distance to $\rho=0$ diverges, but these exterior coordinates do not cover the horizon; ingoing null coordinates are regular there. Infinite spatial throat length therefore does not establish absence of an [event horizon](#event-horizon).

### Reissner-Nordstrom horizon surface gravity

↑ **Parent:** [Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime](#reissner-nordstrom-spacetime)

For $M>0$ and $M\geq|Q|$, put $r_\pm=M\pm\sqrt{M^2-Q^2}$. The stationary [Killing vector field](killing-vector-field.md) normalized at infinity generates the outer [Killing horizon](general-relativity.md#killing-horizon). In an ingoing null chart its covector is $dr$ on that horizon; the [Killing equation](killing-vector-field.md#killing-equation) then gives $\nabla_k k=\tfrac12 f'(r_+)k$, where $f=(r-r_+)(r-r_-)/r^2$. Thus the positive nonextremal [surface gravity](general-relativity.md#surface-gravity) is $(r_+-r_-)/(2r_+^2)$. It tends to zero when $M=|Q|$, where the horizon becomes a [degenerate Killing horizon](general-relativity.md#degenerate-killing-horizon). The past horizon branch has the opposite signed nonaffinity for the same time-translation field.

### Global structure of a charged spherical black hole

↑ **Parent:** [Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime](#reissner-nordstrom-spacetime)

For $0<|Q|<M$, a [Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime](#reissner-nordstrom-spacetime) has an outer [event horizon](#event-horizon) and an inner [Cauchy horizon](general-relativity.md#cauchy-horizon). Between them $r$ is timelike; inside the inner horizon $r$ is spacelike and the $r=0$ [curvature singularity](general-relativity.md#curvature-singularity) is timelike. The exact analytic extension contains repeated exterior blocks beyond the [Cauchy horizons](general-relativity.md#cauchy-horizon). Small perturbations produce severe inner-horizon blueshift and [mass inflation](general-relativity.md#mass-inflation), so that exact continuation is not a robust prediction for collapse. At $|Q|=M$ the horizons merge into a [degenerate Killing horizon](general-relativity.md#degenerate-killing-horizon); for $|Q|>M$ the spherical electrovacuum solution instead has a [naked singularity](#naked-singularity).

### Reissner-Nordstrom trapped spheres

↑ **Parent:** [Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime](#reissner-nordstrom-spacetime)

In ingoing [Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime](#reissner-nordstrom-spacetime), orient $-\partial_r$ to the future and normalize the other radial [null vector](special-relativity.md#null-vector) to $\partial_v+(f/2)\partial_r$. The [spherical null expansion in ingoing coordinates](geodesic-congruence.md#spherical-null-expansion-in-ingoing-coordinates) gives $-2/r$ and $f/r$. Hence the strictly future [trapped surfaces](general-relativity.md#trapped-surface) among the round spheres occur exactly between the distinct horizons. At either horizon the outgoing [null expansion](geodesic-congruence.md#null-expansion) vanishes. Inside the inner horizon the same round spheres are not future trapped.

## White hole

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)

A white hole is the time reverse of a [black hole](black-hole.md): causal trajectories can emerge from its past singularity, while exterior future-directed trajectories cannot enter through its past horizon. The maximal [Schwarzschild spacetime](schwarzschild-spacetime.md) contains both a future black-hole interior and a past white-hole interior. Both have the same areal-radius range, illustrating why a global time orientation and choice of extension are essential in causal statements.

## Black-hole uniqueness theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)

In four-dimensional asymptotically flat [general relativity](general-relativity.md), a regular stationary vacuum black hole is a [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole) under the standard connectedness and analyticity hypotheses. The electrovacuum extension is the Kerr--Newman family.

### Black-hole rigidity theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole uniqueness theorem](#black-hole-uniqueness-theorem)

For a regular analytic stationary asymptotically flat vacuum black hole with a connected nondegenerate horizon, the stationary [Killing vector field](killing-vector-field.md) either generates the horizon or the spacetime admits an additional commuting axial [Killing vector field](killing-vector-field.md). In the rotating case the horizon generator is $K+\Omega_H\Phi$, where $\Phi$ has closed rotational orbits. Together with the appropriate staticity and [black-hole uniqueness theorem](#black-hole-uniqueness-theorem), this identifies an equilibrium uncharged collapse remnant with the [Kerr metric](general-relativity.md#kerr-metric). The theorem concerns an equilibrium geometry; it does not itself prove that arbitrary dynamical collapse settles to equilibrium.

### Black-hole no-hair theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole uniqueness theorem](#black-hole-uniqueness-theorem)

The black-hole no-hair principle says that radiation carries away the higher multipole structure of a collapsing isolated body, leaving a stationary black hole specified by conserved asymptotic charges such as [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass), [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum), and [electric charge](electric-charge.md).

## Extremal black hole

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremal_black_hole)

An extremal black hole lies at the parameter bound where its inner and outer horizons coincide. Its [surface gravity](general-relativity.md#surface-gravity) and hence its semiclassical [Hawking temperature](#hawking-temperature) vanish.

### Extremal Euclidean throat

↑ **Parent:** [Extremal black hole](#extremal-black-hole)

At a degenerate static horizon the Euclidean proper radial distance diverges logarithmically, so the horizon is an infinite end rather than a finite polar origin. Its time circle is not contracted at a conical tip, and local cone regularity imposes no finite period. The vanishing limiting [surface gravity](general-relativity.md#surface-gravity) determines zero Hawking temperature; the simple-horizon periodicity argument must not be applied unchanged at the degenerate root.

## Naked singularity

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naked_singularity)

A naked singularity is a spacetime singularity visible to distant observers because it is not hidden behind an [event horizon](#event-horizon).

### Weak cosmic censorship conjecture

↑ **Parent:** [Naked singularity](#naked-singularity)

The [weak cosmic censorship conjecture](#weak-cosmic-censorship-conjecture) predicts, under appropriate generic isolated initial-data and matter assumptions, that collapse singularities are hidden from distant observers by an [event horizon](#event-horizon). It motivates exterior predictability in the physical argument for the [Penrose inequality](#penrose-inequality). It is distinct from the [strong cosmic censorship conjecture](general-relativity.md#strong-cosmic-censorship-conjecture), which concerns inextendibility of the [maximal Cauchy development](general-relativity.md#maximal-cauchy-development).

<h2 id="five-dimensional-reissner-nordstrom-black-hole">Five-dimensional Reissner--Nordstrom black hole</h2>

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)

The static spherically symmetric electrically charged black hole in five spacetime dimensions has $f(r)=1-2M/r^2+Q^2/r^4$. When $M>Q$ it has outer and inner horizon radii $r_\pm^2=M\pm\sqrt{M^2-Q^2}$; for $M=Q$ it is an [extremal black hole](#extremal-black-hole), while $M<Q$ exposes a [naked singularity](#naked-singularity).

### Kretschmann scalar of a five-dimensional charged black hole

↑ **Parent:** [Five-dimensional Reissner--Nordstrom black hole](#five-dimensional-reissner-nordstrom-black-hole)

For $V=1-2M/r^2+Q^2/r^4$, an orthonormal-frame curvature calculation gives $(V'')^2+6(V'/r)^2+12((1-V)/r^2)^2$. Expansion gives the displayed invariant, diverging at zero radius. It is finite at every positive root of V. Thus horizon coordinate singularities are distinct from the central [curvature singularity](general-relativity.md#curvature-singularity).

### Causal trapping between two spherical horizons

↑ **Parent:** [Five-dimensional Reissner--Nordstrom black hole](#five-dimensional-reissner-nordstrom-black-hole)

In the regular metric $ds^2=-f\,dv^2+2\,dv\,dr+r^2d\Omega^2$, a future causal tangent has $\dot v\geq0$. For $\dot v>0$, causality gives $\dot r\leq f\dot v/2-r^2|\dot\Omega|^2/(2\dot v)$. If $f<0$ between two horizons, the areal radius must decrease. An inner event cannot reach the original exterior [future null infinity](general-relativity.md#future-null-infinity) without traversing that band in the forbidden direction.

### Tortoise coordinate of a five-dimensional charged black hole

↑ **Parent:** [Five-dimensional Reissner--Nordstrom black hole](#five-dimensional-reissner-nordstrom-black-hole)

For distinct horizon radii $a>b>0$, integrating $dr_\star/dr=f^{-1}$ gives $r_\star=r+\frac{a^3}{2(a^2-b^2)}\log|(r-a)/(r+a)|-\frac{b^3}{2(a^2-b^2)}\log|(r-b)/(r+b)|+C$. The [tortoise coordinate](#tortoise-coordinate) defines constant retarded or advanced coordinates on radial [null geodesics](special-relativity.md#null-geodesic).

<h3 id="komar-integral-of-a-five-dimensional-reissner-nordstrom-metric">Komar integral of a five-dimensional Reissner--Nordstrom metric</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Five-dimensional Reissner--Nordstrom black hole](#five-dimensional-reissner-nordstrom-black-hole)

For $f=1-s/r^2+q/r^4$ and the spacetime orientation $-dt\wedge dr\wedge d\Omega_3$, the [Hodge star operator](differential-form.md#hodge-star-operator) gives $\int_{S^3}\star dk^\flat=2\pi^2r^3f^{\prime}\to4\pi^2s$. A prefactor $1/(16\pi)$ produces $\pi s/4$, whereas the dimension-adjusted ADM mass normalization produces $3\pi s/8$. The normalization and orientation must be specified rather than imported from four dimensions.

## Event horizon

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Event_horizon)

An event horizon is the null boundary of the causal past of future null infinity. Its global definition makes it respond teleologically to matter that falls in later.

### Horizon-free Minkowski spacetime

↑ **Parent:** [Event horizon](#event-horizon)

Every event in complete [Minkowski spacetime](special-relativity.md#minkowski-spacetime) emits a future-directed [null geodesic](special-relativity.md#null-geodesic) ending on [future null infinity](general-relativity.md#future-null-infinity). Therefore its black-hole region $M\setminus J^-(\mathcal I^+)$ is empty and there is no intrinsic event horizon. The null edges of the [Minkowski conformal compactification](general-relativity.md#minkowski-conformal-compactification) represent infinity, not hidden regions inside the physical spacetime. An accelerated observer can instead have an observer-dependent [Rindler horizon](special-relativity.md#rindler-horizon).

## Strong asymptotic predictability

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)

Strong asymptotic predictability requires the exterior region visible from future null infinity to lie inside a globally hyperbolic neighborhood of the conformal completion. It is the cosmic-censorship-type hypothesis used in classical black-hole area and topology arguments.

## Black-hole non-splitting theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)

In a strongly asymptotically predictable spacetime, the causal future on a later Cauchy surface of one connected component of an earlier black-hole region lies inside one connected component of the later black-hole region. A classical black hole can merge with another component, but it cannot bifurcate into causally disconnected black holes.

## Myers-Perry black hole

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myers-Perry_black_hole)

A Myers-Perry black hole is the higher-dimensional asymptotically flat rotating vacuum generalization of the [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole). In six dimensions, a solution with one nonzero rotation parameter $a$ and horizon radius $r_+$ has mass parameter $\mu=r_+(r_+^2+a^2)$.

### Five-dimensional equal-spin Myers-Perry black hole

↑ **Parent:** [Myers-Perry black hole](#myers-perry-black-hole)

A five-dimensional [Myers-Perry black hole](#myers-perry-black-hole) with equal rotational parameters has a metric depending on a single radial coordinate. In a fibered coordinate system, its radial functions can be written $f=1-r_0^2/r^2+\alpha^2r_0^2/r^4$, $h=1+\alpha^2r_0^2/r^4$, and $\Omega=\alpha r_0^2/(r^4h)$. For $0<\alpha<r_0/2$ there are outer and inner [Killing horizons](general-relativity.md#killing-horizon); at equality they form a [degenerate Killing horizon](general-relativity.md#degenerate-killing-horizon). With the standard sphere identifications and $G_5=1$, its [Komar mass](killing-vector-field.md#komar-mass) is $3\pi r_0^2/8$.

### Singly rotating six-dimensional Myers-Perry black hole

↑ **Parent:** [Myers-Perry black hole](#myers-perry-black-hole)

For the singly rotating six-dimensional Myers-Perry solution with $G=1$ and $\mu=r_+(r_+^2+a^2)$,

$$
M=\frac{2\pi}{3}\mu,
\qquad
\Omega_H=\frac{a}{r_+^2+a^2},
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{3r_+^2+a^2}{2r_+(r_+^2+a^2)},
$$

and the horizon area is

$$
A_H=\frac{8\pi^2}{3}r_+^2(r_+^2+a^2).
$$

## BTZ black hole

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTZ_black_hole)

The BTZ black hole is a quotient of three-dimensional [Anti-de Sitter spacetime](general-relativity.md#anti-de-sitter-spacetime). In lapse-and-shift coordinates its outer and inner horizon radii are $r_+$ and $r_-$, and its outer-horizon surface gravity is

$$
\kappa=\frac{r_+^2-r_-^2}{L^2r_+}.
$$

### Conical singularity of negative-mass BTZ geometry

↑ **Parent:** [BTZ black hole](#btz-black-hole)

For the nonrotating [BTZ black hole](#btz-black-hole) family with $M<0$ and angular period $2\pi$, the circumference of a small circle divided by its proper radius tends to $2\pi\sqrt{-M}$. A smooth axis requires this ratio to be $2\pi$, so only $M=-1$ gives regular [Anti-de Sitter spacetime](general-relativity.md#anti-de-sitter-spacetime). Other values have a [conical singularity](riemannian-geometry.md#conical-singularity), with deficit angle $\delta$ or an angular excess when $\delta<0$. The lapse $r^2/\ell^2-M$ is strictly positive, so outward [radial null geodesics](special-relativity.md#radial-null-geodesic) escape from arbitrarily near the axis: the defect is a [naked singularity](#naked-singularity), despite finite local curvature away from it.

### Ingoing BTZ coordinates

↑ **Parent:** [BTZ black hole](#btz-black-hole)

For

$$
ds^2=-fdt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f}+r^2(d\phi-\Omega dt)^2,
$$

the transformations $dv=dt+dr/f$ and $d\chi=d\phi+\Omega\,dr/f$ give

$$
ds^2=-f\,dv^2+2\,dv\,dr+r^2(d\chi-\Omega\,dv)^2,
$$

which is regular at every horizon where $f$ vanishes while $\Omega$ remains finite.

### Trapped circle inside a nonrotating BTZ black hole

↑ **Parent:** [BTZ black hole](#btz-black-hole)

For the nonrotating BTZ metric in ingoing coordinates, future null normals to a circle of fixed $v,r$ may be chosen as $\ell=\partial_v+(f/2)\partial_r$ and $n=-\partial_r$. Their expansions are $\theta_{(\ell)}=f/(2r)$ and $\theta_{(n)}=-1/r$, so both are negative inside the outer horizon.

### BTZ causal singularity

↑ **Parent:** [BTZ black hole](#btz-black-hole)

The BTZ black hole can be null-geodesically incomplete at $r=0$ even though every local curvature invariant is finite there. The endpoint comes from the global anti-de Sitter quotient rather than curvature blowup.

<h2 id="schwarzschild-anti-de-sitter-black-hole">Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter black hole</h2>

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)

In four dimensions the Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter black hole has

$$
f(r)=1+\frac{r^2}{L^2}-\frac{2M}{r}.
$$

Writing the horizon radius as $r_+$ gives $M=r_+(1+r_+^2/L^2)/2$ and

$$
T_H=\frac{1+3r_+^2/L^2}{4\pi r_+}.
$$

<h3 id="five-dimensional-schwarzschild-anti-de-sitter-black-hole">Five-dimensional Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter black hole</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter black hole](#schwarzschild-anti-de-sitter-black-hole)

The spherical five-dimensional Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter metric has $f(r)=1+r^2/L^2-\mu/r^2$. Its horizon satisfies $\mu=r_H^2(1+r_H^2/L^2)$ and has area $2\pi^2r_H^3$.

<h3 id="canonical-stability-of-a-schwarzschild-anti-de-sitter-black-hole">Canonical stability of a Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter black hole</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter black hole](#schwarzschild-anti-de-sitter-black-hole)

The Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter heat capacity has the sign of $dT_H/dr_+$. It is positive precisely for $r_+>L/\sqrt3$, so these large black holes are locally stable in a fixed-temperature reservoir.

## Black string

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_string)

A black string is a higher-dimensional black object whose horizon extends along one spatial direction. The direct product of a $d$-dimensional static black-hole metric with a line or circle gives a uniform black string in $d+1$ spacetime dimensions.

## Penrose diagram

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_diagram)

A Penrose diagram conformally compactifies a spacetime while preserving causal directions, placing null infinity, timelike infinity, spacelike infinity, horizons, and singularities at finite coordinate locations.

### Schwarzschild conformal compactification

↑ **Parent:** [Penrose diagram](#penrose-diagram)

For the [Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates](schwarzschild-spacetime.md#kruskal-szekeres-coordinates) of a positive-mass [Schwarzschild spacetime](schwarzschild-spacetime.md), $UV=(1-r/(2M))e^{r/(2M)}$. The displayed [conformal compactification](geometry-and-topology.md#conformal-compactification) makes radial [null geodesics](special-relativity.md#null-geodesic) lines of slope $\pm1$. The [Killing horizons](general-relativity.md#killing-horizon) are $T=\pm X$, and the spacelike [curvature singularities](general-relativity.md#curvature-singularity) $r=0$ lie at $T=\pm\pi/4$, with $|X|<\pi/4$. Two asymptotically flat exterior regions each have [past null infinity](#past-null-infinity), [null infinity](#null-infinity) to the future, [timelike infinity](#timelike-infinity) and [spacelike infinity](#spacelike-infinity). The two exterior regions cannot communicate by any [causal curve](general-relativity.md#causal-curve); future-directed curves that cross a future horizon instead end at the black-hole singularity.

### Spacelike infinity

↑ **Parent:** [Penrose diagram](#penrose-diagram)

[Spacelike infinity](#spacelike-infinity) is the ideal endpoint of spatial directions in the [Minkowski conformal compactification](general-relativity.md#minkowski-conformal-compactification), represented by $(T,R)=(0,\pi)$. Unlike [null infinity](#null-infinity), it is a vertex of the radial [Penrose diagram](#penrose-diagram).

### Timelike infinity

↑ **Parent:** [Penrose diagram](#penrose-diagram)

Future and past [timelike infinity](#timelike-infinity) are ideal endpoints of asymptotically escaping timelike trajectories in a [conformal completion](general-relativity.md#conformal-completion). For the [Minkowski conformal compactification](general-relativity.md#minkowski-conformal-compactification), they are the vertices $(T,R)=(\pi,0)$ and $(-\pi,0)$; these vertices are not physical spacetime events.

#### Past timelike infinity

↑ **Parent:** [Timelike infinity](#timelike-infinity)

This is the past endpoint of complete inertial [timelike geodesics](general-relativity.md#timelike-geodesic) in the [Minkowski conformal compactification](general-relativity.md#minkowski-conformal-compactification), at $(T,R)=(-\pi,0)$. It is the time reversal of [future timelike infinity](#future-timelike-infinity) and is reached at infinite physical [proper time](special-relativity.md#proper-time) to the past.

#### Future timelike infinity

↑ **Parent:** [Timelike infinity](#timelike-infinity)

This is the future endpoint of complete inertial [timelike geodesics](general-relativity.md#timelike-geodesic) in the [Minkowski conformal compactification](general-relativity.md#minkowski-conformal-compactification), at $(T,R)=(\pi,0)$. The endpoint has finite unphysical coordinate time but infinite physical [proper time](special-relativity.md#proper-time). Accelerated timelike observers need not have this endpoint; a [Rindler horizon](special-relativity.md#rindler-horizon) can accompany an observer asymptotic to a null direction instead.

### Null infinity

↑ **Parent:** [Penrose diagram](#penrose-diagram)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_infinity)

[Null infinity](#null-infinity) is the ideal boundary reached by asymptotically escaping [null geodesics](special-relativity.md#null-geodesic) in a suitable conformal completion. It has future and past components. A finite location on a [Penrose diagram](#penrose-diagram) need not represent a finite [affine parameter](riemannian-geometry.md#affine-parameter).

#### Past null infinity

↑ **Parent:** [Null infinity](#null-infinity)

[Past null infinity](#past-null-infinity) is the ideal boundary from which incoming [null geodesics](special-relativity.md#null-geodesic) arrive in an [asymptotically flat spacetime](general-relativity.md#asymptotically-flat-spacetime). In the [Minkowski conformal compactification](general-relativity.md#minkowski-conformal-compactification) it is $T-R=-\pi$, with the retarded coordinate tending to $-\infty$ and the advanced coordinate finite.

### Causal projection along a spacelike circular fiber

↑ **Parent:** [Penrose diagram](#penrose-diagram)

For $g=q+C(d\psi+A)^2$ with $C>0$, projection of every causal curve is causal for $q$, since $q(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)\leq g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)\leq0$. Conversely, the lift defined by $d\psi=-A$ along any given base curve has the same norm as that curve. Thus the orbit-space metric $q$ describes the projected causal structure exactly. A [Penrose diagram](#penrose-diagram) suppressing that circle is a quotient diagram, not the metric of a constant-$\psi$ slice.

### Tortoise coordinate

↑ **Parent:** [Penrose diagram](#penrose-diagram)

A [tortoise coordinate](#tortoise-coordinate) straightens radial null directions. For a static radial metric $-F(r)dt^2+G(r)dr^2$ outside a horizon, choose $dr_*/dr=\sqrt{G/F}$; then $t\pm r_*$ are constant along the corresponding radial [null geodesics](special-relativity.md#null-geodesic). Continuation across horizons requires choosing branches consistently rather than replacing an analytic derivative by its absolute value.

## Black-hole thermodynamics

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-hole_thermodynamics)

Black-hole thermodynamics relates horizon geometry to thermodynamic laws, assigning temperature and entropy to a stationary black hole.

### Black-hole energy extraction

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole thermodynamics](#black-hole-thermodynamics)

Black-hole energy extraction transfers part of a black hole's mass, charge, or rotational energy to its exterior. A reversible limiting process preserves the horizon area and saturates the maximum extraction permitted by the [First law of black-hole mechanics](#first-law-of-black-hole-mechanics) and [Hawking's area theorem](#hawking-s-area-theorem).

### Laws of black-hole mechanics

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole thermodynamics](#black-hole-thermodynamics)

The laws of black-hole mechanics relate [surface gravity](general-relativity.md#surface-gravity), horizon area, mass, angular momentum, and charge in close analogy with the laws of thermodynamics. Quantum theory identifies the analogy physically through [Hawking temperature](#hawking-temperature) and [Bekenstein-Hawking entropy](#bekenstein-hawking-entropy).

### Zeroth law of black-hole mechanics

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole thermodynamics](#black-hole-thermodynamics)

The zeroth law states that the [surface gravity](general-relativity.md#surface-gravity) is constant over a stationary Killing horizon under the standard field-equation and energy assumptions.

### Hawking temperature

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole thermodynamics](#black-hole-thermodynamics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawking_temperature)

In units $G=\hbar=c=k_B=1$, a stationary black hole with surface gravity $\kappa$ has Hawking temperature $T_H=\kappa/(2\pi)$.

#### Euclidean black-hole regularity condition

↑ **Parent:** [Hawking temperature](#hawking-temperature)

Near a nonextremal Euclidean horizon, the radial--time plane is smooth only when Euclidean time has period $\beta_H=4\pi/f'(r_H)$. Any other period produces a conical singularity at the horizon.

##### Euclidean metric near a simple static horizon

↑ **Parent:** [Euclidean black-hole regularity condition](#euclidean-black-hole-regularity-condition)

On the side where V is positive, set $r-r_0=V'(r_0)\rho^2/4$. Its radial Euclidean metric becomes the displayed plane in polar coordinates. The would-be singularity is conical unless the angular variable $|V'(r_0)|\tau/2$ has period exactly $2\pi$. This fixes $\beta=4\pi/|V'(r_0)|$; a nontrivial integer multiple still has a cone excess at the tip.

##### Euclidean temperature and signed horizon surface gravity

↑ **Parent:** [Euclidean black-hole regularity condition](#euclidean-black-hole-regularity-condition)

For a normalized static metric $ds^2=-Vdt^2+dr^2/V+r^2d\Omega^2$ with a simple outer zero at $r_0$, set $b=V'(r_0)>0$. The [Wick rotation](perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#wick-rotation) and $r-r_0=b\rho^2/4$ give $d\rho^2+(b\rho/2)^2dt_E^2$ to leading order, so smoothness fixes period $4\pi/b$. In an ingoing null chart, $\nabla_k k=(b/2)k$ on the future horizon; the outgoing chart gives the opposite sign on the past branch. The [Hawking temperature](#hawking-temperature) is $b/(4\pi)=|\kappa|/(2\pi)$ and uses the time generator normalized at infinity.

##### Euclidean horizon period under a regular radial conformal factor

↑ **Parent:** [Euclidean black-hole regularity condition](#euclidean-black-hole-regularity-condition)

For a Euclidean radial-time metric $C(r)(f(r)dt_E^2+dr^2/f(r))$, assume $f$ has a simple horizon zero and $C$ is smooth, finite and positive there. A proper radial coordinate $\rho=2\sqrt{C(r_H)(r-r_H)/f'(r_H)}$ puts the leading metric in the form $d\rho^2+f'(r_H)^2\rho^2dt_E^2/4$. Smoothness fixes the displayed period independently of $C(r_H)$. When the Lorentzian time is normalized at infinity, the period is the inverse [Hawking temperature](#hawking-temperature) in natural units. A conformal factor singular at the horizon need not preserve this argument.

#### Hawking radiation

↑ **Parent:** [Hawking temperature](#hawking-temperature)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawking_radiation)

Hawking radiation is the approximately thermal flux seen at future infinity when outgoing quantum-field modes are traced backward through a collapsing black-hole geometry. The exponential near-horizon redshift produces nonzero Bogoliubov beta coefficients at temperature $T_H=\kappa/(2\pi)$.

##### Collapse vacuum for Hawking radiation

↑ **Parent:** [Hawking radiation](#hawking-radiation)

In a collapse spacetime, choose the quantum field's incoming state to have no incoming particles at [past null infinity](#past-null-infinity) and to be regular in freely falling coordinates near the forming [event horizon](#event-horizon). The late outgoing modes then have the [Hawking temperature](#hawking-temperature), while there is no incoming thermal bath. This collapse-state boundary condition differs from an equilibrium state on an eternal [black hole](black-hole.md). The [Hawking exponential ray map](#hawking-exponential-ray-map) and the [thermal ratio of Hawking Bogoliubov coefficients](#thermal-ratio-of-hawking-bogoliubov-coefficients) determine the leading late-time particle spectrum; [greybody factors](#greybody-factor) describe scattering outside the [event horizon](#event-horizon).

##### Black-hole evaporation

↑ **Parent:** [Hawking radiation](#hawking-radiation)

[Black-hole evaporation](#black-hole-evaporation) is mass loss through [Hawking radiation](#hawking-radiation). For a large isolated [Schwarzschild black hole](schwarzschild-spacetime.md), the leading mass dependence is $dM/dt\propto-M^{-2}$ and the evaporation timescale is proportional to $M^3$. This semiclassical scaling cannot determine the endpoint when the curvature reaches quantum-gravitational scales.

###### Semiclassical cubic mass law for Schwarzschild evaporation

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole evaporation](#black-hole-evaporation)

For a large isolated [Schwarzschild black hole](schwarzschild-spacetime.md) with a fixed effective set of radiated species, [Hawking radiation](#hawking-radiation) has $T\propto M^{-1}$ and luminosity $P=\alpha/M^2$ in natural units. Integrating $dM/dt=-\alpha/M^2$ gives the displayed law. A horizon-area [blackbody radiation](statistical-physics.md#black-body-radiation) estimate gives $\alpha=1/(15360\pi)$; [greybody factors](#greybody-factor) and species change it. With mass-dependent $\alpha$, integrate $t=\int_{M(t)}^{M_0}m^2\,dm/\alpha(m)$ instead. The [semiclassical gravity](quantum-theory.md#semiclassical-gravity) approximation fails near [Planck mass](mathematical-physics.md#planck-mass), so a formal zero of the cubic law does not settle the final state.

###### Stefan-Boltzmann estimate of Schwarzschild evaporation time

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole evaporation](#black-hole-evaporation)

Approximate an isolated [Schwarzschild black hole](schwarzschild-spacetime.md) as emitting photon [blackbody radiation](statistical-physics.md#black-body-radiation) from an area $16\pi G^2M^2/c^4$ at [Hawking temperature](#hawking-temperature) $\hbar c^3/(8\pi Gk_BM)$. The [Stefan–Boltzmann law](heat.md#stefan-boltzmann-law) gives $P=\hbar c^6/(15360\pi G^2M^2)$. Integrating $c^2dM/dt=-P$ gives the displayed lifetime, about $2\times10^{67}$ years for a solar mass. [Greybody factors](#greybody-factor) and additional emitted species modify the coefficient; the robust large-mass scaling is $M_0^3$, and the semiclassical calculation does not determine the final quantum-gravity endpoint.

##### Hawking exponential ray map

↑ **Parent:** [Hawking radiation](#hawking-radiation)

The late outgoing rays from a nonextremal collapsing [black hole](black-hole.md) have an exponential relation between a regular early affine null coordinate $U$ and late retarded time $u$. Logarithmic divergence of the [tortoise coordinate](#tortoise-coordinate) at the horizon gives $U_H-U=Ae^{-\kappa u}$ up to subleading terms. Backward propagation turns $e^{-i\omega u}$ into $(U_H-U)^{i\omega/\kappa}$; its positive- and negative-frequency Fourier parts give a thermal [Bogoliubov transformation](quantum-field-theory.md#bogoliubov-transformation).

###### Thermal ratio of Hawking Bogoliubov coefficients

↑ **Parent:** [Hawking exponential ray map](#hawking-exponential-ray-map)

For the [Hawking exponential ray map](#hawking-exponential-ray-map), regulated integrals $\int_0^\infty x^{ia}e^{-(\epsilon\pm i\omega')x}\,dx=\Gamma(1+ia)(\epsilon\pm i\omega')^{-1-ia}$ have squared-modulus ratio $e^{-2\pi a}$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$, where $a=\omega/\kappa$. Combining this with the [canonical identities for a bosonic Bogoliubov transformation](quantum-field-theory.md#canonical-identities-for-a-bosonic-bogoliubov-transformation) gives the bosonic occupation $[e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa}-1]^{-1}$. Continuum modes require wave-packet normalization to interpret finite particle counts.

##### Greybody factor

↑ **Parent:** [Hawking radiation](#hawking-radiation)

For a [Schwarzschild black hole](schwarzschild-spacetime.md), a [greybody factor](#greybody-factor) is a mode's transmission probability through the exterior scattering potential. The spectrum of [Hawking radiation](#hawking-radiation) at [future null infinity](general-relativity.md#future-null-infinity) is the thermal horizon occupation multiplied by this factor, with a sum over angular modes.

##### Black hole information paradox

↑ **Parent:** [Hawking radiation](#hawking-radiation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole_information_paradox)

The black hole information paradox is the apparent conflict between unitary quantum evolution and the semiclassical prediction that complete black-hole evaporation leaves only thermal [Hawking radiation](#hawking-radiation). A resolution must explain how information is preserved or why one of the assumptions behind the conflict fails.

### Bekenstein-Hawking entropy

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole thermodynamics](#black-hole-thermodynamics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bekenstein-Hawking_entropy)

In units $G=\hbar=c=k_B=1$, a black hole of horizon area $A$ has entropy $S_{BH}=A/4$.

#### Conical-singularity derivation of black-hole entropy

↑ **Parent:** [Bekenstein-Hawking entropy](#bekenstein-hawking-entropy)

Changing the Euclidean time period away from its smooth value creates a curvature delta function at the horizon. Applying $(1-\beta\partial_\beta)$ to the Euclidean gravitational partition function isolates this term and gives $S=A/(4G_N)$.

<h3 id="gibbons-hawking-york-boundary-term">Gibbons–Hawking–York boundary term</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole thermodynamics](#black-hole-thermodynamics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbons–Hawking–York_boundary_term)

The Gibbons–Hawking–York boundary term makes the Einstein--Hilbert variational problem well posed when the induced boundary metric is fixed. It is the integral of the trace of the boundary's extrinsic curvature with the conventional normalization and sign.

### First law of black-hole mechanics

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole thermodynamics](#black-hole-thermodynamics)

For a nonrotating uncharged stationary black hole, the first law is $\delta M=\kappa\delta A/(8\pi)=T_H\delta S_{BH}$.

#### Physical-process first law of black-hole mechanics

↑ **Parent:** [First law of black-hole mechanics](#first-law-of-black-hole-mechanics)

The physical-process first law derives the area change caused by a small flux of matter through an initially and finally stationary horizon. Linearized [Null Raychaudhuri equation](geodesic-congruence.md#null-raychaudhuri-equation) evolution converts the Killing-energy flux into $\Delta M=\kappa\Delta A/(8\pi)$.

##### First-order horizon-area response to matter flux

↑ **Parent:** [Physical-process first law of black-hole mechanics](#physical-process-first-law-of-black-hole-mechanics)

For a small perturbation of a stationary horizon, the linearized [Null Raychaudhuri equation](geodesic-congruence.md#null-raychaudhuri-equation) gives $\delta\theta^{\prime}=-8\pi\delta T_{kk}$. With vanishing boundary term $[\lambda\delta\theta]$, integration by parts gives $\delta A=8\pi\int\lambda\delta T_{kk}\,d\lambda\,dA_0$. Combining this with [affine horizon-generator scaling](general-relativity.md#affine-horizon-generator-scaling) converts area response to horizon Killing-energy flux.

##### Physical-process first law for a rotating black hole

↑ **Parent:** [Physical-process first law of black-hole mechanics](#physical-process-first-law-of-black-hole-mechanics)

For horizon generator $\xi=k+\Omega_Hm$, a small matter flux into an initially and finally stationary rotating black hole satisfies

$$
\Delta M-\Omega_H\Delta J=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\Delta A.
$$

### Second law of black-hole mechanics

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole thermodynamics](#black-hole-thermodynamics)

Under the [null energy condition](stress-energy-tensor.md#null-energy-condition) and the usual global assumptions excluding naked pathologies, the area of every cross-section of a future [event horizon](#event-horizon) is nondecreasing toward the future. The [Null Raychaudhuri equation](geodesic-congruence.md#null-raychaudhuri-equation) and future completeness rule out negative expansion of its generators.

<h4 id="hawking-s-area-theorem">Hawking's area theorem</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Second law of black-hole mechanics](#second-law-of-black-hole-mechanics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawking's_area_theorem)

Hawking's area theorem states that the total area of classical future event horizons cannot decrease when the [null energy condition](stress-energy-tensor.md#null-energy-condition) and the required global assumptions hold. It is the geometric content of the [second law of black-hole mechanics](#second-law-of-black-hole-mechanics).

##### Area bound on equal-mass Schwarzschild merger radiation

↑ **Parent:** [Hawking's area theorem](#hawking-s-area-theorem)

Two initially well-separated [Schwarzschild black holes](schwarzschild-spacetime.md) of mass $m$ each have total horizon area $32\pi G^2m^2/c^4$. If they merge into a [Schwarzschild black hole](schwarzschild-spacetime.md), [Hawking's area theorem](#hawking-s-area-theorem) gives final mass $M\ge\sqrt2m$. Conserved total energy then bounds escaped radiation by the displayed expression, or $1-1/\sqrt2$ of the initial rest energy. This is an upper bound under classical horizon and energy assumptions, not a proof that an actual merger can saturate it.

##### Future-complete horizon focusing proof of the area theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Hawking's area theorem](#hawking-s-area-theorem)

For future-complete generators of an [event horizon](#event-horizon), the [null convergence condition](stress-energy-tensor.md#null-convergence-condition) and hypersurface orthogonality reduce the [Null Raychaudhuri equation](geodesic-congruence.md#null-raychaudhuri-equation) to $\theta'\leq-\theta^2/2$. If $\theta_0<0$, integration gives focusing within affine distance $2/|\theta_0|$. The [null focusing theorem](geodesic-congruence.md#null-focusing-theorem) identifies this with a [conjugate point to a spacelike surface](geodesic-congruence.md#conjugate-point-to-a-spacelike-surface). A null generator cannot remain on an [achronal boundary](general-relativity.md#achronal-boundary) beyond such a focal point, contradicting future completeness on the horizon. Thus $\theta\geq0$, and $dA/d\lambda=\theta A\geq0$ gives the [second law of black-hole mechanics](#second-law-of-black-hole-mechanics). New horizon generators may join toward the future at crease sets, adding area, but generators have no future endpoints on a regular event horizon under these assumptions.

### Third law of black-hole mechanics

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole thermodynamics](#black-hole-thermodynamics)

The third law of black-hole mechanics says that no finite physical process can reduce the [surface gravity](general-relativity.md#surface-gravity) of a regular black-hole horizon to zero.

### Generalized second law

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole thermodynamics](#black-hole-thermodynamics)

The generalized second law states that the sum of [Bekenstein-Hawking entropy](#bekenstein-hawking-entropy) and the entropy of matter and radiation outside black holes does not decrease.

## Kerr black hole

↑ **Parent:** [Black hole](black-hole.md)

The Kerr black hole is the asymptotically flat stationary axisymmetric vacuum black hole with mass $M$ and angular momentum $J=Ma$.

A Kerr black hole is the [Kerr metric](general-relativity.md#kerr-metric) with mass $M>0$ and spin parameter satisfying $|a|\leq M$ in geometrized units; its outer horizon is $r_+=M+\sqrt{M^2-a^2}$.

### Lense-Thirring precession

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lense–Thirring_precession)

Lense-Thirring precession is the rotation of orbital angular momentum caused by the gravitational field of a spinning mass. In the secular weak-field picture, the precession vector is parallel to the central spin. Thus an annulus has [torque](momentum.md#torque) proportional to $\mathbf J_h\times\delta\mathbf J_d$, perpendicular to $\mathbf J_h$. Summing such torques preserves this orthogonality even if their radial coefficients differ. Dissipation and the internal response of a warped [accretion disk](astrophysical-disk.md#accretion-disk) can supplement precession with an alignment torque.

#### Frame dragging inside a slowly rotating spherical shell

↑ **Parent:** [Lense-Thirring precession](#lense-thirring-precession)

For a weakly gravitating thin spherical shell of mass $M$, radius $R$ and uniform angular velocity $\Omega$, its interior [linearized gravity](linearized-gravity.md) metric has constant scalar potentials $A=-GM/R$, $C=GM/R$ and $g_{0i}=\omega(\widehat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf x)_i$. The scalar mass shell is a monopole. Its mass current is a dipole: regular interior and decaying exterior radial solutions are proportional to $r$ and $r^{-2}$, and their derivative jump fixes the factor $4/3$. Local inertial axes co-rotate with the shell at $\omega$ relative to asymptotic nonrotating axes. This is the leading interior [Lense-Thirring precession](#lense-thirring-precession), neglecting quadratic rotation and higher gravitational orders.

##### Inertial coordinates inside a slowly rotating spherical shell

↑ **Parent:** [Frame dragging inside a slowly rotating spherical shell](#frame-dragging-inside-a-slowly-rotating-spherical-shell)

At linear order in $\epsilon=GM/R$ and the leading rotation term, this coordinate change takes the interior shell metric to [Minkowski spacetime](special-relativity.md#minkowski-spacetime). Consequently the retained interior [Riemann curvature tensor](riemann-curvature-tensor.md) vanishes although inertial axes rotate relative to distant reference axes. A freely moving particle has $\mathbf X=\mathbf X_0+\mathbf V T$, while its original coordinates obey $d^2\mathbf x/dt^2=2\boldsymbol\omega\times d\mathbf x/dt$ to first rotation order. Terms of order $\omega^2$ would include centrifugal acceleration and are outside this approximation. A [gyroscope](momentum.md#gyroscope) at rest to the retained order has its parallel-transported spin precessing at $\boldsymbol\omega$ in the original coordinates.

#### Black-hole and disk alignment under internal torque

↑ **Parent:** [Lense-Thirring precession](#lense-thirring-precession)

For the opposite internal torques on a [black hole](black-hole.md) and its [accretion disk](astrophysical-disk.md#accretion-disk), the total [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum) $\mathbf J_t$ and the black-hole spin magnitude $J_h$ are constant. If $A=K_2|\mathbf J_h\times\mathbf J_d|^2$, then $d(\mathbf J_h\cdot\mathbf J_t)/dt=A$ and $dJ_d^2/dt=-2A$. The $K_1$ term produces [precession](rigid-body-dynamics.md#precession); the positive $K_2$ term increases the alignment of the hole with the total vector while decreasing the disk angular-momentum magnitude. The exact direction equation is $\dot c=K_2J_hJ_t(1-c^2)$ for $c=\cos\angle(\mathbf J_h,\mathbf J_t)$.

##### Counteralignment of a black hole and accretion disk

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole and disk alignment under internal torque](#black-hole-and-disk-alignment-under-internal-torque)

After persistent dissipative alignment of the hole with the conserved total [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum), the final vectors are $\mathbf J_{h,f}=J_h\widehat{\mathbf J}_t$ and $\mathbf J_{d,f}=(J_t-J_h)\widehat{\mathbf J}_t$. The disk is therefore antiparallel to the hole if $J_t<J_h$. Squaring $J_t^2=J_h^2+J_{d,0}^2+2J_hJ_{d,0}\cos\theta_0$ gives the displayed initial-angle criterion. The disk and hole need not finish aligned with each other even though the hole aligns with the total. Equality $J_t=J_h$ leaves zero final disk angular momentum rather than a defined disk spin direction.

##### Persistent-torque criterion for asymptotic spin alignment

↑ **Parent:** [Black-hole and disk alignment under internal torque](#black-hole-and-disk-alignment-under-internal-torque)

For nonzero $J_h,J_t$ and a noncollinear initial configuration, the alignment equation integrates to $\operatorname{artanh}c(t)=\operatorname{artanh}c(0)+J_hJ_t\int_0^tK_2(s)\,ds$. Hence the positive dissipative torque aligns the hole with the total vector if its integrated strength diverges. A fixed positive coefficient is sufficient. Pointwise positivity alone need not suffice for time-dependent coefficients: with $J_h=J_t=1$, $K_1=0$, $K_2=e^{-t}$ and $c(0)=0$, the limiting cosine is $\tanh1<1$. Exactly antiparallel initial spin and total angular momentum form a stationary, nongeneric exception.

### Kerr axial analytic extension

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole)

The two-dimensional symmetry-axis restriction of the [Kerr metric](general-relativity.md#kerr-metric) has simple horizons at $r_\pm$ for $0<|a|<M$, but is regular at $r=0$ because the ring is off-axis. Its static region below the inner horizon continues to an asymptotically flat end at negative infinity. The horizon blocks repeat in the maximal analytic extension. This ideal continuation is distinct from a claim of physical stability at the [Cauchy horizon](general-relativity.md#cauchy-horizon).

#### Kerr axial timelike effective potential

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr axial analytic extension](#kerr-axial-analytic-extension)

Conserved stationary energy and unit timelike normalization give this equation in the [Kerr axial analytic extension](#kerr-axial-analytic-extension). For $a>0$, the negative-sheet maximum of the potential is $1+M/a$ at $r=-a$. Energy squared above this maximum permits an inward orbit to pass through regular $r=0$ and continue to negative infinity without a turning point. At energy squared one, zero is a turning point. For negative spin the threshold is expressed with $|a|$.

### Kerr ring singularity

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole)

For nonzero spin, the curvature singularity of the [Kerr metric](general-relativity.md#kerr-metric) is the ring $x^2+y^2=a^2$, $z=0$ in oblate Cartesian coordinates. The rest of the $r=0$ disk is regular and allows continuation to negative $r$. The entire disk must not be mistaken for a curvature singularity, and axial trajectories do not hit the ring.

### Scalar wave separation in Kerr spacetime

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole)

For the massless [Klein-Gordon equation](wave-equation.md#klein-gordon-equation) on a [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole), the [covariant wave operator](quantum-field-theory.md#covariant-wave-operator) separates into radial and angular [ordinary differential equations](differential-equation.md#ordinary-differential-equation). With $K=(r^2+a^2)\omega-am$ and angular [separation constant](partial-differential-equation.md#separation-constant) $\Lambda$, the radial equation is $(\Delta R')'+[K^2/\Delta-a^2\omega^2+2am\omega-\Lambda]R=0$. The angular equation is $(\sin\theta\,\Theta')'/\sin\theta+[a^2\omega^2\cos^2\theta-m^2/\sin^2\theta+\Lambda]\Theta=0$. No extra radial factor is included in the displayed ansatz.

#### Scalar spheroidal harmonic

↑ **Parent:** [Scalar wave separation in Kerr spacetime](#scalar-wave-separation-in-kerr-spacetime)

A [scalar spheroidal harmonic](#scalar-spheroidal-harmonic) is a regular angular solution of $(\sin\theta\,S')'/\sin\theta+[c^2\cos^2\theta-m^2/\sin^2\theta+\Lambda]S=0$; with $e^{im\phi}$ it is smooth at both axes. For real $c$, regularity gives discrete eigenvalues $\Lambda_{\ell m}(c)$. At $c=0$ it reduces to the angular part of a [spherical harmonic](analysis.md#spherical-harmonic), with $\Lambda=\ell(\ell+1)$. The sign of the $c^2$ term distinguishes the convention used here.

### Kerr horizon angular velocity

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole)

The outer [Killing horizon](general-relativity.md#killing-horizon) of a [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole) is generated by $k+\Omega_Hm$, with the stationary [Killing vector field](killing-vector-field.md) $k$ normalized at infinity and the axial generator $m$ having $2\pi$-periodic orbits. In [ingoing Kerr coordinates](#ingoing-kerr-coordinates) its generators satisfy $d\chi/dv=\Omega_H$. This is the horizon's angular velocity relative to the nonrotating asymptotic stationary frame.

### Ingoing Kerr coordinates

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole)

[Ingoing Kerr coordinates](#ingoing-kerr-coordinates) cancel the explicit inverse-$\Delta$ singularities of the [Boyer-Lindquist coordinates](#boyer-lindquist-coordinates) metric. They give a smooth, nondegenerate [Kerr metric](general-relativity.md#kerr-metric) at the future outer horizon when $M>|a|$. Its stationary and axial [Killing vector fields](killing-vector-field.md) are $\partial_v$ and $\partial_\chi$. The advanced chart need not cover every horizon branch of the maximal extension.

### Irreducible mass

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole)

The [irreducible mass](#irreducible-mass) of a four-dimensional uncharged [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole) is $M_{\mathrm{irr}}=\sqrt{A/(16\pi)}$. Its [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) satisfies $M^2=M_{\mathrm{irr}}^2+J^2/(4M_{\mathrm{irr}}^2)$. [Hawking's area theorem](#hawking-s-area-theorem) prevents a classical process from decreasing $M_{\mathrm{irr}}$ under the theorem's energy and global hypotheses.

#### Area bound for an axisymmetric Kerr merger

↑ **Parent:** [Irreducible mass](#irreducible-mass)

For initially well-separated [Kerr black holes](#kerr-black-hole) with no orbital [angular momentum](momentum.md#angular-momentum), exact [axisymmetry](multivariable-calculus.md#axisymmetric-vector-field) gives $J_f=J_1+J_2$. Put $X=(A_1+A_2)/(8\pi)$. The [black-hole area theorem](#hawking-s-area-theorem) and the [irreducible mass](#irreducible-mass) identity imply

$$
M_f^2\geq\frac12\left(X+\frac{(J_1+J_2)^2}{X}\right).
$$

Indeed, $X\geq|J_1|+|J_2|\geq|J_f|$, and $\tfrac12(Y+J_f^2/Y)$ is nondecreasing for $Y\geq X$. Opposite spin signs lower $J_f^2$ at fixed initial areas and hence raise the allowed radiated-energy bound; this need not be achievable in an actual merger.

### Boyer-Lindquist coordinates

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyer-Lindquist_coordinates)

Boyer-Lindquist coordinates adapt the Kerr metric to its stationary and axial symmetries. The horizon radii are the roots $r_\pm=M\pm\sqrt{M^2-a^2}$ of $\Delta=r^2-2Mr+a^2$.

### Kerr ergoregion

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole)

The Kerr ergoregion is the region where the asymptotically timelike stationary Killing field becomes spacelike. Its outer boundary is $r=M+\sqrt{M^2-a^2\cos^2\theta}$.

The Kerr realization of an [ergosphere](general-relativity.md#ergosphere) lies outside the outer horizon where the stationary Killing vector is spacelike; its outer boundary is $r=M+\sqrt{M^2-a^2\cos^2\theta}$.

#### Kerr stationary-limit surface is generically timelike

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr ergoregion](#kerr-ergoregion)

For subextreme [Kerr spacetime](#kerr-black-hole), the outer stationary-limit surface is $s=r-r_e(\theta)=0$, where $r_e=M+\sqrt{M^2-a^2\cos^2\theta}$. Away from the rotation axis, $r_e>r_+$ and $\Delta(r_e)=a^2\sin^2\theta>0$. Its normal is therefore spacelike and the surface timelike. Although the stationary [Killing vector field](killing-vector-field.md) has zero norm on it, it is not normal to it, and the surface is not a [Killing horizon](general-relativity.md#killing-horizon). The actual outer Killing horizon is $r=r_+$, generated by $K+\Omega_H\Phi$.

#### Superradiance

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr ergoregion](#kerr-ergoregion)

[Superradiance](#superradiance) is amplification of a scattered wave by extracting rotational or other available conserved energy from a background. For a rotating [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole), a bosonic mode of frequency $\omega$ and azimuthal integer $m$ can have negative horizon Killing-energy flux when $0<\omega<m\Omega_H$. The reflected energy can then exceed the incident energy. A stationary Killing flow need not define a globally positive particle energy.

#### Causal angular velocity bounds in Kerr spacetime

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr ergoregion](#kerr-ergoregion)

Outside a subextremal [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole), regular [causal curves](general-relativity.md#causal-curve) obey $(-g_{t\phi}-\sqrt D)/g_{\phi\phi}\le d\phi/dt\le(-g_{t\phi}+\sqrt D)/g_{\phi\phi}$, where $D=g_{t\phi}^2-g_{tt}g_{\phi\phi}>0$. Positive radial and polar metric terms establish this necessary bound. In the open [Kerr ergoregion](#kerr-ergoregion) both endpoints are positive for $a>0$, forcing co-rotation.

#### Penrose process

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr ergoregion](#kerr-ergoregion)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_process)

The Penrose process extracts rotational energy by allowing a fragment with negative stationary Killing energy to enter the black hole while another fragment escapes with more energy than the original body carried.

##### Horizon flux inequality for causal matter

↑ **Parent:** [Penrose process](#penrose-process)

For a future-directed causal four-momentum $p$ crossing a future [Killing horizon](general-relativity.md#killing-horizon) with generator $\xi=t+\Omega_H\varphi$, the Lorentzian inner product obeys $p\cdot\xi\le0$. With $E=-p\cdot t$ and $L=p\cdot\varphi$, this is $E\ge\Omega_HL$. A massive particle has strict inequality. The same bound follows in the nonextremal quasistationary regime from the [Physical-process first law for a rotating black hole](#physical-process-first-law-for-a-rotating-black-hole) and the [second law of black-hole mechanics](#second-law-of-black-hole-mechanics). Negative Killing energy in a [Penrose process](#penrose-process) is allowed, but its angular momentum must satisfy this bound.

### Kerr polar tortoise coordinate

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole)

On the Kerr symmetry axis, the radial metric has $F=\Delta/(r^2+a^2)$ and the tortoise coordinate satisfies $dr_*/dr=F^{-1}=(r^2+a^2)/\Delta$.

### Kerr black-hole heat capacity at fixed angular momentum

↑ **Parent:** [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole)

Writing $s=\sqrt{1-J^2/M^4}=\sqrt{1-a^2/M^2}$, the heat capacity of a [Kerr black hole](#kerr-black-hole) at fixed angular momentum is

$$
C_J=4\pi M^2\frac{s(1+s)}{2-2s-s^2}.
$$

It is positive for $\sqrt{2\sqrt3-3}<|a|/M<1$, diverges at the lower endpoint, and tends to zero in the extremal limit.
