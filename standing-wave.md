# Standing wave

↑ **Parent:** [Mathematical physics](mathematical-physics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_wave)

A [standing wave](standing-wave.md) results from the [superposition principle](superposition-principle.md) applied to equal-[frequency](frequency.md), equal-[amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) [waves](wave-split.md) travelling in opposite directions. Its spatial [nodes of a standing wave](node-physics.md) remain fixed while its [displacement](displacement.md) oscillates in time.

For a pure [standing wave](standing-wave.md), the two [travelling waves](travelling-wave.md) also have equal [amplitudes](wave-amplitude.md): their [interference](interference-wave-propagation.md) leaves a fixed [mode shape](mode-shape.md), rather than a profile moving along the string. Choose the position origin and time origin so that their transverse [displacements](displacement.md) are

$$
y_+(x,t)=a\sin(kx-\omega t),\qquad y_-(x,t)=a\sin(kx+\omega t).
$$

Here $a>0$ is each constituent's [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md), $k=2\pi/\lambda$ is the [wavenumber](wavenumber.md), $\lambda$ is the [wavelength](wavelength.md), and $\omega=2\pi f$ is the [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md) corresponding to [frequency](frequency.md) $f$. Their [wave speed](wave-speed.md) is $c=\omega/k$. The [superposition principle](superposition-principle.md) and the [sine addition formula](sine-addition-formula.md) give

$$
y=y_++y_-=2a\sin(kx)\cos(\omega t).
$$

The spatial factor stays put; the temporal factor changes its size and sign. Other constant [wave phases](phase-waves.md) shift the position or time origin. Unequal constituent [amplitudes](wave-amplitude.md) generally leave moving [displacement](displacement.md) at the would-be [nodes of a standing wave](node-physics.md), rather than this exact factorization.[Standing waves on cord, interference explanation and sum-of-sines derivation](40-75-standing-waves-on-cord-ucsb-department-of-physics.md)

For an original ideal-string example, set $L=1\,\mathrm m$, $c=20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, and choose the third [normal mode](normal-mode.md). Then $k=3\pi\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, $\omega=60\pi\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, and $f=30\,\mathrm{Hz}$ by [fixed-end standing wave frequencies](fixed-end-standing-wave-frequencies.md). With each constituent [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) $a=5\,\mathrm{mm}$,

$$
y(x,t)=0.010\,\mathrm m\;\sin\left(\frac{3\pi x}{1\,\mathrm m}\right)\cos\left(\frac{60\pi t}{1\,\mathrm s}\right).
$$

Its [nodes of a standing wave](node-physics.md) are at $x=0,1/3,2/3,1\,\mathrm m$ and its [antinodes](antinode.md) at $x=1/6,1/2,5/6\,\mathrm m$. These are constructed model parameters, not experimental readings.

![](standing-wave-phase-snapshots.png)

**[Figure 13](#image-opposite-travelling-waves-add-to-a-standing-third-mode-permanent-nodes-survive-every-phase-snapshot). Opposite travelling waves add to a standing third mode; permanent nodes survive every phase snapshot**.

At $t=1/120\,\mathrm s$, every point has zero [displacement](displacement.md), but most have nonzero transverse [velocity](velocity.md): the string is crossing its straight configuration. The black dots alone mark permanent [nodes of a standing wave](node-physics.md). At an [antinode](antinode.md) the greatest transverse [speed](speed.md) is $2a\omega=0.6\pi\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, much smaller than the chosen $20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ [wave speed](wave-speed.md). One is motion of string material; the other is propagation of the constituent profiles.

The beauty of a [standing wave](standing-wave.md) is that motion reveals geometry: fixed [nodes of a standing wave](node-physics.md) turn a moving disturbance into a spatial ruler. Their separation measures [wavelength](wavelength.md), and [boundary conditions](boundary-condition.md) turn the continuous choice of [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) into a discrete set of [normal modes](normal-mode.md). This is useful both for interpreting string vibrations and for understanding how a bounded [wave equation](wave-equation-split.md) selects its [eigenfrequencies](eigenfrequency.md).
