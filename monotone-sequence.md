# Monotone sequence

↑ **Parent:** [Sequence](sequence.md)

A real sequence is increasing when $a_{n+1}\geq a_n$ and decreasing when $a_{n+1}\leq a_n$.

A [monotone sequence](monotone-sequence.md) has one of these directions for every index. Here increasing means nondecreasing and decreasing means nonincreasing: **equal successive terms are allowed**. Strict increase would require $a_{n+1}>a_n$ at every step, and strict decrease would require $a_{n+1}<a_n$. A constant [sequence](sequence.md) is both increasing and decreasing. These conventions are those of [Basic Analysis: Introduction to Real Analysis, Volume I by Jiří Lebl](basic-analysis-introduction-to-real-analysis-volume-i-by-jiri-lebl.md) (§2.1.1, Definition 2.1.9).

For $n\geq1$, the [sequence](sequence.md) $1-1/n$ increases toward one, while $1+1/n$ decreases toward one. Repeating each term gives the still increasing [sequence](sequence.md) $0,0,1/2,1/2,2/3,2/3,\ldots$. Plateaus do not obstruct the [bounded monotone sequence theorem](bounded-monotone-sequence-theorem.md). Checking the sign of $a_{n+1}-a_n$ is often the simplest way to verify a direction; finding an [upper bound](upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) or [lower bound](lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) is a separate task.
