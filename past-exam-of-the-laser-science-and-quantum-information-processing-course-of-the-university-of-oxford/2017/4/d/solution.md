<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For any joint lower-register state, the [probability](../../../../probability.md) is $P_0=(1+\langle S\rangle)/2$, where $S$ is its exchange operator. The Bell-state [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md) from part a therefore give

$$
\boxed{P_0(\Phi^+)=P_0(\Phi^-)=P_0(\Psi^+)=1,\qquad P_0(\Psi^-)=0.}
$$

For the [spin singlet state](../../../../spin-singlet-state.md), controlled exchange adds a minus sign to the control-1 branch, so the final [Hadamard gate](../../../../hadamard-gate.md) puts the ancilla in $|1\rangle$. For the three symmetric states the ancilla returns to $|0\rangle$.

The product-state overlap formula cannot be applied to arbitrary entangled lower inputs. All four [Bell states](../../../../bell-state-split.md) have identical one-qubit reduced states $I/2$, yet the [spin singlet state](../../../../spin-singlet-state.md) result differs maximally. The network measures exchange symmetry of the joint state, rather than [fidelity of quantum states](../../../../fidelity-of-quantum-states.md) inferred from its two marginals.
