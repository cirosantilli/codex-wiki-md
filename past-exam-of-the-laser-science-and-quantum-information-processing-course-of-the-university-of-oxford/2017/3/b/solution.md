<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the common $D_{3/2}$ level to subtract the two upper-state energies. The indicated transition satisfies

$$
\frac1\lambda=\frac1{397\ \mathrm{nm}}+\frac1{850\ \mathrm{nm}}-\frac1{866\ \mathrm{nm}},
\qquad
\boxed{\lambda\simeq393.6\ \mathrm{nm}.}
$$

The value inherits the rounding of the supplied [wavelengths](../../../../wavelength.md). It is shorter than 397 nm because $P_{3/2}$ lies above $P_{1/2}$.

A static electric potential alone cannot confine a charge stably in all three dimensions in a charge-free region. An RF quadrupole [ion trap](../../../../ion-trap.md) uses a rapidly alternating [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) to provide effective radial confinement; DC endcap fields can supply axial confinement. Near a stable equilibrium, the [secular motion](../../../../paul-trap-secular-motion.md) sees an approximately harmonic pseudopotential. It has quantized vibrational levels $E=\sum_i\hbar\omega_i(n_i+1/2)$, with separate [normal modes](../../../../normal-mode.md) along the principal directions. RF [Paul trap micromotion](../../../../paul-trap-micromotion.md) is superposed on this slower secular [oscillation](../../../../oscillation.md). For several [trapped ions](../../../../trapped-ion.md), Coulomb interactions instead give collective [normal modes](../../../../normal-mode.md).
