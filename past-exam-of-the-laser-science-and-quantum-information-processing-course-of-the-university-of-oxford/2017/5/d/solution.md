<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A herald tells the experimenter when an entangled resource has actually been prepared while leaving the two resource [photons](../../../../photon.md) available for subsequent operations. This permits synchronization, conditional storage or switching, and feed-forward. Direct down-conversion emission is probabilistic; inferring that a pair existed by destructively detecting both [photons](../../../../photon.md) does not leave that same pair available as an input resource.

The second network uses passive optical elements, auxiliary [entanglement](../../../../entangled-state.md) and detection rather than a strong deterministic [photon](../../../../photon.md)–[photon](../../../../photon.md) [CNOT gate](../../../../controlled-not-gate.md) interaction. Its smaller success rate can be acceptable if trials are inexpensive and successful events are reliably announced. Heralding is still probabilistic, not deterministic on demand; losses, dark counts and multiple-pair emission can give false or degraded heralds. The ideal $1/64$ result assumes the specified input pairs and valid detection model, so it should not be confused with a complete laboratory production efficiency.
