A [laser relaxation oscillation](../../../../laser-relaxation-oscillation.md) is the damped exchange between stored upper-level excitation and intracavity [photon number](../../../../photon-number.md) after a small perturbation of a lasing [steady state](../../../../steady-state.md). Extra [photons](../../../../photon.md) increase stimulated depletion of the upper level; the resulting smaller gain then reduces the [photon](../../../../photon.md) population. Pumping subsequently replenishes the inversion, so the two populations overshoot their equilibrium values before damping settles them.

[Laser spiking](../../../../laser-spiking.md) is the corresponding large-signal sequence of intense output pulses, often during switch-on. Excitation first accumulates while the optical field is small. Once gain exceeds loss, rapid [stimulated emission](../../../../stimulated-emission.md) releases this stored [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) in a spike, depleting the inversion below threshold. Recovery can produce further spikes. This is distinct from an externally imposed [Q-switching](../../../../q-switching.md) cycle, although both draw on stored excitation.

Many solid-state gain media have upper-state lifetimes far longer than the cavity [photon lifetime](../../../../photon-lifetime.md). The optical [polarization](../../../../polarization-waves.md) relaxes rapidly, leaving the two slow [rate-equation](../../../../laser-rate-equation.md) variables of a class-B [laser](../../../../laser-split.md). A long-lived population reservoir and a much faster [photon](../../../../photon.md) response make underdamped recovery and pronounced startup spiking common. The criterion and [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) follow from the [linearization](../../../../linearization-of-a-dynamical-system.md) in part c; they are not universal consequences of the word “solid-state”.
