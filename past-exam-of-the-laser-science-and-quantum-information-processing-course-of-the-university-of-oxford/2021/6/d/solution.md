<h1 id="6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Suppose the conditional atomic [pure state](../../../../pure-state.md) were a [product state](../../../../product-state.md)

$$
(\alpha|e\rangle+\beta|g\rangle)\otimes(\gamma|e\rangle+\delta|g\rangle).
$$

Comparing its four [probability amplitudes](../../../../probability-amplitude.md) with $(|eg\rangle+|ge\rangle)/\sqrt2$ gives

$$
\alpha\gamma=0,\quad \beta\delta=0,\quad
\alpha\delta=\frac1{\sqrt2},\quad\beta\gamma=\frac1{\sqrt2}.
$$

The last two equations make all four factors nonzero, contradicting either of the first two. Equivalently, the coefficient [matrix](../../../../matrix.md) has [determinant](../../../../determinant.md) $-1/2$, while a [product state](../../../../product-state.md) has a rank-one coefficient [matrix](../../../../matrix.md) and zero [determinant](../../../../determinant.md). Thus the atoms are entangled; indeed each [reduced density matrix](../../../../reduced-density-matrix.md) is $I/2$, making the conditional state maximally entangled.
