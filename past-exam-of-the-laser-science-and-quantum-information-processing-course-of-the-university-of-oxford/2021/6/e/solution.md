<h1 id="6/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

An ideal zero-[photon number](../../../../photon-number.md) result projects onto the first term in part (b). Consequently

$$
\boxed{|\psi_{\rm atoms}\rangle=|ee\rangle,\qquad
P_0(t)=\frac{[2+\cos(\sqrt6gt)]^2}{9}.}
$$

This is a [product state](../../../../product-state.md), not an entangled success. The remaining ideal outcome has

$$
P_2(t)=\frac29[1-\cos(\sqrt6gt)]^2
$$

and leaves $|gg\rangle$. In particular, simply discarding the field outcome does not reproduce the pure heralded [Bell state](../../../../bell-state-split.md).

The practical requirements are strong coherent coupling of both atoms to the same optical [normal mode](../../../../normal-mode.md) with stable relative coupling [phases](../../../../phase-waves.md), preparation of $|ee;0\rangle$, and interaction timing on the scale $g^{-1}$ before spontaneous emission or cavity loss destroys [quantum coherence](../../../../quantum-coherence-in-a-specified-basis.md). The field must then be measured or extracted after halting the coherent exchange, with enough [photon number](../../../../photon-number.md) resolution to distinguish one from two. Otherwise an inefficient detector can mistake a two-[photon](../../../../photon.md) event for the desired herald. Even ideally the one-[photon](../../../../photon.md) success probability is at most $1/3$, so trials must be repeated.

“No photons detected” implies $|ee\rangle$ only for the ideal zero-number projection specified above. With loss or finite detector efficiency, no click also includes missed one- and two-[photon](../../../../photon.md) events and generally leaves a conditional [mixed state](../../../../mixed-state.md). Detecting a leaking [photon](../../../../photon.md) during the interaction is also a different measurement protocol from the end-time [projective measurement](../../../../projective-measurement.md) analyzed here.
