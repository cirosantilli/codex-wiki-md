<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [annihilation operator](../../../../annihilation-operator.md) eigenvalue property gives

$$
\boxed{\langle\hat E(r,t)\rangle_\alpha
=\mathcal E\alpha e^{i(kr-\omega t)}+\mathcal E^*\alpha^*e^{-i(kr-\omega t)}
=2|\mathcal E\alpha|\cos(kr-\omega t+\arg(\mathcal E\alpha)).}
$$

This is a classical monochromatic [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md): the modulus of $\alpha$ fixes the [wave amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md), while its argument fixes the [wave phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) relative to the chosen mode convention. Direct normal ordering also gives $(\Delta E)^2_\alpha=|\mathcal E|^2$, independent of $\alpha$. Hence the fluctuation divided by the peak mean-field amplitude is $1/(2|\alpha|)$, small in the bright [coherent state](../../../../coherent-state.md) limit. A relative fluctuation evaluated exactly at a field zero is not a useful classicality test.
