<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Put $b=\mathcal E e^{i(kr-\omega t)}$, allowing $\mathcal E$ to be complex. The Hermitian [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) is $\hat E=b\hat a+b^*\hat a^\dagger$. The [annihilation operator](../../../../annihilation-operator.md) and [creation operator](../../../../creation-operator.md) change [photon number](../../../../photon-number.md), so their diagonal matrix elements in a [Fock state](../../../../fock-state.md) vanish. Since $\hat a\hat a^\dagger=\hat n+1$ and $\hat a^\dagger\hat a=\hat n$,

$$
\boxed{\langle\hat E\rangle_n=0,\qquad
\langle\hat E^2\rangle_n=(2n+1)|\mathcal E|^2=(\Delta E)^2_n.}
$$

The terms containing $\hat a^2$ or $\hat a^{\dagger2}$ connect orthogonal [number states](../../../../number-state.md) and contribute zero. Even as $n$ grows, there is no definite oscillating mean [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md); its quadrature fluctuations grow instead. A [Fock state](../../../../fock-state.md) can represent fixed field [energy](../../../../energy-split.md), but not a classical sinusoidal wave of prescribed [wave phase](../../../../phase-waves.md). This is distinct from representing a classical field with a randomly averaged phase.
