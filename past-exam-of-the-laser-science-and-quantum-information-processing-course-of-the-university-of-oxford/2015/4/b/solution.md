<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a fundamental [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) in the $xy$ plane, $E_z=0$. The specified [tensor](../../../../tensor.md) then gives

$$
P_x=P_y=0,\qquad
P_z(2\omega)=2\epsilon_0d_{36}E_xE_y.
$$

Writing $E_x=E_0\cos\phi$ and $E_y=E_0\sin\phi$ yields

$$
P_z=\epsilon_0d_{36}E_0^2\sin2\phi,\qquad
\boxed{I_{2\omega}\propto\sin^22\phi,\quad\phi=45^\circ\pmod{90^\circ}.}
$$

The source [electric polarization](../../../../polarization-density.md) is therefore along $z$ and is largest along the in-plane diagonals. A radiated wave also needs a transverse component of this source: propagation along $z$ would not generate a propagating $z$-polarized output. Propagation in the $xy$ plane provides the simple strictly transverse example. For a tilted extraordinary wave, the electric displacement, rather than generally the entire [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md), is perpendicular to the [wave vector](../../../../wavevector.md); the tensor source must be projected onto that wave's allowed [polarization](../../../../polarization-waves.md). This distinction matters when using the type-I geometry below.
