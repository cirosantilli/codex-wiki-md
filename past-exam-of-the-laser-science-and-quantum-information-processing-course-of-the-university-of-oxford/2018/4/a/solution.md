<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [universal quantum gate set](../../../../universal-quantum-gate-set.md) can approximate any unitary operation on any finite register, to arbitrary requested accuracy, using a finite sequence of its gates on the register. Unobservable overall [global phase](../../../../global-phase.md) is ignored. Approximation matters: a fixed finite alphabet has only countably many finite circuits, whereas there are uncountably many exact [unitary matrices](../../../../unitary-matrix.md).

The [Hadamard gate](../../../../hadamard-gate.md) $H$ and [T gate](../../../../t-gate.md) generate a dense set of single-qubit operations, but cannot themselves entangle different [qubits](../../../../qubit.md). Adding the [controlled-NOT gate](../../../../controlled-not-gate.md) gives a universal multi-qubit set. More generally, an available two-qubit gate must be genuinely entangling: it must take at least one product input to an entangled output. A product of local unitaries, or a [SWAP gate](../../../../swap-gate.md) composed with local unitaries, cannot provide this resource. Together with arbitrary single-qubit control, an entangling two-qubit gate on a connected interaction network supplies universal register control.
