<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In [Q-switching](../../../../../q-switching.md), hold the [optical resonator](../../../../../optical-cavity.md)'s [quality factor](../../../../../q-factor.md) low while pumping builds [population inversion](../../../../../population-inversion.md), then rapidly reduce the loss. The stored excitation produces a large [optical gain](../../../../../optical-gain.md), the intracavity [light intensity](../../../../../light-intensity.md) grows, and [stimulated emission](../../../../../stimulated-emission.md) depletes the [population inversion](../../../../../population-inversion.md) until the [optical gain](../../../../../optical-gain.md) falls below the [laser threshold](../../../../../laser-threshold.md). The resulting [optical pulse](../../../../../optical-pulse.md) is a transient discharge of stored excitation.

Its duration depends on the high-[quality factor](../../../../../q-factor.md) [photon lifetime](../../../../../photon-lifetime.md), initial [population inversion](../../../../../population-inversion.md), saturation dynamics and switching time. For example, a simple [laser rate equation](../../../../../laser-rate-equation.md) model is

$$
\dot S=(g(N)-\tau_c^{-1})S,\qquad
\dot N=R_p-N/\tau_u-g(N)S,
$$

where $S$ is intracavity [photon number](../../../../../photon-number.md), $N$ is the available inversion in compatible units, $R_p$ is the pump rate and $\tau_c$ is the [photon lifetime](../../../../../photon-lifetime.md). During the short discharge, pumping and upper-state decay can often be neglected. The build-up and depletion rates determine the width; there is no universal equality between the width and $\tau_c$. The initially stored [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and the output coupling also matter. Typical [Q-switching](../../../../../q-switching.md) produces nanosecond-scale [optical pulses](../../../../../optical-pulse.md).
