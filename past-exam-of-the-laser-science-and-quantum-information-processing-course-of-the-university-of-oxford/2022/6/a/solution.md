<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The two output ports are orthogonal [single-photon states](../../../../single-photon-state.md). Conservation of total [probability](../../../../probability.md) at a lossless [beam splitter](../../../../beam-splitter.md) gives $2c^2=1$. With $c$ real and positive,

$$
\boxed{c=1/\sqrt2.}
$$

In the ordered input basis $(0,1')$ and output basis $(0',1)$, the [beam splitter scattering matrix](../../../../beam-splitter-scattering-matrix.md) is

$$
S=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For the usual reciprocal lossless [beam splitter](../../../../beam-splitter.md), reversing propagation uses $S^{-1}=S^\dagger=S$. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{|1\rangle\longmapsto\frac{|0\rangle-|1'\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad
|0'\rangle\longmapsto\frac{|0\rangle+|1'\rangle}{\sqrt2}.}
$$

The [relative phase](../../../../relative-quantum-phase.md) in the first rule is essential to the repeated-path calculation. Overall port phases may be changed consistently; they cannot be changed on one pass alone while retaining the original scattering rules. In the following, take unit mirror reflection amplitudes by incorporating fixed reflection phases into the propagation-mode convention, and keep any unremoved propagation phase explicitly if needed.
