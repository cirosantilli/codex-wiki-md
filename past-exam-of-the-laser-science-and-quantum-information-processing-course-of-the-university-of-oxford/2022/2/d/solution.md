<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For a symmetric [confocal optical resonator](../../../../confocal-optical-resonator.md), the mirror separation equals either mirror's curvature, $R_2=L_2$, so its symmetric fundamental [Gaussian beam](../../../../gaussian-beam.md) has

$$
\boxed{z_B=L_2/2.}
$$

The same [optical mode matching](../../../../optical-mode-matching.md) calculation applies, now with $f_{\min}=\sqrt{z_AL_2/2}$. Keeping the example's $L_2=10\,\mathrm m$ gives $z_B=5\,\mathrm m$ and $f_{\min}=0.707107\,\mathrm m$, compared with $0.930605\,\mathrm m$ for the nonconfocal target. With $f=1.5\,\mathrm m$, the two branches become

$$
\boxed{(u,v)\simeq(1.68708,10.85414)\,\mathrm m
\quad\hbox{or}\quad(1.31292,-7.85414)\,\mathrm m.}
$$

Again only the positive-$v$ branch fits the stated downstream geometry. The confocal target's smaller [Rayleigh range](../../../../rayleigh-length.md) means a smaller fundamental [beam waist](../../../../beam-waist.md) at the same [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) and a less stringent minimum $f$.

A [confocal optical resonator](../../../../confocal-optical-resonator.md) also has transverse [normal mode](../../../../normal-mode.md) degeneracies: in the ideal paraxial spectrum, increasing transverse order by two shifts the resonance by one longitudinal spacing. Such a cavity can transmit combinations of higher transverse [normal modes](../../../../normal-mode.md) at a fundamental resonance, so transmitted [power](../../../../power.md) alone is a weaker diagnostic of pure fundamental [optical mode matching](../../../../optical-mode-matching.md). The degeneracy does not remove the [complex beam parameter](../../../../complex-beam-parameter.md) matching requirement if the objective is specifically the symmetric fundamental [Gaussian beam](../../../../gaussian-beam.md).
