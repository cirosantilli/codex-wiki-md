[Q-switching](../../../../q-switching.md) stores excitation in a pumped [laser gain medium](../../../../laser-gain-medium.md) while preventing oscillation, then rapidly changes the [optical cavity](../../../../optical-cavity.md) from high loss, or low [quality factor](../../../../q-factor.md), to low loss, or high [quality factor](../../../../q-factor.md). The initially suppressed stimulated field can then grow rapidly from a seed and deplete a large [population inversion](../../../../population-inversion.md) in a short pulse. The resulting peak [power](../../../../power.md) is much higher than for continuous operation at the same average pumping rate. This is [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) storage and release, rather than the phase locking of many longitudinal modes used in [mode locking](../../../../mode-locking.md).

An active implementation uses a [Pockels cell](../../../../pockels-cell.md) and a [polarizer](../../../../polarizer.md). An applied voltage makes the cell change the intracavity [polarization](../../../../polarization-waves.md), causing the [polarizer](../../../../polarizer.md) to introduce sufficient loss to prevent lasing while pumping builds the inversion. Rapidly changing the voltage removes that loss and opens the high-Q state. The switching time should be short relative to the pulse build-up and depletion times. A [saturable absorber](../../../../saturable-absorber.md) provides an alternative passive Q switch: its absorption decreases as the intracavity intensity rises.
