<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

During the pulse, the [photon density](../../../../photon-number-density.md) reaches its maximum when $\dot n=0$ with $n>0$, so $N^*=N_{\rm th}^*$. Evaluating [i](../c/i.md) at this inversion gives

$$
n_{\max}=\frac{f_cN_i^*}{\beta}\left[1-\frac{1+\ln r}{r}\right].
$$

The ideal instantaneous output [power](../../../../power.md) is $P(t)=V_c\hbar\omega_Ln(t)/\tau_c$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P_{\max}=\frac{N_i^*V_g\hbar\omega_L}{\beta\tau_c}
\frac{r-1-\ln r}{r}.}
$$

For $r\gg1$, almost all the extractable [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is stored briefly as intracavity [photons](../../../../photon.md), and $P_{\max}\simeq E_{\max}/\tau_c$. The leading rise while the inversion remains near its initial value is $n\propto e^{(r-1)t/\tau_c}$, with short growth time $\tau_c/(r-1)$. Depletion then takes the inversion below threshold. At late times $N^*\simeq N_f^*$ and

$$
n(t)\propto\exp\left[-\left(1-\frac{N_f^*}{N_{\rm th}^*}\right)\frac{t}{\tau_c}\right],
$$

which approaches a tail with decay time $\tau_c$ at large $r$. The pulse is consequently asymmetric, with a rapid rise and a slower approximately exponential trailing edge. The negligible-seed build-up delay depends on the seed strength. The pumping and spontaneous population decay have been neglected over the short pulse; parasitic [cavity](../../../../optical-cavity.md) loss multiplies useful [power](../../../../power.md) by the output fraction described in [ii](../b/ii.md).
