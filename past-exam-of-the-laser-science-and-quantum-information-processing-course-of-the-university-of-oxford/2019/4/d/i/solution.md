<h1 id="4/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Assume Alice independently chooses X or Z with equal [probabilities](../../../../../probability.md) and that bit labels $0,1$ identify the corresponding positive and negative [eigenstates](../../../../../eigenstate.md). If Daisy measures Z, she reads a Z-encoded bit correctly with [probability](../../../../../probability.md) one and an X-encoded bit correctly with [probability](../../../../../probability.md) one half. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P(\text{correct bit}\mid M_Z)=\frac12\cdot1+\frac12\cdot\frac12=\frac34.}
$$

The [probability](../../../../../probability.md) to read the entire independently encoded $n$-bit check number correctly by this strategy is $(3/4)^n$.
