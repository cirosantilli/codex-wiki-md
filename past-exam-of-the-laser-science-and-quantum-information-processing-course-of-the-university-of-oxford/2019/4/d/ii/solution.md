<h1 id="4/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Daisy's random choice of X or Z agrees with Alice's independent random basis with [probability](../../../../../probability.md) $1/2$. Agreement gives the correct label with certainty and disagreement gives it with [probability](../../../../../probability.md) $1/2$. Hence

$$
\boxed{P(\text{correct bit}\mid\text{random X/Z})=\frac34,}
$$

the same result as a fixed Z [measurement](../../../../../quantum-measurement-split.md). Randomizing her basis does not supply the missing information about Alice's choice. These are the [probabilities](../../../../../probability.md) for the two specified strategies, rather than an assertion that they are optimal among all possible quantum [measurements](../../../../../quantum-measurement-split.md).
