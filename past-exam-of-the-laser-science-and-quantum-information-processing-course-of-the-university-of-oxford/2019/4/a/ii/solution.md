<h1 id="4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Bob knows the initial amplitudes and the [measurement](../../../../../quantum-measurement-split.md) basis but not its result, so he averages the two conditional states:

$$
\boxed{\rho_B=|\alpha|^2|0\rangle\langle0|+|\beta|^2|1\rangle\langle1|
=\begin{pmatrix}|\alpha|^2&0\\0&|\beta|^2\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

The initial [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) had off-diagonal entries $\alpha\beta^*$ and $\alpha^*\beta$, which the unread [measurement](../../../../../quantum-measurement-split.md) removes. Alice's description is conditioned on an available record; Bob's is the [nonselective projective measurement](../../../../../nonselective-projective-measurement.md) mixture. Averaging Alice's records gives exactly Bob's predictions, and communicating the result lets him make the same conditional update. This is a difference of conditioning information, not two inconsistent physical state assignments.
