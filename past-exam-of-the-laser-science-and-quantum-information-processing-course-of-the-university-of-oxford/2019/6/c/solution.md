<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In a [local hidden-variable](../../../../local-hidden-variable-theory.md) description, assign predetermined values $X_j,Y_j\in\{+1,-1\}$ to the two possible [measurements](../../../../quantum-measurement-split.md) at [photon](../../../../photon.md) $j$. The perfect correlations of [b](../b.md) require

$$
X_1X_2Y_3=-1,\qquad X_1Y_2X_3=-1,\qquad Y_1X_2X_3=-1.
$$

Multiplying these identities cancels each squared X value and gives $\boxed{Y_1Y_2Y_3=-1}$. Thus [local realism](../../../../local-hidden-variable-theory.md) predicts only the YYY outcomes $++-,$ $+-+,$ $-++,$ and $---$. Combining this parity rule with the measured unbiased single-Y marginals forces all four frequencies to be $1/4$: four weights subject to [normalization](../../../../normalization.md) and the three zero-mean conditions are uniquely uniform.

Quantum mechanics instead has $k=3$ in the amplitude formula of [b](../b.md), giving $\mathcal A_3=(1+abc)/4$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{|\mathrm{GHZ}\rangle_{YYY}=\frac12\left(
|+++\rangle+|+--\rangle+|-+-\rangle+|--+\rangle\right).}
$$

Quantum mechanics predicts each positive-product outcome with [probability](../../../../probability.md) $1/4$ and every negative-product outcome with [probability](../../../../probability.md) zero. The local and quantum allowed sets are disjoint.

This is the [GHZ theorem](../../../../ghz-theorem.md): in the ideal case the contradiction follows from deterministic product constraints, an all-versus-nothing argument. A usual two-party [Bell-state](../../../../bell-state-split.md) test instead violates a statistical [Bell inequality](../../../../bell-inequality.md) assembled from several settings. Both require appropriate locality and measurement-choice assumptions and careful treatment of experimental imperfections; the distinction is the deterministic form of the ideal three-party argument.
