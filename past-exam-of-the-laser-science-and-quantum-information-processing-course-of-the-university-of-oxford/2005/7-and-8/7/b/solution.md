<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In [entanglement swapping](../../../../../entanglement-swapping.md), start with independent [Bell pairs](../../../../../bell-pair.md) $|\phi^+\rangle_{12}|\phi^+\rangle_{34}$. Send [qubits](../../../../../qubit.md) 2 and 3 to a central [Bell state analyzer](../../../../../bell-state-analyzer.md), retaining 1 and 4 at separate endpoints. Reordering the [tensor product](../../../../../tensor-product.md) factors to group 23 and 14 gives the identity

$$
|\phi^+\rangle_{12}|\phi^+\rangle_{34}
=\frac12\left(
|\phi^+\rangle_{23}|\phi^+\rangle_{14}
+|\phi^-\rangle_{23}|\phi^-\rangle_{14}
+|\psi^+\rangle_{23}|\psi^+\rangle_{14}
+|\psi^-\rangle_{23}|\psi^-\rangle_{14}\right).
$$

Consequently each ideal [Bell-basis measurement](../../../../../bell-basis-measurement.md) outcome has [probability](../../../../../probability.md) $1/4$ and projects 14 onto the corresponding [Bell state](../../../../../bell-state-split.md), even though those two [photons](../../../../../photon.md) have never interacted. Communicating the two-[bit](../../../../../bit.md) result permits an endpoint [Pauli gate](../../../../../pauli-gate.md) to convert every outcome into the same chosen [Bell pair](../../../../../bell-pair.md). Without that record, the endpoints have $\rho_{14}=I_4/4$, a [product state](../../../../../product-state.md), rather than a known [entangled state](../../../../../entangled-state.md).

The optical [Bell state analyzer](../../../../../bell-state-analyzer.md) overlaps [photons](../../../../../photon.md) 2 and 3 at a [balanced beam splitter](../../../../../balanced-beam-splitter.md), with matched spatial modes, spectra and arrival times, and resolves the output [photon polarizations](../../../../../photon-polarization.md) using [polarizing beam splitters](../../../../../polarizing-beam-splitter.md) and [single-photon detectors](../../../../../single-photon-detector.md). [Two-photon interference](../../../../../two-photon-interference.md) maps [Bell states](../../../../../bell-state-split.md) to different output patterns. The antisymmetric $|\psi^-\rangle$ gives one [photon](../../../../../photon.md) in each output with opposite [polarizations](../../../../../polarization-waves.md). The symmetric $|\psi^+\rangle$ gives two opposite-polarization [photons](../../../../../photon.md) in the same output; the two polarization detectors there can resolve this pattern. The $|\phi^\pm\rangle$ states give same-polarization pairs and remain mutually ambiguous in this basic setup. The detected pattern therefore heralds a particular endpoint [Bell state](../../../../../bell-state-split.md). With ideal detection this arrangement succeeds unambiguously on two of four equally likely inputs, with [probability](../../../../../probability.md) $1/2$; the successful events can be selected. A complete analyzer would accept all four outcomes. The endpoint [quantum entanglement](../../../../../entangled-state.md) is conditional on the measurement record, so it enables no instantaneous signalling.[1998, experimental Bell projection and conditional endpoint correlations](../../../../../experimental-entanglement-swapping-entangling-photons-that-never-interacted-by-jian-wei-pan-and-others.md)
