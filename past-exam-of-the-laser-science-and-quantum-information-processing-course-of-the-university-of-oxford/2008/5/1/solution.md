<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

For a normalized [pure state](../../../../pure-state.md), expand $|\psi\rangle=\sum_m c_m|m\rangle$ in an [orthonormal basis](../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of [eigenvectors](../../../../eigenvector.md) of the measured [Hermitian operator](../../../../hermitian-operator.md). The [Born rule](../../../../born-rule.md) assigns outcome $m$ the [probability](../../../../probability.md) $p_m=|c_m|^2$. Under the ideal [projective measurement](../../../../projective-measurement.md) update, a nondegenerate outcome leaves $|m\rangle$, up to a [global phase](../../../../global-phase.md). The sum of the outcome probabilities is one, and the [expectation value](../../../../expectation-value.md) is $\sum_m m|c_m|^2$; a single trial reports one eigenvalue, not that average.

If $M|m\rangle=m|m\rangle$, then

$$
m\langle m|m\rangle=\langle m|M|m\rangle
=\overline{\langle m|M|m\rangle}
=\overline m\langle m|m\rangle,
$$

since $M=M^\dagger$. A nonzero [eigenvector](../../../../eigenvector.md) has positive squared [norm](../../../../norm.md), so **$m$ is real**. For two eigenvectors,

$$
\langle n|M|m\rangle=m\langle n|m\rangle
=\langle Mn|m\rangle=n\langle n|m\rangle.
$$

Thus $(m-n)\langle n|m\rangle=0$: **distinct eigenvalues have orthogonal eigenvectors**.
