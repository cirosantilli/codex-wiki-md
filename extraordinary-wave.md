# Extraordinary wave

↑ **Parent:** [Uniaxial optical medium](uniaxial-optical-medium.md)

The [extraordinary wave](extraordinary-wave.md)'s [electric displacement field](electric-displacement-field.md) lies in that plane and is perpendicular to the [wavevector](wavevector.md); its [electric field](electric-field-split.md) need not be. For [angle](angle.md) $\theta$ between the [wavevector](wavevector.md) and [optic axis](optic-axis.md),

$$
\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{ex}}(\theta)^2}=\frac{\cos^2\theta}{n_o^2}+\frac{\sin^2\theta}{n_e^2}.
$$

Thus $n_{\mathrm{ex}}(0)=n_o$ and $n_{\mathrm{ex}}(\pi/2)=n_e$.[§§2.8.2–2.8.3, printed p. 156, equations 2.410–2.413 and longitudinal electric-field discussion](fundamentals-of-photonics-and-quantum-electronics-lecture-notes-by-franz-x-kartner.md)

A short derivation starts from the existing [plane wave in an anisotropic dielectric](plane-wave-in-an-anisotropic-dielectric.md) equation. Put $\mathbf k=(k_x,0,k_z)$ and $k_0=\omega/c$, with [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md) $\omega$ and vacuum [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) $c$. The $y$ component gives the [ordinary wave](ordinary-wave.md) relation $k_x^2+k_z^2=n_o^2k_0^2$. The [determinant](determinant.md) of the $x,z$ system gives

$$
\frac{k_x^2}{n_e^2}+\frac{k_z^2}{n_o^2}=k_0^2.
$$

Substituting $k_x=n_{\mathrm{ex}}k_0\sin\theta$ and $k_z=n_{\mathrm{ex}}k_0\cos\theta$ yields the displayed directional [refractive index](refractive-index.md).
