# Fixed exponential perturbation of a Gaussian empirical mean

↑ **Parent:** [Large deviation principle](large-deviation-principle.md)

Let $A_i$ be [independent random variables](independent-random-variables.md) with [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md) $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, $\sigma^2>0$, and let $B$ be an independent [exponential random variable](exponential-distribution.md) of rate $\lambda>0$. At speed $L$, $(B+\sum_{i=1}^LA_i)/L$ has the [good rate function](good-rate-function.md)

$$
K(x)=\begin{cases}(x-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2),&x\leq\mu+\lambda\sigma^2,\\ \lambda(x-\mu)-\lambda^2\sigma^2/2,&x\geq\mu+\lambda\sigma^2.\end{cases}
$$

The [product large-deviation principle](product-large-deviation-principle.md) gives the joint cost $\lambda b+(z-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)$ for $b\geq0$, and the [contraction principle for large deviations](contraction-principle-for-large-deviations.md) gives its [infimal convolution](infimal-convolution.md) under $b+z=x$. The minimum occurs at $b=(x-\mu-\lambda\sigma^2)^+$. The fixed exponential perturbation changes the far upper tail even though it vanishes in probability after division by $L$; a fixed [Gaussian random variable](gaussian-random-variable.md) divided by $L$ instead has [exponential equivalence](exponential-equivalence.md) with zero at speed $L$.
