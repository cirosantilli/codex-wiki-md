# Coarea formula

↑ **Parent:** [Geometric measure theory](geometric-measure-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coarea_formula)

For a scalar smooth map $\Phi:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$, integration can be decomposed into its level surfaces with the [gradient](gradient.md) weight displayed in the title. Near a regular level, using $\Phi$ as the normal coordinate gives the volume element $dx=dS\,ds/|\nabla\Phi|$, which explains the formula by [integration by substitution](integration-by-substitution.md). In particular, $\int W(x)\delta(\Phi(x))\,dx=\int_{\Phi=0}W/|\nabla\Phi|\,dS$ when the zero level is regular and the integrals are well-defined. General versions allow [Lipschitz functions](lipschitz-continuity.md) and maps into higher-dimensional spaces.
