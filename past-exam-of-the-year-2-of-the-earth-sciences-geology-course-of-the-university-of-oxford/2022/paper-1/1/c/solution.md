<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The visible surface can be an **[exhumed fault surface](../../../../../exhumed-fault-surface.md)**, rather than an unrelated hillslope that happens to intersect a buried plane. Downward motion of the [hanging wall](../../../../../hanging-wall.md) exposes the [footwall](../../../../../footwall.md) face; repeated displacement and removal of loose material can reveal a substantial length of the same [fault plane](../../../../../fault-plane.md). Where exposure is renewed faster than [weathering](../../../../../weathering.md), [erosion](../../../../../erosion.md) and collapse erase it, that planar surface remains part of the hillside. The less weathered lower face and rougher, vegetated upper ground are consistent with differential exposure, though the photograph alone does not date individual events.

At greater distance or over longer times, [erosion](../../../../../erosion.md) modifies the slope and the hillside will cross or obscure the [fault plane](../../../../../fault-plane.md). The correspondence is therefore a transient competition between [fault slip](../../../../../fault-slip.md), exhumation and surface processes, not a requirement that all hillslopes follow faults.
