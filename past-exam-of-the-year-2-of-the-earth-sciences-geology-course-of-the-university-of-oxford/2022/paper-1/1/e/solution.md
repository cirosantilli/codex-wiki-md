<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The along-strike rupture time is $L/v_r$, where $v_r$ is [rupture velocity](../../../../../rupture-velocity.md). Taking a representative crustal $v_r\simeq3\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ gives **about $2$ s** for a $6$ km unilateral [finite-source rupture](../../../../../finite-source-rupture.md). A different assumed [rupture velocity](../../../../../rupture-velocity.md) changes this estimate. The local slip rise time at one patch is not specified and need not equal the whole-fault rupture time.

For comparable distance and ground conditions, the eastern observer lies in the forward [rupture directivity](../../../../../rupture-directivity.md) direction. Radiation from successive patches arrives closer together, producing a shorter, more concentrated and generally larger-amplitude ground-velocity pulse. The western observer receives the contributions spread over a longer interval, generally with less concentrated shaking. This concerns pulse duration and [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md); it does not imply an equally large permanent displacement at the eastern site.[Explanation of constructive interference and the relation of rupture speed to S-wave speed](../../../../../what-is-directivity-by-united-states-geological-survey.md)

For distant observers approximately along strike, an elementary [seismic travel time](../../../../../seismic-travel-time.md) construction gives

$$
\Delta t_E\simeq L\left(\frac1{v_r}-\frac1\beta\right),\qquad
\Delta t_W\simeq L\left(\frac1{v_r}+\frac1\beta\right),
$$

where $\beta$ is the [shear-wave velocity](../../../../../shear-wave-velocity.md). With $v_r=3$ and $\beta=3.5\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, these propagation contributions are about $0.29$ and $3.71$ s, respectively; finite local rise time broadens them. [Double-couple radiation pattern](../../../../../double-couple-radiation-pattern.md), receiver geometry and local site response can change the observed [amplitudes](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md), so location east or west alone is not an unconditional intensity prediction.
