Examples of **[prezygotic reproductive isolation](../../../../../prezygotic-reproductive-isolation.md)**, all acting before a zygote forms, include:

- **Habitat isolation:** potential mates occupy different habitats or hosts and seldom encounter each other at mating sites.
- **Temporal isolation:** breeding seasons, flowering times or daily mating periods differ, reducing opportunities for mating or pollen transfer.
- **Behavioral isolation:** courtship signals, calls or chemical mating signals elicit responses mainly from the same species, preventing cross-group mating.
- **Mechanical isolation:** incompatible mating structures or flower–pollinator fit prevent successful transfer of gametes.
- **Gametic incompatibility:** gametes meet but cannot recognize, fuse or complete [fertilization](../../../../../fertilization.md) successfully.

The first four primarily reduce mating or gamete transfer; the last can occur after mating while still preceding [fertilization](../../../../../fertilization.md). These differ from [postzygotic isolation](../../../../../postzygotic-isolation.md), such as hybrid inviability or hybrid sterility, which acts after the zygote has formed. Distinguishing these stages prevents “premating” from being used as a synonym for every prezygotic barrier.
