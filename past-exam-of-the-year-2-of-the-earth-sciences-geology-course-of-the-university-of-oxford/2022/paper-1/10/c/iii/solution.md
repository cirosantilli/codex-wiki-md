<h1 id="10/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Successive jumps occur at times separated by **$T=6$ s**. Between $-3$ and $3$ s the plotted function is $f(t)=2-t$, periodically extended, with maximum $5$ and minimum $-1$. Its mean is therefore **$2$ m**, and $f(t)-2=-t$ is an [odd function](../../../../../../odd-function.md) over that centered period.

For the [Fourier series](../../../../../../fourier-series-split.md) convention

$$
f(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}
\left[a_r\cos\frac{2\pi rt}{T}+b_r\sin\frac{2\pi rt}{T}\right],
$$

**$a_0=4$ m and $a_r=0$ for every $r\ge1$**; the nonconstant content consists of [sine](../../../../../../sine.md) terms. If $a_0$ denotes the mean directly in the alternative convention $f=a_0+\sum\cdots$, its value is $2$ m. Stating the convention resolves the otherwise ambiguous notation.

For completeness, integrating $-t$ against the [sine](../../../../../../sine.md) basis gives **$b_r=6(-1)^r/(\pi r)$ m**. At a jump the [Fourier series](../../../../../../fourier-series-split.md) converges to the midpoint $2$ m. The nonzero mean does not destroy the oddness argument for the mean-subtracted function; declaring the original function itself odd would incorrectly force its mean to vanish.
