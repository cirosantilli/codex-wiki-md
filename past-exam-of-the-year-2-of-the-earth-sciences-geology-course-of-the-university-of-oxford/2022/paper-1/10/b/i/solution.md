<h1 id="10/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $k=2\pi/\lambda$. For $T=Ae^{\alpha t}\cos(ky)$, direct [partial differentiation](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md) gives

$$
T_t=\alpha Ae^{\alpha t}\cos(ky),\qquad
T_{yy}=-k^2Ae^{\alpha t}\cos(ky).
$$

Hence $T_t=\kappa T_{yy}$ exactly when **$\alpha=-\kappa k^2$**, proving the claimed [diffusion equation](../../../../../../diffusion-equation-split.md) solution. At $t=0$, $e^0=1$, so **$T(0,y)=A\cos(2\pi y/\lambda)$**. The infinite spatial domain needs no end-boundary conditions. For positive [thermal diffusivity](../../../../../../thermal-diffusivity.md) the [amplitude](../../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) decreases, as described by the [thermal diffusion rate of a Fourier mode](../../../../../../thermal-diffusion-rate-of-a-fourier-mode.md).
