<h1 id="6/g/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

First measure the generator’s spectrum, timing and propagation pattern on quiet records. If practicable, coordinate shutdowns or quiet acquisition windows; otherwise place source/receiver lines to reduce coupling to the noise source and use good ground coupling and adequate instrument dynamic range. Saturated channels cannot be repaired by a later filter.

Separate noise using its measured [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) and moveout: a band-pass/high-pass filter can reject [frequencies](../../../../../../frequency.md) below the useful seismic band, and an appropriate narrow notch can reject a narrow coherent component. Avoid deleting wanted low-frequency arrivals along with the generator noise. Dense spatial sampling enables [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md)–wavenumber filtering or array methods to separate low-apparent-velocity surface noise from target arrivals. A reference sensor near the generator can support coherent-noise subtraction when the transfer is stable.

Repeated shots/sweeps and stacking improve the [signal-to-noise ratio](../../../../../../signal-to-noise-ratio.md) for uncorrelated noise; persistent coherent generator noise does not necessarily average down. Known-sweep [Vibroseis correlation](../../../../../../vibroseis-correlation.md) also helps reject unrelated noise. Verify the final processing against test gathers to ensure that real arrivals have not been mistaken for noise.
