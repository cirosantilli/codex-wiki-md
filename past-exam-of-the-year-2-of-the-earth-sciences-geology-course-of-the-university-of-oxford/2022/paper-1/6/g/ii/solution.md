<h1 id="6/g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Choose shot and receiver intervals to sample the shortest relevant spatial [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md) and steepest event moveout without [spatial aliasing](../../../../../../spatial-aliasing.md). A simple bound is

$$
\Delta x\lesssim\frac{v_{\mathrm{app,min}}}{2f_{\max}},
$$

using the smallest apparent velocity of the events one needs to retain or separate. Using $1500\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $100$ Hz alone suggests $7.5$ m, but slower surface waves or dipping events can demand finer spacing. It is not a universal final design interval.

Also consider target depth and lateral resolution, [reflector dip](../../../../../../reflector-dip.md), near-surface heterogeneity, source strength and coupling, maximum offset and aperture, common-midpoint bin size and [seismic acquisition fold](../../../../../../seismic-acquisition-fold.md), refraction crossover distances, access and budget. Receiver spacing and source spacing jointly determine coverage and cannot be selected from temporal sampling alone.
