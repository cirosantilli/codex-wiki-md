<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For an approximately [isotropic](../../../../../isotropy.md) elastic solid,

$$
V_P=\sqrt{\frac{K+4\mu/3}{\rho}},\qquad V_S=\sqrt{\frac\mu\rho},
$$

where $K$ is [bulk modulus](../../../../../bulk-modulus.md), $\mu$ is [shear modulus](../../../../../shear-modulus.md) and $\rho$ is [mass density](../../../../../density.md). Five relevant controls are:

- **Mineral composition and texture:** different elastic moduli and densities give different [seismic velocities](../../../../../seismic-velocity.md); compliant clay-rich material generally differs from crystalline igneous framework rock.
- **[Porosity](../../../../../porosity.md), cracks and fractures:** compliant voids reduce the effective moduli and usually reduce both speeds; aligned fractures produce [seismic anisotropy](../../../../../seismic-anisotropy.md).
- **Effective confinement:** increasing [effective stress](../../../../../effective-stress.md) closes compliant cracks and tends to raise speeds. Higher [pore fluid pressure](../../../../../pore-fluid-pressure.md) at fixed total confinement can reopen or preserve cracks and lower the effective stiffness.
- **Fluid saturation and phase:** replacing gas by liquid commonly increases the saturated [bulk modulus](../../../../../bulk-modulus.md) and $V_P$; fluids have no static shear rigidity, so $V_S$ changes through the frame, density and frequency-dependent interactions rather than a direct fluid [shear modulus](../../../../../shear-modulus.md).
- **[Temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and alteration:** heating commonly reduces elastic moduli and may create thermal cracks; [hydrothermal alteration](../../../../../hydrothermal-alteration.md) can replace strong minerals by clay or other weaker phases. The sign depends on alteration products and crack sealing, so reservoir-specific measurements are needed.

These controls interact: a dense rock is not automatically faster if its moduli are also different.
