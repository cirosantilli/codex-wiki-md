<h1 id="6/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Choose using target depth, scale, expected contrasts, overburden, noise and required result. A **[seismic reflection survey](../../../../../seismic-reflection-survey.md)** uses reflected travel times and [amplitudes](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) to image interfaces; it needs useful [acoustic impedance](../../../../../acoustic-impedance.md) contrasts, sufficient bandwidth, suitable offsets and dense coverage for [moveout correction](../../../../../moveout-correction.md), stacking and [seismic migration](../../../../../seismic-migration.md). A large clay-cap/igneous contrast gives a potentially strong reflection here, but fractures, scattering and attenuation can obscure coherent events. At $100$ Hz the nominal quarter-wavelength [vertical seismic resolution](../../../../../vertical-seismic-resolution.md) is about $3.75$ m in the claystone and $8.75$ m in the igneous rock, if that [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) reaches the target.

A **[seismic refraction survey](../../../../../seismic-refraction-survey.md)** uses first arrivals and critically refracted waves to estimate velocities and interface structure. The increase from $1500$ to $3500\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ is favorable for a cap-base head-wave branch, provided the spread reaches sufficient offsets. Refraction/tomography may better constrain lateral velocity variations and fractures, but simple head-wave inversion can miss a low-velocity layer or a thin hidden layer and often has poorer boundary detail.

Use expected reflector continuity, velocity inversions, required aperture, access, near-surface complexity and cost to choose or combine the surveys. A pilot gather and travel-time modelling can test which phases will be measurable; the supplied velocities alone do not specify the reservoir depth or the best acquisition geometry.
