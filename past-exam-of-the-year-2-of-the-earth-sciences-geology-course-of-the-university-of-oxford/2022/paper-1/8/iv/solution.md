<h1 id="8/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Let $\ell_N(x)=3.44-x$ and $\ell_A(x)=-7.59-3x$. The [common-potential test for a three-mineral activity diagram](../../../../../common-potential-test-for-a-three-mineral-activity-diagram.md) gives relative potentials, in one positive energy unit,

$$
\Phi_C=0,\qquad\Phi_N=\ell_N-y,\qquad\Phi_A=\ell_A-y.
$$

The stable phase minimizes these potentials. Consequently the **stable [nepheline](../../../../../nepheline.md)–[corundum](../../../../../corundum.md) branch has $x\le-5.515$**, the **stable [albite](../../../../../albite.md)–[corundum](../../../../../corundum.md) branch has $x\ge-5.515$**, and the **stable [nepheline](../../../../../nepheline.md)–[albite](../../../../../albite.md) branch has $x=-5.515$, $y\ge8.955$**. Draw them solid.

The other halves of each line are **metastable**: a third mineral has lower potential there. In particular, below the triple point the vertical equality describes two phases both less stable than [corundum](../../../../../corundum.md). Extend those halves as dashed lines. This thermodynamic comparison prevents the incorrect use of all three complete pairwise lines as stable field boundaries.
