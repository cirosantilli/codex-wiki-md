<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Integrate the solids’ [molar volume](../../../../../molar-volume.md) contribution from $P^\circ=1$ bar and use ideal-gas water [chemical fugacity](../../../../../chemical-fugacity.md) $f=P$. For pure solids,

$$
0=\Delta_rH^\circ-T\Delta_rS^\circ+\Delta V_s(P-P^\circ)
+RT\ln\frac{P}{P^\circ}.
$$

The [logarithm](../../../../../logarithm.md) is dimensionless, and $\Delta V_s=-2.28\,\mathrm{J\,bar^{-1}}$ can be multiplied directly by [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) in bars. Therefore the [dehydration equilibrium with ideal-gas fluid](../../../../../dehydration-equilibrium-with-ideal-gas-fluid.md) gives

$$
T=\frac{141120-2.28(6000-1)}{170.7-8.3145\ln6000}
=\frac{127442.28}{98.3679}.
$$

Hence **$T=1295.57$ K, or $1022.42^\circ$C**. Do not also add a constant gas-volume [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) term: the water contribution has already been integrated through $RT\ln(P/P^\circ)$. Ideal-gas behavior at $6$ kbar is a prescribed model approximation, not a realistic equation of state. The solid volume correction is distinct from it.
