<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use a **near-infrared/red ratio**, or its normalized form, the [normalized difference vegetation index](../../../../../normalized-difference-vegetation-index.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{NDVI}=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{NIR}}-\rho_{\mathrm{red}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{NIR}}+\rho_{\mathrm{red}}}.}
$$

Useful bands are approximately $0.63$–$0.69\,\mu\mathrm m$ in red and $0.75$–$0.90\,\mu\mathrm m$ in near infrared, with exact ranges depending on the sensor. [Chlorophyll](../../../../../chlorophyll.md) absorbs strongly in red, whereas leaf structure scatters near infrared efficiently; healthy green vegetation therefore has a large near-infrared/red ratio and positive [NDVI](../../../../../normalized-difference-vegetation-index.md). Soil and water have different spectral contrasts.[Normalized Difference Vegetation Index: visible absorption, near-infrared scattering and the normalized ratio](../../../../../measuring-vegetation-by-nasa-earth-observatory.md)

A single value is not a direct census of vegetation biomass or species. Soil background, canopy saturation, season, atmospheric effects and mixed pixels need consideration when mapping the distribution of vegetation.
