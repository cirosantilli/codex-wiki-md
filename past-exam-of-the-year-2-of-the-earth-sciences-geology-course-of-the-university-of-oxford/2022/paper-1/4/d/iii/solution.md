<h1 id="4/d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

If a restored channel or fan boundary has accumulated displacement $d$ since it formed, a constant [fault slip rate](../../../../../../fault-slip-rate.md) $v=1.6\,\mathrm{mm\,yr^{-1}}$ gives the [geomorphic offset-age relation](../../../../../../geomorphic-offset-age-relation.md)

$$
\boxed{t=\frac d v=625\,d_{\mathrm{metres}}\ \mathrm{yr}.}
$$

The right-hand channel jog is of order one to two of the printed $100$ m scale bars, so apparent offsets of order $100$–$200$ m give **about $62.5$–$125$ ka**. These are scale-based order-of-magnitude estimates, not surveyed piercing-point offsets: the amount of deflection along an eroding channel is not automatically equal to the tectonic displacement. For example, a reliably restored $150$ m marker would imply $93.75$ ka.

These times lie in the late [Pleistocene](../../../../../../pleistocene.md), when global glacial–interglacial changes could alter vegetation, [sediment](../../../../../../sediment.md) supply and runoff and thereby promote [alluvial fan](../../../../../../alluvial-fan.md) deposition or abandonment. The wide interval spans different climatic phases, so it does not identify one event or prove a climatic cause. More importantly, a channel cut into a fan usually dates channel establishment or abandonment; the fan [sediment](../../../../../../sediment.md) can be older. Independent [optically stimulated luminescence dating](../../../../../../optically-stimulated-luminescence-dating.md), stratigraphic correlation and several matched offsets are needed to assign a depositional age and test a climatic correlation.
