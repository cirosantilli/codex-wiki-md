A useful **[deflected stream](../../../../../../deflected-stream.md)** is the conspicuous incised drainage in the right-hand part of the image. It approaches the fault corridor from the upper-right, follows a short segment along the trace near $(u,v)\simeq(0.83,0.60)$, then resumes down the fan toward the lower-left. Mark the two bends and the intervening along-fault reach, rather than labeling the whole channel as a fault.

A candidate **[beheaded stream](../../../../../../beheaded-stream.md)** is the short downstream gully in the fan immediately to the right of the central wide river, near $(u,v)\simeq(0.56,0.44)$: its head terminates at or near the cross-cutting fault corridor without an obvious continuation into a substantial upstream catchment. Mark it as a candidate abandoned/cut-off channel, and distinguish it from the active continuous drainage farther right. A displaced upstream counterpart, [sediment](../../../../../../sediment.md) infill or a field profile would strengthen that identification; optical appearance alone cannot uniquely establish every former channel connection.
