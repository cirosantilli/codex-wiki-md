<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The thin, pale, steep feature beside the fingers is plausibly a **mineral-filled opening fracture, a [mineral vein](../../../../../mineral-vein.md)**, rather than the whole coarse [fault breccia](../../../../../fault-breccia.md) band. [Calcite](../../../../../calcite.md) is a plausible filling in this appearance, but its mineral identity would need a field or laboratory test. Opening normal to its approximately vertical north–south plane requires the smallest [principal stress](../../../../../principal-stress.md) to be approximately east–west, consistent with the extension direction above.

For example, a compression-positive [effective stress](../../../../../effective-stress.md) tensor

$$
\boldsymbol\sigma'=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\0&4&0\\0&0&9.3961\end{pmatrix}\ \mathrm{MPa}
$$

has east–west tension and can open a weak fracture whose [tensile strength](../../../../../tensile-strength.md) is at most $1$ MPa. More generally opening requires $\sigma_3-P_f<-T_0$, where $T_0$ is [tensile strength](../../../../../tensile-strength.md). This illustrates [hydraulic opening of a mineral vein](../../../../../hydraulic-opening-of-a-mineral-vein.md): raising $P_f$ by $11$ MPa at the illustrative total stresses in part c translates all effective [principal stresses](../../../../../principal-stress.md) by $-11$ MPa, producing this tensor without rotating its axes.

The vein and [fault slip](../../../../../fault-slip.md) need not have formed simultaneously at those exact magnitudes: [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) transients, local stress changes and slip can alternate. Their orientations can nevertheless reflect the same normal-faulting stress regime. **The source’s reference to “part d” is circular; the meaningful comparison is with part c.**
