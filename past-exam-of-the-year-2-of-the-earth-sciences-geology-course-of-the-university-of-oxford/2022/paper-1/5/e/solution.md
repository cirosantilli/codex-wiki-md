<h1 id="5/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The supplied south–north profile has a large southern/interior negative trough and a sharp positive peak toward the northern rim. That is opposite to the simplest positive, presently induced magnetic body in question 5(d). It therefore does **not** justify interpreting the crater as a concentration of strongly magnetic meteorite material.

A negative [magnetization contrast](../../../../../magnetization-contrast.md) from replacing magnetic [basalt](../../../../../basalt.md) already reverses the simple induced-body response. In addition, the target [Deccan Traps](../../../../../deccan-traps.md) can retain strong [remanent magnetization](../../../../../remanent-magnetization.md) whose direction differs from the present [geomagnetic field](../../../../../geomagnetic-field.md); crater excavation and rim geometry then produce a strong [magnetic topographic effect](../../../../../magnetic-topographic-effect.md). Rock-magnetic measurements and forward modelling distinguish these contributions. A primary study of this crater attributes most of its anomaly to magnetized target [basalts](../../../../../basalt.md) and topography, with a comparatively small contribution from the breccia and post-impact [sediment](../../../../../sediment.md).[Sections 4.2, 5 and 6: target-rock remanence, terrain modelling and weak infill contribution](../../../../../magnetic-anomaly-and-model-of-the-lonar-meteorite-impact-crater-in-maharashtra-india-by-kalle-kiik-and-others.md)

**The curve alone is not a unique diagnosis of reversed polarity, impact-induced [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) or a buried iron mass.** The physical conclusion supported by the comparison is that a positive induced-body model is inadequate; inherited target-rock [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md), its removal and geometry must be modelled.
