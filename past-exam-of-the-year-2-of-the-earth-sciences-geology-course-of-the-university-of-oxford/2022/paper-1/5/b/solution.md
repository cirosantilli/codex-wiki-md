<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Treat the quoted anomaly as the response of a slab of thickness $h$ replacing host rock. The [Bouguer slab](../../../../../bouguer-slab.md) formula applies to the [geophysical density contrast](../../../../../geophysical-density-contrast.md), not the absolute density:

$$
\Delta g=2\pi G(\rho_b-\rho_h)h.
$$

With $\Delta g=-2.5\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, $h=400$ m and $\rho_h=2750\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$,

$$
\rho_b=2750+\frac{-2.5\times10^{-5}}{2\pi(6.67430\times10^{-11})(400)}
=2750-149.04.
$$

Thus **$\rho_b\simeq2601\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$** in the slab approximation. The negative anomaly requires less dense infill. A finite bowl has a different geometric response, and water, post-impact [sediment](../../../../../sediment.md) and the regional baseline can matter; the stated calculation is the requested slab estimate. The plotted minimum appears closer to $-2$ mGal, but the numerical instruction explicitly supplies $-2.5$ mGal and controls this calculation.
