<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Collect a **[magnetic survey](../../../../../magnetic-survey.md)** along the traverse with a calibrated total-field magnetometer, recording time, accurately surveyed position and sensor height. Extend observations beyond the crater to establish the background, repeat stations or tie lines to test consistency, and use a stationary [base-station magnetometer](../../../../../base-station-magnetometer.md) to monitor temporal field changes. Keep the instrument away from vehicles, fences and magnetic equipment; map unavoidable cultural sources rather than treating them as geological signals.

Correct instrument offsets and heading effects where applicable, remove measured diurnal variation, reject or separately treat magnetic-storm intervals, and subtract the appropriate large-scale [geomagnetic field](../../../../../geomagnetic-field.md) at the survey epoch. Level overlapping traverses and state whether a local regional trend has also been removed. Unlike gravity, there is no universal rock-independent Bouguer correction for magnetic topography: a [magnetic topographic effect](../../../../../magnetic-topographic-effect.md) needs a model of terrain and [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md).

Measure representative [magnetic susceptibility](../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md) and [remanent magnetization](../../../../../remanent-magnetization.md) on oriented rock samples to interpret the profile. Total field alone does not tell whether a contrast comes from induced or [remanent magnetization](../../../../../remanent-magnetization.md), crater excavation, or strongly magnetic material in the infill.
