<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use **[airborne lidar](../../../../../airborne-lidar.md) with sufficient pulse density and ground-return classification**. Laser travel times give ranges; the aircraft’s surveyed position and orientation place returns in three dimensions. Multiple returns include canopy and, where gaps permit, the ground. Classify ground points, remove vegetation returns and interpolate a bare-ground [digital elevation model](../../../../../digital-elevation-model.md) over the $20\,\mathrm{km^2}$ strip.

This specifically addresses the forest-obscured [fault scarp](../../../../../fault-scarp.md), unlike ordinary optical stereo, which primarily models the visible canopy. Leaf-off acquisition can improve ground sampling where relevant. Dense understorey and a complete lack of ground returns still cause uncertainty; interpolation does not create observed ground heights. Inspect ground-point coverage and compare hillshades under several illumination directions.[Point clouds, vegetation removal and the bare-earth product](../../../../../what-is-the-difference-between-lidar-data-and-a-digital-elevation-model-by-united-states-geological-survey.md)
