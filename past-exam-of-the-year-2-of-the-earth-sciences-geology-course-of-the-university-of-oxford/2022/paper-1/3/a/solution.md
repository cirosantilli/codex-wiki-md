<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For regional coverage, use **[stereo photogrammetry](../../../../../stereo-photogrammetry.md) from overlapping satellite or airborne imagery**, or a radar-derived [digital elevation model](../../../../../digital-elevation-model.md) where suitable data exist. Match the same terrain features in images taken from different known viewing positions, infer their three-dimensional positions by triangulation, and georeference and grid the result. Satellite stereo or interferometric radar can cover $10^4\,\mathrm{km^2}$ efficiently; choose pixel size and vertical precision fine enough for the [fault scarps](../../../../../fault-scarp.md) of interest, rather than assuming every regional model resolves small scarps.

[InSAR](../../../../../interferometric-synthetic-aperture-radar.md) topographic mapping uses the phase difference from a known viewing baseline; repeat-pass deformation work must separate that height contribution from actual motion. Vegetation, cloud, radar shadow and steep slopes can leave gaps or represent the canopy rather than bare ground.[Topographic mapping with separated viewing positions and the known baseline; DEM construction](../../../../../interferometry-by-nasa.md) Ground control and independent elevation checks are needed before interpreting small relief as active [faulting](../../../../../faulting.md).
