<h1 id="3/d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A [blind reverse fault](../../../../../../blind-reverse-fault.md) can shorten and bend its cover into a [fault-propagation fold](../../../../../../fault-propagation-fold.md) or other growing [anticline](../../../../../../anticline.md). At the [fold](../../../../../../fold.md) crest, bending can create shallow extension and **secondary [normal faults](../../../../../../normal-fault.md) or [tension fissures](../../../../../../tension-fissure.md)**, even though the regional deformation is shortening. Bedding-parallel [flexural slip](../../../../../../flexural-slip.md), local [backthrusts](../../../../../../backthrust.md) and [fold scarps](../../../../../../fold-scarp.md) can also break the surface.

Thus a shallow surface break need not be the upward continuation of the main seismogenic [thrust fault](../../../../../../thrust-fault.md). The expected association is distributed deformation related to the uplifted [fold](../../../../../../fold.md); its detailed pattern depends on cover layering, [fold](../../../../../../fold.md) geometry and shallow strength. The broad frontal relief in Attachment A is compatible with this possibility, but the image alone does not establish a unique earthquake rupture history.
