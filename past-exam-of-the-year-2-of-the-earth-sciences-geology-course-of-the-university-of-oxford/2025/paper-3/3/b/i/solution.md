<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a plane whose normal lies in the $\sigma_1$–$\sigma_3$ plane, rotate through $2\theta$ from $(\sigma_1,0)$ in the stated sign convention. The circle point gives

$$
\boxed{\sigma_n=C+R\cos2\theta=\sigma_1\cos^2\theta+\sigma_3\sin^2\theta,\qquad\tau=R\sin2\theta.}
$$

The normal-to-plane angle is essential: if the angle supplied is between the **plane itself** and $\sigma_1$, use its complement for $\theta$. The [principal stress planes](../../../../../../principal-stress-plane.md) have zero [shear stress](../../../../../../shear-stress.md), and planes at $45^\circ$ to those [principal stress planes](../../../../../../principal-stress-plane.md) have maximum $|\tau|$.

For an arbitrary plane in a fully three-dimensional [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) field, let $n_i$ be its direction cosines in the [principal stress](../../../../../../principal-stress.md) basis. Then

$$
\sigma_n=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sigma_i n_i^2,\qquad |\tau|=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sigma_i^2n_i^2-\sigma_n^2}.
$$

These follow by resolving the [traction](../../../../../../traction.md) into its normal and tangential components; the compression-positive tensor is the negative of the usual tension-positive [Cauchy stress tensor](../../../../../../cauchy-stress-tensor.md). The family of [Mohr circles](../../../../../../mohr-s-circle.md) visualizes the possible combinations, but the two-dimensional $2\theta$ rule alone does not specify an arbitrary three-dimensional orientation.[Lecture 10, stress tensor, traction resolution and Mohr construction; signs here are explicitly compression positive.](../../../../../../stress-and-strength-lecture-notes-by-mit-opencourseware.md)
