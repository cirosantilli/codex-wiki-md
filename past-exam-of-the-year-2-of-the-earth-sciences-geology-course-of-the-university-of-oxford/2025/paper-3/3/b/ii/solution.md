<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Draw a [failure envelope](../../../../../../failure-envelope.md) in the $(\sigma_n',\tau)$ plane. A useful brittle shear-failure approximation is the [Mohr-Coulomb failure criterion](../../../../../../mohr-coulomb-failure-criterion.md)

$$
|\tau|=c+\mu_f\sigma_n',\qquad\mu_f=\tan\varphi,
$$

where $c$ is [cohesion](../../../../../../cohesion.md), $\varphi$ the [internal friction angle](../../../../../../internal-friction-angle.md), and $\sigma_n'$ the effective compressive [normal stress](../../../../../../normal-stress.md). A circle wholly inside the envelope is below this ideal failure threshold. Increasing the [differential stress](../../../../../../differential-stress.md) expands it until it first touches the envelope: the tangent points identify the failing plane orientations. For a linear envelope, tangency implies

$$
R=C'\sin\varphi+c\cos\varphi,\qquad \sigma_1'=\sigma_3'\frac{1+\sin\varphi}{1-\sin\varphi}+\frac{2c\cos\varphi}{1-\sin\varphi}.
$$

With the angle convention above, the upper tangent point is at $2\theta=90^\circ+\varphi$. Its plane therefore makes $45^\circ-\varphi/2$ with the maximum-compression direction; there is a conjugate plane on the other side. For $\varphi=30^\circ$, this is $30^\circ$, rather than the $45^\circ$ plane of maximum [shear stress](../../../../../../shear-stress.md). An existing weak [fault](../../../../../../geological-fault.md) can reactivate first, with its own lower [cohesion](../../../../../../cohesion.md) and friction, and need not have this optimum new-fracture orientation.

For tensile [brittle fracture](../../../../../../brittle-fracture.md), add a [tensile strength](../../../../../../tensile-strength.md) cutoff: failure occurs when $\sigma_3'\leq-T_0$, where $T_0>0$. The opening fracture is approximately perpendicular to the minimum-compression direction. Real [rock](../../../../../../rock-geology.md) envelopes curve, especially at small confinement; a straight shear envelope must not be extrapolated as an exact tensile law. High confinement can instead favor distributed or ductile deformation, so merely drawing a larger circle does not establish that brittle failure is the active mechanism.[Lecture 10, frictional strength and Mohr construction; the tensile cutoff is a separate condition.](../../../../../../stress-and-strength-lecture-notes-by-mit-opencourseware.md)
