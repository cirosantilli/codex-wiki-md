<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**The [P wave](../../../../../p-wave.md) source is normally the more useful depth source for the end members and for the stated approximately 50% gas case**, because the predicted $|R_p|$ exceeds $|R_s|$, making the reservoir-top arrival easier to identify, assuming comparable source and recording quality. For a horizontal reflector and vertical two-way [travel time](../../../../../travel-time.md) $t$ entirely through the homogeneous cap rock,

$$
h=\frac{V_{p1}t_p}{2}=750t_p\ \mathrm m,
$$

or $h=V_{s1}t_s/2=300t_s\ \mathrm m$ for an identified [S wave](../../../../../s-wave.md) arrival, with times in seconds. The reservoir's own [wave speed](../../../../../wave-speed.md) is not the overburden [wave speed](../../../../../wave-speed.md) used in this conversion. Nonzero-offset data require [moveout correction](../../../../../moveout-correction.md) and an appropriate overburden [velocity model](../../../../../velocity-model.md); no numerical depth can be obtained without a measured time.

At approximately 50% gas, Figure 1 gives $\rho\simeq1.5\ \mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$, $V_p\simeq1.05\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, and $V_s\simeq0.62$–$0.63\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. Thus

$$
Z_{p2}\simeq1.58\times10^6,\quad R_p\simeq-0.18,\qquad Z_{s2}\simeq(0.93\text{–}0.945)\times10^6,\quad R_s\simeq+0.02.
$$

The [S wave](../../../../../s-wave.md) reflector is then weak because its impedance is nearly matched to the cap rock. An exact [impedance match](../../../../../impedance-match.md), $\rho_2V_{s2}=\rho_1V_{s1}$, gives $R_s=0$: a reservoir top can exist without producing an observable normal-incidence [S wave](../../../../../s-wave.md) reflection. Rounding the 50% $V_s$ to $0.60$ would suggest exactly that cancellation, but the plotted line sits slightly higher. The continuous $Z_s$ curve crosses the cap-rock value at a somewhat greater saturation; the coarse figure does not justify a precise percentage.

There is also a smaller-saturation [P wave](../../../../../p-wave.md) impedance match: $R_p$ changes sign between zero and modest gas saturation, so it must pass through zero. Thus neither source guarantees a detectable reflector at every saturation. Joint [P wave](../../../../../p-wave.md) and [S wave](../../../../../s-wave.md) observations help distinguish a weak impedance contrast from an absent reservoir; a slower [S wave](../../../../../s-wave.md) speed alone is no reason to prefer it when its reflected amplitude is near zero.
