<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $S_g$ be the fraction of the [pore space](../../../../../pore-space.md) occupied by [gas](../../../../../gas.md), and let $\phi$ be the [porosity](../../../../../porosity.md). At fixed [porosity](../../../../../porosity.md) and unchanged [rock](../../../../../rock-geology.md) framework, the bulk [density](../../../../../density.md) is

$$
\rho=(1-\phi)\rho_s+\phi\bigl[(1-S_g)\rho_w+S_g\rho_g\bigr].
$$

Replacing [water](../../../../../water.md) with much less dense [gas](../../../../../gas.md) therefore decreases the bulk [density](../../../../../density.md) approximately linearly, as in Figure 1. The liquid and gas [densities](../../../../../density.md) here are those at reservoir conditions.

A [pore fluid](../../../../../pore-fluid.md) has negligible static [shear modulus](../../../../../shear-modulus.md), so the [rock](../../../../../rock-geology.md) framework chiefly controls the effective [shear modulus](../../../../../shear-modulus.md) $\mu$. If $\mu$ changes little, $V_s=\sqrt{\mu/\rho}$ increases gently as the [density](../../../../../density.md) falls. This does **not** imply that [S waves](../../../../../s-wave.md) travel through the pore [gas](../../../../../gas.md) itself: their restoring force comes from the connected solid framework.

The [P wave](../../../../../p-wave.md) speed depends on both [bulk modulus](../../../../../bulk-modulus.md) and [shear modulus](../../../../../shear-modulus.md):

$$
V_p=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa+4\mu/3}{\rho}}.
$$

[Gas](../../../../../gas.md) is much more compressible than [water](../../../../../water.md). Even a small connected gas fraction can strongly reduce the effective fluid [bulk modulus](../../../../../bulk-modulus.md), approximately through the compliance average $\kappa_f^{-1}\simeq(1-S_g)/\kappa_w+S_g/\kappa_g$ for a pressure-equilibrated mixture. The saturated [rock](../../../../../rock-geology.md)'s effective [bulk modulus](../../../../../bulk-modulus.md) consequently falls rapidly at first, overwhelming the smaller [density](../../../../../density.md) reduction and causing the sharp initial fall in $V_p$. Once the fluid is already very compliant, further gas replacement changes the stiffness less; the continuing [density](../../../../../density.md) decrease can then make $V_p$ rise again. Figure 1 assumes a particular saturation response: patchy fluids, changing effective [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md), and frequency-dependent fluid motion can alter it. The plotted $V_s$ is only nearly constant, so the plot should not be interpreted as an exact fixed-$\mu$ constitutive model.
