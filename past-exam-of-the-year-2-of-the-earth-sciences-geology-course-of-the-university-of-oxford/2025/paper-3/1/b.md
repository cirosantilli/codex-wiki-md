For incidence from cap rock (1) into reservoir (2), use the conventional normal-incidence stress-amplitude [reflection coefficient](../../../../reflection-coefficient.md)

$$
R_j=\frac{Z_{j2}-Z_{j1}}{Z_{j2}+Z_{j1}},\qquad Z_{pj}=\rho_jV_{pj},\quad Z_{sj}=\rho_jV_{sj}.
$$

Here [acoustic impedance](../../../../acoustic-impedance.md) is the [P wave](../../../../p-wave.md) impedance; $\rho V_s$ is the corresponding [shear-wave impedance](../../../../shear-wave-impedance.md). Positive $R$ preserves the chosen stress-wave [seismic reflection polarity](../../../../seismic-reflection-polarity.md); negative $R$ reverses it. These are amplitude coefficients, not reflected [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) fractions. At normal incidence in the ideal isotropic model there is no P-to-S [mode conversion](../../../../mode-conversion.md). The cap-rock impedances are

$$
Z_{p1}=2.25\times10^6,\qquad Z_{s1}=0.900\times10^6\quad\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}.
$$

The following are **approximate readings of the supplied Figure 1 PNG**, checked against the matching source PDF, rather than exact tabulated parameters. At zero gas, use $\rho\simeq1.75\ \mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$, $V_p\simeq1.75\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, and $V_s\simeq0.60\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$; at full gas use $\rho\simeq1.25\ \mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$, $V_p\simeq1.15\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, and $V_s\simeq0.65\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. The left-hand $V_p$ endpoint is crowded against the axis and the [density](../../../../density.md) curve; readings differing by roughly $0.05$ in the plotted units are reasonable. The resulting coefficients deserve about two significant figures, not the apparent precision of the intermediate arithmetic.

Both end members change [seismic reflection polarity](../../../../seismic-reflection-polarity.md), but the [P wave](../../../../p-wave.md) change is much larger: approximately $+0.15$ to $-0.22$, compared with $+0.08$ to $-0.05$ for [S waves](../../../../s-wave.md). Thus a calibrated [P wave](../../../../p-wave.md) survey is better for distinguishing these two saturation end members and should give a stronger reflection from the fully gas-filled reservoir. A negative [P wave](../../../../p-wave.md) reflection relative to the water-filled prediction is a useful gas indicator in this stipulated model. In a real [seismic reflection survey](../../../../seismic-reflection-survey.md), lithology, [porosity](../../../../porosity.md), effective [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md), and [seismic wavelet](../../../../seismic-wavelet.md) phase also affect the response, so a bright or reversed reflection alone does not prove the presence of [gas](../../../../gas.md).
