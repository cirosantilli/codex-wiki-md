[Chlorophyll a](../../../../../chlorophyll-a.md) occurs in nearly all oxygen-producing [phytoplankton](../../../../../phytoplankton.md) groups and is central to their [photosynthesis](../../../../../photosynthesis.md). Its concentration can be measured reproducibly by pigment extraction or calibrated optical methods, and its absorption affects [ocean colour](../../../../../ocean-colour.md), making it observable over large areas. It is therefore a practical common index of photosynthetic standing stock across diverse communities, unlike a pigment limited to one group.

It is a **proxy**, not a fixed conversion to [phytoplankton biomass](../../../../../phytoplankton-biomass.md) or [primary production](../../../../../primary-production.md). The carbon-to-chlorophyll ratio varies with species, light acclimation, nutrient supply and physiological condition; low-light cells often contain more pigment per unit carbon. Dead pigment and methodological differences can also affect interpretation, and nonphotosynthetic plankton are not represented. A pigment increase need not mean the same proportional increase in living carbon biomass.[Chlorophyll alters sunlight absorption and reflection, enabling mapping of near-surface phytoplankton distributions; pigment is used as an index rather than a direct carbon measurement.](../../../../../chlorophyll-maps-by-nasa-earth-observatory.md)
