The [photic zone](../../../../../photic-zone.md) is the illuminated layer supporting [photosynthesis](../../../../../photosynthesis.md); its [euphotic depth](../../../../../euphotic-depth.md) is conventionally associated with about 1% of surface [photosynthetically active radiation](../../../../../photosynthetically-active-radiation.md), integrated over approximately $400$–$700\ \mathrm{nm}$. This is a useful convention rather than a universal organism-specific compensation depth. Spectral filtering can be represented locally by

$$
E_d(z,\lambda)=E_d(0,\lambda)\exp\!\left[-\int_0^zK_d(\zeta,\lambda)\,d\zeta\right],
$$

where $K_d$ is the wavelength-dependent [diffuse attenuation coefficient](../../../../../diffuse-attenuation-coefficient.md). It includes effects of absorption and scattering and is not simply equal to the absorption coefficient $a$.

In clear, low-chlorophyll water, pure [water](../../../../../water.md) removes red light strongly, while weak blue pigment and CDOM absorption permits blue light, typically around $450$–$490\ \mathrm{nm}$, to penetrate furthest. The deepest usable light is consequently dim and blue-rich, and the [euphotic zone](../../../../../euphotic-zone.md) can extend relatively deep. At high pigment concentrations, [phytoplankton pigments](../../../../../phytoplankton-pigment.md) preferentially remove blue and red light; associated detritus or CDOM can remove still more blue. The remaining transmission window commonly shifts toward blue-green or green wavelengths, roughly $500$–$570\ \mathrm{nm}$, and the total light falls off faster, so the [euphotic depth](../../../../../euphotic-depth.md) is shallower. Exact window and depth depend on the full particle/dissolved mixture, not chlorophyll alone. The diagram normalizes the two deep spectra separately to show their different shapes, not equal absolute light at a common depth.

This confines photosynthetic growth to a thinner illuminated interval in high-chlorophyll water and allows a deeper potential habitat in clear low-chlorophyll water, subject also to nutrient supply, mixing and the organism's compensation requirement. Cells near the base benefit from low-light acclimation and pigment systems that capture the **remaining** wavelengths. In blue-rich deep ocean water, blue-absorbing pigments and some low-light-adapted picoplankton can be favored; where the surviving light is greener, groups with effective green-absorbing [accessory pigments](../../../../../accessory-pigment.md), such as certain [phycobilins](../../../../../phycobilin.md), gain an advantage. A pigment appears coloured by light it fails to absorb, so green-absorbing accessory pigments need not themselves look green. There is no universal deep species determined by chlorophyll concentration alone. A deep chlorophyll maximum can partly reflect increased pigment per cell rather than a proportional maximum in carbon [biomass](../../../../../biomass.md).[Photic-zone discussion: the 1% convention, deep penetration in clear blue waters, and shallower illuminated habitat in productive turbid waters; pigment-based community selection here follows the surviving wavelengths and is conditional.](../../../../../phytopia-s-light-and-color-by-nasa.md)
