A [proton-precession magnetometer](../../../../../proton-precession-magnetometer.md) measures the scalar magnitude $F=|\mathbf B|$ of the local [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md). Its sensor contains a proton-rich liquid, such as a hydrocarbon or suitable water-based fluid. A strong current through a coil briefly polarizes the nuclear [magnetic moments](../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md), producing a small net [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md). The polarizing field is directed sufficiently across the local [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) to leave a transverse component when the current is switched off.

The [protons](../../../../../proton.md) then undergo [Larmor precession](../../../../../larmor-precession.md) about the ambient [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md). Their rotating net [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) induces a decaying alternating [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) in a receiving coil. Measuring the [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) determines the field:

$$
\omega=\gamma_pF,\qquad f=\frac{\gamma_p}{2\pi}F,\qquad \frac{\gamma_p}{2\pi}\simeq42.58\ \mathrm{MHz\,T^{-1}}.
$$

For example, $50\ \mathrm{\mu T}$ gives about $2.13\ \mathrm{kHz}$. The known proton [gyromagnetic ratio](../../../../../gyromagnetic-ratio.md) provides an absolute frequency-to-field scale; this instrument does not measure the field's direction. Strong spatial [gradients](../../../../../gradient.md) cause different parts of the liquid to precess at different rates and shorten the usable signal, which matters very close to a buried steel object.[Introduction, “What is a total field magnetometer?”, and “Magnetic Environment”; scalar intensity and sensitivity to gradients.](../../../../../g-857-memory-mag-operation-manual-by-geometrics.md)
