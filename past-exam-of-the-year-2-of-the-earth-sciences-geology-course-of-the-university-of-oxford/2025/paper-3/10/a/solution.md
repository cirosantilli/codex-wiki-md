<h1 id="10/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Take base-ten [logarithms](../../../../../logarithm.md) and use dimensionless [thermodynamic activities](../../../../../thermodynamic-activity.md). The supplied concentration notation is interpreted in the ideal dilute-solution approximation, with a consistent reference concentration. Put

$$
x=\log_{10}a_{\mathrm{H_4SiO_4}},\qquad
y=\log_{10}(a_{\mathrm{Na^+}}/a_{\mathrm{H^+}}).
$$

With pure solids and unit water activity, the [reaction quotients](../../../../../reaction-quotient.md) are $Q_I=10^{2y+2x}$ and $Q_{II}=10^{2y+6x}$. At [chemical equilibrium](../../../../../chemical-equilibrium.md), $Q=K$, so the two lines on the [mineral activity diagram](../../../../../mineral-activity-diagram.md) are

$$
\boxed{I:\ y=3.44-x,\qquad II:\ y=-7.59-3x.}
$$

They have slopes $-1$ and $-3$. Restricting the plotted silica activity to $x<0$ is compatible with their intersection,

$$
x_*=-5.515,\qquad y_*=8.955.
$$

Their full straight extensions are equilibrium relations for the respective phase pairs; not every extension is a stable boundary when the third mineral is available.
