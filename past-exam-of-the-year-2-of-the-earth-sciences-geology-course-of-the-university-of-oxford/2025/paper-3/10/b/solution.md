<h1 id="10/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Subtract reaction (I) from reaction (II), cancelling the common aqueous ions and [corundum](../../../../../corundum.md), and divide by two. Choose the albite-to-nepheline direction:

$$
\mathrm{NaAlSi_3O_8}+4\mathrm{H_2O}
\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{NaAlSiO_4}+2\mathrm{H_4SiO_4}.
$$

This is [albite](../../../../../albite.md) plus water converting to [nepheline](../../../../../nepheline.md) and dissolved silica. [Standard Gibbs energies of reaction](../../../../../standard-gibbs-energy-of-reaction.md) add with their [stoichiometric coefficients](../../../../../stoichiometric-coefficient.md), so logarithms of [equilibrium constants](../../../../../equilibrium-constant.md) do too. For this single-formula normalization,

$$
\boxed{\log_{10}K_{III}=\frac{-15.18-6.88}{2}=-11.03.}
$$

The doubled reaction has $\log_{10}K=-22.06$, while reversing either reaction changes the sign. Specifying the direction and normalization removes that apparent ambiguity.
