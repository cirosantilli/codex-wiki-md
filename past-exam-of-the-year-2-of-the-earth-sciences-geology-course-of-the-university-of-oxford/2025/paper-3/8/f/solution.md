<h1 id="8/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The [wave equation](../../../../../wave-equation-split.md) and its homogeneous [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../../../../../dirichlet-boundary-condition.md) are linear, so any finite [linear combination](../../../../../linear-combination.md) of the separated [normal modes](../../../../../normal-mode.md) is another solution. More strongly, the [Fourier sine basis](../../../../../fourier-sine-basis.md) is complete in [L2 space](../../../../../l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space.md) on $(0,L)$. Expanding the initial fields in this basis yields

$$
\boxed{\eta(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\bigl[A_n\cos(\omega_nt)+B_n\sin(\omega_nt)\bigr]
\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}.}
$$

[Completeness of the Fourier sine basis](../../../../../completeness-of-the-fourier-sine-basis.md) is the reason the expansion covers general data; [linearity](../../../../../linearity.md) alone would not prove that every solution has this form. The convergence and differentiation are understood in a function space appropriate to the data. For finite [wave energy](../../../../../wave-energy.md), one can take $f\in H_0^1(0,L)$ and $g\in L^2(0,L)$, obtaining an energy solution; stronger smoothness and endpoint compatibility give a classical solution.
