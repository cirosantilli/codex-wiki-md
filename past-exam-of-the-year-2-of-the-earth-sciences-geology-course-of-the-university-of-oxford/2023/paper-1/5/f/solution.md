<h1 id="5/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

For the same geometry, field strength and [induced magnetization](../../../../../induced-magnetization.md) model at the opposite [magnetic inclination](../../../../../magnetic-inclination.md), replacing $I$ by $-I$ gives $\Delta T_{-I}(x)=\Delta T_I(-x)$. Thus **the north–south asymmetry reverses: the principal low moves to the northern side and the high to the southern side**. It is a horizontal reflection of the simple profile, rather than a necessary reversal of every anomaly's sign. At a magnetic pole the induced anomaly is symmetric; at the magnetic equator the symmetry is different again. If [remanent magnetization](../../../../../remanent-magnetization.md) dominates, a hemisphere change alone does not determine the new profile because that remanence direction also has to be specified.
