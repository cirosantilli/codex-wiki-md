<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Retain the same regional-background assumption as in part (b). The northern [pluton](../../../../../pluton.md) is about $2.0\ \mathrm{km}$ thick beneath the station near the northern trough, while its [gravity anomaly](../../../../../gravity-anomaly.md) is about $-114\ \mathrm{mGal}$. Its anomaly relative to the greenstone maximum is approximately $-14\ \mathrm{mGal}$. Therefore the contrast equation is

$$
-14\times10^{-5}=2\pi G\{(\rho_{\rm gr}-2670)(2000)-(\rho_{\rm gs}-2670)(1500)\}.
$$

Using the result of part (b), the second term contributes $15\ \mathrm{mGal}$, leaving a net pluton anomaly of about $+1\ \mathrm{mGal}$. Hence **$\rho_{\rm gr}\simeq2680\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, effectively close to the host-rock [density](../../../../../density.md) at the precision of this diagram**.

The apparent trough is strongly negative relative to the [greenstone belt](../../../../../greenstone-belt.md), but hardly negative relative to the southern host-rock background. If a northward regional trend is intended instead, it must be stated: in general,

$$
\rho_{\rm gr}\simeq2670+\frac{[-114-g_{\rm bg,N}]\,10^{-5}}{2\pi G(2000)},
$$

with both [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) values in [milligals](../../../../../milligal.md). For example, a northern background of $-109\ \mathrm{mGal}$ would give $\rho_{\rm gr}\simeq2610\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, but the same reference would also change part (b). No regional curve is explicitly identified in the supplied figure, so the numerical result must retain its reference-field assumption. This is the geological [nonuniqueness of geophysical inversion](../../../../../nonuniqueness-of-geophysical-inversion.md) of potential-field interpretation, rather than a reason to force a presumed textbook [granite](../../../../../granite.md) density onto the plot.
