<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Interpret the vertical, apparently unoffset fractures as opening [geological joints](../../../../../geological-joint.md). Their normals are east–west, so the least [principal stress](../../../../../principal-stress.md) $\sigma_3$ must be east–west and sufficiently tensile in [effective stress](../../../../../effective-stress.md). The other two [principal stresses](../../../../../principal-stress.md) lie within the vertical north–south [geological joint](../../../../../geological-joint.md) plane; their ordering cannot be recovered from this fracture orientation alone.

One example, with compression positive and coordinates east, north, up, is

$$
\boldsymbol\sigma'=\begin{pmatrix}-3&0&0\\0&5&0\\0&0&10\end{pmatrix}\ \mathrm{MPa}.
$$

Here $\sigma_1$ is vertical, $\sigma_2$ north–south, and $\sigma_3=-3\ \mathrm{MPa}$ east–west. The [geological joint](../../../../../geological-joint.md) opens east–west when its [tensile strength](../../../../../tensile-strength.md) is $3\ \mathrm{MPa}$. An isotropic [pore pressure](../../../../../pore-pressure.md) can make the east–west [effective stress](../../../../../effective-stress.md) tensile even while the total [normal stress](../../../../../normal-stress.md) remains compressive. Draw opening arrows pointing away from the vertical fracture, rather than sliding arrows along it.
