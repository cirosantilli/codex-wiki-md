A [reverse fault](../../../../../../reverse-fault.md) can create a sharp [mountain front](../../../../../../mountain-front.md) by raising its [hanging wall](../../../../../../hanging-wall.md) beside a lower [footwall](../../../../../../footwall.md). Drainages entering the rising block may be deflected along the front or cut narrow gorges across it. In cross-section, draw a dipping [fault](../../../../../../geological-fault.md) with the [hanging wall](../../../../../../hanging-wall.md) displaced up-dip and a surface-height contrast above the trace.

In Attachment A, mark the **approximately east–west to west-northwest–east-southeast break between the southwestern mountain block and the central basin**. Its approximate image coordinates run from $(0.03,0.56)$ through $(0.18,0.61)$ and $(0.34,0.65)$ to $(0.45,0.69)$. The abrupt topographic change and diversion of drainage along this front support the [reverse faulting](../../../../../../reverse-faulting.md) interpretation. It is the southern margin of the central basin, not the much straighter oblique valley farther northeast. The original interpretive figure gives a schematic section.
