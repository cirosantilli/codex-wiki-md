<h1 id="4/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

An overlapping pair of basin-dipping [normal fault](../../../../../../normal-fault.md) segments can first accommodate displacement through an **unbreached [relay ramp](../../../../../../relay-ramp.md)**. [Fault throw](../../../../../../fault-throw.md) tapers toward each tip and transfers between the strands; the intervening block tilts and bends while retaining a continuous ramp-like surface. Draw overlapping parallel traces in plan view and a tilted connecting surface between their different [fault throws](../../../../../../fault-throw.md).

With continued extension, the ramp can be **breached by a linking [fault](../../../../../../geological-fault.md)**. An oblique connector joins the segment tips and takes up a combination of dip-slip and strike-slip displacement; the system becomes a more continuous, zigzag [normal fault](../../../../../../normal-fault.md). Draw this connector diagonally through the overlap, with downthrown arrows on the basin side. Distributed small [faults](../../../../../../geological-fault.md) and [folding](../../../../../../folding.md) can accompany either arrangement. The central bend around $u\simeq0.6$ in Attachment B is the candidate transfer zone; the image alone does not uniquely establish the three-dimensional connection at depth.
