↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Mark a **[fault scarp](../../../../../../fault-scarp.md) cutting an [alluvial fan](../../../../../../alluvial-fan.md)** on the left-to-central smooth piedmont, approximately $(u,v)=(0.38,0.58)$. Its narrow, aligned height break continues across surfaces that are smoother than the mountains; repeated downthrow on the basin side produces the scarp. Sketch a once-continuous gently sloping fan surface cut by a basin-facing step.

Mark **[triangular facets](../../../../../../triangular-facet.md)** on the mountain front in the right-central part, near $(0.78,0.48)$. These are roughly triangular planar mountain faces bounded laterally by incised valleys and below by the [fault](../../../../../../geological-fault.md) front. In a front view, draw repeated triangles separated by stream valleys. [Triangular facets](../../../../../../triangular-facet.md) result from the interaction of [normal faulting](../../../../../../normal-faulting.md), uplift and erosion; neither they nor a sharp mountain front alone demonstrates a rupture in the particular historical event.
