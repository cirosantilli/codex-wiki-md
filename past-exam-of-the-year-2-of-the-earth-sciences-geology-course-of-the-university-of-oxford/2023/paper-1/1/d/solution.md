<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[Effective stress](../../../../../effective-stress.md) and frictional strength generally increase with depth through the shallow crust undergoing [brittle fracture](../../../../../brittle-fracture.md). At $1\ \mathrm{km}$, small overburden and often weak, fractured near-surface materials limit the [shear stress](../../../../../shear-stress.md) and [elastic energy](../../../../../elastic-energy.md) that can accumulate. Some shallow materials also undergo stable sliding rather than unstable [earthquake](../../../../../earthquake.md) nucleation. Near $15\ \mathrm{km}$, a locked [fault](../../../../../geological-fault.md) prone to [brittle fracture](../../../../../brittle-fracture.md) can sustain a much larger [differential stress](../../../../../differential-stress.md) before frictional instability releases stored [elastic energy](../../../../../elastic-energy.md).

This depth is plausibly near the lower, strong part of the seismogenic layer: below it, increasing [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) eventually promotes [material creep](../../../../../creep-deformation.md) across the [brittle-ductile transition](../../../../../brittle-ductile-transition.md). Once nucleated at depth, a dynamic [finite-source rupture](../../../../../finite-source-rupture.md) can propagate into shallow regions that could not independently nucleate the same event. Depth alone does not determine the nucleation point: local [pore pressure](../../../../../pore-pressure.md), frictional stability and [stress](../../../../../stress.md) heterogeneity also matter.
