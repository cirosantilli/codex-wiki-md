<h1 id="6/j/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [J](../j.md)

A transmitted P-wave [seismic head wave](../../../../../seismic-head-wave.md) occurs when its refraction angle reaches $90^\circ$, requiring $\sin i_c=V_{P,\rm incident}/V_{P,\rm transmitted}\leq1$. At the limestone–sandstone boundary the ratio is $2500/2000>1$: **there is no P-wave [seismic critical angle](../../../../../seismic-critical-angle.md) for incidence from limestone**. A slower lower layer cannot carry that transmitted head wave.

At the sandstone–salt boundary,

$$
\sin i_c=\frac{2000}{4000}=\frac12,\qquad \boxed{i_c=30.00^\circ}.
$$

The head wave travels along the faster salt boundary and radiates energy back into the slower overlying medium. Reversing the direction of incidence would change which interfaces admit such a head wave.
