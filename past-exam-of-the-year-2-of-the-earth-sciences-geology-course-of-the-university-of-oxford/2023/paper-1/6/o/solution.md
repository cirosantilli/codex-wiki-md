<h1 id="6/o/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [O](../o.md)

Apply the same stress-amplitude convention to S-wave [seismic impedance](../../../../../seismic-impedance.md). At limestone–sandstone,

$$
\boxed{R_S=\frac{2.4-3.3}{2.4+3.3}=-\frac3{19}=-0.1579,\qquad T_{\sigma S}=\frac{16}{19}=0.8421}.
$$

At sandstone–salt,

$$
\boxed{R_S=\frac{3.0-2.4}{3.0+2.4}=\frac19=0.1111,\qquad T_{\sigma S}=\frac{10}9=1.1111}.
$$

A stress-amplitude transmission greater than one does not violate [conservation of energy](../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md): the transmitted energy fraction includes the [seismic impedance](../../../../../seismic-impedance.md) ratio. For particle velocity, the corresponding $(R_v,T_v)$ pairs are $(3/19,22/19)$ and $(-1/9,8/9)$. At normal incidence in isotropic layers, pure P and pure S waves do not undergo [seismic mode conversion](../../../../../seismic-mode-conversion.md).
