<h1 id="6/k/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [K](../k.md)

For a P-to-S converted [seismic head wave](../../../../../seismic-head-wave.md), set the transmitted S-wave angle to $90^\circ$, giving $\sin i_c=V_{P,\rm incident}/V_{S,\rm transmitted}$. At the limestone–sandstone boundary, $2500/1200>1$, so **no transmitted S-wave head wave is possible** for incidence from limestone.

At the sandstone–salt boundary, using the stated interpretation $V_{S3}=3000\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$,

$$
\sin i_c=\frac{2000}{3000},\qquad \boxed{i_c=41.81^\circ}.
$$

The P-transmission [seismic critical angle](../../../../../seismic-critical-angle.md) is lower, $30^\circ$, but this does not forbid the converted S branch: at $41.81^\circ$ the transmitted P field is evanescent while the S field reaches grazing incidence.
