**Source notation assumption:** the second printed $V_{p3}=3000\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ is interpreted as $V_{s3}=3000\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Otherwise the S-wave velocity in salt is not supplied, and this part and the salt S-wave calculations below would have no numerical answer. Retain the printed [densities](../../../../../density.md), including the unusually low salt value, rather than silently replacing them with typical rock properties.

With that interpretation, [Snell law for elastic waves](../../../../../snell-law-for-elastic-waves.md) gives

$$
\sin t_S=\frac{3000}{2000}\sin20^\circ,\qquad \boxed{t_S=30.87^\circ}.
$$
