This leaf remains incomplete against that missing input.** The printed summary statistics nevertheless allow the following conditional numerical answer, using a two-sided $5\%$ [Student's t-test](../../../../../student-s-t-test.md) and independent observations with common [variance](../../../../../variance-split.md).

Test $H_0:\mu_W=\mu_S$ against $H_1:\mu_W\ne\mu_S$. The pooled [sample variance](../../../../../sample-variance.md) is

$$
s_p^2=\frac{(1430-1)(3.29)^2+(1569-1)(3.22)^2}{1430+1569-2}=10.5856824.
$$

The [test statistic](../../../../../test-statistic.md) and [statistical degrees of freedom](../../../../../statistical-degrees-of-freedom.md) are

$$
t=\frac{5.72-5.36}{\sqrt{10.5856824(1/1430+1/1569)}}=3.02646,\qquad \nu=2997.
$$

Using the [Student t-distribution](../../../../../student-s-t-distribution.md) directly gives a two-sided [p-value](../../../../../p-value.md) $p\simeq0.00250$ and a $5\%$ critical value $1.96076$. Thus **reject equal seasonal means at $5\%$ and $1\%$; the winter mean is higher by $0.36$ concentration units**. This does not establish a large physical effect or its cause.

The two [variances](../../../../../variance-split.md) are similar, and [Welch's t-test](../../../../../welch-s-t-test.md) gives essentially the same result: $t=3.02344$, $\nu\simeq2958.36$, $p\simeq0.00252$. Both calculations assume the counts represent independent observations. Serial dependence, repeated measurements and strong non-normality can invalidate nominal [p-values](../../../../../p-value.md); the summary table alone cannot diagnose them. The unequal means are a conclusion under these statistical assumptions, pending recovery of Attachment C.
