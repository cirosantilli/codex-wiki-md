<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Apply [upper-envelope stability for competing mineral dissolution reactions](../../../../../upper-envelope-stability-for-competing-mineral-dissolution-reactions.md) with $\ell_G=10.53-0.6x$ and $\ell_K=12.228-0.2x$. For each [chlorite](../../../../../chlorite.md) dissolution reaction,

$$
\frac{\Delta_rG_G}{RT\ln10}=5(y-\ell_G),\qquad \frac{\Delta_rG_K}{RT\ln10}=5(y-\ell_K).
$$

Therefore high $y$ suppresses dissolution and stabilizes [chlorite](../../../../../chlorite.md). Low $y$ favors the alteration products. To compare those products, the [gibbsite](../../../../../gibbsite.md)-to-[kaolinite](../../../../../kaolinite.md) reaction has $\Delta_rG/(RT\ln10)=-2x-8.49$: [kaolinite](../../../../../kaolinite.md) is favored at $x>-4.245$, and [gibbsite](../../../../../gibbsite.md) at $x<-4.245$.

The stable fields are consequently **[chlorite](../../../../../chlorite.md) above $y=\max(\ell_G,\ell_K)$; [gibbsite](../../../../../gibbsite.md) below $\ell_G$ for $x<-4.245$; and [kaolinite](../../../../../kaolinite.md) below $\ell_K$ for $x>-4.245$**. The stable [chlorite](../../../../../chlorite.md)–[gibbsite](../../../../../gibbsite.md) boundary is the left-hand part $x\leq-4.245$, and the stable [chlorite](../../../../../chlorite.md)–[kaolinite](../../../../../kaolinite.md) boundary is the right-hand part $x\geq-4.245$. Their opposite extensions are [metastable](../../../../../metastability.md) because the third [mineral](../../../../../mineral.md) is more stable. The vertical [gibbsite](../../../../../gibbsite.md)–[kaolinite](../../../../../kaolinite.md) boundary is stable below $y=13.077$ and [metastable](../../../../../metastability.md) above it.

The figure displays the stable upper envelope. The sign is physically testable: increasing magnesium or decreasing proton [thermodynamic activity](../../../../../thermodynamic-activity.md) raises $y$, inhibits [chlorite](../../../../../chlorite.md) dissolution, and expands the [chlorite](../../../../../chlorite.md) field. This direct [Gibbs energy](../../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md) comparison avoids inferring stability merely from where three algebraic lines meet.
