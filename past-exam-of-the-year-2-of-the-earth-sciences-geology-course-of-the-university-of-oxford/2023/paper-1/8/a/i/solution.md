<h1 id="8/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use decimal [logarithms](../../../../../../logarithm.md) and write $x=\log_{10}a_{\mathrm{H_4SiO_4}}$ and $y=\log_{10}(a_{\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}}/a_{\mathrm H^+}^2)$. The question's concentration notation is interpreted as dimensionless [thermodynamic activities](../../../../../../thermodynamic-activity.md), or concentrations divided by a fixed [standard concentration](../../../../../../standard-concentration.md) with [activity coefficients](../../../../../../activity-coefficient.md) set to one.

For the [chlorite](../../../../../../chlorite.md)–[gibbsite](../../../../../../gibbsite.md) reaction, unit solid activities give

$$
K=\frac{a_{\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}}^5a_{\mathrm{H_4SiO_4}}^3}{a_{\mathrm H^+}^{10}},\qquad \log_{10}K=5y+3x=52.65.
$$

Hence **$y=10.53-0.60x$**. It is a descending straight [mineral activity diagram](../../../../../../mineral-activity-diagram.md) boundary, passing through $(0,10.53)$ and $(-5,13.53)$. Its continuation alone does not establish which portions are stable against [kaolinite](../../../../../../kaolinite.md).
