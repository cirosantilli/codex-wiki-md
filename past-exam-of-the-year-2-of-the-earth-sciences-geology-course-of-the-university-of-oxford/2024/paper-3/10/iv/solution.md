<h1 id="10/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

A [stability boundary in a mineral activity diagram](../../../../../stability-boundary-in-a-mineral-activity-diagram.md) must also be stable against the third mineral. Use the [common-potential test for a three-mineral activity diagram](../../../../../common-potential-test-for-a-three-mineral-activity-diagram.md). For the two corundum-producing directions,

$$
\frac{\Delta_rG_a}{2RT\ln10}=y-(3.44-x),\qquad
\frac{\Delta_rG_b}{2RT\ln10}=y-(-7.59-3x).
$$

Negative values favour [corundum](../../../../../corundum.md). For the albite-to-nepheline conversion, $\Delta_rG/(RT\ln10)=2x+11.03$, so low silica activity favours [nepheline](../../../../../nepheline.md) and high silica activity favours [albite](../../../../../albite.md). These comparisons give:

- **Nepheline–corundum: stable for $x<-5.515$**, metastable on the opposite extension.
- **Albite–corundum: stable for $x>-5.515$**, metastable on the opposite extension.
- **Nepheline–albite: stable for $y>8.955$** along $x=-5.515$, metastable below the intersection.

All three coexist at the intersection. Pairwise mass-action equations alone do not distinguish the stable segments from their [metastable](../../../../../metastability.md) extensions.
