<h1 id="9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Adopt ideal independent [mixing on crystallographic sites](../../../../../mixing-on-crystallographic-sites.md), with Mg and Fe sharing three positions per formula unit and the other endmember sites fixed. This gives the [phlogopite](../../../../../phlogopite.md) and [pyrope](../../../../../pyrope.md) activities

$$
a_{\rm phlogopite}=0.80^3,\qquad a_{\rm pyrope}=0.20^3.
$$

The [reaction quotient](../../../../../reaction-quotient.md) contains their ratio, since [pyrope](../../../../../pyrope.md) is a product and [phlogopite](../../../../../phlogopite.md) a reactant. Consequently

$$
\boxed{T=\frac{130862.28}
{170.7-8.3145\ln[4500(0.20/0.80)^3]}
=966.923\,\mathrm K\simeq693.773\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.}
$$

The site-mixing assumption is part of this numerical answer: compositions alone do not determine nonideal [thermodynamic activities](../../../../../thermodynamic-activity.md). More detailed [activity coefficients](../../../../../activity-coefficient.md) or unequal site occupancies would need an additional model. The cube counts the three magnesium-bearing positions rather than three molecules of each mineral.
