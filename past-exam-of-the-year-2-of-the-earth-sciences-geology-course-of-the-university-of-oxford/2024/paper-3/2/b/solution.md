<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a wide horizontal [Bouguer slab](../../../../../bouguer-slab.md) replacing surrounding basement, use the **density contrast** $\Delta\rho=\rho_{\mathrm{sed}}-\rho_{\mathrm{base}}$, not the absolute [sediment](../../../../../sediment.md) [density](../../../../../density.md):

$$
\Delta g=2\pi G\Delta\rho\,h.
$$

Take downward attraction as positive. The negative anomaly therefore requires $\Delta\rho<0$. With $\Delta g=-40\ \mathrm{mGal}=-4.0\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, $h=3000\ \mathrm m$, and $G=6.67430\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$,

$$
\Delta\rho=\frac{-4.0\times10^{-4}}{2\pi(6.67430\times10^{-11})(3000)}=-317.95\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}},
$$

so

$$
\boxed{\rho_{\mathrm{base}}=2300-(-317.95)\simeq2618\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\simeq2.62\ \mathrm{Mg\,m^{-3}}.}
$$

This is the requested [Bouguer slab](../../../../../bouguer-slab.md) approximation: a flat observation surface, laterally infinite constant-thickness material, uniform average [geophysical density contrast](../../../../../geophysical-density-contrast.md), and a regional anomaly already removed. The actual basin in Figure 1 narrows downwards and is finite. For a fixed maximum thickness, finite width reduces the central anomaly magnitude, so this calculation can underestimate the true magnitude of the average [geophysical density contrast](../../../../../geophysical-density-contrast.md). It does not establish a unique basement [density](../../../../../density.md) without the geometrical assumption.
