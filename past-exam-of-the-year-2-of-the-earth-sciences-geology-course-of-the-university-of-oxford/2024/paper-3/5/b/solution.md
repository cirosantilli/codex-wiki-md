<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a nonzero, time-independent [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $\hat{\mathbf u}$, substitute $\mathbf u(t)=\hat{\mathbf u}e^{i\omega t}$. Taking two time [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) gives

$$
\ddot{\mathbf u}=(i\omega)^2\hat{\mathbf u}e^{i\omega t}=-\omega^2\hat{\mathbf u}e^{i\omega t}.
$$

Cancel the nonzero exponential to obtain the [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) problem

$$
\boxed{A\hat{\mathbf u}=\lambda\hat{\mathbf u},\qquad \lambda=-\omega^2,\qquad \hat{\mathbf u}\ne0.}
$$

Thus an [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) specifies a [normal mode](../../../../../normal-mode.md) shape and its negative [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) specifies the square of its [angular frequency](../../../../../angular-frequency.md), $\omega=\sqrt{-\lambda}$. Real [displacements](../../../../../displacement.md) are obtained by taking the [real part](../../../../../real-part.md), or by combining the two temporal solutions as $\hat{\mathbf u}(a\cos\omega t+b\sin\omega t)$.
