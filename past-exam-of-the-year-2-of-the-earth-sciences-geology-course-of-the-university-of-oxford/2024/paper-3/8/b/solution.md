<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $\delta_j=\sqrt{2K/\omega_j}$ for each component's [thermal penetration depth](../../../../../thermal-penetration-depth.md). Its contribution at depth is

$$
C_j e^{-x/\delta_j}\cos(\omega_jt-x/\delta_j).
$$

Thus its [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) is multiplied by $e^{-x/\delta_j}$ and its [wave phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) lags the surface by $x/\delta_j$ radians. The corresponding unwrapped time lag is $x/(\delta_j\omega_j)$; a phase measured from a single periodic signal is observable only modulo $2\pi$.

Higher-frequency variations have smaller penetration depths. In particular,

$$
\boxed{\frac{\delta_Y}{\delta_D}=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_D}{\omega_Y}}\simeq\sqrt{365}\simeq19.1.}
$$

The daily oscillation is therefore strongly attenuated at depths where a substantial annual oscillation survives. This is a [thermal diffusion wave](../../../../../thermal-diffusion-wave.md), whose phase lag is not the arrival time of a sharp thermal front. The displayed solution is the established periodic response; an additional transient would depend on initial subsurface data not needed for this interpretation.
