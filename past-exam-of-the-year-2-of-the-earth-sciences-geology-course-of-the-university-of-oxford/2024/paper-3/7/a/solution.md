<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Set $\omega=2\pi/T$ and $q=m+n$. If $q=0$, the integrand is one and its [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) is $T$. If $q$ is a nonzero [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md),

$$
\int_0^T e^{i\omega qt}\,dt
=\frac{e^{i2\pi q}-1}{i\omega q}=0.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\int_0^T e^{i\omega mt}e^{i\omega nt}\,dt=T\,\mathbf1_{m=-n}.}
$$

This is [Fourier orthogonality](../../../../../fourier-orthogonality.md) in its unconjugated form. With a [complex conjugate](../../../../../complex-conjugate.md) on the second exponential, the nonzero case would instead be $m=n$.
