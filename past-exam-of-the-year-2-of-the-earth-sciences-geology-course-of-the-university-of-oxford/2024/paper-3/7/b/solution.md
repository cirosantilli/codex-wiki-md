<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Multiply the [complex Fourier series](../../../../../complex-fourier-series.md) by $e^{-i\omega rt}$ and integrate over one [period](../../../../../period-of-a-function.md). [Fourier orthogonality](../../../../../fourier-orthogonality.md) removes every term except the one with index $r$, giving

$$
\boxed{u_r=\frac1T\int_0^Tf(t)e^{-i\omega rt}\,dt.}
$$

For general [square-integrable functions](../../../../../square-integrable-function.md), this is an [orthogonal projection](../../../../../orthogonal-projection.md) onto the corresponding [Fourier mode](../../../../../fourier-mode.md). It remains valid when the expansion converges only in [L2 space](../../../../../l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space.md): integrate finite [Fourier partial sums](../../../../../fourier-partial-sum.md) first, then pass to the limit using the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md). No unjustified pointwise exchange of an infinite series and an integral is needed.
