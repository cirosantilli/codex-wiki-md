<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The $31.7^\circ$ result predicts **initial failure in a uniform material under the assumed principal-stress orientations**. The actual Figure 3 is a present-day NW–SE cross section through a thick, heterogeneous Alpine stack, extending to about 50 km depth. It contains near-horizontal basal stretches, curved ramps, tightly bent contacts, and steep structures in the internal zones around Viso and the Dora Maira complex. It does not show a single undeformed conjugate pair newly formed in horizontal uniform [rock](../../../../../rock-geology.md).

Several processes explain the departures:

- **Mechanical layering and inherited weaknesses.** [Faults](../../../../../geological-fault.md) can follow weak bedding, old [fault planes](../../../../../fault-plane.md), or contacts with lower [friction](../../../../../friction.md) or [cohesion](../../../../../cohesion.md) than the surrounding [rock](../../../../../rock-geology.md). These can form shallow [décollements](../../../../../decollement.md) linked by steeper ramps. Figure 2 explicitly distinguishes the near-surface red “geophysical bedrock” boundary at a few hundred metres from the much deeper crystalline basement beneath the [Jurassic](../../../../../jurassic.md), [Triassic](../../../../../triassic.md) and [Permian](../../../../../permian.md) cover. An age label or a seismic contrast alone does not establish a friction coefficient; clay-rich or [evaporite](../../../../../evaporite.md) detachments are possibilities to test, not lithologies proved by that drawing.
- **Rotation after formation.** Continuing [tectonic shortening](../../../../../tectonic-shortening.md), [folding](../../../../../folding.md), stacking and [fault slip](../../../../../fault-slip.md) can rotate an initially optimal [fault plane](../../../../../fault-plane.md) to steeper or shallower present-day [fault dips](../../../../../fault-dip.md). A steep older structure may be inherited from an earlier deformation episode rather than created at its present orientation.
- **Curved faults and local stresses.** [Faults](../../../../../geological-fault.md) bend through a layered [crust](../../../../../planetary-crust.md); local [principal stress](../../../../../principal-stress.md) axes rotate near topography, ramps and interacting faults. The optimum angle is measured relative to the local $\sigma_1$, not necessarily to the regional horizontal direction everywhere.
- **Changing strength and deformation mechanism.** Spatial changes in [pore fluid pressure](../../../../../pore-fluid-pressure.md), [coefficient of friction](../../../../../coefficient-of-friction.md), [cohesion](../../../../../cohesion.md) and [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) alter which surfaces reactivate. At depth, [ductile deformation](../../../../../ductile-deformation.md) can replace frictional brittle failure, so the same new-fracture construction need not apply throughout the section.

Finally, a section shows **apparent dip** unless it is perpendicular to [fault strike](../../../../../fault-strike.md). For an ideal plane, $\tan\delta_{\mathrm{app}}=\tan\delta_{\mathrm{true}}\sin\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angle between the section direction and [fault strike](../../../../../fault-strike.md). Obliquity can make a plane look shallower, but cannot on its own explain an apparent dip steeper than its true dip. Do not infer that each black curve is a currently active brittle [earthquake](../../../../../earthquake.md) fault.
