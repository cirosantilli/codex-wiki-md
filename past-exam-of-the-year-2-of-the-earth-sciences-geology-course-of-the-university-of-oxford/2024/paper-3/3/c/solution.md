<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Assume an initially unfractured homogeneous [isotropic](../../../../../isotropy.md) material described by a linear [Mohr-Coulomb failure criterion](../../../../../mohr-coulomb-failure-criterion.md), with the given [coefficient of friction](../../../../../coefficient-of-friction.md) $\mu_f=0.5$ representing the relevant [internal friction angle](../../../../../internal-friction-angle.md). Use spatially uniform [pore fluid pressure](../../../../../pore-fluid-pressure.md), [effective stresses](../../../../../effective-stress.md) $\sigma_i'=\sigma_i-P_f$, and compression-positive signs. Then

$$
|\tau|=c+\mu_f\sigma_n',\qquad\varphi=\arctan\mu_f=26.565^\circ.
$$

The [Mohr circle](../../../../../mohr-s-circle.md) in the $\sigma_1',\sigma_3'$ plane has centre $C=(\sigma_1'+\sigma_3')/2$ and radius $R=(\sigma_1'-\sigma_3')/2$. At first failure it is tangent to the [failure envelope](../../../../../failure-envelope.md). The radius to the positive-shear tangent point is perpendicular to the line of slope $\tan\varphi$, hence makes angle $90^\circ+\varphi$ with the positive normal-stress axis.

If $\theta$ is the angle between the **fault normal** and $\sigma_1$, the [stress transformation](../../../../../stress-transformation.md) is

$$
\sigma_n'=C+R\cos2\theta,\qquad\tau=R\sin2\theta.
$$

Thus $2\theta=90^\circ+\varphi$, $\theta=58.2825^\circ$, and the angle of the fault itself to horizontal $\sigma_1$ is

$$
\boxed{\delta=90^\circ-\theta=45^\circ-\frac\varphi2=31.7175^\circ.}
$$

The two conjugate [thrust faults](../../../../../thrust-fault.md) therefore strike approximately east–west and dip about **$32^\circ$ north and $32^\circ$ south**. This is the [fault dip](../../../../../fault-dip.md), not the $58.3^\circ$ fault-normal angle. Both [sediments](../../../../../sediment.md) and basement have the same optimum orientation under these assumptions because their stipulated friction slopes are equal; their [cohesion](../../../../../cohesion.md) and absolute failure [stresses](../../../../../stress.md) could still differ.

The first two panels in [solution](../b/solution.md) give the labeled construction. Constant [cohesion](../../../../../cohesion.md) shifts the straight [failure envelope](../../../../../failure-envelope.md) without changing its slope, and uniform [pore fluid pressure](../../../../../pore-fluid-pressure.md) translates the [Mohr circle](../../../../../mohr-s-circle.md) in normal-stress coordinates; neither changes this tangency angle. In the illustrative cohesionless case, $R/C=\sin\varphi$, giving $\sigma_1'/\sigma_3'=(1+\sin\varphi)/(1-\sin\varphi)\simeq2.618$. Pre-existing weaknesses, a nonuniform [stress](../../../../../stress.md) field, anisotropic strength, or a nonlinear [failure envelope](../../../../../failure-envelope.md) invalidate the simple optimum-orientation prediction.[Lecture 10 develops the traction, pore-pressure and Mohr-circle framework; the signs and angle definitions above specify the convention used here.](../../../../../stress-and-strength-lecture-notes-by-mit-opencourseware.md)
