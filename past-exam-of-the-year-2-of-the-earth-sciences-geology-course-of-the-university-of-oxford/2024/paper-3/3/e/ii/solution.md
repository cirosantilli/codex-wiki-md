<h1 id="3/e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the northern [S–P time](../../../../../../s-p-time.md), approximately $\Delta t_N=6\ \mathrm s$, rather than multiplying an absolute-clock arrival by a [wave speed](../../../../../../wave-speed.md). For common approximately straight paths,

$$
\boxed{D_N=\frac{\Delta t_N}{1/V_s-1/V_p}=\frac{V_pV_s}{V_p-V_s}\,\Delta t_N.}
$$

**No numerical crustal velocities are specified in Q3.** If the usual illustrative crustal values $V_p=6.0\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ and $V_s=3.5\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ are adopted, the distance is

$$
\boxed{D_N\simeq8.4\times6\simeq50\ \mathrm{km}.}
$$

These assumed crustal speeds are unrelated to Q1's offshore layer values. They are representative rather than measurements of the Swiss column; the [British Geological Survey](../../../../../../british-geological-survey.md) gives broad typical crustal ranges of 5–7 km/s for [P waves](../../../../../../p-wave.md) and 3–4 km/s for [S waves](../../../../../../s-wave.md).[The seismic-wave table provides typical crustal speed ranges, not a velocity model for this particular earthquake.](../../../../../../how-are-earthquakes-detected-by-british-geological-survey.md)

Using the corresponding northern central pulse times gives an effective source time near $13.3-50.4/6\simeq4.9\ \mathrm s$ after midnight, and the S estimate is the same to graph accuracy. The southern central landmarks similarly give a distance near 70 km and an effective source time near 5 s. That consistency supports the example, but pulse landmarks need not mark the rupture's true onset. $D_N$ is a source-to-station path or [hypocentral distance](../../../../../../hypocentral-distance.md) in the uniform-speed approximation. An [epicentral distance](../../../../../../epicentral-distance.md) additionally needs the [hypocenter](../../../../../../hypocenter.md) depth; it cannot simply be equated to 50 km for an unknown depth.
