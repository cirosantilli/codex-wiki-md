<h1 id="3/e/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

The shorter, stronger northern pulses and broader southern pulses suggest propagation **from south to north**, towards the northern station. The useful evidence is the difference in **pulse widths**, not just the earlier northern arrivals: the latter chiefly reflect station distance.

As rupture reaches successively more northerly patches, each patch radiates a new [P wave](../../../../../../p-wave.md) and [S wave](../../../../../../s-wave.md) pulse. Towards the north, the later emission time is partly cancelled by a shorter propagation path; those contributions arrive closer together. Towards the south, later emission and a longer path add, stretching the signal. This is [rupture directivity](../../../../../../rupture-directivity.md). It refers to the advance of the slipping region along the [fault](../../../../../../geological-fault.md), not necessarily the direction of the local [fault slip](../../../../../../fault-slip.md) vector. A thrust can slip up-dip while its rupture front advances in a direction with a northward component.

More generally, emission from a patch at distance $s$ along the rupture arrives at

$$
t_{\mathrm{arr}}(s)=t_{\mathrm{start}}+\frac{s}{V_r}+\frac{R(s)}c,\qquad \frac{dt_{\mathrm{arr}}}{ds}\simeq\frac1{V_r}-\frac{\cos\psi}{c}.
$$

The forward direction has a smaller arrival-time spread. This is the same travel-time compression captured by isochrone-based [rupture directivity](../../../../../../rupture-directivity.md) calculations.[The primary report relates directivity to rupture geometry, seismic speed and the arrival-time distribution of radiation from successive fault patches.](../../../../../../a-formulation-of-directivity-for-earthquake-sources-using-isochrone-theory-by-paul-spudich-and-others.md) Larger northern amplitude supports the interpretation but is not decisive on its own because [geometrical spreading](../../../../../../geometrical-spreading.md), [seismic attenuation](../../../../../../seismic-attenuation.md), [seismic radiation patterns](../../../../../../seismic-radiation-pattern.md) and site response also affect amplitude. With only two traces, propagation, filtering and site effects remain alternatives to test; the idealized exercise is most naturally explained by northward directivity.
