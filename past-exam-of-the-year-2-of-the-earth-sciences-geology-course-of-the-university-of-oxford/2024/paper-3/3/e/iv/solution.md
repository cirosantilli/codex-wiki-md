<h1 id="3/e/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For a unilateral [finite-source rupture](../../../../../../finite-source-rupture.md) advancing at approximately constant [rupture velocity](../../../../../../rupture-velocity.md) $V_r$, the along-fault propagation length is

$$
\boxed{L=V_rT.}
$$

There is no supplied $V_r$ or calibrated [seismic source time function](../../../../../../seismic-source-time-function.md), so **a unique spatial extent cannot be read directly from Figure 4**. For illustration, $T\sim4\ \mathrm s$ and an assumed $V_r\sim2.5\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ would give $L\sim10\ \mathrm{km}$. Allowing $T\sim4$–$5\ \mathrm s$ and assumed $V_r\sim2$–$3\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ gives lengths of order **8–15 km**. These are explicitly conditional scale estimates, not measured fault dimensions.

If equal-and-opposite ray geometry and phase pulse durations were known, the directivity model in [solution](../iii/solution.md) would instead constrain a projected length,

$$
L_\parallel=\frac c2\left(D_S^{(\mathrm{phase})}-D_N^{(\mathrm{phase})}\right),\qquad L_\parallel=L\cos\psi,
$$

where $\psi$ is the angle between the rupture direction and the northern ray. Distinct P and S [wave speeds](../../../../../../wave-speed.md), unknown takeoff angles, smooth pulse boundaries and the clipped southern S tail prevent a reliable independent numerical inversion here. This observation is consistent with a few-kilometre projected extent while the actual along-fault length can be larger. A rupture length is not the accumulated [fault slip](../../../../../../fault-slip.md), the width of the whole [fault plane](../../../../../../fault-plane.md), or the approximately 50 km station distance.
