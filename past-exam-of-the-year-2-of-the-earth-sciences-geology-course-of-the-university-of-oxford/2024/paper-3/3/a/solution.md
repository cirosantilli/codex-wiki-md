<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use compression-positive [principal stresses](../../../../../principal-stress.md), $\sigma_1\geq\sigma_2\geq\sigma_3$. For the intended [thrust fault](../../../../../thrust-fault.md) regime during approximately north–south [tectonic shortening](../../../../../tectonic-shortening.md), the likely orientations are

$$
\boxed{\sigma_1:\ \text{horizontal N--S};\quad\sigma_2:\ \text{horizontal E--W};\quad\sigma_3:\ \text{vertical}.}
$$

A locally horizontal traction-free surface motivates taking one principal direction as vertical, while the strong north–south compression selects the maximum horizontal [principal stress](../../../../../principal-stress.md). The expected [fault strike](../../../../../fault-strike.md) is then approximately east–west.

This ordering assumes the two horizontal compressions exceed the vertical [principal stress](../../../../../principal-stress.md). North–south shortening by itself does not establish the magnitudes of all three: if the vertical [principal stress](../../../../../principal-stress.md) were intermediate, [strike-slip faulting](../../../../../strike-slip-faulting.md) would instead be possible. The answer above is the simple compressional end member used in the following parts.
