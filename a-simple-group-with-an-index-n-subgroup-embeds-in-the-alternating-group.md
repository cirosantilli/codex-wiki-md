# A simple group with an index-n subgroup embeds in the alternating group

↑ **Parent:** [Simple group](simple-group.md)

For a finite [simple group](simple-group.md) with a [subgroup](subgroup.md) of index $n\geq3$, the [coset action](coset-action.md) is nontrivial and its [kernel](kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) is a [normal subgroup](normal-subgroup.md), hence trivial. Its [sign homomorphism](sign-homomorphism.md) must also be trivial: otherwise simplicity would give an isomorphism to $C_2$, which cannot act transitively on at least three points. Thus the [image](image-of-a-function.md) lies in the [alternating group](alternating-group.md). In particular [Lagrange's theorem](lagrange-s-theorem.md) gives $|G|\mid n!/2$. This can exclude simplicity with very little counting: a group of order $112$ has a [subgroup](subgroup.md) of index seven by [Sylow theorems](sylow-theorems.md), but $112$ does not divide $7!/2$.
