# Numerical analysis

↑ **Parent:** [Analysis](analysis-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_analysis)

Numerical analysis designs [algorithms](algorithm-split.md) that approximate mathematical answers while controlling cost and error. Its central question is how much of a computed answer can be trusted. Finding a [zero of a function](zero-of-a-function.md), evaluating an [integral](integral-split.md), solving a [linear system](system-of-linear-equations.md), and evolving an [ordinary differential equation](ordinary-differential-equation.md) all demand choices about accuracy, work, and the information available. MIT's numerical-methods course explicitly treats performance and accuracy as joint concerns.[Steven G. Johnson, MIT 18.335J, Week 1, 2019](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-335j-introduction-to-numerical-methods-spring-2019/pages/week-1/)

Three questions organize error analysis. [Truncation error](truncation-error.md) measures the approximation introduced by an [algorithm](algorithm-split.md) even in exact arithmetic; [roundoff error](round-off-error.md) comes from finite precision; the [condition number](condition-number.md) measures sensitivity inherent in the problem. [Numerical stability](stability-of-a-numerical-method.md) asks how the chosen [algorithm](algorithm-split.md) propagates perturbations. These distinctions suggest different remedies: refine an approximation, change precision or arithmetic, or improve the accuracy of the input.

For example, the [square root](square-root.md) $s(a)=\sqrt a$ has relative [condition number](condition-number.md) $|as'(a)/s(a)|=1/2$ for $a>0$: a small relative change in $a$ produces about half that relative change in $s(a)$. In contrast, $g(a)=1/(1-a)$ has relative [condition number](condition-number.md) $|a/(1-a)|$. Changing $a=0.99$ to $0.9901$ changes $g(a)=100$ to about $101.010101$: an input change of about $0.0101\%$ produces an output change of about $1.0101\%$. This sensitivity exists even with exact arithmetic. MIT's conditioning lecture separates the [condition number](condition-number.md) from [roundoff error](round-off-error.md) in precisely this way.[Steven G. Johnson, MIT 18.335J, Lecture 5: Condition Numbers, 2019](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-335j-introduction-to-numerical-methods-spring-2019/pages/week-2/)
