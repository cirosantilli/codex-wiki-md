# Torque

↑ **Parent:** [Angular momentum](angular-momentum.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torque)

[Torque](torque.md) measures a [force](force.md)'s turning effect about a specified origin. The geometry in the [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) diagram applies with [force](force.md) replacing [momentum](momentum.md): transverse [forces](force.md) give positive or negative [torque](torque.md), while a radial [force](force.md) gives zero. A longer perpendicular [lever arm](lever-arm.md) makes the same [force](force.md) more effective, explaining why pushing near the end of a handle is useful.

For a [force](force.md) $\mathbf F$ applied at [position](position.md) $\mathbf r$ from a fixed origin $O$ in an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md), its [torque](torque.md), or [moment of force](torque.md), is

$$
\boldsymbol\tau_O=\mathbf r\times\mathbf F,\qquad
|\boldsymbol\tau_O|=rF\sin\phi=F\ell.
$$

Here $F=|\mathbf F|$, $\phi$ is the [angle](angle.md) between [position](position.md) and [force](force.md), and $\ell=r\sin\phi$ is the perpendicular [lever arm](lever-arm.md). Direction follows the [right-hand rule](right-hand-rule.md); in the $xy$ plane $\tau_z=xF_y-yF_x$, positive counterclockwise as viewed from $+z$. The [International System of Units](international-system-of-units.md) unit is $\mathrm{N\,m}=\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s^2}$. Although [torque](torque.md) and [energy](energy.md) have the same [physical dimensions](physical-dimension.md), [torque](torque.md) is an [axial vector](pseudovector.md) and [energy](energy.md) is a [scalar](scalar.md); their roles differ.

For $\mathbf r=(2,0,0)\,\mathrm m$ and $\mathbf F=(0,3,0)\,\mathrm N$, $\tau_z=+6\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. Applying the same [force](force.md) at $(1,0,0)\,\mathrm m$ gives $+3\,\mathrm{N\,m}$; reversing it gives a negative [torque](torque.md); directing it along $\mathbf r$ gives zero. More generally, sum $\mathbf r_i\times\mathbf F_i$ to find the total [torque](torque.md) about one common origin.

For a constant-[mass](mass.md) [point mass](point-mass.md), the [product rule](product-rule.md) and [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) show why net [torque](torque.md) changes [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md):

$$
\frac{d\mathbf L_O}{dt}
=\mathbf v\times(m\mathbf v)+\mathbf r\times\frac{d\mathbf p}{dt}
=\mathbf r\times\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}
=\boldsymbol\tau_O^{\mathrm{net}}.
$$

The first [cross product](cross-product.md) vanishes because its factors are parallel. For a collection with canceling internal [torques](torque.md), the remaining sum is external [torque](torque.md), giving [conservation of angular momentum](conservation-of-angular-momentum.md) when it vanishes.[section 11.2: torque and the angular momentum balance](university-physics-volume-1-by-william-moebs-and-others/11-2-angular-momentum.md)

A nonzero [torque](torque.md) can turn the direction of [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) as well as change its magnitude. Indeed, the [dot product](dot-product.md) gives $d|\mathbf L_O|^2/dt=2\mathbf L_O\cdot\boldsymbol\tau_O$: a perpendicular [torque](torque.md) changes direction without instantaneously changing the magnitude. This is the geometry behind [gyroscope](gyroscope.md) [precession](precession.md). For a rapidly spinning [symmetric top](symmetric-top.md) with a fixed tip, [centre of mass](center-of-mass.md) distance $d$ from the tip, [mass](mass.md) $M$, and spin-dominated [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) magnitude $L\simeq I\omega$, the slow steady [precession](precession.md) approximation gives $\Omega\simeq Mgd/L$, where $g$ is [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md). [OpenStax University Physics Volume 1, section 11.4: Precession of a Gyroscope](university-physics-volume-1-by-william-moebs-and-others/11-4-precession-of-a-gyroscope.md) This is an approximation with $\Omega\ll\omega$; the [uniform precession of a heavy symmetric top](uniform-precession-of-a-heavy-symmetric-top.md) retains contributions omitted by the spin-dominated estimate.
