<h1 id="analysis/4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}>0$. For $u=x/(1+r)$, $v=y/(1+r)$,

$$
u_x=\frac1{1+r}-\frac{x^2}{r(1+r)^2},\qquad v_y=\frac1{1+r}-\frac{y^2}{r(1+r)^2},\qquad u_y=v_x=-\frac{xy}{r(1+r)^2}.
$$

The two [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) would require $x^2=y^2$ and $xy=0$ simultaneously, impossible when $r>0$. Thus $f$ is not [complex differentiable](../../../../../complex-differentiability.md) at any nonzero point.

At zero, however,

$$
\frac{f(h)-f(0)}h=\frac1{1+|h|}\longrightarrow1.
$$

So **$f$ is [complex differentiable](../../../../../complex-differentiability.md) at zero, with [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) one, but is not [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on $\mathbb C$ or on any nonempty open subset**. [Complex differentiability](../../../../../complex-differentiability.md) at a single point is weaker than [holomorphy](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) in a neighborhood.
