<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

The [identity theorem for holomorphic functions](../../../identity-theorem.md) says that a [holomorphic function](../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on a connected [open set](../../../open-set.md) which has distinct zeros accumulating at a point of the set is identically zero. Equivalently, two [holomorphic](../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) functions agreeing on such a set of points agree everywhere in that connected [open set](../../../open-set.md).

To prove it, expand at an accumulation point $a$. If the first nonzero [Taylor coefficient](../../../taylor-coefficient.md) had index $m$, the function would factor as $(z-a)^m h(z)$ with $h(a)\ne0$. [Continuity](../../../continuous-function.md) would make $h$ nonzero near $a$, so no other zeros could approach $a$, a contradiction. Thus all [coefficients](../../../coefficient.md) vanish and the function vanishes on a neighborhood of $a$. Let $E$ be the set of points having such a zero neighborhood. It is nonempty and open. If a point in the domain is a limit point of $E$, it is either already in $E$ or is an accumulation point of distinct zeros, and the same Taylor argument puts it in $E$. Thus $E$ is relatively closed as well. [Connectedness](../../../connected-space.md) forces $E$ to be the whole domain. Apply this result to the difference of two functions for the equivalent formulation.
