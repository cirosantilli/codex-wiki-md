<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For [equal-length crystallographic root configurations in a plane](../../../../equal-length-crystallographic-root-configurations-in-a-plane.md), taking $u=v$ in the closure condition gives $S_v(v)=-v$, so every vector occurs with its negative. For fixed $v\in\Phi$, a vector $u\in\Phi$ has $\langle u,v\rangle\in\{-1,-1/2,0,1/2,1\}$, because both vectors are [unit vectors](../../../../unit-vector.md). Thus its angle relative to $v$ belongs to the finite list $0,\pm\pi/3,\pm\pi/2,\pm2\pi/3,\pi$.

If all vectors are collinear, the set is exactly $\{v,-v\}$, giving two opposite points of the [unit circle](../../../../complex-unit-circle.md).

If an [orthogonal](../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) vector $w$ occurs, closure supplies $\pm v,\pm w$. A [unit vector](../../../../unit-vector.md) making an angle of $\pi/3$ or $2\pi/3$ with $v$ would have [inner product](../../../../inner-product-split.md) $\pm\sqrt3/2$ with $w$, contradicting integrality of twice that [inner product](../../../../inner-product-split.md). Thus these four points are the whole set: the vertices of a [square](../../../../square.md).

In the remaining case a noncollinear pair makes an angle of $\pi/3$ or $2\pi/3$. Changing a sign, take their angles as $0,\pi/3$. Reflecting the second in the line perpendicular to the first supplies the angle $2\pi/3$, and negation supplies the other three hexagon vertices. No perpendicular vector can occur, by the preceding inner-product obstruction. The initial finite list now leaves only these six points. Conversely the opposite pair, [square](../../../../square.md) and [regular hexagon](../../../../regular-hexagon.md) each satisfy both [reflection](../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) closure and the integrality condition. Hence

$$
\boxed{|\Phi|\in\{2,4,6\}.}
$$
