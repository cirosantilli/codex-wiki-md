<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For $T_1$, choose the third [standard basis](../../../../standard-basis.md) [vector](../../../../vector.md) $v=e_3$. Its first three orbit [vectors](../../../../vector.md) are

$$
v=e_3,\qquad T_1v=e_2+e_3,\qquad T_1^2v=e_1+2e_2+e_3.
$$

They are [linearly independent](../../../../linear-independence.md): their [matrix](../../../../matrix.md) of columns has [determinant](../../../../determinant.md) $-1$. They therefore span $\mathbb R^3$, proving **$T_1$ has a [cyclic vector](../../../../cyclic-vector.md), namely $e_3$**.

For $T_2$, write $T_2=I+N$ where $N^2=0$. The [binomial theorem](../../../../binomial-theorem.md) gives $T_2^jv=v+jNv$ for every integer $j\geq0$. The entire orbit of any $v$ lies in $\operatorname{span}\{v,Nv\}$, which has [dimension of a vector space](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) at most two. Therefore **$T_2$ has no [cyclic vector](../../../../cyclic-vector.md)** in the three-dimensional space. Equivalently, its [minimal polynomial](../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) is $(t-1)^2$, while its [characteristic polynomial](../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md) is $(t-1)^3$.
