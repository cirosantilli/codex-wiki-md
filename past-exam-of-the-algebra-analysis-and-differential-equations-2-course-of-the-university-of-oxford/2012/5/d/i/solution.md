<h1 id="5/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take the [Laurent series](../../../../../laurent-series.md) of $f$ on $0<|z|<r$. For every integer $k\geq1$, its [coefficient](../../../../../coefficient.md) of $z^{-k}$ satisfies, on a circle of radius $0<\rho<r$,

$$
|c_{-k}|=\left|\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=\rho}f(\zeta)\zeta^{k-1}\,d\zeta\right|\leq m\rho^k.
$$

The [coefficient](../../../../../coefficient.md) is independent of $\rho$. Letting $\rho\downarrow0$ therefore gives $c_{-k}=0$ for every $k\geq1$. The [Laurent series](../../../../../laurent-series.md) is a [power series](../../../../../power-series.md) with no negative powers and supplies a [holomorphic extension](../../../../../holomorphic-extension.md) at zero. Thus **zero is a [removable singularity](../../../../../removable-singularity.md)**. This is the [Riemann removable singularity theorem](../../../../../riemann-removable-singularity-theorem.md), with boundedness used to eliminate the entire principal part.
