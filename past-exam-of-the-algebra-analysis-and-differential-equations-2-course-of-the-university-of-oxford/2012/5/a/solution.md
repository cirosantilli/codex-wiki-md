<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Laurent theorem](../../../../laurent-theorem.md) states that a [holomorphic function](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on the [annulus](../../../../annulus-mathematics.md) $R<|z-a|<S$ has a unique [Laurent series](../../../../laurent-series.md)

$$
\boxed{f(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}c_n(z-a)^n,\qquad c_n=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta-a|=\rho}\frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-a)^{n+1}}\,d\zeta,\quad R<\rho<S.}
$$

The integration circle is counterclockwise. The series converges absolutely at every point of the annulus and uniformly on every compact subannulus. The [coefficient](../../../../coefficient.md) [integrals](../../../../integral-split.md) are independent of the choice of $\rho$ by the [Cauchy integral theorem](../../../../cauchy-s-integral-theorem.md) applied between two such circles. These statements include $R=0$ or $S=\infty$ when the indicated annulus has those endpoints.
