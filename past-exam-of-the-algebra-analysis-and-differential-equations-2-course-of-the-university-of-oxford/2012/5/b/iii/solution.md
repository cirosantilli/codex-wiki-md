<h1 id="5/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A **[pole](../../../../../pole.md)** of order $n\geq1$ means

$$
\boxed{f(z)=(z-z_0)^{-n}h(z),\qquad h\text{ holomorphic near }z_0,\quad h(z_0)\ne0.}
$$

Equivalently, the [Laurent series](../../../../../laurent-series.md) has a finite nonzero [principal part](../../../../../principal-part-of-a-meromorphic-function.md), with lowest power exactly $(z-z_0)^{-n}$. Thus $(z-z_0)^nf(z)$ extends holomorphically with nonzero value at $z_0$; using a smaller positive power would not remove the singularity.
