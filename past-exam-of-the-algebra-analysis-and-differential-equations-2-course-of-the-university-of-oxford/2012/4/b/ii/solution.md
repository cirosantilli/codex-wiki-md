<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The closed [unit disk](../../../../../unit-disk.md) is a [compact set](../../../../../compact-space.md), and $|f|$ is [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) on it because $f$ is [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on an [open neighbourhood](../../../../../open-neighbourhood.md). The [extreme value theorem](../../../../../extreme-value-theorem.md) therefore supplies a point where its maximum is attained. If such a point is on the boundary, the conclusion follows. If it is in the interior, part (i) makes $f$ constant on the open disk; [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) then gives the same value on the boundary. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\max_{|z|\leq1}|f(z)|=\max_{|z|=1}|f(z)|.}
$$

The assumption that the closed disk lies in $U$ is what makes the boundary values and the compactness argument available.
