<h1 id="4/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Because $f(0)=0$, the [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) of $f$ at zero shows that

$$
h(z)=\begin{cases}f(z)/z,&z\ne0,\\f'(0),&z=0\end{cases}
$$

is [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) throughout the [unit disk](../../../../../unit-disk.md). Fix $0<r<1$. On $|z|=r$ we have $|h(z)|<1/r$, and the [maximum modulus principle](../../../../../maximum-modulus-principle.md) on the closed disk of radius $r$ gives $|h(z)|\leq1/r$ for $|z|\leq r$. For any fixed $z$ inside the [unit disk](../../../../../unit-disk.md) we may let $r$ increase to one, obtaining $|h(z)|\leq1$. Hence the [Schwarz lemma](../../../../../schwarz-lemma.md) inequalities are

$$
\boxed{|f(z)|\leq|z|,\qquad |f'(0)|\leq1.}
$$

The [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) bound is the same estimate at $z=0$, using the holomorphic extension of the quotient.
