<h1 id="7/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

A [centre equilibrium](../../../../../center-equilibrium.md) of the linearized real planar system has

$$
\boxed{\lambda_1=i\omega,\qquad\lambda_2=-i\omega,\qquad\omega\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}.}
$$

After a real invertible [linear change of coordinates](../../../../../linear-change-of-coordinates.md), the system is a constant angular rotation. Its nonzero orbits are ellipses of period $2\pi/|\omega|$, so it is stable but not asymptotically stable. This is a classification of the **[linearization](../../../../../linearization.md)**: [a centre in the linearization need not be a nonlinear centre](../../../../../a-centre-in-the-linearization-need-not-be-a-nonlinear-centre.md). In particular, imaginary [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) alone do not prove a nonlinear [centre equilibrium](../../../../../center-equilibrium.md), which requires a punctured neighbourhood filled with [periodic orbits](../../../../../periodic-orbit.md). Part (c) illustrates exactly this limitation.
