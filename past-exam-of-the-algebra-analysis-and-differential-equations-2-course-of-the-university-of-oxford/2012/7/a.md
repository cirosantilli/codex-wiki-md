<h1 id="7/a">a</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

A [critical point of a dynamical system](../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) or [equilibrium](../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) is a point $(a,b)$ with $f(a,b)=g(a,b)=0$, so the constant function $(x,y)=(a,b)$ is a solution. Put $u=x-a$, $v=y-b$. For a continuously differentiable [vector field](../../../vector-field.md), its [linearization](../../../linearization.md) is

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix}=M\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix},\qquad M=D(f,g)(a,b),
$$

with remainder $o(\sqrt{u^2+v^2})$ in the original nonlinear system. For distinct [eigenvalues](../../../eigenvalue.md), $M$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb C$, and the linear solutions are combinations of $e^{\lambda_jt}w_j$, where $w_j$ are corresponding [eigenvectors](../../../eigenvector.md). Real parts determine growth or decay; nonzero imaginary parts give rotation. For [hyperbolic equilibria](../../../hyperbolic-equilibrium-point.md), meaning no [eigenvalue](../../../eigenvalue.md) has zero real part, the [Hartman-Grobman theorem](../../../hartman-grobman-theorem.md) transfers the local stability and qualitative orbit pattern to the nonlinear system. A purely imaginary pair is not hyperbolic: it determines a linear centre, while higher-order terms decide whether the nonlinear system actually has a centre.
