<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [Euclidean domain](../../../../euclidean-domain.md) is an [integral domain](../../../../integral-domain.md) $R$ with a [function](../../../../function-split.md) $d:R\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb N_0$ such that, for $a\in R$ and nonzero $b\in R$, there exist $q,r\in R$ with $a=qb+r$ and either $r=0$ or $d(r)<d(b)$. This is the Euclidean-ring convention used here.

If an [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) $I$ is zero, it is generated by zero. Otherwise choose nonzero $a\in I$ with $d(a)$ minimal. For any $b\in I$, Euclidean division gives $b=qa+r$, with $r\in I$. A nonzero remainder would have $d(r)<d(a)$, contradicting the choice. Hence $r=0$ and $b\in aR$. The reverse inclusion follows from $a\in I$, proving

$$
\boxed{I=aR.}
$$

Now suppose $a$ is an [irreducible element](../../../../irreducible-element.md). Its [principal ideal](../../../../principal-ideal.md) is proper because $a$ is not a unit. If $aR\subseteq J\subseteq R$, write $J=bR$ by the result just proved. Then $a=bc$ for some $c\in R$. Irreducibility gives either $b$ a unit, so $J=R$, or $c$ a unit, so $bR=aR$. There is no intermediate proper [ideal](../../../../ideal.md), and therefore **$aR$ is a [maximal ideal](../../../../maximal-ideal.md)**.

For a [field](../../../../field.md) $F$, the [polynomial ring](../../../../polynomial-ring.md) $F[X]$ is an [integral domain](../../../../integral-domain.md) and the [polynomial division algorithm](../../../../polynomial-division.md) gives $A=QB+R$ with $R=0$ or $\deg R<\deg B$. Thus the [polynomial degree](../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) is a Euclidean function, proving **$F[X]$ is a [Euclidean domain](../../../../euclidean-domain.md)**.
