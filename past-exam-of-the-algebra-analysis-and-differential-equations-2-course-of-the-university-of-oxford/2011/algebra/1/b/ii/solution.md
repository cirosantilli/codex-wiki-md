<h1 id="algebra/1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $\chi_T(\lambda)=0$, then $\det(\lambda I-A)=0$, so $\lambda I-A$ is singular. By the [rank-nullity theorem](../../../../../../rank-nullity-theorem.md) it has a nonzero kernel vector $v$. Thus

$$
(\lambda I-A)v=0,\qquad\boxed{Tv=\lambda v\quad(v\neq0).}
$$

This is exactly the definition of an [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md). Here $\lambda$ lies in the scalar field: for the stated real [vector space](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md) it is real. A nonreal root instead gives an [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of the complexified operator, by the same argument over $\mathbb C$.
