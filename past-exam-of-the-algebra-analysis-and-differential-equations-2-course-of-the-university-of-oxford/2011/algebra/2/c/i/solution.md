<h1 id="algebra/2/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The finite-dimensional [spectral theorem](../../../../../../spectral-theorem.md) says that a [self-adjoint operator](../../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md) on a complex inner-product space has an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) and all its [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are real. Equivalently, its matrix is unitarily diagonalisable with real diagonal entries.

For $a\in\mathbb R$, the matrix is equal to its conjugate transpose:

$$
M_a^*=\begin{pmatrix}1&i&a\\-i&1&0\\a&0&1\end{pmatrix}=M_a.
$$

Thus it represents a self-adjoint operator for the standard [inner product](../../../../../../inner-product-split.md) on $\mathbb C^3$. The theorem applies directly, proving

$$
\boxed{M_a\text{ is diagonalisable for every real }a.}
$$
