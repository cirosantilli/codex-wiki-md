<h1 id="algebra/2/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the convention that the [inner product](../../../../../../inner-product-split.md) is linear in its first argument, as required by the displayed representation of a [linear functional](../../../../../../linear-functional.md). Choose an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) $e_1,\ldots,e_n$, for instance by [Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization](../../../../../../gram-schmidt-process.md). Define

$$
v=\sum_{j=1}^n\overline{f(e_j)}e_j.
$$

For $u=\sum_ju_je_j$, conjugate linearity in the second argument gives

$$
\langle u,v\rangle=\sum_ju_jf(e_j)=f(u).
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{f(u)=\langle u,v\rangle\quad\text{for every }u\in V.}
$$

The representation is unique as well: if two vectors represent the same functional, their difference is orthogonal to every vector and therefore to itself, so it is zero.
