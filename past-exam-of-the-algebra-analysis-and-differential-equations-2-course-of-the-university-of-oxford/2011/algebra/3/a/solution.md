<h1 id="algebra/3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) $I$ of a commutative ring is an additive subgroup such that $rx\in I$ for every $r\in R$ and $x\in I$. A [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) is a proper [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) $A\neq R$ such that $xy\in A$ implies $x\in A$ or $y\in A$.

If $(x+A)(y+A)=0+A$ in the [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md), then $xy\in A$. Primality implies $x+A=0+A$ or $y+A=0+A$. Therefore there are no two nonzero cosets whose product is zero:

$$
\boxed{R/A\text{ has no nonzero zero-divisors}.}
$$

Properness also ensures its identity coset is not zero, so this quotient is an [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md).
