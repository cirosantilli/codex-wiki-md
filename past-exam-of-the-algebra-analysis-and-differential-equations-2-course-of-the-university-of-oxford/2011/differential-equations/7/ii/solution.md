<h1 id="differential-equations/7/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

No rectangle of positive width around $(0,0)$ has a uniform Lipschitz constant in $y$. For any nonzero $x$ in such a rectangle,

$$
\frac{|f(x,y)-f(x,0)|}{|y|}=|x|\,|y|^{-1/3}\longrightarrow\infty\qquad(y\to0).
$$

The vanishing factor $x$ at the single initial point does not control neighbouring $x$-values. The failure of the hypothesis is accompanied by actual nonuniqueness: both

$$
\boxed{y_1(x)=0,\qquad y_2(x)=\frac{x^6}{216}}
$$

satisfy the equation and have value zero at $x=0$. Direct differentiation gives $y_2'=x^5/36=x(y_2)^{2/3}$, including the zero point by continuity.
