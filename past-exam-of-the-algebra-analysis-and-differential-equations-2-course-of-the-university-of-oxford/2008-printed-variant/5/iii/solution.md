<h1 id="5/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The denominator vanishes at $z_k=1/(2\pi i k)$ for every nonzero [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) $k$. These points approach zero, the denominator has a [simple zero](../../../../simple-zero.md) at each, and $\sin z_k\ne0$ because a nonzero purely imaginary number is not a zero of sine. Hence there are [poles](../../../../pole.md) arbitrarily close to zero. The origin is a **nonisolated singularity**, so it has no [residue](../../../../residue.md) defined by a [Laurent series](../../../../laurent-series.md) on a punctured disc. In particular it must not be classified as an isolated [essential singularity](../../../../essential-singularity.md) merely because $e^{1/z}$ has one.
