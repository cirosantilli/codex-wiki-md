<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If a [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) [bijection](../../../../bijection.md) existed, its inverse would be an entire function taking values in the [unit disc](../../../../unit-disc.md). [Liouville theorem](../../../../liouville-theorem.md) would make that inverse constant, contradicting bijectivity. Thus **no [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) [bijection](../../../../bijection.md) exists**.

A [continuous](../../../../continuous-function.md) [bijection](../../../../bijection.md) does exist, even a [homeomorphism](../../../../homeomorphism.md):

$$
\boxed{h(z)=\frac{z}{1-|z|},\qquad h^{-1}(w)=\frac{w}{1+|w|}.}
$$

Both maps preserve the argument and change the radius by the mutually inverse strictly increasing functions $r\mapsto r/(1-r)$ and $s\mapsto s/(1+s)$. The displayed formulas also prove [continuity](../../../../continuous-function.md) at zero and everywhere else.
