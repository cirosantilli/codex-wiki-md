<h1 id="9/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For the linearized system a [centre](../../../../../center-equilibrium.md) occurs when $\boxed{\lambda_{1,2}=\pm i\beta,\ \beta\ne0}$. Its real solutions are periodic ellipses after an invertible linear coordinate change.

As explained by [a centre in the linearization need not be a nonlinear centre](../../../../../a-centre-in-the-linearization-need-not-be-a-nonlinear-centre.md), for a nonlinear system these [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) alone do **not** guarantee a [centre](../../../../../center-equilibrium.md). For example $\dot X=-Y-X(X^2+Y^2)$, $\dot Y=X-Y(X^2+Y^2)$ has [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\pm i$ at zero but polar equations $\dot r=-r^3$, $\dot\theta=1$, so trajectories spiral inward. Removing the cubic terms gives a genuine [centre](../../../../../center-equilibrium.md) with the same Jacobian. Thus there is no [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md)-only sufficient condition for a nonlinear [centre](../../../../../center-equilibrium.md) in the stated generality.
