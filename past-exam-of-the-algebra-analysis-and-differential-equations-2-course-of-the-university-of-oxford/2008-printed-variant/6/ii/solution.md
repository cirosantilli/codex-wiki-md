<h1 id="6/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Here the zeros accumulate only at the boundary point zero. An explicit [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md), nonzero example on the [upper half-plane](../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md) is

$$
\boxed{f(z)=\sin(\pi i/z).}
$$

At $z_n=i/(n+1)$ its argument is $\pi(n+1)$, so it vanishes. It is [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) because the only excluded point of its formula is zero; it is not identically zero, for instance at $z=2i$. Thus the answer is **yes**.
