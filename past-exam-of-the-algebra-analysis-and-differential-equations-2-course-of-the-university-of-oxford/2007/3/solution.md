<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A [maximal ideal](../../../maximal-ideal.md) is a proper [ideal](../../../ideal.md) $I$ of $R$ such that an [ideal](../../../ideal.md) $J$ with $I\subseteq J\subseteq R$ is either $I$ or $R$. Suppose first that $I$ is a [maximal ideal](../../../maximal-ideal.md). If $a\notin I$, then $I+(a)=R$, so $1=i+ra$ for some $i\in I$ and $r\in R$. In the [quotient ring](../../../quotient-ring.md), $(r+I)(a+I)=1+I$. Every nonzero element of $R/I$ therefore has a [multiplicative inverse](../../../multiplicative-inverse.md), so $R/I$ is a [field](../../../field.md). Conversely, if $R/I$ is a [field](../../../field.md) and $J$ properly contains $I$, choose $a\in J\setminus I$. The element $a+I$ has a [multiplicative inverse](../../../multiplicative-inverse.md) $r+I$, whence $1-ra\in I\subset J$, giving $1\in J$ and $J=R$. Thus

$$
\boxed{I\text{ is maximal}\iff R/I\text{ is a field}.}
$$

The squares in $\mathbb F_7$ are $0,1,2,4$, which do not include $-1=6$. Hence $X^2+1$ has no root in $\mathbb F_7$. A [polynomial](../../../polynomial-split.md) of degree two or three over a [field](../../../field.md) is an [irreducible polynomial](../../../irreducible-polynomial.md) exactly when it has no root: any proper [polynomial factorization](../../../factorization-of-polynomials.md) of such a degree has a factor of degree one. The [polynomial ring](../../../polynomial-ring.md) over a [field](../../../field.md) is a [Euclidean domain](../../../euclidean-domain.md), so the [ideal](../../../ideal.md) generated by an [irreducible polynomial](../../../irreducible-polynomial.md) is a [maximal ideal](../../../maximal-ideal.md). It follows that

$$
F=\mathbb F_7[X]/(X^2+1)
$$

is a [field](../../../field.md). Every [coset](../../../coset.md) has a unique representative $a+bX$, by [polynomial division](../../../polynomial-division.md), so $|F|=7^2=49$.

Let $j$ be the [coset](../../../coset.md) of $X$ in this [field](../../../field.md). Since $j^2=-1$, the map

$$
\phi:\mathbb Z[i]\to F,\qquad a+bi\longmapsto\overline a+\overline b\,j
$$

is a [ring homomorphism](../../../ring-homomorphism.md). It is [surjective](../../../surjective-function.md) by the representative description. Since $1,j$ are [linearly independent](../../../linear-independence.md) over $\mathbb F_7$, its [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../../../kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism.md) is

$$
\boxed{\ker\phi=7\mathbb Z[i]=(7).}
$$

There is also a [field](../../../field.md) with $343$ elements. The cubes in $\mathbb F_7$ are $0,1,-1$, so $X^3-2$ has no root and is an [irreducible polynomial](../../../irreducible-polynomial.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathbb F_7[X]/(X^3-2)}
$$

is a [field](../../../field.md) whose unique representatives have degree below three. There are $7^3=343$ such representatives.
