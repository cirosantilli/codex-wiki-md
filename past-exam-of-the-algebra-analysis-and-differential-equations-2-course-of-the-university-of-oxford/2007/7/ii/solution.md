<h1 id="7/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Near $y=1$, the right side is continuously differentiable in $y$, with [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) $1/(4y^{3/4})$ bounded on, for example, $1/2\le y\le3/2$. It therefore satisfies a local [Lipschitz condition](../../../../lipschitz-continuity.md); it is also bounded on an appropriate bounded neighborhood. The existence and uniqueness result applies there. [Separation of variables](../../../../separation-of-variables.md) gives

$$
u^{3/4}=1+\frac{3x}{4},\qquad
\boxed{u(x)=\left(1+\frac{3x}{4}\right)^{4/3}\quad(x>-4/3).}
$$

The unique extension to the whole real axis is $u(x)=[\max\{1+3x/4,0\}]^{4/3}$. It joins zero with zero [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) at $x=-4/3$. To see uniqueness of this extension, $u'\ge0$ makes every solution nondecreasing. Before it reaches zero it cannot be positive, while a negative interval would have $u'=0$ and could not continuously reach zero. Thus it must be zero for $x\le-4/3$; the positive part is fixed by the separated formula.
