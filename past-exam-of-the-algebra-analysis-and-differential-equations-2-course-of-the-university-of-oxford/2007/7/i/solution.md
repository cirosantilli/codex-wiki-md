<h1 id="7/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

At zero the right side is continuous but fails every local [Lipschitz condition](../../../../lipschitz-continuity.md) in its second variable, since $\delta^{1/4}/\delta=\delta^{-3/4}\to\infty$. For every $c\ge0$, the function

$$
u_c(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\le c,\\{}[3(x-c)/4]^{4/3},&x\ge c\end{cases}
$$

is continuously differentiable and satisfies the [differential equation](../../../../differential-equation-split.md), including at the joining point where both one-sided [derivatives](../../../../derivative.md) are zero. Each has $u_c(0)=0$. The identically zero function is another possibility. Thus **solutions exist but are not unique**, even locally: choosing $c=0$ and taking the zero function already gives two different solutions on every neighborhood of zero. These are instances of [waiting-time solutions of a sublinear Bernoulli equation](../../../../waiting-time-solutions-of-a-sublinear-bernoulli-equation.md).
