<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Assume condition (iii), and factor $m=\prod_j f_j^{e_j}$. If some $e_j\ge2$, the [primary decomposition theorem](../../../../primary-decomposition-theorem-for-linear-maps.md) gives the nonzero summand $V_j=\ker f_j(T)^{e_j}$ on which the [minimal polynomial](../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) is exactly $f_j^{e_j}$. The operator $f_j(T)$ cannot vanish on this whole summand, or its restriction would have [minimal polynomial](../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) dividing $f_j$. Choose $v\in V_j$ with $f_j(T)v\ne0$. Then

$$
v\in\ker f_j(T)^{e_j}\setminus\ker f_j(T),
$$

contradicting (iii) with $p=f_j$ and $n=e_j$. Hence every $e_j=1$, proving **(iii) implies (ii)**.
