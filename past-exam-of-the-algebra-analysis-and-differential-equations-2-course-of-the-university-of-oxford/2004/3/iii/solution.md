<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [inverse image](../../../../preimage.md) $I=\theta^{-1}(J)$ is an [ideal](../../../../ideal.md), by preservation of differences and multiplication. It is proper since $\theta(1)=1\notin J$. If $ab\in I$, then $\theta(a)\theta(b)\in J$; primality of $J$ gives $\theta(a)\in J$ or $\theta(b)\in J$, hence $a\in I$ or $b\in I$. Therefore **the [inverse image](../../../../preimage.md) is prime**.

For maximality, take the inclusion $\mathbb Z\hookrightarrow\mathbb Q$ and $J=(0)$. This is a [maximal ideal](../../../../maximal-ideal.md) of the [field](../../../../field.md) $\mathbb Q$, but its [inverse image](../../../../preimage.md) is $(0)$ in $\mathbb Z$, strictly contained in the proper [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) $2\mathbb Z$, so it is not maximal. Preservation of identity matters for proper inverse images: without it the zero homomorphism could pull a proper [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) back to the whole [ring](../../../../ring.md).
