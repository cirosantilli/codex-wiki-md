<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the standard definition that a [prime ideal](../../../../prime-ideal.md) is proper, in addition to the product condition. The quotient operations are

$$
(r+I)+(s+I)=(r+s)+I,\qquad(r+I)(s+I)=rs+I.
$$

Its zero and identity are $I$ and $1+I$, which differ because $I$ is proper. If $(r+I)(s+I)=I$, then $rs\in I$, so primality gives $r\in I$ or $s\in I$. Thus one factor is zero. The commutative [quotient ring](../../../../quotient-ring.md) has no nonzero [zero divisors](../../../../zero-divisor.md) and is therefore an [integral domain](../../../../integral-domain.md).

The printed definition omits properness. If that omission were read literally, $I=R$ would satisfy its product condition, but $R/I$ would be the zero [ring](../../../../ring.md), which is not an [integral domain](../../../../integral-domain.md) under the standard convention $1\ne0$. Properness is the necessary qualification, retained explicitly in this proof.
