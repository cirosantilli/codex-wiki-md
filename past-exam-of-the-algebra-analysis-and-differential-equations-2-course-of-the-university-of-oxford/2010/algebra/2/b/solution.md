<h1 id="algebra/2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a [normal operator](../../../../../normal-operator.md), (a) gives $\|Tx\|=\|T^*x\|$, so one side is zero exactly when the other is. Hence $\boxed{\ker T=\ker T^*}$.

For real $\lambda$, the [adjoint](../../../../../adjoint-operator.md) of $T-\lambda I$ is $T^*-\lambda I$. Expanding both products shows that their difference is $T^*T-TT^*=0$. Thus $T-\lambda I$ is normal, and applying the [kernel of a linear map](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) conclusion to it gives

$$
\boxed{\ker(T-\lambda I)=\ker(T^*-\lambda I)\quad(\lambda\in\mathbb R).}
$$

This proves equality of each real [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md), hence the same [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) and [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) on the stated real [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md). If neither has real [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md), the conclusion is vacuous. Upon complexification one instead pairs $\lambda$ with $\overline\lambda$ for the [adjoint](../../../../../adjoint-operator.md); no claim of the same complex [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is needed here.
