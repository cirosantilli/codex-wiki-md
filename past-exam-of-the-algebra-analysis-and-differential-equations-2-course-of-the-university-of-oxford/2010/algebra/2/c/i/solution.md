<h1 id="algebra/2/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In fact the [adjoint](../../../../../../adjoint-operator.md) invariance follows from the [orthogonal decomposition](../../../../../../orthogonal-decomposition-by-a-closed-subspace.md) and the invariance under $T$, without using the [normal operator](../../../../../../normal-operator.md) property. For $x\in V_i$ and $y\in V_j$ with $j\ne i$,

$$
\langle T^*x,y\rangle=\langle x,Ty\rangle=0,
$$

since $Ty\in V_j$. Hence $T^*x$ is [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) to every other summand and therefore lies in $V_i$. Moreover, for $x,y\in V_i$, the [adjoint](../../../../../../adjoint-operator.md) identity restricted to that subspace shows

$$
\boxed{T_i^*=T^*|_{V_i}.}
$$
