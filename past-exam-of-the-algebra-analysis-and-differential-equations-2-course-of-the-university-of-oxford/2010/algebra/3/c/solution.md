<h1 id="algebra/3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The intersection $J=I\cap\mathbb Z$ is an additive subgroup of $\mathbb Z$, and $nj\in I\cap\mathbb Z$ for $n\in\mathbb Z$, $j\in J$. Thus it is an [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) of $\mathbb Z$, necessarily $d\mathbb Z$ for a unique $d\ge0$.

Because $d\in I$, one has $dR\subseteq I$. Conversely, if $a/b\in I$ with $b$ [coprime](../../../../../coprime-integers.md) to $30$, multiplication by $b\in R$ gives $a\in I\cap\mathbb Z=d\mathbb Z$. Write $a=dk$; then $a/b=d(k/b)\in dR$. Thus $I=dR$.

Every positive [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) factors as $d=2^\alpha3^\beta5^\gamma u$ with $\gcd(u,30)=1$. The factor $u$ is a [unit](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md), so it does not change the [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md). The complete distinct list is

$$
\boxed{(0),\qquad (2^\alpha3^\beta5^\gamma)_R\quad(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\ge0).}
$$

They are distinct because the intersection of the displayed nonzero [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) with $\mathbb Z$ is $2^\alpha3^\beta5^\gamma\mathbb Z$: admissible denominators cannot cancel any of these [prime](../../../../../prime-number-split.md) powers. The triple $(0,0,0)$ gives $R$ itself.
