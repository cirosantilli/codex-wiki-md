<h1 id="differential-equations/8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

Use a parameter $t$. The characteristic equations are

$$
\boxed{\dot x=x+cy-bz,\quad\dot y=y+az-cx,\quad\dot z=z+bx-ay.}
$$

For a [graph of a function](../../../../graph-of-a-function.md) $z=f(x,y)$ satisfying the equation, solve the first two equations after substituting $z=f(x,y)$. The [chain rule](../../../../chain-rule.md) and the [partial differential equation](../../../../partial-differential-equation-split.md) give $d f(x(t),y(t))/dt=f+bx-ay$, exactly the third equation. Uniqueness for the resulting [linear ordinary differential equation](../../../../linear-ordinary-differential-equation-split.md) system shows that its characteristic through the initial point agrees with this curve on the [graph of a function](../../../../graph-of-a-function.md). This proves invariance as long as the [graph of a function](../../../../graph-of-a-function.md) remains defined along the curve. A claim about the entire curve additionally requires a [surface](../../../../topological-surface-split.md) continued along that flow; a restricted [graph of a function](../../../../graph-of-a-function.md) patch alone cannot imply global continuation.

Let $\mathbf k=(a,b,c)$, $\boldsymbol\ell=(l,m,n)$ and $\mathbf r=(x,y,z)$. Then $\dot{\mathbf r}=\mathbf r-\mathbf k\times\mathbf r$. Since $|\mathbf k|=1$,

$$
\frac d{dt}(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r)=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r,\qquad\frac d{dt}|\mathbf r|^2=2|\mathbf r|^2.
$$

Along the characteristic starting from $s\boldsymbol\ell$ these give $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r=e^ts(\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\ell)$ and $|\mathbf r|=e^t|s|$. Consequently the characteristic [surface](../../../../topological-surface-split.md) generated by the given line satisfies

$$
\boxed{|ax+by+cz|=|al+bm+cn|\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}.}
$$

To see that the whole displayed [cone](../../../../cone-geometry.md) is generated, not just contained in it, decompose $\boldsymbol\ell=\kappa\mathbf k+\mathbf v$ with $\mathbf v\perp\mathbf k$, $\kappa=\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\ell$. The full solution is

$$
\mathbf r(s,t)=se^t\bigl(\kappa\mathbf k+\cos t\,\mathbf v-\sin t\,(\mathbf k\times\mathbf v)\bigr).
$$

When $0<|\kappa|<1$, variation of $t$ gives all azimuths about the axis, while the freely chosen $s$ supplies every positive radius on both halves. This is precisely the two-sided circular [cone](../../../../cone-geometry.md), with

$$
\boxed{\text{vertex }(0,0,0),\quad\text{axis }\mathbb R(a,b,c),\quad\text{semi-angle }\arccos|al+bm+cn|.}
$$

The edge cases matter: $\kappa=0$ gives the [plane](../../../../plane.md) $ax+by+cz=0$ (semi-angle $\pi/2$), and $|\kappa|=1$ generates only the axis (semi-angle zero), not a two-dimensional [surface](../../../../topological-surface-split.md). In the latter case the initial line is itself characteristic, so it does not determine a unique integral [surface](../../../../topological-surface-split.md). The nondegenerate [cone](../../../../cone-geometry.md) is smooth away from its vertex and can be used as a [graph of a function](../../../../graph-of-a-function.md) only on patches whose [tangent plane](../../../../tangent-plane.md) is not vertical. Thus the printed geometric conclusion has its ordinary characteristic-[surface](../../../../topological-surface-split.md) meaning in the nondegenerate case, with these domain and degeneracy qualifications; it cannot be asserted for every global smooth [graph of a function](../../../../graph-of-a-function.md) without them.
