<h1 id="analysis/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For any $0<r<1$, a [Laurent series](../../../../../laurent-series.md) converges uniformly on the circle $|z|=r$, so termwise [contour integration](../../../../../contour-integration.md) is valid. Multiplication by $z^{-k-1}$ and integration gives, for every [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) $k$,

$$
\boxed{a_k=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=r}\frac{f(z)}{z^{k+1}}\,dz.}
$$

Indeed, the integral of $z^{j-k-1}$ is $2\pi i$ for $j=k$ and zero otherwise. These coefficients depend only on $f$, so any two Laurent expansions must agree coefficient by coefficient. This proves uniqueness, including all terms of the [principal part](../../../../../principal-part-of-a-meromorphic-function.md).
