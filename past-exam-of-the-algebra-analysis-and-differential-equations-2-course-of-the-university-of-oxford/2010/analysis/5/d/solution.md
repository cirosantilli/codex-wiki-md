<h1 id="analysis/5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

If $f=z^{-n}g$ with $g(0)\ne0$, [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) gives a small disk on which $|g(0)|/2\le|g(z)|\le3|g(0)|/2$. Multiplying by $|z|^{-n}$ gives the two required bounds.

Conversely, set $g(z)=z^nf(z)$ on the punctured disk. The upper bound makes $g$ bounded, so the allowed removable-singularity theorem gives a [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) extension. The lower bound gives $|g(0)|\ge C_1>0$ by [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md). Therefore $f=z^{-n}g$ has a [pole](../../../../../pole.md) of exact order $n$. The lower bound is essential: the upper bound alone only proves order at most $n$.
