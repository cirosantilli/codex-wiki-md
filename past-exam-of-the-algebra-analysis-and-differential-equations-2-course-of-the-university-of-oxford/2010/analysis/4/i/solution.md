<h1 id="analysis/4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $f=u+iv$ and $z=x+iy$. The [complex derivative](../../../../../complex-derivative.md) computed along a real increment is $f'=u_x+iv_x$, while along an imaginary increment it is $f'=v_y-iu_y$. Equality gives

$$
\boxed{u_x=v_y,\qquad u_y=-v_x.}
$$

These are the [Cauchy–Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) at every point where $f$ is [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md). The directional calculations also establish the existence of those [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md). More explicitly, $f(z+h)-f(z)=f'(z)h+o(|h|)$ gives both real-direction formulas directly. If a converse is desired, [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) first [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) together with these equations make the real differential multiplication by $u_x+iv_x$, hence give [complex differentiability](../../../../../complex-differentiability.md); the necessity above is what is requested.
