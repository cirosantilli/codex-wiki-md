<h1 id="analysis/4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The allowed theorem makes $f'$ [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) and hence [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md); applying it repeatedly gives [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) of every order. Since $u_x=\Re f'$, $v_x=\Im f'$, $u_y=-\Im f'$ and $v_y=\Re f'$, the real and imaginary parts have [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) second [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md). Differentiate the [Cauchy–Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) and commute mixed [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md):

$$
u_{xx}+u_{yy}=(v_y)_x-(v_x)_y=0,
\qquad
v_{xx}+v_{yy}=-(u_y)_x+(u_x)_y=0.
$$

Thus $\boxed{\Delta u=\Delta v=0}$, with the required $C^2$ regularity, proving harmonicity.
