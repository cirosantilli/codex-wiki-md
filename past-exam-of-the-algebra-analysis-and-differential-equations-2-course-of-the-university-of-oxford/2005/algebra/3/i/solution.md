<h1 id="algebra/3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md) is a nonzero [commutative ring](../../../../../commutative-ring.md) with identity and no [zero divisors](../../../../../zero-divisor.md). A [principal ideal domain](../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md) is an [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md) in which every [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) is generated by one element.

The [leading coefficient](../../../../../leading-coefficient-of-a-polynomial.md) of a product of nonzero real [polynomials](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is nonzero, so $\mathbb R[x]$ is an [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md). The [zero](../../../../../zero-of-a-function.md) [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) is principal. In any nonzero [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) $J$, choose a nonzero [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $g$ of least [degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md). Divide an arbitrary $f\in J$ by $g$: $f=qg+r$ with $r=0$ or $\deg r<\deg g$. Since $r\in J$, minimality forces $r=0$. Thus $J=(g)$ and **$\mathbb R[x]$ is a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md)**.

In $\mathbb Z[x]$ consider $J=(2,x)$. If $J=(h)$, then $h$ divides the constant [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) 2, so $h$ is constant, and divisibility of $x$ forces $h=\pm1$. That would give $J=\mathbb Z[x]$. But the constant coefficient of every element $2p+xq$ is even, so $1\notin J$. This contradiction proves **$\mathbb Z[x]$ is not a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md)**.
