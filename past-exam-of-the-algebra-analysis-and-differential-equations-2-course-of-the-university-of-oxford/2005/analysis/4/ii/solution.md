<h1 id="analysis/4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take a circle of radius $R\geq S$ about [zero](../../../../../zero-of-a-function.md). The [Cauchy estimate](../../../../../cauchy-estimate.md) gives, for each integer $k>n$,

$$
|f^{(k)}(0)|\leq k!CR^{n-k}\longrightarrow0\quad(R\to\infty).
$$

Therefore all these derivatives vanish. The [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) of an [entire function](../../../../../entire-function.md) converges to it everywhere, and hence

$$
\boxed{f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}z^k.}
$$

As usual $n$ is a nonnegative integer; if a real exponent is allowed, the [degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) is at most its integer part. A negative growth exponent forces $f=0$.
