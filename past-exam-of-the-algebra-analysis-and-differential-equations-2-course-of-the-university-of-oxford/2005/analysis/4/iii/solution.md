<h1 id="analysis/4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For a bounded [entire function](../../../../../entire-function.md), the preceding result with $n=0$ gives [Liouville's theorem](../../../../../liouville-theorem.md). If a nonconstant [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $p$ had no complex root, $1/p$ would be entire. The leading term gives $|p(z)|\to\infty$ at infinity, so $1/p$ is bounded outside a large disc and is also bounded on the remaining compact disc. [Liouville's theorem](../../../../../liouville-theorem.md) would make $1/p$, and hence $p$, constant. This proves the [fundamental theorem of algebra](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md).

Finally, away from the [zeros](../../../../../zero-of-a-function.md) of $\cos z$, the [holomorphic](../../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) quotient $h=f/\cos z$ has modulus at most $C$. At each [zero](../../../../../zero-of-a-function.md) it therefore has a [removable singularity](../../../../../removable-singularity.md). These isolated points have no finite accumulation, so removing all of them gives a bounded [entire function](../../../../../entire-function.md). [Liouville's theorem](../../../../../liouville-theorem.md) gives a constant $c$ with $|c|\leq C$, and

$$
\boxed{f(z)=c\cos z.}
$$

Conversely every such multiple satisfies the assumed bound.
