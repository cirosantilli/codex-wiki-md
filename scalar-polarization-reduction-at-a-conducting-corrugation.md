# Scalar polarization reduction at a conducting corrugation

↑ **Parent:** [Perfect conductor](perfect-conductor.md)

If a metal surface is $z=h(x)$ and the incident field is independent of $y$, Maxwell's equations separate into [TE polarization](transverse-electric-polarization.md) and [TM polarization](transverse-magnetic-polarization.md). The metal condition that the tangential electric field vanish sets $E_y=0$ in the TE sector. In the TM sector $\mathbf E=i\nabla\times(H_y\widehat{\mathbf y})/(\omega\epsilon)$, so the same condition gives $(\partial_z-h'\partial_x)H_y=0$. This explains why the TE problem agrees with scalar Dirichlet acoustics while the TM problem has different rough-surface boundary forcing.
