# Ray turning in a wall shear layer

↑ **Parent:** [Acoustic eikonal equation in a shear flow](acoustic-eikonal-equation-in-a-shear-flow.md)

In a stationary, horizontally homogeneous wall [boundary layer](boundary-layer.md), [Hamiltonian ray-tracing equations](hamiltonian-ray-tracing-equations.md) conserve $\omega$ and $k_x$. If $U$ increases away from the wall, downstream-oriented $k_x>0$ waves have decreasing positive intrinsic [frequency](frequency.md) and decreasing $k_y^2$. At $U=\omega/k_x-c_0$, the transverse [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) vanishes and the acoustic ray turns. For $U(0)=0$, a downstream launch angle $\beta_0$ above the wall can reach a uniform stream of [Mach number](mach-number.md) $M_\infty$ only if $\cos\beta_0\le(1+M_\infty)^{-1}$. This uses the positive acoustic branch, constant [sound speed](speed-of-sound.md) and no intervening critical layer; a turning point itself requires a uniform local approximation rather than the ordinary [WKB method](wkb-method.md).
