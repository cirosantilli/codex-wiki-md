# Front velocity

↑ **Parent:** [Phase velocity and group velocity](phase-velocity-and-group-velocity.md)

The [front velocity](front-velocity.md) is the propagation speed of the earliest boundary of a newly introduced disturbance's [support](support.md). It asks when an initially unaffected observer can first respond, whereas [group velocity](group-velocity.md) asks how a narrow [wave envelope](envelope-waves.md) translates. A detector threshold defines a practical arrival time, but can miss arbitrarily small earlier responses; a packet peak is another distinct timing convention. In a causal dielectric model, a high-[angular frequency](angular-frequency.md) contour argument gives exactly zero field for $t<x/c$, even when [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) exceeds $c$. This is a statement about that model's [relativistic causality](relativistic-causality.md), not a universal identification of [group velocity](group-velocity.md) with [front velocity](front-velocity.md).[equations 871–873 and the paragraph following equation 887: zero field ahead of the causal front](wave-front-propagation-by-richard-fitzpatrick.md)

For the stipulated [Klein-Gordon equation](klein-gordon-equation.md) with compactly supported real [Cauchy data](cauchy-data.md), [front velocity](front-velocity.md) is bounded by $c$, and $c$ is the model's maximal speed of influence. A short [energy method](energy-method.md) outline uses

$$
e=\frac12\left(u_t^2+c^2u_x^2+\omega_0^2u^2\right),\qquad
J=-c^2u_tu_x,\qquad
\partial_te+\partial_xJ=0,\qquad |J|\le ce.
$$

Integration outside an expanding interval shows that zero initial [energy](energy-split.md) there remains zero. Thus initial [support](support.md) in $[-a,a]$ cannot influence $x>a$ before $t=(x-a)/c$. The positive [energy density](energy-density.md) includes the restoring term $\omega_0^2u^2/2$: this adds oscillation behind the front without extending its [domain of dependence](domain-of-dependence.md). A particular disturbance may fail to excite the leading boundary, so the maximal speed is not a promise of a detectable response for every source.

A [Gaussian function](gaussian-function.md) is nonzero arbitrarily far away, so the [Gaussian envelope in a Klein-Gordon medium](gaussian-envelope-in-a-klein-gordon-medium.md) has no sharp initial [support](support.md) boundary. Its small distant tails are already in the initial data. Likewise, keeping only a narrow interval of the [frequency spectrum](frequency-spectrum.md) cannot reproduce an exact abrupt onset. Cutting off a Gaussian tail changes its [Fourier transform](fourier-transform-split.md) and the accompanying initial [velocity](velocity.md); it is a different initial-value problem. The [exponential initial-velocity tail for the Klein-Gordon equation](exponential-initial-velocity-tail-for-the-klein-gordon-equation.md) is another concrete example of a pre-existing tail outside the main disturbance.

The water-tank measurement in [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) records [free surface](free-surface.md) elevation with calibrated resistance probes and uses a ruler and stopwatch for an independent check. The inspected protocol calls the front or back of a group a timing feature. That visible edge establishes an envelope transit estimate, not the first nonzero disturbance in the sense of [front velocity](front-velocity.md).[p. 1, Equipment; p. 2, task 2; p. 3, tasks 5–6: apparatus, calibration and group-feature timing](surface-waves-kinematics-mit-2-016-hydrodynamics-by-massachusetts-institute-of-technology-2-016-course-staff.md) A proposed additional control would repeat the transit fit at several detection thresholds and train durations, using matched probe calibration and the same [sampling rate](sampling-rate.md). Dependence on the threshold diagnoses [wave envelope](envelope-waves.md) reshaping or limited sensitivity; records should be compared before reflections return. Such a test could expose limitations of a [group velocity](group-velocity.md) estimate, but would not establish an exact causal front or validate the stipulated [Klein-Gordon equation](klein-gordon-equation.md) for water waves.
