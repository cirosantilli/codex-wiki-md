# Folk theorem for repeated games

↑ **Parent:** [Repeated game](repeated-game.md)

A family of results describing the large set of [Nash equilibrium](nash-equilibrium.md) [payoffs](payoff.md) available in indefinitely repeated interaction. For a finite game with observed past play, the discounted Nash version supports any feasible, strictly [individually rational payoff in a repeated game](individually-rational-payoff-in-a-repeated-game.md) when players are sufficiently patient.[Slide 24, Nash folk theorem; slides 25–27, permanent minmax punishments and the distinction from subgame perfection](lecture-15-repeated-games-by-asu-ozdaglar.md)

The mechanism is to prescribe a path and punish the first deviator by holding that player's future [payoff](payoff.md) to its [minmax payoff](minmax-payoff.md). With [long-run average payoffs](long-run-average-payoff.md), a finite gain disappears from the average; with discounting, the promised future loss must outweigh the gain. Weak boundary points require care when a stated theorem assumes strict inequalities. A construction proving a [Nash equilibrium](nash-equilibrium.md) need not prove a [subgame perfect equilibrium](subgame-perfect-equilibrium.md), because the punishers' continuation incentives may fail.
