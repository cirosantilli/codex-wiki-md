<h1 id="a/1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A set is [totally bounded](../../../../../totally-bounded-space.md) if for every $\varepsilon>0$ it can be covered by finitely many open balls of radius $\varepsilon$.

Here is the needed separated-sequence argument, including the relevant [Riesz lemma](../../../../../riesz-s-lemma.md). For a proper [closed subspace](../../../../../closed-subspace.md) $Y$, choose $z\notin Y$, put $d=\operatorname{dist}(z,Y)>0$, and choose $y\in Y$ with $\|z-y\|<2d$. Then $w=(z-y)/\|z-y\|$ is a [unit vector](../../../../../unit-vector.md) at distance greater than $1/2$ from $Y$. Starting with one [unit vector](../../../../../unit-vector.md), apply this to the span of the vectors already chosen. These spans are closed and proper because they are finite-dimensional while $X$ is infinite-dimensional. The resulting unit vectors satisfy $\|w_m-w_n\|>1/2$ for $m\ne n$. A ball of radius $1/4$ contains at most one of them. Hence **the [closed unit ball](../../../../../closed-unit-ball.md) is not [totally bounded](../../../../../totally-bounded-space.md)**.
