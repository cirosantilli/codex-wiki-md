<h1 id="b/7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

The [continuous functional calculus](../../../../continuous-functional-calculus.md) for a [Hermitian element of a C-star algebra](../../../../hermitian-element-of-a-c-star-algebra.md) identifies its generated unital [C-star algebra](../../../../c-star-algebra.md) with [continuous functions](../../../../continuous-function.md) on its real spectrum, preserves the [involution](../../../../involution.md) and [norm](../../../../norm.md), and sends functions to the corresponding spectral values by spectral mapping. Consequently an element is a square of a [Hermitian element of a C-star algebra](../../../../hermitian-element-of-a-c-star-algebra.md) exactly when it is a [Hermitian element of a C-star algebra](../../../../hermitian-element-of-a-c-star-algebra.md) with nonnegative spectrum: the reverse direction uses the continuous square root on that spectrum.

Nonnegative scalar multiples preserve this property directly. For positive $a,b$ put $C=\|a\|+\|b\|$. Functional calculus gives $\|\|a\|1-a\|\leq\|a\|$ and similarly for $b$, so $\|C1-(a+b)\|\leq C$. The sum is a [Hermitian element of a C-star algebra](../../../../hermitian-element-of-a-c-star-algebra.md), and spectral mapping now places its spectrum in $[0,2C]$. It therefore has a [positive square root in a C-star algebra](../../../../positive-square-root-in-a-c-star-algebra.md). If $C=0$ both elements are zero. Thus **$A_+$ is a cone**. It is pointed because an element positive with its negative has spectrum contained in $\{0\}$ and hence is zero by the self-adjoint [norm](../../../../norm.md) formula. It is closed as well: for a converging sequence of [Positive elements of a C-star algebra](../../../../positive-element-of-a-c-star-algebra.md) use a common [norm](../../../../norm.md) bound $C$ and pass to the limit in $\|C1-a_n\|\leq C$.

For the two-by-two [matrix](../../../../matrix.md) the exact criterion is

$$
\boxed{a_{11},a_{22}\in[0,\infty),\qquad a_{21}=\overline{a_{12}},\qquad a_{11}a_{22}\geq|a_{12}|^2.}
$$

A positive [matrix](../../../../matrix.md) is Hermitian with nonnegative [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md); evaluating its quadratic form at the coordinate vectors gives the diagonal conditions, and its [determinant](../../../../determinant.md), the [eigenvalue](../../../../eigenvalue.md) product, gives the final inequality. Conversely if $a_{11}>0$, completing the square writes the quadratic form as $a_{11}|z_1+(a_{12}/a_{11})z_2|^2+(a_{22}-|a_{12}|^2/a_{11})|z_2|^2$, which is nonnegative. If $a_{11}=0$, the [determinant](../../../../determinant.md) inequality forces $a_{12}=0$ and the conclusion is immediate. The [spectral theorem](../../../../spectral-theorem.md) then gives nonnegative [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md) and their Hermitian square root, proving positivity in the question's sense.
