<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The closed shells have $L=S=0$, so only the two equivalent p electrons matter. Their [addition of angular momentum](../../../../../addition-of-angular-momentum.md) allows $L=0,1,2$ and $S=0,1$, subject to the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md). Interchanging two $l=1$ orbital states has sign $(-1)^{2-L}=(-1)^L$: even L is spatially symmetric and needs the antisymmetric spin singlet; odd L is spatially antisymmetric and needs the symmetric spin triplet. Thus the [allowed LS terms of a p-squared configuration](../../../../../allowed-ls-terms-of-a-p-squared-configuration.md) are $^1S$, $^1D$ and $^3P$.

In [LS coupling](../../../../../ls-coupling.md), $J=|L-S|,\ldots,L+S$. The complete list of [term symbols](../../../../../term-symbol.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{{}^1S_0,\qquad{}^1D_2,\qquad{}^3P_0,\ {}^3P_1,\ {}^3P_2.}
$$

They contain $1+5+(1+3+5)=15=\binom62$ magnetic substates, exhausting the antisymmetric two-electron space. All have even spatial [parity](../../../../../parity.md), since each p orbital is odd.
