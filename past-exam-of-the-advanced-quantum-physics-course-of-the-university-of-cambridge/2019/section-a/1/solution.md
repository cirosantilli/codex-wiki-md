<h1 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The closed $1s^2$ and $2s^2$ subshells contribute zero total [orbital angular momentum](../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) and [spin](../../../../spin.md), so only the two $2p$ electrons matter. The first of [Hund's rules](../../../../hund-s-rules.md) maximizes their total spin, giving $S=1$. The [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) then requires an antisymmetric spatial [wavefunction](../../../../wave-function-split.md). Two parallel-spin electrons cannot occupy the same $m_l$ orbital: the greatest possible orbital projection is $1+0=1$, so the maximal allowed $L$ within this triplet is $L=1$.

The ground term is therefore $^3P$, in [term symbol](../../../../term-symbol.md) notation $^{2S+1}L_J$. Since the $p^2$ subshell contains fewer than half of its six possible electrons, the third of [Hund's rules](../../../../hund-s-rules.md) places the smallest $J$ lowest. [Addition of angular momentum](../../../../addition-of-angular-momentum.md) gives $J=0,1,2$, in that energy order within the ground term. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{ground level: }1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^2\;{}^3P_0\quad(L=1,S=1,J=0),}
$$



$$
\boxed{\text{first two excited levels: }{}^3P_1\ (J=1),\qquad{}^3P_2\ (J=2).}
$$

These are the [fine structure](../../../../fine-structure.md) partners of the same electronic term, with magnetic multiplicities $1,3,5$, rather than different principal-shell excitations. The ordering agrees with the tabulated lowest neutral-[carbon](../../../../carbon.md) levels.[NIST, Energy Levels of Neutral Carbon, lowest three entries](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/carbontable5.htm)
