<h1 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Let $A_{21}$ be the [spontaneous emission](../../../../spontaneous-emission.md) coefficient, and let $B_{12}\rho$ and $B_{21}\rho$ be the absorption and [stimulated emission](../../../../stimulated-emission.md) rates per atom under the radiation spectral energy density $\rho$ at resonance. Because the two levels are nondegenerate, the [Einstein coefficients](../../../../einstein-coefficients.md) satisfy $B_{12}=B_{21}=B$.

The [radiative balance in a nondegenerate two-level atom](../../../../radiative-balance-in-a-nondegenerate-two-level-atom.md) is

$$
\frac{dN_2}{dt}=N_1B\rho-N_2B\rho-N_2A_{21}=0.
$$

The total spontaneous and stimulated emission rates are $R_{\rm sp}=N_2A_{21}$ and $R_{\rm st}=N_2B\rho$. Dividing the balance equation by $N_2B\rho$ gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{R_{\rm sp}}{R_{\rm st}}=\frac{A_{21}}{B\rho}
=\frac{N_1-N_2}{N_2}=\frac{N_1}{N_2}-1.}
$$

This presumes nonzero drive and upper-level population, and no other population-transfer mechanism. It does not require a thermal [Boltzmann distribution](../../../../boltzmann-distribution.md). In this closed driven [two-level system](../../../../two-level-system.md), spontaneous decay keeps $N_1>N_2$; arbitrarily intense driving approaches equal populations, rather than producing a [population inversion](../../../../population-inversion.md).
