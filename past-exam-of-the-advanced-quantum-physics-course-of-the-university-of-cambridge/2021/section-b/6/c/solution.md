<h1 id="section-b/6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Each one-particle [energy eigenstate](../../../../../energy-eigenstate.md) has definite parity about the centre of the well, so $\langle n|x|n\rangle=0$. Consequently, a doubly occupied orbital has $\langle x_1x_2\rangle=0$. For distinct orbitals, the two off-diagonal exchange terms instead survive:

$$
\langle\Phi_{rs}^\pm|x_1x_2|\Phi_{rs}^\pm\rangle
=\pm\langle r|x|s\rangle\langle s|x|r\rangle
=\pm|x_{rs}|^2.
$$

The [position matrix elements in a symmetric infinite square well](../../../../../position-matrix-elements-in-a-symmetric-infinite-square-well.md), with the present width $a$, give

$$
x_{12}=\int_{-a/2}^{a/2}u_1(x)x u_2(x)\,dx=-\frac{16a}{9\pi^2},
\qquad D=|x_{12}|^2=\frac{256a^2}{81\pi^4}.
$$

The sign of $x_{12}$ depends on the orbital phase convention, while $D$ does not. The perturbation acts only on the spatial part and cannot mix orthogonal [singlet states](../../../../../singlet-state.md) and [triplet states](../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md). Within a triplet it is proportional to the identity on the spin factor. Thus [first-order nondegenerate perturbation theory](../../../../../first-order-nondegenerate-perturbation-theory.md), with this trivial diagonal treatment of spin degeneracy, gives the shifts corresponding to the three rows in part (b):

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{state or multiplet}&\delta E^{(1)}\\ \hline
\Phi_{11}^+\chi_{00}&0\\
\Phi_{12}^-\chi_{1M}&-\alpha D\\
\Phi_{12}^+\chi_{00}&+\alpha D
\end{array}\qquad D=\frac{256a^2}{81\pi^4}.}
$$

These formulae also apply for $J<0$, with the reordered levels described above. At $J=0$, they resolve the fourfold $5\varepsilon$ level into a shifted triplet and singlet. The next doubly occupied orbital $\Phi_{22}^+$ still has zero first-order shift. The perturbative requirement is $|\alpha|a^2\ll\varepsilon$ for mixing with remote orbital states; the spin-independent perturbation does not require division by the small singlet–triplet gap.
