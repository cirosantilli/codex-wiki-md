<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Use the isolated [nuclear spin](../atomic-nucleus.md#nuclear-spin) [Zeeman effect](../atomic-physics.md#zeeman-splitting) implied by the supplied [nuclear magnetic moment](../electromagnetism.md#nuclear-magnetic-moment), with [hyperfine structure](../quantum-mechanics.md#hyperfine-structure) neglected. The [proton](../physics.md#proton) [gyromagnetic ratio](../electromagnetism.md#gyromagnetic-ratio) is

$$
\gamma_p=\frac{g_p\mu_N}{\hbar}=2.68\times10^8\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}\,T^{-1}}.
$$

The two [spin](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) levels are separated by $g_p\mu_NB_0$. Resonant [nuclear magnetic resonance](../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md) therefore requires the [Larmor frequency](../angular-momentum-operator.md#larmor-frequency)

$$
\boxed{\omega=\gamma_p B_0=2.68\times10^8\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}},\qquad \frac{\omega}{2\pi}=42.7\ \mathrm{MHz}.
$$

The duration is a [Rabi pi pulse](../quantum-system.md#rabi-pi-pulse): in a frame rotating with the resonant transverse [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md), the [spin](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) must rotate through $\pi$. If $B_1$ denotes the magnitude of a circularly rotating field with the correct sense of [rotation](../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics), the [Rabi angular frequency](../quantum-system.md#rabi-angular-frequency) is $\Omega_R=\gamma_pB_1$. The shortest pulse is then

$$
\boxed{\tau=\frac{\pi}{\gamma_pB_1}=1.17\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{s}=117\ \mu\mathrm{s}}.
$$

There is an amplitude convention to specify. If the applied transverse [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md) is instead the linearly oscillating $B_1\cos(\omega t)\hat{\mathbf x}$, its resonant circular component has magnitude $B_1/2$. The [rotating-wave approximation](../quantum-mechanics.md#rotating-wave-approximation) gives $\Omega_R=\gamma_pB_1/2$ and hence

$$
\boxed{\tau_{\rm linear}=\frac{2\pi}{\gamma_pB_1}=234\ \mu\mathrm{s}}.
$$

The wording does not specify this polarization convention, so the two pulse durations should be distinguished. Odd multiples of either shortest [Rabi pi pulse](../quantum-system.md#rabi-pi-pulse) also invert the [spin](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) within the corresponding ideal model.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

The operators associated with the two angular momenta commute with one another, and each squared [angular momentum operator](../angular-momentum-operator.md) commutes with every component of that same [angular momentum](../momentum.md#angular-momentum). Two useful identities are

$$
J_z=J_{1z}+J_{2z},\qquad
\mathbf J_1\cdot\mathbf J_2=\frac12(J^2-J_1^2-J_2^2).
$$

Consequently the two maximal commuting subsets of the displayed collection are

$$
\boxed{\{J_1^2,J_2^2,J_{1z},J_{2z},J_z\}},\qquad
\boxed{\{J_1^2,J_2^2,J^2,J_z,\mathbf J_1\cdot\mathbf J_2\}}.
$$

The first set gives the uncoupled [eigenbasis](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenbasis) $|j_1m_1\rangle\otimes|j_2m_2\rangle$. Its $J_z$ [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) is already fixed by $m=m_1+m_2$. The second gives the coupled [eigenbasis](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenbasis) $|j_1j_2;jm\rangle$ for the [addition of angular momentum](../angular-momentum-operator.md#addition-of-angular-momentum). Its scalar-product [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) is already fixed by $j,j_1,j_2$. Thus an independent [complete set of commuting observables](../quantum-system.md#complete-set-of-commuting-observables) uses four operators in either case; literal maximality within the question's list includes the dependent fifth operator.

For generic nonzero angular momenta, no further listed operator can be added. In particular,

$$
[J_{1z},\mathbf J_1\cdot\mathbf J_2]
=i\hbar(J_{1y}J_{2x}-J_{1x}J_{2y})
$$

is generally nonzero, and $[J_{1z},J^2]=2[J_{1z},\mathbf J_1\cdot\mathbf J_2]$. The same obstruction applies to $J_{2z}$. Their sum $J_z$ commutes because the two [commutators](../lie-algebra.md#commutator) cancel.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Write $C=e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0)$. The [shell theorem](../mathematical-physics.md#spherical-shell-theorem) makes the [potential energy](../energy.md#potential-energy) of the [electron](../physics.md#electron) constant inside the charged [spherical shell](../euclidean-geometry.md#spherical-shell). Relative to the point-proton [Coulomb potential](../electrostatics.md#coulomb-potential-energy), the perturbation is

$$
\Delta V(r)=\begin{cases}
C\left(\dfrac1r-\dfrac1b\right),&0<r<b,\\
0,&r\geq b.
\end{cases}
$$

It is positive: the finite charge distribution weakens the attraction close to the origin.

The [first-order energy correction](../quantum-mechanics.md#first-order-energy-correction) is the [expectation value](../quantum-system.md#expectation-value) of this perturbation in the unperturbed [hydrogen atom](../mathematical-physics.md#hydrogen-atom) [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state). Integrating its [probability density](../wave-function.md#probability-density) over spherical volume gives

$$
\Delta E=\frac{4C}{a_0^3}\int_0^b e^{-2r/a_0}\left(r-\frac{r^2}{b}\right)\,dr.
$$

For a [proton](../physics.md#proton) radius much smaller than the [Bohr radius](../mathematical-physics.md#bohr-radius), expand the exponential uniformly over this short interval. The leading integral is

$$
\int_0^b\left(r-\frac{r^2}{b}\right)\,dr=\frac{b^2}{6},\qquad
\Delta E=\frac{2Cb^2}{3a_0^3}+O\left(\frac{Cb^3}{a_0^4}\right).
$$

Since $E_{1s}=-C/(2a_0)$, the signed fractional change is

$$
\boxed{\frac{\Delta E}{E_{1s}}=-\frac43\left(\frac b{a_0}\right)^2+O\left(\frac{b^3}{a_0^3}\right)}.
$$

Equivalently, $\Delta E/|E_{1s}|=+4b^2/(3a_0^2)$ to leading order: the [energy](../energy.md) increases and the binding-energy magnitude decreases. This is the [finite proton-shell correction to hydrogen ground energy](../mathematical-physics.md#finite-proton-shell-correction-to-hydrogen-ground-energy).

If no small-radius expansion is made, the same [time-independent perturbation theory](../quantum-mechanics.md#time-independent-perturbation-theory) integral gives, with $\beta=b/a_0$,

$$
\frac{\Delta E}{|E_{1s}|}=2\left[1-\frac1\beta+\left(1+\frac1\beta\right)e^{-2\beta}\right].
$$

Its expansion starts with $4\beta^2/3$, in agreement with the leading result. The integral is exact for the first-order calculation; it is not an exact solution of the perturbed [Schrödinger equation](../mathematical-physics.md#schrodinger-equation).

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

A [spherical tensor operator](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spherical-tensor-operator) of rank $k$ transforms under [rotation](../riemannian-geometry.md#rotation-mathematics) like an [angular momentum](../momentum.md#angular-momentum) $k$. The [Wigner-Eckart theorem](../representation-theory.md#wigner-eckart-theorem) writes its [matrix elements](../vector-space.md#matrix-element) as

$$
\langle\lambda'j'm'|T_q^{(k)}|\lambda jm\rangle
=\frac{\langle jm;kq|j'm'\rangle}{\sqrt{2j'+1}}\,
\langle\lambda'j'\Vert T^{(k)}\Vert\lambda j\rangle.
$$

The [reduced matrix element](../representation-theory.md#reduced-matrix-element) contains the radial and other dynamical information; the [Clebsch-Gordan coefficients](../representation-theory.md#clebsch-gordan-coefficients) determine the dependence on the projections. Ratios within a fixed pair of multiplets therefore follow without a radial integral. The angular [selection rules](../atomic-physics.md#selection-rule) include $m'=m+q$ and $|j-k|\leq j'\leq j+k$.

For the position vector, choose the rank-one components

$$
T_0=z,\qquad T_{\pm1}=\mp\frac{x\pm iy}{\sqrt2},\qquad
x=\frac{T_{-1}-T_{+1}}{\sqrt2}.
$$

Let $R=\langle\lambda',3/2\Vert T^{(1)}\Vert\lambda,1/2\rangle$. The stretched [Clebsch-Gordan coefficients](../representation-theory.md#clebsch-gordan-coefficients) and one lowering step give

$$
\left\langle\frac12,\frac12;1,1\middle|\frac32,\frac32\right\rangle=1,\qquad
\left\langle\frac12,\frac12;1,0\middle|\frac32,\frac12\right\rangle=\sqrt{\frac23}.
$$

For example, apply the total [angular momentum ladder operator](../quantum-mechanics.md#angular-momentum-ladder-operator) to the stretched state. The result has components with coefficients $1/\sqrt3$ and $\sqrt{2/3}$, corresponding to lowering the initial [angular momentum](../momentum.md#angular-momentum) and the vector component, respectively.

Only the $q=+1$ component of $x$ contributes to $M_1$. The $q=0$ operator $z$ cannot connect the projections in $M_2$, so that [matrix element](../vector-space.md#matrix-element) vanishes. With the convention above,

$$
M_1=-\frac{R}{2\sqrt2},\qquad M_2=0,\qquad M_3=\frac{R}{\sqrt6}.
$$

Thus the requested relative magnitudes and their squared values are

$$
\boxed{|M_1|:|M_2|:|M_3|=\sqrt3:0:2},\qquad
\boxed{|M_1|^2:|M_2|^2:|M_3|^2=3:0:4}.
$$

These ratios presume a common nonzero [reduced matrix element](../representation-theory.md#reduced-matrix-element). The position operator has odd [parity](../quantum-mechanics.md#parity), so an [electric dipole transition](../quantum-mechanics.md#electric-dipole-transition) requires opposite-parity multiplets; for one [electron](../physics.md#electron) the [electric-dipole selection rule](../quantum-mechanics.md#electric-dipole-selection-rule) also gives $\Delta\ell=\pm1$. The displayed $j,m$ labels alone do not establish that condition. If the common [reduced matrix element](../representation-theory.md#reduced-matrix-element) vanishes, all three [matrix elements](../vector-space.md#matrix-element) vanish and no nonzero ratio can be inferred.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

A uniform [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md) quantizes the transverse cyclotron motion of an [electron](../physics.md#electron). With [electron](../physics.md#electron) charge $-e$ and the [Landau gauge for a uniform magnetic field](../quantum-theory.md#landau-gauge-for-a-uniform-magnetic-field) $\mathbf A=(0,Bx,0)$, [minimal coupling](../quantum-theory.md#minimal-coupling) gives

$$
H_\perp=\frac{p_x^2}{2m}+\frac{(p_y+eBx)^2}{2m}.
$$

For an [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) of $p_y$ with [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $\hbar k_y$, this is a [quantum harmonic oscillator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md) centered at $x_c=-\hbar k_y/(eB)$, with [cyclotron frequency](../quantum-theory.md#cyclotron-frequency) $\omega_c=eB/m$. Its [Landau levels](../quantum-theory.md#landau-level) are

$$
\boxed{E_n=\hbar\omega_c\left(n+\frac12\right),\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots}.
$$

The oscillator width is set by the [magnetic length](../quantum-theory.md#magnetic-length) $\ell_B=\sqrt{\hbar/(eB)}$. In three dimensions, the free motion parallel to the [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md) adds $\hbar^2k_z^2/(2m)$. The [electron](../physics.md#electron) [spin](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) adds a [Zeeman splitting](../atomic-physics.md#zeeman-splitting) $\pm g_s\mu_BB/2$. In a crystal the appropriate [band effective mass](../bloch-s-theorem.md#band-effective-mass) and effective [spin](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) factor replace the free-electron values.

Changing $k_y$ moves the orbit center without changing the [energy](../energy.md). In a rectangle $L_xL_y$, adjacent allowed centers differ by $2\pi\ell_B^2/L_y$. Requiring the center to lie in the sample gives the [degeneracy of a Landau level](../quantum-theory.md#degeneracy-of-a-landau-level), per [spin](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin),

$$
N_\phi=\frac{L_xL_y}{2\pi\ell_B^2}=\frac{eBL_xL_y}{h},\qquad
\boxed{\frac{N_\phi}{L_xL_y}=\frac{eB}{h}}.
$$

This large [state degeneracy](../statistical-physics.md#state-degeneracy), together with the increasing level spacing, rearranges the occupied states as the [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md) changes. To resolve the [Landau levels](../quantum-theory.md#landau-level), thermal broadening $k_BT$ and disorder broadening should both be small compared with $\hbar\omega_c$.

![](../past-exam-of-the-advanced-quantum-physics-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2017-landau-levels.png)

**[Figure 1](#2/b/image-landau-levels-rise-with-magnetic-field-and-pass-through-a-fixed-reference-fermi-energy). Landau levels rise with magnetic field and pass through a fixed reference Fermi energy**.

The [de Haas-van Alphen effect](../quantum-theory.md#de-haas-van-alphen-effect) is an oscillation of [magnetization](../electromagnetism.md#magnetization) as successive [Landau levels](../quantum-theory.md#landau-level) pass the [Fermi energy](../quantum-ideal-gas-statistics.md#fermi-energy). The [Shubnikov-de Haas effect](../bloch-s-theorem.md#shubnikov-de-haas-effect) is the corresponding oscillation in longitudinal [magnetoresistance](../electric-charge.md#magnetoresistance). For a simple orbit, successive crossings occur periodically in $1/B$; the general relation to the extremal orbit area is [Onsager quantization](../quantum-theory.md#onsager-quantization). These measurements reveal the geometry and effective masses of a [Fermi surface](../fermi-liquid.md#fermi-surface).

In a two-dimensional [electron](../physics.md#electron) system, filling an integer number $\nu$ of resolved levels leads to the [integer quantum Hall effect](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#integer-quantum-hall-effect), with

$$
|\sigma_{xy}|=\nu\frac{e^2}{h},\qquad |\rho_{xy}|=\frac{h}{\nu e^2},
$$

and very small longitudinal resistivity on a plateau. Disorder localization helps produce finite plateau widths. [Cyclotron resonance](../quantum-theory.md#cyclotron-resonance) provides another direct signature: radiation is absorbed at the adjacent-level spacing $\hbar\omega_c$.

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

A [coherent state](../quantum-theory.md#coherent-state) is a normalized [eigenstate](../energy-eigenvalue.md#eigenstate) of the [annihilation operator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md#annihilation-operator), $a|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle$. Expanding in the [Fock state](../quantum-field-theory.md#fock-state) [eigenbasis](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenbasis) gives a recurrence $c_{n+1}=\alpha c_n/\sqrt{n+1}$, and [wavefunction normalization](../wave-function.md#wavefunction-normalization) fixes

$$
|\alpha\rangle=e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle
=D(\alpha)|0\rangle,\qquad
D(\alpha)=e^{\alpha a^\dagger-\alpha^*a}.
$$

The [displacement operator](../quantum-theory.md#displacement-operator) therefore shifts the oscillator [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) in [phase space](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#phase-space). Distinct [coherent states](../quantum-theory.md#coherent-state) are not orthogonal:

$$
\langle\alpha|\beta\rangle=
\exp\left[-\frac{|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2}{2}+\alpha^*\beta\right].
$$

The occupation probabilities form a [Poisson distribution](../discrete-probability-distribution.md#poisson-distribution),

$$
P_n=e^{-N}\frac{N^n}{n!},\qquad N=|\alpha|^2,
\qquad \langle n\rangle=N,\quad (\Delta n)^2=N.
$$

For the [quantum harmonic oscillator](../quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md), the [expectation values](../quantum-system.md#expectation-value) of position and momentum are

$$
\langle x\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}}\operatorname{Re}\alpha,\qquad
\langle p\rangle=\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}\operatorname{Im}\alpha.
$$

The fluctuations equal those of the [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state),

$$
(\Delta x)^2=\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega},\qquad
(\Delta p)^2=\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2},\qquad
\boxed{\Delta x\,\Delta p=\frac{\hbar}{2}}.
$$

Thus the position [wavefunction](../wave-function.md) is a displaced [Gaussian wave packet](../wave-function.md#gaussian-wave-packet) that saturates the [Heisenberg uncertainty principle](../quantum-theory.md#heisenberg-uncertainty-relation).

Time evolution under $H=\hbar\omega(a^\dagger a+1/2)$ preserves this form:

$$
\boxed{e^{-iHt/\hbar}|\alpha\rangle=e^{-i\omega t/2}|\alpha e^{-i\omega t}\rangle}.
$$

The center follows the classical oscillator trajectory while the packet width stays constant. Its mean [energy](../energy.md) is $\hbar\omega(N+1/2)$; it is generally a superposition of [energy eigenstates](../energy-eigenvalue.md#energy-eigenstate), rather than a state of definite [energy](../energy.md). For $N\gg1$, the relative occupation fluctuation is $1/\sqrt N$, explaining the classical appearance of a highly excited [coherent state](../quantum-theory.md#coherent-state).

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2017](2017.md)

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

The [Zeeman effect](../atomic-physics.md#zeeman-splitting) comes from $-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B$. For an [electron](../physics.md#electron), the orbital and [spin](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) [magnetic moments](../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-dipole-moment) are approximately $-\mu_B\mathbf L/\hbar$ and $-2\mu_B\mathbf S/\hbar$, respectively. This explains both the sign and the relative factor two in the term linear in $B$. The second term is the [spin-orbit interaction](../atomic-physics.md#spin-orbit-interaction): the [electron](../physics.md#electron)'s motion through the atomic [electric field](../electric-field.md) produces a magnetic interaction in its rest frame. The relativistic correction, including [Thomas precession](../special-relativity.md#thomas-precession), and the radial [expectation value](../quantum-system.md#expectation-value) are incorporated into $K$. This effective [Hamiltonian](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#hamiltonian) retains the linear [Zeeman splitting](../atomic-physics.md#zeeman-splitting) and [spin-orbit coupling](../atomic-physics.md#spin-orbit-interaction), while omitting diamagnetic and [hyperfine structure](../quantum-mechanics.md#hyperfine-structure) corrections.

Define the [angular momentum ladder operators](../quantum-mechanics.md#angular-momentum-ladder-operator) by $L_\pm=L_x\pm iL_y$ and similarly for $S_\pm$. Direct expansion gives

$$
\frac12(L_+S_-+L_-S_+)=L_xS_x+L_yS_y,
$$

so adding $L_zS_z$ gives the required scalar product. The off-diagonal terms change $m_\ell$ and $m_s$ in opposite directions. Consequently $J_z=L_z+S_z$ commutes with $H$, and $m_j$ remains a good [quantum number](../quantum-system.md#quantum-number) at every [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md).

In the given product [eigenbasis](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenbasis), the diagonal [matrix elements](../vector-space.md#matrix-element) are

$$
\langle m_\ell,m_s|H|m_\ell,m_s\rangle
=\mu_BB(m_\ell+2m_s)+2Km_\ell m_s.
$$

For $\ell=1$ and $s=1/2$, the only off-diagonal couplings are between $(1,-1/2)$ and $(0,1/2)$, and between $(0,-1/2)$ and $(-1,1/2)$. Each relevant orbital ladder factor is $\hbar\sqrt2$ and each [spin](../angular-momentum-operator.md#spin) ladder factor is $\hbar$. Multiplying by $K/\hbar^2$ gives $\sqrt2K$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{a=\mu_BB,\qquad b=K,\qquad c=\sqrt2K}.
$$

All other off-diagonal entries vanish either by the ladder endpoints or by conservation of $m_j$. This derives every entry of the displayed matrix.

The stretched states have no mixing and yield

$$
\boxed{E_{m_j=3/2}=K+2a,\qquad E_{m_j=-3/2}=K-2a}.
$$

The $m_j=1/2$ block is $\begin{pmatrix}-K&\sqrt2K\\\sqrt2K&a\end{pmatrix}$, while the $m_j=-1/2$ block is $\begin{pmatrix}-a&\sqrt2K\\\sqrt2K&-K\end{pmatrix}$. Taking the trace and determinant of each $2\times2$ block gives the remaining four [energy eigenvalues](../energy-eigenvalue.md):

$$
\boxed{E_{+}^{(\pm)}=\frac{a-K}{2}\pm\frac12\sqrt{(a+K)^2+8K^2}},
$$



$$
\boxed{E_{-}^{(\pm)}=\frac{-a-K}{2}\pm\frac12\sqrt{(a-K)^2+8K^2}}.
$$

The subscripts indicate $m_j=\pm1/2$ and the superscripts indicate the upper or lower root. These formulas are the [Zeeman spectrum for orbital angular momentum one](../atomic-physics.md#zeeman-spectrum-for-orbital-angular-momentum-one) and remain valid for either sign of $K$.

For the usual ordering $K>0$, including sodium, the zero-field states follow from

$$
\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S=\frac12(J^2-L^2-S^2),\qquad
E_j=K\left[j(j+1)-\ell(\ell+1)-s(s+1)\right].
$$

The $j=3/2$ quartet lies at $K$ and the $j=1/2$ doublet at $-2K$, separated by $3K$. In a weak [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md), the [Landé g-factors](../atomic-physics.md#lande-g-factor) give

$$
E_{3/2,m_j}=K+\frac43am_j+O(a^2/K),\qquad
E_{1/2,m_j}=-2K+\frac23am_j+O(a^2/K).
$$

These label the curves near $B=0$. In a strong [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md), the [Paschen-Back effect](../atomic-physics.md#paschen-back-effect) makes $(m_\ell,m_s)$ the appropriate approximate labels:

$$
E_{m_\ell,m_s}=a(m_\ell+2m_s)+2Km_\ell m_s+O(K^2/a)
$$

for the mixed states, with the stretched-state formulas already exact. The branch connections for $B>0$ are

$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{zero-field label }(j,m_j)&\text{strong-field label }(m_\ell,m_s)\\\hline
(3/2,3/2)&(1,1/2)\\
(3/2,1/2)&(0,1/2)\\
(1/2,1/2)&(1,-1/2)\\
(3/2,-1/2)&(-1,1/2)\\
(1/2,-1/2)&(0,-1/2)\\
(3/2,-3/2)&(-1,-1/2)
\end{array}
$$

Within a fixed $m_j$, the nonzero coupling prevents a crossing; this is an [avoided crossing](../bloch-s-theorem.md#avoided-crossing). Curves with different $m_j$ may cross because the [Hamiltonian](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#hamiltonian) does not couple them.

<a id="3/image-exact-spin-orbit-and-zeeman-energies-for-orbital-angular-momentum-one-and-positive-spin-orbit-constant"></a>
![](../past-exam-of-the-advanced-quantum-physics-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2017-spin-orbit-zeeman-spectrum.png)

**[Figure 2](#3/image-exact-spin-orbit-and-zeeman-energies-for-orbital-angular-momentum-one-and-positive-spin-orbit-constant). Exact spin-orbit and Zeeman energies for orbital angular momentum one and positive spin-orbit constant**.

For the sodium estimate, the zero-field frequency separation fixes

$$
3K=h\delta f,\qquad K=1.14\times10^{-22}\ \mathrm J.
$$

The lower $3s$ doublet has shifts $\pm a$. The [Sodium D-line Zeeman pattern](../atomic-physics.md#sodium-d-line-zeeman-pattern) gives photon-frequency shifts $a/h$ times $\{\pm2/3,\pm4/3\}$ for D$_1$ and $\{\pm1/3,\pm1,\pm5/3\}$ for D$_2$. Thus the highest-frequency D$_1$ component and lowest-frequency D$_2$ component approach each other by $3a/h$. Extrapolating the [weak-field Zeeman splitting](../atomic-physics.md#weak-field-zeeman-splitting) gives

$$
\boxed{B_{\rm cross}\sim\frac{h\delta f}{3\mu_B}=12.3\ \mathrm T},
$$

so the crossover occurs at a field of order $10\ \mathrm T$, where $a\sim K$ and the weak-field approximation is becoming marginal.

The exact [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) refine the estimate within this [Hamiltonian](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#hamiltonian). The relevant photon energies, with a common zero-field reference omitted, are $E_+^{(-)}+a$ and $E_-^{(+)}-a$. The corresponding [electric dipole transitions](../quantum-mechanics.md#electric-dipole-transition) have $\Delta m_j=+1$ and $-1$, respectively. Equating them gives

$$
6a=\sqrt{(a+K)^2+8K^2}+\sqrt{(a-K)^2+8K^2}.
$$

Its positive solution is $a/K=\sqrt{10}/3$, hence

$$
\boxed{B_{\rm cross}=\frac{\sqrt{10}\,h\delta f}{9\mu_B}\simeq13.0\ \mathrm T}
$$

in the retained model. The crossing is between optical transition frequencies; the participating upper states themselves need not be degenerate.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [2017](2017.md)

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

For elastic scattering by the [Yukawa potential](../electromagnetism.md#yukawa-potential), take $\kappa>0$ and use the [first Born approximation](../quantum-theory.md#born-approximation) in the plane-wave normalization

$$
f_B(\mathbf q)=-\frac{m}{2\pi\hbar^2}\int e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}V(r)\,d^3r.
$$

Choose the polar axis along the [momentum transfer](../quantum-scattering.md#momentum-transfer) $\hbar\mathbf q$. The angular integral is

$$
\int e^{-iqr\cos\vartheta}\,d\Omega
=2\pi\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-iqru}\,du
=4\pi\frac{\sin(qr)}{qr}.
$$

The radial volume element cancels the remaining powers of $r$, leaving

$$
f_B(q)=-\frac{2mA}{\hbar^2q}\int_0^\infty e^{-\kappa r}\sin(qr)\,dr
=-\frac{2mA}{\hbar^2(q^2+\kappa^2)}.
$$

For real $A$, the [differential scattering cross-section](../quantum-scattering.md#differential-scattering-cross-section) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=|f_B(q)|^2
=\left[\frac{2mA}{\hbar^2(q^2+\kappa^2)}\right]^2}.
$$

This also has a finite forward limit when $\kappa>0$. The [Born amplitude for a Yukawa potential](../quantum-theory.md#born-amplitude-for-a-yukawa-potential) provides a screened route to the [Coulomb potential](../electrostatics.md#coulomb-potential-energy) by taking $\kappa\to0^+$ at fixed $q>0$.

For an [alpha particle](../atomic-nucleus.md#alpha-particle) of charge $2e$ scattering from a heavy nucleus of charge $Ze$, set $A=2Ze^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0)$. Neglect recoil, so that the reduced mass is approximately the projectile mass, $E=\hbar^2k^2/(2m)$, and elastic geometry gives

$$
q^2=|\mathbf k_{\rm out}-\mathbf k_{\rm in}|^2
=4k^2\sin^2\frac\theta2.
$$

The Coulomb-limit [scattering amplitude](../quantum-scattering.md#scattering-amplitude) becomes

$$
f_B(\theta)=-\frac{A}{4E\sin^2(\theta/2)}
=-\frac{Ze^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0E\sin^2(\theta/2)},
$$

which gives the [Rutherford differential cross-section](../atomic-physics.md#rutherford-differential-cross-section)

$$
\boxed{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}
=\left[\frac{Ze^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0E\sin^2(\theta/2)}\right]^2}.
$$

The unscreened [Coulomb potential](../electrostatics.md#coulomb-potential-energy) has long range, so taking this limit does not justify the [Born approximation](../quantum-theory.md#born-approximation) for its full scattering state, particularly for a strongly charged target. The exact [Rutherford scattering](../atomic-physics.md#rutherford-scattering) amplitude differs by a phase and has the same modulus. Thus the resulting [differential scattering cross-section](../quantum-scattering.md#differential-scattering-cross-section) remains applicable to the ideal nonrelativistic Coulomb problem beyond the weak-potential regime.

To relate the detector counts to the [atomic number](../chemical-substance.md#atomic-number), first compute the number of target atoms per illuminated area. With molar mass $M=0.197\ \mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}$, the [number density](../statistical-physics.md#number-density) integrated through the foil is

$$
\mathcal N=\frac{\rho tN_A}{M}
=\frac{(19.3\times10^3)(2.1\times10^{-7})(6.02214\times10^{23})}{0.197}
=1.239\times10^{22}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}}.
$$

The detector's transverse area is already its area normal to the scattered ray. Its small [solid angle](../euclidean-geometry.md#solid-angle) is

$$
\Delta\Omega\simeq\frac{10^{-6}}{(10^{-2})^2}=10^{-2}\ \mathrm{sr}.
$$

There is no extra $\cos45^\circ$ factor: the foil is traversed normally and the detector area is transverse to its line of sight.

For a thin foil with negligible [multiple scattering](../wave.md#multiple-scattering) and [energy](../energy.md) loss, the detected fraction is $f=\mathcal N(d\sigma/d\Omega)\Delta\Omega$. Thus the measurements imply

$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}
=\frac{3.7\times10^{-7}}{(1.239\times10^{22})(10^{-2})}
=2.986\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{m^2\,sr^{-1}}.
$$

Using $E=7.68\times10^6e\ \mathrm J=1.23047\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm J$ in the [Rutherford differential cross-section](../atomic-physics.md#rutherford-differential-cross-section) gives

$$
Z=\frac{8\pi\epsilon_0 E\sin^2(22.5^\circ)}{e^2}
\sqrt{\frac{f}{\mathcal N\Delta\Omega}},\qquad
\boxed{Z\simeq85.4\approx85}.
$$

**The stated measurements do not reproduce gold's [atomic number](../chemical-substance.md#atomic-number) 79 in this model.** The values and units above agree with the original PDF. Substituting $Z=79$ instead would predict $f\simeq3.17\times10^{-7}$ with the other inputs unchanged, rather than $3.7\times10^{-7}$. No stated correction accounts for that discrepancy; forcing the answer to 79 would require changing the data or adding an experimental correction not specified in the problem.
