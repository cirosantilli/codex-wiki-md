<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At incident [wave number](../../../../../wavenumber.md) $k$, low [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) means $ka\ll1$: the [de Broglie wavelength](../../../../../de-broglie-wavelength.md) is much larger than the scattering region. A generic finite-range scatterer can then be described to leading order without resolving its angular structure. In [partial-wave scattering](../../../../../partial-wave-scattering.md), this is leading [S-wave scattering](../../../../../s-wave-quantum-scattering.md). The $\ell=0$ [spherical harmonic](../../../../../spherical-harmonic.md) is constant, whereas the centrifugal term $\hbar^2\ell(\ell+1)/(2mr^2)$ impedes penetration to the potential for $\ell>0$.

For a [central potential](../../../../../central-potential.md) with regular threshold behaviour, the [finite-range partial-wave threshold law](../../../../../finite-range-partial-wave-threshold-law.md) gives $\delta_\ell=O(k^{2\ell+1})$. Thus

$$
f(\theta)=\frac1k\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)e^{i\delta_\ell}\sin\delta_\ell\,P_\ell(\cos\theta)
\simeq\frac{e^{i\delta_0}\sin\delta_0}{k},
$$

which is independent of scattering angle. For finite nonzero [scattering length](../../../../../scattering-length-from-a-partial-wave-s-matrix.md) $a_s$, $f\to-a_s$ and **$d\sigma/d\Omega\to a_s^2$ is isotropic**. The potential range $a$ and [scattering length](../../../../../scattering-length-from-a-partial-wave-s-matrix.md) $a_s$ are different quantities. The claim describes the generic leading [S-wave scattering](../../../../../s-wave-quantum-scattering.md) limit; tuned vanishing [S-wave scattering](../../../../../s-wave-quantum-scattering.md) amplitude or exceptional threshold resonances require separate analysis rather than a universal angular conclusion.
