<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Closed $1s^2$ and $2s^2$ subshells have $L=S=0$. In the open $2p^4$ subshell, three [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) can have parallel [spins](../../../../../spin.md) in $m_\ell=-1,0,1$, and the fourth must have the opposite [spin](../../../../../spin.md), giving maximum $S=1$. Treating the shell as two $p$ holes gives the same allowed terms as $p^2$; at this [spin](../../../../../spin.md), antisymmetry permits $L=1$, rather than $L=2$. One can also see $M_L=1$ by placing the fourth [electron](../../../../../electron.md) in $m_\ell=1$.

[Hund's rules](../../../../../hund-s-rules.md) therefore select the $ {}^3P$ term. Since four [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) exceed half filling of the six-state $p$ subshell, its lowest [atomic fine-structure level](../../../../../atomic-fine-structure-level.md) has the largest $J=L+S=2$:

$$
\boxed{1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^4\;{}^3P_2,\qquad S=1,\ L=1,\ J=2.}
$$

Its zero-field magnetic degeneracy is $2J+1=5$. The experimentally tabulated neutral-oxygen ground level has precisely this term and $J$.[first configuration block, 2s2 2p4, triplet P, J=2 at zero energy](../../../../../energy-levels-of-neutral-oxygen-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology.md)
