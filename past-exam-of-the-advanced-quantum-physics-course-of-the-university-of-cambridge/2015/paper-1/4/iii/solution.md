<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $-z$, use a resonant half-turn pulse,

$$
\boxed{\tau_\pi=\pi/\Omega.}
$$

The up amplitude vanishes because $\cos(\Omega\tau_\pi/2)=0$, while the down amplitude has unit magnitude. Its [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) is a common [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) of the final state, so any RF [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) gives

$$
\boxed{|\psi(\tau_\pi)\rangle\sim|\downarrow\rangle,
\qquad \langle s_z\rangle=-\hbar/2.}
$$

The final population inversion is insensitive to a free-precession [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md), unlike the orientation of a transverse [superposition](../../../../../superposition-principle.md). This is a [Rabi pi pulse](../../../../../rabi-pi-pulse.md) with the drive-amplitude convention specified above.
