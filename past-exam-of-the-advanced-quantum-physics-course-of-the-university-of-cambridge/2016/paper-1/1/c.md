Using a spherical Bohr-radius volume for the atom, the calculations below give a cutoff electromagnetic [vacuum energy density](../../../../vacuum-energy-density.md) of about $6.2\times10^{15}\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}$, compared with a [hydrogen](../../../../hydrogen.md) binding-energy density of about $3.5\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}$. **The cutoff vacuum estimate is about $1.8\times10^3$ times larger.** Alternative reasonable definitions of atomic volume change this ratio by a factor of a few, not the main order-of-magnitude conclusion.

The cutoff result is a bare zero-point mode sum. It is not a classical radiation bath incident on the atom, nor a calculation of an observable absolute vacuum energy without a subtraction or [renormalization](../../../../renormalization.md) prescription.
