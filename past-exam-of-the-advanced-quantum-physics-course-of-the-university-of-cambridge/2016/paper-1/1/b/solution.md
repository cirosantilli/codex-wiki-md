<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Hermiticity supplies the reverse [matrix elements](../../../../../matrix-element.md), also equal to the real number $t$. In an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) for the degenerate [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md), the perturbation is

$$
H_1=t\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&0&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix}
=t(\mathbf J_3-I),
$$

where $\mathbf J_3$ is the [all-ones matrix](../../../../../all-ones-matrix.md). Its symmetric [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $(1,1,1)/\sqrt3$ has [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $2t$. Every [vector](../../../../../vector.md) whose components sum to zero has [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $-t$; that subspace has dimension two. For example, $(1,-1,0)/\sqrt2$ and $(1,1,-2)/\sqrt6$ form an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) for it.

The first-order energies are therefore

$$
\boxed{E_0+2t\quad\text{once},\qquad E_0-t\quad\text{twice}.}
$$

The [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) of the shifts is $2t-t-t=0$, agreeing with the zero diagonal. The ordering depends on the sign of $t$; the formula does not assume that the symmetric state is the lowest. Diagonalizing the perturbation inside the degenerate space is essential: the original diagonal entries alone would incorrectly predict no splitting.
