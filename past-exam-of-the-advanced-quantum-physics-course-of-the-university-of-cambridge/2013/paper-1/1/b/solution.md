<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Adjacent [nodes of a wavefunction](../../../../../node-of-a-wavefunction.md) are separated by roughly $a/n$, so a locally sinusoidal [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) has typical [wavevector](../../../../../wavevector.md) $k\sim\pi n/a$. The [kinetic-energy estimate from wavefunction nodes](../../../../../kinetic-energy-estimate-from-wavefunction-nodes.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\langle K\rangle\sim\frac{\hbar^2\pi^2 n^2}{2ma^2}\quad(n\gg1).}
$$

Equivalently, $\langle K\rangle=\hbar^2\int|\psi'|^2dx/(2m\int|\psi|^2dx)$. The robust scaling is $\hbar^2 n^2/(ma^2)$; the precise coefficient depends on how the extent is defined and how the oscillations vary near [classical turning points](../../../../../classical-turning-point.md). For few nodes, use an order-one offset to $n$: the nodeless [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) still has [confinement energy bound from position support](../../../../../confinement-energy-bound-from-position-support.md) of order $\hbar^2/(ma^2)$, rather than zero.
