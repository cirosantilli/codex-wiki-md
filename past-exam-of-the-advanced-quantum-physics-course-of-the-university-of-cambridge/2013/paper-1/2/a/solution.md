<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For [identical particles](../../../../../indistinguishable-particles.md), the [particle exchange operator](../../../../../particle-exchange-operator.md) exchanges both spatial and [spin](../../../../../spin.md) labels. The [symmetrization postulate](../../../../../symmetrization-postulate.md) requires a symmetric full state for [bosons](../../../../../boson.md) and an antisymmetric full state for [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md). A product $\Psi=\phi\chi$ must therefore have spatial and spin exchange eigenvalues whose product is $+1$ or $-1$, respectively; a general state may be a sum of such products.

For orthonormal spatial orbitals $u,v$, the combinations $\phi_\pm=[u(1)v(2)\pm v(1)u(2)]/\sqrt2$ have opposite exchange parities. Two spin-one-half [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md) combine $\phi_+$ with the antisymmetric [spin-one-half singlet state](../../../../../spin-one-half-singlet-state.md) $(|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)/\sqrt2$, or $\phi_-$ with one of the three symmetric [spin-one-half triplet states](../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md). **Two particles in the same spatial orbital must be a spin singlet.** This is the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) applied to complete [spin orbitals](../../../../../spin-orbital.md), not a ban on sharing a spatial orbital with opposite [spins](../../../../../spin.md).

A [Slater determinant](../../../../../slater-determinant.md) enforces fermionic antisymmetry for many occupied [spin orbitals](../../../../../spin-orbital.md). Bosonic symmetrization instead uses a [permanent](../../../../../permanent-mathematics.md); repeated orbital occupations are allowed. These exchange requirements apply even when particles do not interact.
