<h1 id="section-b/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The unperturbed [infinite square well](../../../../../infinite-square-well.md) is invariant under the [parity operator](../../../../../parity-operator.md). Its one-dimensional [energy eigenvalues](../../../../../energy-eigenvalue-split.md) are nondegenerate, so each [energy eigenstate](../../../../../energy-eigenstate.md) has definite parity. Its position [expectation value](../../../../../expectation-value.md) therefore vanishes: $|\phi_n(x)|^2$ is even, while $x$ is odd. The [first-order nondegenerate perturbation theory](../../../../../first-order-nondegenerate-perturbation-theory.md) correction is consequently

$$
\delta\epsilon_n^{(1)}=eE\langle\phi_n|x|\phi_n\rangle=0.
$$

The [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) does mix opposite-parity [quantum states](../../../../../quantum-state.md), and [second-order nondegenerate perturbation theory](../../../../../second-order-nondegenerate-perturbation-theory.md) contains two factors of $eEx$. Its leading contribution is thus proportional to $E^2$: this is a [quadratic Stark effect](../../../../../quadratic-stark-effect.md). More generally, applying the [parity operator](../../../../../parity-operator.md) changes $H(E)$ into $H(-E)$, so their corresponding [energy eigenvalues](../../../../../energy-eigenvalue-split.md) agree. An analytic expansion about zero [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) contains only even powers of $E$. We assume $e|E|a$ is small compared with the unperturbed excitation scale.
