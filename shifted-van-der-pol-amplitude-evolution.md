# Shifted Van der Pol amplitude evolution

↑ **Parent:** [Van der Pol amplitude evolution](van-der-pol-amplitude-evolution.md)

For $x''+\epsilon c(1-x^2)x'+x=k$, use $T=\epsilon t$ and $x=k-r(T)\cos(t+\phi(T))$. Removal of [secular terms](secular-term.md) gives $\phi_T=0$ and the displayed [amplitude equation](amplitude-equation.md). With $R=r^2$, $R_T=c(k^2-1)R+(c/4)R^2$, which is explicitly integrable. If $c$ is replaced by a slowly varying coefficient $C(T)$, the same solution uses the accumulated time $\int_0^T C(s)\,ds$. The [method of multiple scales](method-of-multiple-scales.md) is uniform on bounded slow-time intervals only while its [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) and correction estimates remain bounded.
