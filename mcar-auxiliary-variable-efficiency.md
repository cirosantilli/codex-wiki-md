# MCAR auxiliary-variable efficiency

↑ **Parent:** [Missing completely at random](missing-completely-at-random.md)

With a fully observed covariate $X$, response $Y$, and an independent response indicator with [probability](probability.md) $\rho$, let $m(X)=E(Y\mid X)$. A covariate-standardized mean has asymptotic [variance](variance-split.md) $[\operatorname{Var}(m)+E\{\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)\}/\rho]/N$, whereas the complete-case mean has [variance](variance-split.md) $\operatorname{Var}(Y)/(N\rho)$. The difference is $(1-\rho)\operatorname{Var}(m)/(N\rho)$. Thus [missing completely at random](missing-completely-at-random.md) does not justify discarding predictive observed covariates on efficiency grounds.
