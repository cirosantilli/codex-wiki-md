# Terms of a power series are unbounded outside its convergence disc

↑ **Parent:** [Radius of convergence](radius-of-convergence.md)

Let a [power series](power-series.md) $\sum_{n\geq0}a_nz^n$ have finite [radius of convergence](radius-of-convergence.md) $R$. If its terms were bounded in [complex modulus](complex-modulus.md) at a point $z$ with $|z|>R$, say $|a_nz^n|\leq M$, choose $w$ with $R<|w|<|z|$. Then

$$
|a_nw^n|\leq M\left(\frac{|w|}{|z|}\right)^n.
$$

The [comparison test for series](comparison-test-for-series.md) with a convergent [geometric series](geometric-series.md) would give [absolute convergence](absolute-convergence.md) at $w$, contradicting its position outside the convergence disc. Hence the terms are unbounded at every such $z$. The argument also works when $R=0$ and gives a stronger obstruction than the usual [term test for divergence](term-test-for-divergence.md).
