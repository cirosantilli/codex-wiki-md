# Unattainability of absolute zero

↑ **Parent:** [Third law of thermodynamics](third-law-of-thermodynamics.md)

In a regular thermodynamic formulation, a state at [absolute zero](absolute-zero.md) cannot be reached from positive [temperature](temperature-split.md) by finitely many cooling operations with finite control resources. The entropy-difference form of the [third law of thermodynamics](third-law-of-thermodynamics.md) makes regular [entropy](entropy-split.md) curves approach a common limiting [entropy](entropy-split.md). An [isentropic process](isentropic-process.md) starting above that [entropy](entropy-split.md) cannot intersect the common zero-temperature endpoint.

For an illustrative pair of settings with $S-S_0=a_1T$ and $S-S_0=a_2T$, $0<a_1<a_2$, an isothermal entropy-reduction step followed by reversible adiabatic cooling gives $T_{n+1}=(a_1/a_2)T_n$. Every finite number of stages leaves $T_n>0$. The model illustrates the [entropy](entropy-split.md) geometry; a general finite-time impossibility statement additionally needs assumptions about available interactions and cooling rates.
