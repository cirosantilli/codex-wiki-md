# Alternating-reference error bound

↑ **Parent:** [Equioscillation theorem](equioscillation-theorem.md)

Let a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) $q$ of degree at most $n$ have errors $f(x_j)-q(x_j)=(-1)^jh$ at $n+2$ ordered distinct points. Then $|h|$ bounds below the error $E_n(f)$ of the [best uniform approximation](best-uniform-approximation.md). Otherwise an improved [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) $p$ with error strictly below $|h|$ would make $p-q$ alternate strictly in sign at all those points, giving at least $n+1$ distinct [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) by the [intermediate value theorem](intermediate-value-theorem.md). Its degree is at most $n$, a contradiction. Moreover, if $q$ is not optimal, $|h|<E_n(f)$. In the equality case, an optimal $p$ gives weak alternating signs of $p-q$; its vanishing order-$(n+1)$ [divided difference](divided-difference.md) has same-sign summands, forcing every nodal value to vanish and hence $p=q$.
