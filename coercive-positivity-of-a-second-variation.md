# Coercive positivity of a second variation

↑ **Parent:** [Second variation](second-variation.md)

For fixed-endpoint variations, a [second variation](second-variation.md) is coercively positive if the displayed estimate holds for one constant $c>0$ and every admissible variation. The endpoint [Poincaré inequality](poincare-inequality.md) makes this equivalent to a positive lower bound in the $W^{1,2}$ norm. It is stronger than strict positivity on each nonzero variation: $q(\varphi)=\int\varphi^2$ is strictly positive, but high-frequency variations make its ratio to $\int\varphi'^2$ tend to zero.

For $q=\int(p\varphi'^2+2r\varphi\varphi'+s\varphi^2)$ with [continuous](continuous-function.md) coefficients, coercive positivity implies $p\geq c$. Indeed, if $p-c<0$ on an interval, multiply a smooth [function](function-split.md) supported there by $\sin(nx)$. Its negative highest-[derivative](derivative.md) contribution has order $n^2$, while the mixed term has order at most $n$ and the remaining term is bounded. This contradicts the displayed inequality for large $n$. In particular coercive positivity implies the [Strengthened Legendre condition](strengthened-legendre-condition.md), whereas mere strict positivity does not.
