# Retarded delay differential equation

↑ **Parent:** [Delay differential equation](delay-differential-equation.md)

For a single constant delay $\tau>0$, a [retarded delay differential equation](retarded-delay-differential-equation.md) has the displayed form: delayed values of the unknown [function](function-split.md) enter, but delayed first [derivatives](derivative.md) do not. The unknown can also be a [vector](vector.md).[Skip Thompson, Delay-differential equations, opening definition and equation (1): delayed values versus delayed derivatives](delay-differential-equations-by-skip-thompson.md)

Here $t$ denotes time, and the rule applies for $t>0$, with a right [derivative](derivative.md) at $0$. On $0\leq t\leq\tau$, the argument $t-\tau$ lies in the prescribed past. Once this interval is known, it supplies the delayed values for $\tau\leq t\leq2\tau$. This is the [method of steps for a delay differential equation](method-of-steps-for-a-delay-differential-equation.md): solve successive [ordinary differential equations](ordinary-differential-equation.md) with a known delayed input, keeping each new endpoint as the next [initial condition](initial-condition.md). For a [continuous function](continuous-function.md) $f$ locally [Lipschitz continuous](lipschitz-continuity.md) in its two state arguments, locally uniformly in $t$, this gives local existence and uniqueness for each continuous [history function of a delay differential equation](history-function-of-a-delay-differential-equation.md), while the [solution of a differential equation](solution-of-a-differential-equation.md) remains in the domain of $f$. A nonlinear rule can still produce [finite-time blowup](finite-time-blowup.md).
