<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) varying over a length $\ell$ has $|\nabla\times\mathbf B|$ of order $B/\ell$. Consequently, the resistive [magnetic energy](../../../../magnetic-energy.md) loss contains two inverse powers of $\ell$, while the [resistive magnetic-helicity balance](../../../../resistive-magnetic-helicity-balance.md) contains only one. Small-scale gradients can therefore dissipate substantial [magnetic energy](../../../../magnetic-energy.md) while retaining an approximately conserved large-scale [magnetic helicity](../../../../magnetic-helicity.md).

A dimensionally consistent version is the [selective decay bound for magnetic helicity](../../../../selective-decay-bound-for-magnetic-helicity.md). With $D_B=\eta_m\int_V|\nabla\times\mathbf B|^2\,dV/\mu_0$, the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) gives

$$
|\dot H|\le2\eta_m\left(\int_VB^2\,dV\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_V|\nabla\times\mathbf B|^2\,dV\right)^{1/2}
=\boxed{2\mu_0\sqrt{2\eta_mE_BD_B}}.
$$

For bounded $E_B$ and bounded, possibly nonvanishing $D_B$ as $\eta_m\to0$, the resistive change in [magnetic helicity](../../../../magnetic-helicity.md) tends to zero, even though resistive [magnetic energy](../../../../magnetic-energy.md) loss may remain finite. **Magnetic energy is preferentially dissipated, while magnetic helicity is approximately conserved in this bounded-energy, bounded-dissipation limit.**

This is not a comparison of quantities with different physical units or a universal ordering of fractional decay rates. For a single [Beltrami field](../../../../beltrami-field.md) satisfying $\nabla\times\mathbf B=k\mathbf B$, both integrated quantities have fractional decay rate $2\eta_mk^2$ without fluid motion. The boundedness assumptions and distribution of [magnetic helicity](../../../../magnetic-helicity.md) across spatial scales matter.
