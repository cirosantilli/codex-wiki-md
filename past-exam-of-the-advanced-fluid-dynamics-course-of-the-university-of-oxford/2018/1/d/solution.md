<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Within the [reduced equations for an anisotropic rotating fluid](../../../../reduced-equations-for-an-anisotropic-rotating-fluid.md), a flow that remains purely horizontal has $u_\parallel=0$ and $\partial_tu_\parallel=0$. The second reduced equation therefore requires

$$
0=-2\Omega\partial_z\Phi,\qquad
\boxed{\partial_z\Phi=0,\quad\partial_z\mathbf u_\perp^{(0)}=0.}
$$

Thus the retained horizontal motion is two-dimensional. This is a [Taylor–Proudman theorem](../../../../taylor-proudman-theorem.md) consequence in the slow balanced ordering; setting $u_\parallel=0$ at only one instant would not suffice, because vertical motion could subsequently develop.

The word “exactly” applies to the reduced model. It is not a theorem for every finite-[Rossby number](../../../../rossby-number.md) solution satisfying only $\Omega\gg kU$. For example, the full rotating [Euler equation](../../../../euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid-split.md) admits

$$
\mathbf u=A\cos(kz)(\cos2\Omega t,-\sin2\Omega t,0),\qquad p=\text{constant}.
$$

It is an [incompressible flow](../../../../incompressible-flow.md), has zero nonlinear advection, and obeys $\partial_t\mathbf u=2\mathbf u\times\boldsymbol\Omega$. It is purely horizontal but depends on $z$, and $\Omega\gg kA$ can hold. This fast inertial oscillation lies outside the slow anisotropic [critical balance](../../../../critical-balance.md) used in part [c](../c.md); it shows why that ordering cannot be dropped from the conclusion.
