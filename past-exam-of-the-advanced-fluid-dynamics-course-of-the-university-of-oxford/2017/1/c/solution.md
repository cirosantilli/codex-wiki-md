<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [linear force-free magnetic field](../../../../linear-force-free-magnetic-field.md) has a spatially constant proportionality factor, $\nabla\times\mathbf B=\lambda\mathbf B$. Taking its [curl](../../../../curl.md) and using $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$ gives

$$
-\nabla^2\mathbf B
=\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf B)
=\lambda\nabla\times\mathbf B=\lambda^2\mathbf B.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{\nabla^2\mathbf B+\lambda^2\mathbf B=0.}
$$

The constant $\lambda$ has dimensions of inverse length in this convention. If it varied in space, the extra term $\nabla\lambda\times\mathbf B$ would remain, so the same [Helmholtz equation](../../../../helmholtz-equation.md) would not follow merely from a [nonlinear force-free condition](../../../../force-free-magnetic-field.md).
