# Internal energy

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_energy)

Internal energy $U$ is the [energy](energy-split.md) stored in the microscopic [degrees of freedom](degree-of-freedom.md) of a [thermodynamic system](thermodynamic-system.md): random molecular [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md), internal molecular excitations, and interaction [potential energy](potential-energy.md). Its value requires a specified system and an [energy](energy-split.md) reference. In [thermodynamic equilibrium](thermodynamic-equilibrium.md), [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md) is a [state function](state-function.md); it records the present state, without remembering whether a previous change came from stirring, compression or [heat](heat-split.md) transfer. This makes [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md) the common accounting quantity connecting mechanical experiments to thermal ones.[§3.2, “Internal Energy”, equation 3.5 and the discussion of molecular motion](university-physics-volume-2-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others/3-2-work-heat-and-internal-energy.md)

[Heat](heat-split.md) and [work](work.md) describe [energy](energy-split.md) crossing the system boundary during a process. They are not separately stored contents of the system. For a [closed system](closed-system.md) whose bulk [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and external [potential energy](potential-energy.md) have unchanged endpoint values, the [first law of thermodynamics](first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) gives

$$
\Delta U=Q_{\rm in}+W_{\rm on}=Q_{\rm in}-W_{\rm out}.
$$

Here $Q_{\rm in}$ is positive for incoming [heat](heat-split.md), and $W_{\rm on}=-W_{\rm out}$ is positive for net [work](work.md) done on the system. Two routes between the same equilibrium states have the same $\Delta U$, although their [heat](heat-split.md) and [work](work.md) can differ. The infinitesimal notation $dU=\delta Q_{\rm in}+\delta W_{\rm on}$ distinguishes a [state function](state-function.md) change from path-dependent transfers.[§4.2, equation 4.156 and “Quasi-Static Processes”: state energy versus heat and work; Tong uses work positive inward](statistical-physics-by-david-tong/4-classical-thermodynamics.md)

For a calculated example, take one [mole](mole-unit.md) of a classical [monatomic gas](monatomic-gas.md) in the [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) regime, at fixed [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md), from $300$ to $310\,\mathrm K$. With stipulated [molar gas constant](molar-gas-constant.md) $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$, its [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md) change is $3nR\Delta T/2=124.71\,\mathrm J$. A purely thermal route supplies $Q_{\rm in}=124.71\,\mathrm J$ with $W_{\rm on}=0$. An ideal insulated stirring route supplies $W_{\rm on}=124.71\,\mathrm J$ with $Q_{\rm in}=0$, measured between equilibrium endpoints after the motion dies away. The same stored increment has two different transfer histories; neither history defines a separate kind of stored [energy](energy-split.md).

The two routes end at the same [temperature](temperature-split.md) and [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md). Microscopic [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and interaction [potential energy](potential-energy.md) belong inside $U$; whole-body motion and external elevation are accounted for separately under the stated convention.

The familiar [internal energy of an ideal gas](internal-energy-of-an-ideal-gas.md) is a useful special case. Neglect intermolecular interactions, hold the [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) $n$ and composition fixed, and suppose that $f$ independent quadratic terms per particle obey the classical [equipartition theorem](equipartition-theorem.md). Choosing the constant reference contribution as zero gives

$$
U=\frac f2nRT,\qquad C_V=\frac f2nR.
$$

Here $T$ is absolute [temperature](temperature-split.md), $R$ is the [molar gas constant](molar-gas-constant.md), and $C_V$ is the whole sample's [heat capacity at constant volume](heat-capacity-at-constant-volume.md). A [monatomic gas](monatomic-gas.md) has $f=3$ translational terms. A rigid diatomic [molecule](molecule-split.md) has $f=5$ when its two rotational terms are active and vibration is frozen; activating one [harmonic oscillator](simple-harmonic-motion.md) vibration adds two terms, one [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and one [potential energy](potential-energy.md), giving $f=7$. The counting is of quadratic contributions, so a vibrational coordinate is not just one contribution.[§§2.2.1 and 2.4, equations 2.57–2.58 and the rotation/vibration energy counting](statistical-physics-by-david-tong/2-classical-gases.md)

This constant-$f$ formula applies only over a regime with those same active terms, classical translational statistics, nonrelativistic motion and unchanged composition. Quantum freezing, [quantum degeneracy](degenerate-energy-levels.md), [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction-split.md) or appreciable intermolecular interactions invalidate that simple counting. More generally, at fixed amount and composition an [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) can still have $U=U(T)$ with varying [heat capacity at constant volume](heat-capacity-at-constant-volume.md), so use $\Delta U=\int_{T_1}^{T_2}C_V(T)\,dT$. A nonzero constant reference adds $U_*$ to $fnRT/2$ without altering $\Delta U$.
