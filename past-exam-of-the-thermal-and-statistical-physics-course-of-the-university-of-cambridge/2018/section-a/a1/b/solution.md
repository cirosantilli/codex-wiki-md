<h1 id="section-a/a1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a [phonon](../../../../../phonon.md) mode of angular frequency $\omega$, the equilibrium occupation is

$$
n_\omega=\frac1{e^{\hbar\omega/(k_BT)}-1}.
$$

Well above the [Debye temperature](../../../../../debye-temperature.md), the relevant vibrational [energies](../../../../../energy-split.md) are small compared with $k_BT$. Expanding the exponential gives

$$
n_\omega\simeq\frac{k_BT}{\hbar\omega},\qquad
N_{\mathrm{ph}}=\sum_{\mathbf q,s}n_{\mathbf q,s}
\simeq\frac{k_BT}{\hbar}\sum_{\mathbf q,s}\frac1{\omega_{\mathbf q,s}}.
$$

The spectrum and its cutoff are fixed in the model, so the remaining sum is [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) independent. Thus

$$
\boxed{N_{\mathrm{ph}}\propto T,\qquad\rho\propto T\quad(T\gg\Theta_D).}
$$

For example, the three-dimensional [Debye model](../../../../../debye-model.md) with $D(\omega)=9N\omega^2/\omega_D^3$ gives $N_{\mathrm{ph}}\simeq(9/2)NT/\Theta_D$. This is the [high-temperature phonon number in the Debye model](../../../../../high-temperature-phonon-number-in-the-debye-model.md). The number of [phonon](../../../../../phonon.md) quanta can exceed $3N$; $3N$ counts modes, not their total occupation. Under the stipulated proportionality to [phonon](../../../../../phonon.md) number, only the [phonon](../../../../../phonon.md) contribution to resistivity has been calculated. A temperature-independent [residual resistivity](../../../../../residual-resistivity.md) would add an intercept in a real sample.
