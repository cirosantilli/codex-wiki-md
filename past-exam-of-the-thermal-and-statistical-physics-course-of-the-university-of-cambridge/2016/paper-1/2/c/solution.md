<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**[Landau theory](../../../../../landau-theory.md) expands the [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) in a small [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) and keeps terms allowed by the system's symmetries.** For a scalar [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) $q$ with [sign-reversal symmetry in Landau theory](../../../../../sign-reversal-symmetry-in-landau-theory.md) $q\mapsto-q$, a useful free-energy [density](../../../../../density.md) is

$$
f(q,T,h)=f_0(T)+\frac a2(T-T_c)q^2+\frac b4q^4-hq,\qquad a,b>0.
$$

At zero conjugate field $h$, the [quartic Landau equilibrium branches](../../../../../quartic-landau-equilibrium-branches.md) obtained by minimizing are

$$
\boxed{q_{\rm eq}=0\quad(T>T_c),\qquad q_{\rm eq}=\pm\sqrt{\frac{a(T_c-T)}b}\quad(T<T_c).}
$$

The single minimum becomes two symmetry-related minima: the [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) turns on continuously and a chosen equilibrium phase spontaneously breaks the symmetry. At the [critical temperature](../../../../../critical-temperature.md) the quadratic restoring term vanishes. The mean-field exponent is $q\propto(T_c-T)^{1/2}$; the susceptibility above $T_c$ is $\partial q/\partial h=1/[a(T-T_c)]$.

More general allowed terms describe other transitions. A negative quartic term, stabilized by a positive sixth-order term, can give a first-order transition with a discontinuous [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) and metastable minima. Without the sign-reversal symmetry, a cubic term may also be allowed. The expansion is phenomenological: its coefficients depend on microscopic physics. Minimizing a uniform polynomial neglects important spatial fluctuations, particularly near a [critical point](../../../../../critical-point.md), so its [critical exponents](../../../../../critical-exponent.md) need not be exact.[Section 5.4: Landau free energy, symmetry and mean-field transitions](../../../../../phase-transitions-by-david-tong.md)
