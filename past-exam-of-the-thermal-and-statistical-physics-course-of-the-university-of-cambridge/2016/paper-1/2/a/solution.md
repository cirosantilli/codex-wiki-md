An **[statistical ensemble](../../../../../statistical-ensemble.md) specifies probabilities of microstates under particular macroscopic constraints**. It represents statistical information about a system; the [reservoir constraints of equilibrium ensembles](../../../../../reservoir-constraints-of-equilibrium-ensembles.md) specify what it can exchange with its surroundings.

- The [microcanonical ensemble](../../../../../microcanonical-ensemble.md) has fixed $E,V,N$. Accessible states in a narrow [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) shell are equally probable, with $S=k_B\ln\Omega$, where $\Omega$ counts the states in that shell. It describes an isolated system.
- The [canonical ensemble](../../../../../canonical-ensemble-split.md) has fixed $T,V,N$ and can exchange [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) with a heat reservoir. A microstate has probability $p_i=e^{-\beta E_i}/Z$, with $Z=\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i}$ and $F=-k_BT\ln Z$. The [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) fluctuates; its mean is not imposed independently.
- The [grand canonical ensemble](../../../../../grand-canonical-ensemble.md) has fixed $T,V,\mu_{\rm ch}$ and exchanges both particles and [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). Its probabilities are proportional to $e^{-\beta(E_i-\mu_{\rm ch}N_i)}$, with $\Xi=\sum_N e^{\beta\mu_{\rm ch}N}Z_N$ and [grand potential](../../../../../grand-potential.md) $\Omega_G=-k_BT\ln\Xi$. For a homogeneous extensive equilibrium system, $\Omega_G=-pV$.

The [thermodynamic potentials](../../../../../thermodynamic-potential-split.md) generate mean quantities and response functions. For example, canonical [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) variance is $\langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle=k_BT^2C_V$. With short-range interactions and stable bulk phases, relative fluctuations usually vanish for large systems and these ensembles give equivalent bulk predictions after matching their mean variables. Finite systems, constraints and long-range interactions require greater care; the ensembles retain different fluctuating variables.[Sections 1.2–1.4: microcanonical, canonical and grand canonical ensembles](../../../../../the-fundamentals-of-statistical-mechanics-by-david-tong.md)
