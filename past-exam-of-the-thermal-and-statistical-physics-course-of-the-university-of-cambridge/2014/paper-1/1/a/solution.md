<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For one oscillator, set $x=\hbar\Omega/(k_BT)$. Its [quantum-oscillator energy and heat capacity](../../../../../quantum-oscillator-energy-and-heat-capacity.md) are

$$
\boxed{U=\hbar\Omega\left(\frac12+\frac1{e^x-1}\right),\qquad
C_V=k_B\frac{x^2e^x}{(e^x-1)^2}.}
$$

At low [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), $U\to\hbar\Omega/2$ and $C_V\sim k_Bx^2e^{-x}\to0$: excitations freeze out but [zero-point energy](../../../../../zero-point-energy.md) remains. At high [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), $U\sim k_BT$ and $C_V\to k_B$, recovering [equipartition](../../../../../equipartition-theorem.md). The zero-point term does not contribute to [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md).
