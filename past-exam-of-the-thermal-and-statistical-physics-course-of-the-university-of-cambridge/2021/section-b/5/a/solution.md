A [phase transition](../../../../../phase-transition.md) is a qualitative change of [thermodynamic phase](../../../../../thermodynamic-phase.md) as control parameters such as [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) or [chemical potential](../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) are varied. A genuine equilibrium singularity is a nonanalyticity of the appropriate [thermodynamic potential](../../../../../thermodynamic-potential-split.md) in the [thermodynamic limit](../../../../../thermodynamic-limit.md); finite systems generally show rounded crossovers instead.

An [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) is a macroscopic quantity that distinguishes the phases. It often vanishes in a symmetric disordered phase and becomes nonzero after [spontaneous symmetry breaking](../../../../../spontaneous-symmetry-breaking.md), although density-driven transitions need not have that simple symmetry interpretation. It can be a scalar, [vector](../../../../../vector.md) or [tensor](../../../../../tensor.md). At a [first-order phase transition](../../../../../first-order-phase-transition.md) an appropriate first [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) of the free [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is discontinuous, and the [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) commonly jumps; at a [continuous phase transition](../../../../../continuous-phase-transition.md) the [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) can approach zero continuously while a susceptibility or a higher [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) becomes singular.

Three examples are:

- A [ferromagnet](../../../../../ferromagnet.md) below its [Curie temperature](../../../../../curie-temperature.md) has a nonzero spontaneous [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) $\mathbf M$ in zero applied [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md). Above the transition, the equilibrium [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) vanishes in zero field. The direction of $\mathbf M$ selects a broken-symmetry state.
- Near a [liquid-gas critical point](../../../../../liquid-gas-critical-point.md), the density contrast $\rho_{\rm liquid}-\rho_{\rm gas}$ distinguishes the coexisting liquid and gas. It tends to zero at the critical point; below that point, changing the conjugate [chemical potential](../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) through coexistence switches between low- and high-density phases.
- At an isotropic-to-nematic transition of a [liquid crystal](../../../../../liquid-crystal.md), the [nematic order parameter](../../../../../nematic-order-parameter.md) is a symmetric traceless [tensor](../../../../../tensor.md), for example $Q_{ij}=\langle u_i u_j-\delta_{ij}/3\rangle$, where $\mathbf u$ is a molecular orientation. It vanishes in the [isotropic phase](../../../../../isotropic-phase.md) and is nonzero when the [molecules](../../../../../molecule-split.md) preferentially align in a [nematic liquid crystal](../../../../../nematic-liquid-crystal.md).

These examples also illustrate why an [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) must reflect the physical distinction between phases rather than merely be any quantity that changes with [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md).
