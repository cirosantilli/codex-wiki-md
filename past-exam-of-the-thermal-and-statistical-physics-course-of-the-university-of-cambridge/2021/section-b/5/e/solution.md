<h1 id="section-b/5/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For attractive nearest-neighbour interactions, $J>0$, the transformed model is a ferromagnetic [Ising model](../../../../../ising-model.md). In three dimensions and at $T<T_c$, its infinite-volume [spontaneous magnetization](../../../../../spontaneous-magnetization.md) has two branches. As the effective field crosses zero, the equilibrium mean spin changes discontinuously from a negative value to a positive value. Since $(\partial h/\partial\mu)_T=1/2$, varying the physical [chemical potential](../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) crosses this same [first-order phase transition](../../../../../first-order-phase-transition.md).

The particle-hole-symmetric coexistence line is

$$
\boxed{h=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mu_{\rm coex}(T)=-2qJ-k_BT\ln\frac b{\lambda^3}.}
$$

The mean Ising spin is a rescaled occupation density. Writing $n=\langle N\rangle/V$ for the physical [number density](../../../../../number-density.md),

$$
\boxed{m_{\rm I}=\frac1M\sum_\alpha\langle\sigma_\alpha\rangle
=2\frac{\langle N\rangle}{M}-1=2bn-1.}
$$

The negative-spin phase is a dilute gas and the positive-spin phase is a dense, liquid-like phase. If the spontaneous spin magnitude at coexistence is $m_0(T)>0$, their number densities are

$$
n_{\rm gas}=\frac{1-m_0(T)}{2b},\qquad n_{\rm liquid}=\frac{1+m_0(T)}{2b},\qquad
n_{\rm liquid}-n_{\rm gas}=\frac{m_0(T)}b.
$$

Thus the physical [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) is a density or occupation imbalance, not an actual magnetic [moment](../../../../../moment.md) of these [particles](../../../../../particle-physics.md). In a closed system with an intermediate mean density, the same transition appears as [phase coexistence](../../../../../phase-coexistence.md) between the two densities. At $T=T_c$ the density contrast vanishes continuously; above $T_c$, sweeping the [chemical potential](../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) gives no coexistence jump. A true discontinuity requires the [thermodynamic limit](../../../../../thermodynamic-limit.md); a finite sum over finitely many cells remains analytic.
