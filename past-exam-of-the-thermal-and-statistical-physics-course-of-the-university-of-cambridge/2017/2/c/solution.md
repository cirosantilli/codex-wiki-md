**Quanta of collective motion.** An elementary excitation is a low-[energy](../../../../energy-split.md) departure from the many-body [ground state](../../../../ground-state.md) that can often be treated as a [quasiparticle](../../../../quasiparticle.md). Near an ordered equilibrium, small collective motions form [normal modes](../../../../normal-mode.md). Quantization turns a harmonic mode of frequency $\omega_{\mathbf k}$ into bosonic excitations with [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) $\hbar\omega_{\mathbf k}$. Their number is not an independently conserved thermodynamic variable in equilibrium, so their [chemical potential](../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) is zero and

$$
\bar n_{\mathbf k}=\frac1{e^{\hbar\omega_{\mathbf k}/(k_BT)}-1},\qquad
\boxed{U-U_0=\sum_{\mathbf k,\nu}\hbar\omega_{\mathbf k,\nu}\bar n_{\mathbf k,\nu}.}
$$

Here $\nu$ labels branches, and $U_0$ contains the ground-state contribution, including [phonon](../../../../phonon.md) zero-point [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) where appropriate. The approximation is useful while interactions and damping of the excitations are weak enough that the modes remain identifiable.

**Phonons.** A [phonon](../../../../phonon.md) is a quantum of an elastic lattice-vibration mode. Long-wavelength [acoustic phonon branches](../../../../acoustic-phonon-branch.md) have $\omega\simeq c_\nu k$, with one longitudinal and two transverse polarizations in a three-dimensional monatomic solid. A multi-atom primitive cell can also support [optical phonon branches](../../../../optical-phonon-branch.md), generally with nonzero frequency at $k=0$. Linear acoustic dispersion gives $D(\epsilon)\propto\epsilon^2$, so the [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) integral has $U-U_0\propto T^4$ and the [heat capacity](../../../../heat-capacity.md) obeys the Debye $T^3$ law. In the [Debye model](../../../../debye-model.md),

$$
\boxed{C_V\simeq\frac{12\pi^4}{5}Nk_B
\left(\frac T{\Theta_D}\right)^3\quad(T\ll\Theta_D).}
$$

At temperatures high compared with all [phonon](../../../../phonon.md) energies but below structural changes, the classical harmonic limit gives approximately $C_V=3Nk_B$. Optical modes are frozen out at temperatures well below their gaps.

**Spin waves.** A [spin wave](../../../../spin-wave.md) is a spatially varying collective precession of ordered spins; its quantum is a [magnon](../../../../magnon.md). In a simple isotropic [ferromagnet](../../../../ferromagnet.md), a long-wavelength gapless branch has [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) $\epsilon_{\mathbf k}\simeq D_s k^2$, where the spin stiffness $D_s$ has units $\mathrm{J\,m^2}$. State counting gives $D(\epsilon)\propto\epsilon^{1/2}$. For one isotropic branch in three dimensions,

$$
\frac{U-U_0}{V}=\frac{\Gamma(5/2)\zeta(5/2)}{4\pi^2D_s^{3/2}}(k_BT)^{5/2},
\qquad\boxed{C_V^{\rm magnon}\propto T^{3/2}.}
$$

This exponent is specific to a gapless quadratic ferromagnetic branch. Long-wavelength [antiferromagnetic spin waves](../../../../antiferromagnetic-spin-wave.md) can be linear and instead give a $T^3$ heat-capacity law in three dimensions. Magnetic anisotropy or an applied field can open a gap, causing activated behaviour at still lower temperatures. Near a magnetic ordering transition, a dilute noninteracting spin-wave description is no longer adequate.

These examples illustrate the [low-temperature energy of gapless bosonic excitations](../../../../low-temperature-energy-of-gapless-bosonic-excitations.md): for $\epsilon\propto k^s$ in $d$ dimensions, $D(\epsilon)\propto\epsilon^{d/s-1}$, hence $U-U_0\propto T^{1+d/s}$ and $C_V\propto T^{d/s}$. The exponent records both the spatial dimension and the dispersion, making [calorimetry](../../../../calorimetry-split.md) a probe of elementary excitations.
