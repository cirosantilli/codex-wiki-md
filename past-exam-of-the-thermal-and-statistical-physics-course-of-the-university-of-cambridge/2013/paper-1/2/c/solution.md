<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

An [equation of state](../../../../../equation-of-state.md) relates equilibrium pressure, [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and [density](../../../../../density.md). The ideal-gas form $p=nk_BT$ neglects intermolecular [forces](../../../../../force-split.md) and excluded [volume](../../../../../volume.md). A low-density real-gas expansion is

$$
\frac p{nk_BT}=1+B_2(T)n+B_3(T)n^2+\cdots,
$$

where the virial coefficients encode interaction effects and generally depend on [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md).

A simple phenomenological example is the molar [Van der Waals equation](../../../../../van-der-waals-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{p=\frac{RT}{v-b}-\frac a{v^2}.}
$$

Here v is molar [volume](../../../../../volume.md), b represents excluded [volume](../../../../../volume.md) and the attractive interaction lowers pressure through a. Its dilute expansion gives $pv/(RT)=1+[b-a/(RT)]/v+\cdots$. It recovers the ideal-gas limit but is not a quantitatively universal equation for all [real gases](../../../../../real-gas.md).

Below its [critical temperature](../../../../../critical-temperature.md), the raw isotherm contains a region with $(\partial p/\partial v)_T>0$, corresponding to negative [isothermal compressibility](../../../../../isothermal-compressibility.md) and mechanical instability. Equilibrium [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) separation replaces that segment by a coexistence pressure satisfying equal [chemical potentials](../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md), equivalently the [Maxwell equal-area construction](../../../../../maxwell-construction.md). The [critical point](../../../../../critical-point.md) follows from $p_v=p_{vv}=0$:

$$
\boxed{v_c=3b,\qquad T_c=\frac{8a}{27Rb},\qquad p_c=\frac{a}{27b^2}.}
$$

The distinction between an unstable homogeneous branch and a stable two-phase state is essential when interpreting a real-gas [equation of state](../../../../../equation-of-state.md).
