<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A black body absorbs incident electromagnetic radiation and, in equilibrium, emits a spectrum determined by [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) alone. A cavity with a small aperture approximates this ideal. [Photon](../../../../../photon.md) number is not conserved in [thermal equilibrium](../../../../../thermal-equilibrium-split.md), so its [chemical potential](../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) is zero. Combining the Bose occupation with the two-polarization electromagnetic [mode](../../../../../mode-statistics.md) [density](../../../../../density.md) gives the spectral [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) [density](../../../../../density.md)

$$
u_\omega=\frac{\hbar\omega^3}{\pi^2c^3}\frac1{e^{\hbar\omega/(k_BT)}-1}.
$$

Changing variable to $x=\hbar\omega/(k_BT)$ shows directly why the total [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) [density](../../../../../density.md) is proportional to $T^4$:

$$
u(T)=\frac{(k_BT)^4}{\pi^2\hbar^3c^3}\int_0^\infty\frac{x^3\,dx}{e^x-1}
=\frac{\pi^2k_B^4}{15\hbar^3c^3}T^4.
$$

Three [powers](../../../../../power.md) of T arise from the number of thermally populated [modes](../../../../../mode-statistics.md) and one from their characteristic [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). Isotropic radiation incident on a surface has outward hemispherical [flux](../../../../../flux.md) $cu/4$, giving

$$
\boxed{j^\star=\sigma_{\rm SB}T^4,\qquad
\sigma_{\rm SB}=\frac{\pi^2k_B^4}{60\hbar^3c^2}.}
$$

The factor 1/4 is the hemispherical angular average; multiplying [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) [density](../../../../../density.md) by c alone would overcount. The spectrum's [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) scale grows with T. Classical [equipartition](../../../../../equipartition-theorem.md) over all [modes](../../../../../mode-statistics.md) would instead produce the Rayleigh-Jeans ultraviolet divergence; the Planck occupation suppresses high-frequency [modes](../../../../../mode-statistics.md). Real materials can have wavelength-dependent [emissivity](../../../../../emissivity.md), and a body's net radiative exchange also includes absorbed environmental radiation.
