<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use a three-dimensional [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) at the same [number density](../../../../../number-density.md) n for both [isotopes](../../../../../isotope.md). An unpolarized [helium-3](../../../../../helium-3.md) gas has [spin degeneracy](../../../../../spin-degeneracy.md) two, hence

$$
k_BT_F=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_3}(3\pi^2n)^{2/3}.
$$

[Helium-4](../../../../../helium-4.md) has a single [spin](../../../../../spin.md) state, and the ideal Bose-gas saturation condition gives

$$
k_BT_B=\frac{2\pi\hbar^2}{m_4}\left[\frac{n}{\zeta(3/2)}\right]^{2/3}.
$$

Eliminating n, using $m_3/m_4\simeq3/4$ and $\zeta(3/2)=2.612$, yields

$$
\boxed{\frac{T_B}{T_F}=\frac{4\pi}{(3\pi^2)^{2/3}\zeta(3/2)^{2/3}}\frac{m_3}{m_4}
\simeq0.519,\qquad T_B\simeq2.6\,\mathrm K.}
$$

The fermionic [spin degeneracy](../../../../../spin-degeneracy.md) must be included. This is an ideal-gas estimate; strong interactions in liquid helium prevent identifying it with an exact experimental transition [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md).
