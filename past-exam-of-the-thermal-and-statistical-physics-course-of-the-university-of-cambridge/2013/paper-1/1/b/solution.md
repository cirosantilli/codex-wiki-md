<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $\Delta=\hbar\Omega$ and choose the nondegenerate [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) levels as 0 and $\Delta$. For N [independent](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) identical units, $Z=(1+e^{-\beta\Delta})^N$; set N=1 for a single [two-level system](../../../../../two-level-system.md). Differentiating its logarithm gives

$$
\boxed{U=\frac{N\Delta}{1+e^{\Delta/(k_BT)}},\qquad
C_V=Nk_B\frac{x^2e^x}{(1+e^x)^2},\quad x=\frac\Delta{k_BT}.}
$$

At low [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), $U\sim N\Delta e^{-x}$ and $C_V\sim Nk_Bx^2e^{-x}$: the upper level is frozen out. At high [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), the two levels approach equal populations, giving $U\to N\Delta/2$ while $C_V\sim Nk_Bx^2/4\to0$. The [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) therefore rises monotonically to a plateau, while the [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md) has a Schottky peak:

$$
\boxed{k_BT_{\rm peak}\simeq0.417\Delta,\qquad C_{V,\rm peak}\simeq0.439Nk_B.}
$$

Unequal degeneracies would change the populations and peak; the usual equal-degeneracy model is stated here. Shifting both [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) levels changes the [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) baseline but not [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md).
