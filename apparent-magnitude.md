# Apparent magnitude

↑ **Parent:** [Astronomical magnitude](astronomical-magnitude.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparent_magnitude)

Apparent magnitude $m$ is a calibrated description of the [observed radiative flux](observed-radiative-flux.md) received from an astronomical source. It answers how bright the source appears in a specified [photometric passband](photometric-passband.md), rather than how much [luminosity](luminosity.md) the source emits. Define $F_{\mathrm{ref}}>0$ as the measurement assigned zero [apparent magnitude](apparent-magnitude.md) by the chosen [magnitude zero point](magnitude-zero-point.md). Then

$$
m=-2.5\log_{10}\left(\frac{F}{F_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right).
$$

Here $F$ is the positive passband measurement, with the same units and response convention as $F_{\mathrm{ref}}$. For an [energy](energy.md)-weighted detector this measures received [radiative flux](radiative-flux.md) through its response; for a [photon](photon.md)-counting detector use the corresponding calibrated count rate for both quantities. Actual [photometric passbands](photometric-passband.md) include the instrument's response across [wavelength](wavelength.md), so naming the [photometric system](photometric-system.md) is part of reporting an [apparent magnitude](apparent-magnitude.md). [ESA/Gaia/DPAC: Passbands and zero points](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/edr3-passbands). [ESA XMM-Newton calibration handbook: Count rate to magnitude](https://xmm-tools.cosmos.esa.int/external/xmm_calibration/calib/documentation/CALHB/node597.html).

As toy examples in one [photometric passband](photometric-passband.md), $F=F_{\mathrm{ref}}$ gives $m=0$, $F=100F_{\mathrm{ref}}$ gives $m=-5$, and $F=0.01F_{\mathrm{ref}}$ gives $m=+5$. A negative [apparent magnitude](apparent-magnitude.md) therefore means more received [radiative flux](radiative-flux.md) than the reference; it is physically allowed. Zero [apparent magnitude](apparent-magnitude.md) does not mean zero [radiative flux](radiative-flux.md). The ordinary [logarithm](logarithm.md) formula requires $F>0$.

A small [apparent magnitude](apparent-magnitude.md) alone cannot distinguish a nearby faint source from a distant source with large [luminosity](luminosity.md): this is the [flux-distance degeneracy](flux-distance-degeneracy.md). [Stellar parallax](stellar-parallax.md) supplies independent distance information, while a calibrated [standard candle](standard-candle.md) supplies intrinsic brightness. [Interstellar extinction](interstellar-extinction.md) also changes the received [radiative flux](radiative-flux.md) and hence the measured [apparent magnitude](apparent-magnitude.md).
