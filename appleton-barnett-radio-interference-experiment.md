# Appleton-Barnett radio interference experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Ionosphere](ionosphere.md)

On 11 December 1924, [Edward Victor Appleton](edward-victor-appleton.md) and [Miles Aylmer Fulton Barnett](miles-aylmer-fulton-barnett.md) perform an experiment: the Bournemouth broadcasting [radio transmitter](radio-transmitter.md) slowly changes [wavelength](wavelength.md); an Oxford [radio receiver](radio-receiver.md) records alternating maxima and minima from [ground wave](ground-wave.md)-[skywave](skywave.md) [wave interference](interference-wave-propagation.md). The inferred equivalent height is about $90\,\mathrm{km}$. A separate comparison of simultaneous loop and vertical [antenna](antenna-radio.md) signals tests the downward arrival direction. The [E layer](e-layer.md) is established first; the higher [F layer](f-layer.md) is found in winter 1926–27. These dates describe different findings.[Edward V. Appleton, The ionosphere, Nobel lecture, 12 December 1947, printed pp. 80–81, Fig. 1, Eq. (1), and the wavelength-change and antenna-comparison paragraphs](the-ionosphere-by-edward-victor-appleton.md)

Why change the [wavelength](wavelength.md) deliberately? Ordinary fading alone could arise from changing emission, receiver drift or a moving propagation path. A controlled sweep predicts a specific progression of [phase difference](phase-difference.md) between two arrivals. The directional [antenna](antenna-radio.md) comparison supplies an independent kind of evidence for an overhead contribution. Agreement supports an atmospheric return; fitting a distance alone would not establish a unique [electron density](electron-density.md) profile. In the model below, comparable received amplitudes make the [interference fringes](interference-fringe.md) easier to see. A practical repetition would also calibrate the [frequency](frequency.md) sweep, track [radio transmitter](radio-transmitter.md) output and receiver gain, and compare repeated sweeps with fixed-[frequency](frequency.md) intervals to detect time drift. These are controls inferred from the measurement model, rather than additional procedures attributed to the historical account.

The [radio receiver](radio-receiver.md) does not need to resolve two pulses to detect two paths. If one path takes longer, the two contributions reaching the receiver during a slow [frequency](frequency.md) sweep carry slightly different emission [frequencies](frequency.md) and can produce a slow [beat](beat.md) in the detected amplitude. At strictly fixed [frequency](frequency.md) with stationary paths, the arrivals instead have a fixed [phase difference](phase-difference.md): there is no continuing [beat](beat.md) merely because one path is longer. This distinction connects the historical observation to [ionospheric interference fringe spacing](ionospheric-interference-fringe-spacing.md).
