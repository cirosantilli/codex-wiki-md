# Escape speed of a truncated flat-rotation halo

↑ **Parent:** [Spherical mass profile for a flat rotation curve](spherical-mass-profile-for-a-flat-rotation-curve.md)

A spherical halo with flat circular [speed](speed.md) $v_c$ has $M(r)=v_c^2r/G$ and $\rho=v_c^2/(4\pi Gr^2)$. If it is sharply truncated at $r_*$, the work per unit mass from $r<r_*$ to the boundary is $v_c^2\log(r_*/r)$ and from the boundary to infinity is $v_c^2$. Adding them gives the displayed [escape velocity](escape-velocity.md). A bound star's observed radial speed is only a lower bound on its total speed and on the escape speed, so one maximum radial measurement generally gives a lower bound on $r_*$ rather than an exact halo edge.
