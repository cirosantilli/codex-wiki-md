# Methods of detecting exoplanets

↑ **Parent:** [Exoplanet](exoplanet-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methods_of_detecting_exoplanets)

[Methods of detecting exoplanets](methods-of-detecting-exoplanets.md) infer or resolve an [exoplanet](exoplanet-split.md) through its effects on the host [star](star.md)'s light or through its own light. The observable determines what can be learned:

- [Doppler spectroscopy](doppler-spectroscopy.md) measures changes in a [star](star.md)'s line-of-sight [velocity](velocity.md) from its [spectral lines](spectral-line.md), constraining a companion's [mass](mass.md), [orbital period](orbital-period.md), and [orbital eccentricity](orbital-eccentricity.md), with an [orbital inclination](orbital-inclination.md) ambiguity.
- [Exoplanet transit photometry](exoplanet-transit-photometry.md) measures a repeating dip in the [star](star.md)'s [observed radiative flux](observed-radiative-flux.md), constraining the [exoplanet](exoplanet-split.md)'s [radius](radius.md) relative to the [star](star.md) and its [orbital period](orbital-period.md). It requires a suitably aligned [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md).
- [Astrometry](astrometry.md) measures a [star](star.md)'s changing position on the sky from [stellar reflex motion](stellar-reflex-motion.md); with adequate orbital coverage it can constrain [orbital inclination](orbital-inclination.md) and the companion's true [mass](mass.md).
- [Gravitational microlensing](gravitational-microlensing.md) detects a companion's perturbation of the temporary brightening of a background [star](star.md). Such alignments are usually not repeatable, and additional information is needed to turn fitted ratios into absolute [masses](mass.md) and separations.
- [Exoplanet direct imaging](exoplanet-direct-imaging.md) spatially separates an [exoplanet](exoplanet-split.md)'s light from its host [star](star.md)'s light, enabling studies of an [exoplanet atmosphere](exoplanet-atmosphere.md) and, over time, its [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md).

An [exoplanet secondary eclipse](exoplanet-secondary-eclipse.md) measures the loss of the [exoplanet](exoplanet-split.md)'s light when it passes behind its [star](star.md), complementing [exoplanet transit photometry](exoplanet-transit-photometry.md). A detection method also selects which systems are easiest to find: deeper [exoplanet transits](exoplanet-transit.md) favour larger [radius](radius.md) ratios, and larger [stellar radial-velocity semi-amplitudes](stellar-radial-velocity-semi-amplitude.md) favour more massive companions, shorter [orbital periods](orbital-period.md), and larger $\sin i$. These preferences must be accounted for when inferring the underlying [exoplanet](exoplanet-split.md) population.
