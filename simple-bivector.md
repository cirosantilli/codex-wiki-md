# Simple bivector

↑ **Parent:** [Bivector](bivector.md)

A [simple bivector](simple-bivector.md) can be expressed as one [exterior product](exterior-product.md) of two [vectors](vector.md). Changing the pair need not change its area element: $u\wedge(v+cu)=u\wedge v$ for every [scalar](scalar.md) $c$, by [bilinearity](bilinearity.md) and $u\wedge u=0$. This operation shears the represented [parallelogram](parallelogram.md) while preserving its oriented area.

Every [bivector](bivector.md) in a real vector space of [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) at most three is simple, but this fails in [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) four. For an [ordered basis](ordered-basis.md) $e_1,\ldots,e_4$, consider

$$
B=e_1\wedge e_2+e_3\wedge e_4.
$$

The two components describe independent coordinate planes. Their square is

$$
B\wedge B=2e_1\wedge e_2\wedge e_3\wedge e_4\ne0,
$$

whereas $(u\wedge v)\wedge(u\wedge v)=0$ because the factors repeat. Thus this $B$ cannot be one [simple bivector](simple-bivector.md). Over the [real numbers](real-number.md) in [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) four, the converse also holds: a nonzero [bivector](bivector.md) with $B\wedge B=0$ is simple. The nonzero factor two is important; that criterion cannot be applied unchanged in [characteristic two](characteristic-two.md).
