<h1 id="2/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At the interface, the tangential [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) is $u(x,0)=xf'(0)$ and the normal [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) remains zero. The preceding [error function](../../../../../error-function.md) expression gives

$$
g(0)=f_1'(0)=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}4=\sqrt{\frac\pi8}.
$$

Hence, within the leading [boundary layer](../../../../../boundary-layer.md) model,

$$
\boxed{u(x,0)=x\left[1+\sqrt{\frac\pi8}\,\gamma+O(\gamma^2)\right],
\qquad w(x,0)=0.}
$$

Allowing the formally discarded finite-[Reynolds number](../../../../../reynolds-number.md) terms gives an additional order-$\delta^2$ error, smaller than the order-$\gamma$ correction under the assumed ordering. The coefficient is $\sqrt{\pi/8}\approx0.626657$.

Thus **positive surface shear increases the outward surface speed linearly**: for $x>0$ it increases $u$, and for $x<0$ it makes $u$ more negative. The [stagnation point](../../../../../stagnation-point.md) remains at $x=0$. A negative coefficient would result from using the printed $+\infty$ condition literally, and would describe the different half-line problem identified in part (ii).
