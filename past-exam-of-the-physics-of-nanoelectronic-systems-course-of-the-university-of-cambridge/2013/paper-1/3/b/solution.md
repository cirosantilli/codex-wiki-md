[graphene](../../../../../graphene.md) is a two-dimensional honeycomb lattice with two sublattices. Nearest-neighbour hopping gives two bands $E_\pm(\mathbf k)=\pm t|\sum_{j=1}^3e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\delta_j}|$. They touch at two inequivalent valleys. Expanding near valley $\tau=\pm1$ gives

$$
\boxed{H_\tau=\hbar v_F(\tau q_x\sigma_x+q_y\sigma_y),\qquad
E_\pm=\pm\hbar v_F|\mathbf q|.}
$$

The two-component spinor describes sublattice amplitudes, not real [spin](../../../../../spin.md). This is a massless two-dimensional [Dirac Hamiltonian](../../../../../dirac-hamiltonian.md) with material [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) $v_F$ in place of $c$. The emergent band [quasiparticles](../../../../../quasiparticle.md) are relativistic in this mathematical sense; the [electron](../../../../../electron.md)'s vacuum rest mass is not set to zero. Real [spin](../../../../../spin.md) and valley each contribute a degeneracy of two in the ideal low-energy model.

A [single-walled carbon nanotube](../../../../../single-walled-carbon-nanotube.md) is a rolled [graphene](../../../../../graphene.md) sheet with [carbon nanotube chiral vector](../../../../../carbon-nanotube-chiral-vector.md) $\mathbf C_h=n\mathbf a_1+m\mathbf a_2$. Periodicity requires $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf C_h=2\pi\ell$, selecting lines through [graphene](../../../../../graphene.md) momentum space and producing one-dimensional [transverse electronic modes](../../../../../transverse-electronic-mode.md). Near a valley,

$$
\boxed{E_\pm(q_\parallel)=\pm\hbar v_F\sqrt{q_\parallel^2+q_\perp^2}.}
$$

A quantization line through one of the [Dirac valleys in graphene](../../../../../dirac-valleys-in-graphene.md) gives a metallic branch; ideal [nanotube zone folding](../../../../../nanotube-zone-folding.md) gives the criterion $n-m\equiv0\pmod3$. Otherwise the nearest line misses the valley and opens $E_g=2\hbar v_F|q_\perp|$. The transverse momentum gives a [carbon-nanotube Dirac mass](../../../../../carbon-nanotube-dirac-mass.md), with $m_{\rm eff}=\hbar|q_\perp|/v_F$. For a large-radius ideal semiconducting tube, $|q_\perp|\simeq2/(3d)$ and $E_g\simeq4\hbar v_F/(3d)$, showing the inverse-diameter gap. Curvature, strain, [spin-orbit coupling](../../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md) and interactions can change the simple classification; nominally metallic non-armchair tubes can acquire small gaps. One-dimensional [transverse electronic mode](../../../../../transverse-electronic-mode.md) thresholds give [Van Hove singularities](../../../../../van-hove-singularity.md), unlike [graphene](../../../../../graphene.md)'s low-energy [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md) proportional to $|E|$.

A gate changes occupation and the [Fermi level](../../../../../fermi-level.md) in both systems. Ideal monolayer [graphene](../../../../../graphene.md) can be tuned through [charge neutrality](../../../../../charge-neutrality.md) from [holes](../../../../../electron-hole.md) to [electrons](../../../../../electron.md), but an ordinary scalar gate does not open a bulk [band gap](../../../../../band-gap.md). Including [spin](../../../../../spin.md) and valley,

$$
n=\operatorname{sgn}(E_F)\frac{E_F^2}{\pi(\hbar v_F)^2},\qquad
D(E)=\frac{2|E|}{\pi(\hbar v_F)^2}.
$$

The gate relation includes geometric and [quantum capacitances](../../../../../quantum-capacitance.md), so one should not equate gate [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) to [Fermi energy](../../../../../fermi-energy.md) without the [capacitance](../../../../../capacitance.md) model. A [semiconducting carbon nanotube](../../../../../semiconducting-carbon-nanotube.md) can instead be depleted when its [Fermi level](../../../../../fermi-level.md) lies in a gap and turned on in [electron](../../../../../electron.md) or [hole](../../../../../electron-hole.md) bands. Contact [Schottky barriers](../../../../../schottky-barrier.md) can strongly influence the observed nanotube field effect. A truly gapless ideal metallic tube cannot be switched off simply by moving the [Fermi level](../../../../../fermi-level.md) through its band crossing. These gate responses reflect band structure and contacts, not a change of wrapping indices.
