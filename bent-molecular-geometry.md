# Bent molecular geometry

↑ **Parent:** [Molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bent_molecular_geometry)

In [bent molecular geometry](bent-molecular-geometry.md), two neighbours and their central [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) form a noncollinear [triangle](triangle.md), with an interior [bond angle](bond-angle.md) strictly between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$. [Water](water.md), $\mathrm H_2\mathrm O$, is the standard example. The [NIST CCCBDB experimental water entry, internal coordinates, citing Hoy and Bunker (1979)](https://cccbdb.nist.gov/exp2x.asp?casno=7732185&charge=0) gives the equilibrium H–O–H [bond angle](bond-angle.md) as $104.4776^\circ$ with quoted uncertainty $0.0019^\circ$, usually rounded to $104.5^\circ$.

The [oxygen](oxygen.md) in [water](water.md) has two bonding [electron domains](electron-domain.md) and two [lone pairs](lone-pair.md). Its ideal [electron pair geometry](electron-pair-geometry.md) is tetrahedral, while the three [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md) give [bent molecular geometry](bent-molecular-geometry.md). Three points always lie in a plane; this does not make the four-domain scaffold planar. Nor does the word bent by itself determine an [electron domain](electron-domain.md) count: the same atomic shape can arise from other domain arrangements.

In an illustrative model with two equal bond-dipole contributions of magnitude $p$ separated by [bond angle](bond-angle.md) $\theta$, their sum of [vectors](vector.md) points along the bisector and has magnitude $2p\cos(\theta/2)$. At $180^\circ$ it vanishes; at $104.4776^\circ$ it is approximately $1.2247p$. This ratio is a geometric calculation, not a prediction of [water](water.md)'s measured [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md). The entire [electron density](electron-density.md), including contributions represented by [lone pairs](lone-pair.md), matters. [Water](water.md) is a [polar molecule](polar-molecule.md); [chemical polarity](chemical-polarity.md) helps explain its interactions with other [polar molecules](polar-molecule.md). See [McMurry, Organic Chemistry, §2.2: water, lone-pair contributions, and polarity](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/2-2-polar-covalent-bonds-and-dipole-moments).
