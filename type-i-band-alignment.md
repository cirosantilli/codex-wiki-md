# Type-I band alignment

↑ **Parent:** [Heterojunction](heterojunction.md)

A [type-I band alignment](type-i-band-alignment.md) confines [electrons](electron.md) and [electron holes](electron-hole.md) together: its inner [conduction band](conduction-band.md) edge is lower, its [valence band](valence-band.md) edge higher.[§IV, Figs. 6–8, pp. 786–787](nobel-lecture-quasielectric-fields-and-band-offsets-teaching-electrons-new-tricks-by-herbert-kroemer.md)

In the chosen [band offset](band-offset.md) example, the [electron](electron.md) barrier is $0.6$, while the [electron hole](electron-hole.md) barrier is $0.4$. A hole's excitation [energy](energy-split.md) is measured downward from a [valence band](valence-band.md) maximum: reversing that convention would incorrectly make the two barriers appear to repel opposite carriers. An electrostatic shift $-q\phi$ moves both electron [energy band](energy-band.md) edges together; changing the [band gap](band-gap.md) also changes their separation. Thus material composition supplies a control beyond the [electric potential](electric-potential.md) $\phi$ alone.
