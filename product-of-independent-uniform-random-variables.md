# Product of independent uniform random variables

↑ **Parent:** [Continuous uniform distribution](continuous-uniform-distribution.md)

For [independent random variables](independent-random-variables.md) $X,Y$ with the [uniform distribution](continuous-uniform-distribution.md) on $(0,b)$, use the [probability density of a product of random variables](probability-density-of-a-product-of-random-variables.md) and integrate $1/(b^2x)$ over $z/b<x<b$. The displayed [probability density function](probability-density-function.md) is zero off $(0,b^2)$ and has [integral](integral-split.md) one. More generally, for [independent and identically distributed](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) factors $U_j$ with this [uniform distribution](continuous-uniform-distribution.md), $W_n=\prod_{j=1}^nU_j$ satisfies

$$
\mathbb E[W_n^\alpha]=\left(\frac{b^\alpha}{1+\alpha}\right)^n\quad(\alpha>-1),\qquad \mathbb E W_n=(b/2)^n,\qquad\operatorname{Var}(W_n)=(b^2/3)^n-(b^2/4)^n.
$$

The [expectation of a product of independent random variables](expectation-of-a-product-of-independent-random-variables.md) proves these identities directly.
