# Nernst equilibrium potential

↑ **Parent:** [Reversal potential](reversal-potential.md)

The [Nernst equilibrium potential](nernst-equilibrium-potential.md) is the inside-minus-outside [membrane potential](membrane-potential-split.md) at which one [ion](ion.md) species has equal [electrochemical potential](electrochemical-potential.md) on both sides of a [cell membrane](cell-membrane-split.md). At uniform [temperature](temperature-split.md) $T$, using the same reference [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) on both sides, $z\neq0$ is the signed [ion](ion.md) charge number, $R$ the [molar gas constant](molar-gas-constant.md), $F$ the [Faraday constant](faraday-constant.md), and $a$ the dimensionless [thermodynamic activity](thermodynamic-activity.md) of that [ion](ion.md). For an ideal dilute [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md), the activity ratio becomes the [concentration](concentration.md) ratio. See [Purves et al.: the forces that create membrane potentials and the Nernst equation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK11102/).

The short derivation is to set the outside-to-inside [electrochemical potential](electrochemical-potential.md) change from [membrane transport](membrane-transport.md) to zero: $RT\ln(a_{\mathrm{inside}}/a_{\mathrm{outside}})+zFV=0$, and solve for $V$. An open pathway carrying only this species by [passive transport](passive-transport.md) then has this [reversal potential](reversal-potential.md). A balance of several nonzero [ion](ion.md) [electric currents](electric-current.md) does not establish this equilibrium condition for each species.
