# Saponification with limiting hydroxide

↑ **Parent:** [Saponification](saponification.md)

Consider a hypothetical closed, well mixed [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) initially containing $20\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of [ethyl acetate](ethyl-acetate.md), $12\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of [hydroxide](hydroxide.md), and no [acetate ions](acetate-ion.md) or [ethanol](ethanol.md). One millimole is $10^{-3}$ [mole](mole-unit.md). Assume complete conversion as far as the one-to-one [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) allows, with no competing [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction-split.md). This is an ideal [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md) calculation, not a measured yield.

Let the [extent of reaction](extent-of-reaction.md) be $\xi$, expressed in millimoles. The four values of [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md), in the order [ethyl acetate](ethyl-acetate.md), [hydroxide](hydroxide.md), [acetate ion](acetate-ion.md), [ethanol](ethanol.md), are

$$
\bigl(20-\xi,\;12-\xi,\;\xi,\;\xi\bigr)\,\mathrm{mmol},\qquad 0\leq\xi\leq12.
$$

The upper bound follows because both [reactant](reactant.md) quantities must be nonnegative. Thus [hydroxide](hydroxide.md) is the [limiting reagent](limiting-reagent.md), and the ideal endpoint $\xi=12$ leaves

$$
8\,\mathrm{mmol}\ \text{ethyl acetate},\quad
0\,\mathrm{mmol}\ \text{hydroxide},\quad
12\,\mathrm{mmol}\ \text{acetate},\quad
12\,\mathrm{mmol}\ \text{ethanol}.
$$

The [carboxylate](carboxylate.md) formed equals the [hydroxide](hydroxide.md) consumed, while unreacted [ester](ester.md) remains. Adding more [hydroxide](hydroxide.md) raises the [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md) bound; merely waiting longer does not change that bound. To convert these values of [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) into [concentrations](concentration.md), a [volume](volume.md) is also required. The endpoint does not specify the [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) or time needed to approach it.
