# Crossover resonance

↑ **Parent:** [Doppler-free saturation spectroscopy](doppler-free-saturation-spectroscopy.md)

Two allowed optical transitions sharing a lower [quantum state](quantum-state.md) can produce an extra [saturation spectroscopy](doppler-free-saturation-spectroscopy.md) feature even where there is no additional [energy level](energy-level.md) difference. Approximate their [wavenumbers](wavenumber.md) by the same $k$, and let their [angular frequencies](angular-frequency.md) be $\omega_a,\omega_b$. A pump resonant with one transition and an opposed probe resonant with the other satisfy

$$
\omega_L-kv=\omega_a,\qquad \omega_L+kv=\omega_b,
$$

so

$$
\omega_L=\frac{\omega_a+\omega_b}{2},\qquad
v=\frac{\omega_b-\omega_a}{2k}.
$$

Exchanging the two transitions selects the opposite [velocity](velocity.md). Both beams can then modify the same shared population, producing a crossover near the midpoint. This requires the relevant [velocities](velocity.md) to be occupied and the transitions to be connected by the population dynamics; not every pair must produce a visible feature. Strength and sign depend on [optical pumping](optical-pumping.md) and the multilevel structure. Assigning every narrow feature to a separate atomic transition would therefore give false [energy levels](energy-level.md).
