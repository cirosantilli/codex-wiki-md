# Forced-sphere rotation reflected from a held sphere

↑ **Parent:** [Method of reflections for Stokes flow](method-of-reflections-for-stokes-flow.md)

For equal distant spheres, a force $\mathbf F$ on the second generates a [Stokeslet](stokeslet.md) velocity $(I+\mathbf n\mathbf n)\mathbf F/(8\pi\mu R)$ at the held first sphere. The first sphere needs holding force $-3a(I+\mathbf n\mathbf n)\mathbf F/(4R)$ by [Stokes drag law](stokes-s-law.md). Its reflected Stokeslet, combined with [Faxén rotation law](faxen-s-rotational-law.md), gives the displayed spin on the second. This translation constraint produces order $Fa/(\mu R^3)$ rotation; if both centers were freely translating the same force monopole would not be emitted by the first sphere. Longitudinal forcing makes the leading cross product vanish.
