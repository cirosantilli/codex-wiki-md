**The hippocampus and perirhinal cortex contribute to an interacting cognitive system with different representational emphases: relations and context for the hippocampus, and integrated object representations and familiarity for perirhinal cortex.** This distinction is supported by converging task dissociations, but neither “all memory” nor “memory versus perception” maps neatly onto one anatomical boundary.

Both belong to the [medial temporal lobe](../../../medial-temporal-lobe.md) and interact through [entorhinal cortex](../../../entorhinal-cortex.md) and other cortical routes. Both participate in experience-dependent changes and recognition performance. Their common location and involvement in [declarative memory](../../../declarative-memory.md) do not imply identical computations. Conversely, different predominant functions do not require independence: recognizing an object in its prior location requires information about the object and its relations to context.

The [hippocampus](../../../hippocampus.md) binds or represents relations among items, places and episodes. Hippocampal [place cells](../../../place-cell.md) provide one spatially selective population code, while episodic remembering requires more than a location signal. A comparison of a familiar object in an unfamiliar setting with the same object in its original setting can place demands on contextual relations even though the item's individual familiarity is unchanged. This makes relational complexity a better prediction than assuming every recognition response is equally hippocampal.

The [perirhinal cortex](../../../perirhinal-cortex.md) integrates information about objects, including combinations of features, and contributes strongly to item [familiarity](../../../familiarity.md). A familiar-versus-new distinction can therefore survive some hippocampal damage if a task does not require retrieving the accompanying episode. However, an old/new response can also be solved through [recollection](../../../recollection.md), so preserved or impaired [recognition memory](../../../recognition-memory.md) cannot be assigned to one process solely from the response label. [Familiarity](../../../familiarity.md) is not equivalent to weak confidence, nor [recollection](../../../recollection.md) simply to a strong familiarity signal; tests need assumptions capable of distinguishing those accounts.

An informative lesion comparison tested rats on a radial maze and a spontaneous [object recognition task](../../../object-recognition-task.md). Bilateral hippocampal lesions impaired spatial memory, whereas peri-postrhinal lesions impaired object recognition and spared the spatial comparison more effectively. The object task used a restricted Y-shaped apparatus to reduce spatial and contextual information: after inspecting two identical objects, the animal later encountered familiar and novel alternatives. This [double dissociation](../../../double-dissociation.md) argues against treating the two structures as interchangeable components of a homogeneous store. Importantly, the cortical lesions included [postrhinal cortex](../../../postrhinal-cortex.md), so the experiment cannot attribute every effect uniquely to [perirhinal cortex](../../../perirhinal-cortex.md).[pp. 5901–5908; Methods and Figures 1, 5–6: modified recognition apparatus, radial maze and lesion extent](../../../double-dissociation-between-the-effects-of-peri-postrhinal-cortex-and-hippocampal-lesions-on-tests-of-object-recognition-and-spatial-memory-by-boyer-d-winters-and-others.md)

The interpretation depends on controls. A novel-object exploration preference requires adequate sampling, intact discrimination and motivation, not a verbal report of remembering. Position changes and environmental cues can make an apparently simple object task relational. Conversely, radial-maze performance can use nonspatial cues or learned response strategies unless these are controlled. A [double dissociation](../../../double-dissociation.md) is stronger than finding one impaired group on a difficult task, but different lesion sizes, task sensitivities and compensatory strategies still matter. Comparing a range of delays and feature overlaps is more informative than claiming an absolute boundary from one condition.

Clinical cases also constrain the comparison. Carefully characterized CA1 damage has produced marked [anterograde amnesia](../../../anterograde-amnesia.md), demonstrating an important hippocampal role in human new learning. The original report included minor pathology elsewhere, so its strength comes from the combined behavioural and neuropathological analysis rather than a claim of a perfectly isolated experimental lesion. Broader temporal resections cannot establish hippocampal specificity because they also affect nearby tissue. The severity and breadth of a patient's deficit should therefore be related to the full lesion and functional network, rather than assigned automatically to the most familiar structure.[Abstract and anatomical/behavioural analyses: R.B.'s restricted principal lesion and new-learning deficit](../../../human-amnesia-and-the-medial-temporal-region-enduring-memory-impairment-following-a-bilateral-lesion-limited-to-field-ca1-of-the-hippocampus-by-stuart-zola-morgan-and-others.md)

A pure memory-system classification also faces perceptual evidence. In trial-unique human object-oddity tasks, all alternatives were displayed together and subjects selected the different object. Patients with broader medial temporal damage including [perirhinal cortex](../../../perirhinal-cortex.md) were impaired when objects shared many features, but could perform conditions solvable from simple features. Patients with more selective hippocampal damage were relatively spared. This supports a [feature-conjunction account of perirhinal cortex](../../../feature-conjunction-account-of-perirhinal-cortex.md): representing complex objects can matter during perception as well as retention. Yet the broader patient lesions prevent assigning every deficit uniquely to one cortical region, and simultaneous display minimizes rather than logically abolishes all memory or attentional demands.[Abstract and pp. 2963–2974: trial-unique object oddity, feature overlap and patient groups](../../../the-human-medial-temporal-lobe-processes-online-representations-of-complex-objects-by-morgan-d-barense-and-others.md)

Representational and process accounts can be complementary. Item familiarity may emerge from experience-dependent changes in integrated object representations; binding an object to its location or temporal setting may require hippocampal relational processing. This predicts dependence on what information a task needs, rather than delay alone. At high feature interference, even immediate object discrimination can require [perirhinal cortex](../../../perirhinal-cortex.md); when context is essential, even recognizing a familiar item can require hippocampal participation. These are testable predictions, not a claim that every perceptual difficulty is already explained by one model.

The extended-system account makes an additional comparison: hippocampal interactions with the [fornix](../../../fornix.md) and [anterior thalamic nuclei](../../../anterior-thalamic-nuclei.md) support event-related remembering, whereas a perirhinal–mediodorsal thalamic system can contribute to familiarity judgments. This is a theoretical synthesis of lesion, recording and related evidence, not itself a new experiment proving strict process purity. Anatomical interaction remains compatible with functional specialization, and the proposed two-process mapping should be challenged by tasks that equate strength while changing contextual demands.[Abstract and pp. 425–444: proposed hippocampal–anterior thalamic and perirhinal–mediodorsal systems](../../../episodic-memory-amnesia-and-the-hippocampal-anterior-thalamic-axis-by-john-p-aggleton-and-others.md)

The most useful contrast is consequently relational/contextual representation versus integrated item representation, with corresponding differences in [recollection](../../../recollection.md) and [familiarity](../../../familiarity.md). The evidence available in April 2022 supports specialization within an interacting network, while leaving the exact relationship between representations, memory processes and perception open to experimental refinement. Neither structure is a universal memory centre, and neither can be understood adequately from a single task label.
