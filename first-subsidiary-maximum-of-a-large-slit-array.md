# First subsidiary maximum of a large slit array

↑ **Parent:** [Diffraction grating](diffraction-grating.md)

For $N$ equal narrow slits, adjacent [phase](phase-waves.md) increment $\delta$ gives [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) $\sin(N\delta/2)/\sin(\delta/2)$ relative to one slit. Near a principal maximum, set $u=N\delta/2$; for large $N$ the normalized [intensity](intensity-physics.md) approaches $(\sin u/u)^2$. Approximating the first subsidiary maximum by $u=3\pi/2$ gives principal-to-subsidiary [intensity](intensity-physics.md) ratio $9\pi^2/4$. The exact large-array extremum satisfies $\tan u=u$ in $(\pi,2\pi)$ and gives a slightly different ratio, about $21.2$. The slit envelope can further modify observed peak heights.
