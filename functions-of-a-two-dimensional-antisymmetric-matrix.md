# Functions of a two-dimensional antisymmetric matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Skew-symmetric matrix](skew-symmetric-matrix.md)

Every real $2\times2$ [antisymmetric matrix](skew-symmetric-matrix.md) has the form $A=sJ$, where $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$. Thus $A^2=-s^2I$, $\operatorname{tr}A=0$, and $\det A=s^2$. In particular,

$$
A^{2n}=(-s^2)^nI,\qquad A^{2n+1}=(-s^2)^nA.
$$

A convergent scalar [power series](power-series.md) applied to $A$ can therefore be reduced into an [even](even-function.md) [scalar multiple](scalar-multiple.md) of $I$ plus an [odd](odd-function.md) [scalar multiple](scalar-multiple.md) of $A$. For real coefficients and $s\ne0$, this is $f(A)=\operatorname{Re}f(is)I+[\operatorname{Im}f(is)/s]A$, provided the series converges at $\pm is$. [Absolute convergence](absolute-convergence.md) justifies separating [even](even-function.md) and [odd](odd-function.md) terms. This converts many small [matrix](matrix.md) series problems into ordinary scalar series, without computing [eigenvectors](eigenvector.md).
