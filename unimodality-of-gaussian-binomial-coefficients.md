# Unimodality of Gaussian binomial coefficients

↑ **Parent:** [Gaussian binomial coefficient](gaussian-binomial-coefficient.md)

For $0\le k\le n$, let $V$ be the $n$-dimensional irreducible [sl2 Lie algebra](sl2-lie-algebra.md) representation. Its weights are $n-1,n-3,\ldots,1-n$. A wedge basis shows $\operatorname{ch}\Lambda^kV=q^{-k(n-k)}G_{n,k}(q^2)$, where $G_{n,k}(t)$ is the [Gaussian binomial coefficient](gaussian-binomial-coefficient.md). By the [Weyl complete reducibility theorem](weyl-complete-reducibility-theorem.md), this character is a nonnegative sum of irreducible strings $q^m+q^{m-2}+\cdots+q^{-m}$. All its weights have parity $k(n-k)$, so their coefficients increase toward zero and decrease afterward. Consequently the coefficients of $G_{n,k}(t)$ are symmetric and unimodal. The [symmetric quantum binomial coefficient](symmetric-quantum-binomial-coefficient.md) is this character, a [Laurent polynomial](laurent-polynomial.md); its coefficients are unimodal on the occupied parity. The full integer-exponent sequence need not be unimodal, as $q+q^{-1}$ demonstrates.
