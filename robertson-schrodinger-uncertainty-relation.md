<h1 id="robertson-schrodinger-uncertainty-relation">Robertson-Schrödinger uncertainty relation</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Robertson uncertainty principle](robertson-uncertainty-principle.md)

The [Robertson-Schrödinger uncertainty relation](robertson-schrodinger-uncertainty-relation.md) keeps the real part discarded in the proof of the [Robertson uncertainty principle](robertson-uncertainty-principle.md). Write $A'=A-\langle A\rangle I$ and $B'=B-\langle B\rangle I$. Their [quantum covariance](quantum-covariance.md) is $C_{AB}=\operatorname{Re}\langle A'\psi|B'\psi\rangle$. On the same finite-moment and product [operator domains](operator-domain.md) used above, the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) gives

$$
(\Delta A)^2(\Delta B)^2\geq|\langle A'\psi|B'\psi\rangle|^2
=C_{AB}^2+\frac14|\langle[A,B]\rangle|^2.
$$

For [position](position.md) and [momentum](momentum-split.md), form the real, symmetrized [covariance matrix](covariance-matrix.md)

$$
\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}(\Delta x)^2&C_{xp}\\C_{xp}&(\Delta p)^2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det\Sigma\geq\frac{\hbar^2}{4}.
$$

A larger coordinatewise product can therefore encode a tilted [covariance ellipse](covariance-ellipse.md), rather than a larger [determinant](determinant.md). This explains why the [equality case of the Heisenberg uncertainty relation](equality-case-of-the-heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md) is more restrictive than equality in the [Robertson-Schrödinger uncertainty relation](robertson-schrodinger-uncertainty-relation.md).
