# Electron optics

↑ **Parent:** [Optics](optics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_optics)

[Electron optics](electron-optics.md) studies how [electric fields](electric-field-split.md) and [magnetic fields](magnetic-field-split.md) guide [electrons](electron.md) and form images. The [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md) supplies a trajectory description, while the [de Broglie wavelength](de-broglie-wavelength.md) supplies the [wave](wave-split.md) scale needed for [diffraction](diffraction.md). Both descriptions matter: steering a narrow beam and resolving two nearby specimen features are different achievements.

For a [magnetic electron lens](magnetic-electron-lens.md),

$$
\mathbf F=-e\mathbf v\times\mathbf B,\qquad \mathbf F\cdot\mathbf v=0.
$$

Here $e>0$ is the [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md), $\mathbf v$ the [electron](electron.md) [velocity](velocity.md), and $\mathbf B$ the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md). The second equality follows because a [cross product](cross-product.md) is perpendicular to each factor: a static [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) bends the trajectory without increasing the [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md). Acceleration instead requires work by an [electric field](electric-field-split.md). This distinction lets an [electron microscope](electron-microscope.md) adjust imaging with [magnetic fields](magnetic-field-split.md) while an accelerating [voltage](voltage.md) sets the [electron](electron.md) [momentum](momentum-split.md).
