# Tanaka equation

↑ **Parent:** [Stochastic differential equation](stochastic-differential-equation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanaka_equation)

With $s(x)=1$ for $x\geq0$ and $s(x)=-1$ for $x<0$, the equation starting at zero has [uniqueness in law](uniqueness-in-law.md) but not [pathwise uniqueness](pathwise-uniqueness.md). For existence, take a [Brownian motion](brownian-motion-split.md) $W$ and set $B=\int s(W)dW$; the [Lévy characterization of Brownian motion](levy-characterization-of-brownian-motion.md) and [associativity of stochastic integration](associativity-of-stochastic-integration.md) give $W=\int s(W)dB$. Every solution has [quadratic variation](quadratic-variation.md) $t$ and hence the [Brownian motion](brownian-motion-split.md) law. Both $W$ and $-W$ are solutions driven by the same $B$, since the time spent at zero is zero and $s(-W)=-s(W)$ away from zero. Using $s(0)=0$ instead would allow the constant solution and invalidate this uniqueness-in-law example.
