# Oblique symmetry of complementary subspaces

↑ **Parent:** [Eigenspace decomposition of a linear involution](eigenspace-decomposition-of-a-linear-involution.md)

For complementary [vector subspaces](vector-subspace.md) $U,W$ of a real [vector space](vector-space-split.md), the rule $T(u+w)=u-w$ is a well-defined [linear map](linear-map.md) satisfying $T^2=I$. It fixes $U$ pointwise and reverses $W$. In a [basis](basis.md) formed by adjoining [bases](basis.md) of $U$ and $W$, its [matrix](matrix.md) is $\operatorname{diag}(I,-I)$. Conversely every real [linear map](linear-map.md) with $T^2=I$ has this form, with $U=\ker(T-I)$ and $W=\ker(T+I)$, as follows from $v=(v+Tv)/2+(v-Tv)/2$.

In a [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md), such a [linear map](linear-map.md) is an [orthogonal transformation](orthogonal-transformation.md) if and only if $U$ and $W$ are perpendicular. Necessity follows from $\langle u,w\rangle=\langle Tu,Tw\rangle=-\langle u,w\rangle$; sufficiency follows by expanding the squared [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md) of $u+w$. In three dimensions, a fixed plane with reversed complementary line is a possibly oblique reflection, and a fixed line with reversed complementary plane is a possibly oblique axial symmetry. Their diagonal forms alone do not imply a Euclidean [reflection in a hyperplane](reflection-in-a-hyperplane.md) or a Euclidean half-turn.
