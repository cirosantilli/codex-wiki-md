# Three-domain system

↑ **Parent:** [Domain (biology)](domain-biology.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-domain_system)

The [three-domain system](three-domain-system.md) recognizes [bacteria](bacteria.md), [archaea](archaea.md) and [eukaryotes](eukaryote.md) as three domains. In 1990, Woese, Kandler and Wheelis proposed the domain rank and the names Bacteria, Archaea and Eucarya, using molecular comparisons to argue that the older [prokaryote](prokaryote.md)/[eukaryote](eukaryote.md) division hid important differences. See [Woese, Kandler and Wheelis, 1990: Towards a natural system of organisms](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2112744/).

The associated three-domain ancestry model places the nuclear lineage of [eukaryotes](eukaryote.md) alongside [archaea](archaea.md), rather than within them. This historically influential interpretation should be distinguished from the [two-domain system](two-domain-system.md); listing three familiar groups does not establish three independent primary branches.
