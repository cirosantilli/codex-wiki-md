# Zeroth-law thermometer comparison

↑ **Parent:** [Zeroth law of thermodynamics](zeroth-law-of-thermodynamics.md)

Put a contact [thermometer](thermometer.md) $C$ in [thermal contact](thermal-contact.md) with $A$, wait for [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium.md), and record response $X_A$. Repeat with $B$ under the same operating constraints. If $X_A=X_B$, and the calibrated response is an [injective function](injective-function.md) of [temperature](temperature.md) on this range, $C$ occupies the same thermal reference state in both comparisons. The [zeroth law of thermodynamics](zeroth-law-of-thermodynamics.md) then predicts $A\sim B$. Equality of raw numbers from different uncalibrated [thermometers](thermometer.md) supplies no such premise.[§1.1, Figure 1.2 and following paragraph: a thermometer measures its own temperature and transfers the equilibrium comparison](university-physics-volume-2-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others.md)

Use a probe whose participating [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) is small relative to each sample, or use maintained [heat baths](heat-bath.md). Otherwise the matching readings describe the disturbed final states. Also hold the probe's immersion and mechanical conditions fixed: its response can depend on more than [temperature](temperature.md). Matching readouts only tests agreement within resolution and repeatability, rather than proving exact equality of [temperatures](temperature.md).

Agreement within a tolerance is not itself an [equivalence relation](equivalence-relation.md). With a $0.10\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ tolerance, readings $25.00$ and $25.16\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ each agree with $25.08\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, yet disagree with each other. This is a limitation of the measurement rule, not a failure of the exact [transitivity](transitive-relation.md) asserted by the [zeroth law of thermodynamics](zeroth-law-of-thermodynamics.md).
