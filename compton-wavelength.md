# Compton wavelength

↑ **Parent:** [Mathematical physics](mathematical-physics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton_wavelength)

The [Compton wavelength](compton-wavelength.md) of a [subatomic particle](subatomic-particle.md) of [rest mass](invariant-mass.md) $m>0$ is $\lambda_C=h/(mc)$, where $h$ is the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md) and $c$ the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). It is the [wavelength](wavelength.md) of a [photon](photon.md) whose [energy](energy-split.md) $hc/\lambda_C$ equals that [subatomic particle](subatomic-particle.md)'s [rest energy](rest-energy.md) $mc^2$. It is a characteristic scale, rather than a measured geometric radius of the [subatomic particle](subatomic-particle.md).

The reduced [Compton wavelength](compton-wavelength.md) is $\bar\lambda_C=\hbar/(mc)=\lambda_C/(2\pi)$, with $\hbar=h/(2\pi)$. Keep that factor of $2\pi$: the [Compton scattering](compton-scattering.md) [wavelength](wavelength.md) shift below uses $h/(m_ec)$, not $\hbar/(m_ec)$. Here we choose the rounded [electron](electron.md) value $\lambda_C=2.426\,\mathrm{pm}$, where $1\,\mathrm{pm}=10^{-12}\,\mathrm m$; the maximum single-collision [wavelength](wavelength.md) increase is $2\lambda_C=4.852\,\mathrm{pm}$.

Why this scale? A [photon](photon.md) of [wavelength](wavelength.md) $\lambda$ has [momentum](momentum-split.md) magnitude $h/\lambda$. At $\lambda\sim\lambda_C$ that [momentum](momentum-split.md) is comparable with $mc$, so transfer of an appreciable fraction of it can make the recoiling [subatomic particle](subatomic-particle.md) relativistic. Even for longer [wavelengths](wavelength.md), [Compton scattering](compton-scattering.md) can resolve the small [energy](energy-split.md) transferred into [electron](electron.md) [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md).
