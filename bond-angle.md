# Bond angle

↑ **Parent:** [Molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md)

A [bond angle](bond-angle.md) is the [angle](angle.md) between two [chemical bonds](chemical-bond.md) sharing a central [chemical atom](atom-physics.md). In H–O–H, the middle symbol identifies the vertex: the [angle](angle.md) is at [oxygen](oxygen.md). Let $u$ and $v$ be the nonzero displacement [vectors](vector.md) from that [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus.md) to the two neighbours. The actual three-dimensional [bond angle](bond-angle.md) satisfies

$$
\cos\theta=\frac{u\cdot v}{\|u\|\|v\|},\qquad 0\leq\theta\leq\pi,
$$

using the [dot product](dot-product.md) and [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md). Rotating the whole [molecule](molecule.md) preserves this expression; projecting it onto a page generally does not.

There is another useful way to find a [bond angle](bond-angle.md). If the two [bond lengths](bond-length.md) are $a,b$ and the distance between the outer [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus.md) is $d$, the [law of cosines](law-of-cosines.md) gives

$$
d^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos\theta.
$$

For equal [bond lengths](bond-length.md) $a=b$, this becomes $d=2b\sin(\theta/2)$. Thus a measured outer-atom separation contains information about a [bond angle](bond-angle.md) even though those outer [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) are not directly joined by a [chemical bond](chemical-bond.md).

For a stipulated rigid, equal-[bond length](bond-length.md) model using [water](water.md)'s $104.4776^\circ$ equilibrium [bond angle](bond-angle.md), $d/b\approx1.5811$, whereas a straight H–O–H arrangement would give $d/b=2$. These are calculated geometric ratios, not new measured distances. [Gas electron diffraction](gas-electron-diffraction.md) illustrates how pair-distance information can constrain [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md). Substituting independently averaged distances into the rigid [triangle](triangle.md) formula does not automatically recover an equilibrium [bond angle](bond-angle.md), because averaging and this nonlinear calculation need not commute.
