# Euclidean scalar-gradient stress identity

↑ **Parent:** [Vector calculus](vector-calculus.md)

For a sufficiently smooth real [scalar field](scalar-field.md) on [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md), set $\Sigma_{kj}=\partial_k\psi\partial_j\psi-\delta_{kj}|\nabla\psi|^2/2$. The [product rule](product-rule.md) and equality of mixed [partial derivatives](partial-derivative.md) give

$$
\partial_k\Sigma_{kj}=(\Delta\psi)\partial_j\psi+\partial_k\psi\partial_k\partial_j\psi-\partial_k\psi\partial_j\partial_k\psi=(\Delta\psi)\partial_j\psi.
$$

The [divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md), applied twice, consequently gives the useful pure-boundary identity

$$
\int_V\mathbf r\,\nabla\cdot[(\Delta\psi)\nabla\psi]\,dV=\int_{\partial V}\left[\mathbf r(\Delta\psi)\partial_n\psi-(\partial_n\psi)\nabla\psi+\tfrac12|\nabla\psi|^2\hat{\mathbf n}\right]dS.
$$

Here $\partial_n\psi=\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\nabla\psi$ uses the outward [unit normal](unit-normal.md). This is the Euclidean zero-potential counterpart of the [scalar stress-energy divergence identity](scalar-stress-energy-divergence-identity.md).
