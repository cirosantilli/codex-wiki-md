# Stronger prime-divisor congruence for Fermat numbers

↑ **Parent:** [Prime divisor of a Fermat number](prime-divisor-of-a-fermat-number.md)

For $n\geq2$, the first order bound already gives $p\equiv1\pmod8$. The [second supplementary law for quadratic reciprocity](second-supplementary-law-for-quadratic-reciprocity.md) says that two is then a [quadratic residue](quadratic-residue.md) modulo $p$. [Euler's criterion](euler-s-criterion.md) gives $2^{(p-1)/2}\equiv1$, so its [multiplicative order](multiplicative-order.md) $2^{n+1}$ divides $(p-1)/2$. Thus $2^{n+2}\mid p-1$, further reducing the candidate factors. At $n=5$, $641=5\cdot128+1$ meets this bound. The restriction matters: $F_1=5$ has factor five, which is not one modulo eight.
