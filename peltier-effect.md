# Peltier effect

↑ **Parent:** [Thermoelectric effect](thermoelectric-effect-split.md)

The [Peltier effect](peltier-effect.md) is [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) associated with an [electric current](electric-current.md) crossing a junction between materials with different [Peltier coefficients](peltier-coefficient.md). At fixed junction [temperature](temperature-split.md), its contribution changes sign when the [conventional current](conventional-current.md) reverses. A junction can absorb [heat](heat-split.md) from its surroundings for one direction and release it for the other. [Joule heating](joule-heating.md), proportional to the square of the [electric current](electric-current.md), accompanies this transport but does not reverse sign. Consequently, observing a warm device alone does not identify the [Peltier effect](peltier-effect.md).

This makes the [Peltier effect](peltier-effect.md) useful as an electrically controlled [heat pump](heat-pump.md): reversing the supply can switch an object's thermal role without changing any plumbing. In [thermoelectric cooling](thermoelectric-cooling.md), however, the useful cooling is the junction's absorbed [heat](heat-split.md) minus returning [heat conduction](thermal-conduction.md) and its share of [Joule heating](joule-heating.md). Increasing [electric current](electric-current.md) eventually makes cooling worse. The distinction between transported [heat](heat-split.md) and net cooling is the central practical lesson of the [thermoelectric cooler energy balance](thermoelectric-cooler-energy-balance.md).
