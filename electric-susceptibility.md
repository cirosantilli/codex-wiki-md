# Electric susceptibility

↑ **Parent:** [Dielectric](dielectric.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_susceptibility)

The [dimensionless quantity](dimensionless-quantity.md) relating induced [electric polarization](polarization-density.md) to the internal [electric field](electric-field-split.md) through $\mathbf P=\epsilon_0\chi_e\mathbf E$ in the static scalar model. It describes the material contribution; [relative permittivity](relative-permittivity.md) includes the [vacuum](vacuum.md) term as well, $\epsilon_r=1+\chi_e$. Zero [electric susceptibility](electric-susceptibility.md) gives $\epsilon_r=1$, not zero [permittivity](permittivity-split.md).

The scalar $\chi_e$ assumes a local response with [linearity](linearity.md) and [isotropy](isotropy.md), settled [electric polarization](polarization-density.md), fixed material conditions, and no residual [electric polarization](polarization-density.md) at zero [electric field](electric-field-split.md). It relates $\mathbf P$ to the averaged total internal [electric field](electric-field-split.md), which already includes the field of the bound [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md). It does not relate $\mathbf P$ to the field the external [electrodes](electrode.md) would produce in [vacuum](vacuum.md). The distinction is essential for a self-consistent calculation.[equations (807)–(812): total field, displacement, linear susceptibility and relative dielectric constant](classical-electromagnetism-an-intermediate-level-course-by-richard-fitzpatrick/polarization.md)

Substitute the scalar relation into the definition of [electric displacement field](electric-displacement-field.md):

$$
\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P
=\epsilon_0(1+\chi_e)\mathbf E.
$$

Comparison with $\mathbf D=\epsilon\mathbf E$ proves $\epsilon=\epsilon_0(1+\chi_e)$ and $\epsilon_r=1+\chi_e$. Both $\mathbf D$ and $\mathbf P$ have units $\mathrm{C/m^2}$, so $\chi_e$ is a [dimensionless quantity](dimensionless-quantity.md). In a stipulated static model with $\epsilon_r=3$, $\chi_e=2$: at a prescribed internal [electric field](electric-field-split.md), the material contribution $P$ is twice $\epsilon_0E$. This is a hypothetical response, not a measured material constant. The [electric susceptibility](electric-susceptibility.md) is a convenient bridge from [microscopic dielectric polarization](microscopic-dielectric-polarization.md) to a macroscopic [capacitance](capacitance.md) measurement, but it is not a measure of [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md).
