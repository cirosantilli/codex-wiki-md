# Divergence theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Vector calculus](vector-calculus.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergence_theorem)

Let $V$ be a bounded region in [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md) with piecewise smooth boundary and outward [unit normal vector](unit-normal.md) $n$. For a continuously differentiable [vector field](vector-field.md) $F$ on a neighborhood of its closure,

$$
\int_V\nabla\cdot F\,dV=\int_{\partial V}F\cdot n\,dS.
$$

The total outward [flux](flux.md) equals the accumulated [divergence](divergence.md) inside. Contributions across shared internal faces cancel when a region is divided into small pieces.

For $F=(x,y,z)$ on a ball of radius $R$, the divergence is $3$, giving $3(4\pi R^3/3)=4\pi R^3$. On the boundary, $F\cdot n=R$, so the [surface integral](surface-integral.md) is also $R(4\pi R^2)=\boxed{4\pi R^3}$. This agreement turns the derivative into a geometric flux measurement. The required regularity matters: a singular field at the center cannot be included without checking its contribution separately.
