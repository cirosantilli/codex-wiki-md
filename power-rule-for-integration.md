# Power rule for integration

↑ **Parent:** [Integral](integral-split.md)

On $x>0$, the [power rule](power-rule.md) for [derivatives](derivative.md) and the [fundamental theorem of calculus](fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) give the displayed [antiderivative](antiderivative.md). In particular $\int_0^1x^q\,dx=1/(q+1)$ for $q>-1$, understood as an [improper integral](improper-integral.md) when needed. At $q=-1$, the [antiderivative](antiderivative.md) on $x>0$ is the [natural logarithm](natural-logarithm.md) $\log x+C$.
