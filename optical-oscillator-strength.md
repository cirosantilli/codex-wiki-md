# Optical oscillator strength

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz oscillator model](lorentz-oscillator-model.md)

An [optical oscillator strength](optical-oscillator-strength.md) is a dimensionless weight assigned to an optical resonance. In an independent-resonance [Lorentz oscillator model](lorentz-oscillator-model.md) using a reference [electron mass](electron-mass.md) $m_e$ and a specified participating [number density](number-density.md) $n$,

$$
\epsilon_r(\omega)=1+\frac{ne^2}{\epsilon_0m_e}
\sum_j\frac{f_j}{\omega_j^2-\omega^2-i\gamma_j\omega}.
$$

Its normalization must be stated: assigning one unit of total strength per participating [electron](electron.md) means $\sum_jf_j=1$. A one-resonance approximation with $f_1=1$ represents the whole chosen electron population, rather than asserting that the first allowed [interband transition](interband-transition.md) carries all optical strength. The smallest [band gap](band-gap.md) therefore need not equal the fitted resonance energy.
