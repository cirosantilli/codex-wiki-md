# Branch point

↑ **Parent:** [Complex analysis](complex-analysis-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branch_point)

A branch point is a point around which a sufficiently small loop can change a [germ of a holomorphic function](germ-of-a-holomorphic-function.md) under [analytic continuation](analytic-continuation.md). This is a local condition: a large loop around a regular point might also enclose a distant [branch point](branch-point.md), so that large loop alone cannot classify the regular point. For the [complex logarithm](complex-logarithm.md) and square root, every nonzero point has a local [holomorphic](holomorphic-function-split.md) branch, but a loop once around $0$ changes the chosen branch. [Rosales, section 1, Definition 1.1 and its clarifications](branch-points-and-branch-cuts-by-rodolfo-r-rosales.md)

For $z=re^{it}$ with fixed $r>0$, start at $t=0$ with values $\sqrt r$ and $\log r$. Their continuations are

$$
w(t)=\sqrt r\,e^{it/2},\qquad L(t)=\log r+it.
$$

One counterclockwise turn gives $w(2\pi)=-\sqrt r$ and $L(2\pi)=\log r+2\pi i$. At $r=4$, the square root therefore changes from $2$ to $-2$, while the logarithm changes from $\log4$ to $\log4+2\pi i$. Two turns restore the square root to $2$; no nonzero number of turns restores the logarithm. This distinguishes a two-sheeted [algebraic branch point](algebraic-branch-point.md) from a [logarithmic singularity](logarithmic-singularity.md).

A [pole](pole.md) need not be a [branch point](branch-point.md): $1/z$ is unbounded near $0$, yet $1/(re^{it})=r^{-1}e^{-it}$ returns to its starting value after one turn. Conversely, the square-root values tend to $0$ at their [branch point](branch-point.md). The issue is whether a single [holomorphic](holomorphic-function-split.md) choice survives going around the point, rather than whether the values become large. On the [Riemann sphere](riemann-sphere.md), infinity is also a [branch point](branch-point.md) of both $\sqrt z$ and $\log z$: in the local coordinate $\zeta=1/z$, these are $\zeta^{-1/2}$ and $-\log\zeta$, whose continuations around $\zeta=0$ change sign and subtract $2\pi i$, respectively. [Rosales, section 1, equation 1.3, and Example 2.4](branch-points-and-branch-cuts-by-rodolfo-r-rosales.md)
