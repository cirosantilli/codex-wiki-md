# Hydrophobic effect

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrophobicity](hydrophobicity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrophobic_effect)

The [hydrophobic effect](hydrophobic-effect.md) is the solvent-mediated tendency of [nonpolar](nonpolar-molecule.md) regions to associate in [water](water.md). Exposing a [hydrocarbon](hydrocarbon.md) region changes how surrounding [water](water.md) [molecules](molecule.md) can arrange and interact; reducing that exposure can lower the total [free energy](thermodynamic-free-energy.md). Both [water](water.md) and [lipids](lipid.md) enter the accounting. The effect is not an additional [covalent bond](covalent-bond.md) between chains. See [IUPAC Gold Book: hydrophobic interaction](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/H02907).

At fixed [temperature](temperature.md) and [pressure](pressure.md), compare two candidate organizations by the change in [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md), $\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta S$, where $H$ is [enthalpy](enthalpy.md), $T$ is absolute [temperature](temperature.md), and $S$ is [entropy](entropy.md). An organization with lower $G$ is favored in this comparison. Reducing exposed [hydrocarbon](hydrocarbon.md) area is one contribution; changes in chain packing and the [entropy](entropy.md) of the [lipids](lipid.md) also matter. This explains why [hydrophobicity](hydrophobicity.md) alone cannot predict every aggregate shape. See [16.4 Free Energy](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/16-4-free-energy.md).
