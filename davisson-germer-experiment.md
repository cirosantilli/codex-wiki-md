<h1 id="davisson-germer-experiment">Davisson–Germer experiment</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Electron diffraction](electron-diffraction.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davisson–Germer_experiment)

The [Davisson–Germer experiment](davisson-germer-experiment.md) turned [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md) from [nickel](nickel.md) into evidence for a [matter wave](matter-wave.md). The search became a deliberate wave test in 1926, with [diffraction](diffraction.md) beams found early in 1927. The earlier crystal investigation had begun in 1925; the [de Broglie wavelength](de-broglie-wavelength.md) proposal was already present in the 1924 thesis.[The discovery of electron waves, printed p. 390: retrospective chronology and the change to a deliberate wave test](the-discovery-of-electron-waves-by-clinton-j-davisson.md)

A heated [tungsten](tungsten.md) ribbon supplied [Electrons](electron.md); gun apertures selected a narrow beam. Two $1\,\mathrm{mm}$ apertures were $8\,\mathrm{mm}$ apart; the gun ended $7\,\mathrm{mm}$ from a [nickel](nickel.md) crystal cut parallel to its $\{111\}$ planes. An adjustable [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) set the speed. The beam struck normally inside an evacuated, field-free enclosure with target and outer electrodes at one potential. A double-box collector, $11\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the target with a $1\,\mathrm{mm}$ entrance, selected directions. A retarding [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) rejected slower [Electrons](electron.md); the inner collector fed a sensitive [galvanometer](galvanometer.md). Moving the collector scanned colatitude; rotating the crystal scanned azimuth.[The apparatus, printed pp. 707–710, figures 2–4: source, apertures, potentials, collector and target preparation](diffraction-of-electrons-by-a-crystal-of-nickel-by-c-davisson-and-l-h-germer.md)

The readout was collected [electric current](electric-current.md), not a photograph of [Electron](electron.md) paths. Voltage scans held the gun-plate current constant because incident-beam current could not be held constant; the overall current trend therefore needed caution. A strong feature developed at $54\,\mathrm V$ near $50^\circ$ colatitude. Surface heating distinguished crystal beams from adsorbed-gas beams; finite apertures and background shifted or broadened apparent peaks.[Printed p. 705, abstract; pp. 715–719, figures 9–12: voltage-scan control, 54 V feature, background subtraction and instrumental widths](diffraction-of-electrons-by-a-crystal-of-nickel-by-c-davisson-and-l-h-germer.md)

At the bright beam's colatitude, an azimuth scan revealed three companion directions, matching crystal symmetry. The test compared [wavelengths](wavelength.md) inferred from [diffraction](diffraction.md) angles and known crystal spacings with [wavelengths](wavelength.md) inferred from accelerating [voltage](voltage.md) and [momentum](momentum.md); the 1937 lecture displayed this comparison against $U^{-1/2}$. Agreement across beam directions supplied more evidence than one angular peak.[Printed pp. 391–393, figures 2–4: angular and azimuth scans and independent wavelength comparison](the-discovery-of-electron-waves-by-clinton-j-davisson.md)

The inference is about coherent [quantum phases](quantum-phase.md) in scattering probabilities. It does not establish a wavy particle trajectory, and a collector-current experiment does not resolve individual detection events. Predicting the full intensity additionally requires the material's [scattering amplitude](scattering-amplitude.md), its surface condition, and instrumental angular acceptance; the [de Broglie wavelength](de-broglie-wavelength.md) alone predicts none of those strengths. The following [finite-row electron diffraction model](finite-row-electron-diffraction-model.md) isolates phase addition without attempting to reproduce the [nickel](nickel.md) measurements.
