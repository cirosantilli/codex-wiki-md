# Collective advantage over independent two-qubit filtering

↑ **Parent:** [Asymptotic pure-state entanglement conversion rate](asymptotic-pure-state-entanglement-conversion-rate.md)

For two-qubit pure states with smaller squared [Schmidt coefficients](schmidt-coefficient.md) $s<t$, [optimal stochastic conversion of a two-qubit pure state](optimal-stochastic-conversion-of-a-two-qubit-pure-state.md) succeeds independently with probability $s/t$. The [asymptotic pure-state entanglement conversion rate](asymptotic-pure-state-entanglement-conversion-rate.md) of collective [LOCC](local-operations-and-classical-communication.md) is instead $h_2(s)/h_2(t)$, where $h_2$ is [binary entropy](binary-entropy.md). The difference is strict: differentiating gives $\frac{d}{dx}[h_2(x)/x]=\log_2(1-x)/x^2<0$. Hence $h_2(s)/s>h_2(t)/t$. Independent filtering loses entanglement in its product-state failure branches, whereas collective concentration and dilution preserve entanglement asymptotically.
