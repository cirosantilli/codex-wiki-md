# Scattering efficiency

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation-split.md)

For a [sphere](sphere.md) of [radius](radius.md) $a$, the [scattering efficiency](scattering-efficiency.md) is its effective scattering [area](surface-area.md) divided by the projected [geometric cross-section](geometric-collision-cross-section.md) $\pi a^2$. It is a [dimensionless quantity](dimensionless-quantity.md). For [Rayleigh scattering by a small sphere](rayleigh-scattering-by-a-small-sphere.md) with relative [refractive index](refractive-index.md) $m$ and incident [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) $k$,

$$
Q_{\rm sca}=\frac{\sigma}{\pi a^2}=\frac83(ka)^4\left(\frac{m^2-1}{m^2+2}\right)^2.
$$

At fixed [wavelength](wavelength.md) and fixed [refractive index](refractive-index.md), the [Rayleigh scattering cross-section](rayleigh-scattering-by-a-small-sphere.md) scales as $a^6$, whereas the [scattering efficiency](scattering-efficiency.md) scales as $a^4$. Doubling [radius](radius.md) multiplies the former by $64$ and the latter by $16$. An efficiency here is an effective scattering [area](surface-area.md) relative to a reference [area](surface-area.md), rather than a fraction of all light in an arbitrary beam.

For a worked toy example, take $a=10\,\mathrm{nm}$, $m=1.5$, and $\lambda=550\,\mathrm{nm}$. Then $ka=2\pi(10/550)\simeq0.1142$ and $(m^2-1)/(m^2+2)=5/17$. Substitution gives [Rayleigh scattering cross-section](rayleigh-scattering-by-a-small-sphere.md) $\sigma\simeq1.234\times10^{-20}\,\mathrm{m}^2$ and [scattering efficiency](scattering-efficiency.md) $Q_{\rm sca}\simeq3.929\times10^{-5}$. At incident [light intensity](light-intensity.md) $1\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$, the scattered [power](power.md) is approximately $1.234\times10^{-20}\,\mathrm W$. These are leading small-particle estimates, not a measured air-[molecule](molecule-split.md) cross-section.
