# Abelian groups as integer modules

↑ **Parent:** [Module (mathematics)](module-mathematics.md)

Every [abelian group](abelian-group.md) has exactly one unital [module](module-mathematics.md) structure over the [integers](integer.md). For $n>0$, define $nm$ by adding $m$ to itself $n$ times, put $0m=0$, and put $(-n)m=-(nm)$. The [module](module-mathematics.md) axioms force this action, so no additional choice is involved. Moreover, every [Abelian group homomorphism](homomorphism-of-abelian-groups.md) respects it and is a [module homomorphism](module-homomorphism.md). See [David McKinnon's Modules, the integer-scalar example](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~dmckinno/pmath347/Modules.pdf).

For example, in the [free module](free-module.md) $\mathbb Z^2$,

$$
3(2,-1)+(-2)(1,4)=(4,-11).
$$

In the [cyclic group](cyclic-group.md) $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$, the same repeated-addition rule gives $4\overline2=\overline8=\overline2$ and $3\overline2=\overline0$. The scalar $3$ is nonzero in the scalar [ring](ring.md) $\mathbb Z$, even though it kills the nonzero [module](module-mathematics.md) element $\overline2$. Thus the scalar [ring](ring.md) and the [module](module-mathematics.md) must be kept distinct.
