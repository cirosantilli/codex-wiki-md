<h1 id="rank-sum-form-of-cochran-s-theorem">Rank-sum form of Cochran's theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Cochran's theorem](cochran-s-theorem.md)

Let $A_i$ be real [symmetric matrices](symmetric-matrix.md) with sum $I_n$. Their [matrix ranks](matrix-rank.md) sum to at least $n$, because their ranges span $\mathbb R^n$. If the ranks sum to $n$, those ranges form a [direct sum](direct-sum.md). For $y$ in the range of $A_i$, the identity $y=\sum_jA_jy$ and uniqueness of its range decomposition imply $A_jy=\delta_{ij}y$. Thus $A_i^2=A_i$ and $A_iA_j=0$ for $i\ne j$: the matrices are mutually orthogonal [orthogonal projections](orthogonal-projection.md). In a combined [orthonormal basis](orthonormal-basis.md), the forms $Z^TA_iZ$ for $Z\sim N_n(0,I_n)$ use disjoint sets of independent standard normal coordinates. They are independent [chi-squared distributions](chi-squared-distribution.md) with degrees of freedom equal to the ranks. Conversely, if all these forms have those chi-squared laws, their expectations and their sum give $\sum_i\operatorname{rank}A_i=n$.
