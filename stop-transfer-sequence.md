# Stop-transfer sequence

↑ **Parent:** [Protein targeting](protein-targeting.md)

A [stop-transfer sequence](stop-transfer-sequence.md) interrupts ongoing passage of a [polypeptide chain](polypeptide.md) across a membrane and retains a membrane-spanning segment. [Protein targeting](protein-targeting.md), insertion and subsequent sorting involve distinguishable sequence instructions.[Blobel, Intracellular protein topogenesis, 1980; abstract: signal, stop-transfer, sorting and insertion sequences](intracellular-protein-topogenesis-by-gunter-blobel.md)

In the figure's cleaved-leader example, assume the [N-terminus](n-terminus.md) of the mature chain has entered the [ER lumen](er-lumen.md), a [stop-transfer sequence](stop-transfer-sequence.md) remains in the membrane, and the remaining [C-terminus](c-terminus.md) is made on the [cytosol](cytosol.md) side. The outcome differs from releasing the entire mature chain into the [ER lumen](er-lumen.md). The latter is soluble cargo, potentially retained there or transported onward; entry alone does not establish secretion.
