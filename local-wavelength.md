# Local wavelength

↑ **Parent:** [Wavelength](wavelength.md)

For a locally oscillatory [wave](wave.md) written $u(\mathbf x,t)=A(\mathbf x,t)\cos\phi(\mathbf x,t)$, the local [wavevector](wavevector.md) is $\mathbf k_{\mathrm{loc}}=\nabla\phi$, where $\nabla$ is the spatial [gradient](gradient.md). The [local wavelength](local-wavelength.md) $2\pi/|\mathbf k_{\mathrm{loc}}|$ measures local [wave phase](phase-waves.md) spacing normal to the [wavefront](wavefront.md). Interpreting it as a crest separation requires the [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) and local [wavevector](wavevector.md) to vary little over one [wavelength](wavelength.md), as in the [WKB approximation](wkb-approximation.md). Along a unit direction $\mathbf e$ the corresponding local repeat distance is $2\pi/|\mathbf k_{\mathrm{loc}}\cdot\mathbf e|$, if that [dot product](dot-product.md) is nonzero; oblique sampling measures this projected spacing.

A real snapshot alone does not uniquely specify $A$ and $\phi$: their meaning requires a chosen oscillatory component or physical approximation. Strong [interference](interference-wave-propagation.md), zeros of [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md), and rapidly changing media can defeat a single-carrier description. If $\nabla\phi=0$, the local reciprocal formula gives no finite [wavelength](wavelength.md); it does not establish an exact global spatial [period](period-of-a-function.md).
