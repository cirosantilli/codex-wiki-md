# Electromagnetic wave

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_wave)

An [electromagnetic wave](electromagnetic-wave.md) is a propagating disturbance of the [electric field](electric-field.md) and [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) obeying the [Maxwell equations](maxwell-equations.md). In source-free vacuum its speed is

$$
c=\frac1{\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}}\simeq2.998\times10^8\,\mathrm{m/s},
$$

the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). An [electromagnetic wave](electromagnetic-wave.md) can travel through vacuum: a conducting medium is unnecessary. A single traveling [plane electromagnetic wave](plane-electromagnetic-wave.md) has mutually perpendicular [electric field](electric-field.md), [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md), and propagation direction. This statement applies to that wave; arbitrary [near and far field](near-and-far-field.md) configurations need not share these directions or the relation $|\mathbf B|=|\mathbf E|/c$.

The visualization compares perpendicular components on a common scale: $c\mathbf B$ has the same units as $\mathbf E$. The curves are field values along space, rather than paths traced by particles. Their common phase moves toward $+z$, while the [Poynting vector](poynting-vector.md) records the associated [energy flux](energy-flux.md).

**[Figure 53](#image-vacuum-plane-wave-with-electric-field-along-x-magnetic-field-along-y-and-energy-flux-along-positive-z). Vacuum plane wave with electric field along x, magnetic field along y, and energy flux along positive z**.

The [electromagnetic wave equation](electromagnetic-wave-equation.md) gives a propagation law, while the full [Maxwell equations](maxwell-equations.md) additionally enforce the field directions and relative amplitudes. This is why solving an independent scalar [wave equation](wave-equation-split.md) for each component is insufficient to construct an [electromagnetic wave](electromagnetic-wave.md). [Feynman lectures, II.20: solutions in free space](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_20.html).
