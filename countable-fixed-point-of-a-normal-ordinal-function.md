# Countable fixed point of a normal ordinal function

↑ **Parent:** [Fixed point of a normal ordinal function](fixed-point-of-a-normal-ordinal-function.md)

If a [normal function on an ordinal](normal-function-on-an-ordinal.md) begins at a [countable](countable-set.md) value and preserves countability from $\Phi(\xi)$ to $\Phi(\xi+1)$, [transfinite induction](transfinite-induction.md) shows it maps all [countable ordinals](countable-ordinal.md) to [countable ordinals](countable-ordinal.md). At [countable](countable-set.md) limits this uses [countable](countable-set.md) unions of [countable sets](countable-set.md), hence [axiom of countable choice](axiom-of-countable-choice.md) in the usual proof. Iteration from zero either stabilizes at a [countable](countable-set.md) fixed point or has a [countable](countable-set.md) limit fixed by continuity. Applying this to $\omega^\alpha$ gives the [countable](countable-set.md) epsilon number $\epsilon_0$.
