# Leading-log critical-field inversion

↑ **Parent:** [Ginzburg-Landau parameter](ginzburg-landau-parameter.md)

Combining $B_{c1}\simeq\Phi_0\ln\kappa/(4\pi\lambda^2)$ and $B_{c2}=\Phi_0/(2\pi\xi^2)$ gives $r=B_{c1}/B_{c2}=\ln\kappa/(2\kappa^2)$. Its derivative is $(1-2\ln\kappa)/(2\kappa^3)$: the ratio peaks at $\kappa=\sqrt e$, with maximum $1/(4e)$. Thus each $0<r<1/(4e)$ has two solutions with $\kappa>1$. The large-$\kappa$ branch is the one consistent with using a leading-log approximation; the small branch cannot be selected or rejected by the inequality $\kappa>1$ alone. Omitted core-energy terms limit numerical accuracy.
