<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Rayleigh-Taylor instability](../../../../../rayleigh-taylor-instability.md) occurs when a denser fluid is supported against an effective [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) by a lighter fluid. Let [density](../../../../../density.md) $\rho_h$ occupy the upper half-space, [density](../../../../../density.md) $\rho_l<\rho_h$ the lower half-space and [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) point downward. A displaced heavy-fluid finger lowers gravitational [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) as it sinks; neighboring light fluid rises. For incompressible inviscid half-spaces without [surface tension](../../../../../surface-tension-split.md), a disturbance $\eta\propto e^{\sigma t+ikx}$ has

$$
\boxed{\sigma^2=gk\frac{\rho_h-\rho_l}{\rho_h+\rho_l}.}
$$

The [density](../../../../../density.md) ratio in this expression is the [Atwood number](../../../../../atwood-number.md). With the [density](../../../../../density.md) ordering reversed, $\sigma^2<0$ and the interface supports stable gravity-wave oscillations in this ideal model.

**[Figure 2](#2/b/image-rayleigh-taylor-growth-a-heavy-layer-above-a-light-layer-develops-growing-fingers). Rayleigh-Taylor growth: a heavy layer above a light layer develops growing fingers**.

[Surface tension](../../../../../surface-tension-split.md) suppresses sufficiently short [wavelengths](../../../../../wavelength.md), and [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) changes the growth dynamics. A [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) parallel to the interface can resist [perturbations](../../../../../perturbation.md) that bend its lines; interchange directions that do not bend the field need separate consideration. In astrophysics, effective deceleration can make dense ejecta or a swept-up shell supported by a lower-density hot interior unstable. The effective [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) and [density](../../../../../density.md) ordering must be evaluated in that moving interface's frame; simply seeing a dense shell does not fix the stability sign.
