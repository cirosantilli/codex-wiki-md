<h1 id="section-a/a1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Take positive $\dot Q$ to mean net removal of thermal [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) per unit mass. The [isobaric thermal stability of a heated ideal gas](../../../../../isobaric-thermal-stability-of-a-heated-ideal-gas.md) depends on the density change accompanying a temperature disturbance. For an [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) of fixed composition, $p=\rho k_BT/\bar m$, so constant [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) gives $\rho T=\text{constant}$. Therefore the net cooling can be written

$$
\mathcal L(T)=CT^{\alpha-1}-H,
$$

where $C>0$ is constant for this pressure. At thermal equilibrium $T=T_0$, $CT_0^{\alpha-1}=H$.

The [first law of thermodynamics](../../../../../first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) at constant pressure includes the expansion work, so the relevant heat capacity is the [specific heat capacity at constant pressure](../../../../../specific-heat-capacity-at-constant-pressure.md), $c_p$, rather than the constant-volume value. Linearizing its temperature equation gives

$$
c_p\frac{d\,\delta T}{dt}
=-\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial T}\right)_p\delta T
=-\frac{(\alpha-1)H}{T_0}\delta T.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{\delta T\propto e^{st},\qquad
s=\frac{(1-\alpha)H}{c_pT_0}.}
$$

For optically thin [Bremsstrahlung](../../../../../bremsstrahlung.md), $\alpha=1/2$, so

$$
\boxed{s=\frac{H}{2c_pT_0}>0:\quad\text{the equilibrium is thermally unstable}.}
$$

A hotter parcel expands and becomes less dense. Its cooling per unit mass, proportional to $T^{-1/2}$ at fixed pressure, decreases while its [cosmic ray](../../../../../cosmic-ray.md) heating stays fixed, making it hotter still. A colder, denser parcel cools faster and runs away in the opposite direction. Here $H$ has units $\mathrm{W\,kg^{-1}}$ and $c_pT_0$ units $\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$, so $s$ is an inverse time. The conclusion assumes the disturbance remains close to pressure equilibrium and neglects [heat conduction](../../../../../thermal-conduction.md) and changes of composition. The relevant $\alpha$ is the temperature exponent in the supplied cooling law.
