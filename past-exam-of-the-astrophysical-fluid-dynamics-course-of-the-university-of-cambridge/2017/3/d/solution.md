<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The smaller galaxy is treated as a [test particle](../../../../test-particle.md) released from rest at $r_0$. [Conservation of energy](../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) in the fixed [gravitational potential](../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md) gives

$$
\frac12\dot d^2+\psi(d)=\psi(r_0),\qquad
\dot d^2=2A\left(d^{-1/2}-r_0^{-1/2}\right).
$$

The galactic gas is stationary in its hydrostatic model, so this is the relative speed squared. Write $\Delta(d)=d^{-1/2}-r_0^{-1/2}\geq0$ on the inward path. The astrophysical [ram pressure](../../../../ram-pressure.md) is the incident normal momentum flux $\rho(d)\dot d^2$, giving

$$
\boxed{p_{\rm ram}(d)=\frac{2A\mu m_up_0}{k_BT}\,\Delta(d)
\exp\!\left[\frac{A\mu m_u}{k_BT}\Delta(d)\right],\qquad0<d\leq r_0.}
$$

It vanishes at release and increases during infall. This [ram pressure](../../../../ram-pressure.md) convention is $\rho v^2$, not the $\rho v^2/2$ sometimes called dynamic [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md). Gas drag and the object's response are neglected consistently with the prescribed ballistic motion.
