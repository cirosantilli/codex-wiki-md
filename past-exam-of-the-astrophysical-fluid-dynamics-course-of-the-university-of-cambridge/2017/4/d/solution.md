<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For an unattenuated isotropic point source, the radiation flux is $L_*/(4\pi r^2)$. The [radiation pressure](../../../../radiation-pressure.md) force per particle divided by its mass $\bar m$ gives the outward acceleration

$$
a_{\rm rad}=\frac{\sigma L_*}{4\pi\bar m c\,r^2}.
$$

Thus the effective inward inverse-square coefficient is $GM-\sigma L_*/(4\pi\bar m c)$. Insert it into the preceding wind equation. The [radiative shift of an isothermal breeze maximum](../../../../radiative-shift-of-an-isothermal-breeze-maximum.md) is

$$
\boxed{r'_M=\frac{GM-\sigma L_*/(4\pi\bar m c)}{2a^2}
=\frac{GM\bar m-\sigma L_*/(4\pi c)}{2k_BT_0}.}
$$

Equivalently, with [opacity](../../../../opacity.md) $\kappa=\sigma/\bar m$ and an [Eddington luminosity](../../../../eddington-luminosity.md) $L_{\rm Edd}=4\pi GMc/\kappa$, this is $r'_M=r_M(1-L_*/L_{\rm Edd})$. It is an exterior speed maximum only if the radius is positive and lies within the modeled wind region. For $L_*\geq L_{\rm Edd}$, the wind equation's right side is positive at every $r>0$, so an always-subsonic outflow decreases in speed and has no such interior maximum.

Because $\sigma$ is a cross section **per particle**, its conversion to acceleration requires $\bar m$; that mass cannot be omitted dimensionally. The requested dependence on the four stated variables therefore assumes a fixed particle species. For the atomic hydrogen of part (e), set $\bar m\simeq m_H$ before molecule formation.
