<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

An [accretion disk](../../../../accretion-disk.md) forms when infalling material has enough [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) to orbit before it can reach the central object. In a thin disc dominated by a central [mass](../../../../mass.md) $M$, the orbital motion is approximately [Keplerian rotation](../../../../keplerian-disk.md):

$$
\Omega_K=\sqrt{\frac{GM}{R^3}},\qquad
\ell=R^2\Omega_K=\sqrt{GMR}.
$$

Vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) gives the [disk scale height](../../../../disk-scale-height.md) $H\sim c_s/\Omega_K$; a geometrically thin disc has $H/R\ll1$. The [surface density](../../../../surface-density-of-a-disk.md) is $\Sigma=\int\rho\,dz$, and a steady inward [mass flow rate](../../../../mass-flow-rate.md) is $\dot M=-2\pi R\Sigma v_R>0$.

Moving inward requires losing specific [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md). Internal stresses transport [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) outward; much of the mass can then accrete while an outer fraction spreads to larger radius. Molecular [viscosity](../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) alone is generally inadequate for rapid astrophysical accretion. Turbulent and magnetic stresses, including those generated by the [magnetorotational instability](../../../../magnetorotational-instability.md), provide effective transport. A common parametrization is $\nu=\alpha c_sH$ for an effective [kinematic viscosity](../../../../kinematic-viscosity.md), with $\alpha$ a dimensionless stress parameter.

For a thin [Keplerian accretion disk](../../../../keplerian-accretion-disk.md), the evolution equation is

$$
\frac{\partial\Sigma}{\partial t}
=\frac3R\frac\partial{\partial R}\left[R^{1/2}\frac\partial{\partial R}
\left(\nu\Sigma R^{1/2}\right)\right].
$$

This [viscous evolution of an accretion disk](../../../../viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk.md) occurs on the [viscous timescale](../../../../viscous-timescale.md) $t_\nu\sim R^2/\nu$, much longer than an orbital period in a thin disc with moderate effective stress.

The orbital specific [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is $-GM/(2R)$, so inward motion releases binding energy. In a steady, optically thick, radiatively efficient disc with zero torque at its inner edge, the emergent flux from **each face** is

$$
\boxed{F(R)=\sigma_{\rm SB}T_{\rm eff}^4
=\frac{3GM\dot M}{8\pi R^3}\left[1-\left(\frac{R_{\rm in}}R\right)^{1/2}\right].}
$$

The [Stefan–Boltzmann law](../../../../stefan-boltzmann-law.md) identifies the local effective [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md). Far from the inner boundary, $T_{\rm eff}\propto R^{-3/4}$, so the emitted spectrum combines hotter inner and cooler outer annuli. Integrating both faces gives $L_{\rm disc}=GM\dot M/(2R_{\rm in})$ under these assumptions. This is not a universal spectrum or efficiency: optical depth, inner torque, energy advection and outflows can change the radiation. The essential connection is **outward angular-momentum transport, inward accretion, and release of binding energy**.
