<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In an adiabatic outflow without additional heating, the reservoir's [specific enthalpy](../../../../../specific-enthalpy.md) supplies both escaping [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) and the work against [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md). For a monatomic [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md), $\gamma=5/3$ and $h=c_s^2/(\gamma-1)=3c_s^2/2$, where $c_s$ is the reservoir's [adiabatic sound speed](../../../../../adiabatic-sound-speed.md). The [Bernoulli equation](../../../../../bernoulli-equation.md), measured from a reservoir initially at rest at radius $r$, gives

$$
\frac{v_\infty^2}{2}+h_\infty
=\frac{c_s^2}{\gamma-1}-\frac{GM}{r}.
$$

The maximum terminal speed uses all available [specific enthalpy](../../../../../specific-enthalpy.md), so $h_\infty\to0$ and

$$
\boxed{v_{\infty,\max}=\sqrt{3c_s^2-\frac{2GM}{r}}.}
$$

A freely escaping adiabatic flow needs a nonnegative radicand; equality is marginal escape. If the radicand is negative, this reservoir cannot supply an unheated escaping outflow. The initial rest condition is a stagnation-reservoir approximation: an exactly stationary finite-density surface cannot simultaneously carry a nonzero steady [mass flow rate](../../../../../mass-flow-rate.md). Further heating, radiation [forces](../../../../../force-split.md) or an imposed confining [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) would change the terminal-energy budget.
