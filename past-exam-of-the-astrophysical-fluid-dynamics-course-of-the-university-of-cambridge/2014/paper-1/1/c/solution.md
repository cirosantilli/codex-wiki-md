<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Once swept-up matter dominates the ejecta and [radiative cooling](../../../../../radiative-cooling.md) is negligible, the [Sedov-Taylor blast wave](../../../../../sedov-taylor-blast-wave.md) has its [shock](../../../../../shock-wave.md) radius fixed by [dimensional analysis](../../../../../dimensional-analysis.md):

$$
R_s=\xi\left(\frac{Et^2}{\rho_0}\right)^{1/5}.
$$

Here $\xi$ is a dimensionless constant of order unity fixed by the [adiabatic index](../../../../../heat-capacity-ratio.md). Treating the ambient matter as [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) gives $\rho_0\simeq10^6m_H\simeq1.67\times10^{-21}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, while $t\simeq3.16\times10^{10}\,\mathrm s$. Thus

$$
\boxed{R_s\simeq1.43\times10^{17}\,\mathrm m\simeq4.6\,\mathrm{pc}\quad(\xi\simeq1).}
$$

For a monatomic gas, the usual similarity normalization $\xi\simeq1.15$ instead gives about $5.3\,\mathrm{pc}$. An estimate of a few [parsecs](../../../../../parsec.md) is therefore appropriate. Negligible ejecta [mass](../../../../../mass.md) does not by itself guarantee this energy-conserving stage: substantial radiation losses or a strongly nonuniform ambient [mass density](../../../../../density.md) would invalidate the assumed similarity law.
