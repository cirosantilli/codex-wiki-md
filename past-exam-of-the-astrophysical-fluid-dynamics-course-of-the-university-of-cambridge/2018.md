<h5 id="section-a/a1/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#section-a/a1/a)

Take positive $\dot Q$ to mean net removal of thermal [energy](../energy.md) per unit mass. The [isobaric thermal stability of a heated ideal gas](../astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#isobaric-thermal-stability-of-a-heated-ideal-gas) depends on the density change accompanying a temperature disturbance. For an [ideal gas](../ideal-gas.md) of fixed composition, $p=\rho k_BT/\bar m$, so constant [pressure](../pressure.md) gives $\rho T=\text{constant}$. Therefore the net cooling can be written

$$
\mathcal L(T)=CT^{\alpha-1}-H,
$$

where $C>0$ is constant for this pressure. At thermal equilibrium $T=T_0$, $CT_0^{\alpha-1}=H$.

The [first law of thermodynamics](../first-law-of-thermodynamics.md) at constant pressure includes the expansion work, so the relevant heat capacity is the [specific heat capacity at constant pressure](../heat.md#specific-heat-capacity-at-constant-pressure), $c_p$, rather than the constant-volume value. Linearizing its temperature equation gives

$$
c_p\frac{d\,\delta T}{dt}
=-\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial T}\right)_p\delta T
=-\frac{(\alpha-1)H}{T_0}\delta T.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{\delta T\propto e^{st},\qquad
s=\frac{(1-\alpha)H}{c_pT_0}.}
$$

For optically thin [Bremsstrahlung](../electromagnetic-radiation.md#bremsstrahlung), $\alpha=1/2$, so

$$
\boxed{s=\frac{H}{2c_pT_0}>0:\quad\text{the equilibrium is thermally unstable}.}
$$

A hotter parcel expands and becomes less dense. Its cooling per unit mass, proportional to $T^{-1/2}$ at fixed pressure, decreases while its [cosmic ray](../galaxy.md#cosmic-ray) heating stays fixed, making it hotter still. A colder, denser parcel cools faster and runs away in the opposite direction. Here $H$ has units $\mathrm{W\,kg^{-1}}$ and $c_pT_0$ units $\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$, so $s$ is an inverse time. The conclusion assumes the disturbance remains close to pressure equilibrium and neglects [heat conduction](../heat.md#thermal-conduction) and changes of composition. The relevant $\alpha$ is the temperature exponent in the supplied cooling law.

↑ **Parent:** [A1](#section-a/a1)

<h5 id="section-a/a1/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-a/a1/b)

Use the nonrelativistic moving-conductor Ohm law in the form

$$
\mathbf E=\frac{\mathbf j}{\sigma}-\mathbf u\times\mathbf B.
$$

The [Maxwell equations](../maxwell-equations.md) give [Faraday's law of induction](../maxwell-equations.md#faraday-s-law-of-induction), $\partial_t\mathbf B=-\nabla\times\mathbf E$. For a nonmagnetic conductor with constant permeability $\mu_0$, the quasistatic [Ampere-Maxwell law](../maxwell-equations.md#ampere-s-circuital-law) gives $\mathbf j\simeq(\nabla\times\mathbf B)/\mu_0$. This step neglects [displacement current](../maxwell-equations.md#displacement-current), requiring it to be small compared with conduction current; nonrelativistic velocity alone is not the complete justification.

For spatially uniform scalar [electrical conductivity](../electric-charge.md#electrical-conductivity) $\sigma$,

$$
\partial_t\mathbf B
=\nabla\times(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B)
-\frac{1}{\mu_0\sigma}\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf B).
$$

Apply the identity $\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf B)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B)-\nabla^2\mathbf B$ and [Gauss's law for magnetism](../maxwell-equations.md#gauss-s-law-for-magnetism), $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$. The [resistive induction equation](../resistive-magnetohydrodynamics.md#resistive-induction-equation) is

$$
\boxed{\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t}
\simeq\nabla\times(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B)
+\eta\nabla^2\mathbf B,\qquad
\eta=\frac1{\mu_0\sigma}.}
$$

The first term transports and deforms the [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md) with the moving fluid; the second diffuses it. The [magnetic diffusivity](../magnetohydrodynamics.md#magnetic-diffusivity) $\eta$ has units $\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$, so both terms have units tesla per second. No assumption of [incompressible flow](../fluid-mechanics.md#incompressible-flow) was needed. If $\sigma$ varies spatially, the [conductivity-gradient term in magnetic induction](../resistive-magnetohydrodynamics.md#conductivity-gradient-term-in-magnetic-induction), $-\nabla\eta\times(\nabla\times\mathbf B)$, must also be retained.

<h5 id="section-a/a1/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#section-a/a1/c)

For a plane-parallel slab, all equilibrium quantities depend only on height $z$. The vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](../statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) equation and the [Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#poisson-equation-for-newtonian-gravity) are

$$
\frac{dp}{dz}=-\rho\frac{d\psi}{dz},\qquad
\frac{d^2\psi}{dz^2}=4\pi G\rho.
$$

With the [polytropic equation of state](../astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#polytropic-equation-of-state) $p=K\rho^2$ and constant $K$,

$$
2K\rho\frac{d\rho}{dz}=-\rho\frac{d\psi}{dz}.
$$

In the gas interior, where $\rho>0$, divide by $\rho$ and differentiate once:

$$
2K\frac{d^2\rho}{dz^2}=-\frac{d^2\psi}{dz^2}=-4\pi G\rho.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2\rho}{dz^2}+\frac{2\pi G}{K}\rho=0.}
$$

The coefficient $2\pi G/K$ has units inverse square metres. The [index-one plane-parallel polytrope](../astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#index-one-plane-parallel-polytrope) illustrates the resulting structure: symmetry about $z=0$ gives $\rho(z)=\rho_c\cos(kz)$, with $k=\sqrt{2\pi G/K}$. A slab bounded by vacuum ends at its first zero, $|z|=\pi/(2k)$; the negative continuation of the cosine is not physical [mass density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density).

<h4 id="section-b/b2/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B2](#section-b/b2)

**Supersonic communication.** A point source moving at speed $V>c_s$ outruns its small [acoustic waves](../fluid-mechanics.md#acoustic-wave). After a time $t$, the source has moved $Vt$, while a disturbance emitted at its old position has radius $c_st$. The envelope of these spherical wavefronts is the [Mach cone](../compressible-flow.md#mach-cone), with half-angle $\mu$ satisfying

$$
\boxed{\sin\mu=\frac{c_s}{V}=\frac1{\mathcal M},\qquad
\mathcal M=\frac{V}{c_s}>1.}
$$

Information is restricted to the downstream cone, so upstream gas cannot smoothly anticipate a supersonic obstacle. Finite compressions can also steepen because their characteristics converge. Such disturbances form a [shock wave](../transport-equation.md#shock-wave), across which the fluid variables change rapidly. A perfectly uniform [supersonic flow](../compressible-flow.md#supersonic-flow) need not contain a shock: the cone expresses the communication constraint, while a disturbance or compression supplies the reason for a discontinuity.

**Conserved fluxes.** Work in the [shock frame](../compressible-flow.md#shock-frame), take velocities normal to the front, and integrate the conservation laws through a thin steady [control volume](../fluid-mechanics.md#control-volume) of unit area. [Mass conservation](../continuum-mechanics.md#mass-conservation) gives constant flux $j=\rho u$, so

$$
\boxed{\rho_2u_2=\rho_1u_1=j.}
$$

The normal [momentum](../momentum.md) flux is the advected part $\rho u^2$ plus the pressure stress $p$. With negligible body-force impulse across the thin front,

$$
\boxed{p_2+\rho_2u_2^2=p_1+\rho_1u_1^2.}
$$

For an [ideal gas](../ideal-gas.md) with constant [adiabatic index](../ideal-gas.md#heat-capacity-ratio) $\gamma$, the [specific internal energy](../internal-energy.md#specific-internal-energy) is $e=p/[(\gamma-1)\rho]$. The energy flux is

$$
j\left(e+\frac12u^2\right)+pu
=j\left(h_{\rm sp}+\frac12u^2\right),
\qquad h_{\rm sp}=e+\frac p\rho=\frac{\gamma p}{(\gamma-1)\rho},
$$

where $h_{\rm sp}$ is the [specific enthalpy](../enthalpy.md#specific-enthalpy). No radiation or other heat flux escapes in this adiabatic shock model. Conservation of the energy flux, divided by the nonzero mass flux, gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{\gamma p_2}{(\gamma-1)\rho_2}+\frac12u_2^2
=\frac{\gamma p_1}{(\gamma-1)\rho_1}+\frac12u_1^2.}
$$

These are the [Rankine-Hugoniot conditions for a perfect gas](../compressible-flow.md#rankine-hugoniot-conditions-for-a-perfect-gas). The shock is adiabatic but irreversible: its [entropy](../entropy.md) increases, so an isentropic relation cannot be imposed across the discontinuity.

**Strong-shock compression.** Put $\chi=\rho_2/\rho_1=u_1/u_2$ and neglect $p_1$ and the upstream enthalpy compared with $\rho_1u_1^2$ and $u_1^2$. Momentum conservation gives $p_2=\rho_1u_1^2(1-1/\chi)$. Substitute this into energy conservation:

$$
\frac12=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\frac{\chi-1}{\chi^2}+\frac1{2\chi^2}.
$$

Thus

$$
\chi^2-1=\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}(\chi-1),
\qquad
(\chi-1)\left(\chi+1-\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}\right)=0.
$$

The root $\chi=1$ describes no shock. The compressive branch of the [Strong-shock Rankine-Hugoniot conditions](../compressible-flow.md#strong-shock-rankine-hugoniot-conditions) is

$$
\boxed{\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}=\frac{u_1}{u_2}
=\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}.}
$$

It also gives $p_2=2\rho_1u_1^2/(\gamma+1)$. A [monatomic gas](../ideal-gas.md#monatomic-gas) has $\gamma=5/3$, hence $\chi=4$.

**The finite slab in its initial rest frame.** Denote the shock's lab speed by $V_s$. The upstream gas is initially stationary, so $u_1=V_s$ and the downstream shock-frame speed is $u_2=V_s/4$. Transforming to the initial slab frame gives

$$
\boxed{v_2=V_s-u_2=\frac34V_s,\qquad L_2=\frac L4.}
$$

This is [planar shock compression of a finite slab](../compressible-flow.md#planar-shock-compression-of-a-finite-slab). In the uniform post-shock approximation, breakout occurs at $t_b=L/V_s$. The original front face has then moved $(3V_s/4)t_b=3L/4$, while the shock reaches the originally stationary far face at $L$, leaving thickness $L/4$. Equivalently, the fixed mass per area $\rho L$ becomes $4\rho L_2$. The result assumes a maintained approximately constant shock speed and no important boundary rarefaction during the crossing; an unconfined finite cloud need not remain uniformly compressed.

**Escape estimate and the sound-speed convention.** Let $M_c$ be the slab mass, $W>0$ its binding-energy magnitude in the star's field, and $E_{\rm th}$ its initial [internal energy](../internal-energy.md). Treat the potential as nearly uniform across the slab. For the bulk-ballistic escape estimate, require

$$
E_{\rm bulk}=\frac12M_cv_2^2=\frac9{32}M_cV_s^2\gtrsim W,
\qquad
E_{\rm th}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{M_cp}{\rho}.
$$

The [Mach-number convention in shock unbinding estimates](../compressible-flow.md#mach-number-convention-in-shock-unbinding-estimates) matters here. With the usual upstream [adiabatic sound speed](../compressible-flow.md#adiabatic-sound-speed), $c_{\rm ad}^2=\gamma p/\rho=5p/(3\rho)$, define $\mathcal M_{\rm ad}=V_s/c_{\rm ad}$. Then

$$
\frac{E_{\rm bulk}}{E_{\rm th}}=\frac5{16}\mathcal M_{\rm ad}^2,
\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal M_{\rm ad}^2\gtrsim\frac{16}{5}\frac{W}{E_{\rm th}}.}
$$

If instead the shock speed is normalized by the [isothermal sound speed](../compressible-flow.md#isothermal-sound-speed), $c_T^2=p/\rho$, then $\mathcal M_T^2=(5/3)\mathcal M_{\rm ad}^2$ and

$$
\boxed{\mathcal M_T^2\gtrsim\frac{16}{3}\frac{W}{E_{\rm th}}
\simeq5.3\,\frac{W}{E_{\rm th}}.}
$$

The original PDF's “around five” is consistent with the latter convention or an order-of-magnitude sound-speed estimate. It is not the exact coefficient obtained with the conventional adiabatic Mach number and the stated bulk kinetic-energy criterion. Here $W=-E_{\rm grav}$ if the potential is zero at infinity; using negative gravitational energy directly on the right would be incorrect. Subsequent conversion of shock-generated internal energy to expansion, external pressure, or radiative losses would change the physical escape threshold, so this numerical factor is not universal.

<h4 id="section-b/b3/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B3](#section-b/b3)

**Following a parcel.** Let $\mathbf X(t)$ be a fluid [pathline](../fluid-mechanics.md#pathline), with $\dot{\mathbf X}=\mathbf u(\mathbf X,t)$. The [multivariable chain rule](../calculus.md#multivariable-chain-rule) gives the [material derivative](../continuum-mechanics.md#material-derivative),

$$
\boxed{\frac{DQ}{Dt}=\frac{d}{dt}Q(\mathbf X(t),t)
=\frac{\partial Q}{\partial t}+\mathbf u\cdot\nabla Q.}
$$

The first term measures change at fixed position; the [advection](../fluid-mechanics.md#advection) term accounts for the parcel moving through spatial gradients. The derivative is also called the Lagrangian derivative.

**The forces on a parcel.** A material volume $dV$ has mass $\rho\,dV$. The pressure forces on its boundary sum to $-\nabla p\,dV$, while the [gravitational acceleration](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#gravitational-acceleration) is $-\nabla\psi$. [Newton's second law](../force.md#newton-s-second-law) therefore gives the [Euler equations for an inviscid fluid](../euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid.md),

$$
\boxed{\partial_t\mathbf u+(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u
=-\frac1\rho\nabla p-\nabla\psi.}
$$

This neglects viscosity and other body forces. The left side is the parcel's acceleration, not just the time derivative at a fixed spatial location.

**Bernoulli conservation.** The claimed streamline result needs [steady flow](../fluid-mechanics.md#steady-flow) and a [barotropic fluid](../fluid-mechanics.md#barotropic-fluid), $p=p(\rho)$, or an equivalent enthalpy relation along each isentropic streamline. These hypotheses are necessary; the unsteady Euler equation alone does not imply the stated conservation law. Define the [barotropic enthalpy function](../fluid-mechanics.md#barotropic-enthalpy-function)

$$
h_b(p)=\int_{p_{\rm ref}}^p\frac{dp'}{\rho(p')},
\qquad \nabla h_b=\frac{\nabla p}{\rho}.
$$

Dot the steady Euler equation with $\mathbf u$, using $\mathbf u\cdot[(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u]=\mathbf u\cdot\nabla(u^2/2)$. This gives

$$
\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\left(\frac12u^2+h_b+\psi\right)=0.
$$

Thus the [Bernoulli function](../euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid.md#bernoulli-function) is

$$
\boxed{H=\frac12u^2+\int\frac{dp}{\rho}+\psi
=\text{constant along each streamline}.}
$$

Every term has units of energy per mass, $\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$. Different streamlines can have different constants.

**The spherical accretion equation.** Write the inward speed as $u(r)>0$, so the velocity vector is $-u(r)\widehat{\mathbf r}$ and the positive [mass accretion rate](../astrophysical-black-hole.md#mass-accretion-rate) is

$$
\dot M=4\pi r^2\rho u,\qquad
\frac{d\ln\rho}{dr}=-\frac2r-\frac{d\ln u}{dr}.
$$

Assume a prescribed central potential $\psi=-GM/r$ and negligible gas self-gravity. The steady radial Euler equation is

$$
u\frac{du}{dr}=-\frac1\rho\frac{dp}{dr}-\frac{GM}{r^2}.
$$

For a barotropic equation of state, $dp/dr=c_s^2\,d\rho/dr$, with $c_s^2=dp/d\rho$. Eliminate the density derivative to obtain

$$
\boxed{(u^2-c_s^2)\frac{d\ln u}{dr}
=\frac{2c_s^2}{r}-\frac{GM}{r^2}
=\frac{2c_s^2}{r}\left(1-\frac{GM}{2c_s^2r}\right).}
$$

A smooth [sonic point](../compressible-flow.md#sonic-point) requires the right side to vanish where $u=c_s$, hence

$$
\boxed{r_s=\frac{GM}{2c_s(r_s)^2},\qquad u(r_s)=c_s(r_s).}
$$

For a nonisothermal gas this is an implicit relation involving the local sound speed. In the isothermal case $c_s$ is constant and this is the [Bondi sonic point](../astrophysical-black-hole.md#bondi-sonic-point).

**The isothermal integral.** For $p=c_s^2\rho$, choose the enthalpy reference so that $h_b=c_s^2\ln(\rho/\rho_\infty)$. The reservoir is at rest at infinity and $\psi(\infty)=0$, making its Bernoulli constant zero. Thus

$$
\frac12u^2+c_s^2\ln\frac{\rho}{\rho_\infty}-\frac{GM}{r}=0,
$$

or

$$
\boxed{u^2=2c_s^2\ln\frac{\rho_\infty}{\rho}+\frac{2GM}{r}.}
$$

The logarithm has a dimensionless argument. Constant temperature during compression generally requires heat removal, so this is [Isothermal Bondi accretion](../astrophysical-black-hole.md#isothermal-bondi-accretion), rather than adiabatic accretion with $\gamma>1$.

**The critical accretion rate.** Select the smooth reservoir-connected [transonic branch](../compressible-flow.md#transonic-branch). Evaluate the last relation at $u_s=c_s$ and $GM/r_s=2c_s^2$:

$$
c_s^2=2c_s^2\ln\frac{\rho_\infty}{\rho_s}+4c_s^2,
\qquad
\boxed{\rho_s=e^{3/2}\rho_\infty.}
$$

Mass conservation then gives the [isothermal Bondi accretion rate](../astrophysical-black-hole.md#isothermal-bondi-accretion-rate),

$$
\boxed{\dot M=4\pi r_s^2\rho_sc_s
=\pi e^{3/2}\frac{G^2M^2\rho_\infty}{c_s^3}.}
$$

The units are $\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$. Equivalently $\dot M=4\pi\lambda G^2M^2\rho_\infty/c_s^3$, with $\lambda=e^{3/2}/4\simeq1.12$.

The [inner-boundary selection of isothermal Bondi flow](../astrophysical-black-hole.md#inner-boundary-selection-of-isothermal-bondi-flow) is important: a standard absorbing accretor inside the sonic radius selects this critical solution. The steady equations and $\rho_\infty$ alone also admit subcritical subsonic branches supported by a different inner boundary; they do not by themselves fix a unique rate. The usual answer presumes transonic capture.

**The background and the Jeans prescription.** For a resting fluid, $\mathbf u_0=0$ and continuity is automatic. The remaining [hydrostatic equilibrium](../statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) equations are

$$
\boxed{\nabla p_0=-\rho_0\nabla\psi_0,\qquad
\nabla^2\psi_0=4\pi G\rho_0.}
$$

An infinite uniform static medium with constant $p_0$ and $\rho_0>0$ would require $\nabla\psi_0=0$ by force balance, but then its Laplacian would vanish, contradicting the Poisson equation. An isolated static uniform background is therefore not an exact equilibrium of this system.

The [Jeans swindle](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#jeans-swindle) suppresses the gravitational field of the uniform background and retains only the field sourced by perturbations. Formally it replaces the source by $\rho-\rho_0$ in the perturbation problem. This can be regarded as a specified compensating background or a local idealization; it does not prove that the original infinite isolated medium can be static.

**Linearized equations.** Put $\delta\rho=\Delta\rho$, $\delta p=\Delta p$, $\delta\psi=\Delta\psi$, and $\mathbf v=\Delta\mathbf u$. For uniform $\rho_0,p_0$, neglect products of perturbations. Continuity gives

$$
\boxed{\partial_t\delta\rho+\rho_0\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0.}
$$

The advective acceleration is quadratic, and the background pressure gradient vanishes, so the Euler equation becomes

$$
\boxed{\partial_t\mathbf v=-\frac1{\rho_0}\nabla\delta p-\nabla\delta\psi.}
$$

With the Jeans prescription and constant [sound speed](../compressible-flow.md#speed-of-sound), the gravitational and pressure closures are

$$
\boxed{\nabla^2\delta\psi=4\pi G\delta\rho,\qquad
\delta p=c_s^2\delta\rho.}
$$

The density correction to $1/\rho$ multiplying $\nabla\delta p$ is second order and is consistently omitted.

**The longitudinal dispersion relation.** Differentiate continuity in time and substitute the divergence of the linearized Euler equation:

$$
\partial_t^2\delta\rho
=\nabla^2\delta p+\rho_0\nabla^2\delta\psi
=c_s^2\nabla^2\delta\rho+4\pi G\rho_0\delta\rho.
$$

For a [plane wave](../wave-function.md#plane-wave) proportional to $e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x-\omega t)}$, $\partial_t^2\mapsto-\omega^2$ and $\nabla^2\mapsto-k^2$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\omega^2=c_s^2k^2-4\pi G\rho_0
=c_s^2(k^2-k_J^2),\qquad
k_J=\frac{\sqrt{4\pi G\rho_0}}{c_s}.}
$$

The physical [Jeans length](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#jeans-length) is

$$
\boxed{\ell_J=\frac{2\pi}{k_J}
=c_s\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{G\rho_0}}.}
$$

Since $G\rho_0$ has units inverse square seconds, $k_J$ has units $\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ and $\ell_J$ metres. This is the compressive density branch of the [Jeans instability](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#jeans-instability); transverse velocity disturbances have no pressure or gravity restoring force in this static nonrotating model and are neutral at linear order.

**Which scales grow.** For $0<k<k_J$, or wavelength $\ell>\ell_J$, $\omega^2<0$ and one temporal solution grows exponentially. The [static Jeans growth-rate spectrum](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#static-jeans-growth-rate-spectrum) is

$$
\boxed{s(k)=\sqrt{4\pi G\rho_0-c_s^2k^2},\qquad
\delta\rho\propto e^{s(k)t}.}
$$

Its derivative is negative for $0<k<k_J$, so longer wavelengths grow faster. The supremum is $s\to\sqrt{4\pi G\rho_0}$ as $k\to0^+$: there is no selected finite fastest wavelength in this infinite-medium calculation. The exactly uniform $k=0$ density change is not an ordinary finite-potential Fourier perturbation with the fixed background subtraction. In a finite cloud, boundaries set the longest allowed scale.

For $k>k_J$, the density perturbation oscillates as a stable [acoustic wave](../fluid-mechanics.md#acoustic-wave). At $k=k_J$ the linear equation is $\partial_t^2\delta\rho=0$, so the mode is marginal rather than exponentially growing.

**Comparing acoustic and gravitational times.** A sound-crossing estimate for a region of size $\ell$ is $t_{\rm sound}\sim\ell/c_s$. Self-gravitational acceleration scales as $G\rho_0\ell$, so collapse over a distance $\ell$ takes $t_{\rm grav}\sim(G\rho_0)^{-1/2}$. Equating these scales gives

$$
\ell\sim\frac{c_s}{\sqrt{G\rho_0}},
$$

which agrees with the Jeans length up to its numerical factor $\sqrt\pi$. This is the physical competition between pressure communication and gravitational contraction.

The constants can be checked using the [free-fall time of a uniform sphere](../astrophysics.md#free-fall-time-of-a-uniform-sphere). For a pressureless shell initially at radius $a$, enclosing fixed mass $M_a$, energy conservation gives $\dot r^2=2GM_a(1/r-1/a)$. Thus

$$
t_{\rm ff}=\int_0^a\frac{dr}{\sqrt{2GM_a(1/r-1/a)}}
=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt2}\sqrt{\frac{a^3}{GM_a}}
=\sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{32G\rho_0}},
$$

where $M_a=4\pi\rho_0a^3/3$. Consequently the [Jeans length and gravitational time conventions](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#jeans-length-and-gravitational-time-conventions) satisfy

$$
\boxed{\frac{\ell_J}{c_s}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{G\rho_0}}
=\sqrt{\frac{32}{3}}\,t_{\rm ff}\simeq3.27\,t_{\rm ff}.}
$$

They agree as characteristic scales, not as identical exact times. Alternatively, using the length $k_J^{-1}$ gives $1/(c_sk_J)=1/\sqrt{4\pi G\rho_0}$, exactly the gravitational inverse frequency entering the dispersion relation. The actual growth time of a marginal Jeans mode is infinite because pressure and gravity cancel there; it should not be confused with a pressureless free-fall time.
