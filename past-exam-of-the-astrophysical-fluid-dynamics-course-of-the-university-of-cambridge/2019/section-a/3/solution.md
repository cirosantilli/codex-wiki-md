<h1 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

For an inverted [free surface](../../../../free-surface.md), [gravity](../../../../gravity.md) promotes a displacement instead of restoring it. The [surface tension](../../../../surface-tension-split.md) term still resists curvature. Hence the [gravity-capillary wave](../../../../gravity-capillary-wave.md) relation becomes, with $q=|k|$,

$$
\boxed{\omega^2=q\left(\frac{\sigma q^2}{\rho}-g\right).}
$$

For $0<q<k_c$, where $k_c=\sqrt{\rho g/\sigma}$, $\omega^2<0$. Writing the displacement as $\eta\propto e^{st+ikx}$ gives the [Rayleigh-Taylor instability](../../../../rayleigh-taylor-instability.md) growth rate

$$
s=\sqrt{gq-\frac{\sigma q^3}{\rho}},\qquad 0<q<k_c.
$$

Shorter [wavelengths](../../../../wavelength.md), $q>k_c$, remain oscillatory because [surface tension](../../../../surface-tension-split.md) dominates. The marginal scale is determined by the [capillary length](../../../../capillary-length.md),

$$
\ell_c=\sqrt{\frac{\sigma}{\rho g}}
=\sqrt{\frac{7.2\times10^{-2}}{1000(9.81)}}\ \mathrm m
\simeq2.71\ \mathrm{mm},
\qquad
\boxed{\lambda_c=2\pi\ell_c\simeq1.70\ \mathrm{cm}.}
$$

A sufficiently small aperture excludes the long unstable modes: this is [finite-aperture stabilization of an inverted liquid surface](../../../../finite-aperture-stabilization-of-an-inverted-liquid-surface.md). The estimate requested is therefore **a container opening of order one centimetre**, with $1.7\ \mathrm{cm}$ the marginal full wavelength rather than a geometry-independent maximum diameter.

The exact largest opening cannot be fixed from the stated infinite-surface [dispersion relation](../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) alone. It requires the aperture shape and contact-line [boundary conditions](../../../../boundary-condition.md). For example, a one-dimensional span of width $L$ whose [contact line](../../../../contact-line.md) is pinned at both ends has a half-sine lowest mode with $q_{\min}=\pi/L$. In that simple model,

$$
\boxed{L_{\rm crit}=\pi\ell_c\simeq8.5\ \mathrm{mm},\qquad
L<L_{\rm crit}\ \text{excludes growing modes}.}
$$

Different geometries change the numerical factor. This is a small-disturbance stability estimate with negligible air inertia and no leakage or contact-line failure; a guarantee that a real inverted container retains its water needs those additional boundary details.
