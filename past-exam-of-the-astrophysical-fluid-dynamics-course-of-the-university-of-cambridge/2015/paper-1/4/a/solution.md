<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a uniform sphere, $M=4\pi\rho R^3/3$ and its [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../gravitational-energy.md) is $\Omega=-3GM^2/(5R)$. The ideal-gas [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is $E_{\rm kin}=3Mk_BT/(2\bar m)$. Equating these magnitudes using the [virial theorem](../../../../../virial-theorem.md) gives $GM/R=5k_BT/\bar m$. Eliminating $R$ yields the [uniform-sphere pressure-work collapse criterion](../../../../../uniform-sphere-pressure-work-collapse-criterion.md)

$$
M_{\rm crit}=\left(\frac{5k_BT}{G\bar m}\right)^{3/2}
\left(\frac3{4\pi\rho}\right)^{1/2}.
$$

In particular, for fixed composition,

$$
\boxed{M_J\propto T^{3/2}\rho^{-1/2}.}
$$

The same [Jeans mass](../../../../../jeans-mass.md) scaling follows from $c_s^2k^2\sim4\pi G\rho$ in the [Jeans instability](../../../../../jeans-instability.md) dispersion relation and a characteristic size $k^{-1}$. Pressure communicates support in a sound-crossing time; sufficiently strong self-gravity wins when the gravitational time is shorter.

The numerical coefficient depends on the equilibrium, boundary and wavelength-to-cloud conventions. A sphere with both exactly uniform density and uniform temperature is not an exact hydrostatic self-gravitating ideal-gas equilibrium without additional stresses. The uniform-sphere argument establishes the characteristic mass scaling; it does not prove an exact stability boundary for that nonexistent equilibrium.
