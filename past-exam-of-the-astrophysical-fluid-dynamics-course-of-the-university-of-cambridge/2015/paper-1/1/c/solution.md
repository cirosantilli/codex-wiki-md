<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Work in an inertial [centre of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md) frame and write $I=\sum_i m_i|\mathbf r_i|^2$. Differentiation and [Newton's second law](../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) give

$$
\frac12\ddot I=2T+\sum_i\mathbf r_i\cdot\mathbf F_i.
$$

The pairwise Newtonian [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../gravitational-energy.md) is

$$
\Omega=-\sum_{i<j}\frac{Gm_im_j}{|\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_j|}.
$$

For each pair, combining the two force contributions gives $-Gm_im_j/|\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_j|$. Therefore $\sum_i\mathbf r_i\cdot\mathbf F_i=\Omega$, and

$$
\frac12\ddot I=2T+\Omega.
$$

For a stationary configuration, or after a long-time average of a bounded system whose endpoint term vanishes, the [virial theorem](../../../../../virial-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{2\langle T\rangle+\langle\Omega\rangle=0.}
$$

The brackets may be suppressed when $T,\Omega$ denote equilibrium averages. A collapsing cloud or an eccentric [Kepler orbit](../../../../../kepler-orbit.md) need not obey $2T+\Omega=0$ instantaneously. Confining surface pressure, magnetic stress or other external forces add their own virial terms; the displayed result concerns an isolated gravitational particle system.
