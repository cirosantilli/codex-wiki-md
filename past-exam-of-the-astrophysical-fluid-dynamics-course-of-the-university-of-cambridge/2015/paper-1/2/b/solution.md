<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For steady inviscid motion of a [barotropic fluid](../../../../../barotropic-fluid.md) in a conservative [gravitational potential](../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md) $\Phi$, the [Euler equations for an inviscid fluid](../../../../../euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid-split.md) imply the [Bernoulli equation](../../../../../bernoulli-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{\frac{v^2}{2}+h+\Phi=B,\qquad dh=\frac{dp}{\rho},}
$$

along each streamline. The [barotropic enthalpy function](../../../../../barotropic-enthalpy-function.md) $h$ has units of energy per unit mass. For an isentropic [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md), $p=K\rho^\gamma$ gives $h=\gamma p/[(\gamma-1)\rho]$ up to an additive constant. For constant density, the corresponding term is $p/\rho$. Pressure work converts between kinetic, enthalpy and gravitational energy.

A converging subsonic nozzle can accelerate a fluid at the expense of its enthalpy; in the incompressible horizontal approximation, higher speed accompanies lower static [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). Together with [conservation of mass](../../../../../mass-conservation.md), this explains a [Venturi effect](../../../../../venturi-effect.md) measurement. Compressible nozzle flow additionally needs its equation of state and sonic conditions. In a stellar wind, the potential term is $-GM/r$, so enthalpy or mechanical energy can be spent escaping gravity. A pressure-negligible marginal-escape wind has $v^2/2-GM/r=0$.

The constant need not be the same on different streamlines. Irrotational barotropic flow in a connected region gives a common value; a general rotational flow does not. A pump, viscosity, a radiating shock, or heat addition changes the relevant energy balance. In particular, adiabaticity alone does not allow one to drop the enthalpy term.
