<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

[Hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) and dimensional scaling give characteristic stellar pressure $p\sim GM^2/R^4$ and [mass density](../../../../../density.md) $\rho\sim M/R^3$. For a fixed-index [stellar polytrope](../../../../../stellar-polytrope.md) with $p=K\rho^{1+1/n}$, eliminating density gives the [polytropic mass-radius relation](../../../../../polytropic-mass-radius-relation.md)

$$
\boxed{R\propto\left(\frac KG\right)^{n/(3-n)}M^{(1-n)/(3-n)},\qquad n\ne3.}
$$

The dimensionless coefficient is determined by the corresponding [Lane-Emden equation](../../../../../lane-emden-equation.md) and boundary conditions. Both the index and $K$ must be held fixed to infer this mass dependence.

Nonrelativistic electron degeneracy has $n=3/2$, hence **$R\propto M^{-1/3}$** for cold fixed-composition [white dwarfs](../../../../../white-dwarf.md): adding mass makes the object smaller. Relativistic electron degeneracy approaches $n=3$; the mass scale becomes proportional to $(K/G)^{3/2}$ independently of the radius, giving the ideal [Chandrasekhar limit](../../../../../chandrasekhar-limit.md). The limiting model signals loss of the ordinary stable cold sequence; it does not determine a unique radius at that mass.

For a gas-pressure-supported star, the [virial theorem](../../../../../virial-theorem.md) instead gives characteristic temperature $k_BT\sim G\bar mM/R$. Specifying a common characteristic temperature gives $R\propto M$, but stellar temperatures are not generally identical. A [main-sequence star](../../../../../main-sequence-star.md) also requires nuclear energy generation and radiative or convective transport. Consequently there is no universal mass-radius exponent applicable to every main-sequence mass and composition. An isentropic convective model and a cold degeneracy-supported model can share index $3/2$ while having quite different physical meanings and values of $K$.
