<h1 id="section-b/4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $U_1,U_2$ denote the total flow speeds, keeping the normal components distinct from them. The original diagram measures $\beta$ from the initial wall direction to the [shock wave](../../../../../shock-wave.md) itself. The outgoing [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) is turned by $\theta$, so it makes angle $\beta-\theta$ with the shock.

Resolve each [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) tangent and normal to the [oblique shock](../../../../../oblique-shock.md). Tangential [momentum conservation](../../../../../momentum-conservation.md), with nonzero mass flux and no tangential surface force, makes the tangential [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) continuous:

$$
U_1\cos\beta=U_2\cos(\beta-\theta).
$$

The normal flow obeys the strong [normal shock wave](../../../../../normal-shock-wave.md) result from part (b):

$$
U_2\sin(\beta-\theta)=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}U_1\sin\beta.
$$

Divide the second relation by the first to obtain

$$
\boxed{\tan\beta=\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}\tan(\beta-\theta).}
$$

The approximation is controlled by the upstream normal [Mach number](../../../../../mach-number.md) $M\sin\beta\gg1$, not just by $M\gg1$. This distinction matters on the small-angle weak branch in part (d). The angle convention is also important: formulas using the angle to the shock normal instead would interchange sine and cosine.
