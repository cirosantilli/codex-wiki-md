<h1 id="section-b/6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a steady axisymmetric flow $\mathbf u_0=r\Omega(r)\widehat{\boldsymbol\phi}$, [incompressible flow](../../../../../incompressible-flow.md) is automatic. The only material [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) is centripetal, $-r\Omega^2\widehat{\mathbf r}$. The radial [Euler equations](../../../../../euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid-split.md) therefore require

$$
\boxed{\frac{dp_0}{dr}=\rho r\Omega(r)^2.}
$$

With no [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) there is no tangential shear [stress](../../../../../stress.md) to redistribute [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md). Any sufficiently regular $\Omega(r)$ can therefore be a steady inviscid profile, with the radial [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) adjusted by integrating this equation. This is a statement of equilibrium, not of stability: some such profiles are centrifugally unstable.
