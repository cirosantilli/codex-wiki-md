<h1 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

A highly conducting [solar wind](../../../../solar-wind.md) carries the [Sun](../../../../sun.md)'s [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) outward by [magnetic flux freezing](../../../../magnetic-flux-freezing.md). This can open [magnetic field lines](../../../../magnetic-field-line.md) that would form closed loops in a static dipole model. Beyond an opening radius $r_o$, approximate the field as radial. Constancy of the [magnetic flux](../../../../magnetic-flux.md) in a fixed solid-angle [flux tube](../../../../flux-tube.md) gives the [radial solar-wind magnetic flux](../../../../radial-solar-wind-magnetic-flux.md) law

$$
B_r(r)r^2\simeq\text{constant},\qquad B_r\propto r^{-2},
$$

which decays more slowly than the vacuum [magnetic dipole field](../../../../magnetic-dipole-field.md), proportional to $r^{-3}$.

For example, if the field becomes radial already near the solar radius $R_\odot$, normalizing both estimates to the same field there gives

$$
\frac{B_{\rm wind}(r_E)}{B_{\rm dip}(r_E)}\sim\frac{r_E}{R_\odot}
=\frac1{0.005}=200.
$$

Thus a dipole estimate $10^{-11}\ \mathrm T$ becomes of order $2\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm T$, comparable to the stated hundredfold enhancement. More generally, matching dipolar decay inside $r_o$ to radial decay outside gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{B_{\rm wind}(r_E)}{B_{\rm dip}(r_E)}\sim\frac{r_E}{r_o};
\quad r_o\sim2R_\odot\ \Rightarrow\ B(r_E)\sim10^{-9}\ \mathrm T.}
$$

This is a plausible scaling explanation, not a determination of the actual opening radius. The magnetic field need not be outward everywhere: opposite-polarity sectors can preserve zero net [magnetic flux](../../../../magnetic-flux.md) through a closed [surface](../../../../topological-surface-split.md), consistent with [Gauss's law for magnetism](../../../../gauss-s-law-for-magnetism.md). The argument concerns the changed geometry of the field in a flowing [plasma](../../../../plasma-physics.md), rather than changing the dipole exponent by assumption.
