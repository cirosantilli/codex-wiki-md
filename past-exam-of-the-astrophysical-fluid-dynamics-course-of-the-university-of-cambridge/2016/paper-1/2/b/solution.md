<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A finite-amplitude compression can steepen because different parts of the wave travel at different characteristic speeds. For a right-moving [simple wave](../../../../../simple-wave.md) in a [perfect gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md), one may write $dx/dt=c_0+(\gamma+1)u/2$ in terms of the local [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) perturbation $u$. Faster compressive characteristics catch slower ones. When the smooth inviscid description would become multivalued, a physical compression shock replaces it. A rarefaction normally spreads rather than forming an entropy-violating expansion shock.[§§6.1.3–6.2, simple-wave reduction and characteristic crossing](../../../../../lecture-notes-astrophysical-fluid-dynamics-by-gordon-i-ogilvie.md)

Across a locally planar shock, use [velocities](../../../../../velocity.md) relative to its moving front. The [Rankine-Hugoniot conditions for a perfect gas](../../../../../rankine-hugoniot-conditions-for-a-perfect-gas.md) conserve [mass](../../../../../mass.md), momentum and total [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) flux:

$$
\rho_1u_1=\rho_2u_2=j,\qquad p_1+\rho_1u_1^2=p_2+\rho_2u_2^2,\qquad h_1+\frac12u_1^2=h_2+\frac12u_2^2,
$$

where $h=\gamma p/[(\gamma-1)\rho]$ is the [specific enthalpy](../../../../../specific-enthalpy.md). For upstream normal [Mach number](../../../../../mach-number.md) $M_1>1$,

$$
\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}=\frac{(\gamma+1)M_1^2}{2+(\gamma-1)M_1^2},\quad\frac{p_2}{p_1}=\frac{2\gamma M_1^2-(\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1},\quad M_2^2=\frac{1+(\gamma-1)M_1^2/2}{\gamma M_1^2-(\gamma-1)/2}.
$$

Thus a normal compression shock increases [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md), density and [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), reduces the normal [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md), and changes a supersonic upstream flow to subsonic downstream flow in the [shock frame](../../../../../shock-frame.md). In the strong-shock limit, compression approaches $(\gamma+1)/(\gamma-1)$, which is four for a [monatomic gas](../../../../../monatomic-gas.md). **A shock conserves total [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) but generates [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md); an adiabatic shock is not an isentropic compression.** For a calorically [perfect gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md), [stagnation temperature](../../../../../stagnation-temperature.md) is conserved while [stagnation pressure](../../../../../stagnation-pressure.md) falls.[normal-shock property ratios, total-temperature conservation and total-pressure loss](../../../../../normal-shock-wave-equations-by-nasa-glenn-research-center.md)

Real dissipative processes resolve the narrow front; the jump conditions do not require its detailed internal profile. In a continuum-gas experiment a [shock tube](../../../../../shock-tube.md) can produce a moving shock by releasing a [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) discontinuity. [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) probes separated along the tube measure arrival speed and the [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) jump; distinguishing the shock from the following contact surface is essential. A contact surface can have a density/[entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) jump with continuous [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) and normal [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md), whereas a shock carries nonzero [mass](../../../../../mass.md) flux through its front.
