<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Immediately upstream, free fall from rest at infinity gives $u_1^2=2GM_0/r_0$. Treat the surface shock as locally planar and stationary, with negligible upstream [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). The [Strong-shock Rankine-Hugoniot conditions](../../../../../strong-shock-rankine-hugoniot-conditions.md) for a [monatomic gas](../../../../../monatomic-gas.md), $\gamma=5/3$, give $\rho_2/\rho_1=4$ and $u_2=u_1/4$. [momentum conservation](../../../../../momentum-conservation.md) then gives

$$
p_2=\rho_1u_1^2-\rho_2u_2^2=\frac34\rho_1u_1^2,\qquad\frac{p_2}{\rho_2}=\frac3{16}u_1^2.
$$

Use the source's gas-constant convention $p=\rho(R^*/\mu)T$. The immediate postshock [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) is

$$
\boxed{T_2=\frac\mu{R^*}\frac3{16}\frac{2GM_0}{r_0}=\frac\mu{R^*}\frac38\frac{GM_0}{r_0}}.
$$

Here adiabatic means that the jump does not lose appreciable heat to the surroundings; the shock is nevertheless irreversible and raises [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md). The gas still has [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) $u_1/4$ immediately downstream, so setting all upstream [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) equal to downstream internal [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) would give the wrong coefficient. Later settling, [radiative cooling](../../../../../radiative-cooling.md) or changing ionization can alter the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md); they are outside this immediate, fixed-composition perfect-gas jump.
