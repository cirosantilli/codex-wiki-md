<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A geometrically thin [accretion disk](../../../../../accretion-disk.md) around a central [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $M$ has nearly [Keplerian orbits](../../../../../kepler-orbit.md), with $\Omega^2\simeq GM/r^3$. Its vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) gives $H\sim c_s/\Omega\ll r$. The [specific angular momentum](../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md) $\ell=r^2\Omega\propto r^{1/2}$ decreases inward, so accreting matter must transfer [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) to other matter. A [viscous stress](../../../../../viscous-stress-tensor.md) couples neighboring annuli rotating at different [angular velocities](../../../../../angular-velocity.md).

Writing $\nu$ for effective [kinematic viscosity](../../../../../kinematic-viscosity.md) and $\Sigma$ for surface [mass density](../../../../../density.md), the outward [viscous torque in an accretion disk](../../../../../viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk.md) is

$$
\mathcal G=-2\pi r^3\nu\Sigma\frac{d\Omega}{dr}>0.
$$

Steady [mass conservation](../../../../../mass-conservation.md) defines $\dot M=-2\pi r\Sigma u_r>0$. If the inner radius $r_{\rm in}$ has negligible applied torque, [angular momentum conservation](../../../../../conservation-of-angular-momentum.md) gives $\mathcal G=\dot M(\ell-\ell_{\rm in})$, hence

$$
\boxed{\nu\Sigma=\frac{\dot M}{3\pi}\left[1-\left(\frac{r_{\rm in}}r\right)^{1/2}\right].}
$$

Well outside that boundary, $u_r\simeq-3\nu/(2r)$ and the inward drift time is of order $r^2/\nu$, much longer than an [orbital period](../../../../../orbital-period.md) in a thin disk with modest effective stress. Dissipation of orbital [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) heats the disk. If that heat escapes locally from two faces, the radiative flux from either face is

$$
F(r)=\frac{3GM\dot M}{8\pi r^3}\left[1-\left(\frac{r_{\rm in}}r\right)^{1/2}\right].
$$

Integration over both faces gives $L=GM\dot M/(2r_{\rm in})$ in this Newtonian zero-torque model. Material can lose [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) while the disk's outer parts spread outward: outward transport does not imply that most of its [mass](../../../../../mass.md) moves outward. In real ionized disks, [magnetorotational instability](../../../../../magnetorotational-instability.md) and magnetic stresses often provide the effective transport; molecular [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md) alone is generally insufficient.
