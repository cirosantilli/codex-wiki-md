<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In a [radiative shock](../../../../../radiative-shock.md), a narrow dissipative jump first converts upstream [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) into internal [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). The immediate strong adiabatic compression is $(\gamma+1)/(\gamma-1)$, and the immediate [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) is the result of part 1(c). Downstream gas then radiates, loses [specific enthalpy](../../../../../specific-enthalpy.md) and compresses further. Its [shock cooling length](../../../../../shock-cooling-length.md) is of order $u_2t_{\rm cool}$, with the immediate downstream speed used only as an estimate because the gas slows as it cools.

For the idealization of equal far-upstream and cooled downstream [temperatures](../../../../../temperature-split.md), unchanged composition and negligible magnetic stress, write $p=c_T^2\rho$. Conserved [mass](../../../../../mass.md) and normal [momentum flux](../../../../../momentum-flux.md) across the whole layer give

$$
\rho_1u_1=\rho_fu_f,\qquad
c_T^2\rho_1+\rho_1u_1^2=c_T^2\rho_f+\rho_fu_f^2.
$$

With $r=\rho_f/\rho_1=u_1/u_f$, the nontrivial [isothermal shock](../../../../../isothermal-shock.md) root is

$$
\boxed{r=\frac{u_1^2}{c_T^2}=\mathcal M_{T,1}^2.}
$$

It can greatly exceed the adiabatic strong-shock compression. An upstream [adiabatic sound speed](../../../../../adiabatic-sound-speed.md) instead defines $\mathcal M_{{\rm ad},1}=u_1/(\sqrt\gamma c_T)$, so the same result is $r=\gamma\mathcal M_{{\rm ad},1}^2$; the two [Mach numbers](../../../../../mach-number.md) must not be confused. The radiated [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) flux is $\rho_1u_1(u_1^2-u_f^2)/2$ when the two endpoint [specific enthalpies](../../../../../specific-enthalpy.md) agree. The gas's [specific entropy](../../../../../specific-entropy.md) rises at the initial jump and falls during cooling, while the escaping radiation carries [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) away. A transverse frozen-in [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md), or an imposed [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) floor, can limit the compression. Strongly cooling shocks can therefore collect thin dense shells without violating total [conservation of energy](../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md).
