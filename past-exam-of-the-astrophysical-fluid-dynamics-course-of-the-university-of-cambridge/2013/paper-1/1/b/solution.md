<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

By reflection symmetry the vertical [gravitational field](../../../../../gravitational-field.md) of the [self-gravitating incompressible slab](../../../../../self-gravitating-incompressible-slab.md) vanishes at its mid-plane. Inside the slab, the gravitational [Poisson equation](../../../../../poisson-s-equation.md) therefore integrates to $d\psi/dz=4\pi G\rho z$. Treat the [star](../../../../../star.md) as a test particle, so its own [mass](../../../../../mass.md) does not alter that field. Its [vertical stellar orbit in a uniform slab](../../../../../vertical-stellar-orbit-in-a-uniform-slab.md) obeys

$$
\ddot z=-4\pi G\rho z,\qquad z(0)=a,\qquad\dot z(0)=0.
$$

Hence $z(t)=a\cos(\omega t)$ with $\omega=\sqrt{4\pi G\rho}$. The first mid-plane crossing occurs after one quarter of this [simple harmonic motion](../../../../../simple-harmonic-motion.md) period:

$$
\boxed{t_{\rm cross}=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{4\pi G\rho}}=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{4\sqrt{G\rho}}.}
$$

Its independence of $a$ follows from the linear interior [restoring force](../../../../../restoring-force.md). The calculation neglects drag and changes to the slab, and it uses the infinite planar approximation rather than the field of a finite Galactic disk.
