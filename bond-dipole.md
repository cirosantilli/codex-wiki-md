# Bond dipole

↑ **Parent:** [Bond polarization](bond-polarization.md)

A [bond dipole](bond-dipole.md) represents the separation of positive and negative [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) associated with a [chemical bond](chemical-bond-split.md). In a neutral two-[point charge](point-charge.md) model put $+q$ at $\mathbf r_A$ and $-q$ at $\mathbf r_B$, with $q>0$. Its physical [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) is

$$
\mathbf p_b=q\mathbf r_A-q\mathbf r_B=-q\mathbf d,\qquad
\mathbf d=\mathbf r_B-\mathbf r_A,\qquad |\mathbf p_b|=q|\mathbf d|.
$$

If $B$ is the negative end, the physical [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) points from $B$ to $A$. The customary crossed-tail chemical arrow points towards the negative, more [electronegative](electronegativity-split.md) end, from $A$ to $B$. It therefore has the opposite direction. State the convention before doing [vector addition](vector-addition.md); use physical [electric dipole moments](electric-dipole-moment.md) in the interaction [potential energy](potential-energy.md) $U=-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E$, where $\mathbf E$ is the [electric field](electric-field-split.md).[§7.6, “Molecular Polarity and Dipole Moment”, Figure 7.26: arrow towards the more electronegative end, with a plus at the tail](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others.md)

For a fixed neutral [molecule](molecule-split.md), its complete [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) follows from all [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md) and the [electron density](electron-density.md), not just [point charges](point-charge.md) assigned to sites. If $n(\mathbf r)$ is the expected number of [electrons](electron.md) per unit [volume](volume.md), the corresponding [charge density](charge-density.md) is $\rho_e(\mathbf r)=-en(\mathbf r)$; the nuclear terms contribute separately. Partitioning that distribution into [bond dipoles](bond-dipole.md) is a model. [Lone pairs](lone-pair.md) and [electric polarization](polarization-density.md) within a [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) can contribute as well. A measured molecular [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) cannot uniquely recover every individual [bond dipole](bond-dipole.md) or [partial atomic charge](partial-atomic-charge.md).
