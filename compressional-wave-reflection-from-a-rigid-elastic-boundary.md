# Compressional-wave reflection from a rigid elastic boundary

↑ **Parent:** [Elastic wave](elastic-wave.md)

In an isotropic half-space $y<0$ with zero [displacement](displacement.md) at $y=0$, an incident [longitudinal wave](longitudinal-wave.md) directed along $(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$ reflects into both longitudinal and [shear waves](shear-wave.md). Let $c_p^2=(\lambda+2\mu)/\rho$ and $c_s^2=\mu/\rho$. Horizontal [phase](phase-waves.md) matching gives $\sin\beta=(c_s/c_p)\cos\alpha$ for a reflected shear [wavevector](wavevector.md) proportional to $(\sin\beta,-\cos\beta)$ and [polarization](polarization-waves.md) $(\cos\beta,\sin\beta)$. The two zero-[displacement](displacement.md) equations at the boundary give the displayed coefficients. Since $c_s<c_p$ in a stable isotropic solid, both reflected branches are propagating for every genuine incident angle $0<\alpha<\pi$, with no critical-angle obstruction for this incident-wave type. Changing to a [stress](stress.md)-free boundary changes the amplitude equations and coefficients.
