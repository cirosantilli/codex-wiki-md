# Mating assortment contrast

↑ **Parent:** [Reproductive isolation](reproductive-isolation.md)

For observed pairings between two labels $A,B$, let $N_{\mathrm{same}}=N_{AA}+N_{BB}$ and $N_{\mathrm{cross}}=N_{AB}+N_{BA}$, where the first label denotes the female's group and the second the male's. A [mating assortment contrast](mating-assortment-contrast.md) is

$$
I=\frac{N_{\mathrm{same}}-N_{\mathrm{cross}}}{N},\qquad N=N_{\mathrm{same}}+N_{\mathrm{cross}}>0.
$$

It lies between $-1$ and $1$. Positive $I$ describes an excess of same-label pairings over cross-label pairings; whether that is evidence of [assortative mating](assortative-mating.md) depends on the null [mathematical model](mathematical-model.md) and the assay. The four counts form a [contingency table](contingency-table.md).

Here is an original scope check. Suppose independently paired females and males have [probabilities](probability.md) $p_f,p_m$ of carrying label $A$. Then

$$
q_{\mathrm{same}}=p_fp_m+(1-p_f)(1-p_m),\qquad \mathbb E[I]=2q_{\mathrm{same}}-1.
$$

With $p_f=p_m=1/2$, the [expected value](expected-value.md) is zero. With $p_f=p_m=4/5$, it is $9/25=0.36$, despite pairing being independent of label. This counterexample concerns unequal pool composition; it does not replace the group introductions and [contingency table](contingency-table.md) tests in the [dietary divergence and mating isolation in Drosophila pseudoobscura](dietary-divergence-and-mating-isolation-in-drosophila-pseudoobscura.md) experiment. Even with balanced pools, finite observed samples fluctuate: a positive $I$ alone is not a [hypothesis test](statistical-hypothesis-test.md), much less a universal [species](species.md) boundary.
