# Golden-rule relaxation of a spin coupled to an oscillator bath

↑ **Parent:** [Spin-boson model](spin-boson-model.md)

For transverse coupling with equal mode amplitudes $\lambda$, [Fermi's golden rule](fermi-s-golden-rule.md) and the oscillator [creation operator](creation-operator.md) and [annihilation operator](annihilation-operator.md) give

$$
\Gamma_{\downarrow\uparrow}=\Gamma_0(n_\Omega+1),\qquad
\Gamma_{\uparrow\downarrow}=\Gamma_0n_\Omega,\qquad
\Gamma_0=\frac{2\pi\lambda^2}{\hbar^2}g(\Omega),
$$

where the first index is the final spin and $g$ is the [density of states](density-of-states.md) per unit angular frequency. Decay creates a quantum and carries the factor $n_\Omega+1$; excitation annihilates one and carries $n_\Omega$. For nonuniform resonant occupations, $n_\Omega$ is their density-weighted mean.

With a thermal [Bose-Einstein distribution](bose-einstein-distribution.md), $n_\Omega/(n_\Omega+1)=e^{-\beta\hbar\Omega}$. The two-state [master equation](master-equation.md) therefore has the [Boltzmann distribution](boltzmann-distribution.md) as its unique stationary populations when $\Gamma_0>0$. The population deviation relaxes at rate $\Gamma_0(2n_\Omega+1)$. If there is no resonant coupling, both rates vanish and stationarity does not determine the populations.
