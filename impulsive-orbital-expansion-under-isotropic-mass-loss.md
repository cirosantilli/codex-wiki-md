# Impulsive orbital expansion under isotropic mass loss

↑ **Parent:** [Isotropic stellar mass loss](isotropic-stellar-mass-loss.md)

Suppose an initially [circular Kepler orbit](circular-kepler-orbit.md) of radius $R$ surrounds central [mass](mass.md) $M$, and a fraction $0<f<1/2$ is removed quickly compared with the [orbital period](orbital-period.md). Assume no recoil or wind [drag](drag-physics.md), so the satellite's [position](position.md) and [velocity](velocity.md) are continuous through the change. Its [specific angular momentum](specific-angular-momentum.md) stays $\sqrt{GMR}$, while its new [specific orbital energy](specific-orbital-energy.md) is $-GM(1-2f)/(2R)$. The resulting [elliptic Kepler orbit](elliptic-orbit.md) has [semi-major axis](semi-major-axis.md) $a=(1-f)R/(1-2f)$, [pericentre](periapsis.md) $r_p=R$, [apocentre](apoapsis.md) $r_a=R/(1-2f)$ and [orbital eccentricity](orbital-eccentricity.md) $e=f/(1-f)$. The limiting loss fraction $f=1/2$ gives a [parabolic Kepler orbit](parabolic-trajectory.md); larger losses unbind the initially circular satellite. Slow removal instead produces [adiabatic orbital expansion under isotropic mass loss](adiabatic-orbital-expansion-under-isotropic-mass-loss.md), which preserves circularity to leading adiabatic order.
