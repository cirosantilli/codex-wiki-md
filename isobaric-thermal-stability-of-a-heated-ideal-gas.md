# Isobaric thermal stability of a heated ideal gas

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysical fluid dynamics](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics-split.md)

Let the net cooling per unit mass of an [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) be $\mathcal L=A\rho T^\alpha-H$, with positive constants $A,H$. At fixed [pressure](pressure-split.md) and composition, $\rho T$ is constant, so $\mathcal L=CT^{\alpha-1}-H$. At a thermal equilibrium $T_0$, $CT_0^{\alpha-1}=H$. The [specific heat capacity at constant pressure](specific-heat-capacity-at-constant-pressure.md) gives $c_p\,d(\delta T)/dt=-(\partial\mathcal L/\partial T)_p\delta T$. Thus $\delta T\propto e^{st}$ with the displayed growth rate. The equilibrium is stable for $\alpha>1$, unstable for $\alpha<1$, and marginal at linear order for $\alpha=1$. In particular, optically thin [Bremsstrahlung](bremsstrahlung.md) with $\alpha=1/2$ is unstable under this heating law and the isobaric constraint. Fixed-density perturbations have a different criterion.
