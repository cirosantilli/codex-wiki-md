<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Taylor expansions](../../../../taylor-expansion.md) at zero give $1-e^x=-x-x^2/2+O(x^3)$ and $1-\cos x=x^2/2+O(x^4)$. Squaring the first expression gives $x^2+O(x^3)$, so canceling the leading quadratic power yields

$$
\boxed{\lim_{x\to0}\frac{(1-e^x)^2}{1-\cos x}=2.}
$$

Both numerator and denominator vanish, but their leading nonzero coefficients have ratio two.
