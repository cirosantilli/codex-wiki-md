<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The [gradient](../../../gradient.md) is $\nabla f=(2x+y,x)$ and at $(1,1)$ it is $(3,1)$. For a [unit vector](../../../unit-vector.md) $\hat{\mathbf n}$, the [directional derivative](../../../directional-derivative.md) is $D_{\hat{\mathbf n}}f=(3,1)\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}$. The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) bounds its absolute value by $\sqrt{10}$, with equality in the parallel or antiparallel direction. Thus

$$
\boxed{\hat{\mathbf n}=\pm\frac{(3,1)}{\sqrt{10}},\qquad |D_{\hat{\mathbf n}}f|_{\max}=\sqrt{10}.}
$$

The plus sign gives greatest increase; the minus sign gives greatest decrease, with the same magnitude. Stationary directions at the specified point have zero first derivative, so they are perpendicular to the gradient:

$$
\boxed{\hat{\mathbf t}=\pm\frac{(1,-3)}{\sqrt{10}}.}
$$

These are tangents to the local [level curve](../../../level-curve.md), not a claim that $(1,1)$ is itself a stationary point of the two-variable function.
