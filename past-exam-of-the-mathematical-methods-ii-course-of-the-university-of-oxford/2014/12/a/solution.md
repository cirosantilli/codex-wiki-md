<h1 id="12/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The components of the [curl](../../../../curl.md) of a [gradient](../../../../gradient.md) are differences of mixed second derivatives:

$$
\nabla\times\nabla\phi=(\phi_{zy}-\phi_{yz},\ \phi_{xz}-\phi_{zx},\ \phi_{yx}-\phi_{xy}).
$$

If $\phi$ has continuous second [partial derivatives](../../../../partial-derivative.md) in a neighborhood, equality of mixed derivatives makes every component vanish. Thus

$$
\boxed{\nabla\times(\nabla\phi)=0.}
$$

The $C^2$ condition is a sufficient regularity hypothesis; no simple-connectivity assumption is needed for this local identity. Topology matters for the converse assertion that every curl-free field has a global potential.
