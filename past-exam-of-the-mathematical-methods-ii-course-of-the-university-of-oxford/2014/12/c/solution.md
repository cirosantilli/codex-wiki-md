<h1 id="12/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Integrate the first component of $\mathbf F=\nabla\phi$ with respect to $x$:

$$
\phi=3xy^2-x^3yz-xz^2+g(y,z).
$$

Matching the $y$ component gives $g_y=0$, and matching the $z$ component gives $g_z=0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\phi=3xy^2-x^3yz-xz^2+C.}
$$

The [fundamental theorem for line integrals](../../../../fundamental-theorem-for-line-integrals.md) now yields

$$
\boxed{I=\phi(1,1,1)-\phi(0,0,0)=3-1-1=1.}
$$

The potential is a single-valued polynomial everywhere, so any piecewise smooth path with these endpoints has the same integral. The plus-gradient convention here should not be confused with the minus gradient of a mechanical potential energy.
