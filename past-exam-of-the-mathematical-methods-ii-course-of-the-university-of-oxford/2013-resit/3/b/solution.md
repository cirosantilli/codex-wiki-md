<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In the small-slope string model, the additional elastic [potential energy](../../../../potential-energy.md) is $V=(T/2)\int_0^L y_x^2dx$. For the fundamental mode, $y_x=A_1k_1\cos(k_1x)\cos(\omega_1t)$. Since $\int_0^L\cos^2(\pi x/L)dx=L/2$,

$$
V(t)=\frac14TA_1^2k_1^2L\cos^2\omega_1t.
$$

Using $Tk_1^2=\mu\omega_1^2$ from the [dispersion relation](../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) gives

$$
\boxed{V(t)=\frac14A_1^2\omega_1^2L\mu\cos^2\omega_1t.}
$$

The expression vanishes as the string passes through its straight equilibrium shape and is maximal at the turning shapes, when the corresponding [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is zero.
