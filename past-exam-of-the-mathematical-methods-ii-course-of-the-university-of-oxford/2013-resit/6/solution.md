<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

The [gradient](../../../gradient.md) is $\nabla f=(8x,2y)$, so $\nabla f(1,1)=(8,2)$. The supplied direction is not a [unit vector](../../../unit-vector.md); normalize it to $\hat{\mathbf u}=(1,2)/\sqrt5$. Its [directional derivative](../../../directional-derivative.md) is

$$
\boxed{D_{\hat{\mathbf u}}f=(8,2)\cdot\frac{(1,2)}{\sqrt5}=\frac{12}{\sqrt5}.}
$$

The direction of greatest decrease is opposite to the gradient:

$$
\boxed{\hat{\mathbf n}_{\rm decrease}=-\frac{(8,2)}{\sqrt{68}}=-\frac{(4,1)}{\sqrt{17}},\qquad D_{\hat{\mathbf n}_{\rm decrease}}f=-2\sqrt{17}.}
$$

The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) shows that no other unit direction can give a more negative derivative.
