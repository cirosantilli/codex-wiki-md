<h1 id="11/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At an interior [stationary point](../../../../../stationary-point.md), a negative determinant of the symmetric [Hessian matrix](../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) means its two eigenvalues have opposite signs. Along their eigenvectors the leading quadratic change has opposite signs, and the higher-order remainder cannot reverse these signs sufficiently close to the point. Hence the nondegenerate saddle condition is

$$
\boxed{f_x=f_y=0,\qquad f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2<0.}
$$

When the determinant vanishes, a saddle is still possible but is not determined by second derivatives alone: $x^4-y^4$ is a simple example. Thus these tests are decisive for nondegenerate stationary points, not an exhaustive classification from first and second derivatives of every smooth function.
