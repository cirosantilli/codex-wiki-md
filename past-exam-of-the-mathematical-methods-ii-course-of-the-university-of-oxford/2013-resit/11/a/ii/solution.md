<h1 id="11/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At an interior [stationary point](../../../../../stationary-point.md), let $A=f_{xx}$, $B=f_{xy}$, $C=f_{yy}$ and $D=AC-B^2$. The quadratic change in the [Taylor expansion](../../../../../taylor-expansion.md) is negative in every nonzero displacement when the [Hessian matrix](../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) is negative definite. Applying the minimum test to $-f$ gives the sufficient nondegenerate maximum conditions

$$
\boxed{f_x=f_y=0,\qquad A<0,\qquad D>0.}
$$

The quadratic term then dominates the smaller Taylor remainder in a sufficiently small neighborhood, proving a strict local maximum. A vanishing determinant requires higher-order analysis instead.
