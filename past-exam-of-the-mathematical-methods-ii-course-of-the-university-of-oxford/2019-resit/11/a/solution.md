<h1 id="11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For opposite propagation directions on a uniform string, write the equal-frequency [transverse waves](../../../../transverse-wave.md) as

$$
y_1=A\cos(kx-\omega t+\alpha),\qquad
y_2=A\cos(kx+\omega t+\beta).
$$

The cosine addition identity gives

$$
\boxed{y_1+y_2=2A\cos\left(kx+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\right)
\cos\left(\omega t+\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\right).}
$$

This is a [standing wave](../../../../standing-wave.md): the spatial pattern is fixed while its amplitude oscillates in time. Nodes occur at the zeros of the first cosine and are separated by $\pi/k$, half a [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md); antinodes have displacement amplitude $2A$. The two equal opposite travelling waves carry equal opposite average [energy flux](../../../../energy-flux.md), so the net average transported [power](../../../../power.md) is zero.

For the same propagation direction, the same identity instead gives

$$
\boxed{A\cos(kx-\omega t+\alpha)+A\cos(kx-\omega t+\beta)
=2A\cos\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\,
\cos\left(kx-\omega t+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\right).}
$$

For a nonzero resultant this is another travelling wave, with fixed [amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md) and moving constant-phase points, not a nontrivial [standing wave](../../../../standing-wave.md). If the prefactor vanishes, the waves cancel identically; the zero disturbance is a trivial exception rather than a standing oscillation. The argument assumes propagating waves with $k,\omega>0$ on the same uniform string.
