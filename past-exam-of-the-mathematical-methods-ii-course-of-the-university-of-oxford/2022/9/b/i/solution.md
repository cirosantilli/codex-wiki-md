<h1 id="9/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a sufficiently smooth vector field on an oriented surface $S$ with compatible boundary orientation, [Stokes theorem](../../../../../stokes-theorem.md) states

$$
\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot\mathbf n\,dS.
$$

Apply it to $\mathbf F=(P,Q,0)$ in the plane. The closed [line integral](../../../../../line-integral.md) is the area [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) of $Q_x-P_y$. Thus $P_y=Q_x$ makes it vanish whenever a spanning surface lies within the smooth domain. In a simply connected domain this implies [path independence](../../../../../path-independence.md): join one path from $A$ to $B$ to the reverse of another, and their difference is a zero closed [line integral](../../../../../line-integral.md).

The domain qualification is essential. On the punctured plane, $P=-y/(x^2+y^2)$, $Q=x/(x^2+y^2)$ satisfy $P_y=Q_x$, but the [line integral](../../../../../line-integral.md) around the unit circle is $2\pi$. A disk spanning that circle passes through the singularity. Hence the printed implication requires an appropriate domain assumption; zero [curl](../../../../../curl.md) alone is not a global [path independence](../../../../../path-independence.md) theorem.
