<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Since $\mathbf a$ is constant, direct differentiation gives $(\mathbf r\cdot\nabla)\mathbf a=0$, $(\mathbf a\cdot\nabla)\mathbf r=\mathbf a$, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf r=3$ and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf a=0$. The stated right-hand side is therefore $2\mathbf a$. Independently, [index notation](../../../../index-notation.md) gives

$$
[\nabla\times(\mathbf a\times\mathbf r)]_i
=\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{klm}\partial_j(a_lx_m)=2a_i.
$$

Thus $\boxed{\nabla\times(\mathbf a\times\mathbf r)=2\mathbf a}$. A [conservative vector field](../../../../conservative-vector-field.md) has zero [curl](../../../../curl.md), so the field is **not conservative unless $\mathbf a=0$**. For a closed planar circuit, its [circulation](../../../../circulation-physics.md) is $2\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf A$ by [Stokes theorem](../../../../stokes-theorem.md); the zero-vector exception is conservative with a constant potential.
