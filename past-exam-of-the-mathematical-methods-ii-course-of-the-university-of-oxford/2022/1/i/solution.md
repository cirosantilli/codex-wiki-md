<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The real [logarithm](../../../../logarithm.md) restricts the approach to $x\to0^+$. The [Taylor expansion](../../../../taylor-expansion.md) $\sin x-x=-x^3/6+O(x^5)$ gives

$$
\frac1x+\frac{\ln x}{\sin x-x}
=\frac{-6\ln x}{x^3}\bigl(1+O(x^2)\bigr)+\frac1x.
$$

The positive first term dominates, since $x^2/|\ln x|\to0$. Thus the requested extended [limit](../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) is $\boxed{+\infty}$. There is no two-sided real [limit](../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) across zero because the real [logarithm](../../../../logarithm.md) is undefined for $x<0$.
