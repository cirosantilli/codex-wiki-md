<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $x>2$, write

$$
\frac1x+\frac{\ln x}{\sin x-x}
=\frac{1-\ln x/(1-\sin x/x)}x.
$$

Since $|\sin x|\le1$, the denominator factor tends to one, and $\ln x/x\to0$. Therefore the [limit](../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) is $\boxed{0}$. The function is eventually negative, so it approaches zero from below. Oscillation of $\sin x$ does not obstruct this [limit](../../../../limit-of-a-function.md), because it is bounded while $x$ grows.
