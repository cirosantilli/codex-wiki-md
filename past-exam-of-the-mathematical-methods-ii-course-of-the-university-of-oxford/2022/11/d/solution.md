<h1 id="11/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the supplied equations, $\lambda_+\to1$ and $a_2/a_1=\alpha/(\alpha-\lambda_+)\sim-\alpha$. Thus the higher-[frequency](../../../../frequency.md) [normal mode](../../../../normal-mode.md) becomes an upper-mass [oscillation](../../../../oscillation.md) at $\sqrt{g/l}$ with a vanishing, opposite-sign lower horizontal [displacement](../../../../displacement.md). At $\alpha=0$ the supplied lower equation is $\ddot x_2=0$ and its other mode has zero [frequency](../../../../frequency.md).

This limiting picture belongs to the given matrix. For the physical unequal-mass [double pendulum](../../../../double-pendulum.md), both [normal mode](../../../../normal-mode.md) [angular frequencies](../../../../angular-frequency.md) tend to $\sqrt{g/l}$ as the lower mass tends to zero; at exactly zero mass its independent equation needs a limiting test-body interpretation. The source's extra $\alpha$ changes precisely this conclusion.
