<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

For an [exact differential](../../../exact-differential.md) $P\,dx+Q\,dy$, equality of mixed [partial derivatives](../../../partial-derivative.md) requires $P_y=Q_x$. The matching pair is $P=f_1$, $Q=f_3$, since

$$
\partial_yf_1=\frac{\cos y}{x}-e^x\sin2y=\partial_xf_3.
$$

The other pairings do not give the same [partial derivatives](../../../partial-derivative.md) on an open region. Integrating $f_1$ with respect to $x$ gives $\ln x\sin y+e^x\cos^2y+C(y)$. Its $y$ derivative agrees with $f_3$ precisely when $C'(y)=0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{f(x,y)=\ln x\sin y+e^x\cos^2y+C,\qquad x>0.}
$$

This also proves existence, rather than relying only on the necessary mixed-[partial derivative](../../../partial-derivative.md) test.
