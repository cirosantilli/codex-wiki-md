<h1 id="10/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The product-to-sum identity gives $\sin\Omega t\sin\omega t=[\cos(\Omega-\omega)t-\cos(\Omega+\omega)t]/2$. Thus the first [mass](../../../../mass.md) must have equal and opposite cosine-mode amplitudes and no sine-mode amplitudes. Equivalently, release the second [mass](../../../../mass.md) from a [displacement](../../../../displacement.md) $X$ while the first is at equilibrium, both initially at rest:

$$
\boxed{x_1(0)=0,\quad x_2(0)=X,\quad\dot x_1(0)=\dot x_2(0)=0.}
$$

The two [normal mode](../../../../normal-mode.md) coefficients in $x_1$ are $qX/(2D)$ and $-qX/(2D)$. Therefore, with

$$
\boxed{\Omega=\frac{\omega_++\omega_-}{2},\qquad\omega=\frac{\omega_+-\omega_-}{2},}
$$

the two [displacements](../../../../displacement.md) are

$$
\boxed{x_1(t)=\frac{qX}{D}\sin\Omega t\sin\omega t,\qquad
x_2(t)=X\left[\cos\Omega t\cos\omega t+\frac{\kappa}{2D}\sin\Omega t\sin\omega t\right].}
$$

These initial conditions are necessary for a nontrivial product with exactly the stated time origin, as well as sufficient: $x_1(0)=\dot x_1(0)=0$ follows from the product, and its zero third derivative at zero, together with the first coupled equation, forces $\dot x_2(0)=0$. Since both [normal frequencies](../../../../eigenfrequency.md) are positive, $\Omega>\omega>0$. This is the product form used to describe [beating](../../../../beat.md), although a well-separated slow envelope requires the two [normal frequencies](../../../../eigenfrequency.md) to be close. No such extra near-degeneracy is assumed in the exact formula.
