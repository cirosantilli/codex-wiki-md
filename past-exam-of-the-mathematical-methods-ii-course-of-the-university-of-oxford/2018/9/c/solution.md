<h1 id="9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Inside the unit [ball](../../../../ball-mathematics.md), $\nabla\cdot J=0$. On its [sphere](../../../../sphere.md), $J\cdot r=xy-xy=0$, so the jump to zero outside adds no surface [divergence](../../../../divergence.md). The field therefore satisfies the required [weak derivative](../../../../weak-derivative.md) condition.

By reflection [symmetry](../../../../symmetry-physics.md), integrals of $xy,xz,yz$ over the [ball](../../../../ball-mathematics.md) vanish. Rotational [symmetry](../../../../symmetry-physics.md) and [spherical coordinates](../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) give

$$
\int_{B_1}x^2\,dV=\frac13\int_{B_1}r^2\,dV=\frac{4\pi}{3}\int_0^1r^4\,dr=\frac{4\pi}{15}.
$$

Hence the left side is $\int x(y,-x,0)\,dV=(0,-4\pi/15,0)$. Meanwhile $J\times r=(-xz,-yz,x^2+y^2)$, so its integral is $(0,0,8\pi/15)$. Therefore

$$
\frac12(1,0,0)\times(0,0,8\pi/15)=\boxed{(0,-4\pi/15,0),}
$$

exactly the same [vector](../../../../vector.md).
