<h1 id="9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the [vector triple product](../../../../vector-triple-product.md) identity:

$$
C\times(J\times r)=J(C\cdot r)-r(C\cdot J).
$$

For the $i$th component of the second integral, the [antisymmetric first moment of a localized solenoidal field](../../../../antisymmetric-first-moment-of-a-localized-solenoidal-field.md) with $C'=e_i$ gives $\int_Vr_i(C\cdot J)\,dV=-\int_V(C\cdot r)J_i\,dV$. Thus the two terms on the right have opposite integrals. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\int_V(C\cdot r)J\,dV=\frac12C\times\int_V(J\times r)\,dV.}
$$
