<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Rationalize the difference of [square roots](../../../square-root.md) before taking the [limit](../../../limit-of-a-function.md). For positive $x$, divide the resulting numerator and denominator by $x^2$:

$$
\frac{8x^2+2x}{\bigl(\sqrt{x^3+10x^2}+\sqrt{x^3+2x^2-2x}\bigr)\sqrt{6x+3}}
=\frac{8+2/x}{\bigl(\sqrt{1+10/x}+\sqrt{1+2/x-2/x^2}\bigr)\sqrt{6+3/x}}.
$$

Each factor now has a finite nonzero denominator [limit](../../../limit-of-a-function.md), giving

$$
\boxed{\frac4{\sqrt6}=\frac{2\sqrt6}{3}.}
$$
