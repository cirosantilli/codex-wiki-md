<h1 id="10/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [10](../10.md)

For a short length $\delta x$, the [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md) is $\tfrac12\mu y_t^2\delta x$. For small slope, its increase in length is $[\sqrt{1+y_x^2}-1]\delta x\simeq\tfrac12y_x^2\delta x$, so its [elastic potential energy](../../../elastic-energy.md) is $\tfrac12Ty_x^2\delta x$. For a [travelling wave](../../../travelling-wave.md) $y=f(x-vt)$, $y_t=-vf'$ and $y_x=f'$. Using $v^2=T/\mu$ gives

$$
\boxed{\delta K=\delta U=\frac T2(f')^2\delta x,\qquad\delta E=T(f')^2\delta x.}
$$

Writing a pulse as $f(x)=A g(x/w)$ yields $E=(TA^2/w)\int[g'(s)]^2ds$. Thus energy is proportional to amplitude squared for a fixed shape and width. Width must also be retained when comparing pulses on different strings.
