<h1 id="12/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The notation $(\partial f/\partial x)_y$ means the [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) with respect to $x$ while $y$ is held fixed:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_y
=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h,y)-f(x,y)}h.
$$

For a [differentiable](../../../../differentiable-function.md) function, its first-order height change is $df=f_x\,dx+f_y\,dy$. If both coefficients vanish, the [tangent plane](../../../../tangent-plane.md) is horizontal and every [directional derivative](../../../../directional-derivative.md) vanishes: the surface is flat to first order at that point. It need not be constant in a neighbourhood; $f=x^2+y^2$ at the origin is a counterexample. Existence of coordinate partials alone, without [differentiability](../../../../differentiability.md), would not justify this tangent-plane conclusion.
