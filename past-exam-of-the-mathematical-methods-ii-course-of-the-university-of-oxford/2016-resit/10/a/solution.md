<h1 id="10/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a right-moving sinusoidal string displacement $y=A\cos(kx-\omega t)$, the instantaneous [energy flux of a transverse string wave](../../../../energy-flux-of-a-transverse-string-wave.md) is $P(x,t)=-T y_x y_t=T A^2k\omega\sin^2(kx-\omega t)$. Averaging over one [period](../../../../period-of-a-function.md) gives $\langle\sin^2\rangle=1/2$. Since $c=\omega/k$ and $T=\rho c^2$,

$$
\boxed{\overline P=\frac12T A^2k\omega
=\frac12c\rho A^2\omega^2.}
$$

The displayed power is a cycle average, not the time-dependent instantaneous power. Here $\rho$ denotes linear [mass density](../../../../density.md), rather than the volume density used in the surface-wave calculation.
