<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For a [dispersion relation](../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) $\omega(k)$, [phase velocity](../../../phase-velocity.md) is $v_p=\omega/k$ and [group velocity](../../../group-velocity.md) is $v_g=d\omega/dk=v_p+k\,dv_p/dk$. Their [local equality of phase and group velocity](../../../local-equality-of-phase-and-group-velocity.md) holds throughout an interval for a nondispersive branch $\omega=ck$. Equality at one particular $k$ requires only that $v_p$ have zero [derivative](../../../derivative.md) there, so it does not imply a globally nondispersive medium.

Use $k=2\pi/\lambda$ in the prescribed [gravity-capillary wave](../../../gravity-capillary-wave.md) relation. Then $\omega^2=gk+(T/\rho)k^3$, and differentiation gives

$$
\boxed{v_g=
\frac{g\lambda/(2\pi)+6\pi T/(\lambda\rho)}
{2\sqrt{g\lambda/(2\pi)+2\pi T/(\lambda\rho)}}.}
$$

In the gravity-dominated [limit](../../../limit-of-a-function.md), $v_g=v_p/2$; in the surface-tension-dominated [limit](../../../limit-of-a-function.md), $v_g=3v_p/2$. For $g,T,\rho>0$, the two are equal at $\lambda=2\pi\sqrt{T/(\rho g)}$, the minimum of the [phase velocity](../../../phase-velocity.md) for this dispersion relation.
