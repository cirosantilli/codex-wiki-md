<h1 id="9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

An [exact differential](../../../../exact-differential.md) is an expression $P\,dx+Q\,dy$ that equals $df=f_x\,dx+f_y\,dy$ for a single-valued differentiable [function](../../../../function-split.md) $f$ on the domain. Its [line integral](../../../../line-integral.md) is an endpoint difference by the [fundamental theorem for line integrals](../../../../fundamental-theorem-for-line-integrals.md).

Integrating $f_x=P$ with respect to $x$ gives $f=2x^5-x^2y^3+g(y)$. Matching $f_y=-3x^2y^2+g'(y)$ to $Q$ requires $g'(y)=0$. Hence

$$
df=d(2x^5-x^2y^3),\qquad
\boxed{I=f(2,1)-f(0,0)=64-4=60.}
$$

The arbitrary additive constant cancels. This constructs the [vector-field potential](../../../../potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md) globally and provides a second proof of [path independence](../../../../path-independence.md).
