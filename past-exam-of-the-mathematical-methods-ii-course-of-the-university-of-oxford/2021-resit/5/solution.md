<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

The [gradient](../../../gradient.md) of the [scalar field](../../../scalar-field.md) is

$$
\nabla f=(2x+9y-2z^2,\;9x,\;-4xz),\qquad
\nabla f(2,4,8)=(-88,18,-64).
$$

For a [unit vector](../../../unit-vector.md) $\hat{\mathbf n}$, the [directional derivative](../../../directional-derivative.md) is $D_{\hat{\mathbf n}}f=\nabla f\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}$. The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) bounds its magnitude by $\|\nabla f\|=2\sqrt{3041}$, with equality precisely in the two directions parallel to the [gradient](../../../gradient.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\hat{\mathbf n}=\pm\frac{(-44,9,-32)}{\sqrt{3041}},
\qquad \max_{\|\hat{\mathbf n}\|=1}|D_{\hat{\mathbf n}}f|=2\sqrt{3041}.}
$$

The plus choice gives the greatest positive [directional derivative](../../../directional-derivative.md), and the minus choice gives the most negative [directional derivative](../../../directional-derivative.md). Both maximize its magnitude.
