<h1 id="11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use signed [arc length](../../../../arc-length.md) displacements $x_1,x_2$ in the common clockwise sense shown by both arrows in the source diagram. Near the diametrically opposite equilibrium, one [spring](../../../../spring.md) changes length by $x_2-x_1$ and the other by $x_1-x_2$. Their combined [elastic potential energy](../../../../elastic-energy.md) is

$$
U=\frac k2(x_2-x_1)^2+\frac k2(x_1-x_2)^2=k(x_1-x_2)^2.
$$

The [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is $K=(m_1\dot x_1^2+qm_1\dot x_2^2)/2$. The tangential components of [Newton's second law](../../../../newton-s-second-law.md), or the derivatives $-\partial U/\partial x_i$, give

$$
\boxed{m_1\ddot x_1=-2k(x_1-x_2),\qquad
qm_1\ddot x_2=2k(x_1-x_2),\quad q>0.}
$$

Both [springs](../../../../spring.md) contribute to each restoring [force](../../../../force-split.md). The hoop radius drops out because the coordinates are [arc lengths](../../../../arc-length.md); angular coordinates $x_i/r$ give the same equations after cancelling the common factor $r^2$ in the energies.
