<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [fundamental theorem of calculus](../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) differentiates the variable upper endpoint:

$$
I'(x)=\frac2{\sqrt\pi}e^{-x^2},\qquad
I''(x)=-\frac{4x}{\sqrt\pi}e^{-x^2},\qquad
I'''(x)=\frac{-4+8x^2}{\sqrt\pi}e^{-x^2}.
$$

At zero, $I(0)=0$, $I'(0)=2/\sqrt\pi$, $I''(0)=0$ and $I'''(0)=-4/\sqrt\pi$. The integrand is even, so its [integral](../../../../integral-split.md) from zero is an [odd function](../../../../odd-function.md); all even coefficients in the [Maclaurin series](../../../../maclaurin-series.md) vanish. Hence

$$
\boxed{I(x)=\frac2{\sqrt\pi}\left(x-\frac{x^3}{3}\right)+O(x^5).}
$$

This is the local expansion of the [error function](../../../../error-function.md).
