<h1 id="10/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

A local-wave description requires an identifiable smooth phase $\Theta(x,t)$ with $\bar k=\Theta_x$ and $\bar\omega=-\Theta_t$. Real propagating wavenumber and frequency must satisfy the chosen local branch of $\bar\omega^2=c^2\bar k^2+a$. In a [geometrical optics](../../../../geometrical-optics-split.md) or slowly modulated wavepacket approximation, these quantities and the amplitude vary on scales large compared with a local wavelength and period; for example $|\partial_x\bar k|\ll\bar k^2$ and $|\partial_t\bar\omega|\ll\bar\omega^2$ away from degenerate points.

The [phase compatibility in geometrical optics](../../../../phase-compatibility-in-geometrical-optics.md) means the local fields cannot be specified independently: equality of mixed phase derivatives requires $\bar k_t+\bar\omega_x=0$, derived as wave conservation below. These phase and scale conditions justify the local [dispersion relation](../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md); they alone are not a guarantee of an arbitrary-amplitude exact PDE solution. Amplitude transport and higher-order envelope terms must also be satisfied.
