<h1 id="11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $\mathbf r(s)$ be any smooth curve in the [level surface](../../../../level-surface.md) $\phi=c$. Differentiating the constant value along it gives

$$
0=\frac d{ds}\phi(\mathbf r(s))=\nabla\phi\cdot\frac{d\mathbf r}{ds}.
$$

The curve tangent can be any tangent vector to a [regular level set](../../../../regular-level-set.md), so

$$
\boxed{\nabla\phi\perp T_P\Sigma,\qquad \hat{\mathbf n}=\pm\frac{\nabla\phi}{|\nabla\phi|}\quad\text{when }\nabla\phi\ne0.}
$$

The nonzero-gradient condition matters for determining a normal direction. For example, $\phi=z^3$ has the plane $z=0$ as a level set, but its gradient vanishes there and supplies no direction, even though the geometric plane has a well-defined normal.
