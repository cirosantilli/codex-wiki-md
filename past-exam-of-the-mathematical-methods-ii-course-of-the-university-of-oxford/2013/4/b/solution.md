<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $s\geq0$ be distance along the unit direction from part (a). The point is $\mathbf r(s)=(1,2)+s(1,2)/\sqrt5$, whose distance from the origin is $\sqrt5+s$. For [finite increments of a logarithmic radial function](../../../../finite-increments-of-a-logarithmic-radial-function.md), the exact change is

$$
\Delta T=\ln\frac{(\sqrt5+s)^2}{5}=2\ln\left(1+\frac s{\sqrt5}\right).
$$

Thus an increase of $0.2$ requires

$$
\boxed{s=\sqrt5(e^{0.1}-1).}
$$

If the intended calculation is the local [directional derivative](../../../../directional-derivative.md) estimate, its first-order value is $s\simeq\Delta T/|\nabla T|=\boxed{\sqrt5/10}$. The latter approximates the exact finite distance rather than equaling it. Numerically they are about $0.235$ and $0.224$, respectively.
