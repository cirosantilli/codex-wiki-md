<h1 id="8/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the constant small-oscillation matrices, the total [mechanical energy](../../../../mechanical-energy.md) satisfies

$$
E=\tfrac12\dot{\boldsymbol\theta}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf T\dot{\boldsymbol\theta}+\tfrac12\boldsymbol\theta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf V\boldsymbol\theta,\qquad \dot E=\dot{\boldsymbol\theta}^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf T\ddot{\boldsymbol\theta}+\mathbf V\boldsymbol\theta).
$$

The specified conservative pendulum has [Lagrangian](../../../../lagrangian.md) $L=K-U$, whose [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) give

$$
\boxed{\mathbf T\ddot{\boldsymbol\theta}+\mathbf V\boldsymbol\theta=0,}
$$

or $8\ddot\theta_1+2\ddot\theta_2+(4g/l)\theta_1=0$ and $2\ddot\theta_1+\ddot\theta_2+(g/l)\theta_2=0$. These equations make $\dot E=0$.

The scalar energy law alone is insufficient to infer two unique equations: [energy conservation does not uniquely determine equations of motion](../../../../energy-conservation-does-not-uniquely-determine-equations-of-motion.md). In particular, adding $\mathbf G\dot{\boldsymbol\theta}$ on the right with antisymmetric $\mathbf G$ changes the dynamics but contributes zero power. The conservative-force structure of the given pendulum excludes that extra gyroscopic force and supplies the information needed for the displayed matrix equations.
