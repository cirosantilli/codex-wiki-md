<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Maclaurin series](../../../../maclaurin-series.md) give $1-\cos(x^2)=x^4/2+O(x^8)$ and $\tan x=x+O(x^3)$. Therefore $x^2\tan^2x=x^4+O(x^6)$, and cancellation of the common leading power gives

$$
\boxed{\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos(x^2)}{x^2\tan^2x}=\frac12}.
$$

The argument applies from either side of zero: both leading terms involve the even power $x^4$. The expression need not be defined at zero itself for its [limit](../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) to exist.
