<h1 id="10/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A ray leaving focus $A$ and reaching a boundary point $P$ has incoming unit direction $\mathbf u_A$. By the [law of reflection](../../../../law-of-reflection.md), its normal component reverses and its tangential component stays fixed. The result just derived shows that the reflected direction is $-\mathbf u_B$, pointing exactly from $P$ to focus $B$. Thus all such in-plane rays from one focus converge to the other after one reflection, in the ideal [geometrical optics](../../../../geometrical-optics-split.md) model.

Moreover, every reflected path has the same total length $AP+PB=s$. In a uniform medium their propagation times are equal as well, so different reflection points do not introduce a geometrical path-length spread. Finite [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md), surface errors and incomplete reflecting coverage limit a real focus; the ideal argument concerns rays in the plane of the [ellipse](../../../../ellipse.md). It does not assert that an arbitrary three-dimensional surface with only one elliptical section focuses every out-of-plane ray.
