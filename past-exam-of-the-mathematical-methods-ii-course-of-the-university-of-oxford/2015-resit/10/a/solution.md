<h1 id="10/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let a differentiable curve $\mathbf r(s)$ lie on the [level surface](../../../../level-surface.md) $\phi=c$, and pass through the point under consideration. Differentiating the constant relation along the curve gives

$$
0=\frac{d}{ds}\phi(\mathbf r(s))=\nabla\phi\cdot\mathbf r'(s).
$$

Every surface [tangent vector](../../../../tangent-vector-split.md) arises as the [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) of such a surface curve, so the [gradient](../../../../gradient.md) is orthogonal to every tangent direction. At a regular point, where $\nabla\phi\ne0$, it therefore defines a normal direction, with unit normal

$$
\boxed{\hat{\mathbf n}=\pm\frac{\nabla\phi}{|\nabla\phi|}}.
$$

If $\nabla\phi=0$, the dot-product identity is still true but the zero vector does not determine a normal direction; a regularity hypothesis or another defining function is needed. For example, $\phi=z^3$ defines the plane $z=0$ at level zero but has vanishing [gradient](../../../../gradient.md) everywhere on that plane.
