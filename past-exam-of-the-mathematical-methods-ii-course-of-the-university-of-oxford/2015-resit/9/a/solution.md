<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The original diagram has three horizontal spans $L$, $2L$ and $L$. In the small-slope approximation the massless string segments are straight and their transverse [force](../../../../force-split.md) components are [tension](../../../../tension.md) times slope. The horizontal [tension](../../../../tension.md) components cancel to first order.

For the first bead, the left segment contributes $-Ty_1/L$ and the middle segment contributes $T(y_2-y_1)/(2L)$. For the second bead, the middle contribution is $T(y_1-y_2)/(2L)$ and the driven right segment contributes $T(h-y_2)/L$. [Newton's second law](../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) therefore gives

$$
M\ddot y_1=\frac{T}{2L}(y_2-3y_1),\qquad M\ddot y_2=\frac{T}{2L}(y_1-3y_2+2h).
$$

With $\omega_0^2=T/(2ML)$ and $h=h_0\cos\omega t$, the coupled [equations of motion](../../../../equation-of-motion.md) are

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\ddot y_1+3\omega_0^2y_1-\omega_0^2y_2&=0,\\
\ddot y_2+3\omega_0^2y_2-\omega_0^2y_1&=2\omega_0^2h_0\cos\omega t.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The factor two in the driving term follows from the right span having length $L$, while the middle span has length $2L$. Treating all three spans as equal would change both the coupling and the [normal mode](../../../../normal-mode.md) [frequencies](../../../../frequency.md).
