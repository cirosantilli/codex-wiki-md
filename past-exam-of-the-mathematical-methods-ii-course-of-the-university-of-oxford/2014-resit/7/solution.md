<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

Choose both [waves](../../../wave-split.md) as [sinusoids](../../../sinusoid.md) with a common zero phase and amplitude $A$. The trigonometric sum identity gives

$$
A\cos[(k+\delta)x-(\omega+\epsilon)t]+A\cos[(k-\delta)x-(\omega-\epsilon)t]=2A\cos(\delta x-\epsilon t)\cos(kx-\omega t).
$$

The slowly varying [wave envelope](../../../envelope-waves.md) has constant phase when $\delta x-\epsilon t$ is constant. Its speed is $\epsilon/\delta$, and in the close-frequency limit the [group velocity](../../../group-velocity.md) is

$$
\boxed{v_g=\frac{d\omega}{dk}.}
$$

It describes the motion of a narrow-band envelope in a medium where the constituent waves retain a coherent packet interpretation; it is not an unconditional signal-speed theorem in strongly absorbing or anomalously dispersive media. The carrier [phase velocity](../../../phase-velocity.md) is $v_p=\omega/k$. In a nondispersive medium, $\omega=vk$ with constant $v$, so $v_g=v=v_p$.
