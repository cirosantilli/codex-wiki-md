<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a differentiable function at an interior [stationary point](../../../../stationary-point.md), both first [partial derivatives](../../../../partial-derivative.md) vanish. If the second derivatives are continuous, form the symmetric [Hessian matrix](../../../../hessian-matrix.md)

$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}f_{xx}&f_{xy}\\f_{xy}&f_{yy}\end{pmatrix},\qquad D=f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2.
$$

The [second-derivative test](../../../../second-derivative-test.md) gives a strict local minimum if $D>0$ and $f_{xx}>0$, a strict local maximum if $D>0$ and $f_{xx}<0$, and a [saddle point](../../../../saddle-point.md) if $D<0$. If $D=0$, higher-order terms must be examined. For example, $x^4+y^4$, $-x^4-y^4$ and $x^4-y^4$ all have zero [Hessian matrix](../../../../hessian-matrix.md) at the origin but respectively a minimum, maximum and saddle. These are local interior criteria; boundary extrema require separate treatment.
