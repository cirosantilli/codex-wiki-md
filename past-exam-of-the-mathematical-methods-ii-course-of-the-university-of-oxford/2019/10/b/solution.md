<h1 id="10/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

On the unit upper hemisphere, use [spherical coordinates](../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) with $0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2$ and $0\leq\phi<2\pi$. The [surface mass density](../../../../projected-surface-mass-density.md) is $\sigma=z=\cos\theta$, while the [surface area element](../../../../surface-area-element.md) is $dS=\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$. Thus the total [mass](../../../../mass.md) is

$$
M=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi
=2\pi\left[\frac12\sin^2\theta\right]_0^{\pi/2}
=\boxed{\pi.}
$$

The answer is in the mass units implicit in the specified unit radius and density law. A useful geometric check is that $z\,dS$ on the unit hemisphere equals the [area element](../../../../area-element-of-a-surface.md) of its vertical projection onto the horizontal unit disc, whose [area](../../../../surface-area.md) is $\pi$.
