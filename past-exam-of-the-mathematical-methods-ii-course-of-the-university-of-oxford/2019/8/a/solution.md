<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let the [angular velocity](../../../../angular-velocity.md) vector be the spatially constant $\boldsymbol\omega$. Choose an origin on the fixed rotation axis. The [velocity field](../../../../velocity-field.md) of a [rigid body](../../../../rigid-body-dynamics-split.md) is $v=\boldsymbol\omega\times r$. In components,

$$
v=(\omega_yz-\omega_zy,\ \omega_zx-\omega_xz,\ \omega_xy-\omega_yx).
$$

Taking the [curl](../../../../curl.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\nabla\times v=(2\omega_x,2\omega_y,2\omega_z)=2\boldsymbol\omega.}
$$

An added spatially uniform translational [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) has zero [curl](../../../../curl.md), so it does not affect this relation. Equivalently, the [vorticity](../../../../vorticity-split.md) of rigid rotation is twice the [angular velocity](../../../../angular-velocity.md); the local rotational part of a general smooth flow has [angular velocity](../../../../angular-velocity.md) $\tfrac12\nabla\times v$.
