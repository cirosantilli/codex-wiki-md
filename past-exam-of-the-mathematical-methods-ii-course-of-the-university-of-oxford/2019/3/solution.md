<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The real [logarithm](../../../logarithm.md) restricts the domain to $x>0$. Differentiating gives

$$
f'(x)=2x\ln(3x)+x=x\big[2\ln(3x)+1\big].
$$

Since $x$ cannot vanish in the domain, a [stationary point](../../../stationary-point.md) requires $\ln(3x)=-1/2$. The [logarithm](../../../logarithm.md) is strictly increasing, so this equation has exactly one solution:

$$
\boxed{x_* =\frac{e^{-1/2}}3=\frac1{3\sqrt e},\qquad
f(x_*)=-1-\frac1{18e}.}
$$

The [second derivative](../../../second-derivative.md) is $f''(x)=2\ln(3x)+3$, giving $f''(x_*)=2>0$. In fact $f'$ is negative below $x_*$ and positive above it, so the point is the unique global [minimum](../../../minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) on $x>0$. The boundary value $x=0$ is not an additional [stationary point](../../../stationary-point.md), since the original function is not defined there.
