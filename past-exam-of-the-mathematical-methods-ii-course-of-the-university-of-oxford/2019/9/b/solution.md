<h1 id="9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [dispersive](../../../../wave-dispersion.md) medium has frequency-dependent [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md), so different Fourier components generally propagate with different phases. A [dispersion relation](../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) specifies $\omega$ as a function of the [wave number](../../../../wavenumber.md) $k$, or an equivalent relation between them. For a monochromatic factor $e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$, a point of fixed [phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) moves at

$$
\boxed{v_p=\frac\omega k.}
$$

For a narrow [wave packet](../../../../wave-packet.md) centered at $k_*$, expand $\omega(k)\simeq\omega(k_*)+(k-k_*)\omega'(k_*)$. The envelope then translates at the [group velocity](../../../../group-velocity.md)

$$
\boxed{v_g=\frac{d\omega}{dk}.}
$$

The [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md) tracks crests of a monochromatic wave. The [group velocity](../../../../group-velocity.md) tracks a slowly varying envelope when the narrow-band approximation is valid; in the ideal lossless guide here it also equals the longitudinal energy-transport speed. It is not a universal formula for signal-front speed in every possible dispersive medium.

For the propagating branch of the supplied [electromagnetic waveguide](../../../../electromagnetic-waveguide.md) relation, take $\omega_0>0$, $k>0$ and $\omega=\sqrt{\omega_0^2+c^2k^2}$. Differentiation gives

$$
\boxed{v_p=\frac{\sqrt{\omega_0^2+c^2k^2}}k>c,\qquad
v_g=\frac{c^2k}{\sqrt{\omega_0^2+c^2k^2}}<c,\qquad v_pv_g=c^2.}
$$

The product alone would also allow $v_p=v_g=c$; the strictly positive cutoff is what makes the inequalities strict. At $k=0$ the phase-speed expression is undefined, and these formulas are interpreted through the propagating limit.

A moving monochromatic crest does not carry a new message. An information-bearing modulation is made from a range of frequencies; here its packet and energy move below $c$, and the high-frequency limit approaches $c$. Causal propagation of a newly switched-on disturbance is governed by the electromagnetic equations, so the superluminal [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md) does not permit faster-than-light signalling. These are the [waveguide phase and group velocities](../../../../waveguide-phase-and-group-velocities.md) for a mode with nonzero cutoff, rather than a zero-cutoff mode with both velocities equal to $c$.
