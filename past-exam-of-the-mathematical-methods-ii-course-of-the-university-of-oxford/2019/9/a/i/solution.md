<h1 id="9/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Assume small transverse slopes, a flexible string, constant [tension](../../../../../tension.md) $T$ and uniform [linear mass density](../../../../../linear-mass-density.md) $\rho$. For an element of equilibrium length $dx$, the vertical [force](../../../../../force-split.md) is the difference of the vertical tension components at its ends. To first order in slope,

$$
F_y=T\big[y_x(x+dx,t)-y_x(x,t)\big]=T y_{xx}\,dx.
$$

Its [mass](../../../../../mass.md) is $\rho\,dx$ and its transverse [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) is $y_{tt}$. [Newton's second law](../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) therefore gives $\rho\,dx\,y_{tt}=T\,dx\,y_{xx}$, or

$$
\boxed{y_{xx}=\frac1{c^2}y_{tt},\qquad c=\sqrt{\frac{T}{\rho}}.}
$$

This is the one-dimensional [wave equation](../../../../../wave-equation-split.md) for small-amplitude [transverse waves](../../../../../transverse-wave.md). Changes in string length and horizontal [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) are second order in slope and are neglected in this linear approximation.
