<h1 id="11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For two equal-amplitude waves with the same frequency and [wavenumber](../../../../wavenumber.md), choose their phases so that

$$
A\cos(kx-\omega t)+A\cos(kx+\omega t)=\boxed{2A\cos kx\cos\omega t.}
$$

The spatial amplitude and temporal oscillation separate. Positions with $\cos kx=0$ are fixed [nodes of a standing wave](../../../../node-physics.md), while positions with $|\cos kx|=1$ are [antinodes](../../../../antinode.md). Different initial phases merely shift this pattern in space and time.

A [travelling wave](../../../../travelling-wave.md) has a phase profile that moves at its [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md); a [standing wave](../../../../standing-wave.md) has stationary nodes and each point oscillates with its fixed local amplitude. The two counterpropagating waves carry equal opposite time-averaged energy flux, so their superposition has zero net time-averaged energy transport. Instantaneous energy can still move within the standing pattern.
