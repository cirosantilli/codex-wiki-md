<h1 id="11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Substituting $y(x,t)=f(x)g(t)$ into the [wave equation on a string](../../../../wave-equation-on-a-string.md) gives $f''g=fg''/c^2$. Wherever both factors are nonzero,

$$
\frac{f''(x)}{f(x)}=\frac{g''(t)}{c^2g(t)}.
$$

The left side depends only on $x$ and the right side only on $t$, so both equal a [separation constant](../../../../separation-constant.md) $\alpha$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f''=\alpha f,\qquad g''=\alpha c^2g.}
$$

The resulting differential equations extend through zeros of $f$ or $g$ by continuity; division at a node is unnecessary.
