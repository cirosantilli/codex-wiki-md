<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Construct a global [vector-field potential](../../../potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md) rather than relying only on vanishing [curl](../../../curl.md):

$$
\phi(x,y,z)=x^2z+\frac{y^2}{2},\qquad \nabla\phi=(2xz,y,x^2)=\mathbf F.
$$

Thus $\mathbf F$ is a [conservative vector field](../../../conservative-vector-field.md). The endpoints of the given [parametric curve](../../../parametric-curve.md) are $\mathbf r(0)=(3,0,0)$ and $\mathbf r(\pi/2)=(0,2,1)$. The [fundamental theorem for line integrals](../../../fundamental-theorem-for-line-integrals.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\phi(0,2,1)-\phi(3,0,0)=2.}
$$

The intervening turns of the curve have no effect because this [line integral](../../../line-integral.md) is [path independent](../../../path-independence.md).
