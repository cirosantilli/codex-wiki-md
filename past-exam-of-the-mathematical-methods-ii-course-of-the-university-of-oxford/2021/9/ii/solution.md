<h1 id="9/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [position vector](../../../../position-vector.md) field is a [gradient field](../../../../gradient-field.md):

$$
\mathbf r=(x,y,z)=\nabla\left(\frac{x^2+y^2+z^2}{2}\right).
$$

Along any piecewise smooth [curve](../../../../curve.md), $\mathbf r\cdot d\mathbf r=d(|\mathbf r|^2/2)$. Therefore a closed [line integral](../../../../line-integral.md) has identical endpoint values and

$$
\boxed{\oint_C\mathbf r\cdot d\mathbf r=\left[\frac{|\mathbf r|^2}{2}\right]_{\mathrm{start}}^{\mathrm{end}}=0.}
$$

For a loop bounding an admissible [oriented surface](../../../../oriented-surface.md), [Stokes theorem](../../../../stokes-theorem.md) gives the same result because $\nabla\times\mathbf r=\mathbf0$. The [gradient field](../../../../gradient-field.md) proof also applies directly to arbitrary piecewise smooth closed loops without choosing a spanning surface.
