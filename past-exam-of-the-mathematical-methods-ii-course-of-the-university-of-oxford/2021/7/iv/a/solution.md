<h1 id="7/iv/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Put the fixed end at $x=0$, with the [mechanical string](../../../../../mechanical-string.md) occupying $x\le0$. Let $H$ be the incident peak [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md), set $u=x/L$ and $\tau=ct/L$, and take $t=0$ when the incident front first reaches the end. The symmetric [triangular pulse](../../../../../triangular-function.md) in the source has initial profile $H T(u)$, where

$$
T(s)=\max\{0,1-|2s+1|\}.
$$

The [fixed-end reflection of a triangular pulse](../../../../../fixed-end-reflection-of-a-triangular-pulse.md) follows from the [Dirichlet boundary condition](../../../../../dirichlet-boundary-condition.md) $y(0,t)=0$ and the [superposition principle](../../../../../superposition-principle.md), giving the reflected [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md)

$$
\boxed{\frac{y(x,t)}H=T(u-\tau)-T(-u-\tau),\qquad u\le0.}
$$

The minus sign is the inversion on reflection. At $\tau=1/4$, the resulting profile is zero outside $[-3/4,0]$ and consists of the straight segments through

$$
\boxed{(u,y/H)=(-3/4,0),\ (-1/4,1),\ (0,0).}
$$

The segment beside the wall is steeper because incident and inverted reflected [pulses](../../../../../pulse.md) overlap there.
