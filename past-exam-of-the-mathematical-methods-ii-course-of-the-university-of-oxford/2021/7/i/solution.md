<h1 id="7/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take an ideal flexible [mechanical string](../../../../mechanical-string.md) with constant [tension](../../../../tension.md) $T>0$ and uniform [linear mass density](../../../../linear-mass-density.md) $\mu>0$. Let $x$ measure distance along its straight equilibrium position and let $y(x,t)$ be its small transverse [displacement](../../../../displacement.md). Neglect [damping](../../../../damping.md), bending stiffness and external distributed [forces](../../../../force-split.md), and assume $|y_x|\ll1$.

A segment of reference length $\Delta x$ has mass $\mu\Delta x$. The endpoint [tension](../../../../tension.md) forces have transverse resultant

$$
T\sin\theta(x+\Delta x)-T\sin\theta(x)
\simeq T\bigl[y_x(x+\Delta x,t)-y_x(x,t)\bigr],
$$

since $\sin\theta\simeq\tan\theta=y_x$ for small slopes. [Newton's second law](../../../../newton-s-second-law.md), divided by $\Delta x$ and taken to the limit, gives

$$
\mu y_{tt}=Ty_{xx},\qquad
\boxed{y_{xx}=\frac1{c^2}y_{tt},\quad c=\sqrt{T/\mu}.}
$$

Here $t$ is time, the subscripts denote [partial derivatives](../../../../partial-derivative.md), and $c$ is the [wave speed](../../../../wave-speed.md). The transverse [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) is $y_{tt}$; $y_{xx}$ describes the small-slope curvature responsible for the net transverse [force](../../../../force-split.md).
