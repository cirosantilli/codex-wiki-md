<h1 id="11/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For equal [complex amplitudes](../../../../complex-amplitude.md), the [superposition principle](../../../../superposition-principle.md) and the [complex exponential](../../../../complex-exponential-function.md) formula for the cosine give the exact identity

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=Ae^{i[(k_0+\delta k)x-(\omega_0+\delta\omega)t]}
+Ae^{i[(k_0-\delta k)x-(\omega_0-\delta\omega)t]}\\
&=\boxed{2A\cos(\delta k\,x-\delta\omega\,t)\,e^{i(k_0x-\omega_0t)}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The cosine is a real periodic modulation of the rapidly varying carrier. It varies slowly when $|\delta k|\ll|k_0|$ and $|\delta\omega|\ll|\omega_0|$. Its signed [wave envelope](../../../../envelope-waves.md) travels with velocity $\delta\omega/\delta k$; its magnitude gives the visible [amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md) envelope.

Equal magnitudes need not mean equal initial [wave phases](../../../../phase-waves.md). If the two [complex amplitudes](../../../../complex-amplitude.md) are $ae^{i\phi_+}$ and $ae^{i\phi_-}$, write $\bar\phi=(\phi_++\phi_-)/2$ and $\delta\phi=(\phi_+-\phi_-)/2$. The same result becomes $2ae^{i\bar\phi}\cos(\delta kx-\delta\omega t+\delta\phi)e^{i(k_0x-\omega_0t)}$, still with a real slowly varying modulation.
