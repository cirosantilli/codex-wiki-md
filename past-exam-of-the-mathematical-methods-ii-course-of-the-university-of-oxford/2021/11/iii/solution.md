<h1 id="11/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A fixed [wave phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) satisfies $kx-\omega t=\text{constant}$, so the signed [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md) is $v_p=\omega/k$. The [group velocity](../../../../group-velocity.md) $v_g=d\omega/dk$ gives the leading translation of the [wave envelope](../../../../envelope-waves.md) of a narrow-band [wave packet](../../../../wave-packet.md) on a differentiable [dispersion relation](../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) branch.

Here $v_p=k/m$. For the conventional positive branch $m>0$, $\omega>0$ and $k>0$,

$$
\boxed{v_p=\sqrt{\omega/m}.}
$$

On the opposite propagation branch $k<0$ it is $-\sqrt{\omega/m}$, so $\sqrt{\omega/m}$ is the [phase speed](../../../../phase-speed.md) for either sign of $k$. More generally, for real $m\ne0$ use $v_p=k/m$ with $k^2=m\omega$, allowing the sign of $\omega$ to follow that of $m$. The $k=0$ constant solution has no distinguished [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md).
