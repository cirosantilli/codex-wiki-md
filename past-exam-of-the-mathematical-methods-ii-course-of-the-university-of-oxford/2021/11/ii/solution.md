<h1 id="11/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take constant complex [amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md) $A$ and real [wavenumber](../../../../wavenumber.md) $k$ and [angular frequency](../../../../angular-frequency.md) $\omega$. Substitution of the [complex exponential](../../../../complex-exponential-function.md) gives

$$
y_t=-i\omega y,\qquad y_{xx}=-k^2y,\qquad
im(-i\omega)y=k^2y.
$$

Thus every nonzero [plane wave](../../../../plane-wave.md) of the given form solves the equation precisely when

$$
\boxed{m\omega=k^2.}
$$

The [wavenumber](../../../../wavenumber.md) specifies spatial [wave phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) change per unit length, with [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) $\lambda=2\pi/|k|$. The [angular frequency](../../../../angular-frequency.md) specifies temporal [wave phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) change per unit time, with period $2\pi/|\omega|$ and ordinary [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) $|\omega|/(2\pi)$. The constant $m$ in this equation has dimensions time divided by length squared; without an additional dimensional coefficient, it is not an ordinary physical [mass](../../../../mass.md).

For propagating oscillatory solutions assume real $m\ne0$. If $m=0$, the equation becomes $y_{xx}=0$ and only $k=0$ among these spatial [plane waves](../../../../plane-wave.md) is possible; the usual [dispersion relation](../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) branch and velocity discussion would degenerate.
