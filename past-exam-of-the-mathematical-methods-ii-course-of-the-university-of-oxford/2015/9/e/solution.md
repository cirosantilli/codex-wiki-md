<h1 id="9/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For $w=x^3e^{-x^2-y^2}$, the first [derivatives](../../../../derivative.md) are $w_x=x^2(3-2x^2)e^{-x^2-y^2}$ and $w_y=-2yx^3e^{-x^2-y^2}$. Both vanish at the origin. The local expansion is

$$
w(x,y)=x^3-x^5-x^3y^2+\cdots=x^3+O\!\left((x^2+y^2)^{5/2}\right).
$$

There are no constant, linear or quadratic terms, so every second [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) vanishes at the origin and the [Hessian determinant](../../../../hessian-determinant.md) is zero. The second-order test therefore cannot classify the point.

The cubic term does determine the behaviour: along $y=0$, $w(x,0)$ has the sign of $x$. Every neighbourhood contains values above and below $w(0,0)=0$, so it is neither a maximum nor a minimum. It is a [nonisolated stationary point](../../../../nonisolated-stationary-point.md) with nearby values of both signs. Indeed the whole line $x=0$ is stationary, so this is not an isolated nondegenerate quadratic saddle.
