<h1 id="11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The instantaneous [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) flux towards positive $x$ is the rate at which the string on the left does work on the string on the right. To first order its transverse [force](../../../../force-split.md) is $-T y_x$, so

$$
P(x,t)=-T y_x y_t.
$$

For a right-travelling sinusoidal wave $y=A\cos(kx-\omega t)$, $y_x=-Ak\sin(kx-\omega t)$ and $y_t=A\omega\sin(kx-\omega t)$. Thus

$$
P(x,t)=T\omega kA^2\sin^2(kx-\omega t),\qquad\boxed{\langle P\rangle=\frac12T\omega kA^2}.
$$

The printed constant formula is therefore the **time-average [power](../../../../power.md)**, not the instantaneous [power](../../../../power.md). The latter oscillates between zero and twice its average. A left-travelling wave carries negative signed flux through the same positively oriented section; its transported [power](../../../../power.md) magnitude has the same average formula with positive $k$.
