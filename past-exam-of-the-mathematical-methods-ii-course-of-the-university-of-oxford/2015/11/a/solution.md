<h1 id="11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For an equilibrium string segment of length $dx$, the mass is $\rho\,dx$. Under the small-slope approximation, the transverse [tension](../../../../tension.md) component at an endpoint is $T y_x$. The difference between its two endpoint contributions is $T[y_x(x+dx,t)-y_x(x,t)]\simeq T y_{xx}dx$.

[Newton's second law](../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) in the transverse direction gives $\rho y_{tt}dx=T y_{xx}dx$. Cancelling the segment length produces

$$
\boxed{y_{tt}-c^2y_{xx}=0,\qquad c=\sqrt{T/\rho}}.
$$

This [wave equation](../../../../wave-equation-split.md) assumes a uniform string, small slopes, negligible gravity and negligible bending stiffness. Longitudinal changes in [tension](../../../../tension.md) and the difference between actual and equilibrium segment length contribute only beyond the linear approximation used here.
