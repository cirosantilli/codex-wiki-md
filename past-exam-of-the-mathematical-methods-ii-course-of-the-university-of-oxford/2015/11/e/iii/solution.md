<h1 id="11/e/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

If $\rho_1\gg\rho_2$, then $k_1\gg k_2$ and the junction behaves approximately as a free end for the incident string. The limits are

$$
\boxed{r\to+1,\qquad t\to2,\qquad\mathcal R\to1,\qquad\mathcal T\to0}.
$$

The reflected [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) is not inverted, and at the junction it adds in [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) to the incident [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md), producing nearly twice its [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md). The transmitted [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) is also in [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md). A transmission [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) near two does not create [power](../../../../../power.md): the lighter string has much smaller impedance, and $\mathcal T=(k_2/k_1)t^2\simeq4k_2/k_1\to0$. These are limiting statements for positive densities, not an ordinary propagating-wave model for an exactly massless second string.
