<h1 id="11/e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $\rho_1\ll\rho_2$, then $k_1\ll k_2$ and the wave meets a much larger string impedance. The [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) and [power](../../../../../power.md) coefficients approach

$$
\boxed{r\to-1,\qquad t\to0^+,\qquad\mathcal R\to1,\qquad\mathcal T\to0}.
$$

The junction behaves approximately as a fixed end. The reflected [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) reverses sign, corresponding to a [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) shift of $\pi$; the small transmitted [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) is not inverted. For a large but finite density ratio, $t\simeq2k_1/k_2$ and the transmitted [power](../../../../../power.md) fraction is about $4k_1/k_2$, tending to zero rather than vanishing identically at every finite ratio.
