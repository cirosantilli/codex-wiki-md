<h1 id="10/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Put $k_1=k_3=k$ and $m_1=m_2=m$. The mass-normalized stiffness [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) is

$$
\frac1m\begin{pmatrix}k+k_2&-k_2\\-k_2&k+k_2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its symmetric [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) $(1,1)$ has [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $k/m$, while its antisymmetric [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) $(1,-1)$ has [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $(k+2k_2)/m$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\Omega_{\rm sym}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}},\qquad\Omega_{\rm anti}=\sqrt{\frac{k+2k_2}{m}}}.
$$

The symmetric [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) does not depend on the middle [spring constant](../../../../../spring-constant.md) because that [spring](../../../../../spring.md) has no extension in an in-phase [normal mode](../../../../../normal-mode.md). The antisymmetric motion stretches the middle [spring](../../../../../spring.md) and has the higher [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) for $k_2>0$.
