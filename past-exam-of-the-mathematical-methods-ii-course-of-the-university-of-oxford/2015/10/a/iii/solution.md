<h1 id="10/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

With the same positive [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) convention for both masses, the first [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) equation is $(k+k_2-m\Omega^2)a_1-k_2a_2=0$. At the symmetric [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) it reduces to $k_2(a_1-a_2)=0$, while at the antisymmetric [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) it becomes $-k_2(a_1+a_2)=0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\frac{a_1}{a_2}=+1\quad\text{for the symmetric mode},\qquad\frac{a_1}{a_2}=-1\quad\text{for the antisymmetric mode}}.
$$

In both [normal modes](../../../../../normal-mode.md) the [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) magnitudes are equal. For the first, the masses move in [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) and their separation stays unchanged; for the second they move in opposite [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md), alternately approaching and separating. A negative signed [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) ratio records a [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) difference of $\pi$, not a negative [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) magnitude. The left panel of the later figure plots these signed [normal mode](../../../../../normal-mode.md) shapes with the fixed wall values included.
