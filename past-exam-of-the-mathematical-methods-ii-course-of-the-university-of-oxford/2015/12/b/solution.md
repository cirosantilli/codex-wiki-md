<h1 id="12/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The field is continuously differentiable on all of $\mathbb R^3$, a [simply connected](../../../../simply-connected-space.md) domain. Its vanishing [curl](../../../../curl.md) therefore implies a global [potential of a conservative vector field](../../../../potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md), so $\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=d\phi$ is an [exact differential](../../../../exact-differential.md). This global domain condition matters: curl-free fields on domains with holes need not have single-valued potentials.

Integrating $\phi_x=2yz^2$ gives $\phi=2xyz^2+g(y,z)$. Matching $\phi_y=2xz^2$ forces $g_y=0$, so $g=h(z)$; matching $\phi_z=4xyz$ then forces $h'=0$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\phi(x,y,z)=2xyz^2+C},\qquad d\phi=2yz^2dx+2xz^2dy+4xyz\,dz.
$$

The additive constant does not affect the field or any endpoint difference.
