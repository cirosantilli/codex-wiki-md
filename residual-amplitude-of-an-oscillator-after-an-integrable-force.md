# Residual amplitude of an oscillator after an integrable force

↑ **Parent:** [Forced harmonic oscillator](forced-harmonic-oscillator.md)

Let $y''+\omega^2y=F(t)$ with $\omega>0$, zero initial displacement and [velocity](velocity.md), and real-valued forcing with $\int_0^\infty|F(t)|dt<\infty$. The causal [Green function](green-s-function-split.md) gives $y(t)=\omega^{-1}\int_0^tF(s)\sin[\omega(t-s)]ds$. Expanding the sine difference, the two coefficients tend to finite limits by [absolute convergence](absolute-convergence.md). Thus $y(t)$ approaches a sinusoid whose amplitude is the displayed [modulus](modulus.md) of the one-sided [Fourier transform](fourier-transform-split.md), divided by $\omega$. The displacement error is at most $\omega^{-1}\int_t^\infty|F(s)|ds$, which tends to zero. This describes the surviving free oscillation after a transient force; an arbitrary nonzero initial oscillation must be added before taking the final amplitude.
