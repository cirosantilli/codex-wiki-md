<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The microscopic electric interaction is [spin](../../../../spin.md) independent to leading order, but the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) ties the spatial symmetry of an electronic state to its [spin](../../../../spin.md) symmetry. Different [spin](../../../../spin.md) combinations therefore have different Coulomb and kinetic energies. Projecting those energies onto localized low-energy moments often gives an approximately rotationally invariant bilinear exchange [Hamiltonian operator](../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) $-J\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_2$. For [spin](../../../../spin.md) one half, the [spin singlet state](../../../../spin-singlet-state.md) and [triplet state](../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md) energies are distinguished by $\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_2=-3/4$ and $1/4$, so any isotropic two-level [spin singlet state](../../../../spin-singlet-state.md)–[triplet state](../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md) splitting can be written in this form plus a constant. More general [spins](../../../../spin.md) or orbital degeneracy can require extra interactions.

An antiferromagnetic oxide commonly has [superexchange](../../../../superexchange.md) through a ligand such as oxygen. Virtual hopping between half-filled metal orbitals lowers the energy of antiparallel [spins](../../../../spin.md) more strongly because the corresponding intermediate doubly occupied configurations are allowed by the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md). The simple one-orbital [Hubbard model](../../../../hubbard-model.md) limit gives $+4t^2\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_2/U$, equivalent to negative $J$ in the question's minus-sign convention. Bond angles and orbital occupancy can change the sign in more complicated oxides, so [superexchange](../../../../superexchange.md) is not universally antiferromagnetic irrespective of geometry.
