<h1 id="3/b/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $c<0$, maximize $P$ to lower the quartic coefficient. The identity $P=[1-(n_x^4+n_y^4+n_z^4)]/2$ shows that its maximum $1/3$ occurs for $n_x^2=n_y^2=n_z^2=1/3$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\langle111\rangle:\quad\mathbf n=(\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)/\sqrt3.}
$$

There are eight symmetry-related easy directions. This is a cubic easy-axis anisotropy even though the selected directions are body diagonals; it is not a continuous easy plane. At $c=0$ the quartic [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) instead has a sphere of degenerate orientations to this order.
