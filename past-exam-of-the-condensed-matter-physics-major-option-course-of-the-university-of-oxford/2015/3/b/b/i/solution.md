<h1 id="3/b/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $c>0$, minimize the nonnegative anisotropy invariant $P$. Its minimum is zero, achieved only when one squared component of the unit vector is one and the others vanish. Thus the easy directions are

$$
\boxed{\langle100\rangle:\quad\pm\hat{\mathbf x},\ \pm\hat{\mathbf y},\ \pm\hat{\mathbf z}.}
$$

The six signs and axes are symmetry-related ferromagnetic domains. Allowing the [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) amplitude to relax does not change the result: its minimum free-energy reduction is proportional to $-1/(b+cP)$, which is most negative for the smallest positive quartic coefficient. A $\langle110\rangle$ direction has $P=1/4$ and is not an equally stable easy direction.
