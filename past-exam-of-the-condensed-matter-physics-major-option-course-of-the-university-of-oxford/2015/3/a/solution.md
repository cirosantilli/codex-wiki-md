<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Hund's rules](../../../../hund-s-rules.md) maximizes total [spin](../../../../spin.md) within the partially filled shell: parallel [spins](../../../../spin.md) require an antisymmetric spatial wavefunction, reducing Coulomb overlap through exchange. Among terms with that [spin](../../../../spin.md), the second rule selects the largest total [orbital angular momentum](../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md). The third rule, from [spin-orbit coupling](../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md) within the selected term, gives $J=|L-S|$ below half filling and $J=L+S$ above half filling, with the half-filled case treated directly.

For seven $4f$ electrons, put one parallel-spin electron in each $m_l=-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3$ orbital. Their orbital projections sum to zero and form the half-filled $L=0$ shell; all seven [spins](../../../../spin.md) add. Thus

$$
\boxed{S=\frac72,\qquad L=0,\qquad J=\frac72,\qquad{}^{2S+1}L_J={}^8S_{7/2}.}
$$

There is no ambiguity from applying the below- or above-half-filled formula because $L=0$. The absence of a first-order orbital moment is important for the weak [magnetic anisotropy](../../../../magnetic-anisotropy.md) discussed in [c](../c.md). These are isolated-ion term rules; a crystal field or strong covalency can alter other ions' orbital behavior, but the half-filled Eu$^{2+}$ shell is unusually robust.
