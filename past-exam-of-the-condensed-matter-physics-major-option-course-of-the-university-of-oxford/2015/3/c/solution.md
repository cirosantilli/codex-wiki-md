<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Eu$^{2+}$ has the half-filled $4f^7$ term with $L=0$. First-order orbital magnetism is therefore quenched already at the ion level, making the dominant moment approximately spin-only. Spin-orbit-induced [magnetocrystalline anisotropy](../../../../magnetocrystalline-anisotropy.md) arises only through weaker admixtures and other small interactions; magnetic dipole interactions can also contribute. This explains why a cubic anisotropy term is allowed by symmetry but numerically weak.

An appropriate insulating local-moment model is a nearly isotropic [Heisenberg model](../../../../heisenberg-model.md),

$$
\boxed{H=-\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}J_{ij}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j
+H_{\rm weak\ anisotropy}+H_{\rm dipolar},\qquad S=7/2,}
$$

with dominant effective ferromagnetic couplings in the sign convention $J_{ij}>0$. The Eu sublattice in rocksalt EuO is face-centered cubic; nearest- and further-neighbor effective exchanges can be retained as needed. Because the solid is not metallic, an itinerant-electron [Stoner model](../../../../stoner-model.md) or a generic free-carrier [RKKY interaction](../../../../rkky-interaction.md) mechanism is not the natural starting point. Exchange can still arise through virtual hopping and ligand-mediated processes in an insulator; it does not require mobile dc carriers.
