<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [Frenkel exciton](../../../../frenkel-exciton.md) is a tightly bound electron–hole excitation localized on approximately one molecular or ionic unit. Its radius is comparable to the lattice spacing, its [exciton binding energy](../../../../exciton-binding-energy.md) can be large, and transport is described through transfer of the local excitation between units. Molecular solids such as crystalline [anthracene](../../../../anthracene.md) provide a natural example: the optical excitation is largely associated with an individual molecule, so a broad parabolic-band hydrogen model is not the most useful starting point.

A [Wannier-Mott exciton](../../../../wannier-mott-exciton.md) extends over many [unit cells](../../../../unit-cell.md). It is described approximately by a conduction electron and a valence hole with [effective masses](../../../../band-effective-mass.md) and a dielectric-screened Coulomb attraction. Its binding is usually smaller, and its levels resemble a hydrogenic series. [gallium arsenide](../../../../gallium-arsenide.md) is a suitable example because its direct-gap band-edge carriers have light [effective masses](../../../../band-effective-mass.md) and substantial dielectric [dielectric screening](../../../../dielectric-screening.md), supporting spatially extended [excitons](../../../../exciton.md). Localization length and [dielectric screening](../../../../dielectric-screening.md), rather than whether a material happens to be called a semiconductor, distinguish the two limiting pictures. Real materials can lie between the limits, so neither family is a universal sharp classification.
