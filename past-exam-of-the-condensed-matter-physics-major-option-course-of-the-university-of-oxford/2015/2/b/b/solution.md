<h1 id="2/b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Construct the first [Brillouin zone](../../../../../brillouin-zone.md) by the perpendicular bisectors between the origin and [reciprocal lattice](../../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md) points. For a test point $(h a^*,0,l c^*)$, the nearest relevant reciprocal points are $(0,0,\pm2c^*)$ and $(\pm a^*,0,\pm c^*)$. Their inequalities are

$$
|l|\leq1,\qquad
|h|(a^*)^2+|l|(c^*)^2\leq\frac{(a^*)^2+(c^*)^2}{2}.
$$

Set $\rho=(c^*/a^*)^2=(a/c)^2=0.113024$. The six vertices in conventional $(h,l)$ coordinates are

$$
\boxed{\left(\pm\frac{1+\rho}{2},0\right),\qquad
\left(\pm\frac{1-\rho}{2},\pm1\right).}
$$

Numerically these are $(\pm0.55651,0)$ and $(\pm0.44349,\pm1)$. The $k\ne0$ [reciprocal lattice vectors](../../../../../reciprocal-lattice-vector.md) must in principle be checked because the requested object is a section of a three-dimensional zone. For example $(\pm1,\pm1,0)$ gives the weaker bound $|h|\leq1$, and $(0,\pm1,\pm1)$ gives a weaker vertical bound; neither clips this hexagon. Direct comparison with surrounding three-dimensional [reciprocal lattice vectors](../../../../../reciprocal-lattice-vector.md) confirms the displayed section. It is not the rectangular reciprocal conventional cell.
