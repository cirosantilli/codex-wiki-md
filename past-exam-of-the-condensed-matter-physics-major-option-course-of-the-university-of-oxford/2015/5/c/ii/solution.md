<h1 id="5/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At low density, the [plasma frequency](../../../../../plasma-frequency.md) and free-carrier [dielectric screening](../../../../../dielectric-screening.md) are smaller, so radiation can penetrate a suitable semiconductor sample. In a high field with $\omega_c\tau\gg1$, the active circular component has a resolved cyclotron absorption line near $\omega=\omega_c$, with width of order $1/\tau$. The opposite circular component lacks that positive-frequency resonance.

Different complex refractive indices for the two components produce [Faraday rotation](../../../../../faraday-effect.md) and [circular dichroism](../../../../../circular-dichroism.md). Transmission with controlled [circular polarization](../../../../../circular-polarization.md) therefore isolates the [cyclotron resonance](../../../../../cyclotron-resonance.md) and measures $m^*=eB_{\rm res}/\omega$. The low-density/high-field labels do not by themselves imply complete transparency at every frequency; one must still avoid the plasma stop bands, strong interband absorption and excessive sample thickness.
