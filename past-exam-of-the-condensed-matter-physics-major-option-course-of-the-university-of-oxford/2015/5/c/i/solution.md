<h1 id="5/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

At zero magnetic field the two circular conductivities coincide, so an isotropic material has no magnetically induced [circular birefringence](../../../../../circular-birefringence.md). In the collisionless [Drude model](../../../../../drude-model.md) range the [dielectric function](../../../../../dielectric-function.md) is $\epsilon=\epsilon_\infty-\omega_p^2/\omega^2$. A high carrier density gives a large [plasma frequency](../../../../../plasma-frequency.md), often placing the screened plasma edge $\omega_p/\sqrt{\epsilon_\infty}$ in the infrared or visible range.

Below that edge the real [dielectric function](../../../../../dielectric-function.md) is negative and a transverse wave is evanescent in the bulk. The material is strongly reflecting, with penetration through a [skin depth](../../../../../skin-depth.md) and losses set by finite $1/\tau$. Above the plasma edge propagation becomes possible, subject to absorption and interband transitions. Carrier density alone does not specify a complete optical spectrum: the statements assume the probe frequency relative to the plasma scale and the validity of the single-band [Drude model](../../../../../drude-model.md).
