<h1 id="1/c/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

One [crystallographic inversion center](../../../../../crystallographic-inversion-center.md) is

$$
\boxed{(x_0,y_0,z_0)=(\tfrac14,\tfrac14,0).}
$$

The inversion maps $(x,y,z)$ to $(1/2-x,1/2-y,-z)$; it exchanges both the Fe pair and the Se pair modulo [lattice translations](../../../../../lattice-translation.md).

A $\bar4$ axis passes along $z$ through the Fe site $(0,0,0)$, and likewise through $(1/2,1/2,0)$. A representative [rotoinversion](../../../../../rotoinversion.md) is

$$
\boxed{(x,y,z)\mapsto(y,-x,-z).}
$$

It leaves the first Fe site fixed, maps the other Fe onto a translationally equivalent site, and exchanges the Se height classes. The $\bar4$ operation includes a vertical reversal, so it must not be replaced by a pure $90$-degree rotation about an Fe site. Such a pure rotation would put a selenium atom at the wrong height.
