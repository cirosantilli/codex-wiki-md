<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the full prism [point group](../../../../point-group.md), a threefold rotation eliminates any invariant basal component of a [polar vector](../../../../polar-vector.md), while the horizontal mirror eliminates its axial component. Therefore the phase preserving all of $D_{3h}$ is nonpolar, despite lacking inversion. A polar phase would have to break some of these symmetries.

The indicated six vertex directions comprise three basal directions and their horizontal-mirror partners. Crucially, this orbit does not contain the negatives of those vectors. Choose $z$ along the prism and $x$ toward one basal vertex. The cubic polynomial

$$
\mathcal I_3=P_x(P_x^2-3P_y^2)
$$

is invariant under the prism's rotations and mirrors. Along a vertex direction $\mathbf P=P\widehat{\mathbf n}$ with a nonzero basal component, it is proportional to $P^3$, so odd scalar terms are symmetry-allowed. Along the prism axis the horizontal mirror instead sends $P_z\mapsto-P_z$, forcing an even [free energy](../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md). Merely omitting inversion is insufficient to decide either polarity or the allowed invariants.
