<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Under spatial inversion the [electric polarization](../../../../polarization-density.md), a [polar vector](../../../../polar-vector.md), changes from $\mathbf P$ to $-\mathbf P$. [Neumann's principle](../../../../neumann-s-principle.md) requires an equilibrium property of a phase retaining [inversion symmetry](../../../../inversion-symmetry.md) to be unchanged. Thus $\mathbf P=-\mathbf P$, forcing $\mathbf P=0$: a centrosymmetric phase cannot itself be [ferroelectric](../../../../ferroelectricity.md). A transition can, of course, remove that [symmetry](../../../../symmetry-physics.md).

For an inversion-symmetric parent phase the [Landau theory](../../../../landau-theory.md) [free energy](../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) satisfies $F(\mathbf P)=F(-\mathbf P)$. Every polynomial term of odd total degree changes sign and must have zero coefficient. Restricting to one fixed direction gives an even scalar expansion in $P$.
