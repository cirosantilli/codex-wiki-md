<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use fractional [coordinates](../../../../coordinate-linear-algebra.md) and the [structure factor](../../../../structure-factor.md) convention

$$
F(\mathbf h)=\sum_j f_j e^{2\pi i\mathbf h\cdot\mathbf r_j},\qquad I(\mathbf h)\propto |F(\mathbf h)|^2.
$$

For real [atomic scattering factors](../../../../atomic-scattering-factor.md), $F(-\mathbf h)=F(\mathbf h)^*$, immediately proving [Friedel's law](../../../../friedel-s-law.md). If an inversion centre is chosen as origin, each site at $\mathbf r_j$ has an identical partner at $-\mathbf r_j$, with the same possibly complex [atomic scattering factor](../../../../atomic-scattering-factor.md). Each pair contributes $2f_j\cos(2\pi\mathbf h\cdot\mathbf r_j)$; sites fixed by inversion modulo a [lattice translation](../../../../lattice-translation.md) also have even phase factors at reciprocal-lattice vectors. Hence

$$
\boxed{F(-\mathbf h)=F(\mathbf h),\qquad I(-\mathbf h)=I(\mathbf h)}
$$

even for scalar [anomalous X-ray scattering](../../../../anomalous-x-ray-scattering.md) in a centrosymmetric structure. This last equality is an equality of amplitudes, not generally complex conjugacy.
