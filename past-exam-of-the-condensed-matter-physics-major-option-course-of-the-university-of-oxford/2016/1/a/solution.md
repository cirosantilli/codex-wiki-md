<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Away from an absorption edge, the scalar [atomic scattering factor](../../../../atomic-scattering-factor.md) is the [Fourier transform](../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) of an approximately spherical, real atomic [electron density](../../../../electron-density.md). Opposite points contribute conjugate phases, so their sum is real. In [Thomson scattering](../../../../thomson-scattering.md) the electronic response is also effectively in phase with the driving field in this nonabsorbing approximation. Near an absorption edge, resonantly driven electronic excitations introduce a phase lag and absorption. The [atomic scattering factor](../../../../atomic-scattering-factor.md) becomes $f_0+f'+if''$, with $f''$ describing the absorptive component and $f'$ its dispersive companion. The sign of the imaginary part depends on the time and scattering-phase conventions; here it is the source's $+if''$ convention. This discussion concerns scalar charge scattering, without magnetic or tensor resonant contributions.
