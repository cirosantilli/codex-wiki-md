<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The competition between magnetic screening over the [London penetration depth](../../../../london-penetration-depth.md) $\lambda$ and condensate recovery over the [superconducting coherence length](../../../../superconducting-coherence-length.md) $\xi$ is measured by the [Ginzburg-Landau parameter](../../../../ginzburg-landau-parameter.md) $\kappa=\lambda/\xi$. In the single-component local theory the boundary is $\kappa=1/\sqrt2$. For $\kappa<1/\sqrt2$, a normal–superconducting interface has positive energy: a [type-I superconductor](../../../../type-i-superconductor.md) favours macroscopic regions and a thermodynamic critical field $H_c$. Demagnetizing fields may create an intermediate state of normal and superconducting domains.

For $\kappa>1/\sqrt2$ the interface energy is negative. A [type-II superconductor](../../../../type-ii-superconductor.md) admits flux vortices between the lower and upper critical fields, with normal-like cores of size $\xi$ and circulating screening currents over $\lambda$. Each singly quantized vortex carries $\Phi_0=h/(2e)$; a vortex lattice and pinning-controlled irreversible response can occur. Pairing strength, carrier density, [Fermi velocity](../../../../fermi-velocity.md) and impurity scattering affect these lengths; $T_c$ alone does not determine the type. The stated boundary is the standard Ginzburg–Landau result near $T_c$.
