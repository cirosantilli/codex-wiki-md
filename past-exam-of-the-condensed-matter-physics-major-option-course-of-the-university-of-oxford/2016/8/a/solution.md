<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In [BCS theory](../../../../bcs-theory.md), an [electron](../../../../electron.md) distorts the positively charged lattice, and the retarded response can attract another [electron](../../../../electron.md). Within a phonon-energy shell around the [Fermi surface](../../../../fermi-surface.md), the effective attraction can overcome the screened Coulomb repulsion and destabilize the normal Fermi sea toward [Cooper pairs](../../../../cooper-pair.md). In conventional elemental [superconductors](../../../../superconductor.md) the pairs predominantly join time-reversed states of opposite momentum and [spin](../../../../spin.md), forming a [spin](../../../../spin.md) singlet.

In the weak-coupling approximation,

$$
\boxed{k_BT_c\simeq1.14\hbar\omega_D\exp[-1/(N(0)V_{\rm eff})]}.
$$

Here $N(0)$ is the appropriate single-spin [density of states](../../../../density-of-states.md) and $V_{\rm eff}>0$ the effective attractive interaction in a consistent normalization. Both the [phonon](../../../../phonon.md) scale and the exponential coupling matter. A large Fermi-level [density of states](../../../../density-of-states.md), often associated with partially filled narrow $d$ bands, can raise $T_c$; weak coupling or a small [density of states](../../../../density-of-states.md) can suppress it even in a good conductor. With force constants and coupling fixed, $\omega_D\propto M^{-1/2}$ gives the conventional isotope trend. This is not a universal monotonic ranking of elements by atomic mass: [phonon](../../../../phonon.md) stiffness, [electron](../../../../electron.md)–[phonon](../../../../phonon.md) matrix elements, Coulomb effects and magnetism vary between elements. Magnetic pair breaking helps explain why some elements do not follow a naive density-of-states expectation.
