<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

At a valley $K$, $\gamma(K)=1+2\cos(2\pi/3)=0$. For [h-BN](../../../../hexagonal-boron-nitride.md) the inequivalent sublattice energies therefore leave the direct two-[band gap](../../../../band-gap.md)

$$
\boxed{E_g=|\epsilon_B-\epsilon_N|.}
$$

[Graphene](../../../../graphene.md)'s equivalent carbon sites instead have $\epsilon_B=\epsilon_N$ in this model, so the two bands touch at the valleys. Near a valley, $|\gamma(K+\mathbf q)|\simeq(\sqrt3a/2)|\mathbf q|$: [graphene](../../../../graphene.md) has approximately linear, gapless [Dirac cones](../../../../dirac-cone.md) while h-BN has gapped band edges. The model panel in the original figure in [solution](../../1/c/ii/solution.md) displays this distinction.

First, undoped h-BN is an [electrical insulator](../../../../electrical-insulator.md) with thermally suppressed carriers, whereas neutral [graphene](../../../../graphene.md) is a zero-gap semimetal whose transport is readily changed by gating. Disorder, [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and contacts still affect its measured conductivity. Second, h-BN suppresses interband absorption below its large gap and is transparent over much of the visible range, whereas [graphene](../../../../graphene.md) permits low-[energy](../../../../energy-split.md) interband absorption unless occupations block it. A quantitative optical threshold in real h-BN also involves additional bands and strong excitonic effects; a two-orbital model should not be presented as a numerical prediction of its complete optical spectrum.
