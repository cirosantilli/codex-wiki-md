<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A sufficiently clean [two-dimensional electron gas](../../../../two-dimensional-electron-gas.md) at low [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) in a perpendicular [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) has quantized orbital [Landau levels](../../../../landau-level.md). In the integer quantum Hall regime, the [longitudinal resistivity](../../../../longitudinal-sheet-resistivity.md) is nearly zero while the Hall resistance has plateaux

$$
\boxed{|R_{xy}|=\frac{h}{p e^2},\qquad p\in\mathbb N.}
$$

The Hall sign depends on carrier charge and lead convention. Fractional plateaux can occur in strongly interacting states, but the following single-particle counting and [calibration](../../../../calibration-differential-geometry.md) use the integer effect.

One resolved [spin](../../../../spin.md) branch of a [Landau level](../../../../landau-level.md) has one state per flux quantum $h/e$: in area $A$, its guiding-centre degeneracy is $AeB_z/h$ for positive $B_z$. Defining the nominal [filling factor](../../../../filling-factor-quantum-hall-effect.md) as the number of [electrons](../../../../electron.md) per available orbital state gives

$$
\nu=\frac{N_SA}{AeB_z/h},\qquad\boxed{N_S=\frac{\nu eB_z}{h}.}
$$

The same count follows in Landau gauge: a $2\pi/L_y$ increment in $k_y$ moves the orbit centre by $2\pi\ell_B^2/L_y$, giving $A/(2\pi\ell_B^2)=AeB_z/h$ states. Include each [spin](../../../../spin.md) branch separately; an unresolved [spin](../../../../spin.md)-degenerate orbital level contains twice this count, and common plateau indices are then even.

**Plateau qualification:** $\nu$ as defined by density is continuous when $B_z$ changes at fixed $N_S$. The integer plateau index $p$ can remain unchanged over a field interval because localized states accommodate the changing occupation. Therefore $N_S=peB_z/h$ is not an exact identity across an entire plateau. It is the ideal filled-level relation, often used approximately at a suitable plateau centre; confusing the nominal filling with the plateau index would contradict fixed density.

The plateau resistance realizes the [von Klitzing constant](../../../../von-klitzing-constant.md) $R_K=h/e^2\simeq25812.8\,\Omega$, divided by a known integer. A four-terminal Hall measurement with negligible longitudinal voltage and contacts in the quantized regime supplies a reproducible resistance reference, which can be compared to other resistors using precision bridges. Its robustness comes from the quantized transport state, not from measuring the [Hall bar](../../../../hall-bar.md)'s width accurately.
