<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For an ideal long specimen with negligible [demagnetizing field](../../../../demagnetizing-field.md), the Meissner state has $B=\mu_0(H+M)=0$, so $M=-H$. A [type-I superconductor](../../../../type-i-superconductor.md) retains this response up to its thermodynamic critical field $H_c(T)$ and then enters the normal state, where the small normal magnetic response can be neglected. Nonzero [demagnetizing fields](../../../../demagnetizing-field.md) can create an intermediate state and broaden the measured transition.

A [type-II superconductor](../../../../type-ii-superconductor.md) is fully screening only below $H_{c1}(T)$. Between $H_{c1}$ and $H_{c2}$, magnetic flux enters as quantized vortices and the reversible [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) remains diamagnetic but decreases in magnitude towards zero at $H_{c2}$. Above $H_{c2}$ the bulk is normal. Ginzburg–Landau theory distinguishes the types at $\kappa=1/\sqrt2$, not at $\kappa=1$; the later condition in the source merely restricts its estimate.

The $H$–$T$ sketch has two increasing-width regions below $T_c$: Meissner below the lower boundary, mixed [vortex](../../../../phase-vortex.md) state between the two boundaries, and normal state above the upper boundary. Both critical fields vanish at $T_c$. The phase-diagram panel of the original figure in [solution](../../3/a/solution.md) shows this topology; its normalized field curves are illustrative, not a fit to the source [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) loop.
