<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Below $T_N$, a small perpendicular field produces canting of the two ordered sublattices. In the isotropic two-sublattice mean-field model, the induced component is $B_\perp/\lambda$: the reduced ordered moment at higher [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) tilts more easily and the two effects cancel. Thus $\chi_\perp$ remains constant until the transition.

A field parallel to the maintained ordering axis instead increases the magnitude of one sublattice [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) and decreases the other. At zero [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) saturated localized [spins](../../../../spin.md) have essentially no such amplitude response in this model, so $\chi_\parallel\to0$. Thermal population changes make it easier to redistribute the longitudinal moments as [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) rises, giving the growing longitudinal susceptibility that meets $\chi_\perp$ at $T_N$. These directions presume the domain or a weak anisotropy fixes the ordered axis; in a perfectly isotropic freely rotating sample the Néel vector can reorient.

Above $T_N$ there is no chosen magnetic axis, so the response is isotropic. Antiferromagnetic correlations oppose uniform [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md), giving the mean-field [Curie–Weiss](../../../../curie-weiss-law.md) form

$$
\boxed{\chi(T)=\frac{C}{T+T_N},\qquad
C=\frac{\mu_0(g\mu_B)^2S(S+1)}{3k_Ba^3},\quad
k_BT_N=2J_1S(S+1).}
$$

At $T_N$ it equals $C/(2T_N)=\mu_0/\lambda$, consistent with the transverse branch; at large [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) it decreases as $1/T$. A negative Weiss [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) characterizes this uniform antiferromagnetic response, rather than a ferromagnetic divergence at positive $T_N$. The illustrative mean-field curves in [solution](../a/solution.md) make these limits visible.
