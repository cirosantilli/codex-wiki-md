<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For type I, a [magnetic unit cell](../../../../magnetic-unit-cell.md) uses the magnetic Bravais translations $(0,a,a)$, $(a,0,a)$ and $(a,a,0)$. They form an [FCC lattice](../../../../face-centered-cubic-lattice.md) with conventional cubic parameter $2a$. A two-[spin](../../../../spin.md) [basis](../../../../crystal-motif.md) is an up [spin](../../../../spin.md) at $(0,0,0)$ and a down [spin](../../../../spin.md) at $(a,0,0)$, or fractional conventional coordinates $(0,0,0)$ and $(1/2,0,0)$. Every translation preserves the parity of $n_x+n_y+n_z$.

At $\mathbf Q=(2\pi/a)(h,k,l)$, the FCC translation sum allows $h,k,l$ all integers or all half-odd-integers. The [spin](../../../../spin.md) [basis](../../../../crystal-motif.md) contributes $S[1-e^{2\pi ih}]$, which removes the integer set and leaves

$$
\boxed{\text{type I: }(h,k,l)=(n+\tfrac12,m+\tfrac12,p+\tfrac12).}
$$

Equivalently the [spin](../../../../spin.md) field is $S(-1)^{n_x+n_y+n_z}$, so [magnetic Bragg peaks](../../../../magnetic-bragg-peak.md) are nuclear reciprocal vectors shifted by $(1/2,1/2,1/2)$.

For type II with parallel [spins](../../../../spin.md) along $z$, use $(a,a,0)$, $(-a,a,0)$ and $(0,0,a)$ as magnetic translations: a primitive tetragonal cell with side lengths $\sqrt2a,\sqrt2a,a$, containing an up [spin](../../../../spin.md) at zero and a down [spin](../../../../spin.md) at $(a,0,0)$. The reciprocal translations have $h,k$ both integers or both half-odd-integers, with $l$ integer. The same [basis](../../../../crystal-motif.md) factor removes the integer-$h$ set:

$$
\boxed{\text{type II, parallel }z:\ (h,k,l)=(n+\tfrac12,m+\tfrac12,p).}
$$

This also follows from $S(-1)^{n_x+n_y}$. Other domains permute the three coordinate directions. A magnetic [neutron](../../../../neutron-split.md) cross section further multiplies these [lattice](../../../../bravais-lattice.md) conditions by the [magnetic form factor](../../../../magnetic-form-factor.md) and by the component of ordered moment perpendicular to $\mathbf Q$; that polarization factor can suppress an otherwise allowed peak.
