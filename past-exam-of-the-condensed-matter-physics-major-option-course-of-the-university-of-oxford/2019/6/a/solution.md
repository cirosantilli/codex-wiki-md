<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In a conventional [superconductor](../../../../superconductor.md), an attractive pairing interaction forms [Cooper pairs](../../../../cooper-pair.md) and a phase-coherent condensate. The elementary fermionic excitations have a [superconducting energy gap](../../../../superconducting-energy-gap.md) $\Delta$; breaking a pair into two excitations costs at least $2\Delta$. The density of thermally excited [quasiparticles](../../../../quasiparticle.md) is exponentially small for $k_BT\ll\Delta$. Nevertheless the vanishing DC [electrical resistivity](../../../../electrical-resistivity.md) below the critical temperature is a property of the coherent supercurrent, not a claim that all thermal quasiparticles disappear. Below the relevant critical-current and critical-field limits, a homogeneous bulk sample carries dissipationless current; above the superconducting transition the normal resistivity returns.

In a [semiconductor](../../../../semiconductor-split.md), the [band gap](../../../../band-gap.md) separates filled valence states from empty conduction states because of the periodic crystal potential and orbital hybridization. Thermally exciting carriers across the gap produces both conduction [electrons](../../../../electron.md) and valence [holes](../../../../electron-hole.md). For an intrinsic sample,

$$
n=p\propto T^{3/2}e^{-E_g/(2k_BT)},\qquad\sigma=e(n\mu_e+p\mu_h).
$$

Thus its [electrical resistivity](../../../../electrical-resistivity.md) generally rises rapidly on cooling, with an activated factor $e^{E_g/(2k_BT)}$ up to mobility and density-of-states prefactors. This differs from the zero-resistivity condensate response. Dopant freeze-out and extrinsic-carrier regimes require their own activation scales; the intrinsic formula is not universal for every doped sample.
