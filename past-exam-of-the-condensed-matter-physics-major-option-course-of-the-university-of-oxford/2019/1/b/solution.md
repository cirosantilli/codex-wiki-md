<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [primitive unit cell](../../../../primitive-unit-cell.md) contains two [carbon](../../../../carbon.md) atoms and four edge-shared [oxygen](../../../../oxygen.md) atoms, each contributing one half: $n=2+4/2=4$ atoms. Equivalently, one molecular repeat contributes its two carbon atoms but only half of each of its four shared oxygen atoms. There are therefore $3n=12$ [phonon branches](../../../../phonon-branch.md).

At the [Brillouin zone](../../../../brillouin-zone.md) centre, three rigid translations give three [acoustic phonon branches](../../../../acoustic-phonon-branch.md); the remaining $3n-3$ are [optical phonon branches](../../../../optical-phonon-branch.md):

$$
\boxed{3\text{ acoustic}+9\text{ optical}=12\text{ phonon branches}.}
$$

A free layer has two in-plane acoustic modes and an out-of-plane flexural acoustic mode. A two-dimensional lattice does not imply that its atoms can move only in two directions. Artificially suppressing all out-of-plane motion would instead define a different model with two acoustic and six optical branches.
