<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the indicated out-of-plane displacements and the equivalence of the four [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) sites and of the two [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) sites in the isolated planar [oxalate](../../../../../oxalate.md) ion. In $V_1$, the two [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) atoms on one side move oppositely to those on the other side, and the two [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) atoms also move oppositely. Their dipole changes cancel. The mode is even under molecular [inversion symmetry](../../../../../inversion-symmetry.md), which independently forbids an electric-dipole transition.

In $V_2$, all four [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) atoms move together out of the plane while both [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) atoms move in the opposite direction. The positive and negative charge distributions acquire a relative out-of-plane displacement, producing a nonzero derivative of the molecular [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md).

In $V_3$, two [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) atoms move out and two move in, in a checkerboard arrangement, while the [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) atoms are stationary. The equivalent oxygen contributions cancel. This mode illustrates that being odd under [inversion symmetry](../../../../../inversion-symmetry.md) is necessary but not sufficient for [vibrational infrared activity](../../../../../vibrational-infrared-activity.md): its full symmetry does not transform like any component of an [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md).

$$
\boxed{V_2\text{ is IR active};\qquad V_1,V_3\text{ are IR inactive}.}
$$

These conclusions concern the isolated-molecule displacements drawn in the source; a different crystal environment can alter selection rules through additional symmetry breaking.
