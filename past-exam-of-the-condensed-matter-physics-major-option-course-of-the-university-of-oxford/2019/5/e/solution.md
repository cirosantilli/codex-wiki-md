<h1 id="5/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Measure [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) along $z$ at low temperature and use a small field to select a single weak-ferromagnetic domain. After domain alignment, subtract the approximately linear field-induced susceptibility background to obtain the spontaneous weak moment, using the extrapolated intercept of that branch rather than a multidomain zero-field average.

There are four iron ions per orthorhombic cell. If the spontaneous magnetization density is $M_z$ and the cell volume is $V_{\mathrm{cell}}$, the ordered moment and dimensionless spin component per ion are

$$
\boxed{m_z=\frac{M_zV_{\mathrm{cell}}}{4}=g\mu_BS\sin\theta,\qquad\langle S_z\rangle=\frac{M_zV_{\mathrm{cell}}}{4g\mu_B}.}
$$

Here spin components are expressed in units of $\hbar$ as in the source Hamiltonian. Alternatively normalize the magnetic moment to the measured number of iron ions. A field too large to preserve the small-canting structure would not measure the zero-field component directly. For high-spin Fe$^{3+}$ with $S=5/2$ and $g\simeq2$, the angle above corresponds to approximately $0.026\mu_B$ per iron ion.
