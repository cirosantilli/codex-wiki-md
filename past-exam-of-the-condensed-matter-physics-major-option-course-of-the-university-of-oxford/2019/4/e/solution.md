<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Above the transition, $\epsilon=0$ and both site types have the same [g-factor](../../../../g-factor-physics.md) $g$. For the given spin-only Zeeman Hamiltonian, adjacent $S=1$ levels have the same energy spacing, so fixed-frequency [electron paramagnetic resonance](../../../../electron-paramagnetic-resonance.md) gives a single resonance field $B_0=h\nu/(g\mu_B)$, ignoring unresolved additional interactions.

Below the transition, half the elongation axes lie along $x$ and half along $y$. A field along $x$ is parallel to the long bond on one sublattice and perpendicular on the other. The line splits into

$$
\boxed{B_\parallel=\frac{h\nu}{\mu_B(g+\epsilon/\gamma)}<B_0,\qquad B_\perp=\frac{h\nu}{\mu_B(g-\epsilon/\delta)}>B_0.}
$$

The separation grows on cooling as the distortion grows. Equal site populations give comparable integrated weights when the microwave coupling to the two sites is equivalent; populations alone do not fix all polarization-dependent intensities.

A suitable staggered structural [order parameter](../../../../order-parameter.md) is

$$
\Psi=\frac12[(l_x-l_y)_A-(l_x-l_y)_B].
$$

For an $x$-elongated A site and a $y$-elongated B site, $\Psi=\epsilon$, with the opposite sign for the domain interchanging the pattern. The [electron paramagnetic resonance](../../../../electron-paramagnetic-resonance.md) data determine its magnitude from

$$
\boxed{|\Psi|=\frac{(h\nu/\mu_B)(B_\parallel^{-1}-B_\perp^{-1})}{\gamma^{-1}+\delta^{-1}}.}
$$

For small splitting this is equivalently proportional to $B_\perp-B_\parallel$, but the reciprocal-field formula follows directly from the stated [g-factors](../../../../g-factor-physics.md). It does not fix the sign of the structural domain. In a continuous transition $|\Psi|$ tends continuously to zero at $T_{\mathrm{JT}}$ and grows below it, eventually saturating. A mean-field sketch has $|\Psi|\propto(T_{\mathrm{JT}}-T)^{1/2}$ close to the transition; continuity alone does not determine that exponent. The last panel of the original orbital figure shows this illustrative behavior.
