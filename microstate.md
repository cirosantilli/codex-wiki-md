# Microstate

↑ **Parent:** [State degeneracy](state-degeneracy.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microstate)

A [microstate](microstate.md) is a complete microscopic specification within the chosen model. For two distinguishable, noninteracting [two-level systems](two-level-system.md), label the units $A,B$ and their states $0,1$, with [energies](energy-split.md) $0,\Delta$ and $\Delta>0$. The four [microstates](microstate.md) are

$$
(0,0),\quad(0,1),\quad(1,0),\quad(1,1),
$$

with total [energies](energy-split.md) $0,\Delta,\Delta,2\Delta$. Distinguishability matters: $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$ describe different assignments to the labeled units. Noninteraction matters too: their [energies](energy-split.md) add without an extra coupling term.

For a classical particle model, a [microstate](microstate.md) can instead be a point in [phase space](phase-space.md), specifying every [position](position.md) and [momentum](momentum-split.md). In the quantum [canonical ensemble](canonical-ensemble-split.md), the sum uses [energy eigenstates](energy-eigenstate.md); a general [quantum state](quantum-state.md) may also contain [quantum coherence](quantum-coherence-in-a-specified-basis.md), which an [energy](energy-split.md) label alone does not specify.
