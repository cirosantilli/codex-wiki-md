# Quantized phase levels in a Josephson junction

↑ **Parent:** [Current-biased Josephson phase dynamics](current-biased-josephson-phase-dynamics.md)

For a weakly damped well, quantizing the conservative part of [current-biased Josephson phase dynamics](current-biased-josephson-phase-dynamics.md) gives the local [Hamiltonian operator](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md)

$$
H_\phi=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2M_\phi}\frac{d^2}{d\phi^2}+U(\phi).
$$

The tilted [potential energy](potential-energy.md) is unbounded downhill. Its trapped [energy levels](energy-level.md) are therefore long-lived resonances with escape lifetimes, rather than globally bound stationary states. Close to $\phi_m$, set $x=\phi-\phi_m$ and expand

$$
U(\phi_m+x)=U(\phi_m)+\frac12M_\phi\omega_p^2x^2-\frac{E_Js}{6}x^3+O(x^4).
$$

Keeping only the quadratic term gives the [quantum harmonic oscillator](quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md) result

$$
E_n-U(\phi_m)\simeq\hbar\omega_p\left(n+\frac12\right),\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.
$$

It is useful for states localized near the minimum, with [energy](energy-split.md) well below $\Delta U$. It does not count all the resonances accurately near escape. The omitted terms make successive spacings unequal. For the chosen example, the three plotted reference [energies](energy-split.md) divided by $h$ are $1.44804$, $4.34412$ and $7.24020\,\mathrm{GHz}$ above the minimum.

A small [microwave](microwave.md) bias contributes $\delta U=-(\hbar/2e)I_\mu(t)\phi$. A one-[photon](photon.md) transition between [energy levels](energy-level.md) is resonant when $hf=E_{n+1}-E_n$. Exciting a higher [energy level](energy-level.md) can strongly increase its escape probability. Measuring [microwave](microwave.md) absorption indirectly through escape therefore connects an invisible [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md) state to a visible [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md). A classical oscillation can also show resonance, so the mere existence of one peak is insufficient: the bias dependence and resolved unequal spacings are the discriminating predictions.
