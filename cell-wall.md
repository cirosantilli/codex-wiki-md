# Cell wall

↑ **Parent:** [Biological cell](biological-cell.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_wall)

A [cell wall](cell-wall.md) is a supporting structure outside a [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md). It helps preserve shape and bears the load when [osmosis](osmosis.md) raises internal [pressure](pressure.md). The [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md) supplies the [selective permeability](selective-permeability.md) described in its own article; the [cell wall](cell-wall.md) supplies mechanical reinforcement. These functions cooperate: a porous supporting layer can restrain expansion without being the boundary that retains dissolved [solutes](solute.md). [Turgor pressure](turgor-pressure.md) makes this cooperation visible in a firm leaf; [plasmolysis](plasmolysis.md) and a [lysozyme wall-removal experiment](lysozyme-wall-removal-experiment.md) separate the two functions experimentally.[Biology 2e, §5.2, Tonicity in Living Systems, Figure 5.13: wall support and plasmolysis](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/5-2-passive-transport.md)

The material is not universal. Most [bacteria](bacteria.md) use [peptidoglycan](peptidoglycan.md), whose linked [sugar](sugar.md) chains and [amino acid](amino-acid.md) cross-links are explained below. A [plant cell wall](plant-cell-wall.md) uses [cellulose](cellulose.md); a [fungal cell wall](fungal-cell-wall.md) contains [chitin](chitin.md) and [glucans](glucan.md). [Animals](animal.md) lack a [cell wall](cell-wall.md), some [bacteria](bacteria.md) are naturally wall-free, and [archaea](archaea.md) have different wall chemistry. Thus a [cell wall](cell-wall.md) is neither a universal cellular component nor one chemical recipe.[Biology 2e, §22.2, Figure 22.13 and The Cell Wall of Prokaryotes: wall-free bacteria and archaeal differences](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/22-2-structure-of-prokaryotes-bacteria-and-archaea.md)[Biology 2e, §4.3, Animal Cells versus Plant Cells and The Cell Wall: cellulose and animal comparison](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/4-3-eukaryotic-cells.md)[Biology 2e, §24.1, Cell Structure and Function: chitin and glucans](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/24-1-characteristics-of-fungi.md)

The [bacterial outer membrane](bacterial-outer-membrane.md) in typical [Gram-negative bacteria](gram-negative-bacteria.md) is an additional membrane outside the [peptidoglycan](peptidoglycan.md), not another name for that supporting mesh. The [cell envelope](cell-envelope.md) includes the boundary layers together. An extra barrier can change access to the [peptidoglycan](peptidoglycan.md), so response to [lysozyme](lysozyme.md) cannot be inferred from the mere presence of a [cell wall](cell-wall.md).[Biology 2e, §22.2, Figure 22.16: inner membrane, peptidoglycan and outer membrane](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/22-2-structure-of-prokaryotes-bacteria-and-archaea.md)
