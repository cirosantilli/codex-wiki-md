## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2017](2017.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Apply [Itô's formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) to $f(u,s)=e^{-\alpha u}s$. Its second stock derivative is zero, and the two drift terms cancel, giving

$$
d(e^{-\alpha u}S_u)=\sigma e^{-\alpha u}\,dW_u.
$$

Integrating and multiplying by $e^{\alpha T}$ solves the [linear stochastic differential equation](../stochastic-calculus.md#linear-stochastic-differential-equation):

$$
\boxed{S_T=e^{\alpha(T-t)}S_t+\sigma\int_t^T e^{\alpha(T-u)}\,dW_u.}
$$

The [Itô integral](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) has a deterministic integrand, so it is a centered [Gaussian random variable](../probability-distribution.md#gaussian-random-variable) independent of the current [filtration](../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) $\mathcal F_t$. Its [variance](../variance.md) follows from the [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry). With $\tau=T-t$,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[S_T\mid\mathcal F_t]=e^{\alpha\tau}S_t,\qquad
\operatorname{var}(S_T\mid\mathcal F_t)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\sigma^2}{2\alpha}(e^{2\alpha\tau}-1),&\alpha\ne0,\\
\sigma^2\tau,&\alpha=0.
\end{cases}}
$$

The second line is also the [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function) as $\alpha\to0$. The normal law asserted here is conditional on $\mathcal F_t$; an arbitrary random initial value need not give an unconditional [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Use a [self-financing portfolio](../mathematical-finance.md#self-financing-portfolio) with $\Delta_t$ units of the traded asset and the remainder in the [continuous-time bank account](../mathematical-finance.md#continuous-time-bank-account). If its value equals $V(S_t,t)$, its [self-financing condition](../mathematical-finance.md#self-financing-condition) is

$$
dV=\Delta_t\,dS_t+r(V-\Delta_tS_t)\,dt.
$$

Meanwhile [Itô's formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) gives

$$
dV=(V_t+\alpha S_tV_S+\tfrac12\sigma^2V_{SS})\,dt+\sigma V_S\,dW_t.
$$

Matching the [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) coefficients gives the [delta hedge](../mathematical-finance.md#delta-hedge) $\Delta_t=V_S$. Matching drifts then cancels the physical drift $\alpha$, leaving the [pricing equation for a linear asset with additive noise](../mathematical-finance.md#pricing-equation-for-a-linear-asset-with-additive-noise):

$$
\boxed{V_t+\frac12\sigma^2V_{SS}+rSV_S-rV=0,\qquad V(S,T)=P_{\mathrm o}(S).}
$$

The domain is the full [real line](../real-analysis.md#real-line): additive noise can take the asset through zero. The [diffusion coefficient](../brownian-motion.md#diffusion-coefficient) is $\sigma$, so the second-derivative term has no factor $S^2$. As usual, the replication argument assumes suitable differentiability and an [admissible trading strategy](../mathematical-finance.md#admissible-trading-strategy).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

The differential operator in part [b](#1/b) is the [infinitesimal generator](../stochastic-process.md#infinitesimal-generator-stochastic-processes) of the auxiliary [diffusion process](../stochastic-calculus.md#markov-diffusion) $dS_u=rS_u\,du+\sigma\,dW_u$, with killing rate $r$. The [Feynman-Kac formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#feynman-kac-formula) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{V(S,t)=e^{-r(T-t)}\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}[P_{\mathrm o}(S_T)\mid S_t=S].}
$$

Equivalently, [Itô's formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) makes $e^{-r(u-t)}V(S_u,u)$ a [local martingale](../martingale.md#local-martingale) under these dynamics; under appropriate growth and integrability assumptions it is a [martingale](../martingale.md), whose terminal [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) yields the same formula. The expectation uses the drift-$r$ law, rather than the physical drift-$\alpha$ law in part [a](#1/a). This is [risk-neutral pricing](../mathematical-finance.md#risk-neutral-pricing).

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

Apply part [a](#1/a) with $\alpha=r$. For $\tau>0$, the [transition density](../markov-chain.md#transition-density) is the [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution) with mean $m=Se^{r\tau}$ and positive [variance](../variance.md) $v=\sigma^2(e^{2r\tau}-1)/(2r)$. Integrating the [financial payoff](../fundamental-theorem-of-asset-pricing.md#contingent-claim-payoff) against this [probability density function](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function) gives

$$
\boxed{V(S,t)=\frac{e^{-r\tau}}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P_{\mathrm o}(x)
\exp\!\left[-\frac{(x-Se^{r\tau})^2}{2v}\right]dx,\qquad
v=\frac{\sigma^2}{2r}(e^{2r\tau}-1).}
$$

Thus $d_*=(x-Se^{r\tau})/\sqrt v$, as required. The [variance](../variance.md) is positive even if $r<0$, because numerator and denominator have the same sign. For $r=0$, replace $m$ by $S$ and $v$ by $\sigma^2\tau$; at expiry use the terminal value directly. The [integral](../integral.md) presumes an integrable [financial payoff](../fundamental-theorem-of-asset-pricing.md#contingent-claim-payoff).

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2017](2017.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

For a positive starting value, [Itô's formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) applied to $\log S_u$ gives $d\log S_u=(\mu-\sigma^2/2)du+\sigma dW_u$. Hence the [geometric Brownian motion](../stochastic-calculus.md#geometric-brownian-motion) is

$$
\boxed{S_T=S\exp\!\left[(\mu-\sigma^2/2)\tau+\sigma(W_T-W_t)\right].}
$$

The future [Brownian increment](../brownian-motion.md#brownian-increment) $W_T-W_t$ is independent of $\mathcal F_t$ and has [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution) $N(0,\tau)$. If $\widetilde W_v=W_{t+v}-W_t$, then $\widetilde W$ is a fresh [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) and the increment is exactly $\widetilde W_\tau$. This specifies the meaning of the paper's abbreviated $W_\tau$; the original process value $W_{T-t}$ is not generally that future increment. The [stochastic exponential](../stochastic-calculus.md#doleans-dade-exponential) solution also verifies positivity for $S>0$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Under the [risk-neutral measure](../mathematical-finance.md#risk-neutral-measure), write $Z\sim N(0,1)$, $a=\sigma\sqrt\tau$, and $m=\log(S/K)+(r-\sigma^2/2)\tau$. Then $\log(S_T/K)=m+aZ$ and $d_-=m/a$. By [risk-neutral pricing](../mathematical-finance.md#risk-neutral-pricing), this [log-call option](../fundamental-theorem-of-asset-pricing.md#log-call-option) has value $e^{-r\tau}\mathbb E[(m+aZ)^+]$.

Let $\phi$ and $\Phi$ denote the [standard normal density](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-density) and [standard normal distribution function](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-distribution-function). The [Gaussian positive-part expectation](../probability-distribution.md#gaussian-positive-part-expectation) follows by integration:

$$
\mathbb E[(m+aZ)^+]
=\int_{-d_-}^{\infty}(m+az)\phi(z)\,dz
=m\Phi(d_-)+a\phi(d_-),
$$

because $\phi'(z)=-z\phi(z)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{V(S,t)=e^{-r\tau}\sigma\sqrt\tau
\left[d_-\Phi(d_-)+\frac{e^{-d_-^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right],\qquad
d_-=\frac{\log(S/K)+(r-\sigma^2/2)\tau}{\sigma\sqrt\tau}.}
$$

For $\tau=0$ take the continuous terminal [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function) $(\log(S/K))^+$.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

The [Black-Scholes reflection symmetry](../mathematical-finance.md#black-scholes-reflection-symmetry) becomes a weighted reflection after taking a [logarithm](../calculus.md#logarithm). Put $x=\log(S/B)$ and $v(x,t)=V(Be^x,t)$. The [multivariable chain rule](../calculus.md#multivariable-chain-rule) transforms the equation into

$$
v_t+\tfrac12\sigma^2v_{xx}+bv_x-rv=0,\qquad b=r-\sigma^2/2.
$$

For $w(x,t)=e^{\gamma x}v(-x,t)$, its left-hand side, after removing the factor $e^{\gamma x}$, is

$$
v_t+\tfrac12\sigma^2v_{xx}-(\sigma^2\gamma+b)v_x
+(\tfrac12\sigma^2\gamma^2+b\gamma-r)v,
$$

evaluated at $(-x,t)$. Taking $\gamma=-2b/\sigma^2$ makes the derivative coefficient $b$ and the additional zeroth-order term zero. Thus

$$
\boxed{W(S,t)=(S/B)^{1-2r/\sigma^2}V(B^2/S,t)}
$$

satisfies the same [Black-Scholes equation](../mathematical-finance.md#black-scholes-equation).

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

Subtract the reflected solution from part [c](#2/c). On the alive side of the [down-and-out barrier option](../fundamental-theorem-of-asset-pricing.md#down-and-out-claim),

$$
\boxed{V_B(S,t)=V(S,t)-(S/B)^{1-2r/\sigma^2}V(B^2/S,t),\qquad S>B.}
$$

Both terms satisfy the [Black-Scholes equation](../mathematical-finance.md#black-scholes-equation), and at $S=B$ they cancel. At expiry, $S\geq B$ gives $B^2/S\leq B<K$, so the reflected [log-call option](../fundamental-theorem-of-asset-pricing.md#log-call-option) has zero terminal [financial payoff](../fundamental-theorem-of-asset-pricing.md#contingent-claim-payoff); the first term gives the required payoff. With the usual growth condition, uniqueness for the killed pricing problem identifies the expression as the [risk-neutral price](../mathematical-finance.md#risk-neutral-price). After the barrier has been hit the contract value is zero. The algebraic image expression on $S<B$ is not its financial value.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2017](2017.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

A [European call option](../fundamental-theorem-of-asset-pricing.md#european-call-option) has nonnegative [financial payoff](../fundamental-theorem-of-asset-pricing.md#contingent-claim-payoff), so its price is nonnegative under [no-arbitrage](../mathematical-finance.md#no-arbitrage). A second [arbitrage](../mathematical-finance.md#arbitrage) comparison buys one call and a [zero-coupon bond](../mathematical-finance.md#zero-coupon-bond) paying $K$, and shorts one share. Its terminal value is $(S_T-K)^++K-S_T=(K-S_T)^+\geq0$, so its initial cost cannot be negative. Consequently

$$
\boxed{C_t^e\geq\max(S_t-Ke^{-r(T-t)},0).}
$$

The holder of an [American call option](../mathematical-finance.md#american-call-option) can choose to wait until expiry, so $C_t^a\geq C_t^e$. Since $r>0$ and $t<T$, an in-the-money call has $C_t^a\geq S_t-Ke^{-r(T-t)}>S_t-K$: immediate exercise sacrifices value. An out-of-the-money exercise pays zero and cannot improve the holder's position. This proves [no early exercise of a call without dividends](../mathematical-finance.md#no-early-exercise-of-a-call-without-dividends): waiting is always at least as good, and is strictly better for positive immediate exercise payoff. In a degenerate market with zero continuation value an out-of-the-money holder can be indifferent; the [Black-Scholes model](../mathematical-finance.md#black-scholes-model) with positive volatility gives strictly positive continuation value before expiry.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

For the [perpetual American call option](../mathematical-finance.md#perpetual-american-call-option), substitute a power $S^q$ into the stationary [Black-Scholes equation with continuous stock dividends](../mathematical-finance.md#black-scholes-equation-with-continuous-stock-dividends). The [characteristic equation](../linear-ordinary-differential-equation.md#characteristic-equation-of-a-constant-coefficient-differential-equation) is

$$
F(q)=\tfrac12\sigma^2q(q-1)+(r-y)q-r=0.
$$

Its two [roots](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system) have negative product $-2r/\sigma^2$, so one is negative and one positive. The condition at zero excludes the negative power. Since $F(1)=-y<0$ and $F(q)\to\infty$ as $q\to\infty$, the positive root satisfies $p>1$; explicitly,

$$
p=\frac{-(r-y-\sigma^2/2)+\sqrt{(r-y-\sigma^2/2)^2+2r\sigma^2}}{\sigma^2}.
$$

Value matching gives $C(S)=(\hat S-K)(S/\hat S)^p$. The [smooth fit](../martingale.md#smooth-pasting) condition gives $p(\hat S-K)/\hat S=1$, hence

$$
\boxed{\hat S=\frac{pK}{p-1},\qquad
C(S)=\begin{cases}(\hat S-K)(S/\hat S)^p,&0\leq S<\hat S,\\S-K,&S\geq\hat S.\end{cases}}
$$

As $y\downarrow0$, the positive root tends to $1$ from above, so $\boxed{p\to1,\ \hat S\to\infty}$. For fixed $S$, the value tends to $S$. In the dividend-free perpetual limit no finite threshold attains that supremum; this agrees with the finite-maturity [no early exercise of a call without dividends](../mathematical-finance.md#no-early-exercise-of-a-call-without-dividends).

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

For a fixed [exercise threshold](../mathematical-finance.md#exercise-threshold) $\bar S>K$, the continuation value solves the same stationary equation but is matched to $\bar S-K$ at that threshold. Boundedness at zero removes the negative power. Thus the [fixed-threshold value of a perpetual call](../mathematical-finance.md#fixed-threshold-value-of-a-perpetual-call) is

$$
\boxed{\widetilde C(S)=(\bar S-K)(S/\bar S)^p,\qquad 0\leq S\leq\bar S.}
$$

It is also $(\bar S-K)\mathbb E_S^{\mathbb Q}[e^{-r\tau_{\bar S}};\tau_{\bar S}<\infty]$, where $\tau_{\bar S}$ is the first up-crossing [stopping time](../martingale.md#stopping-time). Differentiating while keeping the current $S$ fixed gives

$$
\frac{\partial\widetilde C}{\partial\bar S}
=S^p\bar S^{-p-1}\bigl[pK-(p-1)\bar S\bigr].
$$

This is positive for $K<\bar S<\hat S=pK/(p-1)$, so increasing such an early threshold increases its continuation value. The comparison assumes $S<\bar S$; a policy already exercised has no remaining continuation value to vary.

If $\bar S>\hat S$, then

$$
\widetilde C'(\bar S-)=p\left(1-\frac K{\bar S}\right)>1.
$$

Since $\widetilde C(\bar S)=\bar S-K$, the [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) comparison gives $\widetilde C(S)<S-K$ for $S$ just below $\bar S$. Therefore **quoting this policy value as the price of an unrestricted American call permits arbitrage**: buy the option below its immediate exercise value and exercise it at once. A contract that actually commits its holder to the threshold is a different claim; its lower value is a suboptimal policy value, not by itself an arbitrage. The [perpetual American call option](../mathematical-finance.md#perpetual-american-call-option) optimizes over all exercise policies and remains above its exercise payoff.
