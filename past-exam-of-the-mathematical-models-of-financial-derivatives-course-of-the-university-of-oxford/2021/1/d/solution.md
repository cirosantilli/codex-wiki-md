<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Since $a_\tau$ does not depend on $\sigma$, differentiation of the [digital option](../../../../binary-option.md) value gives its [vega](../../../../option-vega.md):

$$
\boxed{\frac{\partial V}{\partial\sigma}=-\frac{e^{-r\tau}}\sigma\,d\phi(d).}
$$

Thus [vega](../../../../option-vega.md) is negative for $s>0$, zero at $s=0$, and positive for $s<0$. Increasing volatility spreads the conditional [normal distribution](../../../../normal-distribution.md) around its mean: when the current asset value is positive this reduces the chance of ending positive, while for a negative starting value it increases that chance. At zero the distribution stays symmetric and the probability remains one half. A digital payoff need not have the positive [vega](../../../../option-vega.md) familiar from a [European call option](../../../../european-call-option.md).
