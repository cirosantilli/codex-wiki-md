# Absolutely convergent improper integral

↑ **Parent:** [Improper integral](improper-integral.md)

An [improper integral](improper-integral.md) whose absolute-value integral is finite. The [triangle inequality for an integral](integral-triangle-inequality.md) bounds each truncated tail by the corresponding tail of $|f|$, so the [Cauchy criterion for an improper integral](cauchy-criterion-for-an-improper-integral.md) gives ordinary convergence. Absolute convergence is stronger than a [Cauchy principal value](cauchy-principal-value.md), which may exist only because two divergent sides cancel. The continuous sine-product integrand $\sin(ax)\sin(bx)/x^2$ has a removable value $ab$ at zero and an integrable $1/x^2$ tail; its auxiliary complex contour integrand can nevertheless require a principal value.
