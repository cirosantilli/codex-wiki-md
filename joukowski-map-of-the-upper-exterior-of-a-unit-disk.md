# Joukowski map of the upper exterior of a unit disk

↑ **Parent:** [Joukowski map](joukowski-map.md)

The [Joukowski map](joukowski-map.md) restricts to a [conformal map](conformal-map.md) from $\{\operatorname{Im}z>0,\ |z|>1\}$ onto the [upper half-plane](upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md). Indeed, $\operatorname{Im}g(z)=\operatorname{Im}z(1-|z|^{-2})>0$ there. For any $\operatorname{Im}\zeta>0$, the roots of $z^2-\zeta z+1=0$ have product one and cannot lie on the unit [circle](circle.md); exactly one has modulus greater than one, and that root has positive [imaginary part](imaginary-part.md). This proves that the map is a [bijection](bijection.md).

Its [inverse function](inverse-function.md) is

$$
g^{-1}(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta-2}\sqrt{\zeta+2}}2,
$$

with each factor taken as the [principal square root](principal-square-root-of-a-complex-number.md). This product is the [holomorphic square root](holomorphic-square-root.md) of $\zeta^2-4$ that behaves as $\zeta$ at infinity in the [upper half-plane](upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md). The upper unit [semicircle](semicircle.md) maps to $[-2,2]$; the exterior real rays map to the remaining real rays. The [derivative](derivative.md) $g'=1-z^{-2}$ vanishes only at the boundary endpoints $z=\pm1$.
