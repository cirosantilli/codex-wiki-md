<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a parent at rest, [momentum conservation](../../../../momentum-conservation.md) gives equal and opposite daughter and [alpha particle](../../../../alpha-particle.md) [momenta](../../../../momentum-split.md). In the nonrelativistic recoil approximation,

$$
Q=T_\alpha+T_D=\frac{p^2}{2m_\alpha}+\frac{p^2}{2M_D},\qquad
\boxed{T_\alpha=Q\frac{M_D}{M_D+m_\alpha}.}
$$

Approximating nuclear [masses](../../../../mass.md) by their [mass numbers](../../../../mass-number.md), $M_D\simeq(A-4)m_u$ and $m_\alpha\simeq4m_u$, gives

$$
\boxed{T_\alpha\simeq\frac{A-4}{A}Q.}
$$

The printed phrase “atomic number $A$” must here mean **[mass number](../../../../mass-number.md)**. [Atomic number](../../../../atomic-number.md) alone does not determine the recoil fraction. For example, ${}^{210}\mathrm{Po}$ and ${}^{212}\mathrm{Po}$ have the same [atomic number](../../../../atomic-number.md) $84$, but the approximate fractions are $206/210$ and $208/212$, respectively.

For completeness, exact [relativistic two-body decay](../../../../relativistic-two-body-decay.md) [kinematics](../../../../kinematics.md) gives

$$
T_\alpha=\left(\frac{M_P^2+m_\alpha^2-M_D^2}{2M_P}-m_\alpha\right)c^2
=Q\frac{M_D+Q/(2c^2)}{M_P},
$$

where $M_P=M_D+m_\alpha+Q/c^2$. Expanding for $Q\ll M_Pc^2$ recovers the recoil-sharing expression.
