<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**The conversion from a measured beam peak to a [particle resonance](../../../../particle-resonance.md) [mass](../../../../mass.md) is fixed, but the missing graph supplies neither the peak position nor its width.** Let $T_R$ be the incoming [pion](../../../../pion.md) [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) at the peak and $\Delta T$ the [full width at half maximum](../../../../full-width-at-half-maximum.md) after allowing for instrumental resolution. With a stationary [proton](../../../../proton.md) and [natural units](../../../../natural-units.md),

$$
s=m_p^2+m_\pi^2+2m_p(m_\pi+T)
=(m_p+m_\pi)^2+2m_pT.
$$

Thus the peak [invariant mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md) is

$$
\boxed{M=\sqrt{(m_p+m_\pi)^2+2m_pT_R}.}
$$

The width must also be transformed to the [centre-of-mass frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md). To first order about the peak,

$$
\frac{d\sqrt{s}}{dT}=\frac{m_p}{\sqrt{s}},\qquad
\boxed{\Gamma\simeq\frac{m_p}{M}\Delta T,\qquad
\tau=\frac{\hbar}{\Gamma}.}
$$

For a broad feature, transform the two half-height positions separately: if they are $T_-$ and $T_+$, then the corresponding width in [invariant mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md) is $\sqrt{(m_p+m_\pi)^2+2m_pT_+}-\sqrt{(m_p+m_\pi)^2+2m_pT_-}$. An energy-dependent numerator or width can also displace the observed maximum from the [resonance pole](../../../../resonance-pole.md) parameter.

The low-[energy](../../../../energy-split.md) $\pi^+p$ channel is normally dominated by $\Delta^{++}(1232)$, a [Delta baryon](../../../../delta-baryon.md) with $Q=2$, $B=1$ and $I=3/2$. As an **external reference example, not a reading of the absent plot**, $M=1232\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and $\Gamma=117\,\mathrm{MeV}$ give $T_R\simeq190\,\mathrm{MeV}$, $\Delta T\simeq154\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and $\tau\simeq5.63\times10^{-24}\,\mathrm s$.[Breit-Wigner mass near 1232 MeV and width near 117 MeV; these are external comparison values](../../../../delta-1232-particle-listing-by-particle-data-group.md) The actual graphical estimates remain unavailable. In particular, using the laboratory width directly in $\tau=\hbar/\Delta T$ misses the factor $m_p/M$.
