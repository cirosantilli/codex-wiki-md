<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

**A constant-width [Breit-Wigner resonance cross section](../../../../breit-wigner-resonance-cross-section.md) is the simplest nonrelativistic curve; a [P-wave scattering](../../../../p-wave-scattering.md) width and relativistic kinematics explain important expected distortions.** A comparison to specific observed deviations cannot be made without the missing plot.

Write $E$ for the [centre-of-mass frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md) [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md), $\mu=m_pm_\pi/(m_p+m_\pi)$ for the [reduced mass](../../../../reduced-mass.md), and $k$ for the relative wave number. The nonrelativistic relations are

$$
E\simeq\frac{m_p}{m_p+m_\pi}T,\qquad
k^2=\frac{2\mu E}{\hbar^2}.
$$

For an isolated [particle resonance](../../../../particle-resonance.md) of [spin](../../../../spin.md) $J$, averaging over the initial [proton](../../../../proton.md) [spin](../../../../spin.md) and using the zero [pion](../../../../pion.md) [spin](../../../../spin.md) gives

$$
\sigma_{\rm el}(E)=\frac{\pi}{k^2}\frac{2J+1}{2}
\frac{\Gamma_{\pi p}^2}{(E-E_R)^2+\Gamma^2/4}.
$$

For the nearly elastic $\Delta^{++}$ state, $J=3/2$ and $\Gamma_{\pi p}\simeq\Gamma$, so

$$
\boxed{\sigma_{\rm el}(E)\simeq\frac{8\pi}{k^2}
\frac{\Gamma^2/4}{(E-E_R)^2+\Gamma^2/4}.}
$$

Freezing $k$ at $k_R$ gives a symmetric Lorentzian centered at $E_R$, with [full width at half maximum](../../../../full-width-at-half-maximum.md) $\Gamma$ and height $8\pi/k_R^2$. This is the local curve to sketch. If the displayed variable is the beam [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) $T$, its width is $\Delta T\simeq(m_p+m_\pi)\Gamma/m_p$ within this nonrelativistic approximation. Keeping $k(E)$ changes the prefactor and hence the symmetry. Extending a constant-width approximation all the way to $E=0$ produces a spurious divergence, so it is not a valid threshold model.

The [pion](../../../../pion.md) has [intrinsic parity](../../../../intrinsic-parity.md) $-1$ and the [proton](../../../../proton.md) has $+1$. The positive [parity](../../../../parity.md) and $J=3/2$ of the [Delta baryon](../../../../delta-baryon.md) require odd [orbital angular momentum](../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md), with the lowest choice $l=1$. The [finite-range partial-wave threshold law](../../../../finite-range-partial-wave-threshold-law.md) gives $\Gamma_{\pi p}(k)\propto k^{2l+1}=k^3$ near threshold. Inserting this in the elastic numerator makes $\sigma_{\rm el}\propto k^4$ away from the pole as $k\to0$, rather than diverging. Equivalently the elastic [scattering phase shift](../../../../scattering-phase-shift.md) has $\delta_1=O(k^3)$, and $\sin^2\delta_1/k^2=O(k^4)$. Away from threshold, form factors and other channels modify the simple cubic width.

At the illustrative peak of part (d), the incident [pion](../../../../pion.md) [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is comparable to its rest [energy](../../../../energy-split.md), so a nonrelativistic [pion](../../../../pion.md) treatment is already inaccurate. The exact relative [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) is

$$
p_*^2=\frac{[s-(m_p+m_\pi)^2][s-(m_p-m_\pi)^2]}{4s},\qquad k=\frac{p_*}{\hbar},
$$

with $\sqrt{s}=\sqrt{(m_p+m_\pi)^2+2m_pT}$. Relative to the symmetric local estimate, this changes the horizontal conversion, peak normalization and tails. The illustrative $1232\,\mathrm{MeV}$ peak has $p_*\simeq227\,\mathrm{MeV}/c$, giving $8\pi(\hbar c)^2/(p_*c)^2\simeq190\,\mathrm{mb}$ before small inelastic effects.

Finally, nonresonant [partial waves](../../../../partial-wave.md) can interfere with the resonant [scattering amplitude](../../../../scattering-amplitude.md), higher [particle resonances](../../../../particle-resonance.md) and inelastic [scattering channels](../../../../scattering-channel.md) can enter, and instrumental resolution can broaden a measured peak. These are physically distinct possible deviations; the missing data do not justify claiming any particular one was actually observed. Both the missing graphical overlay and its data-specific explanation remain source gaps.
