# Polar covalent bond

↑ **Parent:** [Bond polarization](bond-polarization.md)

A [polar covalent bond](polar-covalent-bond.md) shares [electrons](electron.md) unequally, as in [hydrogen chloride](hydrogen-chloride.md).

The word “polar” qualifies the sharing within a [covalent bond](covalent-bond-split.md); it does not require complete transfer of an [electron](electron.md) or an isolated [cation](cation.md)–[anion](anion.md) pair. In $A^{\delta+}-B^{\delta-}$, the signs identify the relatively electron-poor and electron-rich ends, while $\delta$ leaves the amount unspecified. A neutral [Lewis structure](lewis-structure.md) can give both [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) zero [formal charge](formal-charge.md) and still correspond to a [polar covalent bond](polar-covalent-bond.md): equal division of the [bond pair](bond-pair.md) is a formal allocation, whereas [bond polarization](bond-polarization.md) concerns the actual [electron density](electron-density.md).

Local and molecular descriptions answer different questions. Calling one [chemical bond](chemical-bond-split.md) a [polar covalent bond](polar-covalent-bond.md) does not establish that the whole [molecule](molecule-split.md) is a [polar molecule](polar-molecule.md). Combine the [bond dipoles](bond-dipole.md) with the [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md), then check for [molecular dipole cancellation](molecular-dipole-cancellation.md). This lets a short [electronegativity](electronegativity-split.md) comparison explain a local tendency without confusing it with the observable [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) of the whole [molecule](molecule-split.md).
