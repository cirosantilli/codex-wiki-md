# Planar potential dipole

↑ **Parent:** [Complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow](complex-potential-of-a-two-dimensional-potential-flow.md)

A [planar potential dipole](planar-potential-dipole.md) at $a$ with complex moment $m$ has [complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow](complex-potential-of-a-two-dimensional-potential-flow.md) $w(z)=m/(z-a)$. Its [complex velocity](complex-velocity.md) is $w'(z)=-m/(z-a)^2$, interpreted as $v_x-iv_y$. Unlike the three-dimensional [potential dipole](potential-dipole.md), this is a two-dimensional singularity of the logarithmic-source potential. For example, two opposite source coefficients $s$ at $a\pm\delta/2$ have potential $s[\log(z-a-\delta/2)-\log(z-a+\delta/2)]$, whose leading term is $-s\delta/(z-a)$ as the separation tends to zero with $s\delta$ fixed. This gives the [dipole](planar-potential-dipole.md) as a limiting source–sink pair, with the complex sign and orientation included in $m$.
