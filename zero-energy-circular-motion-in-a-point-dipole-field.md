# Zero-energy circular motion in a point-dipole field

↑ **Parent:** [Electric dipole field](electric-dipole-field.md)

For [potential energy](potential-energy.md) homogeneous of degree $-2$, [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) gives $d^2r^2/dt^2=4E/m$. Indeed, Euler's identity implies $\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf F=2U$, so the second derivative equals $2v^2+4U/m=4(K+U)/m$. Thus a trajectory with zero total [energy](energy-split.md) and initially zero radial velocity remains on a sphere of constant radius. A positive [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) released from rest on the equator of a fixed ideal [electric dipole](electric-dipole.md) has these properties; in its meridional plane it oscillates on the semicircle on the negative-pole side. A finite two-charge [electric dipole](electric-dipole.md) does not have exactly homogeneous [potential energy](potential-energy.md) and perturbs this result.
