# Thermoregulation

↑ **Parent:** [Homeostasis](homeostasis.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoregulation)

[Thermoregulation](thermoregulation.md) regulates body [temperature](temperature-split.md). [Skin](skin.md) signals can anticipate a change of [core body temperature](core-body-temperature.md), so a response need not wait for an internal error. [Introduction, paragraphs beginning The reflex regulation and In humans, the contribution from skin temperature: thermoregulatory effectors and feedforward skin input](cortical-thalamic-and-hypothalamic-responses-to-cooling-and-warming-the-skin-in-awake-humans-a-positron-emission-tomography-study-by-gary-f-egan-and-others.md)

These responses have costs and environmental limits. [Shivering](shivering.md) consumes metabolic resources; cooling through [sweating](sweating.md) removes [water](water.md) that must eventually be replaced. High [humidity](humidity.md) restricts [evaporation](evaporation.md), so secreted sweat and successful cooling are not interchangeable. Maintaining [core body temperature](core-body-temperature.md) also depends on maintaining circulation: depleted blood volume can compromise [skin](skin.md) blood flow and [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md). Seeking shade or reducing work changes the disturbance itself, whereas a larger regulatory command merely attempts to counter it. [§2.2.4, Evaporation: sweat secretion versus evaporation and blood-volume depletion; §2.3.1: behavioral heat conservation and dissipation](fundamental-concepts-of-human-thermoregulation-and-adaptation-to-heat-a-review-in-the-context-of-global-warming-by-chin-leong-lim.md)
