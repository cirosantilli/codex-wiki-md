# Dimensional crossover of thermal cavity radiation

↑ **Parent:** [Black-body radiation](black-body-radiation.md)

In a thin [rectangular electromagnetic cavity](rectangular-electromagnetic-cavity.md), the window $\hbar c\pi/L_x,\hbar c\pi/L_y\ll k_BT\ll\hbar c\pi/L_z$ freezes the nonzero thickness indices while retaining many in-plane [normal modes](normal-mode.md). The zero-thickness-index family has one [polarization of an electromagnetic wave](polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave.md) and [density of states](density-of-states.md) $dN=A k\,dk/(2\pi)=2\pi A\nu\,d\nu/c^2$. Multiplying by $h\nu/(e^{h\nu/k_BT}-1)$ and integrating gives $U_{2D}=4\pi\zeta(3)A(k_BT)^3/(h^2c^2)$. Thus thermal [energy](energy-split.md) scales as [area](surface-area.md) times $T^3$. The [Riemann zeta function](riemann-zeta-function.md) appears through $\int_0^\infty x^2/(e^x-1)\,dx=2\zeta(3)$.

This is an intermediate low-[temperature](temperature-split.md) regime of an anisotropic finite cavity. When $k_BT$ also falls below the first in-plane excitation, thermal [energy](energy-split.md) becomes exponentially small. If a different model excludes every zero thickness index, its first transverse mode has a finite gap and the proposed $T^3$ regime does not occur. In the three-dimensional continuum regime the corresponding [density of states](density-of-states.md) is proportional to [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) times $\nu^2$ and thermal [energy](energy-split.md) is proportional to [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) times $T^4$.
