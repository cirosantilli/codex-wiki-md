# Quadrupole strain scaling of a weak self-gravitating source

↑ **Parent:** [Far-zone quadrupole strain](far-zone-quadrupole-strain.md)

For a weakly self-gravitating bound source of size $R$, mass $M$ and nonspherical dynamical [mass quadrupole moment](mass-quadrupole-moment.md) $Q=O(MR^2)$, the [virial theorem](virial-theorem.md) gives $v^2=O(GM/R)=O(\epsilon)$. Its time scale is $R/v$, so $\ddot Q=O(Mv^2)$. The [far-zone quadrupole strain](far-zone-quadrupole-strain.md) then has a spatial half-strain coefficient $E=O(GMv^2/r)=O(\epsilon^2R/r)$ in units $c=1$. The physical transverse-traceless strain is $2E^{\mathrm{TT}}$. Spherical or stationary sources can have zero radiation, so this is a characteristic scale for a changing nonspherical quadrupole, not a positive lower bound. The radiation zone also requires $r\gg R/v$.
