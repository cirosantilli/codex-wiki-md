# Conjugate symmetry of trigonometric polynomial coefficients

↑ **Parent:** [Trigonometric polynomial](trigonometric-polynomial.md)

A [trigonometric polynomial](trigonometric-polynomial.md) is real on the [unit circle](complex-unit-circle.md) precisely when its finite [coefficients](coefficient.md) have the displayed symmetry. To prove necessity, conjugate its values on the circle, use $\overline z=z^{-1}$, and compare [coefficients](coefficient.md). Multiplying a [coefficient](coefficient.md) difference by $z^d$ gives an ordinary [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) vanishing at infinitely many points, so it is zero. Symmetry also gives $p(z^{-1})=\overline{p(\overline z)}$ for every nonzero complex $z$.
