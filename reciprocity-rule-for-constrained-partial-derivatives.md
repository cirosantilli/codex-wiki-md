# Reciprocity rule for constrained partial derivatives

↑ **Parent:** [Implicit differentiation](implicit-differentiation.md)

On a regular [level set](level-set.md) $F(x,y,z)=0$, holding $z$ fixed gives $F_x\,dx+F_y\,dy=0$. If $F$ is [continuously differentiable](continuously-differentiable-function.md) and both $F_x$ and $F_y$ are nonzero, the [implicit function theorem](implicit-function-theorem.md) gives $(\partial y/\partial x)_z=-F_x/F_y$ and $(\partial x/\partial y)_z=-F_y/F_x$. Multiplication proves the identity. The same held-fixed variable is essential, and a zero denominator invalidates the corresponding quotient rather than creating an infinite factor that may be cancelled formally.
