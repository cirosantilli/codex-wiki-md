# Radial and angular modes of an orthogonally invariant scalar potential

↑ **Parent:** [Goldstone theorem](goldstone-theorem.md)

For $V=m^2\phi^2/2+\lambda(\phi^2)^2/4$, $m^2<0$, the [vacuum manifold](vacuum-manifold.md) is a sphere of radius $v=\sqrt{-m^2/\lambda}$. At a chosen vacuum the [Hessian matrix](hessian-matrix.md) is $2\lambda v^2nn^T$, where $n$ is its unit direction. The radial scalar has mass squared $2\lambda v^2$; the $n-1$ angular modes are physical [Goldstone bosons](goldstone-boson.md) when the symmetry is global.

For the two-component case, write $\boldsymbol\phi=(v+\rho)(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$. The [kinetic term](kinetic-term.md) becomes $\tfrac12(\partial_\mu\rho)^2+\tfrac12(v+\rho)^2(\partial_\mu\theta)^2$. At quadratic order the canonically normalized angular field is $\pi=v\theta$, with no [mass](mass.md) term. Its [Euler-Lagrange equation](euler-lagrange-equation.md) is the [wave equation](wave-equation-split.md), so $\omega^2=|\mathbf k|^2$ in units where the propagation speed is one. The radial field instead obeys $\omega^2=|\mathbf k|^2+2\lambda v^2$. Thus a common rotation costs no [energy](energy-split.md), whereas a slowly varying rotation propagates as a collective wave. With the selecting source of [continuous vacuum selection by an external source](continuous-vacuum-selection-by-an-external-source.md), use $\pi=w\theta$ and obtain $\omega^2=|\mathbf k|^2+h/w$. This elementary [dispersion relation](dispersion-relation-split.md) separates a [Goldstone boson](goldstone-boson.md), a [pseudo-Goldstone boson](pseudo-goldstone-boson.md) and a massive radial excitation within one example.
