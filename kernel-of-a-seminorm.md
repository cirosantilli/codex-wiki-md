# Kernel of a seminorm

↑ **Parent:** [Seminorm](seminorm.md)

The kernel of a [seminorm](seminorm.md) is its zero set, a [linear subspace](vector-subspace.md) by absolute homogeneity and the [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md). The estimate $|p(x)-p(y)|\leq p(x-y)$ shows that adding an element of $\ker p$ does not change $p$. Hence $\|x+\ker p\|=p(x)$ defines a [norm](norm.md) on the [quotient vector space](quotient-vector-space.md) $E/\ker p$.

On $E$ itself, $d_p(x,y)=p(x-y)$ is a [pseudometric](pseudometric.md); it is a [metric](metric.md) exactly when $p$ is a [norm](norm.md). In particular, zero distance in one [pseudometric](pseudometric.md) need not mean equality of [vectors](vector.md). A [separating family of seminorms](separating-family-of-seminorms.md) removes the common zero-distance ambiguity.
