# Amino acid

↑ **Parent:** [Carboxylic acid](carboxylic-acid.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acid)

An [amino acid](amino-acid.md) combines [carboxylic acid](carboxylic-acid.md) and [amine](amine.md) functionality in the same [molecule](molecule-split.md); the name alone does not specify their separation. An [alpha-amino acid](alpha-amino-acid.md) has its amino [nitrogen](nitrogen.md) attached to the [carbon](carbon.md) immediately adjacent to the carboxyl [carbon](carbon.md), the [amino acid alpha carbon](amino-acid-alpha-carbon.md). For example, $\mathrm{H_2N{-}CH_2{-}COOH}$ is [glycine](glycine.md), whereas $\mathrm{H_2N{-}CH_2{-}CH_2{-}COOH}$ has an extra intervening [carbon](carbon.md) and is not an [alpha-amino acid](alpha-amino-acid.md). These [chemical formulas](chemical-formula.md) describe connectivity, not the predominant [protonation](protonation.md) state in [water](water.md).

Most common [proteinogenic amino acids](proteinogenic-amino-acid.md) share the skeleton $\mathrm{H_2N{-}CH}(R)\mathrm{{-}COOH}$, with $R$ the [amino acid side chain](amino-acid-side-chain.md). In [glycine](glycine.md), $R$ is [hydrogen](hydrogen.md); in [alanine](alanine.md), $R=\mathrm{CH_3}$. [Proline](proline.md) is a useful exception to the displayed primary-[amine](amine.md) skeleton: its amino [nitrogen](nitrogen.md) is part of a ring and has a second [carbon](carbon.md) substituent. The shared backbone permits assembly into [polypeptides](polypeptide.md), while different [amino acid side chains](amino-acid-side-chain.md) give the resulting [proteins](protein-split.md) diverse chemistry. [3AA-1, Table 1: glycine, alanine and proline structures; 3AA-2.2.5, backbone and side chain](nomenclature-and-symbolism-for-amino-acids-and-peptides-recommendations-1983-by-iupac-iub-joint-commission-on-biochemical-nomenclature.md)

In a [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md), [protonation](protonation.md) changes the [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) without changing this backbone. Free [glycine](glycine.md) near neutral [pH](ph.md) is predominantly $\mathrm{H_3N^+{-}CH_2{-}COO^-}$, a [zwitterion](zwitterion.md). Its positive and negative groups are joined within one [molecule](molecule-split.md); zero net [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) does not mean absence of local [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md), nor does it mean the uncharged drawing $\mathrm{H_2N{-}CH_2{-}COOH}$ predominates. [Glycine protonation](glycine-protonation.md) explains how lowering or raising [pH](ph.md) changes the balance. [§26.1, opening zwitterion and acid/base interconversion discussion](organic-chemistry-by-john-mcmurry.md)

Building a [protein](protein-split.md) joins the carboxyl [carbon](carbon.md) of one [amino acid](amino-acid.md) to the amino [nitrogen](nitrogen.md) of another through a [peptide bond](peptide-bond.md), an [amide](amide.md) linkage. The formal net condensation loses the elements of [water](water.md); cellular [translation](translation-geometry.md) uses activated substrates at a [ribosome](ribosome.md), rather than simply mixing free [amino acids](amino-acid.md) in [water](water.md). Incorporated units are [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md). The interior [amide](amide.md) groups no longer have the same acid/base behavior as the two free terminal groups of [glycine](glycine.md), so its free-[molecule](molecule-split.md) [glycine titration](glycine-titration.md) cannot be copied independently at every chain position. [§26.4, opening peptide formation and amide resonance discussion](organic-chemistry-by-john-mcmurry.md)

[Chirality](chirality-chemistry.md) distinguishes most common [proteinogenic amino acids](proteinogenic-amino-acid.md) from [glycine](glycine.md). The other nineteen members of the conventional twenty have a [stereocenter](stereocenter.md) at their [amino acid alpha carbon](amino-acid-alpha-carbon.md); ordinary [translation](translation-geometry.md) incorporates their L forms. [Glycine](glycine.md)'s two identical [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) substituents make it achiral. Thus being an [amino acid](amino-acid.md), or even an [alpha-amino acid](alpha-amino-acid.md), does not by itself imply [chirality](chirality-chemistry.md). [3AA-3.2, note: glycine itself is achiral; 3AA-3.3 and 3AA-3.5, protein-source L configuration](nomenclature-and-symbolism-for-amino-acids-and-peptides-recommendations-1983-by-iupac-iub-joint-commission-on-biochemical-nomenclature.md)
