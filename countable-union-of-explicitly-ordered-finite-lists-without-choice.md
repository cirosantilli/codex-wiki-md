# Countable union of explicitly ordered finite lists without choice

↑ **Parent:** [Countable set](countable-set.md)

Given finite lists $\ell_0,\ell_1,\ldots$ in a [set](set-split.md) $X$, their union of entries has an explicit enumeration from a subset of $\mathbb N^2$: use the existing positions in each list and a [Cantor pairing function](cantor-pairing-function.md). If the union is infinite, successively take the first occurrence of an entry not previously taken, in the order of its natural-number code. The [well-order](well-order.md) on [natural numbers](natural-number.md) makes each step uniquely determined and yields an injection $\mathbb N\to X$. No [axiom of choice](axiom-of-choice.md) is needed. This does not assert that an arbitrary countable family of unordered [finite sets](finite-set.md) has a countable union in [ZF](zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory.md) without the [axiom of choice](axiom-of-choice.md).
