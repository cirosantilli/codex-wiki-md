# Fourier series of a periodic sawtooth

↑ **Parent:** [Fourier sine series](fourier-sine-series.md)

The period-$L$ extension of $f(x)=Kx/L$ on $-L/2<x<L/2$ has [Fourier coefficients](fourier-coefficient.md) $a_0=a_n=0$ and $b_n=K(-1)^{n+1}/(\pi n)$. Consequently

$$
f(x)\sim\frac K\pi\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}n\sin\frac{2\pi nx}{L}.
$$

The [Fourier series](fourier-series-split.md) converges to the ramp away from its jumps and to zero at every jump, the average of the one-sided limits. For $K\ne0$, [Termwise differentiation of a Fourier series](termwise-differentiation-of-a-fourier-series.md) fails here as an ordinary convergent series: the differentiated summands have nondecaying amplitude $2K/L$. The classical derivative equals $K/L$ between jumps, whereas the periodic [distributional derivative](distributional-derivative.md) includes negative point masses of weight $K$ at the jumps.
