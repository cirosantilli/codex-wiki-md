# Impulsive disruption by stellar mass loss

↑ **Parent:** [Impulsive mass-loss expansion law](impulsive-mass-loss-expansion-law.md)

Take an initially isolated virialized self-gravitating [stellar population](stellar-population.md) with [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) $T=|W|/2$ and [Newtonian gravitational potential energy](newtonian-gravitational-potential-energy.md) $W<0$. Instantaneous spatially uniform removal of mass, without kicks to surviving material, leaves fraction $f$ with $T'=fT$ and $W'=f^2W$. Hence $E'=|W|f(1/2-f)$: the homologous whole-system energy is negative only for $f>1/2$. Positive whole-system energy does not forbid a bound core after selective escape, and slow mass loss follows the [adiabatic mass-loss expansion law](adiabatic-mass-loss-expansion-law.md) instead.
