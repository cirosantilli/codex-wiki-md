# Polarizing beam splitter

↑ **Parent:** [Beam splitter](beam-splitter.md)

A [polarizing beam splitter](polarizing-beam-splitter.md) sends two orthogonal [linear polarizations](linear-polarization.md) to different output paths. Acting coherently on $\alpha|H\rangle+\beta|V\rangle$, it correlates path with [photon polarization](photon-polarization.md) instead of measuring [photon polarization](photon-polarization.md) unless a path is actually detected or discarded.

For an ideal [polarizing beam splitter](polarizing-beam-splitter.md) with one input, choose orthogonal unit [electromagnetic polarization](polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave.md) vectors $\mathbf e_H,\mathbf e_V$ and an incident power-normalized field $a_H\mathbf e_H+a_V\mathbf e_V$. If $H$ is transmitted and $V$ reflected, the output [powers](power.md) are $P_{\rm transmitted}=|a_H|^2$ and $P_{\rm reflected}=|a_V|^2$. The associated [complex amplitudes](complex-amplitude.md) can acquire different [wave phases](phase-waves.md); the routing is not an equal-power split for every incident [electromagnetic polarization](polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave.md). For [linear polarization](linear-polarization.md) at [angle](angle.md) $\theta$ to $H$, input [power](power.md) $P$ gives $P\cos^2\theta$ and $P\sin^2\theta$, respectively. At $\theta=30^\circ$, a stipulated $4\,\mathrm{mW}$ input gives $3\,\mathrm{mW}$ transmitted and $1\,\mathrm{mW}$ reflected. A $45^\circ$ input gives equal [powers](power.md) even though an $H$ input goes entirely into the transmitted output.[Photon quantum mechanics and beam splitters, §IV.D, printed p. 264, figure 5: polarization routed into different detected paths](photon-quantum-mechanics-and-beam-splitters-by-charles-h-holbrow-and-others.md)

Real [polarizing beam splitters](polarizing-beam-splitter.md) have leakage into the unwanted output and losses. Each [linear polarization](linear-polarization.md) has its own field transformation and power coefficients. To make an ordinary power [wave interference](interference-wave-propagation.md) term between the two output contributions, first bring them into the same path with overlapping transverse profiles and a common [electromagnetic polarization](polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave.md), for example using a [polarizer](polarizer.md). Merely placing two orthogonal [linear polarizations](linear-polarization.md) on a polarization-insensitive [photodetector](photodetector.md) adds their [powers](power.md). Coherent routing can be reversed by suitable optics before detection; discarding a path loses access to that part of the field but supplies no recorded measurement outcome by itself.
