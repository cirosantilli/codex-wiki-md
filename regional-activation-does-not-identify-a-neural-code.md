# Regional activation does not identify a neural code

↑ **Parent:** [Neural representation](neural-representation.md)

Consider two response [vectors](vector.md) $x=(3,1,3,1)$ and $y=(3,3,1,1)$ over the same four measured locations, relative to a zero baseline. Both are positive everywhere and have [mean](expected-value-split.md) two. Their centered [vectors](vector.md) $x_c=(1,-1,1,-1)$ and $y_c=(1,1,-1,-1)$ satisfy $x_c\cdot y_c=0$. Hence identical positive regional means can accompany orthogonal patterns. This counterexample shows why overlapping regional activation does not imply a shared [neural representation](neural-representation.md). It does not assert that real [neural codes](neural-code.md) are necessarily orthogonal or that unsuccessful decoding proves their independence.
