# Subgroup

↑ **Parent:** [Group](group-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgroup)

A [subgroup](subgroup.md) $H\le G$ is a [subset](subset.md) of a [group](group-split.md) $G$ that is itself a [group](group-split.md) under the same [group operation](group-operation.md). It must contain the [identity element](identity-element.md) of $G$ and be closed under products and [inverse elements](inverse-element.md). [Associativity](associative-property.md) is inherited from $G$, so it needs no new proof. The whole [group](group-split.md) and the [trivial group](trivial-group.md) $\{e\}$ are always [subgroups](subgroup.md). A proper [subgroup](subgroup.md) satisfies $H\ne G$.

A useful single-condition test is

$$
H\ne\varnothing\quad\text{and}\quad
ab^{-1}\in H\ \text{for all }a,b\in H.
$$

To see why it works, choose $a\in H$; then $aa^{-1}=e\in H$. Taking $a=e$ gives every [inverse element](inverse-element.md) $b^{-1}\in H$. Applying the test to $a$ and $b^{-1}$ then gives $ab\in H$. Conversely, every [subgroup](subgroup.md) satisfies the test by closure under products and [inverse elements](inverse-element.md). This also makes the additive version memorable: a nonempty [subset](subset.md) of an [additive group](additive-group.md) is a [subgroup](subgroup.md) exactly when it is closed under differences. See [McKernan's MIT lecture 2, Proposition 2.3, for the equivalent closure criterion](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-703-modern-algebra-spring-2013/ed9964d3df50737e155f0d021af3eb49_MIT18_703S13_pra_l_2.pdf).

For the [square](square.md) [dihedral group](dihedral-group.md) $D_4$ above, $R=\{e,r,r^2,r^3\}$ is a [cyclic subgroup](cyclic-subgroup.md): products add exponents modulo four, and $(r^k)^{-1}=r^{-k}$. The smaller collections $\{e,r^2\}$ and $\{e,s\}$ are [subgroups](subgroup.md) of [group order](order-of-a-finite-group.md) two. But $\{e,r\}$ is not a [subgroup](subgroup.md), since $r\cdot r=r^2$ lies outside it. A plausible-looking list of symmetries therefore need not constitute a complete collection of reversible operations.

Inside $(\mathbb Z,+)$, the [even numbers](even-number.md) $2\mathbb Z$ form a [subgroup](subgroup.md): $2m-2n=2(m-n)$ is an [even number](even-number.md). Its two [cosets](coset.md) consist of the [even numbers](even-number.md) and the [odd numbers](odd-number.md), so its [index of a subgroup](index-of-a-subgroup.md) is two. The parity [group homomorphism](group-homomorphism.md) $k\mapsto k\bmod2$ has $2\mathbb Z$ as its [kernel of a group homomorphism](kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md). A [subgroup](subgroup.md) can thus identify exactly which information a composition-preserving map forgets.

[Subgroups](subgroup.md) also describe residual symmetry. In the [group action](group-action-split.md) of $D_4$ on the four vertices, the [stabilizer subgroup](stabilizer-subgroup.md) of $(1,1)$ is $\{e,sr\}$: the diagonal [reflection](reflection-mathematics.md) $sr$ fixes that vertex, and the other six motions move it. Requiring a feature to stay fixed still leaves a [group](group-split.md), because both composition and reversal preserve that requirement. This is why [subgroups](subgroup.md) are useful for studying a [group action](group-action-split.md) one feature at a time.
