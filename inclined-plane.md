# Inclined plane

↑ **Parent:** [Classical mechanics](classical-mechanics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclined_plane)

An [inclined plane](inclined-plane.md) is a flat surface tilted through an [angle](angle.md) $\theta$ above horizontal. A ramp exchanges a smaller lifting [force](force-split.md) for a longer travel distance; for a released body, it reduces the downhill component of [gravitational weight](weight-physics.md) and makes the resulting [acceleration](acceleration.md) easier to measure. Its simplest dynamical model is a nonrotating block on a stationary, rigid surface, with constant [mass](mass.md) $m>0$, uniform [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) of magnitude $g>0$, and $0\le\theta<\pi/2$. Assume continued contact, negligible [drag force](drag-physics.md), no tipping, and no applied pull. The only [forces](force-split.md) are [gravitational weight](weight-physics.md), the [normal force](normal-force.md), and any tangential [friction](friction.md).

The central distinction is between a block resting on the [inclined plane](inclined-plane.md) and one already sliding. [Static friction](static-friction.md) supplies the [force](force-split.md) needed to maintain rest, up to its bound; [kinetic friction](kinetic-friction.md) has a prescribed magnitude in the ideal constant-coefficient model. These laws are empirical approximations whose range depends on the contacting surfaces.[University Physics Volume 1 by William Moebs and others; “Magnitude of Static Friction” and “Magnitude of Kinetic Friction”, equations 6.1–6.2; empirical-law limitations before Example 6.10](university-physics-volume-1-by-william-moebs-and-others/6-2-friction.md)
