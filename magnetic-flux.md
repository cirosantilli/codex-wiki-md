# Magnetic flux

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_flux)

[Magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md) measures the signed amount of [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) crossing an [oriented surface](oriented-surface.md) $S$. Choose a [unit normal](unit-normal.md) $\widehat{\mathbf n}$ and use the [oriented surface element](oriented-surface-element.md) $d\mathbf S=\widehat{\mathbf n}\,dA$. The defining [surface integral](surface-integral.md) is

$$
\Phi_B=\int_S\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf S
=\int_S\mathbf B\cdot\widehat{\mathbf n}\,dA.
$$

For a uniform [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) across a flat [surface](topological-surface-split.md) of [area](surface-area.md) $A$, the [dot product](dot-product.md) reduces this to

$$
\Phi_B=BA\cos\theta,
$$

where $\theta$ is the [angle](angle.md) between the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) and the chosen [unit normal](unit-normal.md), rather than the [plane](plane.md) of the [surface](topological-surface-split.md). The [SI](international-system-of-units.md) unit is the [weber](weber-unit.md), $1\,\mathrm{Wb}=1\,\mathrm{T\,m^2}$. [OpenStax, magnetic flux and its orientation](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/13-1-faradays-law).

For a stipulated [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) $B=2\,\mathrm T$ and [area](surface-area.md) $A=3\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md) is $6\,\mathrm{Wb}$ at $\theta=0$, $3\,\mathrm{Wb}$ at $\theta=\pi/3$, and zero at $\theta=\pi/2$. In the last case the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) lies in the [plane](plane.md) of the [surface](topological-surface-split.md), so it does not cross it. Reversing the chosen [unit normal](unit-normal.md) reverses the sign of [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md) without changing the physical [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md); at $\theta=\pi$ the same example gives $-6\,\mathrm{Wb}$.

<a id="image-magnetic-flux-as-a-flat-surface-rotates-in-a-fixed-magnetic-field"></a>
![](magnetic-flux-orientation.png)

**[Figure 5](#image-magnetic-flux-as-a-flat-surface-rotates-in-a-fixed-magnetic-field). Magnetic flux as a flat surface rotates in a fixed magnetic field**. Left: an edge-on [surface](topological-surface-split.md) and its [unit normal](unit-normal.md) at three [angles](angle.md) to the fixed [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md). Right: the signed [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md) $\Phi_B=6\cos\theta\,\mathrm{Wb}$, including reversal of the [unit normal](unit-normal.md). The stipulated [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) is $2\,\mathrm T$ and the [area](surface-area.md) is $3\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

The geometric idea is projected [area](surface-area.md): tilting a flat [surface](topological-surface-split.md) reduces the [area](surface-area.md) seen perpendicular to the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) by the factor $\cos\theta$. This explains how rotation alone can change [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md) even when the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) is constant; [Faraday's law of induction](faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) turns such changes into an [electromotive force](electromotive-force.md). [OpenStax, induction from changing magnetic flux](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/13-1-faradays-law).
