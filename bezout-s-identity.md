<h1 id="bezout-s-identity">Bézout's identity</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Algebra](algebra-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bézout's_identity)

For [integers](integer.md) $a,b$ not both zero, there are [integers](integer.md) $x,y$ such that $ax+by=\gcd(a,b)$. The [extended Euclidean algorithm](extended-euclidean-algorithm.md) constructs them. For example, $4\cdot252-5\cdot198=18$ and $7\cdot5-2\cdot17=1$; the second identity also gives the [modular inverse](modular-multiplicative-inverse.md) of $5$ modulo $17$.

The identity converts a [greatest common divisor](greatest-common-divisor.md) into a solvability certificate. The [linear Diophantine equation](linear-diophantine-equation.md) $ax+by=c$ is solvable in [integers](integer.md) precisely when $\gcd(a,b)$ divides $c$. Every common [divisor](divisor.md) divides any integer [linear combination](linear-combination.md); conversely, multiplying a Bézout identity by $c/\gcd(a,b)$ constructs a solution when that quotient is integral. The coefficients generally are not unique, as the description of [all solutions of a linear Diophantine equation](all-solutions-of-a-linear-diophantine-equation.md) makes explicit.
