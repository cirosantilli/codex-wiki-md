# Birefringent walk-off

↑ **Parent:** [Extraordinary wave](extraordinary-wave.md)

The [extraordinary wave](extraordinary-wave.md)'s [Poynting vector](poynting-vector.md) can differ in direction from its [wavevector](wavevector.md). The latter specifies [wave phase](phase-waves.md) propagation, while the former specifies [energy flux](energy-flux.md). This angular separation is walk-off.[§2.8.3, printed pp. 157–158, equations 2.414–2.419: Poynting direction and walk-off](fundamentals-of-photonics-and-quantum-electronics-lecture-notes-by-franz-x-kartner.md)

In the lossless, nondispersive model above, take the [gradient](gradient.md) of $\omega=c\sqrt{k_x^2/n_e^2+k_z^2/n_o^2}$ to obtain the [group velocity](group-velocity.md), parallel to the [Poynting vector](poynting-vector.md). If its [angle](angle.md) to the [optic axis](optic-axis.md) is $\phi$, this gives $\tan\phi=(n_o^2/n_e^2)\tan\theta$. Consequently the [extraordinary wave](extraordinary-wave.md) generally carries [energy](energy-split.md) away from its [wavevector](wavevector.md) direction. An internal [wavevector](wavevector.md) normal to a slab face therefore does not guarantee a normal energy path. [Birefringent walk-off](birefringent-walk-off.md) vanishes at $\theta=0$ and $\theta=\pi/2$; the latter direction still has unequal [refractive indices](refractive-index.md). This is a useful [waveplate](waveplate.md) geometry: overlapping components can acquire [phase retardation](phase-retardation.md) without spatial splitting. A thin specimen, insufficient imaging resolution, or excitation of only one [linear polarization](linear-polarization.md) can also hide a double image, for different reasons.
