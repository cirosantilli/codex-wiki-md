# Tail sigma-algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Independent sigma-algebras](independent-sigma-algebras.md)

For a [sequence](sequence.md) of [random variables](random-variable-split.md) $(X_n)_{n\geq1}$ on a [probability space](probability-space-split.md) $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$, define

$$
\mathcal G_n=\sigma(X_n,X_{n+1},\ldots),\qquad
\mathcal T=\bigcap_{n\geq1}\mathcal G_n.
$$

The decreasing [sigma-algebras](sigma-algebra.md) $\mathcal G_n$ are generated by the listed [random variables](random-variable-split.md). Their [intersection](set-intersection.md) consists of [events](event.md) expressible after discarding every finite prefix. No [independence](independent-random-variables.md) is required.[Probability: Theory and Examples (fifth edition), §2.5 Convergence of Random Series, printed p. 81: definition of the tail sigma-field](probability-theory-and-examples-fifth-edition-by-rick-durrett.md)

The point is to distinguish a long observation record from a property of an infinite [sequence](sequence.md). The [event](event.md) of seeing infinitely many heads and the [event](event.md) that [sample means](sample-mean.md) converge survive any finite change, while an exact first-head time does not. [Kolmogorov's zero–one law](kolmogorov-s-zero-one-law.md) adds the striking conclusion that under [independence](independent-random-variables.md) each [tail event](tail-event.md) has [probability](probability.md) zero or one; the [tail sigma-algebra](tail-sigma-algebra.md) itself can still contain many nonempty [sets](set-split.md).
