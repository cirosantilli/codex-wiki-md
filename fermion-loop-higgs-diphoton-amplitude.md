# Fermion-loop Higgs diphoton amplitude

↑ **Parent:** [Higgs boson](higgs-boson.md)

For a charged [Dirac field](dirac-field.md) excitation of [mass](mass.md) $m$, multiplicity $N$, electric coupling $eQ$ and scalar [Yukawa interaction](yukawa-interaction.md) $-yh\bar\psi\psi$, two orientations of a [fermion loop](fermion-loop.md) induce a [Higgs boson](higgs-boson.md) coupling to two [photons](photon.md). With metric $(+---)$ and the usual $S=1+iT$ convention, their physical on-shell [scattering amplitude](scattering-amplitude.md) is

$$
\mathcal M=-\frac{Nye^2Q^2m}{2\pi^2}\epsilon_1^\mu\epsilon_2^\nu T_{\mu\nu}
\int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\,\frac{1-4xy}{m^2-(p_1+p_2)^2xy-i0}.
$$

The [Ward identity](ward-identity.md) holds on both [photon](photon.md) legs. At low external [energy](energy-split.md), the integral tends to $1/(3m^2)$. Thus fixed $y$ gives an inverse-[mass](mass.md) coefficient, whereas $y=m/v$ gives a constant coefficient as $m$ increases at fixed $v$. The distinction is essential when applying [heavy-field decoupling](heavy-field-decoupling.md).
