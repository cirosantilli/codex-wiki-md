# Equivalence point

↑ **Parent:** [Acid-base titration](acid-base-titration.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalence_point)

The [equivalence point](equivalence-point.md) is the composition at which added [titrant](titrant.md) and the initial sample inventory match the specified [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md)'s [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md). It is a theoretical inventory condition; the [titration endpoint](titration-endpoint.md) is an observed stopping criterion. For a [monoprotic acid](monoprotic-acid.md) consuming one [hydroxide](hydroxide.md) per acid unit, initial acid [molar concentration](molar-concentration.md) $c_A$, sample [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) $V_A$, and base [titrant](titrant.md) [molar concentration](molar-concentration.md) $c_B$ give

$$
n_A=c_AV_A=c_BV_{\mathrm{eq}},\qquad V_{\mathrm{eq}}=\frac{c_AV_A}{c_B}.
$$

The proof is [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) conservation with one-to-one [neutralization](neutralization.md); a different [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md) requires its coefficients. All [volumes](volume.md) must use compatible units. Since $1\,\mathrm{mL}=10^{-3}\,\mathrm L$, $0.100\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}\times10.00\,\mathrm{mL}=0.001000\,\mathrm{mol}$, not $1\,\mathrm{mol}$.

An [equivalence point](equivalence-point.md) does not prescribe [pH](ph.md) $7$. A [weak acid](weak-acid.md) titrated with a [strong base](strong-base.md) leaves its [conjugate base](conjugate-base.md), which reacts with [water](water.md); the corresponding [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md) can make the [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) alkaline. The neutral [strong acid](strong-acid.md)/[strong base](strong-base.md) case additionally depends on [temperature](temperature-split.md), through [self-ionization of water](self-ionization-of-water.md).[Chemistry 2e, §14.7, Example 14.21(c), Example 14.22(b), and Figure 14.18: different equivalence-point pH values](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/14-7-acid-base-titrations.md)
