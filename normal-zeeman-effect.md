# Normal Zeeman effect

↑ **Parent:** [Zeeman splitting](zeeman-splitting.md)

The normal Zeeman effect is the simple three-[frequency](frequency.md) pattern obtained in the orbital limit. Assume negligible net [spin](spin.md), negligible [hyperfine structure](hyperfine-structure.md) and quadratic shifts, and an allowed [electric-dipole interaction](electric-dipole-interaction.md) between levels with orbital splitting factor one. A level with $J=0$ has only $m_J=0$ and no first-order shift. For the other levels, $g_J=1$ gives $\Delta E=\mu_Bm_JB$. Define $q=m_u-m_l$, rather than the final-minus-initial change of the emitting atom. The [electric-dipole selection rule](electric-dipole-selection-rule.md) allows $q=0,\pm1$, hence

$$
\nu_{ul}=\nu_0+q\delta\nu_B,\qquad
\delta\nu_B=\frac{\mu_BB}{h}.
$$

Many allowed transitions can share one [frequency](frequency.md); a triplet need not mean exactly three transitions. The cleanest illustrative example is $J_u=1\to J_l=0$, with three upper [quantum states](quantum-state.md) and one unshifted lower [quantum state](quantum-state.md). The $q=0$ component is called $\pi$, and $q=\pm1$ components $\sigma^\pm$ with this stated convention. During emission the atom changes its [magnetic quantum number](magnetic-quantum-number.md) by $-q$.

For an original numerical example choose $B=0.50\ \mathrm T$ and a zero-field vacuum [wavelength](wavelength.md) $\lambda_0=500\ \mathrm{nm}$. Using $\mu_B\simeq9.27401\times10^{-24}\ \mathrm{J\,T^{-1}}$ and $h=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}$ gives $\delta\nu_B\simeq7.00\ \mathrm{GHz}$. The two outer components are separated by $2\delta\nu_B\simeq14.0\ \mathrm{GHz}$. Differentiating the vacuum relation $\lambda=c/\nu$, with $c$ the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md), gives

$$
\Delta\lambda_q\simeq-\frac{\lambda_0^2}{c}q\delta\nu_B.
$$

Each outer component lies about $0.00584\ \mathrm{nm}$ from the centre. Positive $q$ means higher [frequency](frequency.md) and shorter [wavelength](wavelength.md). Equal spacing in [frequency](frequency.md) is only approximately equal spacing in [wavelength](wavelength.md). These are calculated values for a chosen model, not historical measurements.
