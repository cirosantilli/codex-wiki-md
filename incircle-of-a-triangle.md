# Incircle of a triangle

↑ **Parent:** [Triangle](triangle.md)

The [incircle of a triangle](incircle-of-a-triangle.md) is the [circle](circle.md) inside the [triangle](triangle.md) tangent to all three sides. Its center is the intersection of two internal angle bisectors; its radius is $r=\Delta/s$, where $\Delta$ is the area and $s$ the semiperimeter. To prove it is the largest contained [circle](circle.md), let any candidate have center $P$, radius $\rho$ and distances $d_i\geq\rho$ from the three side lines. Splitting the [triangle](triangle.md) into three [triangles](triangle.md) with vertex $P$ gives $\Delta=\frac12\sum_i\ell_i d_i\geq s\rho$. The tangent [circle](circle.md) attains equality, proving maximality. For a [right triangle](right-triangle.md) with legs $a,b$ and hypotenuse $c$, $r=(a+b-c)/2$, since $r=ab/(a+b+c)$ and $a^2+b^2=c^2$.
