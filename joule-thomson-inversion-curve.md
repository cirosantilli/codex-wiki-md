# Joule-Thomson inversion curve

↑ **Parent:** [Joule-Thomson coefficient](joule-thomson-coefficient.md)

The [Joule-Thomson inversion curve](joule-thomson-inversion-curve.md) is the set of equilibrium [temperature](temperature-split.md)–[pressure](pressure-split.md) states where the [Joule-Thomson coefficient](joule-thomson-coefficient.md) vanishes. For positive [heat capacity at constant pressure](heat-capacity-at-constant-pressure.md), its condition is $T\alpha=1$, with $\alpha$ the [coefficient of thermal expansion](coefficient-of-thermal-expansion.md). The curve separates locally cooling and warming throttle regions. A finite [isenthalpic process](isenthalpic-process.md) can cross it, so the inlet sign alone does not fix the total [temperature](temperature-split.md) change. An [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) has zero coefficient everywhere, rather than an isolated dividing curve.
