# Inclined-plane measurement of static friction

↑ **Parent:** [Static friction on an inclined plane](static-friction-on-an-inclined-plane.md)

A university laboratory places an [aluminium](aluminium.md) block on a [Teflon](teflon.md)-lined wooden board. For its [inclined plane](inclined-plane.md) stage, the pulley and block string are removed; a rope and hook connect the board to a crank. The block is placed gently near the sheet's middle without prior sliding. Smooth cranking raises the board; a $0^\circ$–$45^\circ$ [protractor](protractor.md) reads the smallest [angle](angle.md) at which sliding begins. Four readings are taken without added load, then four each with $200$, $400$, and $800\,\mathrm g$ added symmetrically at two screws. Removing the string isolates gravity-driven onset; gentle placement and smooth raising avoid premature disturbance.[Printed p. 5, Part III, steps 1–7 and Figure 6; worksheet p. 2, Table 3](friction-physics-183-experiment-5-fall-2004.md)

Infer $\mu_s=\tan\theta_c$ under the assumptions of [static friction on an inclined plane](static-friction-on-an-inclined-plane.md). Repetition tests onset scatter; added loads test coefficient constancy. No measured onset values are supplied here.

For a replication, additionally check the [protractor](protractor.md) zero with a level board, retain the same contact face and surface region, and observe whether tipping or rolling precedes sliding. These are proposed additional controls. A finite [angle](angle.md) resolution and the disturbance needed to detect first motion limit the estimate; a slow ramp does not by itself establish perfectly rigid, dry, or direction-independent contact. At the model's $\mu_s=0.40$ threshold, differentiating the [tangent function](tangent-function.md) gives $d\mu_s/d\theta=1+\mu_s^2=1.16$, with $\theta$ in [radians](radian.md). A hypothetical $0.5^\circ$ [measurement error](measurement-error.md) would therefore correspond to approximately $0.0101$ in the inferred coefficient at first order. This sensitivity calculation is not an uncertainty reported by the laboratory.
