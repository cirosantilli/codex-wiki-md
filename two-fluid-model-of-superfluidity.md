# Two-fluid model of superfluidity

↑ **Parent:** [Normal component of a superfluid](normal-component-of-a-superfluid.md)

The [two-fluid model of superfluidity](two-fluid-model-of-superfluidity.md) gives the [normal component of a superfluid](normal-component-of-a-superfluid.md) and the [superfluid](superfluid.md) independent [velocities](velocity.md).

An elementary uniform-flow example makes the distinction operational. Write the [mass](mass.md) flux per unit [area](surface-area.md) and the ideal [entropy](entropy-split.md) flux as

$$
\mathbf j=\rho_s\mathbf v_s+\rho_n\mathbf v_n,\qquad
\mathbf j_S=\rho s\mathbf v_n,\qquad \rho=\rho_s+\rho_n.
$$

Here $s$ is [specific entropy](specific-entropy.md), in $\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$, and $\rho_s,\rho_n$ are response [mass densities](density.md). The [mass density](density.md) $\rho_s$ used here gives the three-dimensional [phase stiffness](phase-stiffness.md) coefficient $\rho_s(\hbar/m)^2$ when $\mathbf v_s=(\hbar/m)\nabla\theta$ is substituted into the [kinetic energy density](kinetic-energy-density.md) $\rho_s v_s^2/2$. For a stipulated $\rho_s=3\rho/4$, $\rho_n=\rho/4$ and $v_n=3u$ along a channel, zero net [mass](mass.md) flux requires $v_s=-u$. Yet $j_S=3\rho su$ is positive. Opposing [velocities](velocity.md) can therefore transport [entropy](entropy-split.md) while transporting no net [mass](mass.md).

This example explains why excellent apparent [heat](heat-split.md) transport need not be ordinary diffusion of [heat](heat-split.md) through a stationary [liquid](liquid.md). In the ideal zero-net-[mass](mass.md)-flux limit, the [heat](heat-split.md) flux associated with this [entropy](entropy-split.md) transport is $Tj_S$. Viscous losses, [quantum vortices](quantum-vortex.md) and external [heat](heat-split.md) exchange require additional terms. The model also supports [second sound](second-sound.md).
