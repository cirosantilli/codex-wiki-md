<h1 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Decreasing $q=f_0/f$ increases the blueshift required for excitation. Both characteristic speeds move toward $c$:

$$
\beta_{\min}=\frac{1-q^2}{1+q^2},\qquad
\beta_\perp=\sqrt{1-q^2}.
$$

If $q$ is near one, modest approach speeds can excite the line. If $q\ll1$, only strongly relativistic motion reaches the line centre. At a fixed qualifying speed, decreasing $q$ moves the resonance toward earlier approach, since a larger $D_{\rm in}$ is required. It can also move the event outside the illumination window set by $t_e\ge-T$.

At fixed $q$, increasing $\beta$ moves the resonance from the far approaching tail, through closest approach at $\beta_\perp$, and eventually to the receding side. The received pulse [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) cannot be estimated simply by applying the same blueshift twice: the inward and outward [Doppler factors](../../../../../doppler-factor.md) are different, and decay may occur well after excitation. The critical-speed rows of part [i](../i.md) make these changes explicit.
