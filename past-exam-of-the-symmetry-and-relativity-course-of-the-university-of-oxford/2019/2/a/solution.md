<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the inverse standard [Lorentz boost](../../../../lorentz-boost.md), $t=\gamma_v(t'+vx'/c^2)$ and $x=\gamma_v(x'+vt')$. Label two rod endpoints by a separation $\Delta x'=L$; their world lines satisfy $y'=ut'$. A rod's orientation in $S$ must compare endpoint events simultaneous in $S$, not in $S'$. Consequently

$$
0=\Delta t=\gamma_v\left(\Delta t'+\frac{vL}{c^2}\right),\qquad
\Delta t'=-\frac{vL}{c^2}.
$$

Then

$$
\Delta x=\gamma_v(L+v\Delta t')=\frac L{\gamma_v},\qquad
\Delta y=u\Delta t'=-\frac{uvL}{c^2}.
$$

The signed counterclockwise angle therefore satisfies

$$
\boxed{\tan\theta=-\gamma_v\frac{uv}{c^2}.}
$$

For positive $u,v$, the rod slopes downward to the right, so the acute clockwise angle is $\arctan(\gamma_vuv/c^2)$. This is a [relativity of simultaneity](../../../../relativity-of-simultaneity.md) effect in addition to the longitudinal [length contraction](../../../../length-contraction.md); the rod is not being mechanically bent by the coordinate change.
