<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An invariant has the same value when the same physical event or state is described in different [inertial frames](../../../../inertial-frame.md). A conserved quantity has the same value as the physical system evolves, subject to its equations and isolation conditions. These are different comparisons. The [invariant mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md) of a heated object can change with [time](../../../../time-in-physics.md), while an isolated system's conserved [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) has different numerical values in boosted frames.

Under a [Lorentz transformation](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) $x'^\mu=\Lambda^\mu{}_{\nu}x^\nu$, a [Lorentz four-vector](../../../../four-vector.md) transforms as

$$
\boxed{V'^\mu(x')=\Lambda^\mu{}_{\nu}V^\nu(x).}
$$

A [covector](../../../../covector.md) transforms by the inverse matrix, $W'_\mu=(\Lambda^{-1})^\nu{}_{\mu}W_\nu$. A general [tensor](../../../../tensor.md) has one corresponding factor for each index; for example,

$$
\boxed{T'^{\mu\nu}{}_{\rho}(x')=
\Lambda^\mu{}_{\alpha}\Lambda^\nu{}_{\beta}
(\Lambda^{-1})^\gamma{}_{\rho}T^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\gamma}(x).}
$$

Contraction with the [Minkowski metric](../../../../minkowski-metric.md) produces [Lorentz scalars](../../../../lorentz-scalar.md); it does not by itself make those scalars conserved.
