<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

To prove the [vanishing component theorem for a Lorentz four-vector](../../../../vanishing-component-theorem-for-a-lorentz-four-vector.md), first suppose $Y^0=0$ in every [inertial frame](../../../../inertial-frame.md). An infinitesimal boost along spatial direction $i$ gives $Y'^0=Y^0-\beta Y^i+O(\beta^2)$. It too vanishes for every $\beta$, so each $Y^i=0$. If instead a specified spatial component vanishes in every frame, spatial rotations first imply that all three spatial components vanish in a chosen frame, and a boost in the specified direction then forces $Y^0=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{one specified component zero in every Lorentz frame}\ \Longrightarrow\ Y^\mu=0.}
$$

A physical consequence is that [conservation of energy](../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) in every [inertial frame](../../../../inertial-frame.md) implies full [four-momentum conservation](../../../../four-momentum-conservation.md): apply the result to the difference between outgoing and incoming total [four-momenta](../../../../four-momentum.md).

The statement fails for general [tensors](../../../../tensor.md). A nonzero antisymmetric rank-two [tensor](../../../../tensor.md) has $F^{00}=0$ in every frame, because antisymmetry is preserved. For an off-diagonal component, the nonzero [Minkowski metric](../../../../minkowski-metric.md) is another counterexample: $\eta^{01}=0$ in all orthonormal [Lorentz frames](../../../../lorentz-frame.md), while $\eta$ is certainly not zero. The transformation law can preserve structural zero components without forcing the entire [tensor](../../../../tensor.md) to vanish.
