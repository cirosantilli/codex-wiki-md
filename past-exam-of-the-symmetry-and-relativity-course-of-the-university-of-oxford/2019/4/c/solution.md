<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A relativistic two-component [spinor](../../../../spinor.md) is an element of a two-dimensional complex representation of the [special linear group](../../../../special-linear-group.md) $SL(2,\mathbb C)$, the double cover of the proper orthochronous [Lorentz group](../../../../lorentz-group.md). A [Weyl spinor](../../../../weyl-spinor.md) transforms in either $(1/2,0)$ or $(0,1/2)$; parity interchanges the two chiralities. It describes a chiral spin-$1/2$ degree of freedom. A free massless [Weyl field](../../../../weyl-field.md) has one particle [helicity](../../../../helicity.md) and the opposite antiparticle [helicity](../../../../helicity.md).

The number of components alone does not prove zero [mass](../../../../mass.md). A massive [Dirac field](../../../../dirac-field.md) has two coupled two-component chiral fields, and a nonrelativistic [Pauli spinor](../../../../pauli-spinor.md) describes the two spin states of a massive spin-$1/2$ particle. A [Majorana mass term](../../../../majorana-mass-term.md) can also be written using a two-component field and its complex conjugate. Thus a single independent free massless Weyl equation is a dynamical assumption, not part of the bare algebraic definition of a two-component spinor.
