<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For a free massless [Weyl spinor](../../../../weyl-spinor.md), yes: each component obeys the massless [Klein-Gordon equation](../../../../klein-gordon-equation.md). In $c=\hbar=1$ units, take either chiral [Weyl equation](../../../../weyl-equation.md), for example

$$
(\partial_t-\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\nabla)u=0.
$$

Apply its opposite-sign partner. The [Pauli matrix multiplication identity](../../../../pauli-multiplication-identity.md) makes the antisymmetric part vanish against the symmetric pair of spatial derivatives, so

$$
(\partial_t+\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\nabla)
(\partial_t-\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\nabla)u
=(\partial_t^2-\nabla^2)u=0.
$$

Thus $\boxed{\partial_\mu\partial^\mu u=0}$ componentwise. This does not turn the field into a scalar: its components still mix under a [Spinor representation of the Lorentz group](../../../../spinor-representation-of-the-lorentz-group.md). The second-order equation is a necessary consequence of the first-order equation, but does not by itself impose the chiral helicity relation.

There is no unconditional massless-wave conclusion from merely being a two-component spinor, or from the null bilinear of part [d](../d.md). For example, $u(t,\mathbf x)=e^{-i\omega t}(1,0)^T$ has a null bilinear at every point, yet $(\partial_t^2-\nabla^2)u=-\omega^2u\ne0$ for $\omega\ne0$. Massive free two-component chiral fields coupled through a mass term instead obey the massive [Klein-Gordon equation](../../../../klein-gordon-equation.md); interacting fields can have additional terms. The affirmative result therefore uses the free, massless dynamics.
