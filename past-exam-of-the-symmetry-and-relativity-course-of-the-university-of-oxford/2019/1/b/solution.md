<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $m$ denote the constant [rest mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md), set $x(0)=0$, and put $a=f_x/m$. The relativistic three-[force](../../../../force-split.md) is $f_x=dp_x/dt$, so

$$
p_x=f_xt,\qquad p_x=\gamma m\dot x,\qquad
\gamma=\sqrt{1+\frac{f_x^2t^2}{m^2c^2}}.
$$

It follows that

$$
\boxed{\dot x(t)=\frac{at}{\sqrt{1+(at/c)^2}},\qquad
\ddot x(t)=\frac{a}{[1+(at/c)^2]^{3/2}}.}
$$

Integrating the [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) with the initial position fixed gives

$$
\boxed{x(t)=\frac{c^2}{a}\left[\sqrt{1+(at/c)^2}-1\right].}
$$

These formulas allow either sign of $a$; their $a\to0$ limit is rest at the origin. For $a\ne0$, the trajectory obeys

$$
\left(x+\frac{c^2}{a}\right)^2-c^2t^2=\frac{c^4}{a^2}.
$$

This is [hyperbolic motion](../../../../hyperbolic-motion.md). Its coordinate [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) decreases while its [proper acceleration](../../../../proper-acceleration.md) has constant magnitude $|a|$. In particular, a constant three-[force](../../../../force-split.md) does not give constant coordinate [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) at relativistic [speed](../../../../speed.md).
