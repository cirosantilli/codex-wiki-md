<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use $c=1$ and define the total [four-momentum](../../../../four-momentum.md) $P^\mu=(E,\mathbf P)=\sum_i(E_i,\mathbf p_i)$. For an [inertial frame](../../../../inertial-frame.md) moving with [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) $\mathbf V$ relative to the laboratory, the [Lorentz boost](../../../../lorentz-boost.md) gives

$$
P'_{\parallel}=\gamma_V(P_{\parallel}-VE),\qquad \mathbf P'_{\perp}=\mathbf P_{\perp}.
$$

Choose $\mathbf V$ parallel to $\mathbf P$ and require zero total [three-momentum](../../../../three-momentum.md). Then

$$
\boxed{\mathbf V_{\mathrm{CM/lab}}=\frac{\mathbf P}{E}=\frac{\sum_i\mathbf p_i}{\sum_iE_i}.}
$$

This requires $E>|\mathbf P|$, so that the total [four-momentum](../../../../four-momentum.md) is timelike. Its [invariant mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md) is $\sqrt{E^2-|\mathbf P|^2}$, the total [relativistic energy](../../../../relativistic-energy.md) in the [center-of-momentum frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md). If all particles are exactly parallel [photons](../../../../photon.md), the total [four-momentum](../../../../four-momentum.md) is null and no finite-[velocity](../../../../velocity.md) [center-of-momentum frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md) exists.

**Sign qualification.** The minus sign printed in the source describes the laboratory's [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) in the [center-of-momentum frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md), or the parameter of an active [Lorentz boost](../../../../lorentz-boost.md) with a plus-sign spatial rule. It is not the [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) of the [center-of-momentum frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md) relative to the laboratory under question 1's passive convention. For example, a single particle with $(E,P_x)=(5,3)$ has its rest frame moving at $V_x=3/5$. The printed $V_x=-3/5$ would give $P'_x=(5/4)(3+3)=15/2$, which is not zero. This explicitly resolves the sign rather than assigning a rest frame moving away from the particle.
