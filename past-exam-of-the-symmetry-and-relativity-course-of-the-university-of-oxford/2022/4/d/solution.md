<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Both the [muon](../../../../muon.md) and [electron](../../../../electron.md) have the same [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md) magnitude. At equal kinetic [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) $K$ per particle, their [Lorentz factors](../../../../lorentz-factor.md) are $\gamma=1+K/(mc^2)$, so the heavier [muon](../../../../muon.md) has the smaller [Lorentz factor](../../../../lorentz-factor.md). At equal total [relativistic energy](../../../../relativistic-energy.md) the same ordering follows from $\gamma=E/(mc^2)$, provided that [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is large enough to create either particle.

**If the equal-[acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) comparison of [c](../c.md) is retained**, $P_{\mathrm{circular}}=C_qa^2\gamma^4\le C_qa^2\gamma^6=P_{\mathrm{linear}}$. Thus the four-way instantaneous comparison selects

$$
\boxed{\text{a muon in a circular accelerator, at fixed energy and coordinate acceleration}.}
$$

For positive $K$, its smaller [Lorentz factor](../../../../lorentz-factor.md) reduces the [power](../../../../power.md) relative to an [electron](../../../../electron.md), and transverse [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) reduces the [power](../../../../power.md) relative to longitudinal [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) of the same magnitude. This conclusion follows from the constraint actually stated in [c](../c.md).

**For the customary engineering comparison at equal applied [force](../../../../force-split.md)**, the result changes. From [c](../c.md),

$$
P_{\mathrm{linear}}=\frac{q^2F^2}{6\pi\epsilon_0m^2c^3},\qquad
P_{\mathrm{circular}}=\gamma^2P_{\mathrm{linear}}.
$$

Under that comparison the choice is

$$
\boxed{\text{a muon in a linear accelerator, at fixed energy and applied force}.}
$$

Its longitudinal radiative [power](../../../../power.md) is smaller than an [electron](../../../../electron.md)'s by $(m_e/m_\mu)^2$, while avoiding bending removes the extra $\gamma^2$ factor. At a fixed bend radius $R$, the circular loss is $C_q\gamma^4v^4/R^2$, giving the familiar $m^{-4}$ suppression at fixed ultrarelativistic [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) and $R$.

**An [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) budget alone does not specify a unique optimization.** The [force](../../../../force-split.md), [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md), bend radius, acceleration time, and length are otherwise unrestricted. For example, in a [linear accelerator](../../../../linear-particle-accelerator.md) a constant small [force](../../../../force-split.md) $F$ gives radiative [power](../../../../power.md) proportional to $F^2$, and reaching a specified final [three-momentum](../../../../three-momentum.md) takes time proportional to $F^{-1}$; the total radiated [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) therefore tends to zero as $F\to0$. One cannot infer a unique accelerator choice without an additional constraint. The standard practical preference is the second boxed answer, with its stated comparison. [Muon decay](../../../../muon-decay.md) introduces a separate lifetime constraint if slow acceleration is contemplated, but it was not part of the specified radiative-loss calculation.
