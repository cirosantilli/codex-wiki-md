<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For constant laboratory [force](../../../../force-split.md) $\mathbf F$ and initial rest, [integration](../../../../integral-split.md) gives [three-momentum](../../../../three-momentum.md) $\mathbf p=\mathbf Ft$. The [energy–momentum relation](../../../../energy-momentum-relation.md) gives $E=\sqrt{m^2c^4+c^2|\mathbf F|^2t^2}$, and $\mathbf v=c^2\mathbf p/E$, so

$$
\boxed{\mathbf v(t)=\frac{\mathbf Ft/m}{\sqrt{1+|\mathbf F|^2t^2/(m^2c^2)}}.}
$$

For $\mathbf F\ne0$ and $t\ge0$, its [speed](../../../../speed.md) increases monotonically, is strictly below the [speed of light](../../../../speed-of-light.md) at every finite time, and obeys $\lim_{t\to\infty}|\mathbf v(t)|=c$. Thus $c$ is the limiting supremum, rather than a [speed](../../../../speed.md) attained at a finite time. The degenerate case $\mathbf F=0$ leaves the particle at rest and does not have this limit.
