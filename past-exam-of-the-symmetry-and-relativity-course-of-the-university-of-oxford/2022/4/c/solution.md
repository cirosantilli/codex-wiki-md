<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**Metric-sign qualification.** With this paper's $(-,+,+,+)$ [metric signature](../../../../metric-signature.md), [b](../b.md) gives

$$
F^\alpha F_\alpha=\gamma^2\left(|\mathbf F|^2-\frac{(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf v)^2}{c^2}\right)\ge0.
$$

The minus sign in the printed [Larmor formula](../../../../larmor-formula.md) would therefore make a positive radiative loss negative. A concrete counterexample is an instant of rest with nonzero [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md): $F^\alpha=(0,m\mathbf a)$, so the printed expression is $-q^2a^2/(6\pi\epsilon_0c^3)$. That sign is appropriate for the opposite [metric signature](../../../../metric-signature.md). With the convention already fixed in question 1, positive emitted [power](../../../../power.md) instead is

$$
P_{\mathrm{rad}}=\frac{q^2}{6\pi\epsilon_0m^2c^3}F^\alpha F_\alpha.
$$

Alternatively the printed negative quantity can be interpreted as the particle's signed radiative [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) change, rather than the positive loss magnitude.

For fixed [rest mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md), resolve the [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) parallel and perpendicular to the [velocity](../../../../velocity.md), as in [b](../b.md). This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{F^\alpha F_\alpha}{m^2}
&=\gamma^2\left(\gamma^6a_\parallel^2+\gamma^2a_\perp^2-\gamma^6\frac{v^2}{c^2}a_\parallel^2\right)\\
&=\gamma^6a_\parallel^2+\gamma^4a_\perp^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Writing $C_q=q^2/(6\pi\epsilon_0c^3)$, the [Liénard radiated-power formula](../../../../lienard-radiated-power-formula.md) becomes $P_{\mathrm{rad}}=C_q(\gamma^6a_\parallel^2+\gamma^4a_\perp^2)$. For uniform [circular motion](../../../../circular-motion.md), the [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) is perpendicular to the [velocity](../../../../velocity.md); for straight-line motion it is parallel. Comparing the same particle at equal [relativistic energy](../../../../relativistic-energy.md) and equal laboratory [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) magnitude $a$ therefore gives

$$
\boxed{P_{\mathrm{circular}}=C_q\gamma^4a^2,\qquad
P_{\mathrm{linear}}=C_q\gamma^6a^2,\qquad
\frac{P_{\mathrm{circular}}}{P_{\mathrm{linear}}}=\gamma^{-2}.}
$$

This particular comparison makes the linear loss larger. The familiar larger loss from bending applies to a different comparison: at equal applied laboratory [force](../../../../force-split.md) magnitude $F$, $a_\perp=F/(m\gamma)$ and $a_\parallel=F/(m\gamma^3)$, giving $P_{\mathrm{circular}}=C_q\gamma^2F^2/m^2$ and $P_{\mathrm{linear}}=C_qF^2/m^2$. Stating which quantity is held fixed is essential.
