<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [four-gradient](../../../../four-gradient.md) rules from [a](../a.md) give

$$
\partial'_\mu\partial'^\mu
=(\Lambda^{-1})^\nu{}_{\mu}\partial_\nu
\Lambda^\mu{}_{\rho}\partial^\rho
=\delta^\nu{}_{\rho}\partial_\nu\partial^\rho
=\partial_\nu\partial^\nu.
$$

The [Lorentz transformation](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) coefficients are constant, so the [partial derivatives](../../../../partial-derivative.md) do not differentiate them. This proves the [Lorentz invariance](../../../../lorentz-invariance.md) of the [d'Alembert operator](../../../../d-alembert-operator.md):

$$
\boxed{\Box=\partial_\mu\partial^\mu=\nabla^2-c^{-2}\partial_t^2.}
$$

For example, the two longitudinal terms transform as $-\gamma^2(\partial_0+\beta\partial_1)^2+\gamma^2(\partial_1+\beta\partial_0)^2=-\partial_0^2+\partial_1^2$, using $\gamma^2(1-\beta^2)=1$ and commuting [partial derivatives](../../../../partial-derivative.md). The transverse terms are unchanged. Applied to a [scalar field](../../../../scalar-field.md), $\Box$ therefore gives a [Lorentz scalar](../../../../lorentz-scalar.md).
