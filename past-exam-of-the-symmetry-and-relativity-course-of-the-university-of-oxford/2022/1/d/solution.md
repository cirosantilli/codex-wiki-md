<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [Lorenz gauge condition](../../../../lorenz-gauge-condition.md) is $\nabla\cdot\mathbf A=-c^{-2}\partial_t\phi$. Substitute the [electric potential](../../../../electric-potential.md) and [magnetic vector potential](../../../../magnetic-vector-potential.md) into [Gauss's law](../../../../gauss-s-law.md):

$$
-\nabla^2\phi-\partial_t(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)=\rho/\epsilon_0,
\qquad
\Box\phi=-\rho/\epsilon_0.
$$

Next the [Ampère-Maxwell equation](../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md), the [curl of the curl identity](../../../../curl-of-the-curl-identity.md), and the [Lorenz gauge condition](../../../../lorenz-gauge-condition.md) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)-\nabla^2\mathbf A
&=\mu_0\mathbf J-c^{-2}\nabla\partial_t\phi-c^{-2}\partial_t^2\mathbf A,\\
\Box\mathbf A&=-\mu_0\mathbf J.
\end{aligned}
$$

The [gradient](../../../../gradient.md) terms cancel. Since $\mu_0\epsilon_0c^2=1$, the time component obeys $\Box(\phi/c)=-\mu_0c\rho$, so together

$$
\boxed{\partial^\nu\partial_\nu A^a=-\mu_0J^a,\qquad A^\mu=(\phi/c,\mathbf A).}
$$

The [four-potential](../../../../electromagnetic-four-potential.md) can be chosen to transform as a [four-vector](../../../../four-vector.md). To make the argument rigorous, choose the [retarded potential](../../../../retarded-potential.md) with no freely added homogeneous solution. If $\Box G_{\mathrm{ret}}(x)=\delta^{(4)}(x)$, this choice is

$$
A^\mu(x)=-\mu_0\int G_{\mathrm{ret}}(x-y)J^\mu(y)\,d^4y.
$$

The [retarded Green function](../../../../retarded-green-function.md) is a [Lorentz scalar](../../../../lorentz-scalar.md) under [proper orthochronous Lorentz transformations](../../../../proper-orthochronous-lorentz-transformation.md), and $d^4y$ is invariant. The [four-vector](../../../../four-vector.md) rule for $J^\mu$ therefore proves $A'^\mu(x')=\Lambda^\mu{}_{\nu}A^\nu(x)$. More generally, transform any homogeneous initial data by this same rule. Both the [wave equation](../../../../wave-equation-split.md) and the [Lorenz gauge condition](../../../../lorenz-gauge-condition.md) are then preserved.

**Qualification.** The [wave equation](../../../../wave-equation-split.md) by itself cannot prove the transformation rule for every independently chosen [gauge potential](../../../../gauge-field.md): the [d'Alembert operator](../../../../d-alembert-operator.md) has a nontrivial [kernel of a linear map](../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md). For a concrete counterexample, zero sources and zero fields admit $A^\mu=0$ in one [inertial frame](../../../../inertial-frame.md) and $A'^\mu=(0,C,0,0)$ in another. Both satisfy the [wave equation](../../../../wave-equation-split.md) and the [Lorenz gauge condition](../../../../lorenz-gauge-condition.md), but the latter is not the [Lorentz transformation](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) of zero. It is a [residual Lorenz gauge symmetry](../../../../residual-lorenz-gauge-symmetry.md), generated by $\chi=Cx'$. Thus the usual assertion is a covariant choice of [four-potential](../../../../electromagnetic-four-potential.md), with a consistent [gauge transformation](../../../../gauge-transformation.md) and homogeneous data, rather than a uniqueness result from the displayed equation.
