<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The event separation is timelike precisely when

$$
\boxed{(D-B)^2=c^2(t_d-t_b)^2-|\mathbf x_d-\mathbf x_b|^2>0.}
$$

A simultaneous pair in an inertial frame would instead have $(D-B)^2=-|\Delta\mathbf x'|^2\le0$. Since a [Lorentz transformation](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) preserves this [interval](../../../../interval-mathematics.md), no such simultaneous frame exists. A timelike pair can be brought to the same spatial position: the required [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) is $\mathbf u=\Delta\mathbf x/\Delta t$, whose magnitude is less than $c$.

The separate condition $D\cdot B=0$ says that the two event-position [four-vectors](../../../../four-vector.md), measured from the chosen spacetime origin, are orthogonal in the [Minkowski inner product](../../../../minkowski-inner-product.md). It is invariant under [Lorentz transformations](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) about that origin but not under arbitrary [translations](../../../../translation-geometry.md). It is not a general criterion for their causal separation.

For a concrete example, put $B=(cT,0,0,0)$ and $D=(0,R,0,0)$. Their inner product vanishes, but their separation has squared interval $c^2T^2-R^2$, which can be positive, zero or negative. If $B$ is timelike, a rest frame for $B$ makes $D^0=0$; this means that $D$ and the chosen origin are simultaneous in that frame, not that $D$ and $B$ are simultaneous.
