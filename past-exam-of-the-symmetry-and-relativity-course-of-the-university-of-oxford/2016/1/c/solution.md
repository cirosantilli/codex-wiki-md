<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [proper acceleration](../../../../proper-acceleration.md) is $\alpha=\sqrt{-A^2}$, the magnitude of the instantaneous rest-frame three-acceleration. A [pure force](../../../../pure-force.md) is a [four-force](../../../../four-force.md) preserving [rest mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md), or equivalently satisfying $F\cdot U=0$. Indeed $F=d(mU)/d\tau$ gives $F\cdot U=c^2dm/d\tau$.

For the right-hand branch of the hyperbola, use $c=1$, $x>0$ and $x=\sqrt{L^2+t^2}$, taking $L>0$. Direct differentiation gives

$$
v=\frac{dx}{dt}=\frac t x,\qquad
|v|=\sqrt{1-\frac{L^2}{x^2}},\qquad
\gamma=\frac xL,\qquad
 a=\frac{dv}{dt}=\frac{L^2}{x^3}.
$$

The sign of $v$ follows the sign of $t$, whereas the coordinate acceleration on this branch is positive. Consequently

$$
\boxed{a\gamma^3=\frac1L=\alpha.}
$$

The proper-time parameterization $t=L\sinh(\tau/L)$, $x=L\cosh(\tau/L)$ independently gives constant [proper acceleration](../../../../proper-acceleration.md). Restoring dimensions, a hyperbola $x^2-c^2t^2=L^2$ has $\alpha=c^2/L$. Constant proper acceleration does not mean that all inertial-frame components of the [four-force](../../../../four-force.md) are constant.
