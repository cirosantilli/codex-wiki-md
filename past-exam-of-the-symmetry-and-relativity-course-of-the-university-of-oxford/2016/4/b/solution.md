<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In the massive particle's rest frame, $P=(m_0c,\mathbf0)$ and the physical [Pauli-Lubanski pseudovector](../../../../pauli-lubanski-pseudovector.md) is $W=(0,m_0c\,\mathbf s_{\rm rest})$, up to a common overall sign. Their [Minkowski inner product](../../../../minkowski-inner-product.md) is therefore zero. Since it is a [Lorentz scalar](../../../../lorentz-scalar.md),

$$
\boxed{W\cdot P=0,\qquad U_W\cdot P=0}
$$

in every inertial frame, for any nonzero scalar normalization of $W$. This proves the requested orthogonality without a boosted component calculation.

The printed assertion that its particular normalization is always a unit vector needs qualification. For the displayed components $W=(\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf p,E\mathbf s/c)$,

$$
W^2=-m_0^2c^2|\mathbf s|^2-|\mathbf s\times\mathbf p|^2.
$$

Thus dividing by $m_0c|\mathbf s|$ is unit spacelike only when $\mathbf s\times\mathbf p=0$, or when $\mathbf s$ in that denominator is explicitly replaced by the proper rest-spin magnitude with correctly boosted components of $W$. As a counterexample to the literal displayed normalization, choose $\mathbf s\perp\mathbf p$ with nonzero momentum: it gives $U_W^2=-\gamma^2$, not $-1$. Orthogonality remains true, but it does not establish normalization.
