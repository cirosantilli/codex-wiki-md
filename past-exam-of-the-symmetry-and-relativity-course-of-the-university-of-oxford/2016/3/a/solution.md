<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Three useful observational phenomena are [relativistic Doppler effect](../../../../relativistic-doppler-effect.md) in spectral lines from approaching and receding ejecta, [relativistic beaming](../../../../relativistic-beaming.md) through jet brightness asymmetry, and [apparent superluminal motion](../../../../apparent-superluminal-motion.md) inferred from successive radio images of moving knots. The first compares spectral frequencies with rest-frame lines; the second compares the two sides after accounting for intrinsic emission and absorption; the third tests the light-travel-time relation $\beta_{\rm app}=\beta\sin\vartheta/(1-\beta\cos\vartheta)$. NRAO's time sequence of Galactic microquasar ejecta gives a concrete example of the third measurement.[VLA time-series observations of GRS 1915+105 and explanation of apparent superluminal separation by subluminal jets directed toward the observer](../../../../microquasars-in-our-own-galaxy-by-national-radio-astronomy-observatory.md)

These are tests of relativistic radiation kinematics when source geometry and environmental effects are controlled, rather than claims that any one redshift or brightness contrast by itself uniquely proves [special relativity](../../../../special-relativity-split.md).

For the frequency transformation, a photon has [wave four-vector](../../../../four-wavevector.md) $K=(\omega/c,\mathbf k)$ with $|\mathbf k|=\omega/c$ in vacuum. The temporal component of a [Lorentz boost](../../../../lorentz-boost.md) is

$$
\frac{\omega'}c=\gamma_v\left(\frac\omega c-\frac{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf k}{c}\right),
\qquad
\boxed{\omega'=\gamma_v\omega\left(1-\frac vc\cos\theta\right).}
$$

This is the [relativistic Doppler effect](../../../../relativistic-doppler-effect.md): frequency changes both because of relative motion along the ray and because clocks in relative motion are time dilated. The angle here is measured in S. A frame chasing the photon redshifts it, $\omega'/\omega=\sqrt{(1-\beta)/(1+\beta)}$; a head-on frame blueshifts it. Transverse angles defined in different frames must not be interchanged.
