<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md) is the speed of a constant-phase surface, $v_{\rm ph}=\omega/k$. The [group velocity](../../../../group-velocity.md) is the envelope speed of a narrow packet, $v_{\rm gr}=d\omega/dk$, requiring a local [dispersion relation](../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) rather than only a single monochromatic wave. In an isotropic transparent medium with $k=n(\omega)\omega/c$,

$$
\boxed{v_{\rm ph}=\frac c{n(\omega)},\qquad
v_{\rm gr}=\frac c{n(\omega)+\omega\,dn/d\omega}.}
$$

For a vacuum wave, $n\equiv1$ over the packet bandwidth, so both speeds are $c$ and $\boxed{v_{\rm ph}v_{\rm gr}=c^2}$. No [superluminal signalling](../../../../faster-than-light-communication.md) is implied by a phase speed in a dispersive material.

Strictly, the value $n(\omega_0)=1$ at one frequency alone does not prove the product identity. For example $n(\omega)=1+q(\omega-\omega_0)/\omega_0$ gives $v_{\rm ph}(\omega_0)=c$ but $v_{\rm gr}(\omega_0)=c/(1+q)$. The vacuum interpretation, or an equivalent nondispersive assumption, is necessary.
