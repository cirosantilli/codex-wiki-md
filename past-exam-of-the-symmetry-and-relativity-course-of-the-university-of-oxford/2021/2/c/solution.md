<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For an [ultrarelativistic particle](../../../../ultrarelativistic-particle.md), [relativistic beaming](../../../../relativistic-beaming.md) concentrates the [synchrotron radiation](../../../../synchrotron-radiation.md) within an angle of order $1/\gamma$ around its instantaneous [velocity](../../../../velocity.md). A fixed viewing direction in the orbital plane receives an intense signal only when the tangent sweeps through that direction. One [electron](../../../../electron.md), or one sufficiently short bunch, therefore produces one pulse per revolution, separated by $\mathcal T\simeq10.48\,\mathrm{\mu s}$. Several bunches produce the corresponding additional pulses per revolution.

The time during which the [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) points within that cone is of order $r/(\gamma c)$, but this is the **emission** interval. If $t_a$ is the arrival time at a distant detector,

$$
t_a=t+\frac{D-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf x(t)}c,\qquad
\frac{dt_a}{dt}=1-\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\beta.
$$

Near alignment, $1-\beta\cos\theta\simeq(\gamma^{-2}+\theta^2)/2=O(\gamma^{-2})$. Thus [synchrotron pulse compression](../../../../synchrotron-pulse-compression.md) reduces the observed duration by a further factor of order $\gamma^2$:

$$
\boxed{\Delta t_{a,\mathrm{single}}\sim\frac r{\gamma^3c}
\simeq8.3\times10^{-18}\,\mathrm s.}
$$

A more explicit geometric estimate chooses the cone edges $\theta=\pm1/\gamma$. With $\omega=u/r$, $x=r\sin\theta$ along the line of sight, the difference in [arrival-time compression for a moving source](../../../../arrival-time-compression-for-a-moving-source.md) is

$$
\Delta t_a=\frac{2r}c\left[\frac{1}{\beta\gamma}-\sin\!\left(\frac1\gamma\right)\right]
\simeq\frac{4r}{3c\gamma^3}\simeq1.10\times10^{-17}\,\mathrm s.
$$

The order-one coefficient depends on how pulse width is defined; the robust scale is $r/(c\gamma^3)$. The usual central-cone estimate $r/(2c\gamma^3)$ gives $4.1\times10^{-18}\,\mathrm s$. These estimates agree on the attosecond scale and must not be confused with $r/(\gamma c)\simeq2.84\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm s$.[§12.22, equations (1979)–(1981): distinction between emission interval and detector arrival interval](../../../../synchrotron-radiation-by-richard-fitzpatrick.md)

**Bunch qualification.** The source says only that the bunches are short, without their longitudinal [length](../../../../length.md) $L_b$. Different [electrons](../../../../electron.md) pass the same emitting tangent at times separated by up to about $L_b/u$, with essentially the same path length from that tangent to the detector. The [bunch-length broadening of synchrotron radiation](../../../../bunch-length-broadening-of-synchrotron-radiation.md) means that the measured bunch envelope is the single-[electron](../../../../electron.md) pulse convolved with the bunch's passage-time profile; its scale is generally at least of order $\max\{L_b/u,r/(c\gamma^3)\}$, with additional detector bandwidth effects. For example, a $1\,\mathrm{mm}$ bunch, very short compared with this ring, gives an approximately $3.3\,\mathrm{ps}$ envelope, much longer than the single-[electron](../../../../electron.md) duration. No unique numerical bunch-envelope width follows from the supplied data. The pulse argument also presumes that the accepted $+x$ direction is tangent to the orbit; a direction far outside the orbital plane receives no comparable intense forward pulse.
