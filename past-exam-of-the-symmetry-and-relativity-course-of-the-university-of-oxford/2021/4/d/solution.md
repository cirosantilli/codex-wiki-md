<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let $K_i$ generate [Lorentz boosts](../../../../lorentz-boost.md) with the same exponential convention as the rotation [generators of rotations](../../../../rotation-generator.md) $J_i$. The [Lie algebra of the Lorentz group](../../../../lie-algebra-of-the-lorentz-group.md) is

$$
[J_i,J_j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}J_k,\qquad
[J_i,K_j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}K_k,\qquad
\boxed{[K_i,K_j]=-i\epsilon_{ijk}J_k.}
$$

Consequently non-collinear [Lorentz boosts](../../../../lorentz-boost.md) do not close among themselves: the product contains a [rotation](../../../../rotation-mathematics.md), the [Wigner rotation](../../../../wigner-rotation.md). For an explicit counterexample, in coordinates $(ct,x,y)$ write $c_a=\cosh a$, $s_a=\sinh a$ and similarly for $b$. Then

$$
B_y(b)B_x(a)=\begin{pmatrix}
c_bc_a&-c_bs_a&-s_b\\
-s_a&c_a&0\\
-s_bc_a&s_bs_a&c_b
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

A pure rotationless [Lorentz boost](../../../../lorentz-boost.md) has a symmetric [matrix](../../../../matrix.md), whereas this product has spatial entries $(xy)=0$ and $(yx)=s_as_b\ne0$ for $a,b\ne0$. It is therefore a concrete product of two pure [Lorentz boosts](../../../../lorentz-boost.md) that is not a pure [Lorentz boost](../../../../lorentz-boost.md). Collinear [Lorentz boosts](../../../../lorentz-boost.md) are the special case that do form a one-parameter [group](../../../../group-split.md), with additive [rapidities](../../../../rapidity.md).

Allowing all spatial directions and closure generates both the $K_i$ and, through their [commutators](../../../../commutator.md), all $J_i$. The smallest connected [group](../../../../group-split.md) containing all these [Lorentz boosts](../../../../lorentz-boost.md) is the [proper orthochronous Lorentz group](../../../../proper-orthochronous-lorentz-group.md)

$$
\boxed{SO^+(1,3)=\{\Lambda:\Lambda^Tg\Lambda=g,\ \det\Lambda=+1,\ \Lambda^0{}_0\geq1\}.}
$$

It contains arbitrary compositions of spatial [rotations](../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) and [Lorentz boosts](../../../../lorentz-boost.md) continuously connected to the identity. It has six continuous parameters. Here “smallest” refers to the subgroup generated by all continuous pure [Lorentz boosts](../../../../lorentz-boost.md), not to an arbitrary collection already including discrete reversals.

The full [Lorentz group](../../../../lorentz-group.md) $O(1,3)$ has four connected components, distinguished by the sign of the [determinant](../../../../determinant.md) and the direction of time. Representatives are the identity, [parity](../../../../parity.md) $P=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, [time reversal](../../../../t-symmetry.md) $\mathsf T=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, and $P\mathsf T=-I$. Thus

$$
O(1,3)=SO^+(1,3)\ \cup\ P SO^+(1,3)\ \cup\ \mathsf T SO^+(1,3)\ \cup\ P\mathsf T SO^+(1,3).
$$

Multiplication by these discrete transformations relates the components algebraically; no continuous path within the [Lorentz group](../../../../lorentz-group.md) joins different components. Translations are not required for closure here; adding them gives the larger [Poincaré group](../../../../poincare-group.md).
