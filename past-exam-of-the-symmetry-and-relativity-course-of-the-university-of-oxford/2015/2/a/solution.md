<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Regard $f$ as a [scalar field](../../../../scalar-field.md) evaluated on a specified [worldline](../../../../world-line.md) $x^\lambda(\tau)$. The ordinary multivariable [chain rule](../../../../chain-rule.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{d}{d\tau}f(x(\tau))
=\frac{dx^\lambda}{d\tau}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^\lambda}
=u^\lambda\partial_\lambda f.}
$$

This is the [directional derivative](../../../../directional-derivative.md) along the [four-velocity](../../../../four-velocity.md). With $x^0=ct$, it reads $df/d\tau=\gamma(\partial_tf+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla f)$. The spatial term is present because the [worldline](../../../../world-line.md) moves through the field; it would be lost by identifying a [total derivative](../../../../total-derivative.md) along the particle with a [partial derivative](../../../../partial-derivative.md) at a fixed spatial position. For a scalar the contracted expression has the same value in all coordinate systems. For a tensor field the analogous [covariant derivative](../../../../covariant-derivative.md) also transports its indices.
