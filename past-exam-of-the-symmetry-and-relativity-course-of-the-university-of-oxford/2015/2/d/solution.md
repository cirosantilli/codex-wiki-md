<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

First note that the supplied [spin](../../../../spin.md) equation preserves $s\cdot u=0$: its electromagnetic bracket has contraction $u_aF^{a\lambda}s_\lambda+(u^2/c^2)F^{\mu\nu}u_\mu s_\nu=0$, and the remaining transport term cancels $s\cdot\dot u$ as in [i](../c/i.md).

For an explicitly electromagnetic acceleration, set $\dot u^a=(q/m)F^{a\lambda}u_\lambda$ and define $C=F^{\mu\nu}u_\mu s_\nu$. Antisymmetry gives $s\cdot\dot u=-(q/m)C$, so the [spin](../../../../spin.md) equation can be written

$$
\dot s^a=\frac qm\left[\frac g2 F^{a\lambda}s_\lambda
+\left(\frac g2-1\right)\frac C{c^2}u^a\right].
$$

Contract with $2s_a$:

$$
\boxed{\frac d{d\tau}(s^as_a)
=\frac{gq}{m}s_aF^{a\lambda}s_\lambda
+\frac{2q}{mc^2}\left(\frac g2-1\right)C(s\cdot u)=0.}
$$

The first term vanishes because a symmetric product of the two [spin](../../../../spin.md) covectors contracts an antisymmetric tensor; the second vanishes by [spin](../../../../spin.md)–velocity orthogonality. In the [instantaneous rest frame](../../../../instantaneous-rest-frame.md) $s^0=0$, so $s^as_a$ is precisely the squared proper spatial-spin magnitude. It remains constant for arbitrary $g$ under this transport, though its direction can precess. In this equation $g$ is the dimensionless [g-factor](../../../../g-factor-physics.md); the dimensional [gyromagnetic ratio](../../../../gyromagnetic-ratio.md) includes $gq/(2m)$.
