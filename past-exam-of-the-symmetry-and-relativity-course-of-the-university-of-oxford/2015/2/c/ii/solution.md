<h1 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take the dot here to mean $d/d\tau$, as in the source. Differentiate $u^\alpha=\gamma(c,\mathbf v)$ and the given [spin](../../../../../spin.md) components. Their contraction simplifies because the terms involving $\dot\gamma$ cancel:

$$
s_\lambda\dot u^\lambda
=-\gamma(\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf v)\dot\gamma
+\gamma\mathbf s\cdot(\dot\gamma\mathbf v+\gamma\dot{\mathbf v})
=\gamma^2\mathbf s\cdot\dot{\mathbf v}.
$$

The spatial part of the transport equation consequently reads

$$
\frac d{d\tau}(\gamma\mathbf s)
=\frac{\gamma^3}{c^2}(\mathbf s\cdot\dot{\mathbf v})\mathbf v.
$$

Use $\dot\gamma/\gamma=\gamma^2\mathbf v\cdot\dot{\mathbf v}/c^2$ and divide by $\gamma$. The result is

$$
\boxed{\dot{\mathbf s}=\frac{\gamma^2}{c^2}
\left[(\mathbf s\cdot\dot{\mathbf v})\mathbf v
-(\mathbf v\cdot\dot{\mathbf v})\mathbf s\right].}
$$

No assumption of acceleration perpendicular to the velocity was made. In particular the second term cannot be dropped for a particle speeding up along a straight line. This $\mathbf s$ is the three-vector in the source's particular [spin four-vector](../../../../../spin-four-vector.md) parametrization; its Euclidean magnitude need not be the invariant proper-spin magnitude.
