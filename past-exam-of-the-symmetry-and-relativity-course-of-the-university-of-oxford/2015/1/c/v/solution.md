<h1 id="1/c/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Using the conventional critical scale rather than guessing a precise Fourier-width coefficient,

$$
\boxed{E_c=\hbar\omega_c=\frac{3\hbar c\gamma^3}{2R}
=1.07\times10^2\ \mathrm{eV},\qquad
\lambda_c=\frac{hc}{E_c}\simeq11.6\ \mathrm{nm}.}
$$

This is extreme ultraviolet and the very soft X-ray boundary, with a broad spectrum extending on either side; it is not a hard, multi-keV X-ray source at the stated large bending radius. The characteristic [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is not a sharp upper cutoff. For orientation, the standard angle-integrated spectral [power](../../../../../power.md) per unit [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) peaks near $0.29E_c\simeq31$ eV, also in the extreme ultraviolet; a particular angular acceptance weights that spectrum differently.

The crucial scale follows from both the $3$ GeV [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and the $561$ m radius. Using only the fact that synchrotron sources can make X-rays, or replacing the bending radius by a much smaller unstated value, would misidentify the characteristic [photon](../../../../../photon.md) energies here. The critical-energy convention and the broadband character are described by the primary radiation model.[photon-spectrum section, equation (209), and Properties of the [Power](../../../../../power.md) Spectra](../../../../../synchrotron-radiation-in-the-geant4-physics-reference-manual-by-geant4-collaboration.md)
