A shorter radiation flash has a broader [Fourier transform](../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md). From [iii](../iii.md), its characteristic angular-frequency bandwidth is

$$
\boxed{\Delta\omega\sim\Delta t_{\rm arr}^{-1}\sim\frac{c}{R}\gamma^3.}
$$

At fixed bending radius this scales as $\gamma^3$ as $v\to c$. The standard synchrotron critical angular [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) makes the same scaling explicit:

$$
\boxed{\omega_c=\frac{3c}{2R}\gamma^3.}
$$

A periodically circulating [electron](../../../../../electron.md) produces harmonics of its revolution [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md). The very large harmonic range, of order $\gamma^3$, forms the familiar effectively continuous broad synchrotron spectrum at ordinary detector resolution. The result concerns the intrinsic spectrum of the radiation, not merely the slow envelope imposed by a long incoherent bunch. Ordinary [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) is $\nu=\omega/(2\pi)$; mixing cycles per second with radians per second would introduce a spurious factor of $2\pi$ into photon-energy estimates.[critical-energy formula and validity discussion, critical harmonic number $3\gamma^3/2$](../../../../../synchrotron-radiation-in-the-geant4-physics-reference-manual-by-geant4-collaboration.md)
