<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For $P^\mu=(E/c,\mathbf p)$, directly contract the supplied vector:

$$
W\cdot P=(\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf p)\frac Ec
-\frac Ec\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf p=0.
$$

Thus $\boxed{U_W\cdot P=0}$ whenever its stated normalization is defined, namely $m_0>0$ and $|\mathbf s|>0$. This is the [Pauli-Lubanski orthogonality](../../../../pauli-lubanski-orthogonality.md) property.

The description as a unit vector, however, needs an additional assumption. From the given components and the mass shell,

$$
W^2=-m_0^2c^2|\mathbf s|^2-|\mathbf s\times\mathbf p|^2,\qquad
\boxed{U_W^2=-1-\frac{|\mathbf s\times\mathbf p|^2}{m_0^2c^2|\mathbf s|^2}.}
$$

It equals minus one only for [spin](../../../../spin.md) parallel to [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md), including the zero-momentum case. A concrete counterexample is $\mathbf s=(s,0,0)$ and $\mathbf p=(0,m_0c,0)$: the proposed normalized vector has square minus two. Orthogonality is still true. A genuine [Pauli-Lubanski pseudovector](../../../../pauli-lubanski-pseudovector.md) constructed by boosting a fixed rest-frame [spin](../../../../spin.md) has invariant norm $-m_0^2c^2|\mathbf s_{\mathrm{rest}}|^2$; the source's generic spatial expression and denominator do not supply that normalization for arbitrary $\mathbf s$.
