<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**The asserted necessity of equal masses is false.** Equal unsigned outgoing angles imply equal longitudinal [momenta](../../../../momentum-split.md) when the transverse [momenta](../../../../momentum-split.md) cancel; they do not imply equal final speeds or [energies](../../../../energy-split.md) for unequal masses.

An explicit counterexample uses $c=1$, masses $M_1=m$ and $M_2=2m$, initial projectile [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) $E_1=2m$, and a stationary target. The initial total [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is $4m$ and [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) is $(\sqrt3m,0,0)$. Choose final [four-momenta](../../../../four-momentum.md)

$$
\boxed{P_1'=m\left(\frac{13}8,\frac{\sqrt3}2,\frac{\sqrt{57}}8,0\right),\qquad
P_2'=m\left(\frac{19}8,\frac{\sqrt3}2,-\frac{\sqrt{57}}8,0\right).}
$$

They sum to the incoming [four-momentum](../../../../four-momentum.md). Their squared norms are respectively $m^2$ and $4m^2$, so the collision is elastic. Both trajectories make the same acute angle with positive $x$:

$$
\boxed{\tan\theta_1=\tan\theta_2=\frac{\sqrt{19}}4,\qquad M_1\ne M_2.}
$$

Thus no proof of the printed claim is possible.

The general [centre-of-mass frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md) construction shows why. Let $p^*$ be the outgoing [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) magnitude there, $\theta^*$ the projectile scattering angle, and $E_1^*,E_2^*$ the two [energies](../../../../energy-split.md). After a boost $\beta_{\mathrm{CM}}$ along $x$, the equal-angle condition for two forward nonzero trajectories is

$$
p^*\cos\theta^*+\beta_{\mathrm{CM}}E_1^*
=-p^*\cos\theta^*+\beta_{\mathrm{CM}}E_2^*.
$$

With $E_1$ the initial projectile [energy](../../../../energy-split.md), its solution is

$$
\boxed{\cos\theta^*=
\frac{M_2^2-M_1^2}{2M_2(E_1+M_2)}\quad(c=1),}
$$

provided the right side lies between minus and plus one and both final longitudinal [momenta](../../../../momentum-split.md) are positive. Equal masses are one allowed case, giving $\theta^*=\pi/2$; they are not necessary. For the counterexample the right side is $3/16$. Even equal masses do not force equal angles for every scattering direction: the symmetric outgoing configuration is an additional choice.
