<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Along a massive particle's [world line](../../../../world-line.md), [proper time](../../../../proper-time.md) satisfies $d\tau=dt\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$. A [pure force](../../../../pure-force.md) preserves its [rest mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md). Differentiating $P=mU$ and $U^2=c^2$ gives

$$
F\cdot U=\frac{dm}{d\tau}U^2+m\frac{dU}{d\tau}\cdot U
=c^2\frac{dm}{d\tau},
$$

so $\boxed{F\cdot U=0}$ for a [pure force](../../../../pure-force.md). This is a restriction on rest-mass transfer, not a statement that its work in every frame vanishes.

Write $\mathbf a=d\mathbf v/dt$. Since $\dot\gamma=\gamma^3\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a/c^2$, the [four-acceleration](../../../../four-acceleration.md) is

$$
A^\mu=\left(\frac{\gamma^4\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a}{c},\;
\gamma^2\mathbf a+\frac{\gamma^4\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a}{c^2}\mathbf v\right).
$$

If $\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a=0$, this reduces to $A=(0,\gamma^2\mathbf a)$, whence

$$
\boxed{A^\mu A_\mu=-\gamma^4a^2,\qquad
\alpha^2\equiv-A^2=\gamma^4a^2.}
$$

Here $\alpha$ is the [proper acceleration](../../../../proper-acceleration.md). The positive expression printed in the source is $A^2$ in the opposite metric signature, or the squared [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) magnitude in ours. For example, at rest with nonzero [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md), $A=(0,\mathbf a)$ has $A^2=-a^2$ in our convention. Thus one must not combine the printed positive sign with a mostly-minus norm.
