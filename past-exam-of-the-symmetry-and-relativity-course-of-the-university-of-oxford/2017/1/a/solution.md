<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a massive particle on a timelike [world line](../../../../world-line.md), the [four-velocity](../../../../four-velocity.md) is $U^\mu=dX^\mu/d\tau=\gamma(c,\mathbf v)$, with $U^2=c^2$. Its [four-momentum](../../../../four-momentum.md) is $P^\mu=mU^\mu=(E/c,\mathbf p)$ and its [four-force](../../../../four-force.md) is $F^\mu=dP^\mu/d\tau$. Here $\tau$ is [proper time](../../../../proper-time.md). A massless trajectory does not admit this massive-particle proper-time parametrization.

**The asserted classification of arbitrary orthogonal vectors is false.** With the chosen [Minkowski metric](../../../../minkowski-metric.md), $D=(0,1,0,0)$ and $G=(0,0,1,0)$ are nonzero, orthogonal and both [spacelike vectors](../../../../spacelike-vector.md). Also $D=(1,1,0,0)$ and $G=2D$ are orthogonal [null vectors](../../../../null-vector.md). The valid statement is that a nonzero vector orthogonal to a [timelike vector](../../../../timelike-vector.md) is spacelike. To prove it, choose the rest frame of the [timelike vector](../../../../timelike-vector.md), $D=(d,0,0,0)$; then $D\cdot G=dG^0=0$ forces $G^0=0$, so $G^2=-|\mathbf G|^2<0$.

The [relativistic energy-momentum relation](../../../../energy-momentum-relation.md) gives

$$
P\cdot P=\frac{E^2}{c^2}-|\mathbf p|^2=m^2c^2.
$$

Therefore $\boxed{P^2=0\ \Longrightarrow\ m=0}$. A [photon](../../../../photon.md) is an example: $E=c|\mathbf p|$. A collection of [photons](../../../../photon.md) need not itself have zero [invariant mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md).
