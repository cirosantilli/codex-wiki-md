<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

With $c=1$, the total [four-momentum](../../../../four-momentum.md) is $P_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_iP_i$. A sum of [Lorentz four-vectors](../../../../four-vector.md) transforms as a four-vector, so

$$
\boxed{s^2=P_{\mathrm{tot}}\cdot P_{\mathrm{tot}}
=\left(\sum_iW_i\right)^2-\left|\sum_i\mathbf p_i\right|^2}
$$

is a [Lorentz invariant](../../../../lorentz-invariance.md). Its value is the squared [invariant mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md) of the complete system, irrespective of whether its constituents interact. Isolation makes the total [four-momentum](../../../../four-momentum.md) constant in time.

For a [photon gas](../../../../photon-gas.md), write $\mathbf p_i=W_i\mathbf n_i$ with $|\mathbf n_i|=1$. Its individual null norms do not make the total norm vanish:

$$
\boxed{s^2=2\sum_{i<j}W_iW_j(1-\mathbf n_i\cdot\mathbf n_j)\geq0.}
$$

For an isotropic gas in its [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) rest frame, $\sum_i\mathbf p_i=0$ and $s^2=W_{\mathrm{tot}}^2$. Zero is possible only when all nonzero [photons](../../../../photon.md) propagate in the same direction. Two equal-energy [photons](../../../../photon.md) at right angles, for example, give $s^2=2W^2$, rather than zero. Without an angular distribution, the pairwise expression is the general answer; isotropy is an additional assumption.
