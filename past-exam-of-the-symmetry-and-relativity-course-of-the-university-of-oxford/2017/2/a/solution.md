<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a vacuum [electromagnetic wave](../../../../electromagnetic-wave-split.md), the [four-wavevector](../../../../four-wavevector.md) is $K^\mu=(\omega/c,\mathbf k)$ with $|\mathbf k|=\omega/c$ and $K^2=0$. Its phase can be written

$$
\varphi=K_\mu X^\mu+\varphi_0=\omega t-\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x+\varphi_0.
$$

Because $K$ and $X$ transform as [Lorentz four-vectors](../../../../four-vector.md), their contraction is a [Lorentz scalar](../../../../lorentz-scalar.md). This proves phase invariance, with the same phase convention in both frames.

For a hypothetical free emission $e\to e+\gamma$, [four-momentum conservation](../../../../four-momentum-conservation.md) gives $P=P'+Q$. The initial and final [electrons](../../../../electron.md) have $P^2=P'^2=m_e^2c^2$, and $Q^2=0$. Squaring gives $P'\cdot Q=0$. In the final [electron](../../../../electron.md)'s rest frame, however, $P'\cdot Q=m_eE_\gamma>0$ for a nonzero future-directed [photon](../../../../photon.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{an isolated on-shell electron cannot emit a real photon in vacuum}.}
$$

An external field, another body or a medium can exchange [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) and change this conclusion; those are different systems.
