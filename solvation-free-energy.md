# Solvation free energy

↑ **Parent:** [Solvation](solvation.md)

For transfer of the same species $X$ from a gaseous reference state into a [solvent](solvent.md) $S$, define the molar [solvation free energy](solvation-free-energy.md) by

$$
X(g)\longrightarrow X(S),\qquad
\Delta G_{\mathrm{solv}}^{\circ}(S)=\mu_X^{\circ}(S)-\mu_X^{\circ}(g).
$$

Here $\mu^{\circ}$ is a standard [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md). Negative [solvation free energy](solvation-free-energy.md) means that transfer into the stated [solvent](solvent.md) lowers the [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md) relative to the stated gaseous reference. Include [solute](solute.md)-[solvent](solvent.md) interactions, making room in the [solvent](solvent.md), and its reorganization; counting only a favorable pair interaction omits part of the transfer.[solvation energy](iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/solvation-energy.md)

State the [temperature](temperature-split.md), gaseous reference [pressure](pressure-split.md), dissolved reference [concentration](concentration.md) or other [standard state](standard-state.md) convention, and species identity. Reference-state changes alter the quoted [solvation free energy](solvation-free-energy.md). For example, with an ideal dilute [thermodynamic activity](thermodynamic-activity.md) $a=c/c^{\circ}$, replacing $c^{\circ}$ by $\alpha c^{\circ}$ while preserving the physical [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) requires $\mu^{\circ}$ to increase by $RT\ln\alpha$. Thus the corresponding [solvation free energy](solvation-free-energy.md) increases by $RT\ln\alpha$ if the gaseous reference stays fixed. This follows directly from $\mu=\mu^{\circ}+RT\ln a$, with $R$ the [molar gas constant](molar-gas-constant.md).
