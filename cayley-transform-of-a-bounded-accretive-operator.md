# Cayley transform of a bounded accretive operator

↑ **Parent:** [Accretive operator](accretive-operator.md)

For a bounded [accretive operator](accretive-operator.md), $I+T$ is invertible and the transform satisfies $\|S\|\leq1$. The identity $\|(I+T)x\|^2-\|(I-T)x\|^2=4\operatorname{Re}\langle Tx,x\rangle$ proves contraction after the substitution $z=(I+T)x$. If the real part vanishes for every $x$, this identity proves norm preservation. Both $T$ and $-T$ are then accretive, making both $I+T$ and $I-T$ invertible; hence $S$ is onto and is a [unitary operator](unitary-operator.md).
