↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

Reusable [protein domains](../../../../../../protein-domain.md) make evolution combinatorial: a new domain combination can join an existing catalytic activity to a binding, localization or regulatory module without inventing every component anew. [Gene duplication](../../../../../../gene-duplication.md) allows one copy to retain an established role while another diverges; [domain shuffling](../../../../../../domain-shuffling.md) and fusion can create new combinations. Connecting domains can also increase effective local [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) of interacting sites or enable [allostery](../../../../../../allostery.md).

These are potential advantages, not guarantees that every fusion is beneficial: domains must still fold and work together. The domain-level analysis behind this example found extensive reuse and new combinations of recognized structural units.[Domain classification and inter-domain packing relationships](../../../../../../exploring-structural-diversity-across-the-protein-universe-with-the-encyclopedia-of-domains-by-andy-m-lau-and-others.md)
