<h4 id="section-i/1/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-i/1)

**A convincing [splicing code](../../biology.md#splicing-code) must predict [alternative splicing](../../biology.md#alternative-splicing) from both the [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) sequence and the cellular context, then survive causal perturbation.** A list of [exons](../../biology.md#exon) specific to [neurons](../../neuroscience.md#neuron) or enriched sequence motifs is only a starting point. In the [central nervous system](../../neuroscience.md#central-nervous-system), distinguish [neurons](../../neuroscience.md#neuron) from [glial cells](../../neuroscience.md#glial-cell), individual [neuron](../../neuroscience.md#neuron) classes, anatomical regions, developmental stages and activity states. Otherwise a bulk difference can arise because two samples contain different proportions of [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), even when no individual [cell type](../../biological-cell.md#cell-type) changes its [RNA splicing](../../biology.md#rna-splicing).

Start with replicated, well-matched samples from several [central nervous system](../../neuroscience.md#central-nervous-system) regions and non-neural comparison [biological tissues](../../biology.md#tissue-biology), recording donor, age, sex, preparation batch and [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) integrity. Obtain deep, stranded [RNA sequencing](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-sequencing) with reads spanning [splice junctions](../../biology.md#splice-junction); combine this with [long-read RNA sequencing](../../molecular-genetics.md#long-read-rna-sequencing) to establish complete [transcript isoforms](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcript-isoform). Use purified [cell types](../../biological-cell.md#cell-type) or sufficiently deep, full-length [single-cell RNA sequencing](../../molecular-genetics.md#single-cell-rna-sequencing) to separate cellular composition from regulation. Routine assays that sequence only the $3'$ end cannot measure most internal [exons](../../biology.md#exon); [single-nucleus RNA sequencing](../../molecular-genetics.md#single-nucleus-rna-sequencing) additionally enriches incompletely processed [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid), so retained [introns](../../biology.md#intron) require careful interpretation. A matched bulk or purified-cell dataset supplies coverage that sparse individual-cell observations lack.

Align reads with a [splice-aware sequence alignment](../../molecular-genetics.md#splice-aware-sequence-alignment) method and quantify cassette [exons](../../biology.md#exon), alternative [splice sites](../../biology.md#splice-site), mutually exclusive [exons](../../biology.md#exon) and retained [introns](../../biology.md#intron). Search explicitly for [microexons](../../biology.md#microexon), whose short length can defeat standard alignment filters. For a cassette [exon](../../biology.md#exon), summarize inclusion by [percent spliced in](../../biology.md#percent-spliced-in), written as a fraction

$$
\psi=\frac{I}{I+S},
$$

where $I$ and $S$ estimate abundances of included and skipped molecules after correcting the different informative read opportunities. Adding the two inclusion-junction counts and comparing their sum directly with a single skipping-junction count would bias this estimate. Report uncertainty and coverage, and model between-donor variation; select changes by both effect size and [false discovery rate](../../statistical-hypothesis-test.md#false-discovery-rate). Validate selected [splice junctions](../../biology.md#splice-junction) using [RT-PCR](../../molecular-genetics.md#reverse-transcription-polymerase-chain-reaction) and [Sanger sequencing](../../molecular-genetics.md#sanger-sequencing). Distinguish genuine altered processing from differential destruction of [transcript isoforms](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcript-isoform) through [nonsense-mediated decay](../../molecular-genetics.md#nonsense-mediated-decay), using newly synthesized or nuclear [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) and a short, controlled perturbation of that pathway where needed.

Build a feature set around each regulated [exon](../../biology.md#exon): strengths of the two [splice sites](../../biology.md#splice-site), [RNA splicing branch point](../../biology.md#rna-splicing-branch-point) and [polypyrimidine tract](../../biology.md#polypyrimidine-tract); [exon](../../biology.md#exon) and [intron](../../biology.md#intron) lengths; position-specific [cis-regulatory elements](../../molecular-genetics.md#cis-regulatory-element); predicted or measured [RNA secondary structure](../../polymer.md#rna-secondary-structure); and evolutionary conservation. Include the abundance, [protein](../../protein.md) level, nuclear location and activity of candidate [RNA-binding proteins](../../protein.md#rna-binding-protein), rather than treating their [messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#messenger-rna) abundance as a perfect proxy. A [splicing code](../../biology.md#splicing-code) can combine many weak sequence features: the original tissue-prediction study demonstrated the value of combinations rather than one universal neural motif.[Abstract and Results: combinations of RNA features predicting tissue-dependent exon inclusion](../../biology-bibliography.md#deciphering-the-splicing-code-by-yoseph-barash-and-others)

Fit an interpretable [statistical model](../../statistical-model.md) first, allowing interactions between motif position and [RNA-binding protein](../../protein.md#rna-binding-protein) abundance. Compare it with more flexible [machine learning](../../computer-science.md#machine-learning), using [cross-validation](../../statistical-learning.md#cross-validation) that holds out whole [genes](../../genetics.md#gene), donors and, for a strong transfer test, a [cell type](../../biological-cell.md#cell-type). Randomly separating junctions from the same [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) between training and testing risks information leakage. Compare against sequence-only, context-only and expression-matched baseline predictors. Successful prediction of held-out [percent spliced in](../../biology.md#percent-spliced-in) is more informative than classifying the training set. In particular, an enriched motif can reflect [nucleotide](../../molecule.md#nucleotide) composition, [intron](../../biology.md#intron) length or another factor with similar specificity; enrichment alone does not establish binding or function.

Map candidate [RNA-binding protein](../../protein.md#rna-binding-protein) occupancy with [crosslinking and immunoprecipitation](../../biology.md#crosslinking-and-immunoprecipitation) in the relevant preparations of [neurons](../../neuroscience.md#neuron). Include input material, a no-crosslink control where appropriate, a validated [antibody](../../protein.md#antibody), and biological replication; account for transcript abundance and crosslinking preferences. Integrate binding positions with [RNA sequencing](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-sequencing) after acute depletion. [NOVA proteins](../../protein.md#nova-protein) give a concrete precedent: binding to YCAY clusters has position-dependent effects, with exonic binding able to inhibit inclusion and downstream intronic binding able to promote it. Here Y denotes a pyrimidine [nucleobase](../../molecule.md#nucleotide-base). Thus the same motif can support opposite predictions at different positions.[Abstract; Figures 1–4 and 6: positional RNA map and effects on spliceosome assembly](../../biology-bibliography.md#an-rna-map-predicting-nova-dependent-splicing-regulation-by-jernej-ule-and-others) Test other neural regulators, such as [RBFOX proteins](../../protein.md#rbfox-protein) and [PTBP proteins](../../protein.md#ptbp-protein), as potentially overlapping or antagonistic inputs rather than assuming every neural [exon](../../biology.md#exon) follows the [NOVA protein](../../protein.md#nova-protein) rule.

Finally, make the predictions experimentally vulnerable. For representative [exons](../../biology.md#exon), mutate or move individual [cis-regulatory elements](../../molecular-genetics.md#cis-regulatory-element) in a [minigene splicing reporter](../../biology.md#minigene-splicing-reporter), preserving [splice sites](../../biology.md#splice-site) and controlling total reporter abundance. Test gain and loss of inclusion in both neural and non-neural [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md). Edit the endogenous element because a short [minigene splicing reporter](../../biology.md#minigene-splicing-reporter) can omit distant regulation within [introns](../../biology.md#intron) or [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin) context. Acutely deplete the proposed [RNA-binding protein](../../protein.md#rna-binding-protein) and rescue with physiological amounts of normal [protein](../../protein.md) or an RNA-binding-defective [mutant](../../genetics.md#mutant). Combined depletion tests redundancy; motif mutation plus factor depletion tests whether both act through the same element. A particularly strong result is the predicted reversal after moving a binding site from a repressive to an activating position. Measure [cell differentiation](../../organism.md#cellular-differentiation) and viability so that a change in cellular identity is not mistaken for direct [RNA splicing](../../biology.md#rna-splicing) regulation. **The resulting code is a tested, context-dependent set of regulatory rules, with an explicit domain of validity.**

<h4 id="section-i/2/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-i/2)

**Separate three causal steps: recognition of the modified [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid), occupancy of the target [messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#messenger-rna), and reduction of its degradation rate.** Call the newly identified [RNA-binding protein](../../protein.md#rna-binding-protein) R. Its recovery in a screen need not mean it contacts the modified [ribonucleotide](../../molecule.md#ribonucleotide) directly; it could recognize a modification-dependent structure or arrive through another [protein](../../protein.md). Likewise, more target [messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#messenger-rna) could reflect increased [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) rather than greater stability.

First reproduce the interaction using endogenous R and the endogenous target. Determine R's [protein domains](../../protein.md#protein-domain), expression and subcellular distribution, then use reciprocal [RNA immunoprecipitation](../../biology.md#rna-immunoprecipitation) and [crosslinking and immunoprecipitation](../../biology.md#crosslinking-and-immunoprecipitation) to locate the occupied region. Normalize enrichment to input target abundance and R recovery, and include unrelated [RNAs](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid), a non-specific [antibody](../../protein.md#antibody) and cells lacking R. Crosslink-based occupancy supports close contact, whereas recovery without crosslinking can preserve indirect complexes. Determine whether the relevant modification actually occurs near the binding site: [mass spectrometry](../../chemistry.md#mass-spectrometry) of purified target fragments can establish chemical identity, supplemented by a modification-specific mapping assay with known modified and unmodified standards. Bulk [mass spectrometry](../../chemistry.md#mass-spectrometry) of all cellular [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) cannot assign a modification to this particular transcript or position. Do not assume the modification is [N6-methyladenosine](../../molecule.md#n6-methyladenosine) merely because it is familiar.

Prepare short synthetic [RNAs](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) identical in [nucleotide sequence](../../polymer.md#nucleic-acid-sequence), length, ends and labelling except for the single modified [ribonucleotide](../../molecule.md#ribonucleotide). Measure binding of purified R over a concentration range by [electrophoretic mobility shift assay](../../biology.md#electrophoretic-mobility-shift-assay) or [fluorescence anisotropy](../../fluorescence.md#fluorescence-anisotropy), with competition by unlabelled cognate and unrelated [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid). The [dissociation constant](../../chemical-potential.md#dissociation-constant) distinguishes a quantitative preference from a binary pull-down impression. Verify that R is folded and sufficiently pure; include an RNA-binding-defective R [mutant](../../genetics.md#mutant), several surrounding sequences and different modification positions. Modification-specific recovery from a cellular extract can disappear with purified R, revealing a missing partner rather than disproving the original screen.

Distinguish chemical recognition from structural recognition. Compare [RNA secondary structure](../../polymer.md#rna-secondary-structure) with and without the modification by [RNA structure probing](../../biology.md#rna-structure-probing). Then disrupt the stem without changing the binding motif, or restore pairing through compensatory [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation). If an unmodified but constitutively exposed motif binds R, and restoring occlusion suppresses binding even when the modification remains, an [RNA modification switch](../../polymer.md#rna-modification-switch) is plausible. Direct recognition is supported when modification discrimination persists after accessibility is matched and is lost upon a specific R binding-pocket [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation). Structural work can identify the contacts, but binding and structure controls remain essential. Modification-dependent exposure of a binding motif has an experimental precedent in the [N6-methyladenosine](../../molecule.md#n6-methyladenosine)-dependent [RNA modification switch](../../polymer.md#rna-modification-switch) for [HNRNPC](../../protein.md#hnrnpc).[Abstract; Figures 1–3: structural accessibility, binding and methylation dependence](../../biology-bibliography.md#n6-methyladenosine-dependent-rna-structural-switches-regulate-rna-protein-interactions-by-nian-liu-and-others)

Establish stability by acute depletion of R, followed by a [metabolic RNA labelling](../../biology.md#metabolic-rna-labelling) pulse and chase. Measure loss of labelled target molecules using appropriate spike-ins and a time course spanning the expected [half-life](../../analysis.md#half-life), while independently estimating newly synthesized target [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid). [SLAM-seq](../../molecular-genetics.md#slam-seq) is one possible implementation: chemical treatment makes incorporated [4-thiouridine](../../molecule.md#4-thiouridine) detectable through characteristic sequencing changes.[Abstract: metabolic RNA labelling and sequencing-based measurement of expression dynamics](../../biology-bibliography.md#thiol-linked-alkylation-of-rna-to-assess-expression-dynamics-by-veronika-a-herzog-and-others) Include unlabelled controls, check label toxicity and correct for label reutilization or incomplete chase. When reporting molecules per [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md), separate dilution through increasing cell number from chemical degradation. A [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) inhibitor time course provides an orthogonal check, but broad inhibition itself can change decay. Under a constant first-order degradation model, labelled molecules obey

$$
L(t)=L(0)e^{-k_{\mathrm d}t},\qquad t_{1/2}=\frac{\ln 2}{k_{\mathrm d}}.
$$

A shorter [half-life](../../analysis.md#half-life) after R depletion, reversed by normal R but not its RNA-binding-defective [mutant](../../genetics.md#mutant), establishes the proposed effect. Allow a more complex fit if the [exponential decay](../../analysis.md#exponential-decay) model is inconsistent with the measured time course. At steady state, abundance is synthesis rate divided by degradation rate, so abundance alone cannot identify which rate changed.

Use a controlled target [reporter gene](../../molecular-genetics.md#reporter-gene) carrying the occupied region, then remove or mutate that region and measure reporter [messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#messenger-rna) decay. Preserve [polyadenylation](../../molecular-genetics.md#polyadenylation), [RNA splicing](../../biology.md#rna-splicing) and coding potential where possible, and distinguish site loss from altered [RNA secondary structure](../../polymer.md#rna-secondary-structure). Manipulate the relevant modification-producing [enzyme](../../enzyme.md) acutely, rescuing with normal or catalytically inactive [enzyme](../../enzyme.md); verify target-site modification after the manipulation. Cross the two perturbations: if R stabilizes the target specifically through the modification, removing the modification should reduce R occupancy and eliminate or substantially weaken the extra effect of removing R. This [epistasis](../../genetics.md#epistasis) supports a shared pathway, but floor effects or redundant sites can obscure it. A site-specific chemically modified reporter helps separate local recognition from the many indirect consequences of removing a global modification [enzyme](../../enzyme.md).

Identify the affected decay step using [poly(A) tail](../../polymer.md#poly-a-tail) measurements, cap-state assays and mapping of decay intermediates. Test whether R excludes a [microRNA](../../polymer.md#microrna)-loaded [Argonaute](../../protein.md#argonaute) complex or a decay-promoting [RNA-binding protein](../../protein.md#rna-binding-protein), blocks an [endonuclease](../../enzyme.md#endonuclease), inhibits [deadenylation](../../polymer.md#deadenylation) or [decapping](../../polymer.md#decapping), or recruits a protective partner such as [poly(A)-binding protein](../../protein.md#poly-a-binding-protein). Use [protein](../../protein.md) interaction analysis with and without [ribonuclease](../../enzyme.md#ribonuclease) to distinguish RNA-bridged association from a more direct interaction. R [mutants](../../genetics.md#mutant) that retain target binding but lose a partner interaction separate recruitment from recognition. Depleting the implicated decay factor should suppress the destabilization caused by R loss; purified-system decay assays can test whether R alone physically protects the substrate.

Measure [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology) and subcellular location in parallel, using [ribosome profiling](../../biology.md#ribosome-profiling) or a controlled [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology) reporter and [single-molecule fluorescence in situ hybridization](../../biology.md#single-molecule-fluorescence-in-situ-hybridization). R might stabilize the target indirectly by altering [ribosome](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome) loading or compartment access. Inhibiting [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology) can itself alter decay, and colocalization in a granule does not establish protection. The reported stabilizing action of [IGF2BP proteins](../../protein.md#igf2bp-protein) on modified targets provides a candidate mechanism to compare with, rather than an identity to assign to R.[Abstract and Results: modification-associated target recognition, stability and translation](../../biology-bibliography.md#recognition-of-rna-n6-methyladenosine-by-igf2bp-proteins-enhances-mrna-stability-and-translation-by-huilin-huang-and-others) **The strongest mechanism links a verified target-site modification to R binding, a defined protective activity and a rescued change in target [half-life](../../analysis.md#half-life).**

<h4 id="section-ii/3/solution">Solution</h4>

**First establish whether X changes where [RNA polymerase II](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-polymerase-ii) initiates, rather than merely changing which initiated [RNAs](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) survive.** A dominant [transcription start site](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-start-site) can shift without becoming less precise, and a population can appear broader because the proportions of two pre-existing start sites change. Treat altered position, within-promoter dispersion and switching between distinct [gene promoters](../../molecular-genetics.md#promoter-genetics) as different phenotypes.

Use a dose and short time course of X with vehicle, biological replicates, washout and viability measurements. Map capped $5'$ ends by [CAGE](../../molecular-genetics.md#cage) and independently map newly initiated [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) with [GRO-cap](../../molecular-genetics.md#gro-cap) or another suitably validated nascent-start assay. [GRO-cap](../../molecular-genetics.md#gro-cap) has been used to characterize initiation regions in mammalian [gene promoters](../../molecular-genetics.md#promoter-genetics) and [enhancers](../../molecular-genetics.md#enhancer).[Abstract and Methods: GRO-cap mapping of transcription initiation](../../biology-bibliography.md#analysis-of-nascent-rna-identifies-a-unified-architecture-of-initiation-regions-at-mammalian-promoters-and-enhancers-by-leighton-j-core-and-others) Confirm selected $5'$ ends with an independent targeted method that distinguishes capped starts from processed [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) ends. Use external standards and control cap recovery; if X inhibits capping, a capped-end assay can lose genuine starts. Measure total nascent output separately from the start-position distribution, and compare libraries at matched effective coverage because low counts can make a broad [gene promoter](../../molecular-genetics.md#promoter-genetics) appear artificially focused.

Within a fixed [gene promoter](../../molecular-genetics.md#promoter-genetics) window, let $p_i$ be the fraction of initiation events at coordinate $x_i$. Quantify dominant position, central width and, if useful, [Shannon entropy](../../information-entropy.md)

$$
\mu=\sum_i p_i x_i,\qquad \sigma^2=\sum_i p_i(x_i-\mu)^2,\qquad H=-\sum_i p_i\log p_i.
$$

A one-base shift of a single occupied start changes $\mu$ while leaving $\sigma^2=H=0$; splitting equally between two positions increases dispersion and gives $H=\ln 2$. Compare complete distributions with sampling uncertainty rather than interpreting one summary alone. Analyze separate [gene promoters](../../molecular-genetics.md#promoter-genetics) independently, stratifying by sequence architecture and baseline output. If only mature [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) changes while nascent initiation remains fixed, investigate differential decay, capping, processing or selective [transcript isoform](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcript-isoform) stabilization before claiming altered initiation.

One mechanism is altered recognition or placement of the [preinitiation complex](../../molecular-genetics.md#preinitiation-complex). [TFIID](../../molecular-genetics.md#tfiid), including [TATA-binding protein](../../molecular-genetics.md#tata-binding-protein) and associated subunits, and [TFIIB](../../molecular-genetics.md#tfiib) help position [RNA polymerase II](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-polymerase-ii) relative to the [core promoter](../../molecular-genetics.md#core-promoter). X could disturb the [TATA box](../../molecular-genetics.md#tata-box), [initiator element](../../molecular-genetics.md#initiator-element) recognition, factor association or their geometry. Precise positioning of the human [initiator element](../../molecular-genetics.md#initiator-element) at focused starts makes sequence-dependent sensitivity a useful test.[Abstract and Results: focused promoters and initiator positioning](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-human-initiator-is-a-distinct-and-abundant-element-that-is-precisely-positioned-in-focused-core-promoters-by-long-vo-ngoc-and-others) Map factor occupancy, compare X-sensitive and insensitive [core promoters](../../molecular-genetics.md#core-promoter), and use matched reporter constructs differing only in an [initiator element](../../molecular-genetics.md#initiator-element), [TATA box](../../molecular-genetics.md#tata-box) or their spacing. A reporter must have its $5'$ ends mapped: light output alone cannot reveal an altered [transcription start site](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-start-site). Reconstitute initiation on naked [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) with purified factors and add X. Reproduction of the start redistribution without [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin) would support a direct effect on the initiation machinery.

A second mechanism is altered [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) opening, template positioning or initial [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) synthesis. X might affect the [XPB](../../molecular-genetics.md#xpb) translocase within [TFIIH](../../molecular-genetics.md#tfiih), [RNA polymerase II](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-polymerase-ii) active-site function, or [TFIIB](../../molecular-genetics.md#tfiib)-dependent selection of the first templating bases. Measure open-complex formation by an appropriate [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) accessibility or chemical-reactivity assay, initial short [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) products and precise starts under controlled [nucleoside triphosphate](../../molecule.md#nucleoside-triphosphate) concentrations. A pre-opened [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) template can help distinguish a defect in opening from one that persists after opening. [triptolide](../../enzyme.md#triptolide) supplies a biochemical precedent for a small molecule targeting [XPB](../../molecular-genetics.md#xpb), but does not establish that X shares that target or that every [XPB](../../molecular-genetics.md#xpb) inhibitor broadens starts.[Abstract and biochemical target-identification experiments](../../biology-bibliography.md#xpb-a-subunit-of-tfiih-is-a-target-of-the-natural-product-triptolide-by-denis-v-titov-and-others) Mammalian initiation should be tested directly; extensive scanning rules established in a different organism cannot simply be assumed to apply.

A third mechanism is a change in the [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin) landscape or [DNA supercoiling](../../polymer.md#dna-supercoiling). Altered [nucleosome](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) positioning could expose cryptic [gene promoters](../../molecular-genetics.md#promoter-genetics), while changed [histone acetylation](../../molecular-genetics.md#histone-acetylation) or [chromatin remodelling](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin-remodelling) could broaden the region accessible to the [preinitiation complex](../../molecular-genetics.md#preinitiation-complex). Map [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin) accessibility, [nucleosomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) and relevant [histone](../../protein.md#histone) modifications before and soon after X treatment. Compare naked and nucleosomal [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) templates carrying the same [core promoter](../../molecular-genetics.md#core-promoter); mutate the proposed [chromatin remodelling](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin-remodelling) factor or use acute depletion to test whether it is required. For a proposed [topoisomerase](../../enzyme.md#topoisomerase) effect, compare relaxed and supercoiled templates and measure [DNA supercoiling](../../polymer.md#dna-supercoiling) directly. Occupancy changes that follow initiation changes can be consequences, so temporal order matters.

A fourth route is indirect alteration of factor supply or activity: X might change [nucleoside triphosphate](../../molecule.md#nucleoside-triphosphate) pools, [phosphorylation](../../chemical-reaction.md#phosphorylation) of initiation machinery, or the abundance of a limiting [transcription factor](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-factor). Measure candidate metabolites and [protein](../../protein.md) modifications, test rapid versus delayed onset, and restore the proposed limiting input. Altered promoter-proximal pausing can change the recovery of nascent products without moving the original start; compare initiation-specific maps with downstream engaged-[RNA polymerase II](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-polymerase-ii) profiles. A change that disappears after controlling [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) lifetime or pausing is not evidence for loss of start-site selection.

Finally, identify the direct molecular target through binding or engagement assays and a resistant [mutant](../../genetics.md#mutant) that retains normal baseline function. Require the resistant version to rescue the start-position phenotype at an X concentration that affects the normal version; overexpression rescue alone can reflect sequestration of the compound. An acute genetic perturbation should phenocopy X, and purified biochemical assays should reproduce the relevant effect. **Convergent evidence from nascent starts, controlled templates and target-specific rescue distinguishes direct selection defects from secondary changes in [gene expression](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-expression).**

<h4 id="section-ii/4/solution">Solution</h4>

**Through [heterochromatin spreading](../../molecular-genetics.md#heterochromatin-spreading), a locally specified silent site becomes a larger protective [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin) domain.** The essential distinction is between nucleation, which identifies where repression should begin, and propagation, which extends repression beyond that initial recognition site. [H3K9 methylation](../../molecular-genetics.md#h3k9-methylation) provides a particularly clear example; its mechanism should not be equated with every kind of repressive [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin).

At a nucleation site, a sequence-specific factor or a complementary small [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) recruits a [histone methyltransferase](../../enzyme.md#histone-methyltransferase). Modification of nearby [histone H3](../../protein.md#histone-h3) at lysine 9 creates binding sites for reader [proteins](../../protein.md), including [HP1 proteins](../../protein.md#hp1-protein) or the fission-yeast counterpart [Swi6](../../protein.md#swi6). A reader can recruit a writer to additional [nucleosomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome); in [Schizosaccharomyces pombe](../../organism.md#schizosaccharomyces-pombe), [Clr4](../../enzyme.md#clr4) itself combines [H3K9 methylation](../../molecular-genetics.md#h3k9-methylation) activity with a methyl-mark-binding [chromodomain](../../protein.md#chromodomain). The resulting [positive feedback](../../signal-transduction.md#positive-feedback) allows an established patch to favor modification of surrounding [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin).[Discussion: Clr4 chromodomain requirement and reader–writer mechanism](../../biology-bibliography.md#epigenetic-inheritance-uncoupled-from-sequence-specific-recruitment-by-kaushik-ragunathan-and-others) Recruitment of [histone deacetylases](../../enzyme.md#histone-deacetylase) and other repressive factors further reduces accessibility to [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) machinery. The causal scheme is

$$
\text{target recognition}\longrightarrow\text{local mark}\longrightarrow\text{reader recruitment}\longrightarrow\text{additional local marking}.
$$

The last two steps can repeat. This is an interaction network operating on dynamic [nucleosomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome), rather than a mark physically crawling along [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid).

Propagation can occur through nearby contacts along the [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) and contacts created by three-dimensional folding. Consequently, a domain need not advance as a perfectly uniform front. At the engineered mammalian locus studied using inducible [HP1](../../protein.md#hp1-protein) recruitment, [H3K9 methylation](../../molecular-genetics.md#h3k9-methylation) and silencing extended beyond the recruitment site, while persistence depended on cellular context and additional stabilizing mechanisms.[Abstract and Results: inducible HP1 recruitment, domain propagation and context-dependent memory](../../biology-bibliography.md#dynamics-and-memory-of-heterochromatin-in-living-cells-by-nathaniel-a-hathaway-and-others) This separates experimentally inducing a domain from merely finding correlated marks near an already silent [gene](../../genetics.md#gene).

Spreading must be limited. Elements such as [chromatin boundaries](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin-boundary), active [gene promoters](../../molecular-genetics.md#promoter-genetics), [histone acetylation](../../molecular-genetics.md#histone-acetylation), loss of modified [histones](../../protein.md#histone) through turnover and opposing [histone demethylases](../../enzyme.md#histone-demethylase) can interrupt the reinforcing cycle. A boundary can prevent access, recruit an antagonistic activity or maintain an unfavorable local substrate. Not every domain therefore requires a single physical barrier; propagation can also stop where mark deposition balances removal. During [DNA replication](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-replication), parental modified [histones](../../protein.md#histone) are diluted among new ones. Reader–writer feedback can restore a local state, but retention of a mark through one division does not establish indefinite [epigenetic inheritance](../../genetics.md#epigenetic-inheritance). Recurrent nucleation may still be needed. Uncontrolled spreading risks silencing useful neighboring [genes](../../genetics.md#gene), so genome defense requires both recognition specificity and restraint.

A well-defined targeting mechanism comes from [RNA interference](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-interference) at the pericentromeric repeats of [Schizosaccharomyces pombe](../../organism.md#schizosaccharomyces-pombe). Repeat transcripts enter a pathway involving [Dicer](../../enzyme.md#dicer), [RNA-dependent RNA polymerase](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-dependent-rna-polymerase) and [Argonaute](../../protein.md#argonaute). Small interfering [RNAs](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) in the [RITS complex](../../molecular-genetics.md#rits-complex) recognize complementary nascent repeat transcripts; coupling to [H3K9 methylation](../../molecular-genetics.md#h3k9-methylation) and [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin) binding reinforces silencing at the corresponding locus. The original identification of [RITS](../../molecular-genetics.md#rits-complex) physically connected small [RNAs](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid), [Argonaute](../../protein.md#argonaute) and a [chromodomain](../../protein.md#chromodomain) component.[Abstract and Results: RITS composition and association with heterochromatic loci](../../biology-bibliography.md#rnai-mediated-targeting-of-heterochromatin-by-the-rits-complex-by-andre-verdel-and-others) Some repeat [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) is therefore compatible with, and can help renew, a repressive domain. Complete elimination of all transcription is not a prerequisite for protection.

This defends the [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) in two related ways. Repressing [transposable elements](../../genetics.md#transposable-element) reduces production of the [RNAs](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) and [proteins](../../protein.md) required for mobilization, limiting insertional [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation) and inappropriate recombination. Protecting repetitive regions also supports orderly [chromosome segregation](../../genetics.md#chromosome-segregation). In fission yeast, loss of core [RNA interference](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-interference) components reduced centromeric [H3K9 methylation](../../molecular-genetics.md#h3k9-methylation), relieved silencing and impaired centromere function.[Abstract and Results: RNAi mutants, centromeric repeats, H3K9 methylation and centromere function](../../biology-bibliography.md#regulation-of-heterochromatic-silencing-and-histone-h3-lysine-9-methylation-by-rnai-by-thomas-a-volpe-and-others) These observations support a protective role for repeat-associated [heterochromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#heterochromatin), although a segregation defect alone would not prove a particular spreading mechanism.

Mammalian defense can use a different nucleation system. Sequence-specific [KRAB zinc-finger proteins](../../molecular-genetics.md#krab-zinc-finger-protein) can recruit [KAP1](../../protein.md#kap1), which supports recruitment of [SETDB1](../../enzyme.md#setdb1) and other repressive machinery at [endogenous retroviruses](../../genetics.md#endogenous-retrovirus). A small recognition region can thereby organize repression over a longer element. In mouse [embryonic stem cells](../../biological-cell.md#embryonic-stem-cell), removal of [KAP1](../../protein.md#kap1) derepressed particular [endogenous retroviruses](../../genetics.md#endogenous-retrovirus) and reduced their associated [H3K9 methylation](../../molecular-genetics.md#h3k9-methylation); [DNA methylation](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-methylation) provided a cooperating layer of repression.[Abstract and Results: endogenous retroviral derepression and loss of H3K9me3 after KAP1 deletion](../../biology-bibliography.md#kap1-controls-endogenous-retroviruses-in-embryonic-stem-cells-by-helen-m-rowe-and-others) The relevant factors and their relative importance vary with species, developmental state and element family; the fission-yeast pathway is not a universal mammalian template.

To demonstrate protective spreading directly, recruit a writer to one defined site beside several spaced [reporter genes](../../molecular-genetics.md#reporter-gene), then follow mark distribution, nascent [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) and reporter output over time. Compare a catalytically inactive writer, a reader-binding [mutant](../../genetics.md#mutant), a non-targeting recruitment control and insertion or deletion of a [chromatin boundary](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin-boundary). Remove the initiating recruitment to distinguish continual establishment from inherited maintenance. Analyze individual [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) because a population-average gradient could represent different all-or-none domain lengths. Finally, measure the proposed defense itself: new [transposable element](../../genetics.md#transposable-element) insertions, inappropriate recombination or [chromosome segregation](../../genetics.md#chromosome-segregation), alongside [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) abundance. **A larger silent domain is mechanistic evidence; reduced genome damage is the functional test.**

↑ **Parent:** [Section II](#section-ii)

<h4 id="section-ii/5/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#section-ii/5)

**Localizing a [messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#messenger-rna) places a renewable source of [protein](../../protein.md) at the site where it is needed.** One transported transcript can support many rounds of [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology), whereas transporting a finished [protein](../../protein.md) delivers only that molecule. Repressing the transcript during transport and activating it on arrival also separates movement from action: this is valuable when ectopic activity would disrupt [cell polarity](../../biological-cell.md#cell-polarity), [cell migration](../../biological-cell.md#cell-migration) or embryonic patterning.

The advantage is especially clear in [neurons](../../neuroscience.md#neuron). A stored [messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#messenger-rna) in a [dendrite](../../neuroscience.md#dendrite) or [axon](../../neuroscience.md#axon) can respond to a local signal without waiting for new [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) in the distant [soma](../../neuroscience.md#soma-biology) and delivery of the product. Different [synapses](../../neuroscience.md#synapse) can therefore regulate local synthesis independently. Local synthesis can also place a nascent [polypeptide](../../protein.md#polypeptide) near its binding partner, assembly site or destination membrane. Its limits matter: local [ribosomes](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome), charged [transfer RNA](../../polymer.md#transfer-rna) and [energy](../../energy.md) must be available, and the newly made [protein](../../protein.md) can still diffuse away unless it is captured, incorporated into a structure or rapidly removed. Localizing [messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#messenger-rna) is therefore not a guarantee of localized final [protein](../../protein.md), nor a universal energetic improvement over every [protein targeting](../../protein.md#protein-targeting) pathway.

Recognition usually begins with a [cis-regulatory element](../../molecular-genetics.md#cis-regulatory-element), often a structure or sequence in the [3′ untranslated region](../../polymer.md#3′-untranslated-region). An [RNA-binding protein](../../protein.md#rna-binding-protein) recognizes this localization signal and assembles a transport-competent [ribonucleoprotein](../../protein.md#ribonucleoprotein). The same complex can regulate [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology), protect the transcript and recruit an anchor. [RNA splicing](../../biology.md#rna-splicing) or assembly in the [cell nucleus](../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus) can influence which factors accompany the exported transcript. A localization element is established experimentally by necessity and sufficiency: deleting it disrupts the endogenous pattern, while transferring it to a normally unlocalized [reporter gene](../../molecular-genetics.md#reporter-gene) transcript confers localization. Controls must show that the manipulation has not simply destroyed the [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) or changed its [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology) so strongly that localization becomes secondary.

One mechanism is **directed transport by [molecular motors](../../biology.md#molecular-motor) along the [cytoskeleton](../../biological-cell.md#cytoskeleton)**. The motors [kinesin](../../biology.md#kinesin) and [dynein](../../biology.md#dynein) move cargos along [microtubules](../../mathematical-biology.md#microtubule); [myosin](../../protein.md#myosin) can support movement or capture on [actin](../../protein.md#actin). Adaptor interactions connect the motor to the [ribonucleoprotein](../../protein.md#ribonucleoprotein), and the organization of the tracks biases the destination. Individual particles can move in both directions, pause and reverse. In [Drosophila melanogaster](../../organism.md#drosophila-melanogaster) [oocytes](../../biology.md#oocyte), live tracking showed that [oskar messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#oskar-messenger-rna) undergoes largely [kinesin](../../biology.md#kinesin)-dependent motion in many directions, with a small posterior bias sufficient for localization. It therefore illustrates a biased [random walk](../../random-walk.md), rather than requiring every [microtubule](../../mathematical-biology.md#microtubule) to point toward the final destination.[Summary and Results: particle tracking, motor dependence and posterior transport bias](../../biology-bibliography.md#in-vivo-imaging-of-oskar-mrna-transport-reveals-the-mechanism-of-posterior-localization-by-vitaly-l-zimyanin-and-others)

A distinct mechanism is **[diffusion](../../thermodynamics.md#diffusion) followed by selective capture or anchoring**. Particles explore the [cytoplasm](../../biological-cell.md#cytoplasm) and are retained when they reach a localized binding site. [Cytoplasmic streaming](../../biological-fluid-dynamics.md#cytoplasmic-streaming) can increase delivery without a motor carrying each individual [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) directly to its destination. The behavior of [nanos messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#nanos-messenger-rna) provides an experimentally studied example of diffusion and entrapment at the posterior of the [Drosophila melanogaster](../../organism.md#drosophila-melanogaster) [oocyte](../../biology.md#oocyte).[Abstract and Results: RNA dynamics and posterior entrapment](../../biology-bibliography.md#live-imaging-of-endogenous-rna-reveals-a-diffusion-and-entrapment-mechanism-for-nanos-mrna-localization-in-drosophila-by-kevin-m-forrest-and-elizabeth-r-gavis) Transport and capture are often sequential or concurrent; distinguishing them requires measuring trajectories and residence times, rather than relying only on a final fixed image.

A third mechanism is **spatially selective stability**. [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) can be distributed broadly but degraded rapidly outside a protected region. The localized population then reflects longer residence through slower decay, even if delivery itself is unbiased. To test this explanation, follow a pulse of newly made [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) at different positions and measure its local disappearance while controlling transport. A localization-element [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) that reduces abundance everywhere does not establish selective stabilization. Likewise, a spatially restricted [protein](../../protein.md) pattern can arise from local activation of [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology) of a widespread transcript; this mechanism regulates product localization without necessarily localizing the transcript. Measuring both species distinguishes these possibilities.

A fourth route couples localization to **[biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology) and [protein targeting](../../protein.md#protein-targeting)**. A nascent [signal peptide](../../protein.md#signal-peptide) can engage [signal recognition particle](../../protein.md#signal-recognition-particle) and the [endoplasmic reticulum](../../biological-cell.md#endoplasmic-reticulum) import machinery, bringing the translating [ribosome](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome) and its [messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#messenger-rna) to the membrane. Other transcripts can bind a destination independently through [RNA-binding proteins](../../protein.md#rna-binding-protein). Compare localization after removal of the [signal peptide](../../protein.md#signal-peptide), disruption of the [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) element, or loss of initiation of [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology) to distinguish nascent-chain-mediated recruitment from transcript-mediated recruitment. Broad inhibitors of [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology) also alter [ribonucleoprotein](../../protein.md#ribonucleoprotein) composition, so a reporter that specifically removes the initiating [codon](../../molecular-genetics.md#codon) is a useful complementary test.

Localization must be coordinated with activation. The regulation of [beta-actin messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#beta-actin-messenger-rna) illustrates how an [RNA-binding protein](../../protein.md#rna-binding-protein) can couple the two: [ZBP1](../../protein.md#zbp1) binds its localization element and represses [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology) during transport, while [Src](../../enzyme.md#src)-dependent [phosphorylation](../../chemical-reaction.md#phosphorylation) relieves repression near the destination.[Abstract and Results: ZBP1 binding, translational repression and Src-dependent relief](../../biology-bibliography.md#spatial-regulation-of-beta-actin-translation-by-src-dependent-phosphorylation-of-zbp1-by-stefan-huttelmaier-and-others) Thus an [RNA-binding protein](../../protein.md#rna-binding-protein) need not remain a permanent inhibitor; the local signal converts a transported template into a local source of [actin](../../protein.md#actin).

Experimentally combine endogenous [single-molecule fluorescence in situ hybridization](../../biology.md#single-molecule-fluorescence-in-situ-hybridization) with live [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) tracking and a nascent-[polypeptide](../../protein.md#polypeptide) readout. Validate that fluorescent tags preserve [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) abundance, localization and function, and verify the imaging pattern against untagged transcripts. Acutely disrupt a motor, an anchor or a decay pathway separately, rescue the relevant factor, and monitor [cytoskeleton](../../biological-cell.md#cytoskeleton) integrity and cellular health. Directional runs support active transport, prolonged destination dwell supports capture, and a position-dependent [half-life](../../analysis.md#half-life) supports selective stability. An inhibitor that globally collapses [microtubules](../../mathematical-biology.md#microtubule) cannot alone distinguish a specific motor requirement from general loss of [cell polarity](../../biological-cell.md#cell-polarity). **The persuasive explanation connects delivery, retention and local synthesis to the measured biological outcome.**

<h4 id="section-ii/6/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#section-ii/6)

**During [amino acid starvation](../../molecular-genetics.md#amino-acid-starvation), cells reduce bulk [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology) while selectively promoting an adaptive response.** It has both a direct substrate effect on elongation and regulated effects on initiation. Their balance depends on which [amino acid](../../chemical-substance.md#amino-acid) is limiting, how severely it is depleted, the organism and the duration of deprivation.

The immediate substrate problem is reduced charging of the relevant [transfer RNAs](../../polymer.md#transfer-rna) by [aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases](../../enzyme.md#aminoacyl-trna-synthetase). A [ribosome](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome) encountering a cognate [codon](../../molecular-genetics.md#codon) waits longer for an appropriate charged adaptor, potentially creating queues or [ribosome collisions](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome-collision). The response is not necessarily identical at every synonymous [codon](../../molecular-genetics.md#codon), because [transfer RNA](../../polymer.md#transfer-rna) isoacceptors, their charging and their competition differ. In mammalian [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), comparative [ribosome profiling](../../biology.md#ribosome-profiling) under particular [amino acid](../../chemical-substance.md#amino-acid) limitations demonstrated selective [codon](../../molecular-genetics.md#codon)-associated pausing rather than an invariant slowdown at every sense [codon](../../molecular-genetics.md#codon).[Summary and Results: amino-acid-specific pausing, tRNA charging and translational responses](../../biology-bibliography.md#translational-control-through-differential-ribosome-pausing-during-amino-acid-limitation-in-mammalian-cells-by-alicia-m-darnell-and-others) Persistent stalls can engage [ribosome-associated quality control](../../molecular-genetics.md#ribosome-associated-quality-control) to rescue stalled machinery and dispose of incomplete products. Increased [ribosome](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome) occupancy at a site therefore need not mean increased productive synthesis.

In [eukaryotes](../../organism.md#eukaryote), [GCN2](../../enzyme.md#gcn2) links [amino acid](../../chemical-substance.md#amino-acid) limitation to the [integrated stress response](../../molecular-genetics.md#integrated-stress-response). Deacylated [transfer RNA](../../polymer.md#transfer-rna) and signals from starved [ribosomes](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome), supported by [GCN1](../../protein.md#gcn1), promote [GCN2](../../enzyme.md#gcn2) activation.[Abstract and Results: ribosome-dependent activation and relationship to translational stress](../../biology-bibliography.md#activation-of-gcn2-by-the-ribosomal-p-stalk-by-alison-j-inglis-and-others) Its [protein kinase](../../enzyme.md#protein-kinase) activity phosphorylates the alpha subunit of [eIF2](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif2) at serine 51. Normal [eIF2](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif2) brings initiating [methionyl-tRNA](../../polymer.md#methionyl-trna) to the small [ribosomal subunit](../../biological-cell.md#ribosomal-subunit) as part of a ternary complex:

$$
\mathrm{eIF2\!\cdot\!GTP\!\cdot\!Met\!\! -\!tRNA_i}.
$$

After initiation, [eIF2](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif2) must exchange bound [GDP](../../molecule.md#guanosine-diphosphate) for [GTP](../../molecule.md#guanosine-triphosphate), a reaction promoted by [eIF2B](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif2b). Phosphorylated [eIF2](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif2) inhibits this recycling activity, reducing ternary-complex supply and initiation on many [messenger RNAs](../../polymer.md#messenger-rna). This conserves scarce substrates and limits addition of further [ribosomes](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome) to already stressed templates. Genetic experiments established that [GCN2](../../enzyme.md#gcn2)-dependent regulation can repress bulk synthesis while increasing production of [ATF4](../../molecular-genetics.md#atf4).[Abstract and Results: eIF2 kinase dependence and selective ATF4 expression](../../biology-bibliography.md#regulated-translation-initiation-controls-stress-induced-gene-expression-in-mammalian-cells-by-heather-p-harding-and-others)

The apparent paradox is resolved by [upstream open reading frames](../../molecular-genetics.md#upstream-open-reading-frame). In the canonical mammalian [ATF4](../../molecular-genetics.md#atf4) leader, a short permissive [upstream open reading frame](../../molecular-genetics.md#upstream-open-reading-frame) is followed by an inhibitory one that overlaps the [ATF4](../../molecular-genetics.md#atf4) coding region in a different [reading frame](../../molecular-genetics.md#reading-frame). When ternary complex is plentiful, scanning small [ribosomal subunits](../../biological-cell.md#ribosomal-subunit) that have completed the first short frame reacquire it rapidly and initiate at the inhibitory frame. After [eIF2](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif2) [phosphorylation](../../chemical-reaction.md#phosphorylation), reacquisition is delayed: some scanning subunits pass that inhibitory start and become competent before the downstream [ATF4](../../molecular-genetics.md#atf4) start. **Reduced initiation capacity can therefore increase initiation at a particular downstream coding region.** Experiments using [upstream open reading frame](../../molecular-genetics.md#upstream-open-reading-frame) [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation) and reporter assays directly tested this reinitiation mechanism.[Abstract and Results: uORF mutations, spacing and delayed reinitiation](../../biology-bibliography.md#reinitiation-involving-upstream-orfs-regulates-atf4-mrna-translation-in-mammalian-cells-by-krishna-m-vattem-and-ronald-c-wek) The increase is not unlimited: if ternary complex becomes too scarce, initiation at [ATF4](../../molecular-genetics.md#atf4) also fails.

[ATF4](../../molecular-genetics.md#atf4) then changes [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) of adaptive [genes](../../genetics.md#gene), including those supporting [amino acid](../../chemical-substance.md#amino-acid) supply, transport and stress management. Yeast uses the related [GCN2](../../enzyme.md#gcn2)–[eIF2](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif2) logic to increase [GCN4](../../molecular-genetics.md#gcn4) production through a leader with several [upstream open reading frames](../../molecular-genetics.md#upstream-open-reading-frame); it should not be drawn as the identical two-frame [ATF4](../../molecular-genetics.md#atf4) arrangement. The response can recover after replenishment through restoration of charging and dephosphorylation of [eIF2](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif2). Prolonged, unresolved deprivation can instead favor damage and [apoptosis](../../biological-cell.md#apoptosis). These later [gene expression](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-expression) changes complement the initial translational response rather than explaining its earliest onset.

A parallel mammalian pathway regulates [mTORC1](../../signal-transduction.md#mtorc1). In nutrient-replete conditions, [mTORC1](../../signal-transduction.md#mtorc1) promotes cap-dependent initiation partly by phosphorylating [4E-BP proteins](../../molecular-genetics.md#4e-bp-protein) and activating [S6 kinase](../../enzyme.md#s6-kinase). Phosphorylated [4E-BP proteins](../../molecular-genetics.md#4e-bp-protein) release [eIF4E](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif4e), allowing it to associate with [eIF4G](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif4g) and assemble an active [eIF4F](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif4f) initiation complex. [amino acid starvation](../../molecular-genetics.md#amino-acid-starvation) reduces [mTORC1](../../signal-transduction.md#mtorc1) activity, allowing hypophosphorylated [4E-BP proteins](../../molecular-genetics.md#4e-bp-protein) to bind [eIF4E](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif4e) and restrain initiation. A concrete upstream sensor is [Sestrin2](../../signal-transduction.md#sestrin2): loss of [leucine](../../chemical-substance.md#leucine) promotes its inhibitory interaction with [GATOR2](../../signal-transduction.md#gator2), influencing [Rag GTPases](../../signal-transduction.md#rag-gtpase) and nutrient-dependent recruitment of [mTORC1](../../signal-transduction.md#mtorc1) to the [lysosome](../../biological-cell.md#lysosome).[Abstract and Results: leucine binding, sensor mutants and GATOR2 interactions](../../biology-bibliography.md#sestrin2-is-a-leucine-sensor-for-the-mtorc1-pathway-by-rachel-l-wolfson-and-others) This route measures nutrient sufficiency through a different mechanism from the [GCN2](../../enzyme.md#gcn2) pathway; neither should be reduced to the other.

Reduced [mTORC1](../../signal-transduction.md#mtorc1) signaling also helps limit investment in [ribosome](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome) production and permits [autophagy](../../biological-cell.md#autophagy), which can recycle cellular material to supply [amino acids](../../chemical-substance.md#amino-acid). These adaptations operate on different timescales from waiting for a missing charged [transfer RNA](../../polymer.md#transfer-rna). The outcome is selective redistribution of translational capacity, not simply uniform cessation of every [protein](../../protein.md) synthesis event. Some cap-dependent transcripts are especially sensitive to initiation-factor availability, while stress-regulated leaders can favor others.

To distinguish the mechanisms, compare a defined single-[amino acid](../../chemical-substance.md#amino-acid) withdrawal with matched complete medium and replenishment. Control residual [amino acids](../../chemical-substance.md#amino-acid) in supplements and measure viability. Measure global nascent [protein](../../protein.md) synthesis, [transfer RNA](../../polymer.md#transfer-rna) charging, [eIF2](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif2) [phosphorylation](../../chemical-reaction.md#phosphorylation), [ATF4](../../molecular-genetics.md#atf4) synthesis, and [mTORC1](../../signal-transduction.md#mtorc1) substrate [phosphorylation](../../chemical-reaction.md#phosphorylation) over time. Combine [ribosome profiling](../../biology.md#ribosome-profiling) with [RNA sequencing](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-sequencing) and external normalization; otherwise globally reduced synthesis can disappear through library normalization. Compare normal [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) with [GCN2](../../enzyme.md#gcn2) loss or a nonphosphorylatable [eIF2](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif2) variant, and use [ATF4](../../molecular-genetics.md#atf4) leader reporters with separately mutated [upstream open reading frames](../../molecular-genetics.md#upstream-open-reading-frame). Independently perturb [4E-BP proteins](../../molecular-genetics.md#4e-bp-protein) or [mTORC1](../../signal-transduction.md#mtorc1) signaling. Recovery of initiation signaling without recovery of elongation would show that substrate shortage still limits output. A loss of large [polysomes](../../biological-cell.md#polysome) suggests reduced initiation, whereas maintained or increased occupancy can accompany stalled elongation; neither pattern alone measures productive [protein](../../protein.md) synthesis.

In [bacteria](../../organism.md#bacteria), a related sensing principle feeds a different control system: in [Escherichia coli](../../organism.md#escherichia-coli), [RelA](../../enzyme.md#rela) recognizes starved [ribosomes](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome) containing deacylated [transfer RNA](../../polymer.md#transfer-rna) and synthesizes [(p)ppGpp](../../molecule.md#p-ppgpp), initiating the [stringent response](../../molecular-genetics.md#stringent-response). Structural work explains how the starved [ribosome](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome) activates [RelA](../../enzyme.md#rela).[Abstract and structural Results: deacylated tRNA, RelA activation and alarmone synthesis](../../biology-bibliography.md#ribosome-dependent-activation-of-stringent-control-by-alan-brown-and-others) The [stringent response](../../molecular-genetics.md#stringent-response) reallocates [gene expression](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-expression) and restrains investment in growth machinery; it is not an [eIF2](../../molecular-genetics.md#eif2)-based mechanism. **Across these systems, the common strategy is to lower demand while preserving or increasing the capacity to restore [amino acid](../../chemical-substance.md#amino-acid) supply.**

↑ **Parent:** [Section II](#section-ii)

<h4 id="section-ii/7/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#section-ii/7)

**The regulation of [chloroplast gene expression](../../biological-cell.md#chloroplast-gene-expression) includes substantial local control but depends on a largely nuclear-specified apparatus.** Distinguish where regulation occurs from where its regulator is encoded. A response executed rapidly inside the [chloroplast](../../biological-cell.md#chloroplast) can use pre-existing, imported [proteins](../../protein.md) and thus be operationally local while remaining genetically dependent on the [cell nucleus](../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus). The clearest examples below concern land-plant [chloroplasts](../../biological-cell.md#chloroplast); details vary among [plastids](../../biological-cell.md#plastid) and [algae](../../organism.md#algae).

The [chloroplast genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#chloroplast-genome) retains a limited set of [genes](../../genetics.md#gene) for core [photosynthesis](../../biology.md#photosynthesis) components and its own expression machinery, including [ribosomal RNA](../../polymer.md#ribosomal-rna), [transfer RNA](../../polymer.md#transfer-rna), some [ribosomal proteins](../../biological-cell.md#ribosomal-protein) and [RNA polymerase](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-polymerase) subunits. It supplies its own templates, local [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) and [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology). However, many components of those same systems, and most regulatory [proteins](../../protein.md), are encoded in the nuclear [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome), synthesized on cytosolic [ribosomes](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome) and delivered by [organelle protein import](../../protein.md#organelle-protein-import). Retaining [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) therefore confers neither complete biochemical independence nor a self-contained regulatory program.

At [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology), the two major polymerase systems make the mixed control particularly apparent. [plastid-encoded RNA polymerase](../../biological-cell.md#plastid-encoded-rna-polymerase), or [PEP](../../biological-cell.md#plastid-encoded-rna-polymerase), contains bacterial-type core subunits encoded by plastid [genes](../../genetics.md#gene), whereas [nuclear-encoded plastid RNA polymerase](../../biological-cell.md#nuclear-encoded-plastid-rna-polymerase), or [NEP](../../biological-cell.md#nuclear-encoded-plastid-rna-polymerase), is a nuclear-specified phage-type [enzyme](../../enzyme.md). Their [gene promoter](../../molecular-genetics.md#promoter-genetics) preferences differ and some [genes](../../genetics.md#gene) have access to both systems; a simple photosynthetic-versus-housekeeping division has exceptions. Experiments with tobacco plastids lacking functional [PEP](../../biological-cell.md#plastid-encoded-rna-polymerase) distinguished promoter classes and demonstrated continuing transcription by the other machinery.[Abstract and Results: plastid polymerase mutants and distinct promoter usage](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-two-rna-polymerases-encoded-by-the-nuclear-and-the-plastid-compartments-transcribe-distinct-groups-of-genes-in-tobacco-plastids-by-p-t-hajdukiewicz-and-others) The core of [PEP](../../biological-cell.md#plastid-encoded-rna-polymerase) is plastid encoded, but promoter-selecting [sigma factors](../../molecular-genetics.md#sigma-factor) and important associated [proteins](../../protein.md) are nuclear encoded. Even this apparently autonomous polymerase is embedded in nuclear control.

Nuclear regulation is therefore able to connect environmental and developmental signals to plastid [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology). In [Arabidopsis thaliana](../../biology.md#arabidopsis-thaliana), [SIG5](../../molecular-genetics.md#sig5) contributes to activation of the blue-light-responsive [psbD gene promoter](../../molecular-genetics.md#psbd-gene-promoter). The experiment provides a specific route from a nuclear-encoded [sigma factor](../../molecular-genetics.md#sigma-factor) to expression of a chloroplast [gene](../../genetics.md#gene), rather than merely correlating total greenness with nuclear state.[Abstract and Results: SIG5-dependent promoter activation](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-multiple-stress-responsive-plastid-sigma-factor-sig5-directs-activation-of-the-psbd-blue-light-responsive-promoter-blrp-in-arabidopsis-thaliana-by-akitomo-nagashima-and-others) Such [anterograde signaling](../../biological-cell.md#anterograde-signaling) can alter regulator availability, import or activity. Measuring total steady-state [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) alone would not establish which of those steps changed.

Nuclear influence is particularly extensive after [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology). Plastid transcripts often require cleavage, end maturation, [RNA splicing](../../biology.md#rna-splicing) and [RNA editing](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-editing); their stability and access to [ribosomes](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome) are also regulated. Nuclear-encoded [pentatricopeptide repeat proteins](../../protein.md#pentatricopeptide-repeat-protein) supply sequence-specific recognition for many of these events, although not every member has the same function. [PPR10](../../protein.md#ppr10) is a concrete stability mechanism: binding at a defined intergenic region can block degradation from either direction and define stable adjacent [messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#messenger-rna) ends.[Abstract and Results: PPR10 binding, overlapping transcript termini and barriers to exonucleolytic decay](../../biology-bibliography.md#site-specific-binding-of-a-ppr-protein-defines-and-stabilizes-5′-and-3′-mrna-termini-in-chloroplasts-by-jeannette-pfalz-and-others) The functional instruction is a nuclear-encoded [RNA-binding protein](../../protein.md#rna-binding-protein) acting directly on a plastid transcript. The local [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) sequence is still essential because it determines where that instruction can act.

At [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology), chloroplast [ribosomes](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome) and their [RNAs](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) operate locally but rely on many imported initiation, elongation and transcript-specific factors. [RNA secondary structure](../../polymer.md#rna-secondary-structure), transcript processing and binding of a nuclear-encoded regulator can expose or occlude the initiation region. Assembly of a complex can feed back on synthesis of its own components, preventing wasteful production of an unassembled subunit. This is useful because the final [protein](../../protein.md) complex can contain products of both nuclear and chloroplast [genes](../../genetics.md#gene); neither [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome)'s output alone determines the final stoichiometry.

Local control is illustrated by synthesis of [photosystem II](../../biology.md#photosystem-ii) [D1 protein](../../biology.md#d1-protein) from chloroplast [psbA messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#psba-messenger-rna). Light damages [D1 protein](../../biology.md#d1-protein), increasing demand for replacement during [photosystem II repair](../../biology.md#photosystem-ii-repair). Experiments separating photodamage from photosynthetic energy supply supported an assembly-linked circuit that changes recruitment of [ribosomes](../../biological-cell.md#ribosome) to [psbA messenger RNA](../../polymer.md#psba-messenger-rna). This involves nuclear-encoded assembly and expression factors, including [HCF244](../../protein.md#hcf244), and demonstrates how imported machinery can implement a rapid local response.[Abstract and Results: photodamage, ribosome recruitment and assembly-linked autoregulation](../../biology-bibliography.md#light-induced-psba-translation-in-plants-is-triggered-by-photosystem-ii-damage-via-an-assembly-linked-autoregulatory-circuit-by-prakitchai-chotewutmontri-and-alice-barkan) It would be too strong to infer that all light regulation has a single cause, or that this result establishes an identical circuit in every photosynthetic organism.

The nucleus also receives information back. In [retrograde signaling](../../biological-cell.md#retrograde-signaling), the state of plastid expression, metabolism or stress alters nuclear [gene expression](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-expression), helping coordinate the two compartments. Genetic analysis of plastid [sigma factors](../../molecular-genetics.md#sigma-factor) and [GUN1](../../biological-cell.md#gun1) provides evidence that defects in plastid expression can affect nuclear photosynthetic [gene expression](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-expression).[Abstract and Results: sigma-factor mutants, GUN1 and regulation of nuclear photosynthesis-associated genes](../../biology-bibliography.md#sigma-factor-mediated-plastid-retrograde-signals-control-nuclear-gene-expression-by-jesse-d-woodson-and-others) [GUN1](../../biological-cell.md#gun1) should not be presented as a universal exported signal or as proof that all proposed plastid stress signals share one molecular route. These feedbacks make the two regulatory systems mutually coupled, rather than placing the [chloroplast](../../biological-cell.md#chloroplast) at the end of a purely one-way command chain.

Test the degree of local control using a short perturbation in isolated intact [chloroplasts](../../biological-cell.md#chloroplast), or a controlled change in illumination in intact [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), and follow nascent [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid), newly synthesized [protein](../../protein.md) and complex assembly. Check preparation purity, membrane integrity and [energy](../../energy.md) availability. A rapid response without new nuclear [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) supports operation by an existing local apparatus; it does not show independence of nuclear-encoded [proteins](../../protein.md) already present. Conversely, acute depletion of a nuclear regulator followed by rescue with an import-competent or import-defective version tests whether its action inside the [chloroplast](../../biological-cell.md#chloroplast) is necessary. Manipulating the matching plastid [cis-regulatory element](../../molecular-genetics.md#cis-regulatory-element) can identify its direct target.

Finally, measure each layer separately. A fall in transcript abundance may reflect less [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) or faster decay; fewer assembled [photosystems](../../biology.md#photosystem) may reflect defective synthesis, import, assembly or increased turnover. Pair plastid run-on measurements with [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) decay assays, [ribosome profiling](../../biology.md#ribosome-profiling), nascent-[protein](../../protein.md) labelling and assembly measurements. Long-term inhibition of cytosolic [biological translation](../../molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology) or loss of a nuclear [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) can disrupt the entire [organelle](../../biological-cell.md#organelle), so use early time points and appropriate rescue to distinguish primary regulation from secondary damage. **Autonomy is strongest for immediate local sensing and execution, while the nucleus specifies much of the capacity, specificity and long-term developmental program that makes that local response possible.**
