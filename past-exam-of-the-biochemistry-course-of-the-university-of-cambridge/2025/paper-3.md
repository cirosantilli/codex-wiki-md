<h4 id="section-i/1/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-i/1)

**Genome management helps explain the architecture of the eukaryotic [cell cycle](../../cell-cycle.md), but it does not establish a single historical cause for its origin.** Larger [genomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome), multiple [chromosomes](../../genetics.md#chromosome), [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin) and compartmentalisation create problems of complete copying and accurate allocation. The [cell cycle](../../cell-cycle.md) solves these problems while also coordinating growth, environmental responses and reproduction. A functional explanation of a modern mechanism must therefore be distinguished from a reconstruction of why that mechanism first evolved.

The basic requirement predates [eukaryotes](../../organism.md#eukaryote): a reproducing [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) must copy inherited information and distribute sufficient copies to its offspring. [Bacteria](../../organism.md#bacteria) also regulate initiation, resolve intertwined [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) and coordinate [chromosome segregation](../../genetics.md#chromosome-segregation) with division. The relevant evolutionary change is not the invention of any copying–division cycle from nothing, but the origin and elaboration of particular [eukaryotic](../../organism.md#eukaryote) solutions. Conversely, a large [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) need not encode a proportionately larger number of functions: repetitive and other [non-coding DNA](../../molecular-genetics.md#non-coding-dna) can expand without a matching increase in regulatory complexity.

In a typical proliferating [eukaryotic](../../organism.md#eukaryote) [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md), [S phase](../../cell-cycle.md#s-phase) duplicates nuclear [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) and [M phase](../../cell-cycle.md#m-phase) distributes the copies through [mitosis](../../cell-cycle.md#mitosis) and [cytokinesis](../../cell-cycle.md#cytokinesis). Separating these processes permits a clear sequence: establish competent replication machinery, replicate, finish and repair, segregate, then separate daughters. [G1 phase](../../cell-cycle.md#g1-phase) and [G2 phase](../../cell-cycle.md#g2-phase) provide opportunities to integrate growth and environmental information. Their duration and even their conspicuous presence vary: early embryonic cycles can alternate rapidly between [S phase](../../cell-cycle.md#s-phase) and [M phase](../../cell-cycle.md#m-phase), whereas other [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) stop dividing or undertake [endoreduplication](../../cell-cycle.md#endoreduplication). Thus the familiar four-phase diagram is one regulated implementation, not a universal definition of all possible [eukaryotic](../../organism.md#eukaryote) reproduction.

A larger [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) places an immediate demand on replication capacity. In an idealised model, $N$ simultaneously active bidirectional [origin of replication](../../molecular-genetics.md#origin-of-replication) sites, each producing two [replication forks](../../molecular-genetics.md#replication-fork) moving at speed $v$, copy a [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) amount $G$ in time $T$ only if

$$
2NvT\geq G.
$$

Here $v$ is measured in [base pairs](../../polymer.md#base-pair) per unit time and $G$ in [base pairs](../../polymer.md#base-pair); both sides have the same units. Evenly distributed origins with synchronous firing and no interruptions make the bound attainable in the ideal model. Real [S phase](../../cell-cycle.md#s-phase) includes asynchronous firing, unequal origin spacing and [replication fork](../../molecular-genetics.md#replication-fork) delays, so the inequality is a capacity requirement rather than a reliable prediction of completion time. Increasing origin number solves a throughput problem but creates a control problem: each region must be copied once, with gaps filled and rereplication prevented.

[replication origin licensing](../../molecular-genetics.md#replication-origin-licensing) separates permission from execution. When [cyclin-dependent kinase](../../enzyme.md#cyclin-dependent-kinase) activity is low, the [origin recognition complex](../../molecular-genetics.md#origin-recognition-complex), [Cdc6](../../molecular-genetics.md#cdc6) and [Cdt1](../../molecular-genetics.md#cdt1) load inactive [MCM2-7](../../molecular-genetics.md#mcm2-7) double hexamers onto [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid). Later kinase activity permits firing while blocking renewed licensing. Reconstitution with purified budding-yeast components showed ATP-dependent loading of head-to-head [MCM2-7](../../molecular-genetics.md#mcm2-7) complexes; structural and biochemical assays distinguished loaded [DNA helicase](../../enzyme.md#dna-helicase) precursors from active unwinding machinery.[Cell 139, pp. 719–730; purified licensing reaction and double-hexamer analysis](../../biology-bibliography.md#concerted-loading-of-mcm2-7-double-hexamers-around-dna-during-dna-replication-origin-licensing-by-dirk-remus-and-others) An explicit separation between licensing and firing makes parallel replication compatible with a once-per-cycle constraint.

Physical complexity also matters. Multiple [sister chromatids](../../genetics.md#sister-chromatids) must remain paired after copying, acquire suitable spindle attachments and then separate together. [Cohesin](../../protein.md#cohesin) maintains their association; [kinetochores](../../genetics.md#kinetochore) connect [chromosomes](../../genetics.md#chromosome) to the [spindle apparatus](../../cell-cycle.md#spindle-apparatus); regulated [separase](../../protein.md#separase) activation removes cohesion. [chromosome condensation](../../genetics.md#chromosome-condensation) and [DNA topoisomerases](../../enzyme.md#dna-topoisomerase) help manage the long, interlinked polymers. The [spindle checkpoint](../../cell-cycle.md#spindle-checkpoint) delays [anaphase](../../cell-cycle.md#anaphase) while attachment is incomplete, rather than relying on a fixed waiting time. Additional [DNA damage checkpoints](../../cell-cycle.md#dna-damage-checkpoint) and the [replication checkpoint](../../cell-cycle.md#replication-checkpoint) coordinate entry into division with the state of the template and replication machinery. These mechanisms can buffer errors whose costs grow with the number of essential elements that must be inherited, although none guarantees perfect fidelity.

A shared biochemical controller ties these tasks together. Rising [cyclin-dependent kinase](../../enzyme.md#cyclin-dependent-kinase) activity favours replication and then mitotic events, while regulated [ubiquitin](../../protein.md#ubiquitin)-dependent destruction of [cyclins](../../protein.md#cyclin) and other substrates creates directionality and resets the system. In fission yeast, an engineered single [cyclin](../../protein.md#cyclin)–kinase module could support ordered progression using activity thresholds despite removal of much canonical regulation. This demonstrates that a relatively small control core can organise complex downstream events; it does not demonstrate that the engineered strain reconstructs an ancestral [eukaryote](../../organism.md#eukaryote).[Nature 468, pp. 1074–1079; engineered minimal network, kinase activity thresholds and ordered cell-cycle progression](../../biology-bibliography.md#driving-the-cell-cycle-with-a-minimal-cdk-control-network-by-damien-coudreuse-and-paul-nurse)

The hypothesis also has limits. [cell growth](../../biological-cell.md#cell-growth), nutrient availability and allocation of [organelles](../../biological-cell.md#organelle) would still need coordination even for a smaller [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome). Multicellular organisms add requirements for developmental signals and restraint of inappropriate [cell proliferation](../../biological-cell.md#cell-proliferation). Closed [mitosis](../../cell-cycle.md#mitosis) in some organisms shows that nuclear-envelope breakdown is not an obligatory solution; ongoing [organelle](../../biological-cell.md#organelle) replication also need not follow the nuclear programme exactly. Moreover, regulation can become more elaborate without a proportional increase in [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) size. Present-day correlations cannot decide which trait evolved first.

Testing the historical claim would require comparative analysis of regulatory components across diverse [eukaryotes](../../organism.md#eukaryote) and relevant outgroups, reconstruction of [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) gains and losses, and experiments that alter replication burden independently of [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) size and growth rate. For example, increasing origin spacing while holding [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) content fixed tests replication completion, whereas adding dispensable [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) with compensatory origins tests increased [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) size more specifically. Measure completion, segregation errors, [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) and stress responses; an engineered defect under laboratory conditions cannot by itself reveal ancient selection pressures. **The defensible conclusion is that [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) size and physical organisation supplied important demands that cell-cycle regulation meets, while the evolutionary origin of that regulation remains a broader, multicausal question.**

**Tumours gain support by changing the behaviour of genetically non-malignant neighbours, recruiting circulating [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) and conditioning distant [biological tissues](../../biology.md#tissue-biology).** These interactions create a [tumour microenvironment](../../biology.md#tumour-microenvironment) that can supply growth signals, modify physical barriers and suppress immune attack. Reprogramming here usually means a change of [cell state](../../biological-cell.md#cell-state) through signalling or altered surroundings; it need not mean that the neighbouring [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) acquires the [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour)'s [driver mutations](../../genetics.md#driver-mutation).

Resident [fibroblasts](../../biological-cell.md#fibroblast) are major recipients of tumour-derived signals, including [TGF-beta signalling](../../signal-transduction.md#tgf-beta-signalling) inputs. Activated [cancer-associated fibroblasts](../../biological-cell.md#cancer-associated-fibroblast) can produce [extracellular matrix](../../biology.md#extracellular-matrix), remodel its organisation and secrete growth factors and [chemokines](../../signal-transduction.md#chemokine). These changes support carcinoma-cell survival, provide tracks for [cell migration](../../biological-cell.md#cell-migration) and alter mechanical signalling through [integrins](../../cell-membrane.md#integrin). Matrix accumulation can also impair access of drugs and immune [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md). Reciprocal feedback matters: [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) alter fibroblasts, and the changed fibroblasts then alter [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) behaviour.

A direct functional example used fibroblasts isolated from human breast [carcinomas](../../biology.md#carcinoma) and normal mammary fibroblasts from the same patients. In a coimplantation [xenograft](../../biology.md#xenograft) assay, carcinoma-associated fibroblasts enhanced [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) growth and [angiogenesis](../../biology.md#angiogenesis) more strongly. Increased [CXCL12](../../signal-transduction.md#cxcl12) [protein secretion](../../protein.md#protein-secretion) connected stromal signalling with tumour-cell stimulation and recruitment of vascular precursors. The matched normal-fibroblast comparison helps distinguish an associated stromal state from the mere presence of additional [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), while perturbing the ligand–receptor axis tests mechanism.[Cell 121, pp. 335–348; matched fibroblast isolates, coimplantation assays and CXCL12-dependent effects](../../biology-bibliography.md#stromal-fibroblasts-present-in-invasive-human-breast-carcinomas-promote-tumor-growth-and-angiogenesis-through-elevated-sdf-1-cxcl12-secretion-by-akira-orimo-and-others) Because these were transplantation experiments, they establish a growth-promoting capacity in that setting rather than proving that every fibroblast population is tumour-promoting.

Tumours also recruit and educate [macrophages](../../biological-cell.md#macrophage). Tumour-derived [CSF1](../../signal-transduction.md#csf1) can support macrophage recruitment and survival, while macrophage-derived [epidermal growth factor](../../protein.md#epidermal-growth-factor) stimulates carcinoma-cell movement. In mammary-tumour experiments, blocking either side of this paracrine interaction reduced coupled migration, and [intravital microscopy](../../biology.md#intravital-microscopy) connected the [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md)' behaviour in living [biological tissue](../../biology.md#tissue-biology). This supplies a mechanistic explanation for why non-malignant motile [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) can increase invasion by malignant ones.[Cancer Research 64, pp. 7022–7029; EGF/CSF1-dependent migration and receptor-pathway perturbations](../../biology-bibliography.md#a-paracrine-loop-between-tumor-cells-and-macrophages-is-required-for-tumor-cell-migration-in-mammary-tumors-by-jeffrey-wyckoff-and-others) [tumour-associated macrophages](../../biological-cell.md#tumour-associated-macrophage) can also promote vascular remodelling and immune suppression, but their states vary; a single binary inflammatory-versus-supportive classification is inadequate.

The vascular compartment is another target. [hypoxia](../../biology.md#hypoxia) and tumour-associated signalling promote [protein secretion](../../protein.md#protein-secretion) of [VEGF](../../signal-transduction.md#vegf), which acts on [endothelial cells](../../biological-cell.md#endothelial-cell) to stimulate [angiogenesis](../../biology.md#angiogenesis). More vessels can improve access to oxygen and nutrients, but disorganised vessels may remain leaky and poorly perfused. This can maintain [hypoxia](../../biology.md#hypoxia), increase interstitial pressure and compromise drug delivery even as the [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) expands. The advantage to the [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) is therefore not equivalent to restoration of a normal, efficient circulation. Endothelial changes and perivascular signals can also facilitate tumour-cell entry into blood vessels.

Immune [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) may be redirected from elimination toward tolerance. Suppressive [cytokines](../../signal-transduction.md#cytokine), [regulatory T cells](../../biological-cell.md#regulatory-t-cell), [tumour-associated macrophages](../../biological-cell.md#tumour-associated-macrophage) and [myeloid-derived suppressor cells](../../biological-cell.md#myeloid-derived-suppressor-cell) can reduce effective antitumour responses. Altered [dendritic cell](../../biological-cell.md#dendritic-cell) maturation and [antigen presentation](../../biology.md#antigen-presentation) limit priming, while nutrient depletion and inhibitory metabolites can impair activated [T cells](../../biological-cell.md#t-cell). Induction of [PD-L1](../../protein.md#pd-l1) on [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) or neighbouring [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) further restrains [PD-1](../../protein.md#pd-1)-expressing lymphocytes. A [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) can therefore benefit from changing non-malignant immune-cell behaviour without removing those [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) entirely. Finding immune [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) inside a [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) does not establish that they are recognising or killing malignant targets.

The same logic can operate at a distance. Tumour-derived [extracellular vesicles](../../biological-cell.md#extracellular-vesicle) can be taken up by resident [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) in prospective metastatic organs, altering inflammatory and stromal programmes before tumour-cell arrival. In experimental models, distinct exosomal [integrins](../../cell-membrane.md#integrin) were associated with different target organs, and interference with those integrins reduced uptake and subsequent [metastasis](../../biology.md#metastasis). Pretreatment experiments helped connect vesicle exposure to formation of a [premetastatic niche](../../biology.md#premetastatic-niche).[Nature 527, pp. 329–335; organ-selective uptake, vesicle pretreatment and integrin-blocking experiments](../../biology-bibliography.md#tumour-exosome-integrins-determine-organotropic-metastasis-by-ayuko-hoshino-and-others) This supports a mechanism for conditioning recipient [biological tissue](../../biology.md#tissue-biology); it does not mean that one vesicle code completely explains the organ preference of every human [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour).

Reprogramming also includes metabolic cooperation. In pancreatic-cancer experiments, [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) stimulated [autophagy](../../biological-cell.md#autophagy) in [pancreatic stellate cells](../../biological-cell.md#pancreatic-stellate-cell), which secreted [alanine](../../chemical-substance.md#alanine) used by malignant [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) as a carbon source for the [citric acid cycle](../../biochemistry.md#citric-acid-cycle) and biosynthesis. Tracing carbon and interfering with stromal [autophagy](../../biological-cell.md#autophagy) connected the changed support-cell behaviour to [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) metabolism.[Nature 536, pp. 479–483; metabolite tracing, autophagy perturbations and growth under nutrient limitation](../../biology-bibliography.md#pancreatic-stellate-cells-support-tumour-metabolism-through-autophagic-alanine-secretion-by-cristovao-m-sousa-and-others) A shared metabolic-expression signature alone would not establish this material transfer. More generally, to establish a specific transfer, trace labelled substrates into recipient metabolites and biomass, distinguish exchange from independent use of the same nutrient, and block the proposed transport step with rescue by the downstream product. The [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour)'s advantage should be measured as survival, expansion or invasion under defined conditions.

A general causal design would compare tumour-conditioned and control-conditioned media on purified recipient [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), with matched [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) numbers and viability, then wash and retest recipients to distinguish a persistent state from residual secreted factors. Ligand deletion in [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), receptor deletion in recipients and recipient-specific rescue identify directionality. Add-back experiments, direct-contact versus barrier cultures and spatial lineage labels distinguish soluble signals, matrix effects and replacement by contaminating malignant [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md). In vivo, use cell-type-specific interventions and immune-competent models where immune suppression is the proposed mechanism; a lymphocyte-deficient [xenograft](../../biology.md#xenograft) cannot establish the full immune interaction.

Importantly, supporting [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) can also restrain disease. Depletion of alpha-smooth-muscle-actin-positive myofibroblasts in a pancreatic-cancer mouse model reduced fibrosis yet accelerated aggressive disease and immune suppression. The result shows that removing a conspicuous stromal component can eliminate protective functions as well as supportive ones.[Cancer Cell 25, pp. 719–734; selective myofibroblast depletion and resulting tumour and immune phenotypes](../../biology-bibliography.md#depletion-of-carcinoma-associated-fibroblasts-and-fibrosis-induces-immunosuppression-and-accelerates-pancreas-cancer-with-reduced-survival-by-berna-c-ozdemir-and-others) **The [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour)'s advantage emerges from particular reciprocal interactions, so the relevant question is which [cell state](../../biological-cell.md#cell-state) and function are altered, rather than whether all surrounding normal [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) are uniformly harmful.**

**A common transition into [anaphase](../../cell-cycle.md#anaphase) both releases [chromosomes](../../genetics.md#chromosome) for separation and permits the spindle to organise [cytokinesis](../../cell-cycle.md#cytokinesis); later safeguards delay final separation when segregation remains incomplete.** Temporal coordination makes the events occur in the right order, while spatial coordination positions division between the separating [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) sets. The two forms of control interact but are experimentally distinguishable.

[S phase](../../cell-cycle.md#s-phase) establishes [sister chromatids](../../genetics.md#sister-chromatids) and their association through [cohesin](../../protein.md#cohesin). During [mitosis](../../cell-cycle.md#mitosis), [chromosome condensation](../../genetics.md#chromosome-condensation) and [DNA topoisomerase II](../../enzyme.md#dna-topoisomerase-ii) activity help prepare the copies for separation. Each sister's [kinetochore](../../genetics.md#kinetochore) should attach to the opposite spindle pole, producing [biorientation](../../genetics.md#biorientation). [Aurora B](../../enzyme.md#aurora-b) contributes to error correction by destabilising inappropriate attachments. Attachment and tension affect this process, but the [spindle checkpoint](../../cell-cycle.md#spindle-checkpoint) should not be described simply as a direct meter of equal tension on every [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome).

Unattached [kinetochores](../../genetics.md#kinetochore) catalyse formation of the [mitotic checkpoint complex](../../cell-cycle.md#mitotic-checkpoint-complex), involving [Mad2](../../cell-cycle.md#mad2), [BubR1](../../cell-cycle.md#bubr1), [Bub3](../../cell-cycle.md#bub3) and [Cdc20](../../cell-cycle.md#cdc20). This inhibits productive [anaphase-promoting complex](../../cell-cycle.md#anaphase-promoting-complex) activity toward key substrates. In a classic causal experiment, destruction of the last unattached [kinetochore](../../genetics.md#kinetochore) allowed [anaphase](../../cell-cycle.md#anaphase) to proceed, with chromosome-arm damage providing an important control. It was the inhibitory signalling structure, rather than the physical presence of one unaligned [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) somewhere in the [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md), that maintained the delay.[Journal of Cell Biology 130, pp. 941–948; laser ablation of the last unattached kinetochore and chromosome-arm controls](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-checkpoint-delaying-anaphase-in-response-to-chromosome-monoorientation-is-mediated-by-an-inhibitory-signal-produced-by-unattached-kinetochores-by-conly-l-rieder-and-others)

Once attachment-associated checkpoint signalling is silenced and inhibitory complexes are dismantled, [anaphase-promoting complex](../../cell-cycle.md#anaphase-promoting-complex) with [Cdc20](../../cell-cycle.md#cdc20) promotes [ubiquitin](../../protein.md#ubiquitin)-dependent destruction of [securin](../../protein.md#securin) and mitotic [cyclin B](../../signal-transduction.md#cyclin-b). [securin](../../protein.md#securin) destruction permits [separase](../../protein.md#separase) activity, which cleaves the remaining cohesion-bearing kleisin subunit. Non-cleavable [Scc1](../../protein.md#scc1) blocked sister separation in budding yeast, directly linking regulated proteolysis to the loss of [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) cohesion.[Nature 400, pp. 37–42; cleavage-resistant Scc1 and failure of sister-chromatid separation](../../biology-bibliography.md#sister-chromatid-separation-at-anaphase-onset-is-promoted-by-cleavage-of-the-cohesin-subunit-scc1-by-frank-uhlmann-and-others) Chromosome movement can then proceed through shortening of kinetochore-associated [microtubules](../../mathematical-biology.md#microtubule) and separation of the spindle poles.

Destruction of [cyclin B](../../signal-transduction.md#cyclin-b) also lowers [CDK1](../../signal-transduction.md#cyclin-dependent-kinase-1) activity. This changes the phosphorylation balance, allowing [protein phosphatases](../../enzyme.md#protein-phosphatase) to reverse mitotic modifications and permitting [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) decondensation, nuclear reassembly and cytokinetic organisation. Thus regulated proteolysis produces two coordinated outputs: removal of a physical [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) linkage and removal of the mitotic kinase state. Their sharing of a controller improves ordering, but it does not mean that [cytokinesis](../../cell-cycle.md#cytokinesis) physically waits for a sensor to count every [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) at its destination.

For animal [cytokinesis](../../cell-cycle.md#cytokinesis), overlapping [microtubules](../../mathematical-biology.md#microtubule) of the [central spindle](../../cell-cycle.md#central-spindle) and signals associated with astral [microtubules](../../mathematical-biology.md#microtubule) specify an equatorial cortical region. [centralspindlin](../../protein.md#centralspindlin) recruits and regulates the guanine-nucleotide exchange factor [ECT2](../../protein.md#ect2), which promotes local [RhoA](../../protein.md#rhoa) activation. [RhoA](../../protein.md#rhoa) stimulates [actin](../../protein.md#actin) assembly and [myosin II](../../protein.md#myosin-ii) activity, organising the [actomyosin ring](../../cell-cycle.md#actomyosin-ring) that drives furrow ingression. This geometry aligns the division plane between the segregating [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) masses. Spindle position can itself be set by cortical [cell polarity](../../biological-cell.md#cell-polarity) cues, explaining how the same machinery supports either approximately symmetric or asymmetric division.

Depletion and localisation experiments distinguish the biochemical signal from a simple structural scaffold. Experiments in human [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) showed that [ECT2](../../protein.md#ect2) is required for cortical [RhoA](../../protein.md#rhoa) and furrow assembly, and that different [centralspindlin](../../protein.md#centralspindlin) subunits have distinguishable consequences for these events. Loss of central-spindle localisation need not abolish every equatorial signal, because additional spatial inputs can persist.[Journal of Cell Biology 170, pp. 571–582; ECT2 and centralspindlin depletion, interaction and cortical RhoA assays](../../biology-bibliography.md#an-ect2-centralspindlin-complex-regulates-the-localization-and-function-of-rhoa-by-ozlem-yuce-and-others) Accordingly, central-spindle disruption should be assessed alongside astral signals, spindle orientation and direct measurements of the cortical activity zone.

Furrow ingression leaves an intercellular bridge with a [midbody](../../cell-cycle.md#midbody). Final [abscission](../../cell-cycle.md#abscission) involves [ESCRT-III](../../protein.md#escrt-iii)-dependent membrane-remodelling machinery and must be distinguished from the earlier contractile event. Chromatin trapped in the bridge can sustain [Aurora B](../../enzyme.md#aurora-b)-dependent signalling and delay abscission. Human-cell experiments connected persistent [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) bridges with continued kinase activity and delayed final separation, reducing the risk of unsuccessful division and furrow regression.[Cell 136, pp. 473–484; chromosome bridges, persistent Aurora B activity and delayed abscission](../../biology-bibliography.md#aurora-b-mediated-abscission-checkpoint-protects-against-tetraploidization-by-patrick-steigemann-and-others) This safeguard can provide time to resolve a problem; persistent bridges can still lead to breakage, regression or abnormal descendants.

A decisive experimental series would image labelled [chromosomes](../../genetics.md#chromosome), [microtubules](../../mathematical-biology.md#microtubule), [cyclin B](../../signal-transduction.md#cyclin-b), active [RhoA](../../protein.md#rhoa) and the [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) boundary in the same [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md). Acute checkpoint inhibition predicts premature [anaphase](../../cell-cycle.md#anaphase) and increased segregation errors. Stabilised [securin](../../protein.md#securin) or cleavage-resistant cohesion should obstruct separation; persistent [cyclin B](../../signal-transduction.md#cyclin-b) should obstruct normal mitotic exit. Acute [ECT2](../../protein.md#ect2) loss can impair [cytokinesis](../../cell-cycle.md#cytokinesis) even when [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) segregation proceeds, demonstrating separable downstream machinery. Use timed interventions after spindle assembly, independent depletion reagents, appropriately expressed rescue constructs and controls for global [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) damage. Count [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) allocation, furrow initiation, ingression and abscission separately; a binucleate endpoint does not reveal which step failed.

The checkpoint also has blind spots. A [merotelic attachment](../../genetics.md#merotelic-attachment), where one kinetochore binds microtubules from both poles, may evade an unattached-kinetochore signal and generate a lagging [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome). **Coordination is therefore layered rather than absolute: attachment surveillance, a shared proteolytic transition, spindle-directed cortical signalling and a late abscission checkpoint jointly reduce the chance of partitioning the [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) incorrectly.** The detailed geometry differs in organisms that divide with a [cell plate](../../cell-cycle.md#cell-plate) or other cytokinetic mechanisms.

<h3 id="section-ii/4">4</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section II](#section-ii)

<h4 id="section-ii/4/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#section-ii/4)

**Bacterial and eukaryotic replication obey the same chemical constraints but use different replication machines, initiation controls and chromosome-management strategies.** Both perform [semiconservative replication](../../molecular-genetics.md#semiconservative-replication), extend new [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) in the $5'\to3'$ direction from a paired $3'$ end, and therefore require repeated initiation on the lagging template. A useful comparison uses [Escherichia coli](../../organism.md#escherichia-coli) and nuclear replication in a conventional [eukaryote](../../organism.md#eukaryote), while recognising that neither represents every organism in its group.

At an [E. coli](../../organism.md#escherichia-coli) [origin of replication](../../molecular-genetics.md#origin-of-replication), [DnaA](../../molecular-genetics.md#dnaa) helps open [oriC](../../molecular-genetics.md#oric) and promotes assembly of the initiation machinery. The [DnaB](../../molecular-genetics.md#dnab) [DNA helicase](../../enzyme.md#dna-helicase) is loaded with the assistance of loading factors, and two [replication forks](../../molecular-genetics.md#replication-fork) move away from the origin. Initiation responds to growth and the nucleotide-bound state of [DnaA](../../molecular-genetics.md#dnaa), with additional controls limiting immediate reinitiation. A bacterial [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) need not always be single and circular: some bacteria have several replicons or linear [chromosomes](../../genetics.md#chromosome). Fast-growing bacteria can also begin a new round before the preceding round has finished, so replication and [biological cell division](../../biological-cell.md#biological-cell-division) do not have a universal one-round-to-one-division temporal relationship.

Nuclear [eukaryotic](../../organism.md#eukaryote) [chromosomes](../../genetics.md#chromosome) generally use many [origin of replication](../../molecular-genetics.md#origin-of-replication) sites. In [G1 phase](../../cell-cycle.md#g1-phase), the [origin recognition complex](../../molecular-genetics.md#origin-recognition-complex), [Cdc6](../../molecular-genetics.md#cdc6) and [Cdt1](../../molecular-genetics.md#cdt1) load [MCM2-7](../../molecular-genetics.md#mcm2-7) around double-stranded [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid). This is [replication origin licensing](../../molecular-genetics.md#replication-origin-licensing), not yet active [replication fork](../../molecular-genetics.md#replication-fork) movement. During [S phase](../../cell-cycle.md#s-phase), [cyclin-dependent kinases](../../enzyme.md#cyclin-dependent-kinase) and [Dbf4-dependent kinase](../../enzyme.md#dbf4-dependent-kinase) promote assembly of the active [CMG helicase](../../molecular-genetics.md#cmg-helicase), containing [Cdc45](../../molecular-genetics.md#cdc45), [MCM2-7](../../molecular-genetics.md#mcm2-7) and [GINS](../../molecular-genetics.md#gins). Elevated kinase activity also suppresses new licensing, separating origin competence from origin firing.

Purified budding-yeast reconstitution demonstrated these opposing kinase functions: [cyclin-dependent kinase](../../enzyme.md#cyclin-dependent-kinase) could inhibit [DNA helicase](../../enzyme.md#dna-helicase) loading while promoting assembly of active replication machinery after licensing, and [Dbf4-dependent kinase](../../enzyme.md#dbf4-dependent-kinase) supplied another required input. Ordered addition, component omission and kinase-dependence experiments establish biochemical requirements more directly than colocalisation in an [S phase](../../cell-cycle.md#s-phase) [cell nucleus](../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus).[Nature 519, pp. 431–435; origin-dependent reconstitution and regulation of loading versus firing](../../biology-bibliography.md#regulated-eukaryotic-dna-replication-origin-firing-with-purified-proteins-by-joseph-t-p-yeeles-and-others) This protects a multi-origin [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) against [DNA rereplication](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-rereplication). Not every licensed origin fires: neighbouring [replication forks](../../molecular-genetics.md#replication-fork) may replicate a licensed site passively, and unused licensed origins can provide reserve initiation capacity under stress.

The two helicases are distinct molecular solutions. In the conventional comparison, bacterial [DnaB](../../molecular-genetics.md#dnab) translocates on the [lagging strand](../../molecular-genetics.md#lagging-strand) template, whereas the [CMG helicase](../../molecular-genetics.md#cmg-helicase) travels on the [leading strand](../../molecular-genetics.md#leading-strand) template. Their associated [proteins](../../protein.md) also differ. [E. coli](../../organism.md#escherichia-coli) uses [DNA polymerase III](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-polymerase-iii) with the [beta sliding clamp](../../molecular-genetics.md#beta-sliding-clamp), a [clamp loader](../../molecular-genetics.md#clamp-loader), [DnaG](../../molecular-genetics.md#dnag) primase and [single-stranded DNA-binding protein](../../protein.md#single-stranded-dna-binding-protein). In conventional nuclear [eukaryotic](../../organism.md#eukaryote) replication, [DNA polymerase alpha-primase](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-polymerase-alpha-primase) begins synthesis, [DNA polymerase epsilon](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-polymerase-epsilon) primarily extends the [leading strand](../../molecular-genetics.md#leading-strand) and [DNA polymerase delta](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-polymerase-delta) primarily extends the [lagging strand](../../molecular-genetics.md#lagging-strand), supported by [PCNA](../../molecular-genetics.md#pcna), [RFC](../../molecular-genetics.md#rfc) and [replication protein A](../../protein.md#replication-protein-a). These are predominant assignments in the standard system, not an assertion that polymerases can never exchange roles during initiation, repair or stress.

Both systems must repeatedly make and mature [Okazaki fragments](../../molecular-genetics.md#okazaki-fragment). In [E. coli](../../organism.md#escherichia-coli), [DnaG](../../molecular-genetics.md#dnag) supplies an [RNA primer](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-primer), and [DNA polymerase I](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-polymerase-i) participates in primer removal and gap filling before [DNA ligase](../../enzyme.md#dna-ligase) seals the backbone. Nuclear [eukaryotic](../../organism.md#eukaryote) initiation uses a short [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) segment followed by [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) made by [DNA polymerase alpha-primase](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-polymerase-alpha-primase). Subsequent strand-displacement synthesis, [FEN1](../../enzyme.md#fen1) and other processing activities remove primer-containing material, and [DNA ligase](../../enzyme.md#dna-ligase) completes joining. Eukaryotic fragments are generally shorter and their formation is integrated with [nucleosome](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) assembly; bacterial and eukaryotic [lagging strand](../../molecular-genetics.md#lagging-strand) maturation should not be reduced to identical [protein](../../protein.md) names with different labels.

A shared [replication fork](../../molecular-genetics.md#replication-fork) architecture does not imply a perfectly rigid, synchronised machine. [single-molecule fluorescence microscopy](../../fluorescence.md#single-molecule-fluorescence-microscopy) of reconstituted [E. coli](../../organism.md#escherichia-coli) replication found that leading- and [lagging strand](../../molecular-genetics.md#lagging-strand) polymerases could pause and change rates independently, while [DNA helicase](../../enzyme.md#dna-helicase) behaviour allowed recoupling. This shows why similar average synthesis rates do not prove lockstep molecular progression.[Cell 169, pp. 1201–1213.e17; real-time single-molecule trajectories and independent polymerase dynamics](../../biology-bibliography.md#independent-and-stochastic-action-of-dna-polymerases-in-the-replisome-by-james-e-graham-and-others) Omission controls establish which reaction requires a component, but a purified short-template assay cannot reproduce every obstacle of a living [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome).

Chromosome context creates further differences. Nuclear [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) is packaged into [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin), requiring [nucleosome](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) disassembly and reassembly and coordination with replication-associated inheritance of chromatin states. Multiple linear [chromosomes](../../genetics.md#chromosome) also create an [end-replication problem](../../molecular-genetics.md#end-replication-problem), commonly addressed by [telomerase](../../enzyme.md#telomerase) and specialised [telomere](../../genetics.md#telomere) maintenance. Bacterial circular [chromosomes](../../genetics.md#chromosome) avoid that particular end problem but still require resolution of [DNA supercoiling](../../polymer.md#dna-supercoiling), [DNA catenanes](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-catenane) and [chromosome dimers](../../genetics.md#chromosome-dimer). [DNA topoisomerases](../../enzyme.md#dna-topoisomerase) manage topology in both groups. Converging [replication forks](../../molecular-genetics.md#replication-fork) terminate replication in both; the [Tus-Ter system](../../molecular-genetics.md#tus-ter-system) is a specific feature of certain bacteria, including [E. coli](../../organism.md#escherichia-coli), rather than a universal bacterial termination mechanism.

Finally, replication is embedded in different cellular schedules. Bacterial synthesis shares the cellular compartment with [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) and must manage collisions with it; nuclear [eukaryotic](../../organism.md#eukaryote) replication also encounters [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) and other obstacles, despite the separation of nuclear [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology) from cytoplasmic [translation](../../geometry-and-topology.md#translation-geometry). [ATR](../../enzyme.md#atr)–[Chk1](../../enzyme.md#chk1) signalling in many [eukaryotes](../../organism.md#eukaryote) couples replication stress to origin use and cell-cycle progression. Bacteria use their own damage and fork-recovery programmes, including the [SOS response](../../molecular-genetics.md#sos-response). Neither system is simply unregulated.

[Archaea](../../organism.md#archaea) should not be grouped indiscriminately with [bacteria](../../organism.md#bacteria) in this comparison: their lack of a [cell nucleus](../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus) does not make their replication machinery bacterial, and several core components have closer relationships to the eukaryotic machinery. Comparative experiments should therefore ask separately about initiation, elongation, fidelity, chromatin handling and completion. Measure newly synthesised products as well as [protein](../../protein.md) recruitment, verify that an apparent initiation defect is not degradation of the template, and distinguish a [replication fork](../../molecular-genetics.md#replication-fork)'s instantaneous speed from whole-genome completion time. **The deepest difference is how distinct molecular machines are organised around [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) architecture and cell-cycle control; the underlying templated chemistry and the need to preserve complete [genomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) are shared.**

<h4 id="section-ii/5/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#section-ii/5)

**Recurrent drivers reflect repeated selection of accessible changes in shared cellular control systems, filtered by [biological tissue](../../biology.md#tissue-biology) context and by how [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation) arise.** The same individual [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation), different alterations of one [gene](../../genetics.md#gene), and changes in different components of one pathway are three distinct kinds of recurrence. They should be separated before proposing an explanation.

All proliferating human [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) must regulate growth, survival, resource use and inheritance of their [genomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome). Disabling a restraint or making a growth-promoting signal constitutive can therefore confer an advantage in more than one [biological tissue](../../biology.md#tissue-biology). Activating [KRAS](../../genetics.md#kras) or [BRAF](../../genetics.md#braf) can increase [RAS-MAPK signalling](../../signal-transduction.md#ras-mapk-signalling); activating [PIK3CA](../../genetics.md#pik3ca) can increase [PI3K-AKT signalling](../../signal-transduction.md#pi3k-akt-signalling); disruption of [TP53](../../genetics.md#tp53) or the [RB pathway](../../cell-cycle.md#rb-pathway) can weaken responses that normally restrain proliferation. A relatively limited set of regulatory bottlenecks is reused across many differentiated [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) types. Pan-cancer analysis consequently finds repeated alteration of canonical growth and survival pathways, with frequencies and combinations differing across [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) lineages.[Cell 173, pp. 321–337.e10; pathway alterations across tumour types, co-occurrence and mutual exclusivity](../../biology-bibliography.md#oncogenic-signaling-pathways-in-the-cancer-genome-atlas-by-francisco-sanchez-vega-and-others)

[positive selection](../../biology.md#positive-selection) amplifies advantageous clones after the initiating change occurs. A [driver mutation](../../genetics.md#driver-mutation) raises reproductive success in a particular cellular environment; a [passenger mutation](../../genetics.md#passenger-mutation) can accompany the expanding clone without causing that advantage. Independent [tumours](../../biology.md#tumour) can therefore converge on similar solutions even when their other [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation) differ. The relevant advantage can be escape from growth restraint, suppression of [apoptosis](../../biological-cell.md#apoptosis), tolerance of stress or survival after therapy, rather than a simple increase in division rate. A driver at one stage need not be advantageous in every later environment.

Functional constraints help explain recurrence of exact positions. An [oncogene](../../genetics.md#oncogene) often needs an altered [protein](../../protein.md) that retains a useful structure while escaping regulation. Certain [KRAS](../../genetics.md#kras) substitutions impair normal switching toward the inactive state; [BRAF](../../genetics.md#braf) V600E promotes abnormal kinase activity; particular [PIK3CA](../../genetics.md#pik3ca) substitutions activate signalling. Many other changes in the same [proteins](../../protein.md) would merely damage them and provide no comparable benefit. By contrast, a [tumour suppressor gene](../../genetics.md#tumour-suppressor-gene) may be disabled by many different nonsense, frameshift, splice-site or deletion events. Consequently, clustered activating hotspots and dispersed inactivating changes can produce different [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) patterns even when both [genes](../../genetics.md#gene) are recurrently selected. Some [TP53](../../genetics.md#tp53) missense alleles also have dominant-negative or additional context-dependent effects, so not every tumour-suppressor lesion fits an identical two-hit mechanism.

Recurrence is nevertheless generated by [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) supply as well as [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness). Local [nucleotide sequence](../../polymer.md#nucleic-acid-sequence), methylated [CpG sites](../../molecular-genetics.md#cpg-site), exposure-related damage, [DNA repair](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-repair) and replication-associated processes make some changes more likely than others. A hotspot can therefore recur partly because it is easy to generate. Large [genes](../../genetics.md#gene), late-replicating regions and variable background [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) burdens can yield apparently recurrent passengers if the null model assumes uniform mutability. Analyses allowing for such heterogeneity substantially change which [genes](../../genetics.md#gene) are identified as unusually mutated.[Nature 499, pp. 214–218; mutation-rate variation, replication timing, transcription and background-rate correction](../../biology-bibliography.md#mutational-heterogeneity-in-cancer-and-the-search-for-new-cancer-associated-genes-by-michael-s-lawrence-and-others)

A conceptual decomposition is useful: the number of observed successful clones carrying an alteration depends on the number of opportunities for it to arise, its context-dependent chance of establishing an expanding lineage, and the chance of detecting that lineage. Thus counting cases with a [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) does not isolate its selective advantage. Gene-level recurrence tests should control [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) opportunity and sequence context; [dN/dS](../../molecular-genetics.md#dn-ds) analyses compare observed nonsynonymous changes with a suitably calibrated expectation from synonymous changes. Applied to somatic evolution, such approaches provide evidence for selection beyond raw [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) counts.[Cell 171, pp. 1029–1041.e21; context-aware somatic dN/dS and driver-associated positive selection](../../biology-bibliography.md#universal-patterns-of-selection-in-cancer-and-somatic-tissues-by-inigo-martincorena-and-others) The assumptions still require scrutiny: sequencing errors, biased ascertainment and an incorrect background model can mimic enrichment.

Tissue context explains why recurrence is broad but not universal. A particular [cell of origin](../../biological-cell.md#cell-of-origin) expresses a particular set of receptors, lineage regulators and feedback circuits. The same [driver mutation](../../genetics.md#driver-mutation) can cooperate with one background, be redundant in another, and cause intolerable stress in a third. [Epistasis](../../genetics.md#epistasis) and [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) order alter its effect. Apparent mutual exclusivity can be consistent with alternative routes to the same pathway output, but it can also result from tumour-subtype structure or limited statistical power; it does not by itself prove that a double mutant is lethal. Similarly, co-occurrence can suggest cooperation without establishing a direct biochemical interaction.

The consequences of a shared lesion can also differ after treatment. An experimental screen in [BRAF](../../genetics.md#braf) V600E colorectal-cancer [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) identified [epidermal growth factor receptor](../../protein.md#epidermal-growth-factor-receptor) feedback as an explanation for weak response to BRAF inhibition compared with the response seen in melanoma. Interfering with the feedback route improved pathway suppression in the tested models. This illustrates that the same driver is embedded in different circuits; recurrence across [biological tissues](../../biology.md#tissue-biology) does not imply identical drug sensitivity.[Nature 483, pp. 100–103; RNA-interference screen, EGFR feedback and combined pathway perturbation](../../biology-bibliography.md#unresponsiveness-of-colon-cancer-to-braf-v600e-inhibition-through-feedback-activation-of-egfr-by-anirudh-prahallad-and-others) This is a mechanistic historical example, not a claim that the treatment landscape remains as it was in 2012.

Functional validation should recreate the candidate alteration at its endogenous locus in several relevant cellular backgrounds, compare it with matched wild-type and synonymous controls, and test growth under competitive conditions. Repairing the allele or restoring the affected restraint provides a complementary loss-of-advantage test. Use physiological expression: massive overexpression can make a normally weak effect appear transforming. Measure pathway output, survival and [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) formation separately, and test cooperation with plausible partner lesions. A dependency in an established [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) demonstrates a maintenance requirement, which need not be identical to the alteration's original role in initiation.

**The explanation is convergent somatic evolution acting on shared biological machinery, with [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) accessibility, functional constraints and lineage-specific interactions determining which solutions recur.** Genomic recurrence identifies candidates; calibrated statistics and controlled experiments establish which recurrent changes are causal and under what conditions.

**Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition can help [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) change adhesion, move, survive stress and enter new environments, but its contribution depends on the state reached and the stage of progression.** [Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition](../../organism.md#epithelial-to-mesenchymal-transition) is a regulated change of phenotype and behaviour, often reversible. It should not be equated with every instance of invasion, with a single marker, or with a permanent conversion of a [carcinoma](../../biology.md#carcinoma) into an unrelated [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) lineage.

An [epithelial cell](../../biological-cell.md#epithelial-cell) normally maintains apical–basal [cell polarity](../../biological-cell.md#cell-polarity), organised [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md)–[biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) contacts and interactions with a [basement membrane](../../biology.md#basement-membrane). During a transition, regulatory factors including [Snail](../../molecular-genetics.md#snail), [ZEB1](../../molecular-genetics.md#zeb1) and [Twist1](../../molecular-genetics.md#twist1) can alter expression of adhesion and cytoskeletal programmes. Reduced [E-cadherin](../../protein.md#e-cadherin)-mediated adhesion, junctional reorganisation and changed [integrin](../../cell-membrane.md#integrin) interactions can permit [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) to detach or rearrange while responding to their surroundings. [TGF-beta signalling](../../signal-transduction.md#tgf-beta-signalling), growth-factor pathways, inflammation and mechanical conditions can supply inducing inputs. Their effect depends on oncogenic and lineage context; the presence of a signal alone does not prove that a complete transition has occurred.

The clearest immediate advantage can be local dissemination. Altered adhesion and motility allow [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) to move through [biological tissue](../../biology.md#tissue-biology), interact with matrix tracks and reach vessels. A classic murine mammary-tumour study linked [Twist1](../../molecular-genetics.md#twist1) suppression to reduced [metastasis](../../biology.md#metastasis) and linked ectopic activity to loss of epithelial adhesion and increased motility. Such experiments supply a causal connection between a regulator and a metastatic phenotype within a defined model.[Cell 117, pp. 927–939; Twist suppression, adhesion changes and mammary-to-lung metastasis assays](../../biology-bibliography.md#twist-a-master-regulator-of-morphogenesis-plays-an-essential-role-in-tumor-metastasis-by-jing-yang-and-others) To attribute the result specifically to motility, rather than fewer viable [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), compare primary growth, survival and the number of [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) entering the circulation.

Dissemination has several stages: local invasion, [intravasation](../../biology.md#intravasation), survival in circulation, [extravasation](../../biology.md#extravasation) and growth in a distant organ. A transition that helps the first stage need not help the last. In an inducible squamous-carcinoma model, activation of [Twist1](../../molecular-genetics.md#twist1) promoted dissemination, whereas turning it off at distant sites allowed proliferation and metastatic outgrowth. This demonstrates a stage-dependent benefit of [epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity](../../organism.md#epithelial-mesenchymal-plasticity) rather than an obligatory advantage of staying fully mesenchymal.[Cancer Cell 22, pp. 725–736; inducible Twist1, dissemination and reversal during metastatic outgrowth](../../biology-bibliography.md#spatiotemporal-regulation-of-epithelial-mesenchymal-transition-is-essential-for-squamous-cell-carcinoma-metastasis-by-jeff-h-tsai-and-others) [mesenchymal-to-epithelial transition](../../organism.md#mesenchymal-to-epithelial-transition) can consequently contribute to colonisation, although its necessity is not universal across cancers.

Intermediate states are important. A [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) may retain epithelial contacts while acquiring motility, producing collective invasion or mixed epithelial–mesenchymal features. These states cannot be placed reliably on one axis using [E-cadherin](../../protein.md#e-cadherin) or [vimentin](../../protein.md#vimentin) alone. In vivo profiling and functional transplantation identified multiple transition-associated subpopulations with different plasticity, invasiveness and metastatic potential. Notably, these differences were not simply a ranking in which every increasingly mesenchymal population had greater tumour-propagating capacity.[Nature 556, pp. 463–468; phenotypic subpopulations, plasticity and metastatic-potential comparisons](../../biology-bibliography.md#identification-of-the-tumour-transition-states-occurring-during-emt-by-ievgenia-pastushenko-and-others) The result argues for measuring behaviour and state jointly, rather than treating a score for [epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition](../../organism.md#epithelial-to-mesenchymal-transition) as a universal measure of malignancy.

Another contribution is treatment persistence. Transition-associated states can change proliferation, survival signalling and response to environmental stress, allowing some [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) to survive treatment and later re-expand. In a breast-cancer lineage-tracing study, [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) marked by the selected mesenchymal reporters were not required to account for all observed lung [metastases](../../biology.md#metastasis), but were associated with survival after chemotherapy and recurrent disease. The findings separate a contribution to [chemoresistance](../../biology.md#chemoresistance) from a universal requirement for metastatic seeding.[Nature 527, pp. 472–476; mesenchymal lineage reporters, lung metastases and chemotherapy-surviving populations](../../biology-bibliography.md#epithelial-to-mesenchymal-transition-is-not-required-for-lung-metastasis-but-contributes-to-chemoresistance-by-kari-r-fischer-and-others) A surviving population may have been selected from a pre-existing state or induced by therapy; time-resolved tracing is needed to distinguish these possibilities.

Transition-associated changes can also alter communication with the [tumour microenvironment](../../biology.md#tumour-microenvironment), including immune-cell recruitment or inhibitory signalling in particular settings. Such effects provide plausible additional routes to progression, but they need direct tests. A correlation between an expression signature for [epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition](../../organism.md#epithelial-to-mesenchymal-transition) and low [T cell](../../biological-cell.md#t-cell) activity could instead reflect shared induction by [TGF-beta signalling](../../signal-transduction.md#tgf-beta-signalling) or contamination of a bulk sample by [fibroblasts](../../biological-cell.md#fibroblast). Cell-type-resolved measurements are essential before assigning a stromal transcript to a transitioning malignant [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md).

Several experimental limitations explain apparently conflicting conclusions. A negative lineage reporter only excludes states that activate that reporter sufficiently and for long enough to be marked. Different [transcription factors](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-factor) can substitute for each other, and loss of one factor need not abolish every relevant transition. Conversely, a regulator of [epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition](../../organism.md#epithelial-to-mesenchymal-transition) can affect survival or [cell proliferation](../../biological-cell.md#cell-proliferation) independently of the adhesion programme. Therefore, reduced [metastasis](../../biology.md#metastasis) after its deletion does not automatically identify the missing step. Orthogonal lineage markers, multi-marker [protein](../../protein.md) and [RNA](../../polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) assays, morphology and live behaviour should be combined.

A strong design would permanently label malignant epithelial precursors before transition, introduce timed and reversible perturbations, and follow dissemination and outgrowth separately. Rescue with a perturbation-resistant construct helps establish specificity; matched primary [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) burden avoids confusing a smaller source [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) with reduced dissemination efficiency. Compare spontaneous [metastasis](../../biology.md#metastasis) with experimental vascular injection: injection bypasses local invasion and [intravasation](../../biology.md#intravasation), so it cannot test their requirement. Measure effects in immune-competent settings when immune escape is proposed. Count viable disseminated [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) before counting final [metastases](../../biology.md#metastasis) to separate arrival, survival and expansion.

These distinctions also complicate therapeutic inference. Inhibiting a transition might reduce escape from the primary [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) yet favour epithelial outgrowth of [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) already disseminated; enforcing a mesenchymal state might restrict growth but leave a persistent reservoir. Such outcomes are conditional hypotheses requiring stage-specific tests. **The main contribution is flexible deployment of partially separable programmes for dissemination and persistence, with reversibility allowing [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) to meet different demands during progression.** A complete EMT is neither a universal prerequisite for [metastasis](../../biology.md#metastasis) nor a sufficient description of all aggressive [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) behaviour.

<h4 id="section-ii/7/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#section-ii/7)

**Solid-tumour immunotherapy must overcome a chain of barriers: identify suitable targets, generate effective immune [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), deliver them into every relevant lesion, sustain their function and prevent escape without damaging normal [biological tissue](../../biology.md#tissue-biology).** Failure at any one step can limit treatment. [immune checkpoint blockade](../../biology.md#immune-checkpoint-blockade), [adoptive T-cell therapy](../../biology.md#adoptive-t-cell-therapy) and [cancer vaccines](../../biology.md#cancer-vaccine) address different parts of this chain and therefore face overlapping but non-identical challenges.

Successful treatment is possible, so the problem is not a universal inability of immunity to affect solid [tumours](../../biology.md#tumour). For example, a randomised trial in advanced melanoma found longer [progression-free survival](../../biology.md#progression-free-survival) with expanded [tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes](../../biological-cell.md#tumour-infiltrating-lymphocyte) than with [ipilimumab](../../biology.md#ipilimumab). The regimen also included preparative [lymphodepletion](../../biology.md#lymphodepletion) and subsequent [interleukin-2](../../signal-transduction.md#interleukin-2), so benefit and toxicity belong to the treatment package rather than the transferred [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) alone.[New England Journal of Medicine 387, pp. 2113–2125; randomised design, treatment regimen, progression-free-survival endpoint and adverse events](../../biology-bibliography.md#tumor-infiltrating-lymphocyte-therapy-or-ipilimumab-in-advanced-melanoma-by-maartje-w-rohaan-and-others) A result in a selected melanoma population cannot simply be transferred to every solid-tumour lineage.

The first challenge is target quality. Many [tumour-associated antigens](../../protein.md#tumour-associated-antigen) are also present in essential normal [biological tissues](../../biology.md#tissue-biology), whereas truly tumour-specific [neoantigens](../../protein.md#neoantigen) can vary between patients and subclones. [T-cell receptors](../../protein.md#t-cell-receptor) recognise peptides presented by compatible [HLA](../../biology.md#hla) molecules, requiring appropriate processing and presentation. A [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) detected in [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) does not guarantee expression, processing or presentation of an immunogenic peptide. [chimeric antigen receptors](../../protein.md#chimeric-antigen-receptor) can recognise a cell-surface target without peptide–HLA presentation, but conventional designs cannot directly access most intracellular [proteins](../../protein.md). Thus broader target access, [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) coverage and specificity cannot all be assumed from the same targeting strategy.

Even a promising target may be heterogeneous. Cells expressing little antigen can escape a single-target therapy, and different [metastases](../../biology.md#metastasis) may present different targets or immune environments. Targeting several antigens could reduce escape but can also increase the normal-tissue recognition problem. Multi-region sampling and single-cell measurements improve coverage estimates; one positive biopsy is not proof that all clonogenic [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) are targetable. A relevant experiment tests killing over a measured range of target densities, including normal [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), rather than only a high-expression laboratory line.

Immune activation and entry are separate obstacles. [dendritic cells](../../biological-cell.md#dendritic-cell) must acquire and present antigen with appropriate activating signals, and responsive [T cells](../../biological-cell.md#t-cell) must expand and reach the [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour). Abnormal vessels, inadequate [chemokine](../../signal-transduction.md#chemokine) matching and dense or reorganised [extracellular matrix](../../biology.md#extracellular-matrix) can prevent effective entry. A [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) may contain many lymphocytes restricted to the surrounding stroma, with few contacting malignant [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md). Studies linking a stromal [TGF-beta signalling](../../signal-transduction.md#tgf-beta-signalling) programme to poor response also showed in mouse models that combined pathway and [PD-L1](../../protein.md#pd-l1) blockade improved intratumoural access and antitumour activity. The causal combination experiment strengthens an inference that human expression correlations alone could not establish.[Nature 554, pp. 544–548; immune-excluded human tumours and combination perturbations in mouse models](../../biology-bibliography.md#tgf-beta-attenuates-tumour-response-to-pd-l1-blockade-by-contributing-to-exclusion-of-t-cells-by-sanjeev-mariathasan-and-others)

Once inside, immune [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) face [hypoxia](../../biology.md#hypoxia), nutrient competition, inhibitory metabolites and suppressive signals from [regulatory T cells](../../biological-cell.md#regulatory-t-cell), [tumour-associated macrophages](../../biological-cell.md#tumour-associated-macrophage) and other stromal populations. Persistent antigen stimulation can establish [T-cell exhaustion](../../biology.md#t-cell-exhaustion), with impaired function and a regulatory state that is not always reversed by blocking one inhibitory receptor. [PD-1](../../protein.md#pd-1) or [CTLA-4](../../protein.md#ctla-4) blockade can release important restraints, but cannot reliably restore missing antigen presentation, compensate for absent tumour-reactive clones or abolish every suppressive pathway. Hence a high density of [PD-L1](../../protein.md#pd-l1) or a large [tumour mutational burden](../../molecular-genetics.md#tumour-mutational-burden) can be informative without being a deterministic response predictor.

Tumours also evolve under therapy. Paired pretreatment and relapse samples from a small melanoma series identified acquired defects involving [JAK1](../../enzyme.md#jak1), [JAK2](../../enzyme.md#jak2) or [beta-2-microglobulin](../../protein.md#beta-2-microglobulin). These connected resistance with impaired [interferon-gamma signalling](../../signal-transduction.md#interferon-gamma-signalling) or [MHC class I](../../biology.md#mhc-class-i) presentation, and functional assays helped interpret the genetic findings. They provide concrete routes by which a previously responsive [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) can escape; a selected four-patient series cannot estimate how often each mechanism explains resistance generally.[New England Journal of Medicine 375, pp. 819–829; paired baseline/relapse biopsies and functional analysis of interferon and antigen-presentation defects](../../biology-bibliography.md#mutations-associated-with-acquired-resistance-to-pd-1-blockade-in-melanoma-by-jesse-m-zaretsky-and-others) Some escape routes are shared across therapies; others depend on whether recognition uses a peptide–HLA complex or a directly bound surface antigen.

Safety constrains how strongly these barriers can be attacked. [immune checkpoint blockade](../../biology.md#immune-checkpoint-blockade) can produce [immune-related adverse events](../../biology.md#immune-related-adverse-event) in normal organs, while transferred immune [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) can produce severe inflammatory responses or recognise normal [biological tissues](../../biology.md#tissue-biology) bearing the target. An early [ERBB2](../../genetics.md#erbb2)-directed [chimeric antigen receptor](../../protein.md#chimeric-antigen-receptor) study reported fatal toxicity; recognition of low-level target in lung epithelium was the authors' proposed explanation, rather than a definitively isolated causal mechanism. It demonstrates why high [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) expression and selective killing in a simple culture assay are insufficient safety evidence.[Molecular Therapy 18, pp. 843–851; clinical course, cytokine measurements and proposed normal-tissue recognition mechanism](../../biology-bibliography.md#case-report-of-a-serious-adverse-event-following-the-administration-of-t-cells-transduced-with-a-chimeric-antigen-receptor-recognizing-erbb2-by-richard-a-morgan-and-others) Candidate safeguards include restricted receptor activation, controllable persistence and elimination switches, but each trades activity or durability against risk and requires direct validation.

There are practical challenges as well. Autologous [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) therapy requires accessible [biological tissue](../../biology.md#tissue-biology) or [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), reproducible manufacture and expansion without losing effective function. A patient may deteriorate before the product is ready. Conditioning and supportive regimens add burdens; local delivery may improve access to one site while missing distant disease. Vaccines face time needed for priming and the possibility that newly activated [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) still cannot enter or function within the [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour). Combining therapies is mechanistically attractive but can increase toxicity, cost and uncertainty about which component supplies benefit.

Evaluation must therefore match the proposed mechanism. Test antigen recognition with antigen-negative and normal-tissue controls, measure trafficking and spatial contact separately from killing, and monitor immune-cell persistence and function over time. Paired and multi-region biopsies can distinguish absent entry from failed recognition or evolved escape. Clinical trials need appropriate comparators, durable disease-control endpoints and toxicity reporting, not response rate alone. **The central challenge is to make recognition, access and sustained function coincide across a heterogeneous evolving [tumour](../../biology.md#tumour) while preserving normal [biological tissue](../../biology.md#tissue-biology); no single biomarker or immune intervention guarantees all three.**
