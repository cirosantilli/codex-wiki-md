<h4 id="section-1/1/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#section-1/1/a)

**Partial proteolysis is one plausible explanation:** intact [SNAP25](../../cell-membrane.md#snap-25) and a slightly shortened form would migrate as two nearby bands in [SDS-PAGE](../../biology.md#sds-page). Heterogeneous processing, a retained [affinity tag](../../protein.md#affinity-tag), or an anomalously migrating species are other possibilities. The gel alone does not identify the chemical difference. Because the [proteins](../../protein.md) were expressed in [Escherichia coli](../../organism.md#escherichia-coli), a mammalian [post-translational modification](../../protein.md#post-translational-modification) should not simply be assumed. A [Western blot](../../protein.md#western-blot) establishing that both bands contain [SNAP25](../../cell-membrane.md#snap-25), followed by [mass spectrometry](../../chemistry.md#mass-spectrometry), could distinguish these explanations.

<h4 id="section-1/1/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-1/1/b)

The [liposomes](../../mathematical-biology.md#liposome) could be separated from free [proteins](../../protein.md) by [density-gradient flotation](../../biology.md#density-gradient-flotation): lipid-containing particles float to a different position from soluble [protein](../../protein.md). [Size-exclusion chromatography](../../chromatography.md#size-exclusion-chromatography) is another possibility if it resolves the large vesicles from the unincorporated [proteins](../../protein.md). Simple sedimentation can also collect sufficiently large vesicles, but [protein](../../protein.md) aggregates may sediment too. The important control is to show that the recovered [SNARE proteins](../../cell-membrane.md#snare-protein) associate with the [lipid bilayer](../../mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer), rather than merely copurifying with it.

<h4 id="section-1/1/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#section-1/1/c)

The recovered vesicle fractions contain bands at the expected positions for the supplied [SNARE proteins](../../cell-membrane.md#snare-protein). The [v-SNARE](../../cell-membrane.md#v-snare) preparation contains its principal band, and the [t-SNARE](../../cell-membrane.md#t-snare) preparation contains both [t-SNARE](../../cell-membrane.md#t-snare) and the [SNAP25](../../cell-membrane.md#snap-25) doublet. Thus **protein-containing vesicle fractions have been recovered**, consistent with successful reconstitution.

The [SDS-PAGE](../../biology.md#sds-page) experiment does not establish the orientation of the [proteins](../../protein.md), their proportion facing outward, the [stoichiometry](../../chemical-reaction.md#stoichiometry) in an individual vesicle, or [membrane fusion](../../cell-membrane.md#membrane-fusion). Nor does a stained band prove that all of its [protein](../../protein.md) is correctly folded and active. Those are separate functional and [membrane topology](../../cell-membrane.md#membrane-topology) questions.

<h4 id="section-1/1/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#section-1/1/d)

After warming, more labelled [v-SNARE](../../cell-membrane.md#v-snare) remains full length: lane 2 is approximately 80–85% of the reference signal, compared with about 40–45% in lane 1. Formation of a protected [SNARE complex](../../cell-membrane.md#snare-complex) provides a consistent explanation: the susceptible part of [v-SNARE](../../cell-membrane.md#v-snare) is less accessible after complex assembly.

Protection is not itself a unique readout of [membrane fusion](../../cell-membrane.md#membrane-fusion). Some [protein](../../protein.md) may face the vesicle lumen, and [SNARE complexes](../../cell-membrane.md#snare-complex) can protect [protein](../../protein.md) without proving that the two aqueous compartments have merged. The inference depends on comparing these alternatives with the detergent and partner-free controls.

<h4 id="section-1/1/e">e</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/e/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#section-1/1/e)

In lane 3, detergent removes the [lipid bilayer](../../mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) barrier while both partner preparations are present. Their binding regions can then encounter one another even if they originally faced inward or were on poorly apposed [lipid bilayers](../../mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer). The strong remaining full-length signal is consistent with extensive formation of protected [SNARE complexes](../../cell-membrane.md#snare-complex).

Thus **detergent does not prevent partner association under the displayed conditions**; it removes the topological restriction on that association. This lane is the normalization reference, not a direct measurement that 100% of all initially supplied [protein](../../protein.md) is active. The weak signal in the detergent-treated partner-free control also argues against detergent simply preventing all cleavage.

<h4 id="section-1/1/f">f</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/f/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#section-1/1/f)

Lane 4 combines detergent with the absence of the [t-SNARE](../../cell-membrane.md#t-snare) preparation. The [lipid bilayer](../../mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) no longer protects inward-facing [v-SNARE](../../cell-membrane.md#v-snare), and there is no [t-SNARE](../../cell-membrane.md#t-snare) partner to provide complex-dependent protection. Consequently little full-length labelled [protein](../../protein.md) remains. This distinguishes **membrane-dependent protection** from **partner-dependent protection** in a [protease protection assay](../../protein.md#protease-protection-assay). It also helps interpret lane 3: its strong signal requires the partner rather than [lipid bilayer](../../mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) enclosure alone.

<h4 id="section-1/1/g">g</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/g/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [G](#section-1/1/g)

Use a [membrane topology](../../cell-membrane.md#membrane-topology) measurement on [v-SNARE](../../cell-membrane.md#v-snare) vesicles alone, so that partner binding cannot mask an outward-facing domain. A membrane-impermeant probe directed against the binding-domain region would label only the exposed population while vesicles remain intact. Compare that signal with total accessible [protein](../../protein.md) after [lipid bilayer](../../mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) disruption, using a trapped-lumen marker to check that the intact vesicles exclude the probe.

With comparable detection efficiencies and background subtraction,

$$
\boxed{f_{\rm outward}=\frac{S_{\rm intact}-S_{\rm background}}{S_{\rm permeabilized}-S_{\rm background}}.}
$$

An analogous [protease protection assay](../../protein.md#protease-protection-assay) compares external accessibility with total accessibility. In either approach, [lipid bilayer](../../mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) leakage and steric masking must be distinguished from genuinely inward-facing [protein](../../protein.md).

<h4 id="section-1/1/h">h</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/h/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [H](#section-1/1/h)

[NBD](../../fluorescence.md#nbd) and rhodamine are initially close together in the labelled vesicle [lipid bilayer](../../mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer), allowing [Förster resonance energy transfer](../../biology.md#forster-resonance-energy-transfer) and suppressing donor [fluorescence](../../fluorescence.md). Mixing labelled [lipid bilayer](../../mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) with unlabelled [lipid bilayer](../../mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) dilutes the dyes, decreases energy transfer and increases the [NBD](../../fluorescence.md#nbd) signal.

The rise with the complementary [SNARE proteins](../../cell-membrane.md#snare-protein), but little rise with protein-free vesicles, therefore supports **SNARE-dependent lipid mixing**. It is consistent with [membrane fusion](../../cell-membrane.md#membrane-fusion), but does not by itself distinguish complete fusion from [hemifusion](../../cell-membrane.md#hemifusion) or other causes of dye dilution. A separate [content mixing assay](../../cell-membrane.md#content-mixing-assay) with a leakage control would test merger of the aqueous compartments.

<h4 id="section-1/1/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#section-1/1/i)

**There is no evidence for substantial lipid mixing during the cold preincubation.** The trace begins near its low-fluorescence baseline when warming starts; a substantial pre-existing dilution of the dyes would already have raised that baseline. Nevertheless, [vesicle docking](../../cell-membrane.md#vesicle-docking) or [trans-SNARE complex](../../cell-membrane.md#trans-snare-complex) formation can accumulate while the later membrane-rearrangement step remains slow. Absence of dequenching does not mean that the vesicles have had no interaction.

<h4 id="section-1/1/j">j</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/j/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [J](#section-1/1/j)

Cold preincubation can accumulate docked, fusion-ready intermediates. On warming, that population proceeds promptly to lipid mixing, whereas newly combined vesicles must first encounter, dock and assemble productive [SNARE complexes](../../cell-membrane.md#snare-complex). This explains the faster initial rise and larger signal at the displayed endpoint without requiring a different total amount of fluorescent lipid.

The original reconstitution experiments likewise distinguish docking from subsequent [membrane fusion](../../cell-membrane.md#membrane-fusion).[Docked unfused intermediates accumulated at low temperature and fused on warming](../../biology-bibliography.md#snarepins-minimal-machinery-for-membrane-fusion-by-t-weber-and-others)

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/k/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [K](#section-1/1/k)

Triton disperses the [lipid bilayer](../../mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) into detergent-containing particles, separating the fluorescent lipids enough to remove most [fluorescence quenching](../../fluorescence.md#quenching-fluorescence). Both traces approach the maximum-dilution reference even though their earlier lipid-mixing histories differed. **The final [fluorescence](../../fluorescence.md) jump is detergent-induced dequenching, not an additional burst of physiological [membrane fusion](../../cell-membrane.md#membrane-fusion).** It provides a useful normalization endpoint for the available dye signal.

<h4 id="section-1/1/m">m</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/m/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [M](#section-1/1/m)

The 1–15 fragment has a slightly lower mean degradation signal and perhaps lower invadopodium formation than the longer N-terminal fragment. **It may be the stronger inhibitor, but the displayed comparisons do not establish that difference.** The asterisks compare groups with control, not necessarily the two active fragments with one another. Their error bars overlap, and overlap alone is neither a definitive test of equality nor a calibrated significance test.

A direct comparison needs replicate-level data and a suitable [statistical hypothesis test](../../statistical-hypothesis-test.md), with an [effect size](../../statistical-inference.md#effect-size) and [confidence interval](../../statistical-inference.md#confidence-interval). Comparable fragment abundance, cell viability and cell number are also needed before interpreting a phenotypic difference as greater molecular potency.

<h4 id="section-1/1/n">n</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/n/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [N](#section-1/1/n)

Three complementary readouts would separate local matrix degradation from invasive and metastatic behaviour:

- A three-dimensional matrix invasion assay would ask whether viable cells actually penetrate an extracellular barrier, with cell number and proliferation controlled.
- Measuring cell-surface [MT1-MMP](../../protein.md#mt1-mmp) alongside its intracellular distribution would test the proposed cargo-trafficking mechanism rather than relying only on the gelatin endpoint.
- A matched in vivo tumour comparison could measure metastatic burden relative to primary tumour burden, distinguishing reduced dissemination from a general reduction in tumour growth.

Empty-vector and inactive-fragment controls are needed throughout. Neither reduced gelatin digestion alone nor fewer cells after treatment establishes a specific effect on [metastasis](../../biology.md#metastasis).

<h4 id="section-1/1/o">o</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/o/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [O](#section-1/1/o)

The fragment might sequester another factor required for [vesicle trafficking](../../biological-cell.md#vesicle-trafficking) or [actin](../../protein.md#actin) organization, rather than acting through [Munc18](../../protein.md#munc18). For example, binding a different SNARE-associated [protein](../../protein.md) could lower the [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) available for productive endogenous complexes. This is a possible [dominant-negative effect](../../protein.md#dominant-negative-effect), not evidence that the isolated N terminus contains the entire [SNARE](../../cell-membrane.md#snare-protein) interaction surface.

A less specific alternative is cellular stress or reduced viability. Those effects could suppress both invadopodium formation and matrix degradation without selectively inhibiting the proposed trafficking pathway.

<h4 id="section-1/1/p">p</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#section-1/1)

<h5 id="section-1/1/p/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [P](#section-1/1/p)

For the sequestration hypothesis, ask whether the fragment associates with the proposed alternative partner by [co-immunoprecipitation](../../biology.md#co-immunoprecipitation), then compare endogenous complex assembly and cargo localization. An association should be evaluated against empty-vector and inactive-fragment controls, and an independent interaction readout would help distinguish direct binding from membership of a larger complex.

A useful functional test is whether restoring the limiting partner rescues trafficking and invadopodium formation without changing fragment expression or viability. Rescue would support the hypothesis, although overexpression can itself alter trafficking. Binding evidence, the endogenous phenotype and rescue should therefore agree; any one alone is insufficient.

<h4 id="section-2/2/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#section-2/2/c)

Using the supplied cluster counts,

$$
\boxed{\frac{5.29\times10^5}{2.30\times10^6}=0.230\simeq23\%.}

The underlying large-scale analysis explicitly used structural clustering of predicted models.[Structural cluster construction and taxonomic distributions](../../biology-bibliography.md#clustering-predicted-structures-at-the-scale-of-the-known-protein-universe-by-inigo-barrio-hernandez-and-others)

<h4 id="section-2/2/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#section-2/2/d)

All three groups retain broadly shared structural solutions, while also containing more restricted clusters. The displayed domain-restricted counts are much larger for bacteria and eukaryotes, approximately $8.96\times10^5$ and $8.32\times10^5$, than for archaea, approximately $3.81\times10^4$. Restricted clusters also occur at lower taxonomic levels, consistent with continuing diversification rather than a single early episode of structural innovation.

**These counts are not direct rates of new-fold generation.** The groups differ in sampling, numbers of represented sequences and species, evolutionary depth, and detectability of relationships. Restriction to a lineage can reflect divergence, differential loss or incomplete sampling as well as origin in that lineage. The figure therefore supports unequal observed structural diversity, but does not by itself identify how rapidly each lineage invents new folds.

<h4 id="section-2/2/e">e</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/e/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#section-2/2/e)

There are

$$
20^{300}=10^{300\log_{10}20}\simeq2.04\times10^{390}
$$

possible sequences of length 300 over the standard twenty-amino-acid alphabet. Sampling $10^{20}$ sequences would cover only about $4.9\times10^{-371}$ of that space, even before allowing repeated sequences. **We cannot conclude that all possible [protein folds](../../protein.md#protein-fold) have been explored.**

However, this sequence count is not a count of folds: many sequences can share a fold, and many arbitrary sequences may not form a stable folded [protein](../../protein.md). Evolution also samples sequences non-uniformly. The calculation establishes vastly incomplete sequence-space exploration; proving either exhaustive or non-exhaustive coverage of physically realizable folds needs an additional model relating sequences to structures. The observed structural-cluster count alone supplies no such proof.

<h4 id="section-2/2/f">f</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/f/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#section-2/2/f)

[Protein structure](../../protein.md#protein-structure) can remain similar after extensive [protein sequence](../../protein.md#protein-sequence) divergence has obscured a sequence-only relationship. A structural comparison may therefore reveal a conserved [protein fold](../../protein.md#protein-fold), plausible binding pocket or spatial arrangement of candidate catalytic residues that a direct sequence search misses. Such evidence can suggest a functional family and useful hypotheses.

**A similar fold does not uniquely determine function.** The same architecture can support different activities, and a predicted pocket does not demonstrate binding or catalysis. Confidence in the model, residue-level chemistry and independent biochemical evidence must be considered together. Structural classification complements rather than replaces sequence and experimental analysis.

<h4 id="section-2/2/g">g</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/g/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [G](#section-2/2/g)

Reusable [protein domains](../../protein.md#protein-domain) make evolution combinatorial: a new domain combination can join an existing catalytic activity to a binding, localization or regulatory module without inventing every component anew. [Gene duplication](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-duplication) allows one copy to retain an established role while another diverges; [domain shuffling](../../protein.md#domain-shuffling) and fusion can create new combinations. Connecting domains can also increase effective local [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) of interacting sites or enable [allostery](../../protein.md#allostery).

These are potential advantages, not guarantees that every fusion is beneficial: domains must still fold and work together. The domain-level analysis behind this example found extensive reuse and new combinations of recognized structural units.[Domain classification and inter-domain packing relationships](../../biology-bibliography.md#exploring-structural-diversity-across-the-protein-universe-with-the-encyclopedia-of-domains-by-andy-m-lau-and-others)

<h4 id="section-2/2/h">h</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/h/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [H](#section-2/2/h)

In [surface plasmon resonance](../../biology.md#surface-plasmon-resonance), target [protein](../../protein.md) in the mobile phase passes over the immobilized [antibody](../../protein.md#antibody) domain. Binding changes the optical response near the sensor surface; response increases during association and falls when unbound target is washed away. The successive injections give larger responses as the supplied target [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) increases.

For a simple 1:1 model,

$$
\frac{dR}{dt}=k_{\rm on}C(R_{\max}-R)-k_{\rm off}R,\qquad K_D=\frac{k_{\rm off}}{k_{\rm on}}.
$$

The reported fit gives **$K_D=78\,\mathrm{nM}$**. This is a binding estimate for the tested preparation and model. Surface heterogeneity, transport limitations or rebinding could cause deviations; the visible mismatch between parts of the trace and fit discourages treating the binary model as exact.

<h4 id="section-2/2/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#section-2/2/i)

[Antibody specificity](../../protein.md#antibody-specificity) requires comparison with alternatives. Measure binding to unrelated [proteins](../../protein.md) and to appropriate related antigens under comparable conditions, with a reference surface controlling nonspecific sensor binding. Preferential binding to the intended target would support specificity within that tested panel.

Competition with a soluble target, or an independent interaction assay, can help establish that the signal reflects the intended interaction. Specificity for a particular [epitope](../../protein.md#epitope) is a stronger claim than specificity for the target [protein](../../protein.md) and needs evidence localizing the contact. A single good-looking binding curve establishes neither universal absence of cross-reactivity nor [epitope](../../protein.md#epitope) identity.

<h4 id="section-2/2/j">j</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/j/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [J](#section-2/2/j)

A complete bivalent [antibody](../../protein.md#antibody) could show **greater apparent [avidity](../../protein.md#avidity)**, provided both [protein binding sites](../../protein.md#protein-binding-site) can engage suitably spaced antigens on the particle. Dissociation of one arm then need not release the [antibody](../../protein.md#antibody) because the other arm retains it nearby. The observed release can consequently be slower than for an isolated monovalent domain.

This does not imply that the intrinsic single-site [antibody affinity](../../protein.md#antibody-affinity) has improved. Geometry, accessibility and the [antibody](../../protein.md#antibody) context could prevent bivalent engagement or even impair the isolated domain's interaction. Thus an altered apparent binding strength is plausible, but its direction and size cannot be inferred solely from conversion to a full [antibody](../../protein.md#antibody).

<h4 id="section-2/2/k">k</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/k/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [K](#section-2/2/k)

The unliganded and peptide-bound preparations elute at almost the same volume, near 14 ml. They therefore have similar effective [hydrodynamic sizes](../../mixture.md#hydrodynamic-size) in this separation. A conformational change can occur without a large change in that measurement. The GFP–peptide-containing complex elutes earlier, near 10.5–11 ml, consistent with a larger particle; the later peak is consistent with excess unbound GFP–[peptide](../../protein.md#peptide).

**The experiment supports [ligand](../../protein.md#ligand-biochemistry) association but does not alone determine subunit number.** [Size-exclusion chromatography](../../chromatography.md#size-exclusion-chromatography) separates by access to pores, which depends on size and shape rather than mass alone. The similar peptide-free and peptide-bound elution volumes therefore do not establish identical conformations or exact molecular masses.

<h4 id="section-2/2/l">l</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/l/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [L](#section-2/2/l)

The main particles are approximately **120 kDa in both conditions**. With 30 kDa per [protein](../../protein.md) subunit, this supports a tetramer:

$$
\boxed{N\simeq\frac{120\,\mathrm{kDa}}{30\,\mathrm{kDa}}=4.}
$$

The small [peptide](../../protein.md#peptide) contribution is not clearly resolved as a separate mass increase at this scale. Together with the microscopy, the results support a conformational change of an existing tetramer, rather than a large change in oligomer number. [Mass photometry](../../biology.md#mass-photometry) reports the scattering-derived mass distribution of detected particles; it does not by itself identify which conformation a particle adopts or count small bound [ligands](../../protein.md#ligand-biochemistry) precisely.

<h4 id="section-2/2/m">m</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/m/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [M](#section-2/2/m)

**Yes: the binding data support strong positive cooperativity.** On the linear [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) axis, occupancy rises sigmoidally, with reported [Hill coefficient](../../protein.md#hill-coefficient) $n=3.97\pm0.13$, far above the noncooperative value one. The [Hill equation](../../protein.md#hill-equation) gives the useful empirical description

$$
\theta=\frac{[L]^n}{K_{1/2}^{\,n}+[L]^n},\qquad K_{1/2}\simeq0.90\,\mu\mathrm M.
$$

The steep response is consistent with the independent evidence for a four-subunit assembly. The fitted half-occupancy [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) is not automatically the microscopic [dissociation constant](../../chemical-potential.md#dissociation-constant) of one isolated site.

Binding alone does not demonstrate which conformational change is coupled to occupancy. Here the microscopy supplies the complementary structural evidence, so together the observations support a cooperative ligand-dependent switch. A [Hill coefficient](../../protein.md#hill-coefficient) near four is consistent with strongly coupled binding in a tetramer, but is not an independent exact count of [ligands](../../protein.md#ligand-biochemistry).

<h4 id="section-2/2/n">n</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/n/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [N](#section-2/2/n)

**A: downward when [ligand](../../protein.md#ligand-biochemistry) is present; B: downward; C: upward**, over the intended [protein](../../protein.md) and [ligand](../../protein.md#ligand-biochemistry) [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) range. [Ligand](../../protein.md#ligand-biochemistry) should favour its bound Y state, and the ligand-bound subunits should favour tetramer assembly. Without [ligand](../../protein.md#ligand-biochemistry), the unassembled state should predominate, rather than forming a stable ligand-free tetramer.

These preferences are thermodynamically coupled. The [ligand](../../protein.md#ligand-biochemistry) must bind preferentially to states participating in the assembled structure strongly enough to compensate for the cost of shifting the unliganded conformational and assembly equilibria. At extremely low [protein](../../protein.md) [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration), association entropy can still favour monomers; “ligand-dependent” does not mean concentration-independent assembly. This is a [thermodynamic linkage](../../protein.md#thermodynamic-linkage) argument, rather than three independently adjustable arrows.

<h4 id="section-2/2/o">o</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#section-2/2)

<h5 id="section-2/2/o/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [O](#section-2/2/o)

**A single predicted monomer structure is insufficient.** It may suggest a possible binding pocket, domain hinge or subunit interface, and a confident multimer model may support a particular assembly hypothesis. Those features do not establish that alternative conformations are populated, that [ligand](../../protein.md#ligand-biochemistry) binding shifts their populations, or that occupancy at one site changes another site's binding strength.

Cooperative [allostery](../../protein.md#allostery) concerns free-energy relationships among states, not only one static geometry. Structural comparisons across conditions, independent oligomeric-mass measurements and ligand-dependent binding or activity curves are needed to test the proposal. A low-confidence inter-domain orientation is likewise uncertainty about a model, not a prediction that the [protein](../../protein.md) is necessarily an allosteric oligomer.
