<h1 id="section-iv/11/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [11](../11.md)

**Intracellular bacteria face a protected but highly controlled habitat: they must obtain host resources, avoid destruction, coordinate replication and reach the next host cell or host generation.** Dependence evolves when access to that habitat is combined with loss or specialization of functions needed outside it. The selective pressures differ sharply between a pathogen spreading among hosts and a vertically inherited mutualist.

A first pressure is acquisition of a suitable intracellular niche. A bacterium entering by an uptake pathway may be delivered toward an acidified, degradative compartment. [Chlamydia trachomatis](../../../../../chlamydia-trachomatis.md) instead replicates in an [chlamydial inclusion](../../../../../chlamydial-inclusion.md) whose interactions with host trafficking are modified by bacterial effectors. The niche supplies access to host-derived materials while keeping the replicating population spatially organized. [Rickettsia prowazekii](../../../../../rickettsia-prowazekii.md), in contrast, occupies the host cytoplasm after escape from an entry compartment. These are different solutions; it would be misleading to describe all intracellular bacteria as surviving by blocking lysosome fusion inside one generic vacuole.

The ability to manipulate trafficking and obtain metabolites becomes valuable. The [Chlamydia trachomatis](../../../../../chlamydia-trachomatis.md) [genome](../../../../../genome.md) revealed a compact but specialized repertoire that includes transport and secretion-related functions, consistent with extensive host dependence.[pp. 754–759, genome sequence and metabolic/transport inventory](../../../../../genome-sequence-of-an-obligate-intracellular-pathogen-of-humans-chlamydia-trachomatis-by-richard-s-stephens-and-others.md) [genome](../../../../../genome.md) annotation provides hypotheses about [substrate](../../../../../substrate-biochemistry.md) use, not proof of actual import or [enzyme](../../../../../enzyme-split.md) activity. Test a candidate transporter's contribution using [substrate](../../../../../substrate-biochemistry.md) uptake and intracellular growth, with genetic rescue and host-cell viability controls. A reduction in bacterial DNA can result from fewer entering bacteria, slower division or increased killing, so these stages should be measured separately.

Resource availability is selective rather than unlimited. A host cytoplasm may contain nutrients that are inaccessible across an [chlamydial inclusion](../../../../../chlamydial-inclusion.md) membrane, and an activated host can restrict metals or amino acids. Dependence on host supply favors uptake systems and scavenging, but makes the bacterium vulnerable to host nutrient restriction. The [Rickettsia prowazekii](../../../../../rickettsia-prowazekii.md) [genome](../../../../../genome.md) showed extensive biosynthetic losses while retaining components of the [citric acid cycle](../../../../../citric-acid-cycle.md) and respiration.[pp. 133–140, metabolic gene inventory and genome reduction](../../../../../the-genome-sequence-of-rickettsia-prowazekii-and-the-origin-of-mitochondria-by-siv-g-e-andersson-and-others.md) Thus an intracellular lifestyle does not mean universal loss of [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) metabolism or reliance on one identical ATP-acquisition strategy. Imported metabolites and bacterial [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) production can coexist.

Another pressure is coordination of the infectious and replicative phases. [Chlamydia](../../../../../chlamydia.md) alternates between an [elementary body](../../../../../elementary-body.md), adapted for transmission and entry, and a [reticulate body](../../../../../reticulate-body.md), adapted for growth inside the host. A durable infectious form can spend time outside a cell even when productive replication requires intracellular conditions. Developmental regulation therefore solves the conflict between surviving transfer and exploiting a nutrient-rich intracellular niche. Measure infectious progeny as well as [genome](../../../../../genome.md) copies: replication without successful differentiation need not yield transmission to another host cell.

Host defense creates additional pressures. Recognition of bacterial components can trigger inflammatory signaling, antimicrobial responses or death of the host cell before bacterial replication is complete. Pathogens can alter exposure, modify host signaling or delay destructive responses; the useful strategy depends on the niche and infection stage. Host-cell death may be harmful early yet assist release later, so “preventing apoptosis” cannot be treated as a universal permanent objective. To assign a bacterial effector's role, compare entry, replication, host response and release with an otherwise matched mutant, then restore the normal gene. Host toxicity caused by overexpressing an isolated effector is insufficient evidence for its physiological function during infection.

Some cytosolic species use the host [cytoskeleton](../../../../../cytoskeleton.md) for movement and spread. Biochemical work on [Rickettsia parkeri](../../../../../rickettsia-parkeri.md) [Sca2](../../../../../sca2.md) demonstrated a formin-like [actin](../../../../../actin.md) assembly activity that helps explain the unbranched [actin](../../../../../actin.md) tails associated with spotted-fever rickettsiae.[pp. 1057–1063, purified Sca2 actin assembly and elongation experiments](../../../../../rickettsia-sca2-is-a-bacterial-formin-like-mediator-of-actin-based-motility-by-cat-m-haglund-and-others.md) Such movement can improve access to neighboring cells and reduce extracellular exposure. The result should not be generalized to every rickettsial species; different lineages have different motility repertoires. Inhibition of host [actin](../../../../../actin.md) also affects many host processes, so a bacterial separation-of-function mutant is more informative than a broad [actin](../../../../../actin.md) poison alone.

Long-term intracellular residence can produce [genome reduction](../../../../../genome-reduction.md). Functions redundantly supplied by the host may become less strongly selected, permitting loss. Restricted transmission, small effective population size and limited recombination can also allow mildly deleterious changes to accumulate through [genetic drift](../../../../../genetic-drift.md). Not every lost gene should be narrated as an adaptive improvement in efficiency. Compare closely related lineages, pseudogenes and host dependence; distinguish a dispensable ancestral pathway from a costly defect tolerated because the population cannot efficiently remove it.

[Buchnera aphidicola](../../../../../buchnera-aphidicola.md) illustrates a different outcome from pathogenic manipulation. It lives in aphid [bacteriocytes](../../../../../bacteriocyte.md) and contributes to nutritional complementation in a host feeding on a restricted diet. [genome](../../../../../genome.md) sequencing showed strong reduction together with retention of substantial amino-acid biosynthetic capacity relevant to the association.[pp. 81–86, reduced genome and metabolic complementarity](../../../../../genome-sequence-of-the-endocellular-bacterial-symbiont-of-aphids-buchnera-sp-aps-by-shuji-shigenobu-and-others.md) A vertically transmitted symbiont depends on host reproduction, which can favor cooperation and reliable transmission rather than maximal short-term host damage. Host and symbiont exchange still creates conflicts over resource allocation; stable association does not mean every trait benefits both partners equally.

Intracellular dependence should be tested operationally. Specialized host-cell-free culture allowed [Coxiella burnetii](../../../../../coxiella-burnetii.md), previously treated as obligatorily intracellular in culture, to grow under defined environmental and nutritional conditions.[pp. 4430–4434, axenic growth and recovery of infectious bacteria](../../../../../host-cell-free-growth-of-the-q-fever-bacterium-coxiella-burnetii-by-anders-omsland-and-others.md) It remains a highly specialized intracellular organism in its normal infection setting, but this experiment shows why failure in a standard laboratory medium is not proof that a living host is chemically indispensable. The main examples above concern established intracellular dependencies; Coxiella is a useful boundary case, not evidence that all such bacteria can be grown axenically.

**Successful adaptation combines niche access, selective resource acquisition, control of host interactions and a transmission strategy.** Over evolutionary time, those specializations can become irreversible dependencies through gene loss and population constraints, while the particular balance between host damage and cooperation follows the bacterium's route to the next generation.
