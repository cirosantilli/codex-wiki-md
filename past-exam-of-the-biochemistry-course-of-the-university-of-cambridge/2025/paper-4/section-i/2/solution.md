↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

**Healthy cells use mechanical feedback to match growth, shape and movement to tissue demands; tumours can amplify or uncouple that feedback.** Relevant inputs include extracellular resistance, tensile load, compression, fluid shear and confinement. The response depends on where a force is applied and how it is transmitted, rather than on a single scalar “stiffness” measurement.

A [biological cell](../../../../../biological-cell-split.md) actively probes its surroundings through [actomyosin contractility](../../../../../actomyosin-contractility.md). [myosin](../../../../../myosin.md) pulls on [actin](../../../../../actin.md), and [integrins](../../../../../integrin.md) couple the network to the [extracellular matrix](../../../../../extracellular-matrix.md) through [focal adhesions](../../../../../focal-adhesion.md). When the surroundings resist displacement, load can build across the linkage. [cadherins](../../../../../cadherin.md) similarly connect neighbouring cells, while the [cytoskeleton](../../../../../cytoskeleton.md) and [LINC complex](../../../../../linc-complex.md) transmit some loads to the [cell nucleus](../../../../../cell-nucleus.md). These coupled structures support [mechanotransduction](../../../../../mechanotransduction.md): conversion of a mechanical perturbation into altered molecular binding, signaling or [gene expression](../../../../../gene-expression.md).

One physical mechanism is force-dependent exposure of binding sites. In experiments combining single-molecule manipulation and imaging, stretching [talin](../../../../../talin.md) exposed otherwise inaccessible sites for [vinculin](../../../../../vinculin.md).[pp. 638–641, force application to talin and vinculin-binding measurements](../../../../../stretching-single-talin-rod-molecules-activates-vinculin-binding-by-armando-del-rio-and-others.md) Recruitment can strengthen the connection to [actin](../../../../../actin.md), changing subsequent force transmission. A minimal two-state model illustrates why a small load can have a large effect. If opening lengthens a domain by $\Delta x$, constant tensile force $F$ changes its opening free [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) to $\Delta G(F)=\Delta G(0)-F\Delta x$. Under equilibrium assumptions,

$$
\frac{p_{\mathrm{open}}}{p_{\mathrm{closed}}}=\exp\!\left[-\frac{\Delta G(0)-F\Delta x}{k_BT}\right].
$$

The work $F\Delta x$ has units of [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md); the model predicts an increased open-state population when pulling favors extension. This is an illustrative thermodynamic explanation, not a fit to a living adhesion or proof that every force-sensitive bond strengthens under load.

Mechanical context can alter cell fate. Micropatterned adhesive [substrates](../../../../../substrate-biochemistry.md) controlling the spreading of human mesenchymal [stem cells](../../../../../stem-cell.md) showed different differentiation outcomes, with [RhoA](../../../../../rhoa.md) and contractility perturbations linking cell geometry to the response.[pp. 483–495, patterned adhesion, cell shape and RhoA-dependent lineage assays](../../../../../cell-shape-cytoskeletal-tension-and-rhoa-regulate-stem-cell-lineage-commitment-by-rowena-mcbeath-and-others.md) Geometry, adhesive ligand density and [substrate](../../../../../substrate-biochemistry.md) stiffness are related but distinct variables. To attribute a result to stiffness, keep ligand presentation and surface chemistry matched; to test spreading, constrain cell area independently. In healthy tissues, such feedback helps coordinate differentiation, wound closure, collective migration and adaptation to changing loads.

Nuclear transcriptional regulators integrate these inputs. [YAP](../../../../../yap.md) and [TAZ](../../../../../wwtr1.md) changed localization and activity with matrix resistance, cell spreading and cytoskeletal organization in controlled culture experiments.[Figs. 1–4, stiffness, geometry, actin dependence and YAP/TAZ functional assays](../../../../../role-of-yap-taz-in-mechanotransduction-by-sirio-dupont-and-others.md) Nuclear enrichment is a useful readout, but does not by itself demonstrate the downstream program or a universal causal pathway. Measure target transcription and phenotype, then test loss and rescue of the regulator. [hippo signaling](../../../../../hippo-signaling.md) and other biochemical pathways intersect with mechanical regulation; the balance is cell-type- and context-dependent. Force can additionally gate [mechanosensitive ion channels](../../../../../mechanosensitive-ion-channel.md), changing [calcium](../../../../../calcium.md) entry, and alter nuclear shape or transport. Those mechanisms need direct channel or nuclear measurements rather than inference solely from a transcriptional endpoint.

The normal feedback is bidirectional. Cells deposit and remodel [extracellular matrix](../../../../../extracellular-matrix.md), and their newly produced matrix changes later mechanical input. During repair, that loop can transiently stabilize a wound; persistent activation can instead produce [fibrosis](../../../../../fibrosis.md). An external [substrate](../../../../../substrate-biochemistry.md)'s [Young's modulus](../../../../../young-s-modulus.md) is not the same as the force within a particular adhesion: cell contractility, geometry, matrix architecture and loading rate determine the force actually transmitted. [traction force microscopy](../../../../../traction-force-microscopy.md), calibrated molecular tension sensors and local mechanical measurements constrain different parts of this relationship.

In tumours, excessive matrix deposition and [collagen](../../../../../collagen.md) cross-linking can raise tissue resistance, promote sustained integrin signaling and favor invasive behavior. In mammary tumour models, manipulation of [lysyl oxidase](../../../../../lysyl-oxidase.md)-dependent matrix cross-linking linked matrix mechanics to integrin signaling and progression.[pp. 891–906, matrix cross-linking, integrin signaling and intervention experiments](../../../../../matrix-crosslinking-forces-tumor-progression-by-enhancing-integrin-signaling-by-kandice-r-levental-and-others.md) This goes beyond comparing a stiff tumour with softer normal tissue, but interventions can still change matrix chemistry and growth-factor presentation along with mechanics. Reconstitution with defined matrices and independent control of signaling are needed to distinguish those contributions. Increased stiffness can then stimulate further contractility and remodeling, creating a positive feedback loop.

Growing tumours also generate [solid stress](../../../../../solid-stress.md) and elevated interstitial pressure. Compression can distort neighbouring tissue and impair perfusion, producing heterogeneous [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) and nutrient delivery.[pp. 15101–15108, released-stress measurements and perfusion-related consequences](../../../../../causes-consequences-and-remedies-for-growth-induced-solid-stress-in-murine-and-human-tumors-by-triantafyllos-stylianopoulos-and-others.md) A stiff matrix and high compressive stress can coexist; they are not interchangeable. Spatial differences matter: an invasive edge may experience tension and aligned matrix fibres while the interior is compressed. Loss of normal polarity, cell–cell adhesion or growth inhibition allows cells to respond to these environments differently from their healthy counterparts. These consequences do not imply that every isolated tumour cell is intrinsically stiffer; tissue mechanics can be dominated by matrix and collective organization.

Cancer-associated changes in [RhoA](../../../../../rhoa.md), adhesion signaling or transcription can also alter how cells generate or interpret force. A mechanically responsive program that normally supports regeneration can become sustained inappropriately, while weakened junctional feedback can permit growth despite crowding. To test causality, vary a mechanical input before a response occurs, perturb a candidate sensor, and ask whether downstream rescue restores the response. Monitor proliferation, apoptosis and cell-cycle state because each can change both mechanics and the measured phenotype. Three-dimensional matrices should be examined alongside flat [substrates](../../../../../substrate-biochemistry.md): confinement, viscoelastic relaxation and degradability can change the outcome even at similar elastic moduli.

**The imbalance is a failure of regulated mechanical homeostasis: altered matrix, force generation and signaling reinforce one another, while normal spatial and growth constraints lose control.** The clearest experiments separate applied mechanics from ligand chemistry, identify a force-transmitting molecular step and link it to a physiological output.
