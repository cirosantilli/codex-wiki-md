<h1 id="section-2/j/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [J](../j.md)

**Several [concentrations](../../../../../concentration.md) reveal a sensitivity range and help distinguish resistance from a single-dose threshold effect.** Increasing disc [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) increases the region in which diffusing drug exceeds the local growth-inhibitory [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md). The wild type has progressively larger clear zones, act1-117 has little or no detectable inhibition across the tested range, and act1-129 is inhibited much more strongly. The zero-drug disc provides a local control for the disc and vehicle; it is the fourth condition alongside the three nonzero [concentrations](../../../../../concentration.md).

A halo diameter is not directly proportional to a [dissociation constant](../../../../../dissociation-constant.md) or a minimum inhibitory [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md): [molecular diffusion](../../../../../molecular-diffusion.md), agar composition, uptake and growth rate contribute. Overlapping large halos can also limit comparison at high doses. Matched lawns, solvent exposure and incubation are necessary, and a quantitative growth assay in uniform drug [concentrations](../../../../../concentration.md) would give a cleaner concentration–response curve. Multiple disc doses prevent a lack of inhibition at one weak dose from being mistaken for absolute resistance.
