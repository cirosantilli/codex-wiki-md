<h1 id="section-2/n/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [N](../n.md)

The three resistant substitutions map near one another in the folded [actin monomer](../../../../../actin-monomer.md), near the [protein binding site](../../../../../protein-binding-site.md) for the [nucleotide](../../../../../nucleotide.md). **This cluster identifies a candidate region involved in drug recognition or in the conformational response to binding.** Neighbouring residues in three-dimensional structure can help form a pocket even when they are not adjacent in the [protein primary structure](../../../../../protein-primary-structure.md). Reduced drug binding at this region would connect the structural map to the resistant phenotype.

The map does not show a bound ToxB molecule. Substitutions could act indirectly by changing folding, [nucleotide](../../../../../nucleotide.md) exchange or the assembly-competent conformation. Proximity to [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) does not prove competition for the ATP-binding site, and the mutant's abnormal baseline [actin polymerization](../../../../../actin-polymerization.md) makes an allosteric explanation particularly relevant. Direct binding measurements on comparably folded wild-type and mutant [actin monomers](../../../../../actin-monomer.md), together with nucleotide-binding controls and a ligand-bound structure, would distinguish these models. The allele/residue labels are interpreted as defined in this paper; no identification of their original experimental sequence substitutions is required.
