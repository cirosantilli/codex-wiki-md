<h1 id="section-2/l/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [L](../l.md)

**Test whether cellular drug effects persist when [actin](../../../../../actin.md) is protected by a [drug-resistant allele](../../../../../drug-resistant-allele.md).** Compare otherwise matched wild-type and act1-117 [biological cells](../../../../../biological-cell-split.md) across a drug-concentration range, measuring growth or colony formation together with [actin](../../../../../actin.md) organization and a relevant cellular phenotype. Each strain needs a vehicle-only baseline because the mutant already has abnormal intrinsic assembly. If the resistant strain maintains its [actin](../../../../../actin.md) network and the normal actin-dependent functions but still develops another reproducible defect, that residual effect is a candidate [off-target drug effect](../../../../../off-target-drug-effect.md). Comparable intracellular exposure is important: resistance caused by lower uptake would not protect only the intended target.

Conversely, coordinated rescue of the [actin](../../../../../actin.md) and growth phenotypes supports an on-target explanation over that dose range, without proving the absence of every other molecular interaction. Reintroducing wild-type [actin](../../../../../actin.md) or using another independently resistant allele helps separate the [actin](../../../../../actin.md) substitution from unrelated [genetic background](../../../../../genetic-background.md) effects. A candidate secondary target would need its own binding or functional assay and a selective rescue test. A second [actin](../../../../../actin.md) inhibitor giving the same phenotype is supportive but cannot alone exclude shared secondary effects.
