↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**Base composition changes both the initial [buoyant density](../../../../../buoyant-density.md) and the size of the BUdR-induced density increment.** The fraction-number axis runs toward lower density. The unlabelled viral peak lies at a lower fraction number than the corresponding host peak, consistent with viral [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) having a higher [GC content](../../../../../gc-content.md). [BUdR](../../../../../bromodeoxyuridine.md) replaces [thymidine](../../../../../thymidine.md) in newly synthesized strands, so relatively AT-rich [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) incorporates more brominated [nucleoside](../../../../../nucleoside.md) per unit length and undergoes a larger density increase.

Consequently the more AT-rich host [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) can overtake the viral [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) in the fully substituted class: the host fully heavy peak is slightly farther toward the dense end, although host unlabelled [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) was less dense. The hybrid peaks are intermediate. A useful qualitative model is $\rho_{LL}=\rho_0(g)$, $\rho_{LH}=\rho_0(g)+\Delta\rho(g)/2$ and $\rho_{HH}=\rho_0(g)+\Delta\rho(g)$, where $g$ is GC fraction, $\rho_0$ increases with $g$, and $\Delta\rho$ decreases as the [thymidine](../../../../../thymidine.md) fraction decreases. Unequal substitution efficiencies could contribute, but different replication mechanisms are unnecessary. These are equilibrium density positions, not molecular-length measurements.
