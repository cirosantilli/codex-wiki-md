<h1 id="section-1/o/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [O](../o.md)

**The evidence supports NVNA1 as a sequence-specific organizer of viral [chromatin](../../../../../chromatin.md), with possible additional roles in [nuclear retention](../../../../../nuclear-retention.md) and [episome partitioning](../../../../../episome-partitioning.md).** Binding to the [origin of replication](../../../../../origin-of-replication.md) and [DNA bridging](../../../../../dna-bridging.md) explain assembly at separated viral repeat arrays. Access to recognition sites in [nucleosomes](../../../../../nucleosome.md) and weakening of histone–DNA association provide a plausible way to make an origin accessible to cellular replication machinery. The density-labelling result is consistent with a latent [genome](../../../../../genome.md) using a host-coordinated copying programme; it does not establish that NVNA1 is itself a [DNA polymerase](../../../../../dna-polymerase.md) or [DNA helicase](../../../../../dna-helicase.md).

Association with host chromosomal [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) could also retain [episomes](../../../../../episome.md) in the nucleus and help distribute them during [biological cell](../../../../../biological-cell-split.md) division. Such tethering is a plausible maintenance function, but the experiments here do not directly visualize a physical bridge between an [episome](../../../../../episome.md) and a host [chromosome](../../../../../chromosome.md). Binding at a vulnerable host repeat could instead be an incidental consequence of recognition specificity. The enrichment of host [chromosome](../../../../../chromosome.md) rearrangements supports a damage connection; it does not show that [chromosome breakage](../../../../../chromosome-breakage.md) is required for viral replication or that integration is part of the normal latent programme. [chromatin](../../../../../chromatin.md) opening and host-locus interference are mechanistic interpretations of the presented binding data, not proof of one obligatory replication pathway.
