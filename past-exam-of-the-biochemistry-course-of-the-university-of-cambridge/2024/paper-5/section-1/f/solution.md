<h1 id="section-1/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The cytoplasmic viral signal stays predominantly at high molecular size despite longer [nuclease](../../../../../nuclease.md) treatment. **The viral [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) is protected from digestion, plausibly inside [viral capsids](../../../../../viral-capsid.md) or assembled particles.** This differs from a nucleosomal ladder: a [capsid](../../../../../viral-capsid.md) can shield a whole [genome](../../../../../genome.md) instead of producing repeated small protected units. Release into the cytoplasm during productive infection makes particle-associated [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) a reasonable interpretation.

The virus-negative lane argues against nonspecific hybridization. Protection alone nevertheless does not prove that all detected particles are mature or infectious; an inaccessible compartment or another protective assembly could also shield [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md). Comparing intact material with material whose protective envelope or [capsid](../../../../../viral-capsid.md) has been disrupted would distinguish enclosure from intrinsic [nuclease](../../../../../nuclease.md) resistance of the [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md). Fractionation controls would address nuclear contamination. The result is about physical accessibility of viral [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md), not a quantitative infectivity assay.
