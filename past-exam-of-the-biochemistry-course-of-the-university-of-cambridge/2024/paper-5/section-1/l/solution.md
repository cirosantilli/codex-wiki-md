<h1 id="section-1/l/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [L](../l.md)

**The locus frequently appears as an abnormal, fragile-site-like structure rather than a clean replicated pair.** A1, B3 and C2 have one dominant signal instead of a clearly resolved sister pair; A2/A4, B1/B4 and C1/C3 show extra, split or irregularly distributed signal components. These patterns are compatible with incomplete replication, abnormal condensation of [sister chromatids](../../../../../sister-chromatids.md), or splitting and fragmentation of the repeat-containing region. A single signal can also result from unresolved sisters or inefficient hybridization, so it is not automatically a deletion.

The several small signals should not simply be counted as newly created intact copies: stretching, gaps and fragmentation can divide the fluorescent image of an existing locus. Conversely, amplification would require independent copy-number evidence. The fact that aberrant structures occur in non-infected [biological cells](../../../../../biological-cell-split.md) indicates intrinsic vulnerability of this host region, rather than a requirement for NVNA1 in their initial appearance. These images alone do not distinguish an undercondensed but continuous [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) region from a genuine [DNA double-strand break](../../../../../dna-double-strand-break.md). Their sensitivity to replication inhibition provides the next mechanistic clue.
