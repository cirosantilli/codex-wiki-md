<h1 id="section-1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**The [DNA-binding domain](../../../../../dna-binding-domain.md) supports site occupancy, while the region for [homotypic protein interaction](../../../../../homotypic-protein-interaction.md) permits much larger assemblies.** With the truncated [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md), the faster complex 4 appears at low [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md):[DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) ratios and gives way to the slower complex 3 as [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) increases. A natural assignment is occupancy of one and then both recognition repeats by functional binding units. A binding unit could itself be a [protein dimer](../../../../../protein-dimer.md); a native gel does not determine the number of polypeptide chains from mobility alone. Loss of free probe with increasing [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) supports binding, and failure to shift the zero-repeat probe supports sequence preference under these conditions.

Full-length [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) instead produces the very slow complex 1 already at relatively low ratios, followed by more of complex 2 at high ratios. [homotypic protein interactions](../../../../../homotypic-protein-interaction.md) can make cooperative higher-order assemblies and can bridge different probe molecules. At [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) excess, separate saturation of available sites can reduce productive bridging, explaining why the largest species need not increase monotonically. Complex 2 is compatible with a smaller saturated full-length assembly; assigning its exact stoichiometry requires an independent measurement. [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) size also contributes to the difference between full-length and truncated complexes, but size alone does not explain the concentration-dependent redistribution into the exceptionally slow species. The experiment supports assembly dependent on [protein oligomerization](../../../../../protein-oligomerization.md) rather than a unique structure for every numbered band.
