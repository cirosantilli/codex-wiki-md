## Section 1

<h3 id="section-1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#section-1/a)

**The viral [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) is copied by [semiconservative replication](../../molecular-genetics.md#semiconservative-replication) and its labelling kinetics broadly follow those of host [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) during [viral latency](../../biology.md#viral-latency).** At the earliest time the newly labelled material is mainly hybrid: an old light strand has acquired a heavy complementary strand. Fully heavy duplexes accumulate later, after heavy strands themselves serve as templates. Conservative copying would instead produce a separate fully heavy population from the first copying round, while dispersive copying would produce progressively shifting intermediate densities rather than these three discrete classes.

For an ideal synchronous population starting entirely light, one complete round gives only hybrid duplexes; after $n\geq1$ rounds the hybrid fraction is $2^{1-n}$ and the fully heavy fraction is $1-2^{1-n}$. The actual culture is asynchronous, so these integer-generation proportions cannot simply be imposed at $t/23$ hours. The similar viral and cellular bars support replication coordinated with [cell proliferation](../../biological-cell.md#cell-proliferation) rather than rapid autonomous amplification. They do not establish that every [episome](../../molecular-genetics.md#episome) initiates exactly once in every individual [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md), or prove an initiation time within [S phase](../../cell-cycle.md#s-phase). Persistent light material also warns against treating the population as a perfectly uniform set of cycling [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md). The strand-accounting principle is the one tested in [Figures 3–4: changes in banding over successive copying rounds](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-replication-of-dna-in-escherichia-coli-by-matthew-meselson-and-franklin-w-stahl).

<h3 id="section-1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#section-1/b)

**Base composition changes both the initial [buoyant density](../../biology.md#buoyant-density) and the size of the BUdR-induced density increment.** The fraction-number axis runs toward lower density. The unlabelled viral peak lies at a lower fraction number than the corresponding host peak, consistent with viral [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) having a higher [GC content](../../polymer.md#gc-content). [BUdR](../../molecule.md#bromodeoxyuridine) replaces [thymidine](../../molecule.md#thymidine) in newly synthesized strands, so relatively AT-rich [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) incorporates more brominated [nucleoside](../../molecule.md#nucleoside) per unit length and undergoes a larger density increase.

Consequently the more AT-rich host [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) can overtake the viral [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) in the fully substituted class: the host fully heavy peak is slightly farther toward the dense end, although host unlabelled [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) was less dense. The hybrid peaks are intermediate. A useful qualitative model is $\rho_{LL}=\rho_0(g)$, $\rho_{LH}=\rho_0(g)+\Delta\rho(g)/2$ and $\rho_{HH}=\rho_0(g)+\Delta\rho(g)$, where $g$ is GC fraction, $\rho_0$ increases with $g$, and $\Delta\rho$ decreases as the [thymidine](../../molecule.md#thymidine) fraction decreases. Unequal substitution efficiencies could contribute, but different replication mechanisms are unnecessary. These are equilibrium density positions, not molecular-length measurements.

<h3 id="section-1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#section-1/c)

**The [DNA-binding domain](../../protein.md#dna-binding-domain) supports site occupancy, while the region for [homotypic protein interaction](../../protein.md#homotypic-protein-interaction) permits much larger assemblies.** With the truncated [protein](../../protein.md), the faster complex 4 appears at low [protein](../../protein.md):[DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) ratios and gives way to the slower complex 3 as [protein](../../protein.md) increases. A natural assignment is occupancy of one and then both recognition repeats by functional binding units. A binding unit could itself be a [protein dimer](../../protein.md#protein-dimer); a native gel does not determine the number of polypeptide chains from mobility alone. Loss of free probe with increasing [protein](../../protein.md) supports binding, and failure to shift the zero-repeat probe supports sequence preference under these conditions.

Full-length [protein](../../protein.md) instead produces the very slow complex 1 already at relatively low ratios, followed by more of complex 2 at high ratios. [homotypic protein interactions](../../protein.md#homotypic-protein-interaction) can make cooperative higher-order assemblies and can bridge different probe molecules. At [protein](../../protein.md) excess, separate saturation of available sites can reduce productive bridging, explaining why the largest species need not increase monotonically. Complex 2 is compatible with a smaller saturated full-length assembly; assigning its exact stoichiometry requires an independent measurement. [protein](../../protein.md) size also contributes to the difference between full-length and truncated complexes, but size alone does not explain the concentration-dependent redistribution into the exceptionally slow species. The experiment supports assembly dependent on [protein oligomerization](../../protein.md#protein-oligomerization) rather than a unique structure for every numbered band.

<h3 id="section-1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#section-1/d)

At low [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration), the array with more and higher-affinity binding sites is preferentially occupied, giving one visible [protein](../../protein.md) cluster on an otherwise extended [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) molecule. At higher [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) the weaker origin array also becomes occupied. **[homotypic protein interactions](../../protein.md#homotypic-protein-interaction) between [proteins](../../protein.md) at the two arrays can bridge them, trapping the intervening [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) through [DNA looping](../../polymer.md#dna-looping).** This explains why the electron-dense [protein](../../protein.md) cluster lies at the loop junction rather than uniformly coating the entire molecule.

The high-affinity array can stabilize weak-site occupancy through tethering: once one partner is anchored, the second site experiences a high effective local [concentration](../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) and several contacts can contribute [avidity](../../protein.md#avidity). This reconciles stable looped assemblies with the vulnerability of the isolated weak site to [nonspecific competitor DNA](../../protein.md#nonspecific-competitor-dna). The images support intramolecular [DNA bridging](../../protein.md#dna-bridging), but selected micrographs do not measure its frequency or exclude some intermolecular associations. A high local density of [protein](../../protein.md) need not imply [biomolecular phase separation](../../critical-phenomenon.md#biomolecular-phase-separation). The printed scale-bar unit appears implausible for a kilobase-scale [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) substrate; the mechanistic inference uses topology and site positions rather than that unit.

<h3 id="section-1/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#section-1/e)

**Latent viral [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) has regularly spaced [nucleosome](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) protection, whereas much of the nuclear viral [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) during productive infection lacks that regular protection.** In the latent sample, increasing digestion resolves a ladder of protected fragments, as expected when [micrococcal nuclease](../../enzyme.md#micrococcal-nuclease) cuts exposed linker [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) between [nucleosomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome). In the productive sample, viral signal is lost rapidly into heterogeneous small material rather than accumulating as a comparable ladder. That is consistent with a large pool of newly replicated or otherwise differently packaged viral [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) being more accessible.

The total-DNA ladders show that host [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin) remains nucleosomal and that the assay can recover protected fragments in each cellular condition. The virus-specific [Southern blot](../../biology.md#southern-blot) distinguishes viral [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) from this abundant host background, and the uninfected lane controls probe background. The conclusion concerns the dominant detectable viral population: productive cultures can contain both chromatinized templates in [viral latency](../../biology.md#viral-latency) and accessible replication products. Absence of a ladder does not establish absence of every [histone](../../protein.md#histone) molecule, and differences in [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) recovery or [nuclease](../../enzyme.md#nuclease) accessibility should be considered before assigning a precise fraction of chromatinized [genomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome).

<h3 id="section-1/f">f</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/f/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#section-1/f)

The cytoplasmic viral signal stays predominantly at high molecular size despite longer [nuclease](../../enzyme.md#nuclease) treatment. **The viral [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) is protected from digestion, plausibly inside [viral capsids](../../biology.md#viral-capsid) or assembled particles.** This differs from a nucleosomal ladder: a [capsid](../../biology.md#viral-capsid) can shield a whole [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) instead of producing repeated small protected units. Release into the cytoplasm during productive infection makes particle-associated [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) a reasonable interpretation.

The virus-negative lane argues against nonspecific hybridization. Protection alone nevertheless does not prove that all detected particles are mature or infectious; an inaccessible compartment or another protective assembly could also shield [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid). Comparing intact material with material whose protective envelope or [capsid](../../biology.md#viral-capsid) has been disrupted would distinguish enclosure from intrinsic [nuclease](../../enzyme.md#nuclease) resistance of the [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid). Fractionation controls would address nuclear contamination. The result is about physical accessibility of viral [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid), not a quantitative infectivity assay.

<h3 id="section-1/g">g</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/g/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [G](#section-1/g)

The naked labelled [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) runs fastest. Reconstitution creates a slower [nucleosome](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) band, with a little residual unassembled [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid). Adding NVNA1 progressively generates several additional, more slowly migrating labelled species and reduces the unbound [nucleosome](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) population. **These are consistent with [ternary protein–DNA complexes](../../protein.md#ternary-protein-dna-complex) containing [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid), [histones](../../protein.md#histone) and NVNA1, with increasing occupancy or altered assembly.** The naked-DNA titration also shows several NVNA1–DNA species, reflecting different occupancy states on the repeat-containing fragment.

A mobility shift reports changes in charge, size and shape together. It cannot by itself establish that [histones](../../protein.md#histone) remain in every shifted species, or identify exact [protein](../../protein.md) stoichiometries. In particular, similarity to a band in the naked-DNA lanes could indicate [histone](../../protein.md#histone) loss rather than merely another occupancy state. The specific-competitor experiment in the next part supplies the important compositional test: removal of NVNA1 can regenerate a [nucleosome](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) band, demonstrating retention of [histones](../../protein.md#histone) in the starting ternary population.

<h3 id="section-1/h">h</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/h/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [H](#section-1/h)

With [nucleosomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) alone, neither competitor substantially removes the main [nucleosome](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) band, although some free labelled [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) is present. With nucleosome–NVNA1 complexes, increasing [specific competitor DNA](../../protein.md#specific-competitor-dna) removes the extra shifts and restores the [nucleosome](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) band. **The starting shifted complexes therefore contained [histones](../../protein.md#histone) as well as NVNA1:** competitor recognition sites draw NVNA1 away while leaving labelled [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) associated with [histones](../../protein.md#histone).

[nonspecific competitor DNA](../../protein.md#nonspecific-competitor-dna) gives a different endpoint. The shifted population changes to a band matching the far-right NVNA1–naked-DNA control, rather than returning mainly to the [nucleosome](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) position. The labelled [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) remains bound to NVNA1 while [histones](../../protein.md#histone) can transfer to the excess competitor [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid). **NVNA1 binding destabilizes the [nucleosome](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) against competitor-induced [histone](../../protein.md#histone) displacement.** Remaining shifted [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) is not evidence that the [histone octamer](../../molecular-genetics.md#histone-octamer) remained: its mobility must be compared with both controls. The nucleosome-only competition lanes show that competitor [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) alone is insufficient to cause the same conversion. Histone-specific detection would confirm the inferred component loss. A closely analogous ternary-complex and competition analysis was demonstrated for EBNA1; it provides a methodological precedent without establishing the identity of NV.[Figures 2–4: ternary complexes, specific competition and histone displacement](../../biology-bibliography.md#epstein-barr-nuclear-antigen-1-binds-and-destabilizes-nucleosomes-at-the-viral-origin-of-latent-dna-replication-by-tina-m-avolio-hunter-and-others)

<h3 id="section-1/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#section-1/i)

About 80% of full-length-expressing [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) have two conspicuous nuclear foci, whereas roughly 90% of [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) expressing the deletion [protein](../../protein.md) have none. Both [proteins](../../protein.md) show nuclear [fluorescence](../../fluorescence.md), so the result is not explained simply by complete exclusion of the deletion [protein](../../protein.md) from the nucleus. **The [DNA-binding domain](../../protein.md#dna-binding-domain) is needed for efficient focal accumulation at a small number of host sites.** A plausible explanation is recognition of repeated host [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) sequences resembling the viral binding sites; clustered repeats and homotypic contacts would concentrate the [protein](../../protein.md) enough to create visible foci.

Two spots are compatible with one locus on each [homologous chromosome](../../genetics.md#homologous-chromosome) in a diploid [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md). They do not prove diploidy, identify a [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome), or exclude unresolved sister loci and cell-cycle effects. An alternative is DNA-dependent recruitment through a host complex. Matching [protein](../../protein.md) abundance and confirming stability of the deletion construct would make the domain comparison stronger. Punctate [fluorescence](../../fluorescence.md) alone does not demonstrate a liquid condensate or show that a focus identifies an [origin of replication](../../molecular-genetics.md#origin-of-replication).

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/j/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [J](#section-1/j)

**Use fluorescent [dCas9](../../molecular-genetics.md#dcas9) to ask whether NVNA1 foci coincide with a defined host [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) locus.** Targeting a candidate repeated region with [CRISPR locus labelling](../../molecular-genetics.md#crispr-locus-labelling) would produce a locus marker in a second colour. Reproducible colocalization with full-length NVNA1, but not with a labelled unrelated locus, supports sequence-associated accumulation. A non-targeting guide and [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) lacking NVNA1 control [fluorescence](../../fluorescence.md) and targeting background; matched expression and nuclear localization control the [protein](../../protein.md) comparison. The inactive [Cas9](../../molecular-genetics.md#cas9) is important because cleavage itself could create the [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) damage being investigated.

A complementary causal test would alter the candidate host recognition region and ask whether focal accumulation is reduced while general nuclear NVNA1 remains. Independent edited clones, verification of the host alteration, and an appropriate restored host-site control would distinguish loss of recognition from clone-specific damage or copy-number changes. Colocalization localizes the [protein](../../protein.md) but does not alone prove direct base-specific contact; the biochemical binding evidence supplies that separate argument. Fluorescent CRISPR marking of a repeated host locus has a direct precedent in the EBNA1 study.[Figure 2g–l: CRISPR locus labelling and colocalization controls](../../biology-bibliography.md#chromosomal-fragile-site-breakage-by-ebv-encoded-ebna1-at-clustered-repeats-by-julia-su-zhou-li-and-others)

<h3 id="section-1/k">k</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/k/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [K](#section-1/k)

**The normal patterns are A3, B2 and C4.** In each, two principal [DNA fluorescence in situ hybridization](../../biology.md#dna-fluorescence-in-situ-hybridization) signals lie at corresponding positions on the two replicated [sister chromatids](../../genetics.md#sister-chromatids). A single [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) in [mitosis](../../cell-cycle.md#mitosis) has already replicated its [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid), so a unique locus normally has one copy on each sister, rather than two signals because two homologous [chromosomes](../../genetics.md#chromosome) are being shown.

The other examples show a single unresolved or missing partner signal, extra fragmented signals, or an abnormal distribution. The images are small and relative intensities differ, especially in row C, so the appropriate criterion is the paired principal locus signals at matching positions, not perfect equality of brightness. Counts from many [chromosomes](../../genetics.md#chromosome), adequate optical resolution and hybridization controls are necessary before classifying borderline examples.

<h3 id="section-1/l">l</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/l/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [L](#section-1/l)

**The locus frequently appears as an abnormal, fragile-site-like structure rather than a clean replicated pair.** A1, B3 and C2 have one dominant signal instead of a clearly resolved sister pair; A2/A4, B1/B4 and C1/C3 show extra, split or irregularly distributed signal components. These patterns are compatible with incomplete replication, abnormal condensation of [sister chromatids](../../genetics.md#sister-chromatids), or splitting and fragmentation of the repeat-containing region. A single signal can also result from unresolved sisters or inefficient hybridization, so it is not automatically a deletion.

The several small signals should not simply be counted as newly created intact copies: stretching, gaps and fragmentation can divide the fluorescent image of an existing locus. Conversely, amplification would require independent copy-number evidence. The fact that aberrant structures occur in non-infected [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) indicates intrinsic vulnerability of this host region, rather than a requirement for NVNA1 in their initial appearance. These images alone do not distinguish an undercondensed but continuous [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) region from a genuine [DNA double-strand break](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-double-strand-break). Their sensitivity to replication inhibition provides the next mechanistic clue.

<h3 id="section-1/m">m</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/m/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [M](#section-1/m)

[aphidicolin](../../molecular-genetics.md#aphidicolin) increases the abnormal-locus fraction from roughly 40% to about 55–60% in all three lines. **Inhibition of replicative [DNA polymerases](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-polymerase) increases [replication stress](../../molecular-genetics.md#replication-stress) at an intrinsically difficult-to-complete region.** Tandem repeats can complicate fork progression or [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) organization. Slower or incomplete synthesis leaves [underreplicated DNA](../../molecular-genetics.md#underreplicated-dna), unresolved replication intermediates or abnormal condensation as [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md) approach [mitosis](../../cell-cycle.md#mitosis); these can appear as split signals or gaps and can predispose to actual breakage.

This is an increase of approximately 15–20 percentage points, not proof of a 15–20-fold change. The two independent measurements per condition support a reproducible direction, but the graph does not provide enough information for a precise significance calculation or a quantitative model of fork failure. [aphidicolin](../../molecular-genetics.md#aphidicolin) need not bind NVNA1 or the repeat sequence directly. Because these are uninfected [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), the result establishes susceptibility of the host locus to [replication stress](../../molecular-genetics.md#replication-stress) independently of viral [protein](../../protein.md) expression.

<h3 id="section-1/n">n</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/n/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [N](#section-1/n)

**The large breakage frequencies occur mainly at supraphysiological NVNA1 levels; the probability at the mean latent level is not measured directly.** At 20 ng/ml doxycycline, NVNA1 is already about twice the latent amount, and breakage is about 8%. At 50 ng/ml, the [protein](../../protein.md) is approximately eightfold above latent levels and breakage about 25%; at 100 ng/ml, approximately sixteenfold and about 45–50%. Higher induction reaches roughly 60% while expression rises to about 21–24 times the latent level. The [western blot loading control](../../protein.md#western-blot-loading-control) supports a protein-abundance comparison, although quantitative interpretation also requires unsaturated [immunoblot](../../protein.md#western-blot) detection.

Latent expression, defined as a ratio of one, lies below the lowest nonzero measured dose. Extrapolating a straight line from baseline and the twofold point would suggest a small frequency, but neither linearity nor a threshold is established. The justified conclusion is lower expected short-term breakage than in the strongly induced conditions, with a potentially important effect of modest upward fluctuations. Cell-to-cell variability, duration of expression and repeated divisions could make uncommon events consequential during a persistent infection. A percentage of broken [chromosomes](../../genetics.md#chromosome) in this assay is not a patient's lifetime [cancer](../../biology.md#cancer) probability. Doxycycline and tag controls are also needed to attribute the induced-cell phenotype specifically to NVNA1.

<h3 id="section-1/o">o</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Section 1](#section-1)

<h4 id="section-1/o/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [O](#section-1/o)

**The evidence supports NVNA1 as a sequence-specific organizer of viral [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin), with possible additional roles in [nuclear retention](../../biological-cell.md#nuclear-retention) and [episome partitioning](../../molecular-genetics.md#episome-partitioning).** Binding to the [origin of replication](../../molecular-genetics.md#origin-of-replication) and [DNA bridging](../../protein.md#dna-bridging) explain assembly at separated viral repeat arrays. Access to recognition sites in [nucleosomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#nucleosome) and weakening of histone–DNA association provide a plausible way to make an origin accessible to cellular replication machinery. The density-labelling result is consistent with a latent [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) using a host-coordinated copying programme; it does not establish that NVNA1 is itself a [DNA polymerase](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-polymerase) or [DNA helicase](../../enzyme.md#dna-helicase).

Association with host chromosomal [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) could also retain [episomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#episome) in the nucleus and help distribute them during [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) division. Such tethering is a plausible maintenance function, but the experiments here do not directly visualize a physical bridge between an [episome](../../molecular-genetics.md#episome) and a host [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome). Binding at a vulnerable host repeat could instead be an incidental consequence of recognition specificity. The enrichment of host [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) rearrangements supports a damage connection; it does not show that [chromosome breakage](../../polymer.md#chromosome-breakage) is required for viral replication or that integration is part of the normal latent programme. [chromatin](../../molecular-genetics.md#chromatin) opening and host-locus interference are mechanistic interpretations of the presented binding data, not proof of one obligatory replication pathway.

**Sequestration of assembly-competent [G-actin](../../protein.md#actin-monomer) explains both inhibition of [polymer](../../polymer.md) formation and loss of pre-existing cellular structures.** In a simple model, free [actin monomer](../../protein.md#actin-monomer) $G$ binds drug $D$ to form an assembly-incompetent complex $GD$. At a [actin filament](../../protein.md#actin-filament) end the net addition rate is $v=k_+[G]-k_-$, so lowering free $[G]$ reduces elongation and can drive net shortening below the relevant [actin critical concentration](../../protein.md#actin-critical-concentration) $C_c=k_-/k_+$. Formation of new nuclei is also strongly reduced when free [actin monomer](../../protein.md#actin-monomer) is depleted. Existing [actin filaments](../../protein.md#actin-filament) can consequently disappear through ordinary turnover even though ToxB never binds them.

Binding could instead or additionally alter the [actin monomer](../../protein.md#actin-monomer) conformation or [nucleotide](../../molecule.md#nucleotide) state needed for productive assembly. The resistant mutant supports an interaction with [actin](../../protein.md#actin), but resistance could reflect reduced drug affinity, an allosteric change or altered assembly properties. The available endpoints do not uniquely distinguish these possibilities. In particular, they provide no positive evidence for direct [actin filament severing](../../protein.md#actin-filament-severing) or [actin filament capping](../../protein.md#actin-filament-capping) by ToxB. A real example of actin-monomer sequestration and resistant-actin genetics is latrunculin A; this is an analogy, not a chemical identification of ToxB.[Purified-actin assembly and drug-resistant mutant analysis](../../biology-bibliography.md#high-rates-of-actin-filament-turnover-in-budding-yeast-and-roles-for-actin-in-establishment-and-maintenance-of-cell-polarity-revealed-using-the-actin-inhibitor-latrunculin-a-by-kathryn-r-ayscough-and-others)

**The weak [phalloidin](../../protein.md#phalloidin) signal could include sparse or very short residual [actin filaments](../../protein.md#actin-filament), together with nonspecific background; it is not a direct image of [G-actin](../../protein.md#actin-monomer).** [phalloidin](../../protein.md#phalloidin) preferentially binds [filamentous actin](../../protein.md#actin-filament), so genuinely specific residual signal would indicate some polymeric [actin](../../protein.md#actin). Long exposure also amplifies [autofluorescence](../../fluorescence.md#autofluorescence), out-of-focus light and nonspecific staining. Vehicle controls, unstained [biological cells](../../biological-cell.md), matched acquisition settings and background subtraction are needed before assigning a small residual signal to [actin filaments](../../protein.md#actin-filament).

For wavelength $\lambda$ and [numerical aperture](../../point-spread-function.md#numerical-aperture) $\mathrm{NA}$, the [Rayleigh criterion](../../point-spread-function.md#rayleigh-criterion) gives a conventional lateral resolution of roughly $d\approx0.61\lambda/\mathrm{NA}$, typically a few hundred nanometres under these conditions, far larger than the roughly 7-nm [actin filament](../../protein.md#actin-filament) diameter. It cannot resolve every short [actin filament](../../protein.md#actin-filament), distinguish all closely spaced [actin filaments](../../protein.md#actin-filament), or infer [polymer](../../polymer.md) mass directly from the displayed intensity. The tenfold exposure change is not itself a calibrated tenfold decrease in [F-actin](../../protein.md#actin-filament). Fixation, dye accessibility and detector saturation introduce further uncertainty.

Super-resolution [fluorescence microscopy](../../fluorescence.md#fluorescence-microscopy) could localize remaining labelled structures more precisely, while [electron microscopy](../../optics.md#electron-microscopy) or [cryo-electron tomography](../../optics.md#cryo-electron-tomography) could test ultrastructure, subject to preservation and sampling limits. Biochemical separation of polymeric and soluble [actin](../../protein.md#actin), with the mass-balance and aggregation controls already discussed, supplies an independent population measurement. A live [actin](../../protein.md#actin) reporter can test dynamics but must be checked for perturbation of assembly. Agreement between these approaches would be stronger than concluding complete [actin filament](../../protein.md#actin-filament) absence from weak conventional staining alone.
