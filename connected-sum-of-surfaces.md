# Connected sum of surfaces

↑ **Parent:** [Topological surface](topological-surface-split.md)

For two [connected](connected-space.md) closed [surfaces](topological-surface-split.md), remove an open disc from each and identify the resulting boundary [circles](circle.md) by a [homeomorphism](homeomorphism.md). The resulting closed [surface](topological-surface-split.md) is their connected sum, defined up to [homeomorphism](homeomorphism.md) independently of these choices. A [triangulation](triangulation.md) version removes one triangular face from each and glues their boundary three-cycles by a [simplicial homeomorphism](simplicial-homeomorphism.md).

At a seam vertex the two vertex [links of a simplex](link-of-a-simplex.md) are paths; gluing their matching endpoints produces a [cycle graph](cycle-graph.md), proving the local [surface](topological-surface-split.md) condition. Removing a triangular face subtracts one from each [Euler characteristic](euler-characteristic.md), while boundary gluing reduces both the vertex count and the edge count by three. Therefore $\chi(M\mathbin\#N)=\chi(M)+\chi(N)-2$. If the result were [orientable](orientable-surface.md), either punctured summand would inherit consistent choices of [orientation of a simplex](orientation-of-a-simplex.md), and these would extend over its capping face. Thus a nonorientable summand forces a [nonorientable surface](non-orientable-surface.md) result. The [classification theorem for closed combinatorial surfaces](classification-theorem-for-closed-combinatorial-surfaces.md) then gives $T^2\mathbin\#\mathbb{RP}^2\cong\mathbb{RP}^2\mathbin\#\mathbb{RP}^2\mathbin\#\mathbb{RP}^2$.
