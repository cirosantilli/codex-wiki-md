# Infinite-dimensional bounded sequences can lack convergent subsequences

↑ **Parent:** [Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem](bolzano-weierstrass-theorem.md)

In the [l2 sequence space](l2-sequence-space.md), let $e_n$ have value one in coordinate $n$ and zero elsewhere. This [orthonormal sequence](orthonormal-sequence.md) is bounded: $\|e_n\|_2=1$. For distinct indices $n,m$, its terms obey $\|e_n-e_m\|_2=\sqrt2$. No [subsequence](subsequence.md) can therefore be a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md), so none converges in the [norm](norm.md). Thus even a [complete metric space](complete-metric-space.md) can have a [bounded sequence](bounded-sequence.md) with no [convergent subsequence](convergent-subsequence.md). Finite dimension is essential to the usual [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md) extension, and in a general [metric space](metric-space.md) the relevant replacement is [compactness](compact-space.md) or [total boundedness](totally-bounded-space.md) together with [completeness](completeness.md).
