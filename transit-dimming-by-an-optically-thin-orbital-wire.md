# Transit dimming by an optically thin orbital wire

↑ **Parent:** [Exoplanet transit photometry](exoplanet-transit-photometry.md)

For an [edge-on orbit](edge-on-orbit.md) crossing the centre of a uniformly bright stellar disk at distance $r\gg R_\star$, projected speed is $h/r$ and chord time is $2R_\star r/h$. A steady [cross-sectional-area current](cross-sectional-area-current.md) $J_\sigma=2fh$ therefore places area $4fR_\star r$ in front of the [star](star.md), giving

$$
\frac{\Delta F}{F_\star}=\frac4\pi\frac r{R_\star}f.
$$

This requires a geometrically narrow, [optically thin](optically-thin-medium.md) wire with negligible overlap. A noncentral chord, [limb darkening](limb-darkening.md), finite angular extent and large [optical depth](optical-depth.md) change the estimate; it cannot predict dimming greater than one.
