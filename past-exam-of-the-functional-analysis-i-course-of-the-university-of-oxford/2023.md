## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Both terms in the [graph norm](../functional-analysis.md#graph-norm) are finite and nonnegative. Moreover, $\|x\|_T=0$ forces $\|x\|_X=0$, hence $x=0$; conversely $T0=0$ gives $\|0\|_T=0$. This proves [definiteness of a norm](../normed-vector-space.md#definiteness-of-a-norm).

The [linearity](../vector-space.md#linearity) of $T$ and [absolute homogeneity of a norm](../normed-vector-space.md#absolute-homogeneity-of-a-norm) give, for each real $a$,

$$
\|ax\|_T=|a|\|x\|_X+\|aTx\|_Y=|a|\|x\|_T.
$$

Applying the [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) in both [normed spaces](../normed-vector-space.md) gives

$$
\|x+z\|_T=\|x+z\|_X+\|Tx+Tz\|_Y\leq\|x\|_T+\|z\|_T.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\|\cdot\|_T\text{ is a norm on }X.}
$$

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Since $W$ is proper, $T$ is a nonzero [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional). Choose $e\in X$ with $Te=1$, by rescaling any [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) outside its [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md). For every $x\in X$,

$$
x=\underbrace{(x-(Tx)e)}_{\in W}+(Tx)e.
$$

If $ae\in W$, applying $T$ gives $a=0$. Consequently this is a [direct sum](../vector-space.md#direct-sum), and the [quotient vector space](../vector-space.md#quotient-vector-space) $X/W$ has [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) one. This computes the [codimension of a vector subspace](../vector-space.md#codimension-of-a-vector-subspace):

$$
\boxed{X=W\oplus\langle e\rangle,\qquad\operatorname{codim}_XW=1.}
$$

To prove boundedness without assuming [completeness](../topological-analysis.md#completeness), use the [linear functional with closed kernel](../topological-vector-space.md#linear-functional-with-closed-kernel) criterion directly. Since $W$ is a [closed set](../topology.md#closed-set) and $e\notin W$, the distance

$$
d=\inf_{w\in W}\|e-w\|_X
$$

is positive. If $Tx\ne0$, then $e-x/(Tx)\in W$, so

$$
d\leq\left\|\frac{x}{Tx}\right\|_X=\frac{\|x\|_X}{|Tx|}.
$$

For $Tx=0$ the resulting estimate is immediate. Therefore

$$
\boxed{|Tx|\leq d^{-1}\|x\|_X\quad(x\in X),\qquad T\in X^*.}
$$

Here $X^*$ denotes the [continuous dual space](../continuous-dual-space.md).

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

The [bounded linear functional](../topological-vector-space.md#continuous-linear-functional) from part (i) satisfies $|Tx|\leq\|T\|\|x\|_X$, where $\|T\|$ is its [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm). Hence

$$
\boxed{\|x\|_X\leq\|x\|_T\leq(1+\|T\|)\|x\|_X.}
$$

These are [equivalent norms](../normed-vector-space.md#equivalent-norms). Applied to $x_n-x_m$, the inequalities show that a [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) is a [Cauchy sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#cauchy-sequence) for either [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) exactly when it is one for the other. Applied to $x_n-x$, they show that the two [norms](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) give the same limits in $X$. Thus every [Cauchy sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#cauchy-sequence) has a limit in one [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) precisely when it does in the other:

$$
\boxed{(X,\|\cdot\|_T)\text{ is complete}\iff(X,\|\cdot\|_X)\text{ is complete}.}
$$

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Let $(x_n)$ be a [Cauchy sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#cauchy-sequence) in the [graph norm](../functional-analysis.md#graph-norm), and set $y_n=Tx_n$. Since

$$
\|y_n-y_m\|_Y\leq\|x_n-x_m\|_T,
$$

$(y_n)$ is a [Cauchy sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#cauchy-sequence) in the [Banach space](../banach-space.md) $Y$. Write $y_n\to y\in Y$.

Use the bounded [right inverse](../function.md#right-inverse) to remove the part seen by $T$:

$$
w_n=x_n-Sy_n,\qquad Tw_n=Tx_n-TSy_n=y_n-y_n=0.
$$

Thus $w_n\in W$, and the [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm) bound gives

$$
\|w_n-w_m\|_X\leq\|x_n-x_m\|_X+\|S\|\|y_n-y_m\|_Y
\leq(1+\|S\|)\|x_n-x_m\|_T.
$$

The assumed [completeness](../topological-analysis.md#completeness) of $W$ supplies $w\in W$ with $w_n\to w$ in $\|\cdot\|_X$. Put $x=w+Sy\in X$. The [linearity](../vector-space.md#linearity) of $T$ and the [right inverse](../function.md#right-inverse) identity give $Tx=y$. Therefore

$$
\|x_n-x\|_T\leq\|w_n-w\|_X+(1+\|S\|)\|y_n-y\|_Y\longrightarrow0.
$$

We have proved the [completeness of a graph norm from a bounded right inverse](../functional-analysis.md#completeness-of-a-graph-norm-from-a-bounded-right-inverse):

$$
\boxed{(X,\|\cdot\|_T)\text{ is a Banach space}.}
$$

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/d/i)

By the [mean value theorem](../calculus.md#mean-value-theorem), a [continuously differentiable function](../multivariable-calculus.md#continuously-differentiable-function) with zero [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) on $[-1,1]$ is constant. Thus the [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) of the [differentiation operator](../linear-operator-theory.md#differentiation-operator) is

$$
\boxed{W=\{x\mapsto a:a\in\mathbb R\}=\langle1\rangle.}
$$

It is a [closed set](../topology.md#closed-set) by the [finite-dimensional subspace is closed](../normed-vector-space.md#finite-dimensional-subspace-is-closed) theorem, applied in the [normed space](../normed-vector-space.md) $(C^1([-1,1]),\|\cdot\|_\infty)$. It is proper because the [function](../function.md) $x\mapsto x$ has nonzero [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative).

For $n\geq1$, take $u_n(x)=\sin(nx)$. Its [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm) is at most one, whereas $u_n'(0)=n$ and $|u_n'(x)|\leq n$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\|Tu_n\|_\infty=n,\quad\|u_n\|_\infty\leq1;\qquad T\text{ is unbounded}.}
$$

<h4 id="1/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/d/ii)

Choose the [continuously differentiable functions](../multivariable-calculus.md#continuously-differentiable-function)

$$
f_n(x)=\sqrt{x^2+n^{-2}}.
$$

The estimate $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\leq a+b$ for $a,b\geq0$ gives

$$
0\leq f_n(x)-|x|\leq\frac1n,\qquad
\|f_n-|\cdot|\|_\infty=\frac1n.
$$

Thus $f_n\to|\cdot|$ by [uniform convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#uniform-convergence), and the [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) gives $\|f_n-f_m\|_\infty\leq n^{-1}+m^{-1}$. The [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) is a [Cauchy sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#cauchy-sequence) in $X$, but its unique possible [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm) limit is $x\mapsto|x|$, which is not [differentiable](../analysis.md#differentiable-function) at zero. That limit is outside $X$, so

$$
\boxed{(C^1([-1,1]),\|\cdot\|_\infty)\text{ is incomplete}.}
$$

<h4 id="1/d/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/d/iii)

The [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) $W=\langle1\rangle$ is [complete](../topological-analysis.md#completeness): the map $a\mapsto a1$ is an [isometry](../riemannian-geometry.md#isometry) from $\mathbb R$ with its absolute-value [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) onto $W$ with the [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm).

Define a [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map) $S:Y\to X$ by

$$
(Sg)(x)=\int_0^x g(t)\,dt.
$$

The [fundamental theorem of calculus](../calculus.md#fundamental-theorem-of-calculus) gives $Sg\in C^1([-1,1])$ and $TSg=g$. Also,

$$
|(Sg)(x)|\leq|x|\|g\|_\infty\leq\|g\|_\infty,
\qquad\|Sg\|_X\leq\|g\|_Y.
$$

Thus $S$ is a bounded [right inverse](../function.md#right-inverse), and $Y=C([-1,1])$ is a [Banach space](../banach-space.md). Applying part (c),

$$
\boxed{\bigl(C^1([-1,1]),\|f\|_\infty+\|f'\|_\infty\bigr)\text{ is complete}.}
$$

<h3 id="1/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#1/e)

Take the [normed space](../normed-vector-space.md) $X=C^1([-1,1])$ with the [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm), and let $T:X\to C([-1,1])$ be the inclusion $Tf=f$. This is an injective [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map), so its [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is the [complete](../topological-analysis.md#completeness) zero space. Its [graph norm](../functional-analysis.md#graph-norm) is

$$
\boxed{\|f\|_T=2\|f\|_\infty.}
$$

The [Cauchy sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#cauchy-sequence) $f_n(x)=\sqrt{x^2+n^{-2}}$ from part (d)(ii) still has no limit in $X$ for this [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm), because its only possible limit is $|x|$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\ker T=\{0\}\text{ is complete, but }(X,\|\cdot\|_T)\text{ is incomplete}.}
$$

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

For a [normed space](../normed-vector-space.md) $X$, let $X^*$ be its [continuous dual space](../continuous-dual-space.md) and $X^{**}=(X^*)^*$ its [bidual space](../dual-space.md#bidual-of-a-normed-space). Its [canonical embedding into the bidual](../functional-analysis.md#canonical-embedding-into-the-bidual) is

$$
J_X:X\to X^{**},\qquad(J_Xx)(\ell)=\ell(x).
$$

The definition of a [reflexive space](../functional-analysis.md#reflexive-space) in this setting is

$$
\boxed{J_X(X)=X^{**}.}
$$

The [Hahn-Banach theorem](../functional-analysis.md#hahn-banach-theorem) makes $J_X$ a [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map) that is an [isometry](../riemannian-geometry.md#isometry). For any [normed space](../normed-vector-space.md) $Z$, its [continuous dual space](../continuous-dual-space.md) is a [Banach space](../banach-space.md): a [Cauchy sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#cauchy-sequence) $(\ell_n)$ in the [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm) has scalar limits $\ell(z)=\lim_n\ell_n(z)$, and the uniform Cauchy bound proves both boundedness of $\ell$ and $\|\ell_n-\ell\|\to0$. Apply this with $Z=X^*$ to see that $X^{**}$ is [complete](../topological-analysis.md#completeness). If $J_X$ is onto, the [isometry](../riemannian-geometry.md#isometry) transfers this [completeness](../topological-analysis.md#completeness) to $X$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{X\text{ reflexive}\ \Longrightarrow\ X\text{ complete}.}
$$

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

The answer is

$$
\boxed{p=1,\qquad p'=\infty.}
$$

Indeed, [Lebesgue measure](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure) on $E\subset\mathbb R^n$ is a [sigma-finite measure](../measure.md#sigma-finite-measure), and [duality of Lp spaces](../continuous-dual-space.md#duality-of-lp-spaces) gives $(L^1(E))^*=L^\infty(E)$. The restricted [Lebesgue measure](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure) is a [non-atomic measure](../measure.md#non-atomic-measure) and $E$ has positive measure, so the [Dual of L infinity is larger than L1](../continuous-dual-space.md#dual-of-l-infinity-is-larger-than-l1): the canonical integral representation does not exhaust $(L^\infty(E))^*$.

For $1<p<\infty$, the [Holder conjugate exponent](../functional-analysis.md#conjugate-exponents) also lies strictly between one and infinity. [Duality of Lp spaces](../continuous-dual-space.md#duality-of-lp-spaces) then holds in both directions, in agreement with [Reflexivity of Lp spaces](../continuous-dual-space.md#reflexivity-of-lp-spaces). For $p=\infty$, the first required identification already fails.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Let $p'$ be the [Holder conjugate exponent](../functional-analysis.md#conjugate-exponents) of $p$, with $1/\infty=0$. The [indicator function](../measure-theory.md#indicator-function) $\mathbf1_{(-h,h)}$ has [Lp norm](../real-analysis.md#lp-norm) $(2h)^{1/p'}$ in $L^{p'}$, including the value one when $p'=\infty$. The [Holder inequality](../functional-analysis.md#holder-inequality) therefore gives

$$
|T_hf|\leq h^{-1/p'}\|f\|_p\|\mathbf1_{(-h,h)}\|_{p'}
=2^{1/p'}\|f\|_p.
$$

The [Lebesgue integral](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-integral) is unchanged by equality [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere), so $T_h$ is well-defined on the [Lp space](../measure-theory.md#lp-space); its [linearity](../vector-space.md#linearity) and the bound put it in the [continuous dual space](../continuous-dual-space.md) $X^*$.

For $p<\infty$, the [function](../function.md) $f_h=(2h)^{-1/p}\mathbf1_{(-h,h)}$ has [Lp norm](../real-analysis.md#lp-norm) one and satisfies

$$
T_hf_h=h^{-1/p'}(2h)^{1-1/p}=2^{1/p'}.
$$

For $p=\infty$, use $f_h=\mathbf1_{(-h,h)}$, whose [L-infinity norm](../real-analysis.md#l-infinity-norm) is one and whose image is $2$. Thus all endpoints are included in

$$
\boxed{T_h\in X^*,\qquad\|T_h\|=2^{1/p'}\quad(0<h<1).}
$$

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Put $M=2^{1/p'}$, the common [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm) from part (b). Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N$ with $\|f-f_N\|_p<\varepsilon/(3M)$. Since $f_N\in Y$, the scalar limit of $T_hf_N$ exists. The [Cauchy criterion for a function limit](../topological-analysis.md#cauchy-criterion-for-a-function-limit) gives $\delta>0$ such that

$$
|T_hf_N-T_{\widetilde h}f_N|<\frac\varepsilon3
\qquad(0<h,\widetilde h<\delta).
$$

Using the [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) and the uniform [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm) bound,

$$
\begin{aligned}
|T_hf-T_{\widetilde h}f|
&\leq|T_h(f-f_N)|+|T_hf_N-T_{\widetilde h}f_N|+|T_{\widetilde h}(f_N-f)|\\
&\leq2M\|f-f_N\|_p+|T_hf_N-T_{\widetilde h}f_N|<\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the requested estimate. The [completeness](../topological-analysis.md#completeness) of $\mathbb R$ makes the scalar [Cauchy criterion for a function limit](../topological-analysis.md#cauchy-criterion-for-a-function-limit) sufficient, hence $\lim_{h\to0^+}T_hf$ exists and $f\in Y$.

For $u,v\in Y$ and real $a,b$, the [linearity](../vector-space.md#linearity) of $T_h$ gives $T_h(au+bv)=aT_hu+bT_hv$, which also has a limit. Thus $Y$ is a [linear subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace). The preceding argument shows it contains every [norm convergence](../normed-vector-space.md#norm-convergence) limit of its [sequences](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence), so the sequential criterion for [closed sets](../topology.md#closed-set) in a [metric space](../topological-analysis.md#metric-space) gives

$$
\boxed{Y\text{ is a closed linear subspace of }X.}
$$

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

<h5 id="2/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/d/i)

Use $D=C([-1,1])$, restricted to $(-1,1)$ and regarded as a [linear subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) of $L^p((-1,1))$. The [density of continuous functions in Lp on an interval](../measure-theory.md#density-of-continuous-functions-in-lp-on-an-interval) says that $D$ is a [dense subspace](../topological-vector-space.md#dense-subspace) whenever $1\leq p<\infty$.

For $g\in D$, the [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm) gives

$$
|T_hg|\leq h^{-1/p'}\int_{-h}^h|g(x)|\,dx
\leq2\|g\|_\infty h^{1-1/p'}=2\|g\|_\infty h^{1/p}\longrightarrow0.
$$

In particular $D\subseteq Y$, with zero limit. Part (c), together with $D$ being a [dense subspace](../topological-vector-space.md#dense-subspace), implies $Y=X$. To identify the limit throughout $X$, for $f\in X$ and $g\in D$ use

$$
|T_hf|\leq M\|f-g\|_p+2\|g\|_\infty h^{1/p},\qquad M=2^{1/p'}.
$$

First choose $g$ in the [dense subspace](../topological-vector-space.md#dense-subspace) to make the first term arbitrarily small, then let $h\to0^+$. This is [convergence on a dense subspace under a uniform operator bound](../topological-vector-space.md#convergence-on-a-dense-subspace-under-a-uniform-operator-bound), and proves

$$
\boxed{Tf=\lim_{h\to0^+}T_hf=0\quad(f\in L^p((-1,1))),\qquad\|T\|=0.}
$$

The zero [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) belongs to the [continuous dual space](../continuous-dual-space.md) $X^*$.

<h4 id="2/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

<h5 id="2/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/d/ii)

When $p=\infty$, $p'=1$ and $T_hf=h^{-1}\int_{-h}^h f(x)\,dx$. Take the bounded [measurable function](../measure-theory.md#measurable-function)

$$
f(x)=\begin{cases}\sin(\log|x|),&x\ne0,\\0,&x=0.\end{cases}
$$

It represents an element of the [Lp space](../measure-theory.md#lp-space) $L^\infty((-1,1))$, with [L-infinity norm](../real-analysis.md#l-infinity-norm) at most one. Since

$$
\int_0^h\sin(\log x)\,dx
=\frac h2\bigl(\sin(\log h)-\cos(\log h)\bigr),
$$

where the lower endpoint contributes zero, its symmetric [Lebesgue integral](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-integral) gives

$$
T_hf=\sin(\log h)-\cos(\log h).
$$

For $h_n=e^{-2\pi n}$ and $\widetilde h_n=e^{-(2\pi n+\pi)}$, both [sequences](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) tend to zero through $(0,1)$, but $T_{h_n}f=-1$ and $T_{\widetilde h_n}f=1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f\notin Y,\qquad Y\subsetneq L^\infty((-1,1)).}
$$

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

Writing $\mathcal B(X)$ for the [Banach space of bounded linear operators](../topological-vector-space.md#banach-space-of-bounded-linear-operators) on $X$, the [spectrum](../linear-operator-theory.md#spectrum-functional-analysis) is

$$
\boxed{\sigma(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb C:\lambda I-T\text{ has no bounded two-sided inverse in }\mathcal B(X)\}.}
$$

Here $I$ is the [identity operator](../vector-space.md#identity-operator). Since $X$ is a [Banach space](../banach-space.md), the [bounded inverse theorem](../functional-analysis.md#bounded-inverse-theorem) makes this equivalent to failure of bijectivity of $\lambda I-T$.

The [spectral radius](../analysis.md#spectral-radius) is

$$
\boxed{r(T)=\sup\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma(T)\}.}
$$

For a nonzero complex [Banach space](../banach-space.md), the [spectrum](../linear-operator-theory.md#spectrum-functional-analysis) of a [bounded linear operator](../topological-vector-space.md#continuous-linear-operator) is nonempty and [compact](../topology.md#compact-space), so the [supremum](../real-analysis.md#supremum) is a maximum. If the zero space is allowed, its empty [spectrum](../linear-operator-theory.md#spectrum-functional-analysis) is assigned [spectral radius](../analysis.md#spectral-radius) zero.

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

The [Neumann series](../banach-algebra.md#neumann-series) criterion states that $\|A\|<1$ for a [bounded linear operator](../topological-vector-space.md#continuous-linear-operator) on a [Banach space](../banach-space.md) implies

$$
(I-A)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A^n,
$$

with convergence in the [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm). In particular, $\|T\|<1$ suffices for invertibility of $I-T$.

If $|\lambda|>\|T\|$, apply this criterion to $A=T/\lambda$. It gives

$$
(\lambda I-T)^{-1}=\lambda^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(T/\lambda)^n.
$$

Thus no such $\lambda$ belongs to the [spectrum](../linear-operator-theory.md#spectrum-functional-analysis), and

$$
\boxed{r(T)\leq\|T\|.}
$$

For each integer $j\geq1$, if $|\lambda|^j>\|T^j\|$, then $\lambda^jI-T^j$ is invertible by the same argument. The commuting factorization

$$
\lambda^jI-T^j=(\lambda I-T)\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\lambda^{j-1-k}T^k
$$

then gives a bounded two-sided inverse for $\lambda I-T$, namely the displayed sum multiplied by $(\lambda^jI-T^j)^{-1}$. Hence every $\lambda\in\sigma(T)$ satisfies $|\lambda|^j\leq\|T^j\|$. Taking the [supremum](../real-analysis.md#supremum) over the [spectrum](../linear-operator-theory.md#spectrum-functional-analysis) and then the [infimum](../real-analysis.md#infimum) over $j$ proves

$$
\boxed{r(T)\leq\inf_{j\geq1}\|T^j\|^{1/j}.}
$$

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

For a [continuous function](../calculus.md#continuous-function) $g$ on $[0,1]$, the [L-infinity norm](../real-analysis.md#l-infinity-norm) of its restriction to $(0,1)$ equals its [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm) on $[0,1]$. Indeed, values arbitrarily close to a maximum occur on an interval of positive [Lebesgue measure](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure), even if the maximum lies at an endpoint. Also, [continuous functions](../calculus.md#continuous-function) equal [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere) on $(0,1)$ are equal everywhere on $[0,1]$. Thus restriction embeds $C([0,1])$ isometrically as a [linear subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) of $X$.

The [point evaluation functional](../topological-vector-space.md#point-evaluation-functional) $E_{x_0}(g)=g(x_0)$ is complex-linear and satisfies $|E_{x_0}(g)|\leq\|g\|_\infty$. The constant [function](../function.md) one attains equality, so $\|E_{x_0}\|=1$. The [complex Hahn-Banach theorem](../functional-analysis.md#complex-hahn-banach-theorem) extends this [bounded linear functional](../topological-vector-space.md#continuous-linear-functional) from that [linear subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) to $X$, preserving its [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\ell_{x_0}\in X^*,\qquad\|\ell_{x_0}\|=1,\qquad\ell_{x_0}(g)=g(x_0)\quad(g\in C([0,1])).}
$$

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

Write $\psi=\psi_{x_0}$; its value at $x_0$, if that point is in $(0,1)$, may be assigned arbitrarily because [Lp spaces](../measure-theory.md#lp-space) identify [functions](../function.md) equal [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere). Its [L-infinity norm](../real-analysis.md#l-infinity-norm) is one.

The supplied estimate shows that $\psi$ cannot agree [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere) with any [continuous function](../calculus.md#continuous-function) $q$: choosing $g=-q$ would give $0\geq|q(x_0)|+1$. Hence

$$
V=C([0,1])\oplus\langle\psi\rangle
$$

is a [direct sum](../vector-space.md#direct-sum), so every element has a unique expression $g+c\psi$.

For any complex $a$ with $|a|\leq1$, define the [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional)

$$
L_a(g+c\psi)=g(x_0)+ca.
$$

If $c\ne0$, the supplied estimate, applied to $g/c$, and [absolute homogeneity of a norm](../normed-vector-space.md#absolute-homogeneity-of-a-norm) give

$$
\|g+c\psi\|_\infty=|c|\|g/c+\psi\|_\infty
\geq|g(x_0)|+|c|
\geq|g(x_0)+ca|.
$$

For $c=0$, the same bound follows from [point evaluation](../topological-vector-space.md#point-evaluation-functional). Thus $\|L_a\|\leq1$, and $L_a(1)=1$ makes its [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm) exactly one. The [complex Hahn-Banach theorem](../functional-analysis.md#complex-hahn-banach-theorem) extends $L_a$ to a norm-one [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) on all of $X$, with value $a$ at $\psi$.

Conversely every norm-one extension satisfies $|\ell_{x_0}(\psi)|\leq\|\psi\|_\infty=1$. We have therefore determined all possible values:

$$
\boxed{\{\ell_{x_0}(\psi_{x_0}):\ell_{x_0}\text{ is a norm-one extension of }E_{x_0}\}
=\{a\in\mathbb C:|a|\leq1\}.}
$$

In particular, taking $a=0$ and $a=1$ proves [nonuniqueness of norm-preserving point evaluation extensions](../topological-vector-space.md#nonuniqueness-of-norm-preserving-point-evaluation-extensions).

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/c/i)

Because the [linear functionals](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) $\ell_0,\ell_1$ agree with endpoint [point evaluations](../topological-vector-space.md#point-evaluation-functional) on [continuous functions](../calculus.md#continuous-function),

$$
\ell_i(e_j)=\delta_{ij}\qquad(i,j\in\{0,1\}).
$$

The endpoint values also make $e_0,e_1$ [linearly independent](../vector-space.md#linear-independence). By its definition, the [image of a linear map](../vector-space.md#image-of-a-linear-map) $T$ lies in $E=\langle e_0,e_1\rangle$. Conversely $Te_j=e_j$, so $T$ acts as the [identity operator](../vector-space.md#identity-operator) on $E$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Im}T=\langle e_0,e_1\rangle,\qquad\operatorname{rank}T=2.}
$$

For every $f$, $Tf\in E$, whence $T^2f=Tf$. It is a [linear projection](../vector-space.md#projection-linear-algebra), and an annihilating [polynomial](../polynomial.md) is

$$
\boxed{p(z)=z(z-1),\qquad p(T)=T^2-T=0.}
$$

<h4 id="3/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/c/ii)

Let $M=\|\,|e_0|+|e_1|\,\|_\infty$. Since both [linear functionals](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) have [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm) one, for every $f\in X$ the [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) gives

$$
|(Tf)(x)|\leq|\ell_0(f)||e_0(x)|+|\ell_1(f)||e_1(x)|
\leq\|f\|_\infty\bigl(|e_0(x)|+|e_1(x)|\bigr).
$$

Taking the [essential supremum](../measure-theory.md#essential-supremum) proves $\|T\|\leq M$.

For the reverse bound, [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) and the [extreme value theorem](../real-analysis.md#extreme-value-theorem) give $x_*\in[0,1]$ with $|e_0(x_*)|+|e_1(x_*)|=M$. Choose complex $a_0,a_1$ of modulus one so that $a_je_j(x_*)=|e_j(x_*)|$, taking $a_j=1$ if $e_j(x_*)=0$. The [continuous function](../calculus.md#continuous-function)

$$
f_*(x)=(1-x)a_0+xa_1
$$

has [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm) one by the [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) and its endpoint values. Since $\ell_j(f_*)=a_j$,

$$
(Tf_*)(x_*)=a_0e_0(x_*)+a_1e_1(x_*)=M.
$$

The output is continuous, so its [L-infinity norm](../real-analysis.md#l-infinity-norm) equals its [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm), including when $x_*$ is an endpoint. Therefore $\|T\|\geq M$, and the exact [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm) is

$$
\boxed{\|T\|=\|\,|e_0|+|e_1|\,\|_\infty.}
$$

This is the [norm of a two-endpoint interpolation projection](../vector-space.md#norm-of-a-two-endpoint-interpolation-projection).

<h4 id="3/c/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/c/iii)

From $Te_0=e_0\ne0$, one is an [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue). Zero is also an [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue): the nonzero [continuous function](../calculus.md#continuous-function) $q(x)=x(1-x)$ has both endpoint values zero, so $Tq=0$.

For $\lambda\notin\{0,1\}$, the [linear projection](../vector-space.md#projection-linear-algebra) identity $T^2=T$ gives an explicit bounded inverse:

$$
(\lambda I-T)^{-1}=\frac1\lambda(I-T)+\frac1{\lambda-1}T.
$$

Multiplying on either side by $\lambda I-T$ yields $(I-T)+T=I$. There are no other points in the [spectrum](../linear-operator-theory.md#spectrum-functional-analysis), and thus the [spectrum of a nontrivial bounded projection](../linear-operator-theory.md#spectrum-of-a-nontrivial-bounded-projection) here is

$$
\boxed{\sigma(T)=\{0,1\},\qquad r(T)=1.}
$$

Let $M=\|T\|$, as calculated in part (ii). Its endpoint values ensure $1\leq M<\infty$. Since $T^j=T$ for all $j\geq1$,

$$
\|T^j\|^{1/j}=M^{1/j}\longrightarrow1,
\qquad\inf_{j\geq1}\|T^j\|^{1/j}=1.
$$

This verifies the [spectral radius formula](../analysis.md#spectral-radius-formula) explicitly:

$$
\boxed{r(T)=\inf_{j\geq1}\|T^j\|^{1/j}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|T^j\|^{1/j}=1.}
$$
