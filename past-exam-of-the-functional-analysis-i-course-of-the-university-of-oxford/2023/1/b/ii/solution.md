<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [bounded linear functional](../../../../../continuous-linear-functional.md) from part (i) satisfies $|Tx|\leq\|T\|\|x\|_X$, where $\|T\|$ is its [operator norm](../../../../../operator-norm.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{\|x\|_X\leq\|x\|_T\leq(1+\|T\|)\|x\|_X.}
$$

These are [equivalent norms](../../../../../equivalent-norms.md). Applied to $x_n-x_m$, the inequalities show that a [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) is a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) for either [norm](../../../../../norm.md) exactly when it is one for the other. Applied to $x_n-x$, they show that the two [norms](../../../../../norm.md) give the same limits in $X$. Thus every [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) has a limit in one [norm](../../../../../norm.md) precisely when it does in the other:

$$
\boxed{(X,\|\cdot\|_T)\text{ is complete}\iff(X,\|\cdot\|_X)\text{ is complete}.}
$$
