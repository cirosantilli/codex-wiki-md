<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Both terms in the [graph norm](../../../../graph-norm.md) are finite and nonnegative. Moreover, $\|x\|_T=0$ forces $\|x\|_X=0$, hence $x=0$; conversely $T0=0$ gives $\|0\|_T=0$. This proves [definiteness of a norm](../../../../definiteness-of-a-norm.md).

The [linearity](../../../../linearity.md) of $T$ and [absolute homogeneity of a norm](../../../../absolute-homogeneity-of-a-norm.md) give, for each real $a$,

$$
\|ax\|_T=|a|\|x\|_X+\|aTx\|_Y=|a|\|x\|_T.
$$

Applying the [triangle inequality](../../../../triangle-inequality.md) in both [normed spaces](../../../../normed-vector-space-split.md) gives

$$
\|x+z\|_T=\|x+z\|_X+\|Tx+Tz\|_Y\leq\|x\|_T+\|z\|_T.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\|\cdot\|_T\text{ is a norm on }X.}
$$
