<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Take the [normed space](../../../../normed-vector-space-split.md) $X=C^1([-1,1])$ with the [supremum norm](../../../../supremum-norm.md), and let $T:X\to C([-1,1])$ be the inclusion $Tf=f$. This is an injective [linear map](../../../../linear-map.md), so its [kernel](../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) is the [complete](../../../../completeness.md) zero space. Its [graph norm](../../../../graph-norm.md) is

$$
\boxed{\|f\|_T=2\|f\|_\infty.}
$$

The [Cauchy sequence](../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) $f_n(x)=\sqrt{x^2+n^{-2}}$ from part (d)(ii) still has no limit in $X$ for this [norm](../../../../norm.md), because its only possible limit is $|x|$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\ker T=\{0\}\text{ is complete, but }(X,\|\cdot\|_T)\text{ is incomplete}.}
$$
