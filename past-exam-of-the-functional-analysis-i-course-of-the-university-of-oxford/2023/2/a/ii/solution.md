<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The answer is

$$
\boxed{p=1,\qquad p'=\infty.}
$$

Indeed, [Lebesgue measure](../../../../../lebesgue-measure.md) on $E\subset\mathbb R^n$ is a [sigma-finite measure](../../../../../sigma-finite-measure.md), and [duality of Lp spaces](../../../../../duality-of-lp-spaces.md) gives $(L^1(E))^*=L^\infty(E)$. The restricted [Lebesgue measure](../../../../../lebesgue-measure.md) is a [non-atomic measure](../../../../../non-atomic-measure.md) and $E$ has positive measure, so the [Dual of L infinity is larger than L1](../../../../../dual-of-l-infinity-is-larger-than-l1.md): the canonical integral representation does not exhaust $(L^\infty(E))^*$.

For $1<p<\infty$, the [Holder conjugate exponent](../../../../../conjugate-exponents.md) also lies strictly between one and infinity. [Duality of Lp spaces](../../../../../duality-of-lp-spaces.md) then holds in both directions, in agreement with [Reflexivity of Lp spaces](../../../../../reflexivity-of-lp-spaces.md). For $p=\infty$, the first required identification already fails.
