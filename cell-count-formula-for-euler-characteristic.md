# Cell-count formula for Euler characteristic

↑ **Parent:** [Euler characteristic](euler-characteristic.md)

Let $X$ be a finite [CW complex](cw-complex.md), with $c_i$ [topological cells](topological-cell.md) of [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) $i$ and none above $d$. Its [Euler characteristic](euler-characteristic.md) is the displayed alternating sum. For a finite [simplicial complex](simplicial-complex.md), $c_i$ counts its $i$-dimensional [simplexes](simplex.md), each counted once rather than once for each containing [simplex](simplex.md). In [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) two this becomes $V-E+F$. A [topological cell](topological-cell.md)'s interior is counted; identified parts of its boundary are counted in their own lower-dimensional [topological cells](topological-cell.md).[Algebraic Topology, §2.2, p. 146, definition of Euler characteristic and Theorem 2.44](algebraic-topology-by-allen-hatcher.md)

The [cellular homology theorem](cellular-homology-theorem.md) identifies [cellular homology](cellular-chain-complex.md) with [singular homology](singular-homology.md), so the count equals the alternating rational [Betti numbers](betti-number.md). These are the free ranks of [integral homology](integral-homology.md); [torsion subgroups](torsion-subgroup.md) do not contribute to them. Over any [field](field.md), the alternating [homology](homology-split.md) dimensions still give the same [Euler characteristic](euler-characteristic.md), although individual [Betti numbers](betti-number.md) can depend on the [field](field.md).
