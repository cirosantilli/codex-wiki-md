# Bond order

↑ **Parent:** [Molecular orbital theory](molecular-orbital-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_order)

In the elementary [molecular orbital theory](molecular-orbital-theory.md) count, [bond order](bond-order.md) is half the excess of [electrons](electron.md) in [bonding molecular orbitals](bonding-orbital.md) over those in [antibonding molecular orbitals](antibonding-orbital.md). Thus adding one [electron](electron.md) to a [bonding molecular orbital](bonding-orbital.md) raises $b$ by $1/2$, while adding one to an [antibonding molecular orbital](antibonding-orbital.md) lowers it by $1/2$. The configurations drawn above give $1$, $1/2$, and $0$. This count summarizes net [covalent bond](covalent-bond-split.md) occupancy; it is neither a measured [energy](energy-split.md) nor a universal test for every kind of binding. See [Chemistry 2e, 8.4: molecular orbital bond order](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/8-4-molecular-orbital-theory).
