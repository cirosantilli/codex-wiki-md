<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In units $\hbar=c=1$, a dimensionless [action](../../../../../action.md) fixes $[\mathcal L]=4$. Canonical [kinetic terms](../../../../../kinetic-term.md) then give $[\phi]=[A_\mu]=1$, $[\psi]=3/2$ and $[\partial_\mu]=1$. If an interaction operator has dimension $d_v$, its coefficient has [mass dimension](../../../../../mass-dimension.md) $[g_v]=4-d_v$. Thus scalar quartic, Yukawa and [gauge couplings](../../../../../gauge-coupling.md) are dimensionless; a scalar cubic coupling has dimension one, a four-fermion coupling has dimension minus two, and the Weinberg coefficient has dimension minus one. These are engineering dimensions; anomalous scaling is an additional quantum effect.

For a connected graph with $L$ independent loop momenta, $I_B$ [boson](../../../../../boson.md) lines, $I_F$ [fermion](../../../../../fermion.md) lines and $r_v$ [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) at vertex $v$, ordinary covariant-gauge ultraviolet counting gives the [superficial degree of divergence](../../../../../superficial-degree-of-divergence.md)

$$
D=4L-2I_B-I_F+\sum_vr_v
=4-E_B-\frac32E_F-\sum_v[g_v].
$$

The second form follows from $L=I_B+I_F-V+1$ and counting field ends at vertices. It presumes the canonical large-momentum propagators; unitary-gauge vector propagators can obscure the cancellations of a renormalizable gauge theory. A nonnegative $D$ signals a possible overall [ultraviolet divergence](../../../../../ultraviolet-divergence.md), not proof of one. Symmetries can cancel it, while a graph with $D<0$ can still contain divergent subgraphs.

With only operators of dimension at most four, increasing the vertex count does not require [counterterms](../../../../../counterterm.md) of unbounded dimension. Subject to the necessary symmetry identities and absence of gauge anomalies, scalar quartic theory, Yukawa theories and the [Standard Model](../../../../../standard-model-split.md) are perturbatively renormalizable when all required interactions are included. In particular, coupling a scalar to other fields can require a scalar quartic [counterterm](../../../../../counterterm.md) even if its coefficient was initially set to zero. The [Higgs mechanism](../../../../../higgs-mechanism.md) does not destroy renormalizability of the underlying anomaly-free gauge theory.

By contrast, repeated insertions of the dimension-six [Fermi interaction](../../../../../fermi-interaction.md) or a dimension-five operator allow increasingly high-dimensional [counterterms](../../../../../counterterm.md). Such terms are useful in an [effective field theory](../../../../../effective-field-theory.md) with a stated expansion in [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) over a high scale; nonrenormalizable does not mean unpredictive at a fixed order. A scalar $\phi^6$ interaction and perturbative Einstein gravity in four dimensions provide other examples. Renormalizability also does not assert small coupling at every scale or a complete ultraviolet theory.
