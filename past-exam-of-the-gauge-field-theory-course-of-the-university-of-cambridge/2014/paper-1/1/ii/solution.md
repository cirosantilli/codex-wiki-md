<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For eigenvalues $(v,-v,0)$, all entries are distinct. The $12$ difference has magnitude $2|v|$, while both the $13$ and $23$ differences have magnitude $|v|$. The [adjoint scalar gauge-boson mass matrix](../../../../../adjoint-scalar-gauge-boson-mass-matrix.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{m_{3,8}=0,\qquad m_{1,2}=2g|v|,\qquad m_{4,5,6,7}=g|v|.}
$$

Only the two diagonal generators commute with the vacuum. Its unbroken group is the [maximal torus](../../../../../maximal-torus.md)

$$
\boxed{H=\{\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\alpha},e^{i\beta},e^{-i(\alpha+\beta)})\}\cong U(1)\times U(1).}
$$

The two real fields belonging to each $ij$ pair combine into oppositely charged conjugate vector states with equal [mass](../../../../../mass.md). The further equality between the $13$ and $23$ masses comes from the equally spaced vacuum eigenvalues; the surviving Abelian group alone does not [force](../../../../../force-split.md) distinct root-charge sectors to have equal masses. Thus a fourfold massive degeneracy occurs in both examples, but its relation to the unbroken continuous [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) differs. The scalar potential has not been specified, so no additional symmetry of that potential or physical scalar [mass](../../../../../mass.md) spectrum can be inferred.
