# Geodesics on a circular cylinder

↑ **Parent:** [Intrinsic flatness of a circular cylinder](intrinsic-flatness-of-a-circular-cylinder.md)

In coordinates $(\theta,z)$ on a [circular cylinder](circular-cylinder.md) of radius $a$, the [induced metric](induced-metric.md) is $a^2d\theta^2+dz^2$. Its coefficients are constant, so the [Christoffel symbols](christoffel-symbol.md) vanish and the affinely parametrized [geodesic equation](geodesic-equation.md) is $\theta''=z''=0$. The nonconstant solutions are generators when $c=0$, circular parallels when $d=0$, and helices when both are nonzero. Unrolling the cylinder with coordinate $u=a\theta$ makes all these curves straight lines in the Euclidean metric. Unit-speed parametrization additionally requires $a^2c^2+d^2=1$.

For a worked [helix](helix.md) on a [circular cylinder](circular-cylinder.md) of radius $1$, choose $\theta(t)=2\pi t,z(t)=2t$ for $0\leq t\leq1$. Unrolling gives the straight segment $(u,z)=(2\pi t,2t)$, so its [arc length](arc-length.md) is $\sqrt{4\pi^2+4}$. Its acceleration in space points normally to the surface, consistent with the [geodesic equation](geodesic-equation.md).

This [geodesic](geodesic.md) is not globally shortest between its endpoints: they share the same angular position, and the vertical generator has [length](length.md) $2$. The [helix](helix.md) minimizes [length](length.md) among paths with the same endpoints and one full winding, because their unrolled lifts join $(0,0)$ to $(2\pi,2)$. In general, [geodesics](geodesic.md) minimize sufficiently short subarcs; changing winding can produce a shorter route. Keeping this local/global distinction makes unrolling useful without mistaking every straight lift for an unrestricted shortest path.
