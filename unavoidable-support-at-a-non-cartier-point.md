# Unavoidable support at a non-Cartier point

↑ **Parent:** [Linear equivalence of Weil divisors](linear-equivalence-of-weil-divisors.md)

If a [Weil divisor](weil-divisor.md) $D$ on a [normal variety](normal-variety.md) is not Cartier at $x$, every linearly equivalent divisor has $x$ in its [support of a Weil divisor](support-of-a-weil-divisor.md). Otherwise that representative would vanish near $x$, expressing $D$ as a [principal Weil divisor](principal-weil-divisor.md) there. On $V(ac-b^2)$ the prime divisor $V(a,b)$ is not Cartier at the origin: its ideal has two independent generators modulo the [maximal ideal](maximal-ideal.md) times that ideal. This gives an unavoidable support point even for representatives with negative coefficients.
