# Interplanetary scintillation

↑ **Parent:** [Radio astronomy](radio-astronomy.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interplanetary_scintillation)

Interplanetary scintillation is rapid variation in the received strength of a compact astronomical radio source, produced by propagation through fluctuating [electron](electron.md) density in the [solar wind](solar-wind.md). Its dependence on source angular size can help distinguish compact sources. It is a propagation effect, distinct from intrinsic periodic emission by a [pulsar](pulsar.md).
