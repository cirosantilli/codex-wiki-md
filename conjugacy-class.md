# Conjugacy class

↑ **Parent:** [Conjugation action](conjugation-action.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugacy_class)

For an element $x$ of a [group](group-split.md) $G$, its [conjugacy class](conjugacy-class.md) is its [orbit of a group action](orbit-group-theory.md) under the [conjugation action](conjugation-action.md):

$$
\operatorname{Cl}_G(x)=\{gxg^{-1}:g\in G\}.
$$

Thus [conjugate group elements](conjugate-group-elements.md) describe the same operation after a change of labels. In a [symmetric group](symmetric-group.md), [conjugation](conjugation.md) renames the entries of every [permutation cycle](permutation-cycle.md):

$$
g(a_1\,a_2\,\ldots\,a_k)g^{-1}=(g(a_1)\,g(a_2)\,\ldots\,g(a_k)).
$$

For example, with $g=(123)$ and the [transposition](transposition-permutation.md) $t=(12)$, this gives $gtg^{-1}=(g(1)\,g(2))=(23)$. Products use [function composition](function-composition.md) of [permutations](permutation.md), with the rightmost factor applied first. The complete six-element example is [conjugacy classes of the symmetric group on three letters](conjugacy-classes-of-the-symmetric-group-on-three-letters.md).

The [conjugacy classes](conjugacy-class.md) form a [partition of a set](partition-of-a-set.md) of $G$, since [conjugacy](conjugate-group-elements.md) is an [equivalence relation](equivalence-relation.md). In a [symmetric group](symmetric-group.md), two [permutations](permutation.md) lie in the same [conjugacy class](conjugacy-class.md) precisely when they have the same [cycle type](cycle-type.md): renaming preserves the lengths of [permutation cycles](permutation-cycle.md), and matching equal-length [permutation cycles](permutation-cycle.md) constructs a renaming in the other direction. This compresses many labelled [permutations](permutation.md) into a few structural types. See [McKernan, Conjugation in Sn, lemma 6.2 and proposition 6.6](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-703-modern-algebra-spring-2013/ae501fc20d40fdb32f9e1edfa64da8f3_MIT18_703S13_pra_l_6.pdf).

The [stabilizer subgroup](stabilizer-subgroup.md) of $x$ under the [conjugation action](conjugation-action.md) is its [centralizer](centralizer.md) $C_G(x)$. Consequently the [orbit-stabilizer theorem](orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md) gives

$$
|\operatorname{Cl}_G(x)|=[G:C_G(x)],\qquad |\operatorname{Cl}_G(x)|=\frac{|G|}{|C_G(x)|}\quad\text{if }G\text{ is finite}.
$$

The counting mechanism is the [bijection](bijection.md) $gC_G(x)\mapsto gxg^{-1}$ from left [cosets](coset.md) onto the [conjugacy class](conjugacy-class.md): two choices of $g$ give the same result exactly when they differ on the right by an element of the [centralizer](centralizer.md). A large [centralizer](centralizer.md) means many choices of labels leave the operation unchanged, hence a small [conjugacy class](conjugacy-class.md). A [conjugacy class](conjugacy-class.md) has size one precisely when its element belongs to the [center of a group](center-of-a-group.md). The [class equation](class-equation.md) counts these [orbits of a group action](orbit-group-theory.md) together.
