# Two-domain system

↑ **Parent:** [Domain (biology)](domain-biology.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-domain_system)

The [two-domain system](two-domain-system.md) recognizes [bacteria](bacteria.md) and [archaea](archaea.md) as the two primary lineages, placing the nuclear ancestry of [eukaryotes](eukaryote.md) within [archaea](archaea.md). In this model, [archaea](archaea.md) as a complete [clade](clade.md) includes that [eukaryotic](eukaryote.md) lineage; restricting the name to [prokaryotic](prokaryote.md) members describes a different grouping. Williams and colleagues' study, published online in 2019 and in a 2020 journal issue, found support for this model and a close relationship to [Asgard archaea](asgard-archaea.md). See [Williams et al., 2020: Phylogenomics provides robust support for a two-domains tree of life](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-019-1040-x).

This updates the interpretation of the 1977 and 1990 classifications rather than undoing their discovery of deep differences between [bacteria](bacteria.md) and [archaea](archaea.md). Deep [phylogenetic trees](phylogenetic-tree.md) depend on the sampled [genes](gene.md), [organisms](organism.md) and inference model; the precise closest archaeal relatives and early sequence of events require further evidence. Bacterial ancestry of [mitochondria](mitochondria.md) through [endosymbiosis](endosymbiosis.md) is a separate part of [eukaryotic](eukaryote.md) history.
