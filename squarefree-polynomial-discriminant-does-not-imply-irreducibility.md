# Squarefree polynomial discriminant does not imply irreducibility

↑ **Parent:** [Polynomial discriminant](polynomial-discriminant.md)

A squarefree [polynomial discriminant](polynomial-discriminant.md) does not ensure that the polynomial defines a field of its stated degree. For example, $x^3-2x-1=(x+1)(x^2-x-1)$ has [discriminant](discriminant.md) five. Its roots have degrees one or two, so no root generates a cubic [number field](number-field.md). In particular $1,\alpha,\alpha^2$ cannot be a field [basis](basis.md) for any of its roots. The [squarefree discriminant certifies an integral basis](squarefree-discriminant-certifies-an-integral-basis.md) argument assumes that the polynomial is the minimal polynomial: then the [discriminant](discriminant.md) of its power [basis](basis.md) is the [field discriminant](field-discriminant.md) times the square of the order index. For a reducible squarefree polynomial the analogous rank belongs instead to its quotient algebra, which is a product of fields, not to one root field.
