# Carothers equation

↑ **Parent:** [Step-growth polymerization](step-growth-polymerization.md)

The balanced [Carothers equation](carothers-equation.md) follows immediately from [linear step-growth molecule count](linear-step-growth-molecule-count.md) and [functional-group conversion in step growth](functional-group-conversion-in-step-growth.md):

$$
X_n=\frac{1}{1-p}.
$$

The same [Carothers equation](carothers-equation.md) extends to unequal group counts once the limiting-group convention is specified.[Introduction, equations (1)–(6): Carothers equation, end-group count and limiting-$A$ conversion](generalization-of-the-carothers-equation-for-linear-step-growth-polymers-by-emilio-meaurio.md)

For an original balanced feed of $2000$ [monomer molecules](monomer-molecule.md), $1900$, $1960$, and $1990$ intermolecular joins give $p=0.95$, $0.98$, and $0.995$. The remaining counts are $100$, $40$, and $10$, hence number-average [degree of polymerization](degree-of-polymerization.md) values $20$, $50$, and $200$. The last thirty joins quadruple the average even though they consume only another $1.5\%$ of the initial groups of either type. These are exact synthetic counts, not experimental measurements.

The [derivative](derivative.md) $dX_n/dp=(1-p)^{-2}$ explains the sensitivity: near $p=0.99$, an absolute conversion uncertainty of $0.001$ changes the predicted [degree of polymerization](degree-of-polymerization.md) substantially. The exact balanced values at $p=0.989$ and $0.991$ are approximately $90.9$ and $111.1$, instead of $100$. An instrument reporting rounded “complete conversion” cannot distinguish those chain lengths. In a finite no-ring system, even one chain retains two ends; the maximum $p$ is $1-1/N_0$ and $X_n=N_0$. The formal divergence at $p=1$ is a large-system limit, not an infinite chain in a finite flask.
