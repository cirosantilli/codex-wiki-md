<h1 id="urysohn-s-metrization-theorem">Urysohn's metrization theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Metrizable space](metrizable-space.md)

Every [second-countable space](second-countable-space.md) that is also a [T3 space](t3-space.md) is a [metrizable space](metrizable-space.md). First [regularity of a topological space](regular-topological-space.md) and the [Lindelöf](lindelof-space.md) property yield a [normal space](normal-space.md): cover each of two disjoint [closed sets](closed-set.md) by countably many [open sets](open-set.md) whose closures avoid the other, and subtract the first $n$ opposite closures from the $n$th [neighborhood](neighbourhood-mathematics.md). The resulting unions are disjoint [open neighborhoods](open-neighbourhood.md). [Urysohn's lemma](urysohn-s-lemma.md) then gives, for each pair of basic sets $\overline B_n\subseteq B_m$, a [continuous function](continuous-function.md) equal to one on the smaller [closure](closure-topology.md) and zero outside the larger set. There are countably many such pairs. The shrinking form of [regularity of a topological space](regular-topological-space.md) shows these functions generate the topology, and $T_1$ makes them separate [points](point-geometry.md). Their coordinate map embeds the space into the [countable](countable-set.md) cube, with compatible [metric](metric.md) $d(x,y)=\sum_j2^{-j}|f_j(x)-f_j(y)|$. The bounded tails prove compatibility with the [product topology](product-topology.md). Thus a [countable](countable-set.md) base converts [separation axioms](separation-axiom.md) into a numerical distance.
