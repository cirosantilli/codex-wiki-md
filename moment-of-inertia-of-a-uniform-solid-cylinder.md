# Moment of inertia of a uniform solid cylinder

↑ **Parent:** [Moment of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md)

For a uniform solid cylinder of radius $a$, length $L$ and [mass density](density.md) $\rho$, a coaxial shell carries [mass](mass.md) $dm=2\pi\rho Lr\,dr$. The axial [moment of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md) is $I=\int_0^a r^2dm=\pi\rho La^4/2=ma^2/2$. The axial [moment of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md) is independent of the length once [mass](mass.md) and radius are fixed. A thin cylindrical shell instead has $I=ma^2$, demonstrating why the uniform solid assumption matters.
