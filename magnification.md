# Magnification

↑ **Parent:** [Image formation](image-formation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnification)

[Magnification](magnification.md) compares an image's scale with a specified reference. [Transverse magnification](transverse-magnification.md) compares signed lengths in object and image planes; [angular magnification](angular-magnification.md) compares viewing [angles](angle.md) at an observer. Both are [dimensionless quantities](dimensionless-quantity.md), but they answer different questions. A projected [real image](real-image.md) twice as tall has $|m|=2$; an [optical telescope](optical-telescope.md)'s advertised viewing enlargement normally concerns [angular magnification](angular-magnification.md). [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, University Physics Volume 3, §2.8: Angular magnification and telescopes](university-physics-volume-3-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others/2-8-microscopes-and-telescopes.md).

Always specify the sign and the reference arrangement. For signed [transverse magnification](transverse-magnification.md), $m<0$ means inverted and $m>0$ means upright; $|m|>1$ means enlarged and $0<|m|<1$ means reduced. A larger [virtual image](virtual-image.md) can subtend a modest viewing [angle](angle.md) if it is farther away. At $u=f$, an ideal converging [thin lens](thin-lens.md) has no finite image height and hence no finite [transverse magnification](transverse-magnification.md), while [angular magnification](angular-magnification.md) can remain finite. Enlargement also does not guarantee extra detail: [diffraction-limited resolution](diffraction-limited-resolution.md) and [optical aberrations](optical-aberration.md) limit the information available to enlarge. [Feynman, Leighton and Sands, §§27-6–27-7](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_27.html).
