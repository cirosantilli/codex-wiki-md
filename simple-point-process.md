# Simple point process

↑ **Parent:** [Point process](point-process.md)

A [point process](point-process.md) is simple when, on a single [almost sure event](almost-sure-event.md), every location has multiplicity at most one. Its realizations are [simple locally finite point measures](simple-locally-finite-point-measure.md). For instance, $\delta_{0.4}+2\delta_{1.1}+\delta_{1.8}$ is locally finite but not simple: it has four points counted with multiplicity at three distinct locations. Simplicity is a statement about each entire realization. Checking $\mathbb P(\Pi(\{x\})>1)=0$ separately for every fixed $x$ is insufficient: $2\delta_U$ with $U$ having the [uniform distribution](continuous-uniform-distribution.md) on $(0,1)$ passes every such fixed-location check, yet always has a double point.[Section 6.2, Definitions 6.6 and 6.8, printed p. 49: simple measures and simple point processes](lectures-on-the-poisson-process-by-gunter-last-and-others.md)
