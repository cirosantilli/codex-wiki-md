# Power series

↑ **Parent:** [Sequence and series](sequence-and-series-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_series)

A [power series](power-series.md) combines a [sequence](sequence.md) of [coefficients](coefficient.md) with [monomials](monomial.md) in the displacement from a fixed centre:

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n(z-c)^n.
$$

The variable $z$, centre $c$, and [coefficients](coefficient.md) can be [real numbers](real-number.md) or [complex numbers](complex-number.md). Its degree-$N$ [partial sum](partial-sum.md) $P_N(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_n(z-c)^n$ is a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md), so a convergent [power series](power-series.md) can turn a complicated [function](function-split.md) into increasingly accurate finite calculations. Convergence, and the error after stopping, are essential parts of that claim.

The [geometric series](geometric-series.md) supplies the simplest computed example:

$$
\frac1{1-z}=1+z+z^2+\cdots,\qquad |z|<1.
$$

At $z=1/2$, stopping after degree ten gives $P_{10}(1/2)=1.9990234375$, differing from the exact value $2$ by $1/1024=0.0009765625$. At $z=0.99$, the same degree gives only about $10.4662$ instead of $100$. The same formula has very different practical accuracy at different points; the [geometric power-series remainder](geometric-power-series-remainder.md) quantifies it.

The structure is useful beyond numerical evaluation. Within its [radius of convergence](radius-of-convergence.md), [termwise differentiation of a power series](termwise-differentiation-of-a-power-series.md) turns a question about a [derivative](derivative.md) into a question about [coefficients](coefficient.md). A [Taylor series](taylor-series.md) chooses $a_n=f^{(n)}(c)/n!$ when it represents the [function](function-split.md) locally. A [formal power series](formal-power-series.md) instead uses the coefficients algebraically, without asserting numerical convergence; the existing [generating function](generating-function.md) examples use that distinction to encode counting problems.
