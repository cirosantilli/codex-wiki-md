# Keller four-roller polymer experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Coil-stretch transition](coil-stretch-transition.md)

[Pierre-Gilles de Gennes](pierre-gilles-de-gennes.md)'s 1991 lecture describes [Andrew Keller](andrew-keller.md) and collaborators using four rollers to deform a dilute [polymer solution](polymer-solution.md). A well-chosen exit trajectory kept coils under sustained extension. Above a threshold flow rate the solution became [birefringent](birefringence.md), revealing a change in chain orientation and conformation. The experiment connected an optical readout to a mechanically driven molecular response.[Printed p. 9: four-roller experiment and coil-stretch interpretation; text inspected in the Columbia reproduction](soft-matter-by-pierre-gilles-de-gennes.md)

This original functional diagram shows a central flow idealization, rather than historical dimensions or a photograph. A probe of [birefringence](birefringence.md) detects different optical responses along different directions; it does not directly photograph a chain or establish its extension without an optical model. Comparing a flowing solution with its quiescent state and controlling [temperature](temperature-split.md) and [concentration](concentration.md) are useful proposed checks, not an assertion that this is the complete historical protocol.
