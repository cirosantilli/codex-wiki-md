# Extent of reaction

↑ **Parent:** [Stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extent_of_reaction)

For a closed system with one specified [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md), the [extent of reaction](extent-of-reaction.md) $\xi$ records progress from $\xi=0$ through $n_i=n_{i,0}+\nu_i\xi$. Here $n_i$ is the [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) of species $i$, and the signed [stoichiometric vector](stoichiometric-vector.md) has negative entries for [reactants](reactant.md) and positive entries for [reaction products](product-chemistry.md). For the [water](water.md) [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) in the order $\mathrm{H}_2,\mathrm{O}_2,\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$, it is $(-2,-1,2)$. The normalization of $\xi$ depends on the chosen coefficients. See [IUPAC Gold Book, extent of reaction](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/E02283).

With particle numbers instead of molar amounts, a molecular extent $\xi_N$ obeys $dN_i=\nu_i\,d\xi_N$, and the [chemical potentials](chemical-potential-split.md) are measured per particle. At fixed [temperature](temperature-split.md) and [volume](volume.md), the [Helmholtz free energy](helmholtz-free-energy.md) then satisfies $dF=(\sum_i\nu_i\mu_i)\,d\xi_N$. An interior [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md) requires $\sum_i\nu_i\mu_i=0$; the molar convention gives the same relation with chemical potentials per mole.
