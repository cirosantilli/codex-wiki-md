# Trophic level

↑ **Parent:** [Ecosystem](ecosystem.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trophic_level)

A [trophic level](trophic-level.md) describes feeding position relative to [primary producers](primary-producer.md). Assign [primary producers](primary-producer.md) level 1; in the illustrated [food chain](food-chain.md), [zooplankton](zooplankton.md) occupy level 2, small [fish](fish.md) level 3, and large [fish](fish.md) level 4. Following a single feeding path adds one level at each step. The labels make dependence on the initial resource visible, but a branching [food web](food-web.md) can give an [organism](organism-split.md) several feeding paths. See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 46.1: feeding positions and branching food webs](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/46-1-ecology-of-ecosystems).

An [omnivore](omnivore.md) can obtain food from more than one [trophic level](trophic-level.md). As an explicitly assumed diet-weighted example, suppose an [omnivore](omnivore.md) gets half its assimilated food [energy](energy-split.md) from level-1 [primary producers](primary-producer.md) and half from level-2 [consumers](consumer-food-chain.md). Define its [trophic level](trophic-level.md) as one plus the weighted [mean](expected-value-split.md) level of its food: $1+(1/2)1+(1/2)2=2.5$. This computed position depends on the specified diet and weighting convention; it is not an immutable property of the [species](species.md). A different life stage or diet can change it.
