# Trace forms of solvable Lie algebras annihilate the derived algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Trace form of a Lie algebra representation](trace-form-of-a-lie-algebra-representation.md)

For a finite-dimensional complex representation of a [solvable Lie algebra](solvable-lie-algebra.md), the [Lie theorem](lie-s-theorem.md) gives a basis in which all representing matrices are upper triangular. Commutators are strictly upper triangular, and their products with upper triangular matrices have zero [trace](matrix-trace.md). Thus the [derived algebra](derived-algebra.md) lies in the [radical of a bilinear form](radical-of-a-bilinear-form.md). In particular, a nonabelian [solvable Lie algebra](solvable-lie-algebra.md) can carry an [invariant bilinear form on a Lie algebra](invariant-bilinear-form-on-a-lie-algebra.md) that is [nondegenerate](nondegenerate-bilinear-form.md) without that form coming from a finite-dimensional representation trace.
