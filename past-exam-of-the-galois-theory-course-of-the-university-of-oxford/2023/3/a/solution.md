<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A nonconstant [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) is [separable](../../../../separable-topological-space.md) when it has distinct roots in a [splitting field](../../../../splitting-field.md), equivalently when it is coprime to its [formal derivative](../../../../formal-derivative.md). For such $f$, [Bézout's identity for polynomials](../../../../bezout-s-identity-for-polynomials.md) gives $a,b\in F[T]$ with $af+bf'=1$. This identity remains valid over any extension $E/F$. A repeated root $\gamma\in E$ would satisfy $f(\gamma)=f'(\gamma)=0$, giving the contradiction $1=0$. Thus there are no repeated roots in any field extension. The argument in fact applies to every separable polynomial, whether irreducible or not.
