<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the root-discriminant convention in the requested identity, define

$$
\Delta_f=\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)^2.
$$

The [Vandermonde determinant](../../../../vandermonde-determinant.md) is $\det V(\alpha)=\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_j-\alpha_i)$. Squaring removes the signs, proving

$$
\boxed{\Delta_f=(\det V(\alpha))^2}.
$$

It is nonzero because the roots are distinct. It lies in $F$, since its root expression is fixed by every [Galois automorphism](../../../../automorphism-of-a-field-extension.md) and $K^G=F$.

If $f$ is not monic and has leading coefficient $c$, the usual [polynomial discriminant](../../../../polynomial-discriminant.md) is $c^{2n-2}\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)^2$. Thus the printed Vandermonde equality uses the discriminant of the monic normalization $f/c$. The extra factor is itself a nonzero square in $F$, so it does not alter the square-class criterion in the next part.
