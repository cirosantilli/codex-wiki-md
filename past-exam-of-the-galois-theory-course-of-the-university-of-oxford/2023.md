## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

For $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta)$, every [Galois automorphism](../galois-theory.md#automorphism-of-a-field-extension) sends the primitive root $\zeta$ to another primitive $m$th [root of unity](../algebra.md#root-of-unity), uniquely $\zeta^a$ for $a\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times$. The [Cyclotomic character of a finite cyclotomic extension](../galois-theory.md#cyclotomic-character-of-a-finite-cyclotomic-extension) is

$$
\boxed{\chi_m:\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times,\qquad\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^{\chi_m(\sigma)}}.
$$

If $\chi_m(\sigma)=a$ and $\chi_m(\tau)=b$, then $(\sigma\tau)(\zeta)=\sigma(\zeta^b)=\zeta^{ab}$, proving the [group homomorphism](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) property. Its [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is trivial: an automorphism fixing $\zeta$ fixes the field it generates. Thus the character is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function). Here its domain is the finite cyclotomic [Galois group](../galois-theory.md#galois-group); the corresponding map from an [absolute Galois group](../galois-theory.md#absolute-galois-group) need not be injective.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

The element $\zeta$ satisfies $T^2-\eta T+1=0$, so $[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q(\eta)]\leq2$. Since $\eta=\zeta+\overline\zeta$ is real, $\mathbb Q(\eta)\subseteq\mathbb R$. A primitive root of order $m\geq3$ is nonreal: the only real [roots of unity](../algebra.md#root-of-unity) are $\pm1$, of orders at most two. Hence $\zeta\notin\mathbb Q(\eta)$ and

$$
\boxed{[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q(\eta)]=2}.
$$

Equivalently, nontrivial [complex conjugation](../complex-analysis.md#complex-conjugation) fixes precisely the [maximal real subfield of a cyclotomic field](../galois-theory.md#maximal-real-subfield-of-a-cyclotomic-field) in this degree-two extension.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/c/i)

Irreducibility of the [cyclotomic polynomial](../galois-theory.md#cyclotomic-polynomial) gives $[K:\mathbb Q]=\varphi(15)=8$. The extension is [Galois](../galois-theory.md#finite-galois-extension), since $K$ is the [splitting field](../galois-theory.md#splitting-field) of $T^{15}-1$ in characteristic zero. The injection in part (a) is consequently an isomorphism, and the [Chinese remainder theorem](../mathematics.md#chinese-remainder-theorem) gives

$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/15\mathbb Z)^\times\cong C_2\times C_4.
$$

The [Fundamental theorem of Galois theory](../galois-theory.md#fundamental-theorem-of-galois-theory) identifies proper intermediate fields strictly above $\mathbb Q$ with proper nontrivial [subgroups](../group.md#subgroup). Such subgroups have order two or four. The two-torsion subgroup is $C_2\times C_2$, with three nonidentity elements and hence three order-two subgroups. There are also three order-four subgroups: they are the kernels of the three nonzero [group homomorphisms](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $C_2\times C_4\to C_2$. Distinct such maps have distinct kernels because the target has only one nonidentity element. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{three quadratic subfields and three quartic subfields, six in total}.}
$$

<h4 id="1/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/c/ii)

Put $\omega=\zeta^5$, $\tau=\zeta^3$, $a=1+2\omega$, and $b=1+2(\tau+\tau^{-1})$. The primitive cubic and quintic root identities give $a^2=-3$, $b^2=5$. These elements therefore provide the following six generators:
- $\mathbb Q(a)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$, degree two.
- $\mathbb Q(b)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, degree two.
- $\mathbb Q(ab)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-15})$, degree two.
- $\mathbb Q(\tau)$, degree four.
- $\mathbb Q(a+b)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt5)$, degree four.
- $\mathbb Q(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})$, degree four.

The three quadratic fields are distinct because $-3,5,-15$ are distinct rational square classes. To justify the fifth entry, the two quadratic fields $\mathbb Q(a)$ and $\mathbb Q(b)$ are distinct, so their [compositum](../algebra.md#field-compositum) has degree four. Moreover $a+b\ne0$ and

$$
(a+b)(b-a)=8,\qquad a=\frac{a+b-8/(a+b)}2,\qquad b=\frac{a+b+8/(a+b)}2.
$$

Thus their sum alone generates the compositum.

The field $\mathbb Q(\tau)$ has degree four by the quintic [cyclotomic polynomial](../galois-theory.md#cyclotomic-polynomial). Its [Galois group](../galois-theory.md#galois-group) is $C_4$ and its unique quadratic subfield is $\mathbb Q(b)$, so it cannot contain $a$. It is therefore different from $\mathbb Q(a,b)$. The final field has degree four by part (b) and is real; the preceding two quartic fields contain nonreal $\tau$ and $a$, respectively. Thus all three quartics are distinct, and the count in part (i) proves the list exhaustive. This gives the [subfields of the fifteenth cyclotomic field](../galois-theory.md#subfields-of-the-fifteenth-cyclotomic-field) without relying on a choice of square-root signs.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

The [Galois group](../galois-theory.md#galois-group) acts [transitively](../group-action.md#transitive-group-action) on the $n$ roots because $f$ is [irreducible](../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) and [separable](../topology.md#separable-topological-space). The [orbit-stabilizer theorem](../group-action.md#orbit-stabilizer-theorem) gives

$$
\boxed{|G|=n\,|\operatorname{Stab}_G(\alpha_1)|},
$$

so $n$ divides $|G|$. Equivalently, the [tower law](../algebra.md#tower-law) gives $[K:F]=[K:F(\alpha_1)]n$, while the finite [Galois extension](../galois-theory.md#finite-galois-extension) has group order $[K:F]$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

For the root-discriminant convention in the requested identity, define

$$
\Delta_f=\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)^2.
$$

The [Vandermonde determinant](../galois-theory.md#vandermonde-determinant) is $\det V(\alpha)=\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_j-\alpha_i)$. Squaring removes the signs, proving

$$
\boxed{\Delta_f=(\det V(\alpha))^2}.
$$

It is nonzero because the roots are distinct. It lies in $F$, since its root expression is fixed by every [Galois automorphism](../galois-theory.md#automorphism-of-a-field-extension) and $K^G=F$.

If $f$ is not monic and has leading coefficient $c$, the usual [polynomial discriminant](../galois-theory.md#polynomial-discriminant) is $c^{2n-2}\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)^2$. Thus the printed Vandermonde equality uses the discriminant of the monic normalization $f/c$. The extra factor is itself a nonzero square in $F$, so it does not alter the square-class criterion in the next part.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

First assume $\operatorname{char}F\ne2$. Set $d=\det V(\alpha)\ne0$. If $\Delta_f=b^2$ with $b\in F$, then $(d-b)(d+b)=0$ in the [field](../algebra.md#field) $K$, so $d=\pm b\in F$. For an automorphism inducing the root permutation $\pi$,

$$
\sigma(d)=\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)d.
$$

Since $\sigma$ fixes $d$ and $1\ne-1$ in $F$, every induced permutation has positive sign. The root action is faithful because the roots generate $K$, giving

$$
\boxed{G\hookrightarrow A_n\qquad(\operatorname{char}F\ne2)}.
$$

This is the [discriminant criterion for an alternating Galois group](../galois-theory.md#discriminant-criterion-for-an-alternating-galois-group).

**As printed for arbitrary fields, the assertion is false in characteristic two.** Over $\mathbb F_2$, the polynomial $T^2+T+1$ is irreducible and separable, with roots $\alpha,\alpha+1$ in $\mathbb F_4$. Their difference is one, so the discriminant is the square $1$. Nevertheless its [Galois group](../galois-theory.md#galois-group) is $C_2$, whereas $A_2$ is trivial. In characteristic two the permutation signs become indistinguishable as field elements; [square discriminants in characteristic two](../galois-theory.md#square-discriminants-in-characteristic-two) therefore cannot detect the alternating subgroup in this way.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

Denote the three stated pair sums by $r_1,r_2,r_3$. They are pairwise distinct: for example,

$$
r_1-r_2=(\alpha_1-\alpha_4)(\alpha_2-\alpha_3)\ne0,
$$

and the other differences factor similarly. Every [Galois automorphism](../galois-theory.md#automorphism-of-a-field-extension) fixes each $r_i\in F$, and hence fixes each of the three corresponding partitions of four roots into two pairs. The kernel of the permutation action $S_4\to S_3$ on these partitions is

$$
V_4=\{1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}.
$$

Thus $G$ embeds in the [Klein four-group](../finite-group-theory.md#klein-four-group). By part (a), $4$ divides $|G|$, and the containing group has order four. Consequently

$$
\boxed{G\cong V_4}.
$$

This [Split resolvent criterion for a Klein four Galois group](../galois-theory.md#split-resolvent-criterion-for-a-klein-four-galois-group) uses the [cubic resolvent of a quartic](../galois-theory.md#cubic-resolvent-of-a-quartic). The pair-sum differences remain nonzero in characteristic two, so this proof does not need the missing characteristic assumption from part (c).

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

A nonconstant [polynomial](../polynomial.md) is [separable](../topology.md#separable-topological-space) when it has distinct roots in a [splitting field](../galois-theory.md#splitting-field), equivalently when it is coprime to its [formal derivative](../galois-theory.md#formal-derivative). For such $f$, [Bézout's identity for polynomials](../polynomial.md#bezout-s-identity-for-polynomials) gives $a,b\in F[T]$ with $af+bf'=1$. This identity remains valid over any extension $E/F$. A repeated root $\gamma\in E$ would satisfy $f(\gamma)=f'(\gamma)=0$, giving the contradiction $1=0$. Thus there are no repeated roots in any field extension. The argument in fact applies to every separable polynomial, whether irreducible or not.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

For nonconstant irreducible $f$, its [greatest common divisor](../divisibility.md#greatest-common-divisor) with $f'$ is either $1$ or $f$. Since $\deg f'<\deg f$ when $f'\ne0$, inseparability is equivalent to $f'=0$. In characteristic $p$,

$$
f'(T)=\sum_jj a_jT^{j-1}=0
$$

exactly when $a_j=0$ for every exponent $j$ not divisible by $p$. Thus

$$
\boxed{f\text{ separable}\iff f'\ne0\iff f\notin F[T^p]}.
$$

No assumption that the coefficient field is perfect is required: vanishing derivative concerns the exponents, not whether the coefficients have $p$th roots in $F$.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Let $E=F(\alpha^p)\subseteq F(\alpha)$. If $\alpha$ is a [separable algebraic element](../galois-theory.md#separable-algebraic-element) over $F$, its minimal polynomial over $E$ divides the original separable [minimal polynomial](../linear-operator-theory.md#minimal-polynomial). It also divides $T^p-\alpha^p=(T-\alpha)^p$, which has only one distinct root in any extension. Being separable with only one distinct root, that minimal polynomial has degree one. Hence $\alpha\in E$ and $F(\alpha)=F(\alpha^p)$.

Conversely, if the degree-$n$ minimal polynomial $m$ over $F$ is inseparable, part (b) writes it as $m(T)=g(T^p)$ with $\deg g=n/p$. Then $g(\alpha^p)=0$, so

$$
[F(\alpha^p):F]\leq n/p<n=[F(\alpha):F].
$$

The fields cannot be equal. This proves the equivalence

$$
\boxed{\alpha\text{ separable over }F\iff F(\alpha)=F(\alpha^p)},
$$

the [separability criterion using a pth power](../galois-theory.md#separability-criterion-using-a-pth-power).

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

Put $\gamma=\alpha+\beta$, $E=F(\gamma)$ and $E_0=F(\gamma^p)$. By the preceding [separability criterion using a pth power](../galois-theory.md#separability-criterion-using-a-pth-power), it suffices to prove $E=E_0$.

Since $\alpha$ is separable, $\alpha\in F(\alpha^p)$. But $\alpha^p=\gamma^p-\beta^p\in E_0(\beta)$, so $\alpha\in E_0(\beta)$. Consequently

$$
M:=F(\alpha,\beta)=E(\beta)=E_0(\beta).
$$

Let $h$ and $m$ be the monic [minimal polynomials](../linear-operator-theory.md#minimal-polynomial) of $\beta$ over $E_0$ and $E$, respectively. Both divide its separable minimal polynomial over $F$, so both are separable. Also $m\mid h$ in $E[T]$.

Every $c\in E=F(\gamma)$ has $c^p\in F(\gamma^p)=E_0$. Hence $m(T)^p$ has coefficients in $E_0$ and vanishes at $\beta$, forcing $h\mid m^p$ in $E_0[T]$. Since $h$ remains a [square-free polynomial](../polynomial.md#square-free-polynomial) over $E$, this divisibility implies $h\mid m$ in $E[T]$. Together with $m\mid h$, monicity gives $h=m$. Therefore

$$
[M:E_0]=\deg h=\deg m=[M:E].
$$

The [tower law](../algebra.md#tower-law) makes $[E:E_0]=1$. Part (c) now proves that $\gamma=\alpha+\beta$ is separable over $F$. This establishes [closure of separable algebraic elements under addition](../galois-theory.md#closure-of-separable-algebraic-elements-under-addition) without presupposing that closure as a field theorem.
