# Coriolis force

↑ **Parent:** [Rotating reference frame](rotating-reference-frame.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coriolis_force)

The Coriolis force is the apparent [force](force-split.md) proportional to a particle's [velocity](velocity.md) relative to a [rotating reference frame](rotating-reference-frame.md). For constant [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) $\boldsymbol\Omega$ and particle [mass](mass.md) $m$,

$$
\mathbf F_{\rm cor}=-2m\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v_{\rm rot},
\qquad
\mathbf v_{\rm rot}=\left(\frac{d\mathbf r}{dt}\right)_{\rm rot}.
$$

Here $\mathbf v$ in the title formula means $\mathbf v_{\rm rot}$, not the [velocity](velocity.md) measured in an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md). The [Coriolis force](coriolis-force.md) belongs to the chosen [reference frame](reference-frame.md): a particle stationary in that [rotating reference frame](rotating-reference-frame.md) has no [Coriolis force](coriolis-force.md), even though it can have a nonzero [velocity](velocity.md) in the laboratory. Other [reference frames](reference-frame.md) assign different [angular velocities](angular-velocity.md) and relative [velocities](velocity.md), and hence different [Coriolis forces](coriolis-force.md) to the same motion.

The [cross product](cross-product.md) makes the [Coriolis force](coriolis-force.md) perpendicular to both $\boldsymbol\Omega$ and $\mathbf v_{\rm rot}$, with magnitude $2m\Omega|\mathbf v_{\rm rot}|\sin\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the [angle](angle.md) between them. Apply the [right-hand rule](right-hand-rule.md) to $\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v_{\rm rot}$ and then reverse its direction. Motion parallel to the rotation axis gives zero [Coriolis force](coriolis-force.md); reversing the relative [velocity](velocity.md) reverses the [Coriolis force](coriolis-force.md).

The appeal of this description is practical: axes attached to the [Earth](earth.md) or a turning apparatus can be kept fixed on the page, provided [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) includes the extra terms. A [free particle in rotating coordinates](free-particle-in-rotating-coordinates.md) shows exactly how a straight laboratory [trajectory](trajectory.md) can become curved, while [Coriolis acceleration in local Earth coordinates](coriolis-acceleration-in-local-earth-coordinates.md) gives a useful terrestrial scale. The [rotating-table demonstration of Coriolis deflection](rotating-table-demonstration-of-coriolis-deflection.md) makes the two viewpoints visible in one experiment.
