# Lipschitz constant

↑ **Parent:** [Lipschitz continuity](lipschitz-continuity.md)

The [Lipschitz constant](lipschitz-constant.md) is the least nonnegative number that works in the defining [Lipschitz bound](lipschitz-bound.md), equivalently the displayed [supremum](supremum.md) of distance ratios. A constant function has constant zero; for a domain with at most one point we also set it to zero. An infinite supremum means there is no finite [Lipschitz bound](lipschitz-bound.md). The least constant need not be attained by one pair of inputs.

For $f(x)=3x-2$ on the [real line](real-line.md), every distinct pair has ratio $3$, so the [Lipschitz constant](lipschitz-constant.md) is $3$. For $f(x)=x^2$ on $[-R,R]$, with $R>0$,

$$
\frac{|x^2-y^2|}{|x-y|}=|x+y|\leq2R.
$$

Pairs $x=R$, $y=R-\varepsilon$ have ratio $2R-\varepsilon$, approaching $2R$. Thus the [Lipschitz constant](lipschitz-constant.md) is $2R$, although no distinct pair realizes that exact value. Reporting the domain is essential: the same [function](function-split.md) has no finite global [Lipschitz constant](lipschitz-constant.md) on $\mathbb R$.
