# Powers of an ideal

↑ **Parent:** [Product of ideals](product-of-ideals.md)

For an [ideal](ideal.md) $I$ in a [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md) $R$, put $I^0=R$ and let $I^n$ for $n\geq1$ be the [product of ideals](product-of-ideals.md) with $n$ factors equal to $I$. It consists of finite sums of products of $n$ elements of $I$. These [ideals](ideal.md) form a descending chain $R\supseteq I\supseteq I^2\supseteq\cdots$.

Every positive power has the same [radical of an ideal](radical-of-an-ideal.md) as $I$:

$$
\sqrt{I^n}=\sqrt I\qquad(n\geq1).
$$

One inclusion follows from $I^n\subseteq I$. Conversely, if $x^m\in I$, then $x^{mn}\in I^n$, giving the other inclusion. Different [ideals](ideal.md) can therefore define the same [radical ideal](radical-ideal.md), even when their successive powers are all distinct.
