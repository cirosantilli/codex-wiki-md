<h1 id="coblentz-s-cavity-radiation-spectrum-measurement">Coblentz's cavity-radiation spectrum measurement</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Wien's displacement law](wien-s-displacement-law.md)

[William Weber Coblentz](william-weber-coblentz.md)'s primary report describes an electrically heated porcelain cavity wound with platinum ribbon. Mirrors imaged its central radiating diaphragm onto a $0.6\,\mathrm{mm}$ [optical slit](optical-slit.md); a fluorite [optical prism](prism-optics.md) dispersed the beam onto a vacuum [bolometer](bolometer.md). [Galvanometer](galvanometer.md) deflections read the detector response. A resistance standard checked electrical sensitivity, while repeated readings checked stability. [Sodium](sodium.md) lines fixed the angular zero; a [refractive index](refractive-index.md) curve supplied [wavelengths](wavelength.md) and slit-bandwidth corrections. [Thermocouples](thermocouple.md) were standardized at freezing points of zinc, antimony and copper. Drying the optical path reduced atmospheric absorption; prism scattering and [temperature](temperature-split.md) calibration remained limitations. These were Coblentz's measurements. His extraction of radiation constants assumed the [Planck law](planck-s-law.md), so it was not a model-independent proof of the spectral formula.[W. W. Coblentz, Constants of spectral radiation of a uniformly heated inclosure or so-called black body, I: printed pp.15–27, 35, 42–47 and 73–77; apparatus, sensitivity checks, temperature scale, wavelength calibration and model-dependent constants](constants-of-spectral-radiation-of-a-uniformly-heated-inclosure-or-so-called-black-body-i-by-william-weber-coblentz.md)

The inference to test is whether corrected spectra taken at independently determined [temperatures](temperature-split.md) have their $B_\lambda$ maxima at a constant product $\lambda_{\max}T$. Comparing raw [galvanometer](galvanometer.md) deflections at different [optical prism](prism-optics.md) settings would also compare different [wavelength](wavelength.md) widths and optical losses. The response integral in [blackbody peak and detector response](blackbody-peak-and-detector-response.md) explains why those corrections belong to the physical inference rather than merely to presentation.
