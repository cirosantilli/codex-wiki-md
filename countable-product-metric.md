# Countable product metric

↑ **Parent:** [Product metric](product-metric.md)

The displayed [metric](metric.md) on a countable [Cartesian product](cartesian-product.md) of [metric spaces](metric-space.md) combines the [bounded metric transform](bounded-metric-transform.md) with summable positive weights. It is finite even when individual factor metrics are unbounded. The triangle inequality follows from subadditivity of $t/(1+t)$ and summation. Zero distance forces equality in each coordinate.

Convergence in this metric is exactly coordinatewise convergence. One term is bounded by the sum, so $D(x^{(n)},x)\to0$ implies each $d_i(x_i^{(n)},x_i)\to0$. Conversely, choose a finite head whose omitted weight sum is small; convergence in those finitely many coordinates controls the head uniformly, while boundedness of the transformed distances controls the entire tail. Thus it induces the countable [product topology](product-topology.md). Arbitrary positive summable weights work by the same proof.
