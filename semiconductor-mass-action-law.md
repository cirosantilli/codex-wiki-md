# Semiconductor mass-action law

↑ **Parent:** [Intrinsic carrier concentration](intrinsic-carrier-concentration.md)

In thermal equilibrium, nondegenerate conduction [electrons](electron.md) and valence [electron holes](electron-hole.md) share one [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md). Multiplying $n=N_c e^{-(E_c-\mu)/(k_BT)}$ by $p=N_v e^{-(\mu-E_v)/(k_BT)}$ cancels that [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md):

$$
np=N_cN_v e^{-E_g/(k_BT)}=n_i^2.
$$

This relation remains valid with [semiconductor doping](semiconductor-doping.md) under the stated assumptions. Equality of the two individual densities, $n=p=n_i$, additionally requires the intrinsic neutral case. Separate electron and hole quasi-Fermi levels in nonequilibrium need not obey this equilibrium product.

With one parabolic valley and two spin states at each band edge, the prefactor multiplying $(k_BT)^3$ in the product is $4(m_cm_h)^{3/2}/(2\pi\hbar^2)^3$. Both populations must be nondegenerate; the [Pauli reduction of the pinned-edge mass-action product](pauli-reduction-of-the-pinned-edge-mass-action-product.md) quantifies a failure of the classical approximation.
