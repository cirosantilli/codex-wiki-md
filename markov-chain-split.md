# Markov chain

↑ **Parent:** [Markov process](markov-process-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_chain)

The term Markov chain commonly denotes a [Markov process](markov-process-split.md) on a finite or [countable](countable-set.md) [state space](state-space.md). Time may be discrete, as for a daily record, or continuous, as for a [continuous-time Markov chain](continuous-time-markov-chain-split.md). Some authors also use “[Markov chain](markov-chain-split.md)” for discrete-time processes on more general [state spaces](state-space.md); specifying both time and state space avoids this ambiguity.

For a [Discrete-time Markov chain](discrete-time-markov-chain.md), its initial [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) $\mu_0$ and [transition matrix](stochastic-matrix.md) $P$ specify the [probability](probability.md) of each finite path:

$$
\mathbb P(X_0=i_0,\ldots,X_n=i_n)=\mu_0(i_0)\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}P_{i_k i_{k+1}}.
$$

This is the [Markov factorization](markov-factorization.md), obtained by applying [conditional probability](conditional-probability.md) repeatedly. See [Hao Wu's MIT lecture 1, definition and distribution evolution](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-445-introduction-to-stochastic-processes-spring-2015/365d85ac35a7c006bc33dd03da633997_MIT18_445S15_lecture1.pdf).

For the constructed sunny/rainy [Markov chain](markov-chain-split.md) below, starting sunny makes the path sunny, rainy, sunny have [probability](probability.md) $1\times0.2\times0.4=0.08$. Starting instead with [stationary distribution](stationary-distribution.md) $(2/3,1/3)$ makes that path's [probability](probability.md) $(2/3)\times0.2\times0.4=4/75$. The [transition probabilities](transition-probability.md) are identical in both experiments; the initial [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) distinguishes them.
