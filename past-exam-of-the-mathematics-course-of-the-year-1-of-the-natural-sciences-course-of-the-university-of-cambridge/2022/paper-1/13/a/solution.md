<h1 id="13/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Because the original [differential equation](../../../../../differential-equation-split.md) divides by $xy$, its domain requires $x\ne0$ and $y\ne0$. Set $u=y^2$, so $u'=2yy'$. The equation becomes the [first-order linear differential equation](../../../../../first-order-linear-differential-equation.md)

$$
u'+\frac2x u=-4x-2.
$$

An [integrating factor](../../../../../integrating-factor.md) is $x^2$, since $(x^2u)'=x^2u'+2xu$. Thus

$$
(x^2u)'=-4x^3-2x^2,\qquad x^2u=-x^4-\frac23x^3+C.
$$

Returning to $y$ gives the explicit general real solutions

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\pm\sqrt{\frac C{x^2}-x^2-\frac23x},\qquad C\in\mathbb R.}
$$

Each sign is fixed on a connected [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) where $x\ne0$ and the radicand is strictly positive. The strict condition excludes $y=0$, where the original [differential equation](../../../../../differential-equation-split.md) is undefined. There is no additional zero solution.
