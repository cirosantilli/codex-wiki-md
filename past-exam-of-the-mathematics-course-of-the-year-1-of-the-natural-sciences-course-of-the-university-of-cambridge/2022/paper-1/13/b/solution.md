<h1 id="13/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Where $\sin x\ne0$, divide by it to obtain $y'+2\cot x\,y=\csc x$. The [integrating factor](../../../../../integrating-factor.md) is $\sin^2x$, and the [product rule](../../../../../product-rule.md) gives

$$
(y\sin^2x)'=\sin x,\qquad y\sin^2x=-\cos x+C.
$$

The [initial condition](../../../../../initial-condition.md) gives $C=1$. Hence

$$
y=\frac{1-\cos x}{\sin^2x}=\boxed{\frac1{1+\cos x}}.
$$

The simplified expression has a differentiable extension through $x=0$, with $y(0)=1/2$. Direct substitution verifies the original undivided [differential equation](../../../../../differential-equation-split.md) there as well: its left side is $2y(0)=1$. Thus the maximal [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) containing $\pi/2$ on which this solution is finite is $\boxed{-\pi<x<\pi}$; the apparent exclusion of $0$ arose only from division by $\sin x$. This is [continuation through a zero of the leading ODE coefficient](../../../../../continuation-through-a-zero-of-the-leading-ode-coefficient.md).
