↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $t_j=t^{(j)}(0)$ and $s=1+t_0>0$. The [chain rule](../../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives

$$
f(0)=\ln s,\qquad f'(0)=\frac{t_1}{s}.
$$

The condition $t_3t_1<0$ ensures $t_1\ne0$. Since $t_0\ne0$, also $s\ne1$ and $\ln s\ne0$. Hence the first two nonzero terms are the constant and linear terms:

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\ln(1+t_0)+\frac{t_1}{1+t_0}x+O(x^2).}
$$

The inequality $s>0$, together with [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) of $t$, makes the [logarithm](../../../../../../logarithm.md) defined on a neighbourhood of zero. The remainder is written in [Big O notation](../../../../../../big-o-notation.md).
