<h1 id="14/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

First express the [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) in the original variables:

$$
f(x,y)=\ln(1+x^2+y^2)+\ln(1+x^2y^2).
$$

The [chain rule](../../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives

$$
f_x=\frac{2x}{1+x^2+y^2}+\frac{2xy^2}{1+x^2y^2}.
$$

Taking its [partial derivative](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md) with respect to $y$ yields

$$
f_{xy}=-\frac{4xy}{(1+x^2+y^2)^2}+\frac{4xy(1+x^2y^2)-4x^3y^3}{(1+x^2y^2)^2}.
$$

The numerator in the second fraction reduces to $4xy$, reproducing

$$
\boxed{f_{xy}=4xy\left[\frac1{(1+x^2y^2)^2}-\frac1{(1+x^2+y^2)^2}\right].}
$$

Both [logarithms](../../../../../../logarithm.md) are defined for every real $x,y$ because their arguments are at least one.
