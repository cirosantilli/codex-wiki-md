<h1 id="17/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $I=\int e^x\sin x\,dx$ and $J=\int e^x\cos x\,dx$. Two applications of [integration by parts](../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md), first taking the [sine](../../../../../../sine.md) and then the [cosine](../../../../../../cosine.md) as the differentiated factor, give

$$
\boxed{I=e^x\sin x-J+C_1,\qquad J=e^x\cos x+I+C_2.}
$$

Thus $I+J=e^x\sin x$ and $J-I=e^x\cos x$, in each case up to an arbitrary constant of [integration](../../../../../../integral-split.md). Keeping that freedom avoids identifying independently chosen [antiderivatives](../../../../../../antiderivative.md) too rigidly.
