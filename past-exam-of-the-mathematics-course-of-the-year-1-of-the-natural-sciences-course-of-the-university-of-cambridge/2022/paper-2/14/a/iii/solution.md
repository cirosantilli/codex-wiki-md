<h1 id="14/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $N_A$ be the independent avocado count. There are three disjoint ways to have fewer than two total trees: no trees, only one avocado, or only one lemon. Their [probabilities](../../../../../../probability.md) give

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(N_A+N_L\geq2)=1
&-(1-x)^5(1-y)^5\\
&-5x(1-x)^4(1-y)^5\\
&-5y(1-y)^4(1-x)^5.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The sum is generally a [Poisson binomial distribution](../../../../../../poisson-binomial-distribution.md), rather than a single [binomial distribution](../../../../../../binomial-distribution.md), because the two success [probabilities](../../../../../../probability.md) may differ.
