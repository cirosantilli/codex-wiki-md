<h1 id="15/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The initial data force $C=0$, so the implicit relation factors as $\sin y(x-\sin y)=0$. Near $(0,0)$ this permits either the constant branch or the sine branch:

$$
\boxed{y(x)=0\quad\text{or}\quad y(x)=\arcsin x\quad\text{locally at }x=0}.
$$

Both satisfy the original equation, including its degenerate value $0=0$ at the initial point. They have different slopes there, zero and one, so the [initial value problem](../../../../../../initial-value-problem.md) is not unique. There are no further differentiable local branches through that point: away from $x=0$ the two factors cannot switch, and joining the two different branches at zero would give unequal one-sided [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md).

The nonconstant branch is defined as a solution for $-1<x<1$; the constant branch exists for every real $x$.
