<h1 id="13/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [curl](../../../../../curl.md) of $\mathbf G=\mathbf a\times\mathbf r$ is $2\mathbf a$. Thus for $\mathbf a\ne0$ it cannot be a [gradient field](../../../../../gradient-field.md), while for $\mathbf a=0$ it is zero and all its [vector-field potentials](../../../../../potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md) are constants. This zero-vector case is allowed by the question.

For the radial [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md) $\mathbf H$, write $\rho=|\mathbf r|$. Choose a radial primitive

$$
\Phi(\rho)=\int_{\rho_0}^{\rho}s f(s)\,ds+C.
$$

For $\rho>0$, the [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives

$$
\nabla\Phi=\Phi'(\rho)\frac{\mathbf r}{\rho}=f(\rho)\mathbf r.
$$

Hence all [vector-field potentials](../../../../../potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md) on a connected domain where the field is defined are

$$
\boxed{\Phi(\mathbf r)=\int_{\rho_0}^{|\mathbf r|}s f(s)\,ds+C}.
$$

The assumed existence of the needed derivatives and integrals justifies this construction. If the domain includes the origin and the field is regular there, choose $\rho_0=0$ and extend the primitive there. Any two [vector-field potentials](../../../../../potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md) differ by a constant on each connected component.
