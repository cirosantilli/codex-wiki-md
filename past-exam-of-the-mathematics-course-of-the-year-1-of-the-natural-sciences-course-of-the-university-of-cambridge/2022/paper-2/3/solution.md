↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The equation is **not separable**, because $x^2-xy=x(x-y)$ cannot be written as a product of a function of $x$ and a function of $y$ on an open region. For example, the ratio $(x^2-xy_1)/(x^2-xy_2)=(x-y_1)/(x-y_2)$ depends on $x$ for distinct fixed $y_1,y_2$, whereas a separated product would give a constant ratio.

It is **not homogeneous** in the sense of a [homogeneous first-order differential equation](../../../../homogeneous-first-order-differential-equation.md): the right-hand side is not a function of $y/x$ alone. It is also not a [homogeneous linear differential equation](../../../../homogeneous-linear-differential-equation.md), since moving the $xy$ term to the left leaves a nonzero forcing.

It is **linear**, since it has the standard [linear ordinary differential equation](../../../../linear-ordinary-differential-equation-split.md) form

$$
y'+xy=x^2.
$$

Thus the classifications are **(a) no, (b) no, (c) yes**.
