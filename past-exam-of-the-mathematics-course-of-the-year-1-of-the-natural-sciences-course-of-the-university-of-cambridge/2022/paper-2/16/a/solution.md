<h1 id="16/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use cylindrical radius $\rho=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$. The upper [cone](../../../../../cone-geometry.md) is $z=1-\rho$ and the lower [elliptic paraboloid](../../../../../elliptic-paraboloid.md) is $z=\rho^2-1$, both with $0\leq\rho\leq1$. They meet along the unit [circle](../../../../../circle.md) in $z=0$. The enclosed solid is

$$
0\leq\rho\leq1,\qquad \rho^2-1\leq z\leq1-\rho.
$$

Thus the coordinate ranges on the complete surface are

$$
\boxed{-1\leq x\leq1,\qquad-1\leq y\leq1,\qquad-1\leq z\leq1}.
$$

These separate ranges do not mean every point of their Cartesian product lies on the surface. A vertical cross-section has a straight upper cone profile and a curved lower paraboloid profile.

The upper [cone](../../../../../cone-geometry.md) has apex $(0,0,1)$; the lower [elliptic paraboloid](../../../../../elliptic-paraboloid.md) has vertex $(0,0,-1)$. Both meet at cylindrical radius one and height zero. The meridian cross-section shows the enclosed vertical range at each radius.
