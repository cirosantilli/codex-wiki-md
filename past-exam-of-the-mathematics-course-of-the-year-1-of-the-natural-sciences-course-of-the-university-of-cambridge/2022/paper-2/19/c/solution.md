<h1 id="19/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Introduce the second constraint $q(x,y,z)=y-h(x)=0$ and a second [Lagrange multiplier](../../../../../lagrange-multiplier.md) $\mu$. At points where the two constraint [gradients](../../../../../gradient.md) are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md), solve

$$
\boxed{(y,x+z,y)^T
=\lambda(2x,2y,2z)^T+\mu(-h'(x),1,0)^T},
$$



$$
\boxed{x^2+y^2+z^2=1,\qquad y=h(x)}.
$$

These are five scalar equations for $x,y,z,\lambda,\mu$. The two constraint [normal vectors](../../../../../normal-vector.md) span the normal [plane](../../../../../plane.md) to the feasible curve. Points where those [gradients](../../../../../gradient.md) become dependent must be examined separately; the regular two-multiplier condition need not cover them.
