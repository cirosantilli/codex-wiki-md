<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

The [chain rule](../../../../chain-rule.md) gives $u_{xx}=f''(x+\lambda y)$ and $u_{yy}=\lambda^2f''(x+\lambda y)$. The [partial differential equation](../../../../partial-differential-equation-split.md) becomes

$$
(4-\lambda^2)f''(x+\lambda y)=0.
$$

For this to hold for every twice [differentiable](../../../../differentiable-function.md) $f$, the coefficient must vanish:

$$
\boxed{\lambda=2\quad\text{or}\quad\lambda=-2}.
$$

For an affine $f$ alone any $\lambda$ would work, but the requirement is for arbitrary $f$.
