<h1 id="11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Three [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) form a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) of three-dimensional space exactly when their [scalar triple product](../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md) is nonzero:

$$
\boxed{\Delta=\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf v\times\mathbf w)\ne0}.
$$

It is the [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) of the [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) with these columns, so this condition is equivalent to [linear independence](../../../../../linear-independence.md) and spanning the whole space.

Take the [dot product](../../../../../dot-product.md) of the expansion of $\mathbf y$ with $\mathbf v\times\mathbf w$. The terms containing $\mathbf v$ and $\mathbf w$ vanish, leaving $\mathbf y\cdot(\mathbf v\times\mathbf w)=\alpha\Delta$. Cycling the same argument yields

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{\mathbf y\cdot(\mathbf v\times\mathbf w)}{\Delta},\qquad
\beta=\frac{\mathbf y\cdot(\mathbf w\times\mathbf u)}{\Delta},\qquad
\gamma=\frac{\mathbf y\cdot(\mathbf u\times\mathbf v)}{\Delta}}.
$$

The three cross-product numerators use the [reciprocal basis](../../../../../reciprocal-basis.md).
