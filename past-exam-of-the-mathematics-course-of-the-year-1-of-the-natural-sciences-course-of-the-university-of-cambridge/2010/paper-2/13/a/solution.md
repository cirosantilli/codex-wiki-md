<h1 id="13/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**As printed, the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) is undefined at the origin.** For $r^2=x^2+y^2>0$, its [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) is

$$
\nabla f=\frac{2e^{-1/r^2}}{r^4}(x,y).
$$

The [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md) prefactor is positive, and $(x,y)\ne(0,0)$, so this [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) never vanishes. Thus there are **no [stationary points](../../../../../stationary-point.md) on the stated domain**. Its [infimum](../../../../../infimum.md) is three, approached as $r\to0$, but that value is not attained there.

If the intended definition includes the natural extension $f(0,0)=3$, that extension has a **unique strict [global minimum](../../../../../global-minimum.md) at the origin**, with value three. Indeed, $e^{-1/r^2}>0$ off zero, and $e^{-1/r^2}/r\to0$ proves differentiability at zero with zero [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md). More strongly, subtracting three gives the [radial Gaussian flat function](../../../../../radial-gaussian-flat-function.md): every [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) of that difference vanishes at the origin. The vanishing [Hessian matrix](../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) does not turn this minimum into an [inflection point](../../../../../inflection-point.md). These are two different domain interpretations; the extension must be stated rather than silently inserted.
