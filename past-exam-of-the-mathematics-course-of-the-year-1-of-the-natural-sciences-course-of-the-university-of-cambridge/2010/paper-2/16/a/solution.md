<h1 id="16/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Parametrize the segment by $\mathbf r(t)=(1-t,t,0)$, $0\leq t\leq1$, so $\mathbf r'=(-1,1,0)$. For the first field, $\mathbf F_1=(-t,1-t,-1+t)$ and its [dot product](../../../../../dot-product.md) with the tangent is one. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\int_{\rm segment}\mathbf F_1\cdot d\mathbf r=1}.
$$

For the second field, use the [fundamental theorem for line integrals](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-for-line-integrals.md). The endpoints both have $r=1$, so their potential values agree and

$$
\boxed{\int_{\rm segment}\mathbf F_2\cdot d\mathbf r=0}.
$$

Directly, its parameter integrand is $(2t-1)/[1+(1-t)^2+t^2]^{3/2}$, which changes sign under $t\mapsto1-t$ and integrates to zero.
