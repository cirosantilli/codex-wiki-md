<h1 id="14/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The inverse appearing in the assumption requires $\det C\ne0$. Taking [determinants](../../../../../../determinant.md) of $(BC)^{\mathsf T}=-C^{-1}B$ gives

$$
\det B\,\det C=-\frac{\det B}{\det C},
$$

where the minus sign comes from the odd [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) order. Multiplying by $\det C$ gives $\det B((\det C)^2+1)=0$. Since $C$ is real, the second factor is positive. Thus $\boxed{\det B=0}$ and $B$ is a [singular matrix](../../../../../../singular-matrix.md). This is the [singularity forced by an odd-dimensional transpose-inverse identity](../../../../../../singularity-forced-by-an-odd-dimensional-transpose-inverse-identity.md), with the roles of the two [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) letters exchanged.
