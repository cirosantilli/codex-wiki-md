<h1 id="20/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $u=e^{-x^2}$, so $u'+2xu=0$. Differentiate this identity $n$ times, for $n\geq1$. By the [Leibniz rule](../../../../../leibniz-rule.md), only the first two [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) of the linear factor $x$ can contribute:

$$
u^{(n+1)}+2xu^{(n)}+2nu^{(n-1)}=0.
$$

Rearranging proves

$$
\boxed{\left(2x+\frac d{dx}\right)\frac{d^n}{dx^n}e^{-x^2}
=-2n\frac{d^{n-1}}{dx^{n-1}}e^{-x^2}}.
$$

The case $n=0$ is the starting identity $u'+2xu=0$, treated separately without introducing a negative [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) order.
