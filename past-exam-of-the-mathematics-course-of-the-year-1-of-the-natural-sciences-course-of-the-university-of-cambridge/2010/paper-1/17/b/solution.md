<h1 id="17/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Away from $x=-1$ the [separable differential equation](../../../../../separable-differential-equation.md) gives

$$
\frac{dy}{y}=\frac{x^2}{1+x^3}dx,\qquad
\ln|y|=\frac13\ln|1+x^3|+C.
$$

On the [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) containing $x=1$, one has $x>-1$, so the positive initial value selects $y=K(1+x^3)^{1/3}$. Substitution of $y(1)=2$ gives $K=2^{2/3}$. Thus

$$
\boxed{y(x)=2^{2/3}(1+x^3)^{1/3},\qquad x>-1.}
$$

The zero solution omitted by division by $y$ cannot satisfy the initial value. Although the selected solution tends to zero as $x\downarrow-1$, its [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) diverges there; it does not extend through that point as a classical [differentiable](../../../../../differentiable-function.md) solution.
