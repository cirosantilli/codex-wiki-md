<h1 id="15/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $d=b\cdot x$. Taking a [dot product](../../../../../dot-product.md) with $b$ reduces the [vector](../../../../../vector.md) equation to $(1-b\cdot c)d=b\cdot a$. If $b\cdot c\ne1$, this determines a unique [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md) and hence a unique [vector](../../../../../vector.md):

$$
\boxed{x=a+\frac{b\cdot a}{1-b\cdot c}\,c.}
$$

Direct substitution verifies it, so necessity has also produced sufficiency. The formula includes $c=0$, when $x=a$.

If $b\cdot c=1$, the reduced [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md) equation becomes $0=b\cdot a$. When $b\cdot a\ne0$, **there is no solution**. When $b\cdot a=0$, **all solutions are $x=a+dc$ with arbitrary $d\in\mathbb R$**: their [dot product](../../../../../dot-product.md) with $b$ is indeed $d$. Since $b\cdot c=1$ forces $c\ne0$, this is an entire affine [line](../../../../../straight-line.md), not a single point. These alternatives are the [solvability of an affine rank-one vector equation](../../../../../solvability-of-an-affine-rank-one-vector-equation.md).
