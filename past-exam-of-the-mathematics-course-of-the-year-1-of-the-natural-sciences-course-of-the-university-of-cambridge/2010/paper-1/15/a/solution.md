<h1 id="15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Put $d=b\cdot x$ and $B=|b|^2$. Taking the [dot product](../../../../../dot-product.md) of the original equation with $b$ eliminates $b\times x$ and gives $d=a\cdot b-Bd$. Thus

$$
\boxed{b\cdot x=\frac{a\cdot b}{1+|b|^2}.}
$$

Next take its [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) with $b$. The term $d b$ disappears, and the [vector triple product identity](../../../../../vector-triple-product.md) gives

$$
b\times x=b\times a-b\times(b\times x)
=b\times a-bd+Bx.
$$

Insert this expression back into $x=a-db-b\times x$. The two longitudinal terms cancel, leaving $(1+B)x=a-b\times a$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{x=\frac{a-b\times a}{1+|b|^2}=\frac{a+a\times b}{1+|b|^2}.}
$$

The denominator is strictly positive for every real $b$, including $b=0$. Substitution using the same triple-product identity verifies the solution and establishes uniqueness. This is the [cross-product operator with a longitudinal rank-one term](../../../../../cross-product-operator-with-a-longitudinal-rank-one-term.md).
