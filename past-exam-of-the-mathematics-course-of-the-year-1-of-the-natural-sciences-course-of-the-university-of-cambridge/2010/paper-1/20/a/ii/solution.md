<h1 id="20/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Here $0<\ln2<1$, so $(\ln2)^{-n}$ grows exponentially rather than decaying. The positive terms $a_n=n^3/(\ln2)^n$ do not tend to zero; indeed they tend to $+\infty$. Therefore **the series diverges** by the necessary condition that the [terms of a convergent series tend to zero](../../../../../../terms-of-a-convergent-series-tend-to-zero.md).

The [ratio test](../../../../../../ratio-test.md) gives the same conclusion: $a_{n+1}/a_n=((n+1)/n)^3/\ln2\to1/\ln2>1$.
