<h1 id="20/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For [absolute convergence](../../../../../absolute-convergence.md), examine $\sum_{n\geq1}|x-3|^n/n$. When $0<|x-3|<1$, the ratio of successive terms tends to $|x-3|<1$, so the [ratio test](../../../../../ratio-test.md) proves convergence; at $x=3$ every term is zero. For $|x-3|>1$, the terms do not tend to zero.

At either boundary $|x-3|=1$, the absolute-value series is the divergent [harmonic series](../../../../../harmonic-series.md). Thus for real $x$ the complete answer is

$$
\boxed{|x-3|<1,\qquad 2<x<4.}
$$

The original series at $x=2$ is conditionally convergent by the alternating-series criterion, but it is not absolutely convergent and is correctly excluded here.
