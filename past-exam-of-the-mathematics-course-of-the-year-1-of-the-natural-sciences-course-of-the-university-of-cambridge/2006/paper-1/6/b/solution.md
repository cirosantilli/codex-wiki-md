<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

With $s=\sin\theta_0$ and $c=\cos\theta_0$, conversion from [spherical coordinates](../../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) gives $\mathbf a=r_0(s,0,c)$, $\mathbf b=r_0(-s/2,\sqrt3s/2,c)$ and $\mathbf c=r_0(-s/2,-\sqrt3s/2,c)$. The [volume](../../../../../volume.md) of the [parallelepiped](../../../../../parallelepiped.md) is the [absolute value](../../../../../absolute-value.md) of their [scalar triple product](../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md):

$$
\boxed{V=|\det(\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c)|=\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}r_0^3\sin^2\theta_0\,|\cos\theta_0|.}
$$

The absolute value matters when the polar angle is in the lower hemisphere. At the poles or equator the three edge vectors are linearly dependent and the volume vanishes.
