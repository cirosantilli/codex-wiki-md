↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Yes, in even dimension.** For example, choose the [skew-symmetric matrix](../../../../../../skew-symmetric-matrix.md)

$$
\boxed{B=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}=-B.}
$$

Here $B^2=-I$, so $(I+B)^{-1}=(I-B)/2$ and $C=(I-B)^2/2=-B$. It is an [orthogonal skew-symmetric matrix](../../../../../../orthogonal-skew-symmetric-matrix.md). More generally the skew-symmetry condition $C=-C^{-1}$ gives $(I-B)^2=-(I+B)^2$, or $B^2=-I$; no real odd-dimensional matrix can satisfy that condition.
