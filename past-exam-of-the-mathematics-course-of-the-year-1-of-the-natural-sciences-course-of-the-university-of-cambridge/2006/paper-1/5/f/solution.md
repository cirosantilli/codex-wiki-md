<h1 id="5/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $\mathbf a$ is [orthogonal](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) to both $\mathbf b$ and $\mathbf c$, so $\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b-\mathbf c)=0$. Also $\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf b=\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf c=1/S$, hence $\mathbf d\cdot(\mathbf b-\mathbf c)=0$. Subtraction gives $(\mathbf a-\mathbf d)\cdot(\mathbf b-\mathbf c)=0$. Both vectors are nonzero, so

$$
\boxed{\text{the angle is }\pi/2.}
$$

This is the altitude property of the [orthogonal projection onto an intercept plane](../../../../../orthogonal-projection-onto-an-intercept-plane.md): the projection point is the triangle's [orthocenter](../../../../../orthocenter.md).
