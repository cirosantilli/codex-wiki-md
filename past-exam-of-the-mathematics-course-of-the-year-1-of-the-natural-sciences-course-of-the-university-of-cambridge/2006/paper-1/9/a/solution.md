<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Taylor's theorem](../../../../../taylor-s-theorem.md) expands about $a$ in powers of $h=x-a$. With the usual differentiability assumptions, its finite form with [Lagrange remainder](../../../../../lagrange-remainder.md) is

$$
f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k+\frac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}(x-a)^{n+1},
$$

where $\xi$ lies between $a$ and $x$. If the remainder tends to zero as $n\to\infty$, this yields the [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) $f(x)=\sum_{k\geq0}f^{(k)}(a)(x-a)^k/k!$. Smoothness alone does not guarantee that infinite-series equality.
