↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Assuming the egg colours are [independent](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) with the same probability $p$, the number of white eggs has the [binomial distribution](../../../../../../binomial-distribution.md) with four trials. The possibilities are

| Contents | Probability | $W_0B_4$ | $(1-p)^4$ | $W_1B_3$ | $4p(1-p)^3$ | $W_2B_2$ | $6p^2(1-p)^2$ | $W_3B_1$ | $4p^3(1-p)$ | $W_4B_0$ | $p^4$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

The [binomial coefficients](../../../../../../binomial-coefficient-split.md) count which eggs are white. Marginal colour probabilities alone would not determine nest probabilities without the [independence](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) assumption.
