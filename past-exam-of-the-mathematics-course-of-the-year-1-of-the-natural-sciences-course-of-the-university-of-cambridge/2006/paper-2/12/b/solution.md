<h1 id="12/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At $(t,t)$ with $t^2=1/2$, differentiating the [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) yields the [Hessian matrix](../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) $H=-tI$. At the positive point it is [negative definite](../../../../../negative-definite-matrix.md), and at the negative point it is [positive-definite](../../../../../positive-definite-bilinear-form.md); in both cases $f_{xy}=0$ and $\det H=1/2$. Therefore the **positive point is a maximum, the negative point is a minimum, and there are no saddle points**.

They are global extrema: with $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) gives $|f|\leq\sqrt2r/(1+r^2)\leq1/\sqrt2$, with equality at exactly those two points. This is the extremum bound underlying the [level sets of a linear numerator over a radial quadratic](../../../../../level-sets-of-a-linear-numerator-over-a-radial-quadratic.md).
