<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Where $f_z\ne0$, the [implicit function theorem](../../../../../implicit-function-theorem.md) makes the surface a local graph $z=h(x,y)$ with $h_x=-f_x/f_z$ and $h_y=-f_y/f_z$. Its upward [vector area element](../../../../../vector-area-element.md) is $(-h_x,-h_y,1)dx\,dy$. Therefore the [vector area element of an implicit surface](../../../../../vector-area-element-of-an-implicit-surface.md) is

$$
\boxed{d\mathbf S=\frac{\nabla f}{f_z}\,dx\,dy,\qquad\mathbf F=\frac{\nabla f}{f_z},\quad\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf k=1.}
$$

Dividing by $f_z$ fixes the upward orientation even if the initially chosen gradient points downward. The restriction excludes a locally vertical graph, where this particular projected-area representation cannot be used.
