<h1 id="11/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The substitution gives $x^2-y^2=u^2\cos2v$. For this function $f_{uu}=2\cos2v$, $f_u/u=2\cos2v$, and $f_{vv}/u^2=-4\cos2v$, whose sum is zero. Thus it satisfies the polar form of [Laplace's equation](../../../../../laplace-equation.md) for $u>0$. The apparent coordinate singularity at $u=0$ does not make the original Cartesian [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) singular; it is a smooth [harmonic function](../../../../../harmonic-function.md) through the origin.
