<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Expanding the two products of [Householder reflections](../../../../../householder-transformation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{BA-AB=4(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)(\mathbf b\mathbf a^T-\mathbf a\mathbf b^T).}
$$

The [commuting plane reflections](../../../../../commuting-plane-reflections.md) criterion follows: the operator vanishes if the normals are [orthogonal](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md), or if they are parallel. Conversely, if $c=\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b\ne0$ and the operator vanishes, applying $\mathbf b\mathbf a^T-\mathbf a\mathbf b^T$ to $\mathbf a$ gives $\mathbf b-c\mathbf a=0$, proving parallelism.

This establishes equality of the reflected directions **for every incident ray** exactly in those two cases. For one fixed ray the claim needs that qualification: if nonparallel normals are not orthogonal, a ray along $\mathbf a\times\mathbf b$ has zero normal component for both mirrors and is unchanged in either order. Algebraically it lies in the [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) of the commutator; it is a special direction rather than a pair of commuting reflection operators.
