↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For [horizontal slices of a surface of revolution in spherical coordinates](../../../../../horizontal-slices-of-a-surface-of-revolution-in-spherical-coordinates.md), use disk area times vertical thickness. Here the cylindrical radius is $\rho=a(1-\cos\theta)\sin\theta$ and the height is $z=a(\cos\theta-\cos^2\theta)$. For $\theta>\pi/2$, $dz/d\theta=-a(1-2\cos\theta)\sin\theta<0$. Hence a positive angular increment gives

$$
\boxed{dV=\pi a^3(1-\cos\theta_0)^2(1-2\cos\theta_0)\sin^3\theta_0\,d\theta.}
$$

These are horizontal disks in the lower part of the solid. A spherical angular-wedge element $r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi$ describes a different infinitesimal slice.
