<h1 id="18/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**Not as ordinary convergent [Fourier series](../../../../../../fourier-series-split.md)**, for a nontrivial moving ramp. Formal spatial differentiation would give

$$
\frac{2K}{L}\sum_{n=1}^\infty(-1)^{n+1}
\cos\left[\frac{2\pi n}{L}(x-vt)\right],
$$

and formal time differentiation multiplies that expression by $-v$. The summands have nondecaying amplitude and do not tend to zero, so the ordinary series cannot converge. The periodic extension has jumps, violating the regularity needed for [Termwise differentiation of a Fourier series](../../../../../../termwise-differentiation-of-a-fourier-series.md).

Away from the moving jumps, the classical [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) exist and are $g_x=K/L$ and $g_t=-vK/L$. A [distributional derivative](../../../../../../distributional-derivative.md) also includes the [Dirac delta distribution](../../../../../../dirac-delta-function.md) contributions at the jumps, so distributional differentiation is possible, but it is a different interpretation. If $v=0$, the time [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is trivially zero; if $K=0$, both [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) are zero.
