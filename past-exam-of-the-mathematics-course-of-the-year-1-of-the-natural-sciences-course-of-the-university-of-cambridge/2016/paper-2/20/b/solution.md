<h1 id="20/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use [separation of variables](../../../../../separation-of-variables.md), $f=X(x)T(t)$, and set

$$
\frac{X''}{X}=\frac{T''}{c^2T}=\kappa.
$$

For $\kappa=-\lambda^2$, with $\lambda>0$, the separated solutions are

$$
\boxed{f=(A\cos\lambda x+B\sin\lambda x)
(C\cos c\lambda t+D\sin c\lambda t)}.
$$

For $\kappa=0$, they are

$$
\boxed{f=(Ax+B)(Ct+D)}.
$$

For $\kappa=+\lambda^2$, they are

$$
\boxed{f=(A\cosh\lambda x+B\sinh\lambda x)
(C\cosh c\lambda t+D\sinh c\lambda t)}.
$$

Arbitrary admissible superpositions of separated solutions are again solutions. An individual product is only a separated family, not the entire [general solution](../../../../../general-solution.md). Without prescribed boundaries, the general twice-differentiable solution is equivalently [D'Alembert's formula](../../../../../d-alembert-s-formula.md),

$$
\boxed{f(x,t)=F(x-ct)+G(x+ct)}.
$$

For example, changing to $\xi=x-ct$, $\eta=x+ct$ makes the differential equation $f_{\xi\eta}=0$, whose two integrations give this form.
