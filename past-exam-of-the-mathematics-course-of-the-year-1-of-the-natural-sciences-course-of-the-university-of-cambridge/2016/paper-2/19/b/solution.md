<h1 id="19/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put $q=2.4048$ and let the fixed constraint value be $C>0$. For $V=\pi R^2H$, the [Lagrange multiplier](../../../../../lagrange-multiplier.md) equations give

$$
2\pi RH=\lambda\frac{-2q^2}{R^3},\qquad
\pi R^2=\lambda\frac{-2\pi^2}{H^3}.
$$

Dividing removes $\lambda$ and yields

$$
\frac{2H}{R}=\frac{q^2H^3}{\pi^2R^3},\qquad
\boxed{\frac HR=\frac{\sqrt2\pi}{q}=\frac{\sqrt2\pi}{2.4048}}.
$$

To establish a minimum, rather than merely a stationary ratio, set $u=q^2/R^2$ and $v=\pi^2/H^2$. Then $u+v=C$ and $V=\pi^2q^2/(u\sqrt v)$. The denominator squared is $u^2(C-u)$; its [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) is $u(2C-3u)$, giving a unique maximum at $u=2C/3$, $v=C/3$. The [volume](../../../../../volume.md) tends to infinity at either endpoint. This proves the stated ratio is the unique [global minimum](../../../../../global-minimum.md), as in the [optimal cylinder for a reciprocal-square constraint](../../../../../optimal-cylinder-for-a-reciprocal-square-constraint.md).
