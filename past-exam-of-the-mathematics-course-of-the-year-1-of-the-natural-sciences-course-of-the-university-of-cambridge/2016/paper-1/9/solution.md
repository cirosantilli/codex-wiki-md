<h1 id="9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9](../9.md)

Rewrite the [rational function](../../../../rational-function.md) as $y=1-1/(x^3+1)$. It is undefined at $x=-1$, with the vertical [asymptote](../../../../asymptote.md) $x=-1$, and tends to the horizontal [asymptote](../../../../asymptote.md) $y=1$ as $x\to\pm\infty$. At the pole, $y\to+\infty$ from the left and $y\to-\infty$ from the right.

Its [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) is

$$
y'=\frac{3x^2}{(x^3+1)^2}\geq0,
$$

vanishing only at $x=0$. Since $y(0)=0$ and $y=x^3+O(x^6)$ near zero, this is a stationary [inflection point](../../../../inflection-point.md), not an extremum. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{stationary point }(0,0);\qquad\text{asymptotes }x=-1,\ y=1.}
$$

The graph increases on each side of the vertical [asymptote](../../../../asymptote.md). The left branch lies above $y=1$, while the right branch lies below it and crosses the origin horizontally.
