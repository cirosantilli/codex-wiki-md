<h1 id="18/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Index the stated [geometric progression](../../../../../../geometric-progression.md) by $x_i=(1+\epsilon)^i$, $i=0,1,\ldots$. Then $\Delta x_i=\epsilon(1+\epsilon)^i$, and its left-endpoint contribution is

$$
f(x_i)\Delta x_i=(1+\epsilon)^{-(1+\alpha)i}\epsilon(1+\epsilon)^i
=\epsilon\big[(1+\epsilon)^{-\alpha}\big]^i.
$$

Hence the approximation is a [geometric series](../../../../../../geometric-series.md) with

$$
\boxed{\text{first term }\epsilon,\qquad q=(1+\epsilon)^{-\alpha},\qquad
U_\epsilon=\frac{\epsilon}{1-(1+\epsilon)^{-\alpha}}.}
$$

Since $\epsilon>0$ and $\alpha>0$, the ratio satisfies $0<q<1$ and the series converges. The integrand is decreasing, so the left-endpoint sum is an upper bound for the [improper integral](../../../../../../improper-integral.md).
