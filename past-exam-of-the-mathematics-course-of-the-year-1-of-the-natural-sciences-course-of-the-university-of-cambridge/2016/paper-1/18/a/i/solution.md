<h1 id="18/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Partition the finite interval as $a=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_N=b$, with $\Delta x_i=x_{i+1}-x_i$. For a [Riemann integrable](../../../../../../riemann-integrable-function.md) [function](../../../../../../function-split.md), the left-endpoint [Riemann sums](../../../../../../riemann-sum.md) satisfy

$$
\boxed{\int_a^b f(x)\,dx
=\lim_{\max_i\Delta x_i\to0}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}f(x_i)\Delta x_i.}
$$

The maximum subinterval width, rather than merely the number of subintervals, must tend to zero. Continuity of $f$ on the closed interval is a sufficient integrability hypothesis. More generally, any chosen sample point in each subinterval gives the same [definite integral](../../../../../../definite-integral.md) under these assumptions.
