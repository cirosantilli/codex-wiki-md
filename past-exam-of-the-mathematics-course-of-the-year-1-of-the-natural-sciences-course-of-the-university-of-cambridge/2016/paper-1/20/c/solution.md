<h1 id="20/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The modified upper surface has exactly the same [ellipse](../../../../../ellipse.md) at $z=0$ and the same [boundary orientation](../../../../../boundary-orientation.md). The field retains the global [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) [vector potential](../../../../../vector-potential.md) from part (a). By the [Stokes theorem](../../../../../stokes-theorem.md), both surface fluxes equal the same boundary [line integral](../../../../../line-integral.md); the cap's height and interior shape do not enter. Hence

$$
\boxed{A=\frac\pi4a^2|ab|.}
$$

This is an instance of the fact that [solenoidal flux depends on the oriented boundary](../../../../../solenoidal-flux-depends-on-the-oriented-boundary.md), with the domain condition automatically satisfied by the smooth [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) field on all of $\mathbb R^3$. When $a,b$ are chosen positive, the formula simplifies to $\pi a^3b/4$; the absolute-value form respects the original allowance of arbitrary nonzero signs.
