<h1 id="13/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Integrating $\phi_x=2xy$ gives $\phi=x^2y+h(y,z)$. Matching $\phi_y=x^2+3y^2+z^2$ then gives $h=y^3+yz^2+k(z)$. Finally $\phi_z=2yz$ forces $k$ to be constant. A [scalar potential](../../../../../scalar-potential.md) is

$$
\boxed{\phi(x,y,z)=x^2y+y^3+yz^2+C.}
$$

The initial and final points of the [helix](../../../../../helix.md) are $(b,0,0)$ and $(0,b,cL)$, where $L=5\pi/2$. The [fundamental theorem for line integrals](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-for-line-integrals.md) gives

$$
\phi(0,b,cL)-\phi(b,0,0)=b^3+bc^2L^2
=\boxed{b\left(b^2+\frac{25\pi^2c^2}{4}\right)},
$$

agreeing with the directly evaluated $I_2$.
