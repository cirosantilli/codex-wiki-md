<h1 id="13/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Direct calculation of the [curl](../../../../../curl.md) gives

$$
\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf v_1=2\mathbf a,\qquad
\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf v_2=\mathbf0.
$$

Since $\mathbf a\ne0$, the first [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md) cannot be a [gradient field](../../../../../gradient-field.md): the [curl of a gradient](../../../../../curl-of-a-gradient.md) is zero. The second is a smooth [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) field on all of the [simply connected](../../../../../simply-connected-space.md) space $\mathbb R^3$, so its vanishing [curl](../../../../../curl.md) makes it a [conservative vector field](../../../../../conservative-vector-field.md). A potential is also constructed explicitly in the next part. Thus **$\mathbf v_2$ is a [conservative vector field](../../../../../conservative-vector-field.md) and $\mathbf v_1$ is not**. A vanishing [divergence](../../../../../divergence.md) alone, as for $\mathbf v_1$, is a different property and does not establish conservativity.
