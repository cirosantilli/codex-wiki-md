<h1 id="14/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The third coordinate is decoupled, giving the unit [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) $(0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}$ with [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $1$. In the first two coordinates, the symmetric direction $(1,1)$ has [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $3-1=2$, and the antisymmetric direction $(1,-1)$ has [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $3+1=4$. Their normalized forms are

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c}
\lambda_1=2&\mathbf e_1=\dfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1,0)^{\mathsf T}\\[3pt]
\lambda_2=4&\mathbf e_2=\dfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1,0)^{\mathsf T}\\[3pt]
\lambda_3=1&\mathbf e_3=(0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\end{array}}
$$

They are an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of $\mathbb R^3$; independent sign reversals are equally valid choices.
