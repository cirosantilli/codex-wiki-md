<h1 id="16/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a stop-phase arrival at time $t$, the delay is $s=r-t$. An interval of delays of width $ds$ therefore corresponds to an interval of arrival phases of the same width, because $|dt/ds|=1$. Its unconditional [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) is $ds/R$. Hence the positive-delay part has density

$$
\boxed{p(s)=\begin{cases}1/R,&0<s\leq r,\\0,&r<s\leq R.\end{cases}}
$$

This ordinary density integrates to $r/R$, not to one: the remaining [probability mass](../../../../../../probability-mass.md) is at $s=0$. Thus $S$ has a [uniform distribution with an atom at zero](../../../../../../uniform-distribution-with-an-atom-at-zero.md), with mixing weight $q=r/R$. Conditional on a positive delay, the density would instead be $1/r$ on $(0,r)$.
