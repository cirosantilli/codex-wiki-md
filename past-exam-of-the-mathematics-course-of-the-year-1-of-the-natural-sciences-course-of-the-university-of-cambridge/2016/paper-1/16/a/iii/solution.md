<h1 id="16/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The graph of the positive-delay [probability density function](../../../../../../probability-density-function.md) is a horizontal segment at height $1/R$ from $s=0$ to $r$, followed by zero from $r$ to $R$. Values at individual endpoints do not affect [probabilities](../../../../../../probability.md). The separate atom $1-r/R$ at zero is not a finite-height part of this density graph.

<a id="16/a/iii/image-positive-delay-density-and-the-full-cumulative-distribution-whose-jump-records-the-no-delay-atom"></a>
![](../../../../../../past-exam-of-the-mathematics-course-of-the-year-1-of-the-natural-sciences-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2016/paper-1-delay-distribution.png)

**[Figure 4](#16/a/iii/image-positive-delay-density-and-the-full-cumulative-distribution-whose-jump-records-the-no-delay-atom). Positive-delay density and the full cumulative distribution, whose jump records the no-delay atom**.

The companion [cumulative distribution function](../../../../../../cumulative-distribution-function.md) in the figure is $F_S(s)=0$ for $s<0$, $F_S(s)=1-r/R+s/R$ for $0\leq s<r$, and $F_S(s)=1$ for $s\geq r$. Its jump at zero displays the discrete part without confusing density height with [probability mass](../../../../../../probability-mass.md).
