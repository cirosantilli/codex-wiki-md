↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A three-dimensional [rotation matrix](../../../../../../rotation-matrix.md) is an [orthogonal matrix](../../../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) with [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) $+1$. Here $\det A=b(a^2-d^2)$. For the branch $d=0$, the determinant condition gives $b=1$; for the branch $a=0$, it gives $b=-1$. Hence

$$
\boxed{(a,b,c)^{\mathsf T}\in\{(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T},(-1,1,1)^{\mathsf T},(0,-1,0)^{\mathsf T},(0,-1,2)^{\mathsf T}\}.}
$$

The first is the identity [rotation](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md); the remaining three are half-turns. Their fixed axes are respectively the $y$ axis, the direction $(1,0,-1)$ and the direction $(1,0,1)$. The identity is included under the usual [rotation matrix](../../../../../../rotation-matrix.md) convention.
