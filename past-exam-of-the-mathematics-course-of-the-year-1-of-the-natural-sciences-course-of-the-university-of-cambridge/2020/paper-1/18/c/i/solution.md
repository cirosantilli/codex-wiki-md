<h1 id="18/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The original PDF requires $B$ itself to be an [orthogonal skew-symmetric matrix](../../../../../../orthogonal-skew-symmetric-matrix.md); it does not require $C$ to be diagonal. A real two-dimensional [skew-symmetric matrix](../../../../../../skew-symmetric-matrix.md) has the form $B=\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\-u&0\end{pmatrix}$. The [orthogonal matrix](../../../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) condition gives $u^2=1$, so choose $u=1$. Then $C=B^{\mathsf T}AB$ is forced:

$$
\boxed{B=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad C=\begin{pmatrix}-4&-3\\-3&4\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Direct multiplication verifies $B^{\mathsf T}=-B$, $B^{\mathsf T}B=I$ and $BCB^{\mathsf T}=A$. Choosing $-B$ gives the same $C$. Replacing $B$ by a matrix of [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) to make $C$ diagonal would generally fail the printed antisymmetry requirement.
