<h1 id="14/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Put $s=x^2+y^2$. The [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) components are

$$
f_x=4x(s-1),\qquad f_y=y\left[4(s-1)+\frac7{3+y^2}\right].
$$

A [stationary point](../../../../../stationary-point.md) requires both components to vanish. If $x\ne0$, then $s=1$; the second component subsequently forces $y=0$, giving $(\pm1,0)$. If $x=0$, either $y=0$ or, writing $u=y^2$,

$$
4(u-1)(3+u)+7=4u^2+8u-5=(2u-1)(2u+5)=0.
$$

Only $u=1/2$ is nonnegative. Thus there are five [stationary points](../../../../../stationary-point.md), with values

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
(\pm1,0):&\quad f=\frac72\ln3,\\
(0,0):&\quad f=1+\frac72\ln3,\\
(0,\pm1/\sqrt2):&\quad f=\frac14+\frac72\ln\frac72.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The cases $x=0$ and $s=1$ exhaust the first [partial derivative](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) equation, so no other [stationary points](../../../../../stationary-point.md) have been lost by division.
