<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Use [integration by substitution](../../../../integration-by-substitution.md) with $u=3-2x$, so $dx=-du/2$ and $x=(3-u)/2$. The [indefinite integral](../../../../antiderivative.md) becomes

$$
-\frac14\int(3u^{1/2}-u^{3/2})\,du=-\frac12u^{3/2}+\frac1{10}u^{5/2}+C.
$$

Thus, with an arbitrary [constant of integration](../../../../constant-of-integration.md),

$$
\boxed{\int x\sqrt{3-2x}\,dx=-\frac{x+1}{5}(3-2x)^{3/2}+C.}
$$

This real [antiderivative](../../../../antiderivative.md) applies for $x<3/2$ and extends continuously to the stated endpoint. Its left [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) there agrees with the limiting integrand, zero.
