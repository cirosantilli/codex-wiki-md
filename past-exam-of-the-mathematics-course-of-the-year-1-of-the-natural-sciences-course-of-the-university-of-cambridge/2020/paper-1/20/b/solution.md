<h1 id="20/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The proposed [parametric equation](../../../../../parametric-equation.md) traces precisely the same oriented segment, with a different speed. Set $u=t^n$, which increases from zero to one, and $du=nt^{n-1}\,dt$. Direct substitution gives the [line integrals](../../../../../line-integral.md)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf x&=\int_0^1(2-2t^n)nt^{n-1}\,dt=\int_0^1(2-2u)\,du=1,\\
\int\mathbf G\cdot d\mathbf x&=\int_0^1(2-4t^n+2t^{2n})nt^{n-1}\,dt=\int_0^1(2-4u+2u^2)\,du=\frac23.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus **both values are unchanged**. The [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) of $u$ vanishes at the initial endpoint for $n>1$, but the monotone [integration by substitution](../../../../../integration-by-substitution.md) is still valid. This is invariance under [reparametrization](../../../../../reparametrization.md) of one curve, which applies even to a nonconservative [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md); it is not evidence of general path independence.
