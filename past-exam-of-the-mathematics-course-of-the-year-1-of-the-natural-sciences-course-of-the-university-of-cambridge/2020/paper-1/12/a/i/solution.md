<h1 id="12/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $I$ be the positive [Gaussian integral](../../../../../../gaussian-integral.md). Its [improper integral](../../../../../../improper-integral.md) converges because $e^{-x^2}\le e^{-|x|}$ for $|x|\ge1$. Multiplying two copies and using the [polar coordinates](../../../../../../polar-coordinates.md) [area element](../../../../../../area-element-of-a-surface.md) gives

$$
I^2=\int_{\mathbb R^2}e^{-(x^2+y^2)}\,dx\,dy
=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2}r\,dr\,d\theta
=2\pi\left[-\frac12e^{-r^2}\right]_0^\infty=\pi.
$$

Nonnegativity justifies the product [double integral](../../../../../../double-integral.md) and the change of integration order. Since $I>0$,

$$
\boxed{\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-x^2}\,dx=\sqrt\pi.}
$$
