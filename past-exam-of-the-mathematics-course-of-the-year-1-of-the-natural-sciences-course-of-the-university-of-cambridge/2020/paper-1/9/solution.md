<h1 id="9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9](../9.md)

The first term is $ar$, so the [finite geometric series](../../../../finite-geometric-series.md) is $S=a(r+r^2+\cdots+r^{100})$. Subtracting $rS$ cancels the interior terms and gives $(1-r)S=ar(1-r^{100})$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{S=\frac{ar(1-r^{100})}{1-r}\quad(r\ne1);\qquad S=100a\quad(r=1).}
$$

The rational expression holds for every real $a$ and every real $r\ne1$, including $r=0$; the same algebra works for [complex numbers](../../../../complex-number.md). No restriction $|r|<1$ is needed for a [finite geometric series](../../../../finite-geometric-series.md). The exceptional case follows by adding one hundred equal terms, or by taking the [limit](../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) as $r\to1$.
