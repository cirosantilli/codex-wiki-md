<h1 id="17/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Choose the [trigonometric substitution](../../../../../../trigonometric-substitution.md) $x=\sin u$ with $-\pi/2\le u\le\pi/2$. The [cosine](../../../../../../cosine.md) is nonnegative there, so $\sqrt{1-x^2}=\cos u$ and $dx=\cos u\,du$. The [double-angle formula](../../../../../../double-angle-formula.md) then gives

$$
\int\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx=\int\cos^2u\,du=\frac u2+\frac14\sin2u+C.
$$

Since $u=\arcsin x$ and $\sin2u=2x\sqrt{1-x^2}$,

$$
\boxed{\int\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx=\frac12\left(\arcsin x+x\sqrt{1-x^2}\right)+C.}
$$

The formula is an [antiderivative](../../../../../../antiderivative.md) on $(-1,1)$ and has continuous endpoint values at $\pm1$.
