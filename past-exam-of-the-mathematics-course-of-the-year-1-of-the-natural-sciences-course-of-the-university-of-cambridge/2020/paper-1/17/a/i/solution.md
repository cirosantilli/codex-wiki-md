<h1 id="17/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use [integration by substitution](../../../../../../integration-by-substitution.md) $u=\cos x$, $du=-\sin x\,dx$. The denominator factorizes as $(u-2)(u-3)$, and [partial fraction decomposition](../../../../../../partial-fraction-decomposition.md) gives

$$
-\int\frac{du}{(u-2)(u-3)}=\int\left(\frac1{u-2}-\frac1{u-3}\right)\,du.
$$

Integrating and restoring $x$ yields

$$
\boxed{\int\frac{\sin x}{\cos^2x-5\cos x+6}\,dx=\ln\frac{2-\cos x}{3-\cos x}+C.}
$$

For real $x$, both $2-\cos x$ and $3-\cos x$ are positive, so no absolute-value signs are needed in this real [logarithm](../../../../../../logarithm.md). The denominator never vanishes.
