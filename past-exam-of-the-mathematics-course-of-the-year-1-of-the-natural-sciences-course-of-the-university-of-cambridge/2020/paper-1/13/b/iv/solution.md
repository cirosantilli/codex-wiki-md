<h1 id="13/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The [superposition principle](../../../../../../superposition-principle.md) gives the [general solution](../../../../../../general-solution.md) as $y_c+2y_{p,2}+y_{p,1}$:

$$
y=e^{-\sqrt3x/2}\left(A\cos\frac{3x}{2}+B\sin\frac{3x}{2}\right)+\frac{2x}{3}-\frac2{3\sqrt3}+\frac13e^{-\sqrt3x}.
$$

The first [boundary condition](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) gives $A=2/(3\sqrt3)-1/3$. At $x=\pi$, the [cosine](../../../../../../cosine.md) vanishes and the [sine](../../../../../../sine.md) equals $-1$. Comparing with the second [boundary condition](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) then gives

$$
-Be^{-\sqrt3\pi/2}+\frac{2\pi}{3}=0,
\qquad B=\frac{2\pi}{3}e^{\sqrt3\pi/2}.
$$

The unique solution satisfying both [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{y=e^{-\sqrt3x/2}\left[\left(\frac2{3\sqrt3}-\frac13\right)\cos\frac{3x}{2}+\frac{2\pi}{3}e^{\sqrt3\pi/2}\sin\frac{3x}{2}\right]+\frac{2x}{3}-\frac2{3\sqrt3}+\frac13e^{-\sqrt3x}.}
$$

Uniqueness follows because a [homogeneous solution](../../../../../../homogeneous-solution.md) zero at both endpoints would have $A=0$ from the first and $B=0$ from the second.
