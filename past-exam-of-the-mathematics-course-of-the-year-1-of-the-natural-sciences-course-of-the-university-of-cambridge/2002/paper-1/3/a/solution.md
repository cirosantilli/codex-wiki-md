<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At an interior [local extremum](../../../../../local-extremum.md) of a [differentiable function](../../../../../differentiable-function.md), $f'(x)=0$. The [second-derivative test](../../../../../second-derivative-test.md) classifies a [stationary point](../../../../../stationary-point.md): a positive [second derivative](../../../../../second-derivative.md) gives a strict [local minimum](../../../../../local-minimum.md), and a negative [second derivative](../../../../../second-derivative.md) gives a strict [local maximum](../../../../../local-maximum.md). A zero [second derivative](../../../../../second-derivative.md) is inconclusive: $x^4$, $-x^4$, and $x^3$ at zero illustrate a minimum, a maximum, and neither.

Here

$$
f'(x)=3x^2-10x+3=(3x-1)(x-3),\qquad f''(x)=6x-10.
$$

Thus $f''(1/3)=-8$ and $f''(3)=8$. Substitution into $f$ gives

$$
\boxed{(1/3,-14/27)\text{ is a local maximum},\qquad (3,-10)\text{ is a local minimum}.}
$$
