<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Two directions in the plane are $\mathbf b-\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf c-\mathbf a$. Their [cross product](../../../../../../cross-product.md) $\mathbf N=(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)\times(\mathbf c-\mathbf a)$ is a [normal vector](../../../../../../normal-vector.md). Moreover,

$$
\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf N=\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)=D\ne0,
$$

so $\mathbf N$ cannot vanish. The required [unit normal](../../../../../../unit-normal.md) and offset are

$$
\boxed{\mathbf n=\frac{(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)\times(\mathbf c-\mathbf a)}{|(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)\times(\mathbf c-\mathbf a)|},\qquad \mu=\frac D{|(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)\times(\mathbf c-\mathbf a)|}.}
$$

Taking [dot products](../../../../../../dot-product.md) with $\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c$ gives the same offset for all three points.
