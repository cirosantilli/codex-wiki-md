<h1 id="9/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Parametrize the segment by $\mathbf r(t)=(1-t,t,0)$, $0\leq t\leq1$. Its tangent is $(-1,1,0)$ and $\mathbf F=(-t,1-t,t-1)$, so $\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=1$. Hence its [line integral](../../../../../line-integral.md) is

$$
\boxed{\int\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=1.}
$$

For the other [line integral](../../../../../line-integral.md), $|\mathbf r|^2=(1-t)^2+t^2$ and $\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf r'=2t-1$. Thus

$$
\int\mathbf G\cdot d\mathbf r=\int_0^1\frac{2t-1}{[1+(1-t)^2+t^2]^{3/2}}dt=\left[-\frac1{\sqrt{1+(1-t)^2+t^2}}\right]_0^1=\boxed0.
$$

This also equals the change in $\Phi$, by the [fundamental theorem for line integrals](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-for-line-integrals.md).
