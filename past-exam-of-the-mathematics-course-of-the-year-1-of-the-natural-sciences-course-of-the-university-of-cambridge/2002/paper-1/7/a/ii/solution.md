<h1 id="7/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Divide by $\cos x$ on an interval where it is nonzero. For the resulting [first-order linear differential equation](../../../../../../first-order-linear-differential-equation.md), use the [integrating factor](../../../../../../integrating-factor.md) $M=\sec x+\tan x$, since $M'=\sec x\,M$. The right-hand side after division is also $M'$, so

$$
(My)'=MM',\qquad My=\frac12M^2+C.
$$

The identity $(\sec x+\tan x)(\sec x-\tan x)=1$ shows $M$ never vanishes on the interval, giving

$$
\boxed{y=\frac12(\sec x+\tan x)+C(\sec x-\tan x).}
$$

An [integrating factor](../../../../../../integrating-factor.md) may have either fixed sign; if obtained by integrating $\sec x$, the real [logarithm](../../../../../../logarithm.md) is $\ln|\sec x+\tan x|$.
