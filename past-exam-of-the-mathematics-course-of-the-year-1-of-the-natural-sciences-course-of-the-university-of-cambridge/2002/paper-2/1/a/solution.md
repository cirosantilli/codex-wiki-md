<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Three [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) in three-dimensional [Euclidean space](../../../../../euclidean-norm.md) are non-coplanar precisely when their [scalar triple product](../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md) is nonzero. Put $\Delta=a\cdot(b\times c)\ne0$. In particular, $a\ne0$. The identity for [cross products with a common vector](../../../../../cross-products-with-a-common-vector.md) gives

$$
(a\times b)\times(a\times c)=\bigl[a\cdot(b\times c)\bigr]a=\Delta a.
$$

For completeness, this follows from the [vector triple product identity](../../../../../vector-triple-product.md): writing $p=a\times b$ gives $p\times(a\times c)=a(p\cdot c)-c(p\cdot a)$, with $p\cdot c=\Delta$ and $p\cdot a=0$. Therefore the new [scalar triple product](../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md) is

$$
\boxed{a\cdot\bigl[(a\times b)\times(a\times c)\bigr]=|a|^2\Delta\ne0.}
$$

Thus $a,a\times b,a\times c$ are non-coplanar, or equivalently [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md).
