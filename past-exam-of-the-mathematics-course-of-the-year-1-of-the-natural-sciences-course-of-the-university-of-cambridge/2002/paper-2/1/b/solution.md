<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take the [dot product](../../../../../dot-product.md) of the equation with $a$. Since $a\cdot(a\times r)=0$, we obtain $a\cdot r=(a\cdot c)/\lambda$. Next take its [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) with $a$ and apply the [vector triple product identity](../../../../../vector-triple-product.md):

$$
a(a\cdot r)-|a|^2r+\lambda(a\times r)=a\times c.
$$

Substitute $a\times r=c-\lambda r$ and the known [dot product](../../../../../dot-product.md), then rearrange:

$$
\boxed{r=\frac{\lambda c-a\times c+\lambda^{-1}(a\cdot c)a}{\lambda^2+|a|^2}.}
$$

Here the [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) and $\lambda\ne0$ are real, so the denominator is positive. The formula also covers $a=0$, giving $r=c/\lambda$. To check existence as well as uniqueness, write its numerator as $N$. The [vector triple product identity](../../../../../vector-triple-product.md) gives $a\times N=\lambda a\times c-a(a\cdot c)+|a|^2c$, so $a\times N+\lambda N=(\lambda^2+|a|^2)c$. Substitution verifies the original equation. This is an instance of the [inverse of a scalar plus a cross-product operator](../../../../../inverse-of-a-scalar-plus-a-cross-product-operator.md).
