<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [mass density](../../../../../density.md) becomes $\rho=\mu(2+\cos\theta)$. Integrate it over the [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) using the [spherical polar coordinates](../../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) and [volume element](../../../../../volume-element.md) above:

$$
M=\mu\int_0^a r^2\,dr\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi\int_0^\pi(2+\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta.
$$

The angular [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) is $4$, since $\int_0^\pi\cos\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{M=\frac{8\pi\mu a^3}{3}.}
$$

The term proportional to $z/r$ contributes zero by reflection across the equatorial [plane](../../../../../plane.md). Thus the answer is also the [volume](../../../../../volume.md) $4\pi a^3/3$ times the average [mass density](../../../../../density.md) $2\mu$. The undefined direction at the origin affects a single point and has no effect on this [volume integral](../../../../../volume-integral.md).
