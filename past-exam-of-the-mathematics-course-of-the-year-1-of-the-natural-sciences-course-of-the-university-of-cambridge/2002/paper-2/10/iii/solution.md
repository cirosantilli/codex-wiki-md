<h1 id="10/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $a_n=n^2e^{-\alpha n}$, the [ratio test](../../../../../ratio-test.md) gives

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac1n\right)^2e^{-\alpha}=e^{-\alpha}.
$$

This is less than one exactly when $\alpha>0$. For $\alpha=0$ the terms are $n^2$, and for $\alpha<0$ they grow exponentially; in both cases they fail to tend to zero. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\text{convergent exactly for }\alpha>0.}
$$
