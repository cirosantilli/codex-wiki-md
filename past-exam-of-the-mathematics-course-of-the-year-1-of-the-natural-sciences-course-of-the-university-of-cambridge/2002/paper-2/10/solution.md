<h1 id="10/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [10](../10.md)

The [comparison test for series](../../../../comparison-test-for-series.md) states that if $0\leq a_n\leq b_n$ for all sufficiently large $n$, then convergence of $\sum b_n$ implies convergence of $\sum a_n$; conversely, divergence of $\sum a_n$ implies divergence of $\sum b_n$. A summable bound on $|a_n|$ proves [absolute convergence](../../../../absolute-convergence.md) of $\sum a_n$.

For the [ratio test](../../../../ratio-test.md), suppose the terms are eventually nonzero and $L=\lim_{n\to\infty}|a_{n+1}/a_n|$ exists. If $L<1$, the [series](../../../../series-mathematics.md) converges absolutely. If $L>1$, including $L=\infty$, the terms fail to tend to zero and the [series](../../../../series-mathematics.md) diverges. If $L=1$, the test is inconclusive. An eventually zero [series](../../../../series-mathematics.md) converges separately. Convergence means that the sequence of partial sums has a finite limit; the terms tending to zero is only a necessary condition for a [convergent series](../../../../convergent-series.md).
