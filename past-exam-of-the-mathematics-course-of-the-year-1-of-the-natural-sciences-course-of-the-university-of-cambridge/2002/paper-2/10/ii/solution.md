<h1 id="10/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

This is a [geometric series](../../../../../geometric-series.md) with ratio $r=2\alpha/(\alpha^2+1)$. Since $\alpha^2+1>0$, the condition $|r|<1$ is equivalent to

$$
\alpha^2+1-2|\alpha|=(|\alpha|-1)^2>0.
$$

It holds for every real $\alpha$ except $\alpha=\pm1$. At $\alpha=1$ the terms are all one; at $\alpha=-1$ they alternate between $-1$ and $1$. Neither sequence of terms tends to zero. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{convergent exactly for }\alpha\in\mathbb R\setminus\{-1,1\}.}
$$

All the convergent cases have [absolute convergence](../../../../../absolute-convergence.md), including $\alpha=0$, when every term is zero.
