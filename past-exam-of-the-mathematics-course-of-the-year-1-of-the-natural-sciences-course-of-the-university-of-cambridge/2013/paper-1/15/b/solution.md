<h1 id="15/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $h>0$, parametrize the curved surface by $(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,r^2/a)$, with $0\leq r\leq\sqrt{ah}$ and $0\leq\theta\leq2\pi$. The magnitude of the [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) of its coordinate tangents gives the [area element of a surface](../../../../../area-element-of-a-surface.md):

$$
dS=r\sqrt{1+\frac{4r^2}{a^2}}\,dr\,d\theta.
$$

Integrating gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
S&=2\pi\int_0^{\sqrt{ah}}r\sqrt{1+4r^2/a^2}\,dr\\
&=\frac{\pi a^2}{6}\left[(1+4r^2/a^2)^{3/2}\right]_0^{\sqrt{ah}}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{\pi a^2}{6}\left[\left(1+\frac{4h}{a}\right)^{3/2}-1\right].}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the curved [surface area](../../../../../surface-area.md), not the area of a lid across the horizontal opening. The expression tends to zero at $h=0$ and has dimensions of area. The [volume and centroid of a parabolic cup](../../../../../volume-and-centroid-of-a-parabolic-cup.md) use the same geometry.
