<h1 id="16/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a classical solution take $F,G$ twice differentiable, so that the second [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) exist. Direct use of the [chain rule](../../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives

$$
y_{tt}=25(F''+G''),\qquad y_{xx}=4(F''+G''),
$$

with the two profile arguments as specified. Thus $4y_{tt}=25y_{xx}$.

To prove generality, introduce [characteristic coordinates](../../../../../../characteristic-coordinate.md) $\xi=2x+5t$, $\eta=2x-5t$, whose Jacobian [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) is $-20\ne0$. For $y=U(\xi,\eta)$ the differential operator is

$$
4\partial_t^2-25\partial_x^2=-400\partial_\xi\partial_\eta.
$$

The equation becomes $U_{\xi\eta}=0$. Integrating once in each variable yields

$$
\boxed{U(\xi,\eta)=F(\xi)+G(\eta).}
$$

This is the [countertravelling decomposition of the one-dimensional wave equation](../../../../../../countertravelling-decomposition-of-the-one-dimensional-wave-equation.md), with [wave speed](../../../../../../wave-speed.md) $5/2$. Once differentiability alone is not sufficient for a classical second-order equation; the displayed argument uses the required second [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md).
