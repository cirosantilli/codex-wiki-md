<h1 id="19/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Apply the [ratio test](../../../../../ratio-test.md) to the infinite series whose [partial sums](../../../../../partial-sum.md) are $S_N$. Assuming the terms are eventually nonzero and the limit exists, let

$$
\ell=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\frac{u_{k+1}}{u_k}\right|.
$$

If **$\ell<1$**, the series has [absolute convergence](../../../../../absolute-convergence.md), by eventual comparison with a decaying [geometric series](../../../../../geometric-series.md). If **$\ell>1$**, including an infinite limit, the terms fail to tend to zero and the series diverges. If **$\ell=1$**, the test is inconclusive: both $\sum1/k$ and $\sum1/k^2$ have that ratio limit, but only the second converges. A finite [partial sum](../../../../../partial-sum.md) itself requires no convergence test; the issue is the limit as $N\to\infty$.
