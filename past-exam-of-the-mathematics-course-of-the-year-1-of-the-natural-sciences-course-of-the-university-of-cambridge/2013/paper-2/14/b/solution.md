<h1 id="14/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The common [homogeneous solution](../../../../../homogeneous-solution.md) is $C_1\cos2x+C_2\sin2x$. For forcing $\sin x$, a trial $a\sin x$ gives $3a\sin x=\sin x$, so the first [general solution](../../../../../general-solution.md) is

$$
\boxed{y=C_1\cos2x+C_2\sin2x+\frac13\sin x}.
$$

For forcing $\sin2x$, the frequency produces [resonance in a differential equation](../../../../../resonance-in-a-differential-equation.md). A trial $ax\cos2x$ gives $y_p''+4y_p=-4a\sin2x$, so $a=-1/4$ and the second solution is

$$
\boxed{y=C_1\cos2x+C_2\sin2x-\frac x4\cos2x}.
$$

The [superposition principle](../../../../../superposition-principle.md) permits adding the two particular solutions. The sum-forcing equation therefore has

$$
\boxed{y=C_1\cos2x+C_2\sin2x+\frac13\sin x-\frac x4\cos2x}.
$$

Only one pair of homogeneous constants is needed, because the two original homogeneous families coincide.
