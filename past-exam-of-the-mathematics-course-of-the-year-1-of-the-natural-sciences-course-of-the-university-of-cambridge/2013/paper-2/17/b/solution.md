<h1 id="17/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Along a change in [volume](../../../../../volume.md) at fixed [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), the [first law of thermodynamics](../../../../../first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) gives

$$
\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial V}\right)_T
=T\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial V}\right)_T-P.
$$

Use the Helmholtz [Maxwell relation](../../../../../maxwell-relations.md) derived in (a) to replace the [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md). This proves

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial V}\right)_T
=T\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_V-P}.
$$

For the stated [Van der Waals equation](../../../../../van-der-waals-equation.md), temperature-independent $a,b$ give $P_T|_V=R/(V-b)$. Hence $U_V|_T=a/V^2$. Integrating with respect to $V$ yields

$$
U(V,T)=-\frac aV+h(T).
$$

The dilute-limit condition holds at every fixed $T$ and forces $h(T)=cT$, so

$$
\boxed{U(V,T)=cT-\frac aV}.
$$

The result is the [internal energy of a van der Waals gas](../../../../../internal-energy-of-a-van-der-waals-gas.md) in the fixed-amount convention of the question. Its [volume](../../../../../volume.md) [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) is positive even though the attractive energy correction itself is negative.
