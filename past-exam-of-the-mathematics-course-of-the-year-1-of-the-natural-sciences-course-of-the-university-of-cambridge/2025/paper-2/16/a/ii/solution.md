<h1 id="16/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The relevant [mixed partial derivatives](../../../../../../mixed-partial-derivative.md) agree:

$$
\partial_y(y^2-2x)=2y=\partial_x(2xy-y).
$$

The [vector field](../../../../../../vector-field.md) is [smooth](../../../../../../smooth-function.md) on the entire [simply connected](../../../../../../simply-connected-space.md) [plane](../../../../../../plane.md), so this condition establishes that it is a [conservative vector field](../../../../../../conservative-vector-field.md). We can also construct its [vector-field potential](../../../../../../potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md) explicitly. From $\phi_x=y^2-2x$, take an [antiderivative](../../../../../../antiderivative.md) in $x$:

$$
\phi=xy^2-x^2+h(y).
$$

Then $\phi_y=2xy+h'(y)=2xy-y$ requires $h'(y)=-y$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\phi(x,y)=xy^2-x^2-\frac12y^2+C,\qquad \mathbf F=\nabla\phi}.
$$

The [fundamental theorem for line integrals](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-for-line-integrals.md) checks the previous answer: $\phi(1,1)-\phi(0,0)=-1/2$.
