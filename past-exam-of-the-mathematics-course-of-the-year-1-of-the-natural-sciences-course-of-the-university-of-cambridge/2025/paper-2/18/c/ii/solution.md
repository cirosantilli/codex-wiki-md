<h1 id="18/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $J=A/a$, so $J^2=-I$. The even and odd powers in the [matrix exponential](../../../../../../matrix-exponential.md) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
D
&=I\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^ka^{2k}}{(2k)!}
+J\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^ka^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\\
&=I\cos a+J\sin a.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here the two scalar sums are the [Taylor series](../../../../../../taylor-series.md) for [cosine](../../../../../../cosine.md) and [sine](../../../../../../sine.md). Consequently

$$
\boxed{D=\begin{pmatrix}\cos a&\sin a\\-\sin a&\cos a\end{pmatrix},\qquad \theta=a\pmod{2\pi}}.
$$

With the usual convention that positive angles turn anticlockwise, this [rotation matrix](../../../../../../rotation-matrix.md) rotates by $-a$; the parameter in the form printed in the paper is $\theta=a$.
