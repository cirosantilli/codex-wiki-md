<h1 id="18/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take the [dot product](../../../../../../dot-product.md) of the [orthogonal basis](../../../../../../orthogonal-basis.md) expansion with $\mathbf u_j$. All terms except the $j$th vanish by [orthogonality](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md), leaving

$$
\mathbf u_j\cdot\mathbf x=\alpha_j\,\mathbf u_j\cdot\mathbf u_j.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\alpha_i=\frac{\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf x}{\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf u_i}}.
$$

The [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) is not assumed normalized, so its squared lengths must remain in the denominators. Applying $S$ and using $S\mathbf u_i=\lambda_i\mathbf u_i$ gives

$$
S\mathbf x=\sum_i\lambda_i\alpha_i\mathbf u_i,
\qquad
\boxed{\beta_i=\lambda_i\frac{\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf x}{\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf u_i}}.
$$
