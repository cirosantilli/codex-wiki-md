<h1 id="15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the given [Cartesian coordinates](../../../../../cartesian-coordinate-system.md) and integrate with respect to $y$ first. Since $\cos y\,e^{\sin y}$ is the [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) of $e^{\sin y}$, the [double integral](../../../../../double-integral.md) becomes

$$
I=\int_0^\pi\cos x\left[ e^{\sin y}\right]_{y=0}^{y=x}\,dx
=\int_0^\pi\cos x(e^{\sin x}-1)\,dx.
$$

A direct [antiderivative](../../../../../antiderivative.md) avoids any issue with the nonmonotonicity of $\sin x$ on this interval:

$$
\boxed{I=\left[e^{\sin x}-\sin x\right]_0^\pi=0}.
$$

The sign changes in $\cos x$ make this cancellation possible even though $e^{\sin y}$ is positive.
