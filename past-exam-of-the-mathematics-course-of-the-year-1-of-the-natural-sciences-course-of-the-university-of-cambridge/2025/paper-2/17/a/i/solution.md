<h1 id="17/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md) of the position [vector field](../../../../../../vector-field.md) is $\nabla\cdot\mathbf r=3$. At height $z$, the horizontal cross-section is a [disk](../../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) of [area](../../../../../../surface-area.md) $\pi(1-z^2)$. By the [divergence theorem](../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md), the outward [flux integral](../../../../../../flux-integral.md) is three times the enclosed [volume](../../../../../../volume.md):

$$
\Phi(z_0)=3\pi\int_{-1}^{z_0}(1-z^2)\,dz
=3\pi\left[z-\frac{z^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{z_0}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\Phi(z_0)=\pi(2+3z_0-z_0^3)=\pi(1+z_0)^2(2-z_0)}.
$$

A direct check separates the two pieces of the [surface integral](../../../../../../surface-integral.md). On the unit [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md), the outward [unit normal](../../../../../../unit-normal.md) is $\mathbf r$, so $\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf n=1$ and the curved contribution is $2\pi(z_0+1)$. On the top [disk](../../../../../../disk-mathematics.md), $\mathbf n=\mathbf e_z$, giving $\pi z_0(1-z_0^2)$. Their sum is the same [flux](../../../../../../flux.md).
