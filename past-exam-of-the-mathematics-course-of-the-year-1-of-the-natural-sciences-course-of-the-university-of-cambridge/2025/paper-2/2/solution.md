**No.** Every one of the three [vectors](../../../../vector.md) has equal first and second [Cartesian coordinates](../../../../cartesian-coordinate-system.md), so their [linear span](../../../../linear-span.md) is contained in the [plane](../../../../plane.md) $x=y$. That [linear subspace](../../../../vector-subspace.md) has [dimension](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) two and cannot equal $\mathbb R^3$.

More explicitly, call the three given [vectors](../../../../vector.md) $\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf w$ in their listed order. Then

$$
3\mathbf u+\mathbf v-2\sqrt3\,\mathbf w=0.
$$

This nontrivial [linear relation](../../../../linear-relation.md) proves [linear dependence](../../../../linear-dependence.md), whereas a [basis](../../../../basis.md) must be [linearly independent](../../../../linear-independence.md). Being [unit vectors](../../../../unit-vector.md) alone does not ensure that they form a [basis](../../../../basis.md).
