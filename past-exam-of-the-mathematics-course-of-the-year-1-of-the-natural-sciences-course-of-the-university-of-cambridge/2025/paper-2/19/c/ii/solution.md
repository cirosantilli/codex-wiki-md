<h1 id="19/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [difference quotient](../../../../../../difference-quotient.md) at zero is

$$
\frac{f_a(h)-f_a(0)}h
=\frac{|h|^a}{h}\sin(\pi\sin(1/h)),\qquad h\ne0.
$$

For $a>1$, its [absolute value](../../../../../../absolute-value.md) is at most $|h|^{a-1}$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, set $\delta=\varepsilon^{1/(a-1)}$. Then $0<|h|<\delta$ makes the [difference quotient](../../../../../../difference-quotient.md) have [absolute value](../../../../../../absolute-value.md) below $\varepsilon$. Thus $f_a'(0)=0$.

Conversely, along the second [sequence](../../../../../../sequence.md) the [difference quotient](../../../../../../difference-quotient.md) is exactly zero. Any finite [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) would consequently have to be zero. Along the first positive [sequence](../../../../../../sequence.md), however, the [difference quotient](../../../../../../difference-quotient.md) equals $x_n^{a-1}$. It is one for $a=1$ and tends to $+\infty$ for $a<1$. In both cases it stays outside the $\varepsilon=1/2$ neighbourhood of zero for arbitrarily small positive increments. Thus no finite [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) exists. We obtain

$$
\boxed{f_a\text{ is differentiable at }0\iff a>1,\qquad f_a'(0)=0\text{ when }a>1}.
$$

In particular, $0<a\leq1$ gives [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) without [differentiability](../../../../../../differentiability.md).
