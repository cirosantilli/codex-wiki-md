<h1 id="19/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) $f$ is [differentiable](../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at $x_0$ if there exists a finite real number $L$ such that

$$
\boxed{\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0\ \forall x\in\mathbb R:
\quad 0<|x-x_0|<\delta\ \Longrightarrow\
\left|\frac{f(x)-f(x_0)}{x-x_0}-L\right|<\varepsilon}.
$$

Then $f'(x_0)=L$. The puncture $0<|x-x_0|$ makes the [difference quotient](../../../../../../difference-quotient.md) defined; requiring a finite real $L$ distinguishes an ordinary [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) from an infinite slope.
