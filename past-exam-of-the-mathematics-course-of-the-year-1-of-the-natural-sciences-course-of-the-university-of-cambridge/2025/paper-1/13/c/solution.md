<h1 id="13/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [partial fraction decomposition](../../../../../partial-fraction-decomposition.md) gives

$$
\frac1{x(x^2+1)}=\frac1x-\frac{x}{x^2+1}.
$$

For the second term the [substitution rule](../../../../../integration-by-substitution.md) $t=x^2+1$, $dt=2x\,dx$ gives a [logarithm](../../../../../logarithm.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\int\frac{dx}{x(x^2+1)}=\ln|x|-\frac12\ln(x^2+1)+C,\qquad x\ne0.}
$$

The constant in the [antiderivative](../../../../../antiderivative.md) may be chosen independently on the two [intervals](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) separated by $0$.
