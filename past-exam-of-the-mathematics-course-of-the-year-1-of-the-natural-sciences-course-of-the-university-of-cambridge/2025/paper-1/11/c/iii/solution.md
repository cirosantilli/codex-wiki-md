<h1 id="11/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For the stipulated [linearly independent vectors](../../../../../../linear-independence.md) $\mathbf v_1,\mathbf v_2$, the denominator in part (i) is nonzero. Thus

$$
\boxed{d(L_1,L_2)=0\iff(\mathbf a_2-\mathbf a_1)\cdot(\mathbf v_1\times\mathbf v_2)=0.}
$$

Equivalently, $\mathbf a_2-\mathbf a_1$ belongs to the [linear span](../../../../../../linear-span.md) of $\mathbf v_1,\mathbf v_2$. Geometrically the [lines](../../../../../../straight-line.md) lie in one [plane](../../../../../../plane.md), and, since their direction [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) are not parallel, **they intersect at a unique [point](../../../../../../point-geometry.md)**. The [linear combination](../../../../../../linear-combination.md) expressing $\mathbf a_2-\mathbf a_1$ gives the two parameters of that intersection.
