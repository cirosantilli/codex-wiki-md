<h1 id="20/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For $a=b=1$, the [stationary points](../../../../../stationary-point.md) found in parts (a) and (b) have [temperatures](../../../../../temperature-split.md) $0$, $1$, $1/4$ and $1/5$. The [unit sphere](../../../../../unit-sphere.md) is compact and $T$ is a [continuous function](../../../../../continuous-function.md), so the [extreme value theorem](../../../../../extreme-value-theorem.md) ensures that a hottest [point](../../../../../point-geometry.md) exists; it must be one of those [stationary points](../../../../../stationary-point.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{T_{\max}=1,\qquad (x,y,z)=(1,0,0)\text{ or }(-1,0,0).}
$$

For a direct bound, put $s=x^2\in[0,1]$. The identity $(y^2-z^2)^2\geq0$ gives $y^2z^2\leq(y^2+z^2)^2/4=(1-s)^2/4$, and

$$
T\leq s^2+\frac{(1-s)^2}{4}\leq1,
$$

since $1-s^2-(1-s)^2/4=(1-s)(5s+3)/4\geq0$. Equality forces $s=1$, giving exactly the two [points](../../../../../point-geometry.md) above.
