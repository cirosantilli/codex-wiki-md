<h1 id="19/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Cancellation of the [factorials](../../../../../factorial.md) in the consecutive ratio gives

$$
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=e^{-1}\left(1+\frac1n\right)^{n+1/2},\qquad u_n=\left(n+\frac12\right)\ln\left(1+\frac1n\right)-1.
$$

Set $t=1/n$. The [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) of the [natural logarithm](../../../../../natural-logarithm.md) at $1$ gives $\ln(1+t)/t\to1$. Therefore

$$
u_n=\left(1+\frac t2\right)\frac{\ln(1+t)}t-1\longrightarrow\boxed{0}.
$$

Thus the necessary condition from the [term test for divergence](../../../../../term-test-for-divergence.md) is satisfied. On its own it does not prove that $\sum u_n$ converges.
