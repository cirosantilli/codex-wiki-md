<h1 id="19/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For $k\geq2$, write

$$
c_k=\frac1{k+1}-\frac1{2k}=\frac{k-1}{2k(k+1)},\qquad 0<c_k<\frac12.
$$

Use positivity from part (d) and bound the absolute terms of the [power series](../../../../../power-series.md) in part (c) by a [geometric series](../../../../../geometric-series.md). For $n\geq2$,

$$
0<u_n\leq\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{c_k}{n^k}<\frac12\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac1{n^k}=\frac1{2n^2(1-1/n)}\leq\frac1{n^2}.
$$

For $n=1$, $u_1=\tfrac32\ln2-1<\tfrac12<1$, since $\ln2=\int_1^2dt/t<1$. Thus a bound valid for every positive integer $n$ is

$$
\boxed{0<u_n<An^m,\qquad A=1,\quad m=-2.}
$$
