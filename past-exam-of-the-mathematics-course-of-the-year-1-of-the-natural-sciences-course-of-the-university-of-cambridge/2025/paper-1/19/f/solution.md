<h1 id="19/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

By part (e) and the [comparison test for series](../../../../../comparison-test-for-series.md), $\sum u_n$ converges, since the [P-series](../../../../../p-series.md) $\sum n^{-2}$ converges. Put $S=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_n$. Part (a) now proves

$$
\boxed{a_n\longrightarrow e^{-1+S}\in(0,\infty).}
$$

Part (d) also shows that $(a_n)$ increases towards this finite positive [limit](../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md). This proves [convergence of the Stirling sequence](../../../../../convergence-of-the-stirling-sequence.md) without presupposing its limiting constant.

For identification of that constant, the independently known [Stirling's formula](../../../../../stirling-formula.md) $n!\sim\sqrt{2\pi n}(n/e)^n$ gives

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}a_n=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}.}
$$

The comparison argument above supplies the convergence proof requested by the preceding parts; the [Stirling's formula](../../../../../stirling-formula.md) identification is an additional evaluation.
