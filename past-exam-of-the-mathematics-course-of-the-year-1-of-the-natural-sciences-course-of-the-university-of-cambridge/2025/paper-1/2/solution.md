<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Join the centre of the [unit circle](../../../../complex-unit-circle.md) to adjacent vertices of the [regular polygon](../../../../regular-polygon.md). Each of the $n$ resulting [triangles](../../../../triangle.md) has two sides of [length](../../../../length.md) $1$ and included [angle](../../../../angle.md) $2\pi/n$. Bisecting one gives a [right triangle](../../../../right-triangle.md), so each polygon side, a [chord of a circle](../../../../chord-of-a-circle.md), has [length](../../../../length.md) $2\sin(\pi/n)$. Thus the perimeter is $2n\sin(\pi/n)$.

Each of these [triangles](../../../../triangle.md) has [area](../../../../surface-area.md) $\tfrac12\sin(2\pi/n)$, so the polygon's [area](../../../../surface-area.md) is $\tfrac n2\sin(2\pi/n)$. Comparing the two expressions gives

$$
\boxed{m=2,\qquad k=2.}
$$
