<h1 id="15/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Where $\cos x\ne0$, the [separable differential equation](../../../../../../separable-differential-equation.md) is $y'=y\tan x$. Separating and taking [antiderivatives](../../../../../../antiderivative.md) gives $\ln|y|=-\ln|\cos x|+C$, for nonzero $y$. Including the zero solution, the explicit general solution is

$$
\boxed{y(x)=C\sec x,\qquad C\in\mathbb R,}
$$

on any [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) with $\cos x\ne0$. It can also be obtained without dividing by $y$: the [product rule](../../../../../../product-rule.md) gives $(y\cos x)'=0$.

A nonzero solution cannot extend through a zero of $\cos x$. If a differentiable solution is required on an [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) containing such a zero, the original equation there requires $y=0$, and the only such solution across that zero is $y\equiv0$.
