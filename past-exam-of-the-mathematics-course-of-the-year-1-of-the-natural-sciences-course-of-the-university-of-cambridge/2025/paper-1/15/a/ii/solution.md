<h1 id="15/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $x\ne0$ and $y\ne0$, put $v=y/x$. This is the standard substitution for a [homogeneous first-order differential equation](../../../../../../homogeneous-first-order-differential-equation.md). Since $y'=v+xv'$, the equation becomes

$$
1+v^2-v(v+xv')=0,\qquad vv'=\frac1x.
$$

Taking [antiderivatives](../../../../../../antiderivative.md) yields $v^2=2\ln|x|+C$. The explicit general solution is therefore

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\pm x\sqrt{2\ln|x|+C},}
$$

on any [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) where $x\ne0$ and $2\ln|x|+C>0$, with a fixed choice of sign. For a check that does not divide by $y$, set $w=y^2$ to get $w'-2w/x=2x$ and hence $w=x^2(2\ln|x|+C)$.

There is no missing zero solution: at $y=0$ with $x\ne0$, the original equation would require $x^2=0$. Also no real solution on an [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) about $x=0$ can arise from this family, since $2\ln|x|+C$ becomes negative near $0$.
