<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

Let $p(x)=x^3-5x-1$. Its values at useful points are

$$
p(-3)=-13,\quad p(-2)=1,\quad p(-1)=3,\quad p(0)=-1,\quad p(2)=-3,\quad p(3)=11.
$$

Since a [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) is a [continuous function](../../../../continuous-function.md), the [intermediate value theorem](../../../../intermediate-value-theorem.md) gives one [polynomial root](../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) in each of the disjoint intervals $(-3,-2)$, $(-1,0)$ and $(2,3)$. A nonzero [cubic polynomial](../../../../cubic-polynomial.md) has at most three distinct [polynomial roots](../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md). Hence it has

$$
\boxed{\text{exactly three distinct real roots}.}
$$

These three distinct [polynomial roots](../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) already exhaust its [degree of a polynomial](../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md), so each has [multiplicity of a root](../../../../multiplicity-of-a-root.md) one.
