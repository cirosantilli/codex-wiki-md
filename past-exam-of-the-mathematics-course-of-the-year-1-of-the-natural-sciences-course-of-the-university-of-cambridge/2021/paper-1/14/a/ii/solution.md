<h1 id="14/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Set $dF=0$ in the relative [total differential](../../../../../../total-differential.md) and use the two equal $1\%$ mass increases:

$$
0=0.01+0.01-2\frac{dr}{r}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\boxed{\frac{dr}{r}=0.01.}
$$

Thus the separation must **increase by $1\%$**. In this special case the result is also exact: the [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) product increases by $1.01^2$, and preserving the inverse-square [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) requires $r_{\rm new}^2=1.01^2r^2$, hence $r_{\rm new}=1.01r$.
