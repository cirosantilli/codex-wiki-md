<h1 id="19/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) definition and the specified value $f(0)=0$:

$$
\frac{f(h)-f(0)}h=A\omega|h|^p\frac{\sin(\omega h)}{\omega h}.
$$

The last factor tends to one. Therefore the limit is zero when $p>0$, equals $A\omega$ when $p=0$, and diverges to $+\infty$ when $p<0$. A finite [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) exists exactly when

$$
\boxed{p\ge0,\qquad f'(0)=\begin{cases}0,&p>0,\\A\omega,&p=0.\end{cases}}
$$

In particular $-1<p<0$ gives continuity without differentiability. The threshold is an example of [regularity of a power times a function with a simple zero](../../../../../../regularity-of-a-power-times-a-function-with-a-simple-zero.md).
