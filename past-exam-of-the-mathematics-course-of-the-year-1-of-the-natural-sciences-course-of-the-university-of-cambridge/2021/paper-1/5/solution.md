<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

For $f(x)=xe^{-2x^2}$, the [product rule](../../../../product-rule.md) and [chain rule](../../../../chain-rule.md) give

$$
f'(x)=e^{-2x^2}(1-4x^2).
$$

The positive exponential factor makes $f$ increasing for $0<x<1/2$ and decreasing for $1/2<x<1$. Its only interior [stationary point](../../../../stationary-point.md) is therefore a maximum at $x=1/2$. Checking it together with both endpoints,

$$
f(0)=0,\qquad f(1/2)=\frac12e^{-1/2},\qquad f(1)=e^{-2}.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\min_{[0,1]}f=0\text{ at }x=0,\qquad\max_{[0,1]}f=\frac1{2\sqrt e}\text{ at }x=\frac12.}
$$

Checking the endpoints is necessary for extrema on a [closed interval](../../../../closed-real-interval.md).
