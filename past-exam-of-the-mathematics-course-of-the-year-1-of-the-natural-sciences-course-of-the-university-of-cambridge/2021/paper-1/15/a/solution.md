<h1 id="15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For $f$ sufficiently differentiable on the interval between $a$ and $x$, [Taylor's theorem](../../../../../taylor-s-theorem.md) with its first $n$ terms reads

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k+R_n(x),\qquad R_n(x)=\frac{f^{(n)}(\xi)}{n!}(x-a)^n,}
$$

where $\xi$ lies between $a$ and $x$. This is the [Taylor theorem with Lagrange remainder](../../../../../taylor-theorem-with-lagrange-remainder.md). For example, $|f^{(n)}|\le M$ on that interval gives $|R_n(x)|\le M|x-a|^n/n!$. The finite formula does not assume that an infinite [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) converges to the function; that further conclusion requires the remainders to tend to zero.
