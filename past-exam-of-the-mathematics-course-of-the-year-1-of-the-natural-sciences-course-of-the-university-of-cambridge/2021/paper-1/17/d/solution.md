<h1 id="17/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let $H(u)=\sin^2u+\ln u$. The [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives $H'(u)=\sin2u+1/u$, so the two given summands combine into its [logarithmic derivative](../../../../../logarithmic-derivative.md), $f(u)=H'(u)/H(u)$. For $u>1$, $H(u)>0$, so the real logarithm is defined throughout the integration path. Since $H(\pi)=\ln\pi$,

$$
\boxed{F(x)=\ln\left(\frac{\sin^2x+\ln x}{\ln\pi}\right),\qquad x>1.}
$$

This satisfies $F(\pi)=0$, and [differentiation](../../../../../differentiation.md) recovers both terms of the integrand.
