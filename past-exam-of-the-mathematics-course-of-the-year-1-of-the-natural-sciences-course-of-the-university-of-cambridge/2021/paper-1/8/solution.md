<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

Use [integration by substitution](../../../../integration-by-substitution.md) with $u=x^4$, so $du=4x^3\,dx$. Then

$$
\boxed{\int x^3e^{-x^4}\,dx=\frac14\int e^{-u}\,du=-\frac14e^{-x^4}+C.}
$$

Differentiating the answer by the [chain rule](../../../../chain-rule.md) gives $x^3e^{-x^4}$, confirming the [antiderivative](../../../../antiderivative.md). The real [constant of integration](../../../../constant-of-integration.md) $C$ is arbitrary.
