<h1 id="18/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Among the two [orthogonal matrices](../../../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) from part (d)(i), $d=-1$ gives $L=-I$, a [rotation matrix](../../../../../../rotation-matrix.md) through angle $\pi$. For $d=1$, the [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) is $-1$, so the matrix reverses orientation and is a reflection rather than a rotation:

$$
\boxed{b=c=0,\qquad d=1,\qquad L=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

It is the [reflection matrix](../../../../../../reflection-matrix.md) across the $y$ axis.
