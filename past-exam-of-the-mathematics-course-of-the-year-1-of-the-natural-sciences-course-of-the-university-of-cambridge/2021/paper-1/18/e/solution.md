<h1 id="18/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For any real [square matrix](../../../../../square-matrix.md) $N$, the [symmetric and antisymmetric parts of a matrix](../../../../../symmetric-and-antisymmetric-parts-of-a-matrix.md) give $N=(N+N^T)/2+(N-N^T)/2$, whose summands are respectively a [symmetric matrix](../../../../../symmetric-matrix.md) and an [antisymmetric matrix](../../../../../skew-symmetric-matrix.md). Here it gives

$$
\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&3\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a&(b+c)/2\\(b+c)/2&3\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&(b-c)/2\\(c-b)/2&0\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Transposing the first summand leaves it unchanged; transposing the second changes its sign. Entrywise addition recovers the original [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md). The decomposition is unique because a matrix that is both symmetric and antisymmetric must be zero.
