<h1 id="11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) of the direction [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) is $\boldsymbol n_1\times\boldsymbol n_2=(1,-1,0)$. It is nonzero, so a common perpendicular direction exists. Projecting $\boldsymbol a_2-\boldsymbol a_1=(-1,0,1)$ onto its unit [vector](../../../../../vector.md) gives the [distance between skew lines](../../../../../distance-between-skew-lines.md):

$$
s=\frac{|(\boldsymbol a_2-\boldsymbol a_1)\cdot(\boldsymbol n_1\times\boldsymbol n_2)|}
{\|\boldsymbol n_1\times\boldsymbol n_2\|}
=\frac{|-1|}{\sqrt2}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{s=\frac1{\sqrt2}.}
$$

The numerator is a [scalar triple product](../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md); movement along either line does not change this perpendicular projection.
