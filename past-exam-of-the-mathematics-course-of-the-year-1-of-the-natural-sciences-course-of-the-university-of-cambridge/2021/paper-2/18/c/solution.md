<h1 id="18/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Set $x=0$ in the convergent [Fourier series](../../../../../fourier-series-split.md) from part (b). Since $f(0)=0$ and every cosine equals one,

$$
0=\frac L2-\frac{4L}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{(2k+1)^2}.
$$

Rearranging gives

$$
\boxed{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{(2k+1)^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{8}.}
$$

Convergence at the evaluation point is essential when extracting a numerical series identity from a [Fourier series](../../../../../fourier-series-split.md). Here the function is continuous there and the coefficients are absolutely summable.
