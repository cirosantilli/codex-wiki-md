<h1 id="12/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At a differentiable interior [stationary point](../../../../../stationary-point.md), all first-order changes of the scalar field vanish. In particular, the [directional derivative](../../../../../directional-derivative.md) along each coordinate axis vanishes, so $f_x=f_y=0$ and hence $\nabla f=\boldsymbol0$. Equivalently, the first-order differential $df=\nabla f\cdot d\boldsymbol r$ vanishes for every displacement. A differentiable interior maximum or minimum must satisfy this condition; a stationary point need not be an extremum.

The standard nondegenerate local [contour plot](../../../../../contour-plot.md) models are $-x^2-y^2$ for a maximum, $x^2+y^2$ for a minimum, and $x^2-y^2$ for a [saddle point of a scalar function](../../../../../saddle-point-of-a-scalar-function.md). Near a maximum, nested contours have lower values farther from the centre; near a minimum they have higher values. Near a saddle, [hyperbolas](../../../../../hyperbola.md) occur on opposite sides of the critical level, whose contour has two intersecting tangent directions. More general nondegenerate quadratic forms deform these circles into [ellipses](../../../../../ellipse.md); degenerate stationary points can have more complicated contours.
