<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

Substitute $r=-x^2$ into the [geometric series](../../../../geometric-series.md), without taking any [derivatives](../../../../derivative.md):

$$
\boxed{\frac1{1+x^2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^k x^{2k}
=1-x^2+x^4-\cdots,\qquad |x|<1.}
$$

Thus the coefficient of $x^n$ in the [Taylor series](../../../../taylor-series.md) is $(-1)^{n/2}$ when $n$ is even and zero when $n$ is odd. If nonzero terms are numbered from one, the $n$th term is $(-1)^{n-1}x^{2n-2}$.
