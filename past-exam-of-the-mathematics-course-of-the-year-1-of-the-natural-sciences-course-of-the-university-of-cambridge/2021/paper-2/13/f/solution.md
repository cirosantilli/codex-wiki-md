<h1 id="13/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

For $a=0$, differentiate the proposed [scalar potential](../../../../../scalar-potential.md) directly:

$$
\nabla\left(-\frac12e^{-(x^2+y^2+z^2)}\right)
=e^{-r^2}(x,y,z).
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\Phi(\boldsymbol r)=-\frac12e^{-r^2}+C\quad\text{when }a=0.}
$$

For $a\ne0$, the [curl](../../../../../curl.md) is the nonzero [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $(0,0,2a)$. A [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) of a twice differentiable scalar field has zero [curl](../../../../../curl.md), so no such scalar field exists. Equivalently the closed [line integral](../../../../../line-integral.md) in part (d) is nonzero, contradicting the path independence of a [conservative vector field](../../../../../conservative-vector-field.md). The field is smooth on all of $\mathbb R^3$, so no excluded singular set complicates this conclusion.
