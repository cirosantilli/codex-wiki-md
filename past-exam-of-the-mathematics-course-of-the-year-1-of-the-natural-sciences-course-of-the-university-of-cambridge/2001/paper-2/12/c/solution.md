<h1 id="12/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The summands $u_n=n^4/2^n$ are positive. Apply the [ratio test](../../../../../ratio-test.md):

$$
\frac{u_{n+1}}{u_n}=\frac{(n+1)^4}{2n^4}=\frac12\left(1+\frac1n\right)^4\longrightarrow\frac12<1.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^4}{2^n}\text{ converges absolutely}.}
$$

For a direct comparison interpretation, the ratio is eventually at most any fixed number between $1/2$ and $1$, such as $3/4$. The tail is then bounded by a convergent [geometric series](../../../../../geometric-series.md). Thus the exponential denominator wins over the fourth-power numerator strongly enough to make the whole [series](../../../../../series-mathematics.md) summable.
