<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Separate the variables in this [first-order ordinary differential equation](../../../../../first-order-ordinary-differential-equation.md):

$$
\frac{y}{y^2+2}\,dy=\frac{x}{x^2-3}\,dx.
$$

Integration gives $\tfrac12\ln(y^2+2)=\tfrac12\ln|x^2-3|+C$, so $y^2+2=K|x^2-3|$. On the interval containing the prescribed point $x=3$, we have $x^2-3>0$. The [initial condition](../../../../../initial-condition.md) $y(3)=4$ therefore gives $18=6K$, hence $K=3$. Choosing the positive [square root](../../../../../square-root.md) to match that [initial condition](../../../../../initial-condition.md) yields

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\sqrt{3x^2-11}.}
$$

The maximal interval containing $3$ on which this solves the explicit [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) is $\boxed{x>\sqrt{11/3}}$. At the left endpoint $y=0$, the original denominator vanishes and the [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) becomes unbounded. Directly, $y'=3x/y$ and $x(2+y^2)/[y(x^2-3)]=3x/y$, verifying the equation throughout this interval.
