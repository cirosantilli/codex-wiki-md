<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the [spherical polar coordinates](../../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) [volume element](../../../../../volume-element.md) $dV=r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi$. Since $1+\cos\theta\geq0$ throughout $0\leq\theta\leq\pi$, the solid has radial range $0\leq r\leq1+\cos\theta$ and full azimuthal range $0\leq\phi<2\pi$. Its [volume](../../../../../volume.md) is

$$
V=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\pi\int_0^{1+\cos\theta}r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi=\frac{2\pi}3\int_0^\pi(1+\cos\theta)^3\sin\theta\,d\theta.
$$

Put $u=1+\cos\theta$, so $du=-\sin\theta\,d\theta$ and the endpoints become $2$ and $0$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{V=\frac{2\pi}3\int_0^2u^3\,du=\frac{8\pi}3.}
$$

The polar equation describes the entire three-dimensional surface, so the [spherical polar coordinates](../../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) [volume element](../../../../../volume-element.md), rather than a two-dimensional polar area element, is required.
