<h1 id="11/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The number $ie^{i\phi}$ has [modulus](../../../../../../modulus.md) one and [argument](../../../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) $\phi+\pi/2$ modulo $2\pi$. Therefore its [complex logarithm](../../../../../../complex-logarithm.md) has values

$$
\boxed{\ln(ie^{i\phi})=i(\phi+\pi/2+2\pi k),\quad k\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

Every value has [real part](../../../../../../real-part.md) zero. For the [principal complex logarithm](../../../../../../principal-complex-logarithm.md), choose the unique integer giving imaginary part in $(-\pi,\pi]$. The unwrapped value $\phi+\pi/2$ is the principal imaginary part only when it already lies in that interval.
