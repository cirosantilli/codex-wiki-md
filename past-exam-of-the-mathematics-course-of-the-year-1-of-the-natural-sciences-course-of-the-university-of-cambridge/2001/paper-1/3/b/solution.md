<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Adding and subtracting the two coordinates shows that $A(1,1)^T=(c+s)(1,1)^T$ and $A(1,-1)^T=(c-s)(1,-1)^T$. The normalized [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) can therefore be chosen as

$$
\boxed{v_+=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)^T,\qquad v_-=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1)^T.}
$$

Their [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) is $(1-1)/2=0$, and their squared [norms](../../../../../norm.md) are one, proving [orthonormality](../../../../../orthonormal-set.md). If $s=0$, every nonzero [vector](../../../../../vector.md) is an [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md); this chosen [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) still works.
