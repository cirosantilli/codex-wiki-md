↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [fundamental theorem of calculus](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) differentiates the upper endpoint, while [differentiation under the integral sign](../../../../../differentiation-under-the-integral-sign.md) differentiates the parameter:

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{\partial G}{\partial\theta}\right)_k=g(\theta,k),\qquad \left(\frac{\partial G}{\partial k}\right)_\theta=\int_0^\theta\frac{\partial g}{\partial k}(x,k)dx.}
$$

These formulas hold, for example, when $g$ and its displayed parameter [partial derivative](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) are continuous on a neighborhood of the integration interval and the parameter value.
