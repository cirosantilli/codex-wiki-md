<h1 id="9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9](../9.md)

For an $N+1$ times continuously differentiable [function](../../../../function-split.md), [Taylor's theorem](../../../../taylor-s-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^N\frac{f^{(n)}(x_0)}{n!}(x-x_0)^n+\frac{f^{(N+1)}(\xi)}{(N+1)!}(x-x_0)^{N+1},}
$$

where $\xi$ lies between $x_0$ and $x$. An infinite [Taylor series](../../../../taylor-series.md) represents the function when its remainder tends to zero; this holds locally for an [analytic function](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md), rather than for every smooth function automatically.
