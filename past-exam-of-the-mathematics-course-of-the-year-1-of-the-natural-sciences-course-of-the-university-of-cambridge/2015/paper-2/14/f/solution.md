<h1 id="14/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Under the same independence assumption, [inclusion-exclusion](../../../../../inclusion-exclusion-principle.md) gives

$$
\boxed{R(t)=P_1(t)+P_2(t)-P(t)=\begin{cases}
0,&t\leq0,\\s+s^2-s^3,&0<t<T,\\1,&t\geq T.
\end{cases}}
$$

On $0<t<T$,

$$
R'(t)=\frac{1+2s-3s^2}{T}=\frac{(1-s)(3s+1)}T>0,
\qquad R''(t)=\frac{2-6s}{T^2}.
$$

There is one interior [inflection point](../../../../../inflection-point.md), where the [curvature](../../../../../curvature.md) changes from positive to negative:

$$
\boxed{\left(t,R(t)\right)=\left(\frac T3,\frac{11}{27}\right)}.
$$

At $t=T$ the graph reaches one with zero slope and then stays constant. There is no further change from one sign of [curvature](../../../../../curvature.md) to the opposite sign there.

Without independence, $R=P_1+P_2-P$ still holds, but only $\boxed{s\leq R(t)\leq\min(1,s+s^2)}$ is forced by the marginals. Its inflection behaviour is then not determined. The shared-$U$ construction in (e), for example, gives $R=s$ before the plateau.
