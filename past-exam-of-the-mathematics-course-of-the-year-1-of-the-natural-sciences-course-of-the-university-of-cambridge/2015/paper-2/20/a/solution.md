<h1 id="20/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In a steady state, $\nu u_s''=-G\sin(n\pi x)$. Since the second [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) of the [sine](../../../../../sine.md) is $-(n\pi)^2$ times itself,

$$
u_s(x)=\frac{G}{\nu n^2\pi^2}\sin(n\pi x)+Ax+B.
$$

The zero boundary values at $x=0,1$ force $A=B=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{u_s(x)=\frac{G}{\nu n^2\pi^2}\sin(n\pi x)}.
$$
