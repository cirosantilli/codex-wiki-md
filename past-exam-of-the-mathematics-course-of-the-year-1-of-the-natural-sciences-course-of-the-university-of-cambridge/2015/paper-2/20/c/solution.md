<h1 id="20/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Separate $\widetilde u=f(t)g(x)$. On a nonzero product region,

$$
\frac{f'}{\nu f}=\frac{g''}{g}=-\lambda.
$$

A decaying temporal factor requires $\lambda>0$, with $f=Ce^{-\nu\lambda t}$. The zero spatial boundary values give $g=A\sin(\sqrt\lambda x)$ and $\sin\sqrt\lambda=0$, hence $\lambda=j^2\pi^2$ with $j=1,2,\ldots$. Zero or negative separation parameters give no nonzero spatial mode with both boundary values zero.

The initial profile is already exactly the $j=n$ [sine](../../../../../sine.md) [eigenfunction](../../../../../eigenfunction.md). Its coefficient therefore determines a single separated solution:

$$
\boxed{\widetilde u(x,t)=-\frac{G}{\nu n^2\pi^2}
e^{-\nu n^2\pi^2t}\sin(n\pi x)}.
$$

The exponential tends to zero because $\nu>0$. More generally [sine](../../../../../sine.md) [orthogonality](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) would determine a sum of such modes, but all other coefficients vanish here. This is the transient of the [heat equation with a single stationary sine forcing](../../../../../heat-equation-with-a-single-stationary-sine-forcing.md).
