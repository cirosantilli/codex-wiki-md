<h1 id="19/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At a regular constrained [stationary point](../../../../../stationary-point.md), the change in $f$ along every allowed tangent direction must vanish. Thus its [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) is normal to the [constraint](../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md) curve, giving the [Lagrange multiplier](../../../../../lagrange-multiplier.md) equations

$$
\boxed{\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g,\qquad g=c},
$$

provided $\nabla g\ne0$. Solve these equations for $x,y,\lambda$, then classify the candidates using the [constraint](../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md) or a constrained second-derivative test. Points where the [constraint](../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md) [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) vanishes require separate analysis.

For $n$ variables and $r$ regular independent constraints $g_j=c_j$, use one multiplier per [constraint](../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md):

$$
\boxed{\nabla f=\sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_j\nabla g_j,\qquad g_j=c_j}.
$$

There are $n+r$ scalar equations in the $n+r$ unknowns. Independence of the [constraint](../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md) gradients is the regularity condition; boundary points must be treated separately when present.
