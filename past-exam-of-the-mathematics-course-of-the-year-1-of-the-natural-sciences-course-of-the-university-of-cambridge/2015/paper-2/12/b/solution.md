<h1 id="12/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the same axes as in (a) and choose the outward orientation. On the curved [spherical lune](../../../../../spherical-lune.md), $\mathbf n=\mathbf e_r$ and $dA=a^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi$. Its [vector area](../../../../../vector-area.md) is

$$
\mathbf S=a^2\int_{-\beta/2}^{\beta/2}\int_0^\pi
(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\cos\theta)
\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi.
$$

The $y$ and $z$ components cancel. The $x$ component is $a^2(\pi/2)\,2\sin(\beta/2)$, hence

$$
\boxed{\mathbf S=\pi a^2\sin\frac\beta2\,\mathbf e_x}.
$$

Here $\mathbf e_x$ points along the azimuthal bisector; another coordinate choice rotates this vector accordingly. Its magnitude is the signed projected [area](../../../../../surface-area.md), not the scalar curved [area](../../../../../surface-area.md) $2\beta a^2$. Reversing the orientation reverses its sign.
