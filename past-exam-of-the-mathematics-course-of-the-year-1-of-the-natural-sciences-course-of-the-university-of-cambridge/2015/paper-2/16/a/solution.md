<h1 id="16/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a continuously differentiable [differential one-form](../../../../../one-form.md), exactness requires $\boxed{P_y=Q_x}$. It is locally sufficient, and globally sufficient on a [simply connected domain](../../../../../simply-connected-domain.md); on a domain with holes, closedness alone need not imply exactness.

An [integrating factor for a differential one-form](../../../../../integrating-factor-for-a-differential-one-form.md) depending only on $x$ must satisfy

$$
\partial_y(\mu P)=\partial_x(\mu Q),\qquad
\mu P_y=\mu'Q+\mu Q_x.
$$

Thus, where $Q\ne0$,

$$
\boxed{\frac{\mu'}\mu=\frac{P_y-Q_x}{Q},\qquad
\mu(x)=\exp\int\frac{P_y-Q_x}{Q}\,dx}.
$$

If the ratio depends only on $x$, this defines a nonzero factor and makes it a [closed differential form](../../../../../closed-differential-form.md), hence locally exact. For a factor depending only on $y$, the analogous calculation gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{\mu'}\mu=\frac{Q_x-P_y}{P},\qquad
\mu(y)=\exp\int\frac{Q_x-P_y}{P}\,dy},
$$

provided the ratio is a [function](../../../../../function-split.md) only of $y$ on the region where $P\ne0$. A nonzero constant multiple of either factor works equally well.
