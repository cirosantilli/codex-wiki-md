<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The usual interior condition for the [binomial series](../../../../../binomial-series.md) is $|3x|<1$, giving **$-1/3<x<1/3$** as its open interval of convergence. In this particular square-root case the full series also converges absolutely at both endpoints, so the maximal real range on which its sum equals the original [function](../../../../../function-split.md) is

$$
\boxed{-\frac13\leq x\leq\frac13.}
$$

For an endpoint check, write $\sqrt{1-u}=1-\sum_{n\geq1}b_nu^n$, where $b_n=a_{n-1}-a_n>0$ and $a_n=\binom{2n}{n}/4^n\to0$. Then $\sum b_n=1$ by telescoping, establishing [absolute convergence](../../../../../absolute-convergence.md) at $u=\pm1$. The [square-root binomial series at its endpoints](../../../../../square-root-binomial-series-at-its-endpoints.md) proves [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) of the sum there. The endpoint values are $\sqrt2$ at $x=-1/3$ and $0$ at $x=1/3$. Outside this range the power-series terms do not tend to zero. A three-term truncation remains an approximation, rather than an exact formula throughout the interval.
