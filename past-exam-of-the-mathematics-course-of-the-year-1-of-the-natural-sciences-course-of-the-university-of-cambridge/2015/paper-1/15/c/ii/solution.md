<h1 id="15/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Both numerator and denominator vanish quadratically. Factor out $x^2$ before dividing the [power series](../../../../../../power-series.md):

$$
\frac{x\sin x}{\ln(1+x^2)}
=\frac{1-x^2/6+x^4/120+O(x^6)}{1-x^2/2+x^4/3+O(x^6)}.
$$

The reciprocal of the denominator is $1+x^2/2-x^4/12+O(x^6)$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\frac{x\sin x}{\ln(1+x^2)}
=1+\frac{x^2}{3}-\frac{19x^4}{120}+O(x^6).}
$$

The expansion describes the continuous extension at the [removable singularity](../../../../../../removable-singularity.md) $x=0$, where the original quotient is undefined but its limit is one. Odd powers vanish because the quotient is even.
