<h1 id="15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For $n\geq3$, [Taylor's theorem](../../../../../taylor-s-theorem.md) after $n$ terms, counting the constant term, is

$$
f(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)+\frac{f''(a)}{2!}(x-a)^2
+\cdots+\frac{f^{(n-1)}(a)}{(n-1)!}(x-a)^{n-1}+R_n(x).
$$

Under the usual hypothesis that $f$ is $n-1$ times continuously differentiable on the interval joining $a$ and $x$, and $n$ times differentiable in its interior, the [Taylor theorem with Lagrange remainder](../../../../../taylor-theorem-with-lagrange-remainder.md) gives

$$
\boxed{R_n(x)=\frac{f^{(n)}(\xi)}{n!}(x-a)^n,
\qquad \xi\text{ strictly between }a\text{ and }x.}
$$

This is a finite expansion with a controlled remainder; it does not assume an infinite [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) converges. For $x=a$ the remainder is zero.
