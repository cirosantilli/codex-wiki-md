<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

For the [indefinite integral](../../../../antiderivative.md), substitute $u=x^2$, so $x^3dx=u\,du/2$. An [integration by parts](../../../../integration-by-parts.md) then gives

$$
\int x^3\cos(x^2)dx=\frac12\int u\cos u\,du
=\boxed{\frac12\big[x^2\sin(x^2)+\cos(x^2)\big]+C.}
$$

For the [definite integral](../../../../definite-integral.md), the original PDF's upper limit is $\sqrt{\pi/2}$, so the corresponding upper value of $u$ is $\pi/2$. Therefore

$$
\int_0^{\sqrt{\pi/2}}x^3\cos(x^2)dx
=\frac12\left[\frac\pi2\sin\frac\pi2+\cos\frac\pi2-1\right]
=\boxed{\frac{\pi-2}{4}.}
$$

Both requested integrals are thus evaluated with the same [antiderivative](../../../../antiderivative.md).
