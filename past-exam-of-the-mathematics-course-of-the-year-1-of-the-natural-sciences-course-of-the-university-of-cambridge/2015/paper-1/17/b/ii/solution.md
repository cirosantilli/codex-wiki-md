<h1 id="17/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Complete the square in the denominator and split the numerator into a multiple of its [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) plus a constant:

$$
x^2+4x+8=(x+2)^2+4,\qquad x+1=\frac12(2x+4)-1.
$$

Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\frac{x+1}{x^2+4x+8}dx
&=\frac12\int\frac{2x+4}{x^2+4x+8}dx
-\int\frac{dx}{(x+2)^2+4}\\
&=\boxed{\frac12\ln(x^2+4x+8)
-\frac12\arctan\frac{x+2}{2}+C.}
\end{aligned}
$$

The [natural logarithm](../../../../../../natural-logarithm.md) needs no absolute-value correction because its quadratic argument is strictly positive for every real $x$. The second term is an [inverse tangent](../../../../../../inverse-tangent.md) [antiderivative](../../../../../../antiderivative.md).
