<h1 id="11/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The perpendicular [unit vectors](../../../../../../unit-vector.md) represented by $e^{i\theta}$ and $ie^{i\theta}$ point radially outwards and transversely in the increasing-angle direction. Reading off their real coefficients in part (b) gives the signed components

$$
\boxed{v_r=\dot r,\qquad v_\theta=r\dot\theta,\qquad
a_r=\ddot r-r\dot\theta^2,\qquad
a_\theta=2\dot r\dot\theta+r\ddot\theta.}
$$

These are [velocity in polar coordinates](../../../../../../velocity-in-polar-coordinates.md) and [acceleration in polar coordinates](../../../../../../acceleration-in-polar-coordinates.md). In particular $r\dot\theta$ is a transverse [speed](../../../../../../speed.md) component; $\dot\theta$ alone is an [angular velocity](../../../../../../angular-velocity.md). The moving [basis](../../../../../../basis.md), rather than any extra force assumption, accounts for the additional [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) terms. At the origin the polar directions themselves are undefined, although the Cartesian [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) and [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) can remain well defined.
