<h1 id="18/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

Use the common [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $A$ and $A^{\mathsf T}$. For each $k$,

$$
S\mathbf v_k=\frac12(\lambda_k+\lambda_k^{-1})\mathbf v_k
=\cos\frac{2\pi k}{3}\,\mathbf v_k.
$$

Thus, without constructing $S$ explicitly, its [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are

$$
\boxed{1,\ -\frac12,\ -\frac12.}
$$

The [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $1$ has direction $(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}$. The repeated [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $-1/2$ has a real [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) consisting of [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) whose coordinates sum to zero. This is the general rule for the [symmetric part of an orthogonal matrix](../../../../../symmetric-part-of-an-orthogonal-matrix.md). Adding unrelated lists of [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) would not suffice; here the shared [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) justify the pairing.
