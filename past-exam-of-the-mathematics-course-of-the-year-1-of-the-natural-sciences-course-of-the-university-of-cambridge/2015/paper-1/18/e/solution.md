<h1 id="18/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Since $A^{\mathsf T}=A^{-1}$, each [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) $\mathbf v_k$ from part (d) remains an [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md), with reciprocal [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md):

$$
\boxed{\mu_k=\lambda_k^{-1}=e^{-2\pi ik/3},\qquad
A^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v_k=\mu_k\mathbf v_k.}
$$

The set of [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is again $\{1,e^{2\pi i/3},e^{4\pi i/3}\}$, with the two nonreal values exchanged relative to the same [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md). Equivalently, for $\mu=e^{2\pi ik/3}$ one can choose the [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) $(1,\mu,\mu^2)^{\mathsf T}$. Pairing each [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) with its [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) matters in the next part.
