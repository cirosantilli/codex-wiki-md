<h1 id="18/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For an [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) $\mathbf v=(v_1,v_2,v_3)^{\mathsf T}$, the equation $A\mathbf v=\lambda\mathbf v$ reads

$$
v_3=\lambda v_1,\qquad v_1=\lambda v_2,\qquad v_2=\lambda v_3.
$$

A nonzero solution requires $\lambda^3=1$. Write the three [roots of unity](../../../../../root-of-unity.md) in the requested [polar form of a complex number](../../../../../polar-form-of-a-complex-number.md):

$$
\boxed{\lambda_k=e^{2\pi ik/3},\qquad
\mathbf v_k=\begin{pmatrix}1\\e^{4\pi ik/3}\\e^{2\pi ik/3}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad k=0,1,2.}
$$

Indeed $\mathbf v_k=(1,\lambda_k^2,\lambda_k)^{\mathsf T}$ satisfies all three equations. These [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) are unnormalized; any nonzero scalar multiples are equally valid.
