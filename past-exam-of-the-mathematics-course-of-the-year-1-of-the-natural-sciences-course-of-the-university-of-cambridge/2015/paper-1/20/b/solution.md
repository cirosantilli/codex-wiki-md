<h1 id="20/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a sufficiently smooth [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md) on a volume $V$ and its closed boundary $S$, the [divergence theorem](../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) states

$$
\boxed{\int_S\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf n\,dS
=\int_V\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\mathbf v\,dV,}
$$

with the [unit normal](../../../../../unit-normal.md) directed outwards. Here

$$
\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\mathbf v
=\partial_x(-\alpha yz)+\partial_y(\alpha xz)+\partial_z0=0.
$$

Hence **the net volume [flux](../../../../../flux.md) through every [closed surface](../../../../../closed-surface.md) in the liquid is zero**. This verifies [incompressible flow](../../../../../incompressible-flow.md) directly. It does not require the [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) to vanish pointwise on an arbitrary [closed surface](../../../../../closed-surface.md); inward and outward contributions can cancel.
