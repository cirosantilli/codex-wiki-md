<h1 id="20/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Separate the closed cylinder into its curved side and its two end disks, all with outward [normal vectors](../../../../../normal-vector.md). On the side, $\rho=R$ and $\mathbf n=\widehat{\boldsymbol\rho}$, so

$$
\Phi_{\rm side}=(-\alpha R)(2\pi Rh)=-2\pi\alpha R^2h.
$$

At the top, $z=h/2$ and $\mathbf n=\mathbf k$, giving $(\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf v)\cdot\mathbf n=\alpha h$. At the bottom, both $z$ and the axial [normal vector](../../../../../normal-vector.md) change sign, so the same positive value $\alpha h$ results. Each disk therefore contributes $\pi\alpha R^2h$. Adding all three [surface integrals](../../../../../surface-integral.md),

$$
\boxed{\int_S(\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf v)\cdot d\mathbf S
=-2\pi\alpha R^2h+\pi\alpha R^2h+\pi\alpha R^2h=0.}
$$

This explicit cancellation is consistent with the fact that the [divergence of a curl is zero](../../../../../divergence-of-a-curl-is-zero.md). The two disk contributions add rather than cancel.
