<h1 id="16/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Any two distinct draw positions have the same pair law by [exchangeability](../../../../../../exchangeable-random-variables.md). The [marginal and pair probabilities in sampling without replacement](../../../../../../marginal-and-pair-probabilities-in-sampling-without-replacement.md) therefore give

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(B_2\cap B_3)=\frac{4\cdot3}{7\cdot6}=\frac27.}
$$

Divide this joint [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) by the relevant marginal [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) to get the two [conditional probabilities](../../../../../../conditional-probability.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(B_2\mid B_3)=\frac{2/7}{4/7}=\frac12,\qquad
\mathbb P(B_3\mid B_2)=\frac{2/7}{4/7}=\frac12.}
$$

Conditioning on the third draw can change the [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) of the second draw; [conditional probability](../../../../../../conditional-probability.md) does not require the conditioning event to occur earlier in time.
