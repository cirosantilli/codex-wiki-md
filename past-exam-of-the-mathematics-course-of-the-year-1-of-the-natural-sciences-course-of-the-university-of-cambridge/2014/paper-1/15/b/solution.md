<h1 id="15/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Translate the variables by $u=x-x_0$, $v=y-y_0$, $w=z-z_0$. The numerator becomes $u+x_0$. The part odd in $u$ integrates to zero, while the constant part factors into three [Gaussian integrals](../../../../../gaussian-integral.md). Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
I&=x_0\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-u^2/a^2}du\right)^3\\
&=x_0(a\sqrt\pi)^3
=\boxed{\pi^{3/2}a^3x_0.}
\end{aligned}
$$

The absolute integrability of a [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) times a Gaussian justifies this separation by [Fubini's theorem](../../../../../fubini-s-theorem.md). Neither $y_0$ nor $z_0$ affects the result; when $x_0=0$, odd symmetry makes the integral zero.
