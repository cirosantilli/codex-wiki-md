<h1 id="14/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The nonparallel direction [unit vectors](../../../../../unit-vector.md) have nonzero [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md). Let

$$
\mathbf n=\frac{\widehat{\mathbf b}\times\widehat{\mathbf d}}
{|\widehat{\mathbf b}\times\widehat{\mathbf d}|}.
$$

For any joining [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $\mathbf s=\mathbf c-\mathbf a+\mu\widehat{\mathbf d}-\lambda\widehat{\mathbf b}$, its normal component is fixed:

$$
\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf n=(\mathbf c-\mathbf a)\cdot\mathbf n.
$$

Thus its length is at least the absolute value of that component. Since the two line directions span $\mathbf n^\perp$, suitable $\lambda,\mu$ cancel the entire component in that [plane](../../../../../plane.md), attaining the bound. Therefore the [distance between skew lines](../../../../../distance-between-skew-lines.md), including the zero-distance intersecting case, is

$$
\boxed{d_{\min}=\frac{|(\mathbf c-\mathbf a)\cdot
(\widehat{\mathbf b}\times\widehat{\mathbf d})|}
{|\widehat{\mathbf b}\times\widehat{\mathbf d}|}.}
$$

The numerator is a [scalar triple product](../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md). The nonparallel hypothesis is essential to the denominator and to the spanning argument.
