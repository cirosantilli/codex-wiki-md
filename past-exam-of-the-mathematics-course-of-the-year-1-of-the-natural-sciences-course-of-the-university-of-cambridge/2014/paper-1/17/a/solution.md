<h1 id="17/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

With the usual real normalization, the [Fourier series](../../../../../fourier-series-split.md) is

$$
f(x)\sim\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big[a_n\cos nx+b_n\sin nx\big],
$$

with [Fourier coefficients](../../../../../fourier-coefficient.md)

$$
\boxed{a_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\cos nx\,dx\quad(n\geq0),\qquad
b_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\sin nx\,dx\quad(n\geq1).}
$$

These formulas follow from [Fourier orthogonality](../../../../../fourier-orthogonality.md). Integrability is needed to define the coefficients, and periodicity alone does not guarantee pointwise equality with the series. For a piecewise smooth periodic [function](../../../../../function-split.md), the [Dirichlet-Jordan convergence theorem](../../../../../dirichlet-jordan-convergence-theorem.md) gives the average of the one-sided limits at a jump and the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) value at a [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) point.
