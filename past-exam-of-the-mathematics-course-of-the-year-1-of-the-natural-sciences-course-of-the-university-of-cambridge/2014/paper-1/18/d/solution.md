<h1 id="18/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

When only a tiny fraction of either species is removed, the trout fraction among the remaining fish stays nearly constant at $p=K/N$. Successive catches are then approximately independent trials with that fixed success [probability](../../../../../probability.md). The count therefore approaches the [binomial distribution](../../../../../binomial-distribution.md)

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(X=j)\approx\binom Mj p^j(1-p)^{M-j},\qquad p=K/N.}
$$

The approximation comes from negligible depletion, not from an exact [independence](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) property of [sampling without replacement](../../../../../sampling-without-replacement.md).
