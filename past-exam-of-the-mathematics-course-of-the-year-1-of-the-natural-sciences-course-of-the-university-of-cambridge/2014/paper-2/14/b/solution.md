<h1 id="14/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Exactness after multiplication requires $\partial_y(\mu M)=\partial_x(\mu N)$. Since $\mu$ depends only on $x$,

$$
\mu M_y=\mu' N+\mu N_x.
$$

For $\mu=\exp(\int f(x)\,dx)$, the ratio $\mu'/\mu$ is $f(x)$, so

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\frac{M_y-N_x}{N}},\qquad
\boxed{\mu(x)=\exp\int\frac{M_y-N_x}{N}\,dx}.
$$

The assumed existence means this ratio is a [function](../../../../../function-split.md) only of $x$ wherever the division is valid. For a factor depending only on $y$, the corresponding equation is $\psi'M+\psi M_y=\psi N_x$, giving

$$
\boxed{\psi(y)=\exp\int\frac{N_x-M_y}{M}\,dy}.
$$

These are the [integrating factor for a differential one-form](../../../../../integrating-factor-for-a-differential-one-form.md) formulas. Nonzero constant multiples are equivalent. Exactness follows locally from closedness, and globally on a suitable [simply connected domain](../../../../../simply-connected-domain.md).
