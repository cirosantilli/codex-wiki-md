<h1 id="17/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}>0$, the [multivariable chain rule](../../../../../multivariable-chain-rule.md) gives $\partial_xg(r)=g'(r)x/r$, with the corresponding expressions for $y,z$. Thus the [gradient of a radial function](../../../../../gradient-of-a-radial-function.md) is

$$
\boxed{\nabla g(r)=g'(r)\frac{(x,y,z)}r=g'(r)\hat{\mathbf r},\qquad r>0}.
$$

The origin needs a separate check because $\hat{\mathbf r}$ is undefined there. If the one-variable [function](../../../../../function-split.md) has a finite right [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) at zero, the radial composite is differentiable at the origin exactly when $g'(0^+)=0$, in which case its [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) is zero. A nonzero radial slope gives incompatible opposite-direction [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md).
