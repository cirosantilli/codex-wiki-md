<h1 id="12/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The same elimination at general $a$ gives the rows

$$
\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&|&3\\0&0&a-3&|&-2\\
0&1-a&3-a&|&5-3a\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The coefficient [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) is $(a-3)(a-1)$. Only $a=1$ or $a=3$ can therefore fail to have a unique solution. At $a=3$, the second row reads $0=-2$, so **there is no solution**. At $a=1$, the last two rows are proportional and both specify $z=1$, leaving one free unknown; hence **there are infinitely many solutions**. Every other real $a$ gives a unique solution.
