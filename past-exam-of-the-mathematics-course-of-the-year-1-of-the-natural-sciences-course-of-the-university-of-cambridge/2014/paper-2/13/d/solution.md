<h1 id="13/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The sum is finite, so [differentiation](../../../../../differentiation.md) passes through it without a convergence issue. Each lower limit $n$ is constant with respect to $x$, and the [fundamental theorem of calculus](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) gives the same upper-end integrand. Hence the [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) is

$$
\sin(x^2+x^6)\sum_{n=0}^N\binom Nn.
$$

The [binomial theorem](../../../../../binomial-theorem.md) at $1+1$ gives the sum $2^N$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}\left[\sum_{n=0}^N\binom Nn
\int_n^x\sin(y^2+y^6)\,dy\right]=2^N\sin(x^2+x^6)}.
$$
