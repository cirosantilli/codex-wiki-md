The [fundamental theorem of calculus](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) states that integrating a continuous [function](../../../../../function-split.md) from a fixed lower endpoint produces an [antiderivative](../../../../../antiderivative.md). More precisely, if $f$ is continuous at the interior point $x$, then

$$
\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(y)\,dy=f(x)}.
$$

Indeed, the difference quotient is the average of $f$ over the short interval from $x$ to $x+h$, which tends to $f(x)$ by [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md). The accompanying evaluation rule is $\int_a^b f=F(b)-F(a)$ whenever $F'=f$ on the interval.
