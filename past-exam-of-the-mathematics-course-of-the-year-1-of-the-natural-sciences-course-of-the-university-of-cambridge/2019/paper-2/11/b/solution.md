<h1 id="11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Two directions within the plane are

$$
\mathbf f-\mathbf e=\left(\frac12,-\frac12,0\right),\qquad
\mathbf g-\mathbf e=\left(\frac12,0,-\frac12\right).
$$

Their [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) is $(1,1,1)/4$, so a [unit vector](../../../../../unit-vector.md) normal to the plane is

$$
\boxed{\hat{\mathbf n}=\frac{(1,1,1)}{\sqrt3}}.
$$

Its negative is equally valid. An [equation of a plane through three points](../../../../../equation-of-a-plane-through-three-points.md) in vector form is

$$
\boxed{\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf e)=0},
$$

or, parametrically, $\mathbf r=\mathbf e+s(\mathbf f-\mathbf e)+t(\mathbf g-\mathbf e)$. Since the coordinate sum of $E$ is one, the [Cartesian equation of a plane](../../../../../cartesian-equation-of-a-plane.md) is

$$
\boxed{x+y+z=1}.
$$

The [distance from a point to a plane](../../../../../distance-from-a-point-to-a-plane.md) is the absolute normal displacement, giving

$$
\boxed{d(O,EFG)=|\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf e|=\frac1{\sqrt3}}.
$$
