<h1 id="11/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $\mathbf d=\mathbf f-\mathbf e$. A point on the line has [position vector](../../../../../position-vector.md) $\mathbf r=\mathbf e+t\mathbf d$. By bilinearity and antisymmetry of the [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md),

$$
\boxed{\mathbf r\times\mathbf d=\mathbf e\times\mathbf d+t\mathbf d\times\mathbf d=\mathbf e\times\mathbf f}.
$$

For a point with [position vector](../../../../../position-vector.md) $\mathbf c$, the area of the [parallelogram](../../../../../parallelogram.md) spanned by $\mathbf c-\mathbf e$ and $\mathbf d$ equals base length times perpendicular [Euclidean distance](../../../../../euclidean-distance.md). Hence the shortest [Euclidean distance](../../../../../euclidean-distance.md) to the line is

$$
\boxed{d=\frac{|(\mathbf c-\mathbf e)\times(\mathbf f-\mathbf e)|}{|\mathbf f-\mathbf e|}}.
$$

For $\mathbf c=(0,0,1)$, these quantities are

$$
(\mathbf c-\mathbf e)\times(\mathbf f-\mathbf e)=\left(\frac14,\frac14,\frac14\right),\qquad
|\mathbf f-\mathbf e|=\frac1{\sqrt2}.
$$

Thus $\boxed{d=\sqrt6/4}$. The perpendicular meets $EF$ at $(1/4,1/4,1/2)$, whose displacement from $C$ has precisely this length.
