<h1 id="12/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The most useful guide to the [contour plot](../../../../../contour-plot.md) is the common saddle value. Direct [polynomial factorization](../../../../../factorization-of-polynomials.md) gives

$$
6f-1=(2y+1)\bigl[3x^2-(y-1)^2\bigr].
$$

Thus the [level set](../../../../../level-set.md) $f=1/6$ is exactly the three complete lines

$$
\boxed{y=-\frac12,\qquad y=1+\sqrt3x,\qquad y=1-\sqrt3x}.
$$

Their intersections are the three [saddle points](../../../../../saddle-point.md), and they enclose the origin in an equilateral triangle. Near the [local minimum](../../../../../local-minimum.md), the [level sets](../../../../../level-set.md) are nearly circles because the leading term is $(x^2+y^2)/2$. For $0<c<1/6$, a closed contour surrounds the origin, while three separate open branches lie outside the triangular barrier. At the saddle level these components meet at the [saddle points](../../../../../saddle-point.md); above it the central closed contour has opened into the three outer channels.

The identity in [polar coordinates](../../../../../polar-coordinates.md)

$$
f=\frac12r^2+\frac13r^3\sin3\theta
$$

shows the rotation symmetry through $2\pi/3$. It identifies the function as the [Hénon-Heiles potential](../../../../../henon-heiles-potential.md) and helps organize all three channels in the original sketch below. The dashed straight lines are the saddle level; labels on the other contours give $f$.
