<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [power series](../../../../../power-series.md) for $e^{y^3}$ has [uniform convergence](../../../../../uniform-convergence.md) on every compact interval, so [termwise integration](../../../../../termwise-integration.md) from zero to $x$ is valid. Thus

$$
\int_0^x e^{y^3}\,dy=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int_0^x y^{3n}\,dy
=\boxed{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{3n+1}}{(3n+1)n!}}.
$$

This [power series](../../../../../power-series.md) converges for every real $x$, including negative $x$ with the usual orientation of a [definite integral](../../../../../definite-integral.md).
