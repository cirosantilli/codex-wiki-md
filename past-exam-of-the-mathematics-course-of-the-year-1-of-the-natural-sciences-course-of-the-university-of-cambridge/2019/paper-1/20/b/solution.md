<h1 id="20/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Apply the [Leibniz integral rule](../../../../../leibniz-integral-rule.md), including both moving boundaries and the explicit parameter in the integrand. The upper bound has [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) $-\sin x$, the lower bound has [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) $\cos x$, and the integrand has [partial derivative](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) $-t^4e^{-xt^4}$ with respect to $x$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f'(x)=-\sin x\,e^{-x\cos^4x}-\cos x\,e^{-x\sin^4x}-\int_{\sin x}^{\cos x}t^4e^{-xt^4}\,dt.}
$$

The formula remains valid when the bounds coincide or occur in reversed order, by the oriented [definite integral](../../../../../definite-integral.md) convention. The integrand and its [partial derivative](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) are continuous on every finite integration interval.
