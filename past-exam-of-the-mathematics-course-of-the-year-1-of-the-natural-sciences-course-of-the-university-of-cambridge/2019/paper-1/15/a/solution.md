<h1 id="15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The finite form of [Taylor's theorem](../../../../../taylor-s-theorem.md) is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k+R_n(x),\qquad R_n(x)=\frac{f^{(n)}(\xi)}{n!}(x-a)^n,}
$$

where $\xi$ lies between $a$ and $x$ when $x\ne a$. Sufficient hypotheses are continuous [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) up to order $n-1$ on the interval joining the endpoints and an $n$th [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) in its interior. The first $n$ terms end at degree $n-1$; the [Taylor remainder](../../../../../taylor-remainder.md) uses the $n$th [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md). At $x=a$ the remainder is zero. This finite [Taylor polynomial](../../../../../taylor-polynomial.md) identity does not require convergence of an infinite [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md).
