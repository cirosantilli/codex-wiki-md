<h1 id="18/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Since $A^2=-I$, the [matrix inverse](../../../../../../matrix-inverse-split.md) is $A^{-1}=-A$. The [orthogonal matrix](../../../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) condition also gives $A^{-1}=A^{\mathsf T}$, so $A^{\mathsf T}=-A$. Thus $A$ is [skew-symmetric](../../../../../../skew-symmetric-matrix.md), forcing $a=0$ and $c=-b$. Its columns must be [unit vectors](../../../../../../unit-vector.md), giving $b^2=1$. Therefore the two possibilities are

$$
\boxed{A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{or}\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Both satisfy the original conditions by direct multiplication. They are the two [orthogonal skew-symmetric matrices](../../../../../../orthogonal-skew-symmetric-matrix.md) in two dimensions, representing opposite quarter-turn [rotations](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md).
