<h1 id="19/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For every integer $n$, $\cos((2n-1)\pi)=-1$. The [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) is therefore the negative of the [harmonic series](../../../../../../harmonic-series.md):

$$
\sum_{n=1}^N\frac{\cos((2n-1)\pi)}n=-\sum_{n=1}^N\frac1n.
$$

For a direct divergence argument, each block $2^{j-1}<n\le2^j$ contributes at least $2^{j-1}/2^j=1/2$ to the positive sum. Its [partial sums](../../../../../../partial-sum.md) grow without bound, so

$$
\boxed{\text{the given series diverges to }-\infty.}
$$

Despite the odd multiples of $\pi$, the signs do not alternate.
