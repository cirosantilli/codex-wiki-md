<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Use [integration by parts](../../../../integration-by-parts.md) with $u=\ln x$ and $dv=x^2\,dx$. This gives the [antiderivative](../../../../antiderivative.md)

$$
\int x^2\ln x\,dx=\frac{x^3}{3}\ln x-\frac13\int x^2\,dx
=\frac{x^3}{3}\ln x-\frac{x^3}{9}+C.
$$

Evaluating at the two bounds, with $\ln1=0$, yields

$$
\boxed{\int_1^y x^2\ln x\,dx=\frac{y^3}{3}\ln y-\frac{y^3-1}{9}.}
$$
