<h1 id="13/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $x\ne0$, multiply by $x$ to obtain the first-order [linear ordinary differential equation](../../../../../../linear-ordinary-differential-equation-split.md) $y'+xy=5xe^{-x^2}$. Its [integrating factor](../../../../../../integrating-factor.md) is $\mu=e^{x^2/2}$. The [product rule](../../../../../../product-rule.md) then gives

$$
(ye^{x^2/2})'=5xe^{-x^2/2}.
$$

Integration yields $ye^{x^2/2}=-5e^{-x^2/2}+C$, so

$$
\boxed{y(x)=Ce^{-x^2/2}-5e^{-x^2},\qquad x\ne0.}
$$

The [constant of integration](../../../../../../constant-of-integration.md) is arbitrary on each connected interval of the original equation's domain. The formula extends smoothly through zero, but the printed equation itself contains $1/x$ and is not defined there.
