<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Factorization gives $y=(x-2)^2(x+1)$, so the zeros are $x=-1$ and $x=2$, with a double root at two. The [derivatives](../../../../derivative.md) are $y'=3x(x-2)$ and $y''=6(x-1)$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{local maximum }(0,4),\quad\text{local minimum }(2,0),\quad\text{inflection point }(1,2).}
$$

The second [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) changes sign at one, confirming the [inflection point](../../../../inflection-point.md). The curve rises from $-\infty$, crosses at $-1$, reaches its local maximum, falls to touch the axis at two, then rises to $+\infty$. Neither local extremum is a global extremum on the whole real line.
