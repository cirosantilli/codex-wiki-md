<h1 id="20/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Direct [differentiation](../../../../../differentiation.md) of $v=(x^2-1)^l$ gives $(x^2-1)v'=2lxv$; the identity also holds for $l=0$. Differentiate it $l+1$ times using the [Leibniz rule](../../../../../leibniz-rule.md). The quadratic factor has only its first two derivatives nonzero, so

$$
(x^2-1)v^{(l+2)}+2(l+1)xv^{(l+1)}+l(l+1)v^{(l)}
=2l[xv^{(l+1)}+(l+1)v^{(l)}].
$$

Writing $y=v^{(l)}$ and collecting terms gives $(x^2-1)y''+2xy'-l(l+1)y=0$. Multiplication by $-1$ gives the [Legendre differential equation](../../../../../legendre-differential-equation.md):

$$
\boxed{(1-x^2)y''-2xy'+l(l+1)y=0.}
$$
