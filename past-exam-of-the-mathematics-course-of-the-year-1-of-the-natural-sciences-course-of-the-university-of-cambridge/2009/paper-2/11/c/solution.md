<h1 id="11/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

From the line point $r_0=(1,-1,0)$ to $P=(2,2,1)$, take $w=P-r_0=(1,3,1)$ and line direction $d=(2,3,1)$. [Orthogonal projection](../../../../../orthogonal-projection.md) gives the nearest parameter $t=(w\cdot d)/(d\cdot d)=12/14=6/7$. The perpendicular displacement has squared [Euclidean norm](../../../../../euclidean-norm.md)

$$
|w|^2-\frac{(w\cdot d)^2}{|d|^2}=11-\frac{144}{14}=\frac57.
$$

Hence the [distance from a point to a line in three dimensions](../../../../../distance-from-a-point-to-a-line-in-three-dimensions.md) is

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{5/7}.}
$$

The nearest point is $(19/7,11/7,6/7)$; subtracting it from $P$ gives a vector orthogonal to $d$, verifying the minimum.
