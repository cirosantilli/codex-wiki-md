<h1 id="19/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Choose the upward orientation. The boundary is the circle $x^2+y^2=4$ at $z=1$, oriented counterclockwise from above. By [Stokes theorem](../../../../../stokes-theorem.md), the surface [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) is $\oint_{\partial S}G\cdot dr$. On this boundary $dz=0$ and $z^2=1$, so

$$
\oint_{\partial S}G\cdot dr=\oint(dx-3xy\,dy).
$$

The first term integrates to zero around a closed curve. By [Green's theorem](../../../../../green-theorem.md), the second equals the disk integral $\int_{x^2+y^2\leq4}(-3y)dx\,dy=0$, by oddness in $y$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\int_S(\nabla\times G)\cdot n\,dS=0.}
$$

Here reversing orientation leaves the zero value unchanged.
