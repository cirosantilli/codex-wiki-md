<h1 id="19/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [divergence](../../../../../divergence.md) is $\nabla\cdot F=-1+0+1=0$. Take the hemisphere's outward orientation, and close it with the unit disk $D$ at $z=0$, whose outward normal is $-e_z$. The [divergence theorem](../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) then gives $\int_SF\cdot dS+\int_DF\cdot dS=0$. On the disk, $F\cdot(-e_z)=-x^2$, and [polar coordinates](../../../../../polar-coordinates.md) give

$$
\int_Dx^2dA=\int_0^1\int_0^{2\pi}r^2\cos^2\theta\,r\,d\theta\,dr=\frac\pi4.
$$

Consequently the curved hemisphere has [flux](../../../../../flux.md)

$$
\boxed{\int_SF\cdot dS=\frac\pi4.}
$$

The reversed hemisphere orientation would reverse the sign.
