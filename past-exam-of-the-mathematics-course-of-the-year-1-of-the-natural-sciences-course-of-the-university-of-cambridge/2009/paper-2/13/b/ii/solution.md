<h1 id="13/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A tangent displacement has the form $(dx,dy,z_xdx+z_ydy)$. At the specified point, $z_y=0$ and $z_x=q>0$, so the fastest decrease of height per unit surface distance has $dy=0$ and $dx<0$. The unit downhill tangent is $(-1,0,-q)/\sqrt{1+q^2}$. Therefore, for speed $u$ measured along the surface,

$$
\boxed{v=-\frac{u}{\sqrt{1+e^{-1}}}(1,0,e^{-1/2}).}
$$

Its [Euclidean norm](../../../../../../euclidean-norm.md) is $u$, and it is orthogonal to the surface normal. The general construction is [steepest descent on a graph surface](../../../../../../steepest-descent-on-a-graph-surface.md); using a horizontal speed equal to $u$ would overestimate the full three-dimensional speed.
