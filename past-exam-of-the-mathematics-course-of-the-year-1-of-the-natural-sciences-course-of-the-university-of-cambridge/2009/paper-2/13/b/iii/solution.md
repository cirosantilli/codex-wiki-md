<h1 id="13/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The horizontal direction of [steepest descent on a graph surface](../../../../../../steepest-descent-on-a-graph-surface.md) is parallel to $-\nabla z$. On the line $y=1$, the component $z_y=X(1-y^2)H$ vanishes identically, so that line is invariant under the downhill path. Starting at $X=0$, $z_x=(1-X^2)e^{-(X^2+1)/2}>0$ for $-1<X<0$, so $x$ decreases towards zero. The path reaches the minimum

$$
\boxed{(x,y,z)=(0,1,-e^{-1}).}
$$

At the minimum no strictly downhill direction remains. A constant-speed parametrization reaches it after the finite arc length divided by $u$; the unnormalized [gradient flow](../../../../../../gradient-flow.md) approaches the same endpoint only asymptotically in its own time parameter.
