<h1 id="19/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume $c\ne0$, so $(\xi,\eta)=(x-ct,x+ct)$ is an invertible coordinate change. The [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives

$$
\partial_x=\partial_\xi+\partial_\eta,\qquad\partial_t=c(-\partial_\xi+\partial_\eta).
$$

For a twice continuously differentiable solution the mixed [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) commute. Squaring the two operators therefore gives

$$
\partial_x^2-c^{-2}\partial_t^2=4\partial_\xi\partial_\eta.
$$

Thus the [one-dimensional wave equation](../../../../../one-dimensional-wave-equation.md) becomes

$$
\boxed{\psi_{\xi\eta}=0.}
$$
