<h1 id="12/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Neither biscuit type can run out in this case. Treat each freely made choice as an [independent](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) [Bernoulli trial](../../../../../../bernoulli-trial.md), with success meaning chocolate. The number taken after $n$ choices has a [binomial distribution](../../../../../../binomial-distribution.md), so

$$
\boxed{P_{k,n}=\binom nkp^k(1-p)^{n-k},\quad k=0,\ldots,n.}
$$

The [binomial coefficient](../../../../../../binomial-coefficient-split.md) counts the possible success positions, and every such arrangement has the same probability. Independence of successive choices is the probabilistic model implicit in the question; identical marginal preferences alone would not imply it.
