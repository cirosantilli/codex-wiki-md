<h1 id="15/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Three vectors in real three-dimensional space have zero [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) exactly when they are [linearly dependent](../../../../../../linear-dependence.md). Their [linear span](../../../../../../linear-span.md) then has dimension at most two, so they lie in a common plane through the origin. Conversely vectors in such a plane span at most two dimensions, so their [scalar triple product](../../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md) vanishes. This includes parallel nonzero vectors: they need not determine a unique plane, but they lie in at least one. Even zero vectors satisfy the determinant characterization, though the question excludes them.
