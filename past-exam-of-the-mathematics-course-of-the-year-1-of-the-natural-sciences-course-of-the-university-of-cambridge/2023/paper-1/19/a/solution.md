<h1 id="19/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a [series](../../../../../series-mathematics.md) $\sum u_k$ with eventually nonzero terms, suppose

$$
L=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\frac{u_{k+1}}{u_k}\right|
$$

exists, allowing $L=+\infty$. The [ratio test](../../../../../ratio-test.md) says that **if $L<1$, the [series](../../../../../series-mathematics.md) converges absolutely; if $L>1$, it diverges; if $L=1$, the test is inconclusive**. In the divergent case the terms fail to tend to zero. For example, the [P-series](../../../../../p-series.md) with exponents $1$ and $2$ both have ratio limit $1$ but have different convergence behaviour.
