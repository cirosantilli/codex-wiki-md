<h1 id="19/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $u_k=a^{2k+1}/(2k+1)>0$, the consecutive ratio is

$$
\frac{u_{k+1}}{u_k}=a^2\frac{2k+1}{2k+3}\longrightarrow a^2.
$$

Thus the [ratio test](../../../../../../ratio-test.md) proves convergence when $0<a<1$ and divergence when $a>1$. At $a=1$, the [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) is $\sum_{k\geq0}1/(2k+1)$, which diverges by comparison with $\tfrac12\sum_{k\geq0}1/(k+1)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{convergent for }0<a<1;\qquad\text{divergent for }a\geq1.}
$$

All terms are nonnegative, so convergence here is also [absolute convergence](../../../../../../absolute-convergence.md).
