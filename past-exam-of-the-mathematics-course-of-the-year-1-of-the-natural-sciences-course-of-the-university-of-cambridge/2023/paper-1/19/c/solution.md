<h1 id="19/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

[L'Hôpital's rule](../../../../../l-hopital-s-rule.md) applies to a quotient $f(x)/g(x)$ of [differentiable functions](../../../../../differentiable-function.md) near a limiting [point](../../../../../point-geometry.md), excluding that [point](../../../../../point-geometry.md) itself, provided $g'(x)\ne0$ there. If the quotient has an indeterminate form $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$, and the [limit](../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) of $f'(x)/g'(x)$ exists as a finite or infinite value $L$, then

$$
\boxed{\lim_{x\to a}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=\lim_{x\to a}\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}=L.}
$$

The rule also applies to one-sided [limits](../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) and to limits at infinity, under the corresponding hypotheses. Existence of the derivative-ratio [limit](../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) and the indeterminate-form hypotheses are required; an arbitrary quotient cannot be replaced by a quotient of [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md).
