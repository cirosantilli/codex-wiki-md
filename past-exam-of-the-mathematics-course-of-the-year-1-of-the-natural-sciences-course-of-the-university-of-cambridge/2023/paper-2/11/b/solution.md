<h1 id="11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If the three [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) lie in one [plane](../../../../../plane.md) through the origin and $\mathbf u,\mathbf v$ are independent, their [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) is a [normal vector](../../../../../normal-vector.md) to that [plane](../../../../../plane.md), so its [dot product](../../../../../dot-product.md) with $\mathbf w$ is zero. If $\mathbf u,\mathbf v$ are dependent, their [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) is already zero. Thus coplanarity always forces the [scalar triple product](../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md) to vanish.

Taking the [contrapositive](../../../../../contrapositive.md) proves that a nonzero [scalar triple product](../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md) rules out coplanarity. Geometrically its [absolute value](../../../../../absolute-value.md) is the nonzero [volume](../../../../../volume.md) of the spanned [parallelepiped](../../../../../parallelepiped.md).
