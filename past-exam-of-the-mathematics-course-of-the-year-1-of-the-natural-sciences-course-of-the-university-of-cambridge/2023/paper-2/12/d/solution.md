<h1 id="12/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For case (i),

$$
\boxed{\nabla h=(e^x-1,e^y-1)^T}.
$$

Each component has the sign of its coordinate, so the [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) points outward from the [minimum](../../../../../minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md). Near the origin it is $(x,y)^T+O(x^2+y^2)$.

For case (ii),

$$
\boxed{\nabla h=(e^x-1,1-e^y)^T}.
$$

Its $x$ component has the sign of $x$, while its $y$ component has the opposite sign to $y$. Thus arrows point outward on the $x$-axis and inward on the $y$-axis, with local form $(x,-y)^T+O(x^2+y^2)$. These directions are included in the contour figure; in both cases the [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) is perpendicular to the [level curves](../../../../../level-curve.md) and points toward increasing $h$.
