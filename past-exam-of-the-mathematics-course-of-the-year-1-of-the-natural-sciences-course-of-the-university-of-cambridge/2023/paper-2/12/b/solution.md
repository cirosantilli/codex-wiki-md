<h1 id="12/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Hessian matrix](../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) is diagonal:

$$
H(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}ae^x&0\\0&be^y\end{pmatrix},\qquad
H(x_*,y_*)=\begin{pmatrix}c&0\\0&d\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are $c$ and $d$. Hence the [second-derivative test](../../../../../second-derivative-test.md) gives a strict [local minimum](../../../../../local-minimum.md) if $c,d>0$, a strict [local maximum](../../../../../local-maximum.md) if $c,d<0$, and a [saddle point of a scalar function](../../../../../saddle-point-of-a-scalar-function.md) if $cd<0$. No degenerate case occurs because both are nonzero.

The [local minimum](../../../../../local-minimum.md) is the unique [global minimum](../../../../../global-minimum.md), since both separated summands are [strictly convex functions](../../../../../strictly-convex-function.md) and tend to $+\infty$ at either end of the real line. Similarly, when both signs are negative there is a unique [global maximum](../../../../../global-maximum.md).
