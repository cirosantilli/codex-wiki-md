<h1 id="20/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Seek a separated solution $u=X(x)Y(y)$. On a region where the factors do not vanish, the [partial differential equation](../../../../../partial-differential-equation-split.md) separates as

$$
\frac{X'}X=-(6-2y)\frac{Y'}Y=\lambda.
$$

The resulting [ordinary differential equations](../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) integrate to $X=Ae^{\lambda x}$ and $Y=B|3-y|^{\lambda/2}$ on either side of $y=3$. Choose $\lambda=2$ and the signed linear branch $Y=3-y$, which extends smoothly across that line. This yields the nonconstant solution

$$
\boxed{u(x,y)=e^{2x}(3-y)}.
$$

Direct [partial differentiation](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) gives $u_x=2e^{2x}(3-y)$ and $u_y=-e^{2x}$, whose prescribed combination vanishes everywhere. This direct check also covers the line on which the separated factor is zero.
