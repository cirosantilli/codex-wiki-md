<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

The [fundamental theorem of calculus](../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) gives $y'(x)=e^{-x^2}>0$, so the graph is strictly increasing. Its second [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) is $y''(x)=-2xe^{-x^2}$: it curves upward for $x<0$ and downward for $x>0$, with an [inflection point](../../../../inflection-point.md) at zero.

By the [Gaussian integral](../../../../gaussian-integral.md) and the evenness of its [integrand](../../../../integrand.md),

$$
\boxed{y(0)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}},\qquad
\lim_{x\to-\infty}y(x)=0,\qquad
\lim_{x\to+\infty}y(x)=\sqrt\pi.
$$

Thus the only axis intersection is $(0,\sqrt\pi/2)$; there is no finite $x$-axis intersection because the [integral](../../../../integral-split.md) is strictly positive for every finite $x$. The [error function](../../../../error-function.md) gives $y(x)=\tfrac{\sqrt\pi}{2}(1+\operatorname{erf}x)$.
