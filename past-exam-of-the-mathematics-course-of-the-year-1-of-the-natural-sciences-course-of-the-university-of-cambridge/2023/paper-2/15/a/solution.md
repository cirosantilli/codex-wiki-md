<h1 id="15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [homogeneous solution](../../../../../homogeneous-solution.md) is $A\cos x+B\sin x$. If $k\ne1$, the [method of undetermined coefficients](../../../../../method-of-undetermined-coefficients.md) permits a [particular integral](../../../../../particular-solution.md) $C\cos kx$. Substitution gives $(1-k^2)C=1$ and hence

$$
\boxed{y(x)=A\cos x+B\sin x+\frac{\cos kx}{1-k^2},\qquad k=2,3,\ldots}.
$$

For $k=1$, the forcing is itself a [homogeneous solution](../../../../../homogeneous-solution.md), so this trial fails by [resonance in a differential equation](../../../../../resonance-in-a-differential-equation.md). Multiplying the sine trial by $x$ gives

$$
\left(\frac{d^2}{dx^2}+1\right)(x\sin x)=2\cos x.
$$

Thus the resonant [general solution](../../../../../general-solution.md) is

$$
\boxed{y(x)=A\cos x+B\sin x+\frac{x}{2}\sin x,\qquad k=1}.
$$

The [secular term](../../../../../secular-term.md) containing $x$ describes the increasing amplitude under undamped resonant forcing.
