<h1 id="19/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a point in the first quadrant, $xy$ is the [area](../../../../../../surface-area.md) of the axis-aligned rectangle with opposite vertices at the origin and $(x,y)$. Thus the maximum says that the largest such quarter-rectangle with its outer corner on the [ellipse](../../../../../../ellipse.md) has [area](../../../../../../surface-area.md) one.

Equivalently, reflect this rectangle in both axes. Its four vertices $(\pm|x|,\pm|y|)$ lie on the [ellipse](../../../../../../ellipse.md), and its full [area](../../../../../../surface-area.md) is $4|xy|$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{the maximum area of an axis-aligned rectangle inscribed in the ellipse is }4}.
$$

Its width is $1/\sqrt2$ and its height is $4\sqrt2$.
