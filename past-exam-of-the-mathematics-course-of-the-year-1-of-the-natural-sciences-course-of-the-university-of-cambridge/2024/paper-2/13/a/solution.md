<h1 id="13/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For $\mathbf G$, the [product rule](../../../../../product-rule.md) for the [curl](../../../../../curl.md) gives

$$
\nabla\times\mathbf G
=\nabla(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf r)\times\mathbf r
+(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf r)(\nabla\times\mathbf r)
=\mathbf a\times\mathbf r.
$$

Since $\mathbf a\ne0$, this is not identically zero on $\mathbb R^3$. A [gradient field](../../../../../gradient-field.md) has zero [curl](../../../../../curl.md), so **no scalar potential exists for $\mathbf G$ on the whole space**.

For the radial [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md) $\mathbf K$, observe that

$$
\nabla\left[\frac12\log(1+x^2+y^2+z^2)\right]
=\frac{(x,y,z)^T}{1+x^2+y^2+z^2}=\mathbf K.
$$

Thus all [vector-field potentials](../../../../../potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md) are

$$
\boxed{\phi(\mathbf r)=\frac12\log(1+|\mathbf r|^2)+C}.
$$

These are [smooth](../../../../../smooth-function.md) everywhere, including the origin. Any two differ by a constant because their [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) difference vanishes on the [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) whole space.
