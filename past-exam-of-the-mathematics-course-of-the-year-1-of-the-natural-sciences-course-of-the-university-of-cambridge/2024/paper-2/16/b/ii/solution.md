<h1 id="16/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

On $OAC$, $y=0$ and the outward [unit normal](../../../../../../unit-normal.md) is $-\mathbf e_y$. Its [flux](../../../../../../flux.md) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{OAC}&=-\int_0^1\int_0^{1-x}(x+z)^2\,dz\,dx\\
&=-\frac13\int_0^1(1-x^3)\,dx=\boxed{-\frac14}.
\end{aligned}
$$

On $OBC$, $x=0$ and the outward [unit normal](../../../../../../unit-normal.md) is $-\mathbf e_x$. Hence

$$
\Phi_{OBC}=-\int_0^1\int_0^{1-y}(y+z)\,dz\,dy
=-\frac12\int_0^1(1-y^2)\,dy=\boxed{-\frac13}.
$$

On $ABC$, write $z=1-x-y$ over $x\geq0$, $y\geq0$, $x+y\leq1$. The [oriented surface element](../../../../../../oriented-surface-element.md) is $(1,1,1)^Tdx\,dy$, pointing away from the origin. Here $x+y+z=1$, so $\mathbf F=(1,1,0)^T$. Thus

$$
\Phi_{ABC}=\int_0^1\int_0^{1-x}2\,dy\,dx=\boxed{1}.
$$

The unnormalized [normal vector](../../../../../../normal-vector.md) in the [oriented surface element](../../../../../../oriented-surface-element.md) already incorporates the factor $\sqrt3$ from the surface [area](../../../../../../surface-area.md); no additional factor is needed.
