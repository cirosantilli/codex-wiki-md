<h1 id="15/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the [natural logarithm](../../../../../natural-logarithm.md), $f(1)=0$, $f'(1)=1$, $f''(1)=-1$, and $f^{(3)}(x)=2/x^3$. The quadratic [Taylor polynomial](../../../../../taylor-polynomial.md) therefore gives

$$
\ln(1.1)\approx0.1-\frac{0.1^2}{2}=\boxed{0.095}.
$$

The [Taylor remainder](../../../../../taylor-remainder.md) is $R_3=2(0.1)^3/(6\xi^3)$ with $1<\xi<1.1$. It is positive, so the approximation is **an underestimate**. Moreover,

$$
\boxed{0<\ln(1.1)-0.095<\frac{(0.1)^3}{3}=\frac1{3000}.}
$$

Thus $1/3000\approx0.0003334$ is a valid upper bound on the [absolute error](../../../../../absolute-error.md).
