<h1 id="15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Taylor's theorem](../../../../../taylor-s-theorem.md) gives a finite [Taylor polynomial](../../../../../taylor-polynomial.md) of degree $n$, consisting of the first $n+1$ terms, and a remainder:

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(j)}(a)}{j!}(x-a)^j+R_{n+1}(x).}
$$

In the [Taylor theorem with Lagrange remainder](../../../../../taylor-theorem-with-lagrange-remainder.md) form, assuming the required [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) exist on an [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) containing $a$ and $x$,

$$
\boxed{R_{n+1}(x)=\frac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}(x-a)^{n+1},}
$$

for some $\xi$ strictly between $a$ and $x$ when $x\ne a$; at $x=a$ the remainder is zero. In particular, if $|f^{(n+1)}|\leq M$ on that segment, then $|R_{n+1}(x)|\leq M|x-a|^{n+1}/(n+1)!$. This is a finite expansion; it does not assume equality with an infinite [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md).
