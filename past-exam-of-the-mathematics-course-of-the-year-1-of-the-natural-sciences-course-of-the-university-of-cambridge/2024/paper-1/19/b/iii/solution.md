<h1 id="19/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [difference quotient](../../../../../../difference-quotient.md) at zero is

$$
\frac{f(h)-f(0)}h=\frac{|h|^p}{h}\cos(1/h),\qquad h\ne0.
$$

If $p>1$, its [absolute value](../../../../../../absolute-value.md) is at most $|h|^{p-1}\to0$, so $f$ is [differentiable](../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at zero with [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) $0$.

To exclude $p\leq1$, use $h_n=1/(2\pi n)$ and $k_n=1/(2\pi n+\pi/2)$. The [difference quotient](../../../../../../difference-quotient.md) equals $(2\pi n)^{1-p}$ along the first [sequence](../../../../../../sequence.md), and $0$ along the second. For $p=1$ the first value is $1$, while for $p<1$ it is unbounded. Hence a common finite [limit](../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) is impossible. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f\text{ is differentiable at }0\iff p>1,\qquad f'(0)=0\text{ when }p>1.}
$$
