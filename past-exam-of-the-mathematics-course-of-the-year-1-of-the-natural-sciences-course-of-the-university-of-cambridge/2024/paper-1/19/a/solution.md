<h1 id="19/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Apply the [Leibniz rule](../../../../../leibniz-rule.md) for the $n$th [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) of a product. Only the first three terms survive, because all [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) of $x^2$ of order greater than two vanish and every [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) of $e^x$ is $e^x$:

$$
\frac{d^n}{dx^n}(x^2e^x)=e^x\left[\binom n0x^2+\binom n1(2x)+\binom n2(2)\right]=e^x[x^2+2nx+n(n-1)].
$$

At $n=100$ this gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{d^{100}}{dx^{100}}(x^2e^x)=e^x(x^2+200x+9900).}
$$
