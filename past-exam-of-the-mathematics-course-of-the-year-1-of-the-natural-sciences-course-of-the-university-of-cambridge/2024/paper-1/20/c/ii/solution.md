<h1 id="20/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The required range is

$$
\boxed{x>-1.}
$$

Near $t=1$, the ratio $(t^x-1)/\ln t$ tends to $x$, so it causes no convergence problem. Near $t=0$, one useful identity is

$$
\frac{t^x-1}{\ln t}=\int_0^x t^s\,ds.
$$

For $x>-1$, this bounds its [absolute value](../../../../../../absolute-value.md) by $|x|t^{\min(x,0)}$, which is integrable on $(0,1)$. It also supplies the domination used in part (i).

For $x=-1$, the integrand is negative and behaves as $-1/[t|\ln t|]$ near zero; its [improper integral](../../../../../../improper-integral.md) diverges. For $x<-1$ its negative singularity is even stronger, and comparison with that same divergent [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md) again rules out convergence. Thus the original finite real-valued definition of $g$ has precisely the stated domain.
