<h1 id="20/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At $x=0$, the integrand vanishes for $0<t<1$, so $I(0)=0$ and

$$
\boxed{g(0)=1.}
$$

Solving the [first-order linear differential equation](../../../../../../first-order-linear-differential-equation.md) from part (i) on $(-1,\infty)$ gives $g=C(x+1)$. The [initial condition](../../../../../../initial-condition.md) fixes $C=1$, so

$$
\boxed{g(x)=x+1,\qquad x>-1.}
$$

Equivalently, the defining exponent is the [logarithmic integral representation of the natural logarithm](../../../../../../logarithmic-integral-representation-of-the-natural-logarithm.md), $I(x)=\ln(x+1)$.
