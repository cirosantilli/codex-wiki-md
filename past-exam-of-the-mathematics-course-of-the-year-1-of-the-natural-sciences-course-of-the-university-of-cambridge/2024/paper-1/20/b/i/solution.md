<h1 id="20/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For the [Leibniz integral rule](../../../../../../leibniz-integral-rule.md), the upper endpoint has [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) $2\pi x$ and the lower endpoint has [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) $1$. The integrand has [partial derivative](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md) $4xt+\sin t$. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
h'(x)={}&\left(2\pi x^4+x\sin(\pi x^2)\right)2\pi x-\left(2x^3+x\sin x\right)\\
&+\int_x^{\pi x^2}(4xt+\sin t)\,dt.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md) is $[2xt^2-\cos t]_x^{\pi x^2}=2\pi^2x^5-2x^3-\cos(\pi x^2)+\cos x$. Combining terms gives

$$
\boxed{h'(x)=6\pi^2x^5-4x^3+2\pi x^2\sin(\pi x^2)-x\sin x+\cos x-\cos(\pi x^2).}
$$
