<h1 id="11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The given [complex numbers](../../../../../complex-number.md) have positive [real parts](../../../../../real-part.md), so $\arg z_1=\arctan A$ and $\arg z_2=\arctan B$. Their product is

$$
z_1z_2=(1-AB)+i(A+B).
$$

By the assumed [complex argument](../../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) ranges, this product also has positive [real part](../../../../../real-part.md), hence $1-AB>0$. Its [complex argument](../../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) is consequently $\arctan[(A+B)/(1-AB)]$, with no extra multiple of $\pi$. Since multiplication adds [complex arguments](../../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md),

$$
\boxed{\arctan A+\arctan B=\arctan\left(\frac{A+B}{1-AB}\right).}
$$

This proves the [arctangent addition formula](../../../../../arctangent-addition-formula.md) under the stated branch restriction. For arbitrary real $A,B$, the principal [inverse tangent](../../../../../inverse-tangent.md) formula can require adding or subtracting $\pi$ when $AB>1$.
