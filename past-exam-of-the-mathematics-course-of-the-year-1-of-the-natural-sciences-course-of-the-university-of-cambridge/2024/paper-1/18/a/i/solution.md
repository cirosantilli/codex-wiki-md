<h1 id="18/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The first coordinate direction is already an [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $A$ with [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $4$. On the last two coordinates, the [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) is $\begin{pmatrix}2&4\\4&2\end{pmatrix}$, with [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md)

$$
\det\begin{pmatrix}\lambda-2&-4\\-4&\lambda-2\end{pmatrix}=(\lambda-2)^2-16=(\lambda-6)(\lambda+2).
$$

Thus the three [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are $4,6,-2$. For $\lambda=6$, the last two components are equal; for $\lambda=-2$, they are opposite. Normalising by the [Euclidean norm](../../../../../../euclidean-norm.md) gives the complete list

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c}
\lambda&\text{unit eigenvector}\\\hline
4&(1,0,0)^T\\
6&(0,1,1)^T/\sqrt2\\
-2&(0,1,-1)^T/\sqrt2
\end{array}}
$$

Each sign-reversed [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) is an equally valid choice. The three displayed [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) are mutually [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md), as expected for a real [symmetric matrix](../../../../../../symmetric-matrix.md) with distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md).
