<h1 id="13/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $P=\mu(x)(xy-16x-x^3)$ and $Q=\mu(x)(16+x^2)$. An [exact differential form](../../../../../../exact-differential-form.md) must satisfy $P_y=Q_x$. These [partial derivatives](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md) give

$$
x\mu=(16+x^2)\mu'+2x\mu,\qquad \frac{\mu'}\mu=-\frac{x}{16+x^2}.
$$

Taking [antiderivatives](../../../../../../antiderivative.md) produces $\ln|\mu|=-\tfrac12\ln(16+x^2)+C$. A convenient nonzero [integrating factor for a differential one-form](../../../../../../integrating-factor-for-a-differential-one-form.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\mu(x)=\frac1{\sqrt{16+x^2}}.}
$$

An explicit potential, which also proves global exactness, is

$$
F(x,y)=y\sqrt{16+x^2}-\frac13(16+x^2)^{3/2}.
$$

Indeed $F_x=P$ and $F_y=Q$ for the chosen [integrating factor](../../../../../../integrating-factor.md).
