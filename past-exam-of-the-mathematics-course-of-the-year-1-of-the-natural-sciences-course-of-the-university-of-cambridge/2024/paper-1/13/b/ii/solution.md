<h1 id="13/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Multiplying the [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) by the nonzero [integrating factor](../../../../../../integrating-factor.md) from part (i) makes its left-hand side $dF(x,y(x))/dx$. Hence the potential is constant:

$$
y\sqrt{16+x^2}-\frac13(16+x^2)^{3/2}=C.
$$

Since $16+x^2>0$ for every real $x$, this is equivalent to the explicit general solution

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\frac{16+x^2}{3}+\frac{C}{\sqrt{16+x^2}},\qquad C\in\mathbb R.}
$$

It is defined for all real $x$. There are no lost solutions, since the [integrating factor](../../../../../../integrating-factor.md) and the coefficient of $y'$ are everywhere nonzero.
