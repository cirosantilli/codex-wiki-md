<h1 id="13/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The zero solution satisfies both the [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) and the [initial condition](../../../../../../initial-condition.md). The right-hand side is continuously differentiable in $y$ near $(x,y)=(2,0)$, so the [Picard-Lindelöf theorem](../../../../../../picard-lindelof-theorem.md) gives local uniqueness; equivalently the nonzero family in part (a) can never take the value zero at $x=2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{y(x)=0\quad\text{on }(0,\infty).}
$$

The restriction $x>0$ is the maximal [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) containing $2$ on which the original [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) is defined.
