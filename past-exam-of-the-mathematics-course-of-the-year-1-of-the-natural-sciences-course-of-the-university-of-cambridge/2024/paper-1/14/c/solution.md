<h1 id="14/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The scaling relation says that $z$ is a [homogeneous function](../../../../../homogeneous-function.md) of degree $n$. Fix $(x,y)$ and differentiate the identity with respect to the positive parameter $\lambda$. The [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives

$$
xz_x(\lambda x,\lambda y)+yz_y(\lambda x,\lambda y)=n\lambda^{n-1}z(x,y).
$$

Setting $\lambda=1$ proves the [Euler theorem for homogeneous functions](../../../../../euler-theorem-for-homogeneous-functions.md) identity

$$
\boxed{xz_x+yz_y=nz.}
$$

Differentiate once more with respect to $\lambda$, using equality of the mixed [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) for the assumed twice continuously differentiable [function](../../../../../function-split.md):

$$
x^2z_{xx}(\lambda x,\lambda y)+2xyz_{xy}(\lambda x,\lambda y)+y^2z_{yy}(\lambda x,\lambda y)=n(n-1)\lambda^{n-2}z(x,y).
$$

At $\lambda=1$ this is the [second-order Euler identity for homogeneous functions](../../../../../second-order-euler-identity-for-homogeneous-functions.md):

$$
\boxed{x^2z_{xx}+2xyz_{xy}+y^2z_{yy}=n(n-1)z.}
$$

The repeated parameter [differentiation](../../../../../differentiation.md) accounts for the factor $n(n-1)$, rather than $n^2$.
