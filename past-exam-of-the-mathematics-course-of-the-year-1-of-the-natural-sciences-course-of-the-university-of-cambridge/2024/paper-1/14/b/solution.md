<h1 id="14/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put $q=\varphi'(y-\beta z)$. Apply [implicit differentiation](../../../../../implicit-differentiation.md) with respect to $x$ and $y$, using the [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md):

$$
1-\alpha z_x=-\beta qz_x,\qquad -\alpha z_y=q(1-\beta z_y).
$$

Consequently

$$
(\alpha-\beta q)z_x=1,\qquad (\alpha-\beta q)z_y=-q.
$$

At a [point](../../../../../point-geometry.md) where the assumed [differentiable function](../../../../../differentiable-function.md) $z(x,y)$ exists, the first equation forces $\alpha-\beta q\ne0$. Combining the equations therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\alpha z_x+\beta z_y=\frac{\alpha-\beta q}{\alpha-\beta q}=1.}
$$

The assertion is understood on such differentiable branches of the [implicit function](../../../../../implicit-function.md); arbitrary parameter choices need not produce one everywhere.
