<h1 id="17/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Take $t=e^x>0$ in the [substitution rule](../../../../../integration-by-substitution.md), so $dx=dt/t$. The transformed [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) is

$$
\int\frac{t-2}{t^2+1}\,dt=\frac12\ln(t^2+1)-2\arctan t+C.
$$

The two terms follow from the [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) of the [natural logarithm](../../../../../natural-logarithm.md) and the [inverse tangent](../../../../../inverse-tangent.md). Returning to $x$ gives the [antiderivative](../../../../../antiderivative.md)

$$
\boxed{\frac12\ln(1+e^{2x})-2\arctan(e^x)+C.}
$$
