↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The displacement [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) are $B-A=(-1,2,-2)$ and $C-A=(1,0,1)$. Their [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) is $(2,-1,-2)$, with [Euclidean norm](../../../../../euclidean-norm.md) three. Thus either sign of

$$
\boxed{\hat n_2=\frac{(2,-1,-2)}3}
$$

is a [unit normal vector](../../../../../unit-normal.md) to the second [plane](../../../../../plane.md), whose equation is $2x-y-2z=3$. A [direction vector](../../../../../direction-vector.md) for the intersection is the [cross product](../../../../../cross-product.md) of the two [normal vectors](../../../../../normal-vector.md):

$$
\boxed{(-1,-2,2)\times(2,-1,-2)=(6,2,5).}
$$

It is [orthogonal](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) to each [normal vector](../../../../../normal-vector.md). Since both [planes](../../../../../plane.md) contain $A$, the whole intersection line is $r=(2,-1,1)+t(6,2,5)$, $t\in\mathbb R$.
