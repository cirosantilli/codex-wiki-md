<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Take the outward orientation on the upper [hemisphere](../../../../../hemisphere.md); its boundary [circle](../../../../../circle.md) is then counterclockwise viewed from above. Parametrize that [circle](../../../../../circle.md) by $r(\phi)=(r\cos\phi,r\sin\phi,0)$, $0\leq\phi\leq2\pi$. Then $G\cdot dr=r^2(3\cos^2\phi-4\sin^2\phi)d\phi$, so [Stokes theorem](../../../../../stokes-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\int_S(\nabla\times G)\cdot dS=\int_0^{2\pi}r^2(3\cos^2\phi-4\sin^2\phi)d\phi=-\pi r^2.}
$$

Equivalently $\nabla\times G=(0,0,-1)$, and the upward projected area of the [hemisphere](../../../../../hemisphere.md) is $\pi r^2$. The opposite orientation gives the negative of this answer.
