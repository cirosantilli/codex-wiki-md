<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md) is $(5-\lambda)(2-\lambda)-4=\lambda^2-7\lambda+6=(\lambda-1)(\lambda-6)$. For $\lambda=1$, the [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) equation gives $y=2x$; for $\lambda=6$, it gives $x=-2y$. The normalized [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) can therefore be chosen as

$$
\boxed{\lambda_1=1,\quad v_1=\frac{(1,2)}{\sqrt5};\qquad\lambda_2=6,\quad v_2=\frac{(-2,1)}{\sqrt5}.}
$$

Both have unit [Euclidean norm](../../../../../euclidean-norm.md), and their [dot product](../../../../../dot-product.md) is $(-2+2)/5=0$, explicitly verifying [orthogonality](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md). This also follows from the [spectral theorem](../../../../../spectral-theorem.md) for a real [symmetric matrix](../../../../../symmetric-matrix.md) with distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md).
