<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $D$ denote having the [disease](../../../../../disease.md) and $+$ a positive result. Assume I am sampled uniformly from the stated population, so $\mathbb P(D)=n/N$. By the [law of total probability](../../../../../law-of-total-probability.md), $\mathbb P(+)=a n/N+b(N-n)/N$. Thus [Bayes' theorem](../../../../../bayes-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(D\mid+)=\frac{an}{an+b(N-n)}.}
$$

The denominator must be positive; otherwise the observed event has zero [probability](../../../../../probability.md) in this model and the [conditional probability](../../../../../conditional-probability.md) is undefined. The answer depends on [disease](../../../../../disease.md) prevalence as well as the test's two error rates.
