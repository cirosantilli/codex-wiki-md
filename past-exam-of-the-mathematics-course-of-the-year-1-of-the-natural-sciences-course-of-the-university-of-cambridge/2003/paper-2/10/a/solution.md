<h1 id="10/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Away from the origin, the [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives $r_x=x/r$ and $r_y=y/r$. Differentiating once more yields the two-dimensional case of the [Hessian of the Euclidean norm](../../../../../hessian-of-the-euclidean-norm.md):

$$
\boxed{r_x=\frac xr,\quad r_y=\frac yr,\quad r_{xx}=\frac{y^2}{r^3},\quad r_{yy}=\frac{x^2}{r^3},\quad r_{xy}=-\frac{xy}{r^3}.}
$$

These [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) are smooth wherever $r>0$, so [Clairaut's theorem](../../../../../symmetry-of-second-derivatives.md) gives $\boxed{r_{yx}=r_{xy}=-xy/r^3}$. None of these formulas supplies a [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) at the origin: even the restriction $r(x,0)=|x|$ has no two-sided [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) there.
