<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For $f(x)=x^3e^{-3x^2/2}/3$, the [product rule](../../../../../product-rule.md) gives $f'(x)=x^2(1-x^2)e^{-3x^2/2}$, so the [stationary points](../../../../../stationary-point.md) occur at $x=-1,0,1$. Its sign is negative for $|x|>1$ and positive for $0<|x|<1$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\left(-1,-\frac{e^{-3/2}}3\right)\text{ is a minimum},\qquad\left(1,\frac{e^{-3/2}}3\right)\text{ is a maximum}.}
$$

They are also the global [extrema](../../../../../maximum-and-minimum.md), since the function tends to zero at both infinities and is a [monotone function](../../../../../monotonic-function.md) on the intervals just identified. At zero the [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) does not change sign. Since $f(x)=x^3/3+O(x^5)$, **$(0,0)$ is a stationary [inflection point](../../../../../inflection-point.md)**, not an [extremum](../../../../../maximum-and-minimum.md).
