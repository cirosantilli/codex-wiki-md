<h1 id="18/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Since $\boldsymbol a$ is constant, componentwise differentiation gives $\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol a\phi)=\sum_ia_i\partial_i\phi=\boldsymbol a\cdot\nabla\phi$. Applying the [divergence theorem](../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) to this [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md) gives

$$
\boldsymbol a\cdot\int_S\phi\,d\boldsymbol S=\boldsymbol a\cdot\int_V\nabla\phi\,dV.
$$

This holds for every constant [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $\boldsymbol a$, so the vector integrals agree. This is the [vector gradient form of the divergence theorem](../../../../../vector-gradient-form-of-the-divergence-theorem.md). With $\phi=z$, the [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) is the constant unit vector $\boldsymbol e_z$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\int_S z\,d\boldsymbol S=\frac{4\pi a^2b}3\,\boldsymbol e_z.}
$$
