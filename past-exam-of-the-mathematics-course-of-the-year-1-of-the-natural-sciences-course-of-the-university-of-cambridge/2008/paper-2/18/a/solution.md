<h1 id="18/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a bounded region $V$ with piecewise smooth closed boundary $S$, outward unit [normal vector](../../../../../normal-vector.md) $\boldsymbol n$ and continuously differentiable [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md) $\boldsymbol F$, the [divergence theorem](../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) states $\int_S\boldsymbol F\cdot\boldsymbol n\,dS=\int_V\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol F\,dV$. Here $dS$ is scalar [surface area](../../../../../surface-area.md), $dV$ is [volume](../../../../../volume.md), and $d\boldsymbol S=\boldsymbol n\,dS$ is the oriented [area element](../../../../../area-element-of-a-surface.md).

For $\boldsymbol F=\boldsymbol r$, its [divergence](../../../../../divergence.md) is three. The [ellipsoid](../../../../../ellipsoid.md) has semiaxes $a,a,b$, so its [volume](../../../../../volume.md) is $4\pi a^2b/3$, obtained by scaling the unit ball. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\int_S\boldsymbol r\cdot d\boldsymbol S=3\operatorname{Vol}(V)=4\pi a^2b.}
$$
