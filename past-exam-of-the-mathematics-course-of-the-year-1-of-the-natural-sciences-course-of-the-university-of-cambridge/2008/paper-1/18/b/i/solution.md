<h1 id="18/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [Leibniz formula for determinants](../../../../../../leibniz-formula-for-determinants.md) gives $\det(A^T)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_3}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_i a_{\sigma(i),i}$. Relabel the product by $j=\sigma(i)$ and then the sum by $\tau=\sigma^{-1}$. The inverse [permutation](../../../../../../permutation.md) has the same sign, so

$$
\det(A^T)=\sum_{\tau\in S_3}\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\prod_j a_{j,\tau(j)}=\det A.
$$

The proof applies to any finite square [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md).
