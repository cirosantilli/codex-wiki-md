<h1 id="16/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [separable differential equation](../../../../../../separable-differential-equation.md) gives $d(1-y)/(1-y)=-3\,dx/x$. Thus $1-y=Cx^{-3}$ on $x>0$, and the initial value gives $C=1$. Hence

$$
\boxed{y=1-x^{-3},\qquad x>0.}
$$

The initial-value branch cannot cross its singularity at zero.
