<h1 id="14/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The base is the disk $D:(x-a)^2+y^2\leq a^2$, on which $x\geq0$. The vertical thickness is $(p-q)x$. Using the symmetry of the disk about $x=a$, or integrating with $x=a+r\cos\phi$, gives

$$
V=(p-q)\iint_Dx\,dA=(p-q)a\,\pi a^2,
\qquad \boxed{V=\pi a^3(p-q).}
$$

For the curved cylindrical surface use $x=a(1+\cos\phi)$, $y=a\sin\phi$, with $0\leq\phi\leq2\pi$ and $qx\leq z\leq px$. The [area element](../../../../../area-element-of-a-surface.md) is $a\,d\phi\,dz$. Its [surface area](../../../../../surface-area.md) is therefore

$$
A=\int_0^{2\pi}a(p-q)a(1+\cos\phi)\,d\phi,
\qquad \boxed{A=2\pi a^2(p-q).}
$$

The sloping planar faces are excluded from this curved [surface area](../../../../../surface-area.md).
