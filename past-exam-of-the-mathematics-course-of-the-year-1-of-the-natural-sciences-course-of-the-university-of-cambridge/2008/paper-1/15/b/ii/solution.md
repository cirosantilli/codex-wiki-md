<h1 id="15/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For large $\sigma$, neighboring peaks overlap and the density has a broad, smooth, symmetric central hump, approximately $(\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma)^{-1}e^{-z^2/(2\sigma^2)}$ on the scale $z=O(\sigma)$. A precise central-profile statement follows from

$$
\sigma g(\sigma t)=\mathbb E\left[\frac{e^{-(t-X/\sigma)^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right]\longrightarrow\frac{e^{-t^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}:
$$

for each fixed $t$, $X/\sigma\to0$ almost surely and the integrand is bounded, so [dominated convergence](../../../../../../dominated-convergence-theorem.md) applies. The right panel of the preceding figure shows this Gaussian-looking core.

This approximation does not remove the [heavy-tailed distribution](../../../../../../heavy-tailed-distribution.md). Here $\mathbb E|X|=(6/\pi^2)\sum_{n\geq1}1/n=\infty$, so the symmetric distribution has **no ordinary mean and no finite variance**. Adding the independent Gaussian, which has finite absolute moments, leaves these conclusions unchanged. In particular a central Gaussian sketch cannot justify assigning variance $\sigma^2$ to $Z$.
