<h1 id="13/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For $|t|<1$, the [geometric series](../../../../../geometric-series.md) gives $(1+t)^{-1}=\sum_{n\geq0}(-t)^n$. Its uniform convergence on every closed interval strictly inside $(-1,1)$ permits [termwise integration](../../../../../termwise-integration.md) from zero to $x$. Therefore

$$
\log(1+x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}x^n}{n},\qquad
\log(1-x)=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{x^n}{n}.
$$

Subtracting cancels every even power and doubles every odd power, giving the [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md)

$$
\boxed{\log\frac{1+x}{1-x}=2\sum_{j=0}^\infty\frac{x^{2j+1}}{2j+1},\qquad |x|<1.}
$$
