<h1 id="11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Collecting the coefficients gives the [coefficient matrix](../../../../../coefficient-matrix.md)

$$
\boxed{A=\begin{pmatrix}a&0&2\\0&b+1&c-1\\0&c+1&b-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

A square [homogeneous linear system](../../../../../homogeneous-linear-system.md) has a nonzero solution precisely when its [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) is a [singular matrix](../../../../../singular-matrix.md). Expanding the [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) down the first column,

$$
\det A=a[(b+1)(b-1)-(c-1)(c+1)]=a(b^2-c^2).
$$

Hence the necessary and sufficient condition is **$a=0$ or $b=c$ or $b=-c$**. A zero [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) gives a nontrivial [kernel of a linear map](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md); otherwise multiplication by the [matrix inverse](../../../../../matrix-inverse-split.md) would force the zero solution.
