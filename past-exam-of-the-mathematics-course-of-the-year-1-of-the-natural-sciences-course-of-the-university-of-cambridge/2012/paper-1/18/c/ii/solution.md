<h1 id="18/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Repeated [integration by parts](../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) gives, for every nonnegative integer $j$,

$$
\int_0^\infty x^j e^{-\lambda x}dx=\frac{j!}{\lambda^{j+1}}.
$$

With $A=\lambda^2$, the first two [moments](../../../../../../moment.md) are $\mathbb E[X]=\lambda^2(2!/\lambda^3)=2/\lambda$ and $\mathbb E[X^2]=\lambda^2(3!/\lambda^4)=6/\lambda^2$. Subtracting the square of the [mean](../../../../../../expected-value-split.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\mu=\frac2\lambda,\qquad\sigma^2=\frac2{\lambda^2},\qquad\sigma=\frac{\sqrt2}{\lambda}.}
$$

If $X$ carries units, $\lambda$ has inverse units of $X$, so the [mean](../../../../../../expected-value-split.md) and [standard deviation](../../../../../../standard-deviation.md) have the units of $X$ and the [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md) has their square.
