<h1 id="16/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [directional derivative](../../../../../directional-derivative.md) measures change per unit distance, so first normalize the direction: $\widehat v=(4,3)/5$. The [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) is

$$
\nabla f=-\frac{2(x,y)}{(1+x^2+y^2)^2},\qquad
\nabla f(1,0)=(-1/2,0).
$$

Taking the [dot product](../../../../../dot-product.md) with the [unit vector](../../../../../unit-vector.md),

$$
\boxed{D_{\widehat v}f(1,0)=(-1/2,0)\cdot(4/5,3/5)=-\frac25.}
$$

Using the unnormalized [vector](../../../../../vector.md) would instead give $-2$, the change per unit parameter along $(1,0)+tv$; it is not the rate per unit distance requested here.
