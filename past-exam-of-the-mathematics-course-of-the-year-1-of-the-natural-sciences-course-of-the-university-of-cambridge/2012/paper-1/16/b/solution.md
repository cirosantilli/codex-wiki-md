<h1 id="16/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [product rule](../../../../../product-rule.md) gives the two [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md)

$$
g_x=x(2-x^2+y^2)e^{-(x^2+y^2)/2},\qquad
g_y=-y(2+x^2-y^2)e^{-(x^2+y^2)/2}.
$$

They both vanish at the origin. The [exponential function](../../../../../exponential-function.md) is never zero, so any further [stationary point](../../../../../stationary-point.md) must satisfy $x=0$ or $x^2-y^2=2$, and $y=0$ or $y^2-x^2=2$. Having both coordinates nonzero would require both opposite differences to be $2$, which is impossible. If $y=0$, the nonzero choices are $x=\pm\sqrt2$; if $x=0$, they are $y=\pm\sqrt2$. Hence the full list is

$$
\boxed{(0,0),\quad(\pm\sqrt2,0),\quad(0,\pm\sqrt2).}
$$
