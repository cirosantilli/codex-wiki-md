<h1 id="13/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Work on an [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) with $x\ne0$. The [homogeneous first-order differential equation](../../../../../homogeneous-first-order-differential-equation.md) becomes a [separable differential equation](../../../../../separable-differential-equation.md) under $v=y/x$: substituting $y=xv$ gives $v+xv'=v^2+v$, hence $xv'=v^2$.

For $v\ne0$, [integration](../../../../../integral-split.md) gives $-1/v=\ln|x|+C$. Renaming the arbitrary constant produces

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\frac{x}{C-\ln|x|},\qquad\text{or}\qquad y(x)\equiv0.}
$$

For the nonzero family the denominator must remain nonzero, so take any connected [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) avoiding both $x=0$ and $|x|=e^C$. The zero solution must be restored after division by $v^2$; by the [Picard-Lindelöf theorem](../../../../../picard-lindelof-theorem.md) away from $x=0$, a solution cannot switch between zero and a nonzero branch there.
