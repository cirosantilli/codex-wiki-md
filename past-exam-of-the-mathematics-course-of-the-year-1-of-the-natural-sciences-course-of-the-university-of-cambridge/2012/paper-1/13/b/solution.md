<h1 id="13/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $M=x+y$ and $N=x$. Their cross [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) agree: $M_y=N_x=1$. More directly, construct the [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md) [function](../../../../../function-split.md)

$$
F(x,y)=\frac{x^2}{2}+xy,\qquad dF=(x+y)\,dx+x\,dy.
$$

This exhibits the expression as an [exact differential](../../../../../exact-differential.md). Along a solution of the [differential equation](../../../../../differential-equation-split.md),

$$
\frac d{dx}F(x,y(x))=x+y+xy'=0.
$$

Thus the [general solution](../../../../../general-solution.md) is

$$
\boxed{\frac{x^2}{2}+xy=C,\qquad y=\frac Cx-\frac x2\quad(x\ne0).}
$$

For $C=0$, the graph $y=-x/2$ also extends smoothly through $x=0$ and satisfies the original, undivided equation there. Nonzero $C$ cannot extend through zero as a finite-valued [differentiable](../../../../../differentiable-function.md) solution. The extra vertical component $x=0$ of the implicit zero-level equation is not a graph $y(x)$ on an [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) and is not an additional solution of this [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md).
