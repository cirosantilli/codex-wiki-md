<h1 id="14/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The squared [Euclidean distance](../../../../../euclidean-distance.md) along the [line](../../../../../straight-line.md) is

$$
|a+td|^2=|a|^2+2t(a\cdot d)+t^2|d|^2,\qquad d=b\times c.
$$

Its minimum occurs at $t=0$ exactly when its [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) there vanishes, so **$a\cdot(b\times c)=0$**. Since $b,c$ are independent, their [linear span](../../../../../linear-span.md) is the two-dimensional [orthogonal complement](../../../../../orthogonal-complement.md) of $b\times c$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{a=\alpha b+\beta c}
$$

for unique real $\alpha,\beta$. This is the [closest point on the intersection of two planes](../../../../../closest-point-on-the-intersection-of-two-planes.md); the positive quadratic coefficient $|d|^2$ proves that the stationary distance is a strict [global minimum](../../../../../global-minimum.md).
