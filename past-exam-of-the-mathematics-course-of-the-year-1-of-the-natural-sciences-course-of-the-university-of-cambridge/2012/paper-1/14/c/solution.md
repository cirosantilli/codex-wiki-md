<h1 id="14/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Apply the [vector triple product identity](../../../../../vector-triple-product.md) and the two [plane](../../../../../plane.md) constraints:

$$
\boxed{r\times(b\times c)=b(r\cdot c)-c(r\cdot b)=\mu b-\lambda c.}
$$

Put $d=b\times c\ne0$. If $a$ is one intersection point, subtraction gives $(r-a)\times d=0$, so $r-a$ is parallel to $d$. Conversely, adding any multiple of $d$ preserves both [plane](../../../../../plane.md) constraints because $d$ is [orthogonal](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) to both [normal vectors](../../../../../normal-vector.md). The whole intersection is therefore

$$
\boxed{r=a+t(b\times c),\quad t\in\mathbb R,\qquad a\cdot b=\lambda,\quad a\cdot c=\mu.}
$$

Such an $a$ exists: choose $a=\alpha b+\beta c$ and solve the two-by-two [linear system](../../../../../system-of-linear-equations.md) with [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) $|b|^2|c|^2-(b\cdot c)^2=|b\times c|^2>0$. This also proves sufficiency rather than assuming a nonempty intersection.
