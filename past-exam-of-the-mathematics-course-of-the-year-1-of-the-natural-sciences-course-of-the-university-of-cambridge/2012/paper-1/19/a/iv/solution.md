<h1 id="19/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Insert $u=-1/x^2$ into the [exponential series](../../../../../../exponential-series.md) $e^u=\sum_{n=0}^\infty u^n/n!$. For every fixed $x\ne0$, this gives the expansion with [absolute convergence](../../../../../../absolute-convergence.md)

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{n!\,x^{2n}},\qquad a_n=\frac{(-1)^n}{n!}.}
$$

This is a [Laurent series](../../../../../../laurent-series.md) in inverse powers of $x$, as requested. It is not a [Maclaurin series](../../../../../../maclaurin-series.md) about zero: that series has every coefficient zero and cannot equal $f$ on a punctured neighborhood of zero. There is no contradiction, since the inverse-power expansion does not apply at $x=0$.
