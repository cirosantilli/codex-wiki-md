<h1 id="19/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

As $x\to0$ through nonzero values, $1/x^2\to+\infty$ and $e^{-1/x^2}\to0=f(0)$. Thus $f$ is [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at zero.

For the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) at zero use its definition, rather than substituting into a formula obtained away from zero:

$$
f'(0)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{e^{-1/h^2}}h=0.
$$

Indeed, putting $t=1/|h|$ makes the [absolute value](../../../../../../absolute-value.md) $te^{-t^2}$, which tends to zero since [Gaussian decay dominates every power](../../../../../../gaussian-decay-dominates-every-power.md). Therefore **$f$ is [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) and [differentiable](../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at zero, with $f'(0)=0$**. Away from zero, the [chain rule](../../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives $f'(x)=2x^{-3}e^{-1/x^2}$.
