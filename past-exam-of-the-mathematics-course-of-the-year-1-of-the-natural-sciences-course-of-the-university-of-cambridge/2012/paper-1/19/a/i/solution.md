↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**[Continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md).** The [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) is [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at $x_0$ when $\lim_{x\to x_0}f(x)=f(x_0)$. Equivalently, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that $|x-x_0|<\delta$ within the [domain of a function](../../../../../../domain-of-a-function.md) implies $|f(x)-f(x_0)|<\varepsilon$; this is the [epsilon-delta definition of continuity](../../../../../../epsilon-delta-definition-of-continuity.md).

**[Differentiability](../../../../../../differentiability.md).** At an interior point $x_0$, the [difference quotient](../../../../../../difference-quotient.md) must have a finite two-sided [limit of a function](../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md):

$$
f'(x_0)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x_0+h)-f(x_0)}h.
$$

This defines [differentiability](../../../../../../differentiability.md) and the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md). At an endpoint one may instead state a one-sided definition relative to the [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md). [Differentiability implies continuity](../../../../../../differentiability-implies-continuity.md), because the increment is $h$ times a quotient with finite limit, but [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) alone does not guarantee [differentiability](../../../../../../differentiability.md).
