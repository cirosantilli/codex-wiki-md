<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

By the [polynomial remainder theorem](../../../../polynomial-remainder-theorem.md), the first [polynomial remainder](../../../../remainder-of-polynomial-division.md) is $f(1)=23-2p$. Equating it to $9$ gives **$p=7$**.

For the separate [polynomial long division](../../../../polynomial-long-division.md), successively cancel the leading powers of the dividend. Subtracting $x^2(x-1)$ leaves $2x^2+2x+1$; subtracting $2x(x-1)$ leaves $4x+1$; subtracting $4(x-1)$ leaves $5$. Thus

$$
x^3+x^2+2x+1=(x-1)(x^2+2x+4)+5,
\qquad\boxed{\text{quotient }x^2+2x+4,\quad\text{remainder }5.}
$$

The [polynomial remainder](../../../../remainder-of-polynomial-division.md) also equals the dividend evaluated at $x=1$.
