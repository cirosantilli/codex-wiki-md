<h1 id="12/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Set $z=e^{i\theta}$, so $2i\sin\theta=z-z^{-1}$. The [binomial theorem](../../../../../binomial-theorem.md) gives

$$
(2i\sin\theta)^5
=(z^5-z^{-5})-5(z^3-z^{-3})+10(z-z^{-1}).
$$

Use $z^k-z^{-k}=2i\sin(k\theta)$ and $(2i)^5=32i$ to obtain

$$
\boxed{\sin^5\theta=\frac{10\sin\theta-5\sin3\theta+\sin5\theta}{16}.}
$$

This is a finite [Fourier sine series](../../../../../fourier-sine-series.md), with only odd harmonics.
