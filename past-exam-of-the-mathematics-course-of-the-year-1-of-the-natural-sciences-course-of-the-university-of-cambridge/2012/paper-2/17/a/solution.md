<h1 id="17/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A cubic version of [Taylor's theorem](../../../../../taylor-s-theorem.md) requires the corresponding higher differentiability, rather than only one [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md). For example, if $f$ has four continuous [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) on the interval between $a$ and $x$, then

$$
f(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)+\frac{f''(a)}{2!}(x-a)^2
+\frac{f^{(3)}(a)}{3!}(x-a)^3+R_3(x),
$$

where the Lagrange form of the [Taylor remainder](../../../../../taylor-remainder.md) is

$$
\boxed{R_3(x)=\frac{f^{(4)}(\xi)}{4!}(x-a)^4}
$$

for some $\xi$ strictly between $a$ and $x$ when they differ. At $x=a$, the remainder is zero. These hypotheses apply to both functions used below.
