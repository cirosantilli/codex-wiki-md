<h1 id="17/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The required [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) are $f'(x)=1/(2\sqrt x)$, $f''(x)=-1/(4x^{3/2})$, $f^{(3)}(x)=3/(8x^{5/2})$, and $f^{(4)}(x)=-15/(16x^{7/2})$. The four-term [Taylor polynomial](../../../../../taylor-polynomial.md) about one, evaluated at two, is

$$
\boxed{\sqrt2\approx1+\frac12-\frac18+\frac1{16}=\frac{23}{16}}.
$$

The [Taylor remainder](../../../../../taylor-remainder.md) is $R_3(2)=-5/(128\xi^{7/2})$ for some $1<\xi<2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\left|\sqrt2-\frac{23}{16}\right|\leq\frac5{128}}.
$$

Its negative sign also shows that this approximation is an overestimate. Bounding the fourth [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) throughout the interval establishes the requested error estimate without using a decimal value of $\sqrt2$.
