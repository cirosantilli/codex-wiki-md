<h1 id="15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Inside the [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md), the [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) has the form $\mathbf E=\nabla[-kr^2/(2a^3)]$, including at the origin. Thus it is a [conservative vector field](../../../../../conservative-vector-field.md). Outside, use the [gradient of a radial function](../../../../../gradient-of-a-radial-function.md) to obtain

$$
\mathbf E=k\nabla\frac1r=-\frac{k\mathbf r}{r^3}.
$$

This is also an explicit [potential of a conservative vector field](../../../../../potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md), single-valued throughout the exterior region. Equivalently, the [curl](../../../../../curl.md) vanishes in both regions: for a radial coefficient $h(r)$, $\nabla\times(h\mathbf r)=\nabla h\times\mathbf r+h\nabla\times\mathbf r=0$ because $\nabla h$ is radial. The interior formula avoids any apparent $1/r$ issue at the origin. The vector-field potentials here have the opposite sign from the [electric potential](../../../../../electric-potential.md) convention used in (b).
