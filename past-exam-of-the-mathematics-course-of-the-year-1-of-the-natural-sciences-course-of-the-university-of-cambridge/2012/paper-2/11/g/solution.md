<h1 id="11/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

The [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) equations give $AB=B\operatorname{diag}(4,-2,-2)$. Multiplying on the left by $B^{-1}$ therefore gives

$$
\boxed{B^{-1}AB=\begin{pmatrix}4&0&0\\0&-2&0\\0&0&-2\end{pmatrix}}.
$$

This is an [orthogonal diagonalization](../../../../../orthogonal-diagonalization-of-a-real-symmetric-matrix.md): the [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md) acts independently by multiplication by its [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) in the new [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md). The repeated [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) explains the freedom to rotate the last two basis directions.
