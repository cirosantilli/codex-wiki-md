<h1 id="20/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

This is a [Bernoulli differential equation](../../../../../../bernoulli-differential-equation.md) with exponent four. On the nonzero branch selected by $y(0)=1$, put $v=y^{-3}$. Multiplying the original equation by $-3y^{-4}$ gives

$$
v'-v=-3e^x.
$$

Its [integrating factor](../../../../../../integrating-factor.md) is $e^{-x}$, so $(e^{-x}v)'=-3$ and $e^{-x}v=C-3x$. The [initial condition](../../../../../../initial-condition.md) sets $C=1$. Thus

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\frac{e^{-x/3}}{(1-3x)^{1/3}},\qquad -\infty<x<\frac13}.
$$

This is the maximal real solution interval containing zero: the solution diverges as $x\to(1/3)^-$. The real negative branch of the same algebraic formula for $x>1/3$ is disconnected from this initial-value solution. The original equation also admits $y=0$, but it does not satisfy the prescribed [initial condition](../../../../../../initial-condition.md).
