<h1 id="11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) of centre $\mathbf a$ and radius $p$ has equation

$$
\boxed{|\mathbf r-\mathbf a|^2=p^2}.
$$

Let $d=|\mathbf b-\mathbf a|$ be the distance between the centres. A nondegenerate [circle of intersection of two spheres](../../../../../circle-of-intersection-of-two-spheres.md) exists exactly when

$$
\boxed{|p-q|<d<p+q}.
$$

The upper inequality excludes disjoint or externally tangent [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md). The lower excludes strict containment and internal tangency. It also excludes coincident centres, which give either no intersection or an entire common [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) rather than one [circle](../../../../../circle.md). These inequalities are sufficient because a triangle with side lengths $p,q,d$ then exists with nonzero area, and rotating its third vertex about the line of centres traces a [circle](../../../../../circle.md).

For a point on both [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md), expand their equations:

$$
\mathbf r^2-2\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf r+a^2=p^2,\qquad
\mathbf r^2-2\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf r+b^2=q^2.
$$

Subtracting cancels $\mathbf r^2$ and gives the [plane](../../../../../plane.md)

$$
\boxed{2(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)\cdot\mathbf r=p^2-q^2+b^2-a^2}.
$$

Its normal is parallel to the line of centres. Under the strict inequalities, the [plane](../../../../../plane.md) cuts either [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) in a genuine [circle](../../../../../circle.md).
