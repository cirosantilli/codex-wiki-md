<h1 id="19/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

As in the diagram, an inscribed [cylinder](../../../../../cylinder-geometry.md) rests on $z=0$, is coaxial with the paraboloid, and has its top rim on the curved boundary. Thus

$$
h+\frac{Hr^2}{R^2}=H,\qquad V_c=\pi r^2h.
$$

Allowing contact with the boundary is the usual meaning of _inscribed_. Form the [Lagrange multiplier](../../../../../lagrange-multiplier.md) [function](../../../../../function-split.md)

$$
\mathcal L=\pi r^2h-\lambda\left(h+\frac{Hr^2}{R^2}-H\right).
$$

At a nondegenerate stationary [cylinder](../../../../../cylinder-geometry.md), its two [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) equations are

$$
2\pi rh=\lambda\frac{2Hr}{R^2},\qquad \pi r^2=\lambda.
$$

Eliminating $\lambda$ gives $h=Hr^2/R^2$. The [constraint](../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md) then gives $h=H/2$ and $r=R/\sqrt2$, so

$$
\boxed{V_{c,\max}=\frac{\pi R^2H}{4}}.
$$

The degenerate endpoints $r=0$ or $h=0$ have zero [volume](../../../../../volume.md), consistent with the positive stationary value being the [maximum](../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md). If the strict inequalities defining the open interior were interpreted as forbidding boundary contact, the same value would instead be a supremum approached by smaller cylinders.
