<h1 id="13/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Parametrize the path by $\mathbf r(t)=(x(t),y(t),z(t))$, from $t=a$ to $t=b$. For the [conservative vector field](../../../../../conservative-vector-field.md) $\mathbf F=\nabla\Phi$, the [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives

$$
\mathbf F\cdot\frac{d\mathbf r}{dt}
=\Phi_x\frac{dx}{dt}+\Phi_y\frac{dy}{dt}+\Phi_z\frac{dz}{dt}
=\frac{d}{dt}\Phi(\mathbf r(t)).
$$

Integrating proves the [fundamental theorem for line integrals](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-for-line-integrals.md):

$$
\boxed{\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\Phi(\mathbf r(b))-\Phi(\mathbf r(a))}.
$$

Only the endpoints matter, so the integral is independent of the connecting path.
