<h1 id="15/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

With the first $n$ terms counted from the constant term, [Taylor's theorem](../../../../../taylor-s-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k+R_n(x),\qquad
R_n(x)=\frac{f^{(n)}(\xi)}{n!}(x-a)^n,}
$$

where $\xi$ lies between $a$ and $x$. Continuous [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) through order $n$ on the joining interval are a sufficient hypothesis for this [Taylor theorem with Lagrange remainder](../../../../../taylor-theorem-with-lagrange-remainder.md). The [Taylor remainder](../../../../../taylor-remainder.md) measures the error of the finite [Taylor polynomial](../../../../../taylor-polynomial.md); convergence of the corresponding infinite [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) is a separate question.
