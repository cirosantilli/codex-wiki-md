<h1 id="12/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Differentiate the defining function with the other coordinates independent. Put $C=\cosh(x+z)$ for brevity. Then

$$
F_x=yz-C,\qquad F_y=xz,\qquad F_z=xy-C.
$$

The [implicit differentiation](../../../../../../implicit-differentiation.md) ratios therefore give

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{\partial x}{\partial y}\right)_z=-\frac{xz}{yz-C},\quad
\left(\frac{\partial x}{\partial z}\right)_y=-\frac{xy-C}{yz-C},\quad
\left(\frac{\partial y}{\partial z}\right)_x=-\frac{xy-C}{xz}.}
$$

Each expression applies on the implicit surface wherever its own denominator is nonzero. The [hyperbolic cosine](../../../../../../hyperbolic-cosine.md) appears because the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) of $\sinh(x+z)$ with respect to either $x$ or $z$ is $\cosh(x+z)$.
