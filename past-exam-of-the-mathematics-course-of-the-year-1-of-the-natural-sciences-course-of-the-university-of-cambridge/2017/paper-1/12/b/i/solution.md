<h1 id="12/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

There is a global-domain issue in the printed question. Write $D=a^2x^2+b^2y^2>0$ away from the origin. Direct [partial differentiation](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md) gives

$$
P_y=-\frac{ab(a^2x^2-b^2y^2)}{D^2}
=Q_x.
$$

Thus the [differential one-form](../../../../../../one-form.md) is a [closed differential form](../../../../../../closed-differential-form.md) for **every real $a,b\ne0$**. The [Poincaré lemma](../../../../../../poincare-lemma.md) makes it locally an [exact differential form](../../../../../../exact-differential-form.md) on small neighborhoods avoiding the origin. There is no additional condition such as $a^2=b^2$.

However, the stated domain is the whole [punctured plane](../../../../../../punctured-complex-plane.md), which is not [simply connected](../../../../../../simply-connected-space.md). To test global exactness, use the closed [ellipse](../../../../../../ellipse.md) $x=\cos t/a$, $y=\sin t/b$, $0\leq t\leq2\pi$. Here $D=1$ and

$$
P\,dx+Q\,dy=ab(x\,dy-y\,dx)=dt,\qquad
\oint(P\,dx+Q\,dy)=2\pi.
$$

The [line integral](../../../../../../line-integral.md) of a globally [exact differential form](../../../../../../exact-differential-form.md) around any closed curve would be zero. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\text{globally exact on }\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}:\ \text{no allowed }a,b;\qquad
\text{locally exact}:\ \text{all }a,b\ne0.}
$$

The distinction is precisely that of the [elliptically scaled angular one-form](../../../../../../elliptically-scaled-angular-one-form.md). The remaining parts can be answered consistently using a local angle branch, as follows.
