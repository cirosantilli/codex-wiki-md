<h1 id="11/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A constant [real part](../../../../../real-part.md) $\operatorname{Re}\ln z=L$ means $\ln|z|=L$, so its [level sets](../../../../../level-set.md) are the concentric [circles](../../../../../circle.md)

$$
\boxed{x^2+y^2=e^{2L},\qquad L\in\mathbb R.}
$$

Their radii are strictly positive. The [real part](../../../../../real-part.md) is independent of the logarithm's branch, so there is no branch ambiguity in these [circles](../../../../../circle.md).

For $\operatorname{Re}(1/z)=K$, the equation is $x=K(x^2+y^2)$. If $K\ne0$, complete the square to obtain

$$
\boxed{\left(x-\frac1{2K}\right)^2+y^2=\frac1{4K^2},\qquad (x,y)\ne(0,0).}
$$

These [circles](../../../../../circle.md) have centres on the real axis and are tangent to the imaginary axis at the excluded origin. Positive $K$ puts the [circle](../../../../../circle.md) to the right, negative $K$ to the left. The separate case $K=0$ gives the punctured imaginary axis $\boxed{x=0,\ y\ne0}$. The sketch uses an open marker at the origin to emphasize that $1/z$ is never defined there.
