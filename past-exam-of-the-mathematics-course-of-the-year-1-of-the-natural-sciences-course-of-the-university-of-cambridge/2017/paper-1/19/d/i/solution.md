<h1 id="19/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The function $g(x)=1/(x\ln x)$ is positive and decreasing for $x\geq2$, since $g'(x)=-(\ln x+1)/(x^2\ln^2x)<0$. The [integral test for convergence](../../../../../../integral-test-for-convergence.md) therefore applies. Its comparison [improper integral](../../../../../../improper-integral.md) is

$$
\int_2^R\frac{dx}{x\ln x}=\ln(\ln R)-\ln(\ln2)\longrightarrow+\infty.
$$

Consequently **$\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}1/(n\ln n)$ diverges**.
