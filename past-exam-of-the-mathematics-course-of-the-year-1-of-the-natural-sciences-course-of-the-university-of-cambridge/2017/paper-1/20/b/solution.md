<h1 id="20/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The seven requested [parabolas](../../../../../parabola.md) are $y=cx^2$ for $c=-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3$; the case $c=0$ is the horizontal axis. Positive $c$ opens upward, negative $c$ downward, and larger $|c|$ gives a narrower [parabola](../../../../../parabola.md). All pass through the origin with a horizontal [tangent line](../../../../../tangent-line.md).

To find [orthogonal trajectories](../../../../../orthogonal-trajectory.md), use the continuous family $y=cx^2$ containing these seven representative members. Its slope is $y'=2cx=2y/x$ away from $x=0$. Two finite slopes of perpendicular tangents multiply to $-1$, so the orthogonal slope is

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{x}{2y}.
$$

This [separable differential equation](../../../../../separable-differential-equation.md) gives $2y\,dy=-x\,dx$, hence

$$
\boxed{x^2+2y^2=C,\qquad C>0.}
$$

These [ellipses](../../../../../ellipse.md) are the regular real trajectories; $C=0$ is a single point, not a regular curve, and $C<0$ has no real points. One example is $x^2+2y^2=2$, with semiaxes $\sqrt2$ horizontally and $1$ vertically.

The implicit tangent relation $2x\,dx+4y\,dy=0$ also describes points with vertical tangents, where the slope quotient is unavailable. At intersections with the member $y=0$, the [ellipse](../../../../../ellipse.md) has a vertical [tangent line](../../../../../tangent-line.md) and is perpendicular to that horizontal axis. The common origin of the [parabolas](../../../../../parabola.md) is not on any regular trajectory with $C>0$. The sketch shows the requested seven members and the example [ellipse](../../../../../ellipse.md).
