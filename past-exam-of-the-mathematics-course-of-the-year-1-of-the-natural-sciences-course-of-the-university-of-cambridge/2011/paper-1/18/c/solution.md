<h1 id="18/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Away from zeros of $\sin x$, an [integrating factor for a differential one-form](../../../../../integrating-factor-for-a-differential-one-form.md) is $\mu(x)=1/\sin x$. Multiplication converts the form to

$$
(\cot x+y)dx+xdy=d[\ln|\sin x|+xy].
$$

Therefore the implicit solution family is $xy+\ln|\sin x|=C$. On each graph [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) between consecutive multiples of $\pi$, this becomes

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\frac{C-\ln|\sin x|}{x},\qquad k\pi<x<(k+1)\pi,\quad k\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

No such open [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) contains zero. At $x=k\pi$, the undivided graph equation would read $\cos(k\pi)=0$, which is impossible for a finite [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md); hence no [differentiable](../../../../../differentiable-function.md) graph extends across one of these points.

If solutions are instead understood as arbitrary parametrized integral [curves](../../../../../curve.md) of the [differential form](../../../../../differential-form-split.md), the vertical [lines](../../../../../straight-line.md) **$x=k\pi$** are additional solutions: along them $dx=0$ and $x\sin x\,dy=0$. They were lost on division by $\sin x$ and are not graphs $y(x)$ over an [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md).
