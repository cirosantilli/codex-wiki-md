<h1 id="20/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Set $a=\pi/2$ for this part. In the [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) $(-\pi,\pi)$ the special points are $-a,0,a$. Elsewhere $f_0=\sin(\tan x)$ is a composition of [smooth](../../../../../../smooth-function.md) [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md).

At zero, $\tan x=x+O(x^3)$ and therefore $\sin(\tan x)=x+O(x^3)$. Its assigned value is zero, so the [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) is continuous and [differentiable](../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) there, with $f_0'(0)=1$.

At either tangent pole $\pm a$, the bounded [sine](../../../../../../sine.md) oscillates without a limit. For an explicit check at $a$, let $x_j=\arctan(2\pi j+\pi/2)$ and $u_j=\arctan(2\pi j+3\pi/2)$. Both approach $a$ from below, but the [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) values are respectively $1$ and $-1$. Negating these sequences gives the same obstruction at $-a$. Assigning the value zero at the poles cannot repair either limit.

Thus **$f_0$ is discontinuous and nondifferentiable exactly at $x=\pm\pi/2$**, and is [smooth](../../../../../../smooth-function.md) elsewhere in the specified [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md).
