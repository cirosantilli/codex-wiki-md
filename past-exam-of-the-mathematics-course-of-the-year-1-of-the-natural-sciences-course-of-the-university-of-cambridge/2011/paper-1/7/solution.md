<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

For negative real $x$, the angle $u=\pi e^x$ lies strictly between $0$ and $\pi$. On that [interval](../../../../interval-mathematics.md), $\sin u=1/\sqrt2$ has exactly the two solutions $u=\pi/4$ and $u=3\pi/4$. Dividing by $\pi$ and applying the [natural logarithm](../../../../natural-logarithm.md) yields

$$
\boxed{x=\ln\frac14=-\ln4,\qquad x=\ln\frac34.}
$$

Both values are negative, and the restriction $0<u<\pi$ excludes all further periodic [sine](../../../../sine.md) branches.
