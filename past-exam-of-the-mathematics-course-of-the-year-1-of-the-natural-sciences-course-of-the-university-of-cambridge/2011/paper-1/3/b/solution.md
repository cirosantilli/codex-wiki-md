<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $x>0$, [logarithmic differentiation](../../../../../logarithmic-differentiation.md) gives

$$
\ln f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x},\qquad
\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}=\frac{1-\ln x}{x^2}.
$$

Since $f(x)>0$, the [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) vanishes precisely when $\ln x=1$. Hence

$$
\boxed{x_0=e,\qquad f(x_0)=e^{1/e}.}
$$

The [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) is positive before $e$ and negative after $e$, so this is a [global maximum](../../../../../global-maximum.md) on the given positive domain.
