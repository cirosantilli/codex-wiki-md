<h1 id="14/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

[Fourier orthogonality](../../../../../fourier-orthogonality.md) isolates a [coefficient](../../../../../coefficient.md). Multiplication by $\sin(mx)$ and [integration](../../../../../integral-split.md) over $[0,2\pi]$ annihilates the constant, all [cosine](../../../../../cosine.md) terms and all [sine](../../../../../sine.md) terms except the one with $n=m$, whose squared integral is $\pi$. Thus the corresponding [coefficients](../../../../../coefficient.md) on the two sides must agree for each positive integer $m$.

Multiplication by $\cos(mx)$ works in the same way, while [integration](../../../../../integral-split.md) against the constant mode extracts $a_0$. This is the uniqueness of the [Fourier coefficients](../../../../../fourier-coefficient.md); it does not follow from comparing a few point values of the series.
