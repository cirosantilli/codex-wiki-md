<h1 id="11/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Factor out $9$ and use the [binomial series](../../../../../../binomial-series.md) with exponent $-1/2$:

$$
(x^2+9)^{-1/2}=\frac13\left(1+\frac{x^2}{9}\right)^{-1/2}
=\frac13\left[1-\frac12\frac{x^2}{9}+O(x^4)\right].
$$

Thus the expansion through cubic order is

$$
\boxed{\frac1{\sqrt{x^2+9}}=\frac13-\frac{x^2}{54}+O(x^4).}
$$

The [coefficients](../../../../../../coefficient.md) of $x$ and $x^3$ vanish because the [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) is an [even function](../../../../../../even-function.md). The underlying binomial expansion converges for $|x|<3$.
