<h1 id="19/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Choose the [plane](../../../../../plane.md) normal to be $+\widehat y$. Only the second field component contributes, and on $y=0$ it is $e^{-(x^2+z^2)}$. Thus the [surface integral](../../../../../surface-integral.md) factors into two [Gaussian integrals](../../../../../gaussian-integral.md):

$$
\boxed{\int_SF\cdot dS
=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2-z^2}\,dx\,dz
=(\sqrt\pi)^2=\pi.}
$$

The original question does not specify an orientation; choosing $-\widehat y$ instead gives **$-\pi$**.

The printed first component $\sin y/y$ is undefined on the entire [integration](../../../../../integral-split.md) [plane](../../../../../plane.md). Its removable continuous extension there is $1$, since $\lim_{y\to0}\sin y/y=1$. The stated [flux](../../../../../flux.md) uses this natural extension; in fact the normal component alone is already well-defined and integrable on the [plane](../../../../../plane.md), so the first component does not affect either oriented value.
