<h1 id="12/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a small rectangle of side lengths $ds,dt$, [independence](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) makes its [probability](../../../../../probability.md) approximately $f_S(s)ds\,f_T(t)dt$. Thus the [joint probability density function](../../../../../joint-probability-density.md) is the product

$$
f_{S,T}(s,t)=\frac1{\bar s\bar t}e^{-s/\bar s-t/\bar t},\qquad s,t\geq0.
$$

Adding the [probabilities](../../../../../probability.md) of disjoint small rectangles covering a [region of integration](../../../../../domain-of-integration.md), and taking their sizes to zero, gives the [double integral](../../../../../double-integral.md) of this product over that region. Without [independence](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md), the two [marginal distributions](../../../../../marginal-distribution.md) alone would not determine the [joint probability density function](../../../../../joint-probability-density.md).
