<h1 id="12/h/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [H](../h.md)

The time already elapsed since the noise is $32$ minutes. Conditional on a particular noise time $T=t$ and on still being in progress, [memorylessness of the exponential distribution](../../../../../memorylessness-of-the-exponential-distribution.md) gives

$$
P(S-t-32>u\mid S>t+32)=e^{-u/\bar s},\qquad u\geq0.
$$

This law does not depend on $t$. Averaging over the [conditional distribution](../../../../../conditional-distribution.md) of $T$ therefore leaves the same remaining-time law. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[m]=\bar s\ \text{minutes}.}
$$

The elapsed $32$ minutes are not subtracted from the original [mean](../../../../../expected-value-split.md); the exponential residual lifetime has the same [mean](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) after any elapsed duration.
