<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Let the [equilateral triangle](../../../../equilateral-triangle.md) have side [length](../../../../length.md) $a$. Its altitude is $H=\sqrt3a/2$, and its [incircle](../../../../incircle-of-a-triangle.md) has [radius](../../../../radius.md) $r=H/3=a/(2\sqrt3)$: the [centre](../../../../center-geometry.md) lies on a median, one third of the altitude above the base. The [area](../../../../surface-area.md) of the triangle is $aH/2=\sqrt3a^2/4$, while the [circle](../../../../circle.md) has [area](../../../../surface-area.md) $\pi r^2=\pi a^2/12$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{\mathcal A_{\rm circle}}{\mathcal A_{\rm triangle}}
=\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt3}=\frac{\pi\sqrt3}{9}\approx0.604600.}
$$

The side [length](../../../../length.md) cancels, as required for a dimensionless ratio between similar configurations.
