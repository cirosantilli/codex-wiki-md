<h1 id="17/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Set $w=z^3$. Then $w^2+w+1=0$ gives $w=e^{2\pi i/3}$ or $e^{-2\pi i/3}$. Taking all three cube roots of each yields six distinct solutions.

Equivalently, multiply the original [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) by $z^3-1$ to obtain $z^9-1$. The original roots are exactly the ninth [roots of unity](../../../../../root-of-unity.md) that are not cube [roots of unity](../../../../../root-of-unity.md): a cube root would give $1+1+1=3$, not zero. Thus

$$
\boxed{z=e^{2\pi i k/9},\qquad k\in\{1,2,4,5,7,8\}.}
$$

All six have modulus one, and their distinct arguments show that no roots have been repeated or omitted from the degree-six [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md).
