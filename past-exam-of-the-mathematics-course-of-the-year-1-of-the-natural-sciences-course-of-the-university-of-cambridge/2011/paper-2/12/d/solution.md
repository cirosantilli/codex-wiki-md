<h1 id="12/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At the first endpoint, the [Cartesian coordinates](../../../../../cartesian-coordinate-system.md) are $(r,0,0)$, so its [great circle](../../../../../great-circle.md) must have $l=0$. At the second endpoint, the [plane](../../../../../plane.md) condition gives $m\sin\theta_0\sin\phi_0+n\cos\theta_0=0$. Since $\cos\theta_0>0$, an equivalent regular [plane](../../../../../plane.md) equation is

$$
\boxed{\cos\theta_0\sin\theta\sin\phi-\sin\theta_0\sin\phi_0\cos\theta=0}.
$$

Where $\cos\theta\ne0$, divide by $\cos\theta_0\cos\theta$ to obtain

$$
\boxed{\tan\theta\sin\phi=\tan\theta_0\sin\phi_0}.
$$

The regular implicit equation includes the equatorial first endpoint; the tangent form is undefined there and must be read by a limit. The shorter connecting arc of this [great circle](../../../../../great-circle.md) is the shortest path. The endpoints are not antipodal, so this shorter arc is unique. For example, the central angle is $\delta=\arccos(\sin\theta_0\cos\phi_0)$, giving [spherical distance](../../../../../great-circle-distance.md) $r\delta$.
