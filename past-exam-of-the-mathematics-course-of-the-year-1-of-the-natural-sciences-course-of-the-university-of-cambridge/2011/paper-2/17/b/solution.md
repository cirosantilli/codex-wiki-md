<h1 id="17/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The lazy choice weights are $1/w$ and $1/(3w)$, in ratio three to one. Normalizing gives $\boxed{l=3/4}$. Let $Q$ denote selection of the first question and let $q=P(\text{Lazy}\mid Q)$. Its conditional [expected value](../../../../../expected-value-split.md), divided by $m$, is

$$
\frac13q+(1-q)=\frac37,
$$

so $q=6/7$. If the overall lazy fraction is $p$, the [law of total probability](../../../../../law-of-total-probability.md) gives $P(Q)=(3/4)p+(1/2)(1-p)=(p+2)/4$. [Bayes' theorem](../../../../../bayes-theorem.md) then gives

$$
\frac67=q=\frac{(3/4)p}{(p+2)/4}=\frac{3p}{p+2}.
$$

Solving $6(p+2)=21p$ yields $\boxed{p=4/5}$. The observed mean is conditioned on the chosen question, so it cannot be equated directly to the unconditional mean over all students.
