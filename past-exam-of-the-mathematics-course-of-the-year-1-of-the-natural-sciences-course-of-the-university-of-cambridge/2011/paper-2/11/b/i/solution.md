<h1 id="11/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Writing $r^2=x^2+y^2+z^2$, the [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) is $\mathbf v=2e^{r^2}(x,y,z)$. Its [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md), equivalently the [Laplacian](../../../../../../laplacian.md) of $\phi$, is

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=(6+4r^2)e^{r^2}.
$$

On the unit [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) this [scalar](../../../../../../scalar.md) is the constant $10e$. The lower [hemisphere](../../../../../../hemisphere.md) has [surface area](../../../../../../surface-area.md) $2\pi$, so

$$
\boxed{\int_S(\nabla\cdot\mathbf v)\,dS=20\pi e}.
$$

This is a [scalar](../../../../../../scalar.md) [surface integral](../../../../../../surface-integral.md) of the divergence, not the electric- or vector-field flux [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md). There is no normal component to insert.
