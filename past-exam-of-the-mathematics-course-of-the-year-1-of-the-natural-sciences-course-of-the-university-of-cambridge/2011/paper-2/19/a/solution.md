<h1 id="19/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Put $c=2\alpha/\pi$. In the [right half-plane](../../../../../right-half-plane.md),

$$
\Phi_u=-\frac{cv}{u^2+v^2},\quad
\Phi_v=\frac{cu}{u^2+v^2},\quad
\Phi_{uu}=\frac{2cuv}{(u^2+v^2)^2},\quad
\Phi_{vv}=-\frac{2cuv}{(u^2+v^2)^2}.
$$

The last two cancel, so $\boxed{\Phi_{uu}+\Phi_{vv}=0}$. The possible singular point $(0,0)$ lies outside the open domain $u>0$.

For $\alpha\ne0$, a [level curve](../../../../../level-curve.md) of value $\Phi_0$ has $v/u=\tan(\pi\Phi_0/(2\alpha))$. These are rays from the origin, restricted to the [right half-plane](../../../../../right-half-plane.md). For positive $\alpha$, the values increase with angle from $-\alpha$ on the lower [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) to $+\alpha$ on the upper [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md). If $\alpha=0$, the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) is identically zero. The left panel of the figure in (c) shows these ray-shaped [level curves](../../../../../level-curve.md).
