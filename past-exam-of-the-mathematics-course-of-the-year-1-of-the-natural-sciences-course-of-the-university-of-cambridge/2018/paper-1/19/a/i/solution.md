<h1 id="19/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For statement (1), [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at the interior point means

$$
\boxed{\lim_{x\to x_0}f(x)=f(x_0).}
$$

Explicitly, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that $|x-x_0|<\delta$ implies $|f(x)-f(x_0)|<\varepsilon$ for points in the domain. This is the [epsilon-delta definition of continuity](../../../../../../epsilon-delta-definition-of-continuity.md).

For statement (2), [differentiability](../../../../../../differentiability.md) means the two-sided difference quotient has a finite [limit](../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md):

$$
\boxed{f'(x_0)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x_0+h)-f(x_0)}h\in\mathbb R.}
$$

The same finite value must be obtained from positive and negative $h$. [Differentiability](../../../../../../differentiability.md) implies [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md), whereas the converse need not hold.
