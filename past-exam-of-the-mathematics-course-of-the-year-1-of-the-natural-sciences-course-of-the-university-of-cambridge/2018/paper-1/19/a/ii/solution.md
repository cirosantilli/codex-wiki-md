<h1 id="19/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $m=0$, [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) fails. Take $x_k=(\pi/2+2\pi k)^{-1}$ and $\widetilde x_k=(3\pi/2+2\pi k)^{-1}$. Both tend to zero, but the corresponding function values are $1$ and $-1$. Thus there is no [limit](../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md), and the function is not [differentiable](../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) either, since [differentiability](../../../../../../differentiability.md) would imply [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md).

For $m=1$, the bound $|x\sin(1/x)|\le|x|$ and the [squeeze theorem](../../../../../../squeeze-theorem.md) give [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at zero. Its difference quotient is $\sin(1/h)$, however, which has no [limit](../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) by the same two sequences. It is therefore not [differentiable](../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) there.

For $m=2$, the bound $|x^2\sin(1/x)|\le x^2$ proves [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md). Its difference quotient is $h\sin(1/h)$, whose absolute value is bounded by $|h|$, so the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) exists and equals zero. The complete classification is

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c|c}m&\text{continuous at }0&\text{differentiable at }0\\\hline0&\text{no}&\text{no}\\1&\text{yes}&\text{no}\\2&\text{yes}&\text{yes, }f_2'(0)=0\end{array}.}
$$
