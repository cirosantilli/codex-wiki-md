<h1 id="19/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Rationalize the positive difference:

$$
\sqrt{n^4+a^2}-n^2=\frac{a^2}{\sqrt{n^4+a^2}+n^2}.
$$

Since the denominator is at least $2n^2$,

$$
0<\sqrt{n^4+a^2}-n^2\le\frac{a^2}{2n^2}.
$$

The upper bound is a constant multiple of the convergent [p-series](../../../../../../p-series.md) with exponent two. The [comparison test](../../../../../../direct-comparison-test.md) therefore proves

$$
\boxed{\sum_{n=1}^\infty\left(\sqrt{n^4+a^2}-n^2\right)\text{ converges for every fixed }a>0.}
$$

The [asymptotic equivalence](../../../../../../asymptotic-equivalence.md) of its summand to $a^2/(2n^2)$ gives the same conclusion by [limit comparison test](../../../../../../limit-comparison-test.md).
