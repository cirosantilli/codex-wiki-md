<h1 id="13/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The original PDF has $8y^3-4x$ in the denominator and $dy/dx$ on the left. Rearrange it into the [differential form](../../../../../differential-form-split.md)

$$
(5x+4y)\,dx+(4x-8y^3)\,dy=0.
$$

The cross [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) of its coefficients are both four, so it is an [exact differential form](../../../../../exact-differential-form.md). Integrating its first coefficient in $x$ gives a potential $F=\tfrac52x^2+4xy+h(y)$. Matching $F_y=4x-8y^3$ gives $h(y)=-2y^4$. Therefore an implicit [general solution](../../../../../general-solution.md) is

$$
\boxed{\frac52x^2+4xy-2y^4=C.}
$$

Differentiating verifies $y'=(5x+4y)/(8y^3-4x)$. A solution graph is understood only on intervals where its denominator is nonzero; the printed quotient does not define a [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) when $8y^3=4x$.
