<h1 id="13/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For $x\ne0$, divide by $x^2$ to obtain $y'+y/x=1/x^2$, a first-order [linear ordinary differential equation](../../../../../linear-ordinary-differential-equation-split.md). An [integrating factor](../../../../../integrating-factor.md) is $x$ on either interval not crossing zero, since the [product rule](../../../../../product-rule.md) gives

$$
(xy)'=\frac1x.
$$

Integration and division by $x$ give the explicit [general solution](../../../../../general-solution.md)

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\frac{\ln|x|+C}{x},\qquad x\ne0.}
$$

The original equation would read $0=1$ at zero, so no solution can include that point.
