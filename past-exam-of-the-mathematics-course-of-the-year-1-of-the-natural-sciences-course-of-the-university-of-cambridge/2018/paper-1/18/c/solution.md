<h1 id="18/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The three [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) form an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md), so an arbitrary [vector](../../../../../vector.md) has expansion $\mathbf r=a\mathbf v_-+b\mathbf v_0+c\mathbf v_+$. Multiplication by $A$ removes the zero-eigenvalue component:

$$
A\mathbf r=-6a\mathbf v_-+12c\mathbf v_+.
$$

Its [dot product](../../../../../dot-product.md) with $\mathbf v_0$ is zero for every $a,b,c$. Thus one convenient answer is the nonzero [vector](../../../../../vector.md)

$$
\boxed{\mathbf u=(1,2,1)^{\mathsf T}.}
$$

Equivalently, $A^{\mathsf T}\mathbf u=A\mathbf u=0$, so $(A\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf u=\mathbf r\cdot(A^{\mathsf T}\mathbf u)=0$. Any nonzero scalar multiple of this [vector](../../../../../vector.md) in the [null space](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) also works.
