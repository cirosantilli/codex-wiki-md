<h1 id="11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The factor $1+i$ has [modulus](../../../../../modulus.md) $\sqrt2$ and [principal argument](../../../../../principal-argument.md) $\pi/4$. Multiplication by $e^{i\pi/6}$ adds $\pi/6$ to its [complex argument](../../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md), while the real factor $1/2$ halves its [modulus](../../../../../modulus.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{|z|=\frac1{\sqrt2},\qquad\arg z=\frac{5\pi}{12}+2\pi k\quad(k\in\mathbb Z).}
$$

Taking the [principal logarithm](../../../../../principal-complex-logarithm.md) for $w$ gives

$$
w=-\frac12\ln2+i\frac{5\pi}{12}.
$$

This [complex number](../../../../../complex-number.md) lies in the second quadrant. Its [modulus](../../../../../modulus.md) follows from the sum of squared components, and its [principal argument](../../../../../principal-argument.md) follows by the second-quadrant correction:

$$
\boxed{|w|=\sqrt{\frac{(\ln2)^2}{4}+\frac{25\pi^2}{144}},\qquad
\operatorname{Arg}w=\pi-\arctan\frac{5\pi}{6\ln2}.}
$$

All arguments of this fixed $w$ add integer multiples of $2\pi$ to the displayed one.

If $\ln z$ is interpreted as the multivalued [complex logarithm](../../../../../complex-logarithm.md), there is instead a family $w_k=-a+ib_k$, where $a=\tfrac12\ln2>0$ and $b_k=5\pi/12+2\pi k$. Then $|w_k|=\sqrt{a^2+b_k^2}$, and the [principal argument](../../../../../principal-argument.md) is $\pi-\arctan(b_k/a)$ for $b_k>0$, or $-\pi-\arctan(b_k/a)$ for $b_k<0$. This records the logarithm-branch choice that the paper leaves implicit.
