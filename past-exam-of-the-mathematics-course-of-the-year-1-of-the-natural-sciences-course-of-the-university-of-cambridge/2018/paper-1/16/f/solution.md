<h1 id="16/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The new angular interval has width $\pi/2$, so its [probability density function](../../../../../probability-density-function.md) is $2/\pi$ there. Its endpoints stay away from the [poles](../../../../../pole.md) of the [tangent function](../../../../../tangent-function.md), making $Y$ bounded and its [expected value](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) finite. Integrate over the [continuous uniform distribution](../../../../../continuous-uniform-distribution.md) of angles:

$$
E[Y]=\frac{2x_0}{\pi}\int_{-\pi/6}^{\pi/3}\tan\theta\,d\theta
=\frac{2x_0}{\pi}[-\ln(\cos\theta)]_{-\pi/6}^{\pi/3}.
$$

The two [cosines](../../../../../cosine.md) are $\sqrt3/2$ and $1/2$. Hence

$$
\boxed{E[Y]=\frac{2x_0}{\pi}\ln\sqrt3=\frac{x_0\ln3}{\pi}.}
$$

It has units of [length](../../../../../length.md) and is positive because the permitted positive angles extend farther than the negative ones.
