<h1 id="16/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The [expected value](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) is not defined: the integral of $yf(y)$ over the positive half-line diverges logarithmically to $+\infty$, and the negative half-line gives $-\infty$. In particular, symmetry alone does not supply a mean. More explicitly,

$$
\int_0^R yf(y)\,dy=\frac{x_0}{2\pi}\ln\left(1+\frac{R^2}{x_0^2}\right)\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

The second [moment](../../../../../moment.md) also diverges because $y^2f(y)\to x_0/\pi>0$ as $|y|\to\infty$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{the mean, variance and standard deviation are undefined}.}
$$

The symmetric [Cauchy principal value](../../../../../cauchy-principal-value.md) of the first-moment integral is zero, but it is not an [expected value](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) and does not define a [variance](../../../../../variance-split.md).
