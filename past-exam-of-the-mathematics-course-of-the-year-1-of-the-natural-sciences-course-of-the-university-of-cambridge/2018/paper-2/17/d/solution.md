<h1 id="17/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At $\mu=-1$, $\mathbf c_2=-\mathbf c_1$, while $\mathbf c_1$ and $\mathbf c_3$ are independent. Their span is a plane. A solution requires $\mathbf b$ to lie in that plane, so every [scalar triple product](../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md) formed from two columns and $\mathbf b$ must vanish. The product with $\mathbf c_1,\mathbf c_2$ vanishes identically; the other two give the plane condition.

Explicitly, $\mathbf c_1\times\mathbf c_3=(-7,1,3)$, so

$$
[\mathbf c_1,\mathbf c_3,\mathbf b]=(-7,1,3)\cdot(1,\lambda,0)=\lambda-7.
$$

Thus $\lambda=7$ is necessary and, since the two columns span the plane, sufficient. The equations then give $x-y=1$ and $z=2$, so

$$
\boxed{\lambda=7,\qquad\mathbf r=(1,0,2)^{\mathsf T}+t(1,1,0)^{\mathsf T},\quad t\in\mathbb R.}
$$

The solution is an affine line parallel to the homogeneous [null space](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) from part (c), obtained by intersecting the compatible constraint planes. Every point on it is nonzero because $z=2$.
