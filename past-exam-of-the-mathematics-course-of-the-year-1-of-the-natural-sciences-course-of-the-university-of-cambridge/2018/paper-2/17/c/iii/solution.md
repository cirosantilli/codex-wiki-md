<h1 id="17/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At $\mu=-1$, the first equation gives $x=y$, and the second then gives $z=0$. All nonzero solutions are

$$
\boxed{\mathbf r=t(1,1,0)^{\mathsf T},\qquad t\ne0\quad(\mu=-1).}
$$

At $\mu=6$, the first equation gives $x=-6y$. The second then gives $3z=7y$, and the third is twice the second. Choosing an integer direction [vector](../../../../../../vector.md), all nonzero solutions are

$$
\boxed{\mathbf r=t(-18,3,7)^{\mathsf T},\qquad t\ne0\quad(\mu=6).}
$$

Each full [null space](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md), including the zero solution, is a line through the origin. Geometrically, the three homogeneous constraint planes have that common intersection; in each case only two of their equations are independent.
