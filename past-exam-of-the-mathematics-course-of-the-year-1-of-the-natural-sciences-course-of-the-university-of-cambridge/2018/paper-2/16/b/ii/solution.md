<h1 id="16/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take the outward orientation of the complete closed surface, including its flat base. The constant part of the [vector field](../../../../../../vector-field.md) has zero [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md), and the radial part has [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md) $3\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b$. The [divergence theorem](../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) gives its [flux](../../../../../../flux.md) as that constant times the solid hemisphere's [volume](../../../../../../volume.md), $2\pi R^3/3$:

$$
\boxed{\int_{\partial V}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf S=3(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)\frac{2\pi R^3}{3}
=2\pi R^3\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b.}
$$

The constant field may have nonzero [flux](../../../../../../flux.md) through the curved part alone, but its curved and base contributions cancel on the closed surface.
