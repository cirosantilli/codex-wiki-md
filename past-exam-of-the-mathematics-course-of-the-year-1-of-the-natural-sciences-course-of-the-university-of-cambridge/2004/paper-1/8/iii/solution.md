<h1 id="8/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Direct differentiation gives $\boxed{\Phi=x^3y^2z+C}$ with $\nabla\Phi=\mathbf G$, so $\mathbf G$ is a [conservative vector field](../../../../../conservative-vector-field.md) and its integral depends only on the endpoints. In contrast,

$$
\nabla\times\mathbf F=\left(-2x^3y,\ 3x^2z(2y-z),\ 3x^2z(2y-z)\right)
$$

is nonzero, for example at $(1,1,1)$. Thus **$\mathbf F$ is not conservative**. Both earlier paths lie in the surface $y=z$, where the two fields happen to coincide. This illustrates why [coinciding path integrals need not imply a conservative field](../../../../../coinciding-path-integrals-need-not-imply-a-conservative-field.md).
