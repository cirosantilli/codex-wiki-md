<h1 id="12/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [stationary point](../../../../../stationary-point.md) equations are $x^2+2y-21/4=0$ and $y^2+2x-21/4=0$. Subtracting them factors as $(x-y)(x+y-2)=0$, so all solutions lie either on $x=y$ or on $x+y=2$. The first case gives $x=y=3/2$ or $x=y=-7/2$; the second gives $(x,y)=(-1/2,5/2)$ or $(5/2,-1/2)$. This also verifies the two stated points and exhausts the possible solutions.

The [Hessian matrix](../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) is $H=\begin{pmatrix}2x&2\\2&2y\end{pmatrix}$, with determinant $4(xy-1)$. The [second-derivative test](../../../../../second-derivative-test.md) gives:

- **$(3/2,3/2)$ is a strict local minimum:** $H_{11}=3>0$ and $\det H=5>0$.
- **$(-7/2,-7/2)$ is a strict local maximum:** $H_{11}=-7<0$ and $\det H=45>0$.
- **$(-1/2,5/2)$ and $(5/2,-1/2)$ are saddle points:** $\det H=-9<0$ at both.

These extrema are local. Along $x=y=t$ the cubic term makes the function unbounded above and below, so it has no global maximum or minimum on the whole plane.
