<h1 id="10/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Use the stipulated interpolation $k_3\approx(k_2+k_4)/2$. The unrounded values give $k_3\approx0.4728010040$, and hence

$$
\sum_{n=1}^\infty n^{-3}\approx1+\frac18+\frac1{27}+\frac1{64}+\frac1{2(5-0.4728010040)^2}\approx1.2020575995.
$$

Thus a sensible reported estimate is

$$
\boxed{\sum_{n=1}^\infty n^{-3}\approx1.20206.}
$$

Using the rounded shifts from part (ii) instead gives $k_3\approx0.473$ and a sum about $1.202059745$, with the same six-significant-figure estimate. The interpolation of shifts is an assumption of this approximation; the [integral test](../../../../../../integral-test-for-convergence.md) bounds alone do not prove that many digits of accuracy.
