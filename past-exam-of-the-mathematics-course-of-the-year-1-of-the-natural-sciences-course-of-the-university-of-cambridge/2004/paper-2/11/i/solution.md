<h1 id="11/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $x=e^z>0$, let $Y(z)=y(e^z)$. The [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives

$$
y'(x)=\frac{Y'(z)}x,\qquad y''(x)=\frac{Y''(z)-Y'(z)}{x^2}.
$$

Multiplying each original [Cauchy-Euler equation](../../../../../cauchy-euler-equation.md) by $x^2$ therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\text{(1)}\quad Y''+3Y'+2Y=0,\qquad \alpha=3,\ \beta=2,}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\text{(2)}\quad Y''+Y=0,\qquad \alpha=0,\ \beta=1.}
$$

The exponential change of variable has made both equations have constant coefficients.
