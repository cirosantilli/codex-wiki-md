<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [product rule](../../../../../product-rule.md) and [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md) first give

$$
F_x=y^2(1+2x^2y)e^{x^2y},\qquad F_y=x(2y+x^2y^2)e^{x^2y}.
$$

Differentiating the first expression with respect to $y$ yields $e^{x^2y}(2y+7x^2y^2+2x^4y^3)$; differentiating the second with respect to $x$ gives exactly the same polynomial factor. Thus

$$
\boxed{F_{xy}=F_{yx}=e^{x^2y}(2y+7x^2y^2+2x^4y^3).}
$$

This also follows from [symmetry of second derivatives](../../../../../symmetry-of-second-derivatives.md), since all derivatives of this function are continuous everywhere.
