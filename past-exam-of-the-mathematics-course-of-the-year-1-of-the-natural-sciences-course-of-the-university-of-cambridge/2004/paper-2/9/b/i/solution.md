<h1 id="9/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the fixed exponents $p,q$ in the original PDF. Assume positive $a,b$ so arbitrary real powers and a relative change are defined. Set $h=k/100$. With $b$ fixed, the exact new-to-old ratio is $(1+h)^p$. The [binomial series](../../../../../../binomial-series.md), or the one-variable case of the [second-order relative change of a monomial](../../../../../../second-order-relative-change-of-a-monomial.md), gives

$$
100\frac{\Delta f}{f}=100\left[ph+\frac{p(p-1)}2h^2+O(h^3)\right].
$$

Therefore the approximate percentage change through second order is

$$
\boxed{pk+\frac{p(p-1)}{200}k^2.}
$$

For negative $k$ this same signed expression describes a decrease.
