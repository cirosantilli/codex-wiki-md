<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Minimize the squared distance $D^2=(x-x_0)^2+(y-y_0)^2$ subject to $g=y-mx-c=0$. The [Lagrange multiplier](../../../../../lagrange-multiplier.md) condition $\nabla D^2=\lambda\nabla g$ gives $x=x_0-m\lambda/2$ and $y=y_0+\lambda/2$. Enforcing the line constraint gives $\lambda=2(mx_0+c-y_0)/(1+m^2)$, hence

$$
\boxed{x_*=\frac{x_0+m(y_0-c)}{1+m^2},\qquad y_*=\frac{mx_0+m^2y_0+c}{1+m^2}.}
$$

The displacement to this point is parallel to the line's [normal vector](../../../../../normal-vector.md) $(-m,1)$, so it is the [orthogonal projection](../../../../../orthogonal-projection.md). The squared distance restricted to the line has positive quadratic coefficient $1+m^2$, proving this stationary point is the unique global minimum.
