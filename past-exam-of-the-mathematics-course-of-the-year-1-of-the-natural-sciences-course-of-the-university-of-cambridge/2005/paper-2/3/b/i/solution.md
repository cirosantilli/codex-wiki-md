<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Since $A^2=-\epsilon^2I$, its even powers are $A^{2k}=(-1)^k\epsilon^{2k}I$ and its odd powers are $A^{2k+1}=(-1)^k\epsilon^{2k}A$. Splitting the [matrix exponential](../../../../../../matrix-exponential.md) into these two absolutely convergent subseries gives the [matrix exponential for a scalar square](../../../../../../matrix-exponential-for-a-scalar-square.md):

$$
\boxed{S=e^A=I\cos\epsilon+\frac{\sin\epsilon}{\epsilon}A=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\epsilon&\sin\epsilon\\-\sin\epsilon&\cos\epsilon\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

At $\epsilon=0$, the coefficient $\sin\epsilon/\epsilon$ has limiting value one and $A=0$, so $S=I$. The result is a planar rotation through angle $-\epsilon$.
