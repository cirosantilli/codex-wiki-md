<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In first-quadrant [polar coordinates](../../../../../polar-coordinates.md), $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$, and the [Jacobian determinant](../../../../../jacobian-determinant.md) contributes $r$. The integrand and area element together become $r^2e^{-r^2}\sin\theta\cos^2\theta\,dr\,d\theta$. Hence

$$
I=\left(\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\theta\right)\left(\int_0^\infty r^2e^{-r^2}dr\right)=\frac13\frac{\sqrt\pi}{4}=\boxed{\frac{\sqrt\pi}{12}}.
$$

The radial integral follows by [integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) from the [Gaussian integral](../../../../../gaussian-integral.md). The isolated undefined ratio at the origin does not affect this convergent [double integral](../../../../../double-integral.md).
