<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use [separation of variables](../../../../../separation-of-variables.md) on intervals avoiding zeros of $\cos x$:

$$
\int\frac{dy}{1+y^2}=\int\sec^2x\,dx,\qquad\arctan y=\tan x+C.
$$

Taking the [tangent](../../../../../tangent.md) gives the real general solution

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\tan(\tan x+C).}
$$

Each solution is restricted to an interval where both the original equation and this expression are finite. There is no real constant solution because $1+y^2$ never vanishes for real $y$.
