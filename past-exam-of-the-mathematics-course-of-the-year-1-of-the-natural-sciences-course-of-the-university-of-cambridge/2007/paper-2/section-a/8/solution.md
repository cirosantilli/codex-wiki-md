<h1 id="section-a/8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

For $x_0>0$, [separation of variables](../../../../../separation-of-variables.md) gives $1/x=kt+1/x_0$, hence $x(t)=x_0/(1+kx_0t)$. Setting $x(t)=x_0/2$ gives the [half-life](../../../../../half-life.md)

$$
\boxed{t_{1/2}=\frac1{kx_0}.}
$$

This is the same reciprocal decay law as a [second-order reaction](../../../../../second-order-reaction.md) with the specified [disappearance rate](../../../../../disappearance-rate.md) coefficient. A positive initial value is needed for a positive half-time; $x_0=0$ instead gives the identically zero solution.
