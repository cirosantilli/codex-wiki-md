<h1 id="section-b/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Substituting $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$ into the [circle](../../../../../circle.md) equation gives $r^2-2r\cos\theta=0$. Thus its nonzero radial intersection is $r_Q=2\cos\theta$, with the origin also on the [circle](../../../../../circle.md). The [tangent to a circle](../../../../../tangent-to-a-circle.md) at the rightmost point is $x=2$, or $r_R\cos\theta=2$.

Take rays with $-\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2$. The difference of the two radial distances gives the [cissoid of Diocles](../../../../../cissoid-of-diocles.md):

$$
\boxed{r_P=2\sec\theta-2\cos\theta=\frac{2\sin^2\theta}{\cos\theta}.}
$$

Multiplying by $\cos\theta$ gives $x=2\sin^2\theta=2y^2/(x^2+y^2)$, hence the Cartesian equation $y^2(2-x)=x^3$. A convenient [parametric curve](../../../../../parametric-curve.md), with $t=\tan\theta$, is

$$
x=\frac{2t^2}{1+t^2},\qquad y=\frac{2t^3}{1+t^2},\qquad -\infty<t<\infty.
$$

The real [curve](../../../../../curve.md) has $0\leq x<2$, a [cusp](../../../../../cusp-algebraic-geometry.md) at the origin, symmetry about the $x$ axis, and the vertical [asymptote](../../../../../asymptote.md) $x=2$. Near the origin, $x\sim2t^2$ and $y\sim2t^3$, so its cusp has horizontal tangent direction. As $|t|\to\infty$, $x\to2^-$ while $|y|\to\infty$. The circle and curve meet away from the origin at $(1,\pm1)$.
