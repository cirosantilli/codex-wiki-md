<h1 id="section-b/9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [ratio test](../../../../../../ratio-test.md) uses $L=\lim_{n\to\infty}|a_{n+1}/a_n|$, when it exists for eventually nonzero terms: $L<1$ gives [absolute convergence](../../../../../../absolute-convergence.md), $L>1$ gives divergence, and $L=1$ is inconclusive.

For $a_n=n/(2^n-1)$,

$$
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{2^n-1}{2^{n+1}-1}\longrightarrow\frac12.
$$

Thus the first [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) **converges absolutely**. For the alternating second [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md),

$$
\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|=2\left(\frac n{n+1}\right)^5\longrightarrow2,
$$

so it **diverges**. Alternating signs cannot rescue terms whose [absolute values](../../../../../../absolute-value.md) fail to tend to zero.
