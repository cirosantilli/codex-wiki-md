<h1 id="section-b/5/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

On the real domain $y>0$, $M=y(x^2+\ln y)$ and $N=x$ have $M_y=x^2+\ln y+1$ and $N_x=1$, so this [differential one-form](../../../../../../../one-form.md) is not exact. An [integrating factor for a differential one-form](../../../../../../../integrating-factor-for-a-differential-one-form.md) is $\mu=1/y$, which is nonzero on this domain. Multiplication gives

$$
(x^2+\ln y)\,dx+\frac{x}{y}\,dy=d\left(\frac{x^3}{3}+x\ln y\right).
$$

Hence the implicit solution is

$$
\boxed{\frac{x^3}{3}+x\ln y=C,\qquad y>0.}
$$
