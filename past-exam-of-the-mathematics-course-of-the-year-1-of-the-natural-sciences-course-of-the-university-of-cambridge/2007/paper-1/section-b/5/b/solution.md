<h1 id="section-b/5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

On the interval $x>-1$ containing the [initial condition](../../../../../../initial-condition.md), division by $1+x$ gives a [linear ordinary differential equation](../../../../../../linear-ordinary-differential-equation-split.md) with [integrating factor](../../../../../../integrating-factor.md) $(1+x)^{-3}$. Consequently

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\bigl[(1+x)^{-3}y\bigr]=1+x,\qquad (1+x)^{-3}y=\frac{(1+x)^2}{2}+C.
$$

The initial value gives $C=1$. Thus

$$
\boxed{y=(1+x)^3\left[1+\frac{(1+x)^2}{2}\right].}
$$

This [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) also satisfies the original equation at $x=-1$ and gives an extension to all real $x$. The [initial value problem](../../../../../../initial-value-problem.md) is uniquely determined on $x>-1$; the vanishing leading coefficient at $-1$ does not ensure unique continuation to its left.
