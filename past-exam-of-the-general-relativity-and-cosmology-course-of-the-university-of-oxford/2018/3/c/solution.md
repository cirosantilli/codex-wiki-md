<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use standardized [Type Ia supernovae](../../../../type-ia-supernova.md) as distance indicators and measure their redshifts. The curvature of the luminosity-distance relation constrains the [deceleration parameter](../../../../deceleration-parameter.md):

$$
\boxed{d_L(z)=\frac c{H_0}\left[z+\frac{1-q_0}{2}z^2+O(z^3)\right],\qquad
q_0=-\frac{\ddot R(t_0)}{R(t_0)H_0^2}.}
$$

Fainter supernovae at a given redshift imply larger distances, and comparison with the predicted curve tests accelerated expansion. In practice one fits the full expansion history over the observed redshift range and controls [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md) evolution, extinction and calibration, rather than relying only on a short low-$z$ series.

The [cosmological fate from local expansion data](../../../../cosmological-fate-from-local-expansion-data.md) is conditional on a matter model. For the spatially flat model of part [b](../b.md), if the only relevant components are pressureless matter and a constant $\Lambda$, put $A_0=\ddot R(t_0)/R(t_0)$. The two [Friedmann equations](../../../../friedmann-equations.md) give

$$
\boxed{\rho_{m0}=\frac{H_0^2-A_0}{4\pi G},\qquad
\Lambda c^2=H_0^2+2A_0.}
$$

Here $\Lambda$ follows the sign of the supplied [Friedmann equations](../../../../friedmann-equations.md), where positive $\Lambda$ contributes positive acceleration. These fix the parameters governing subsequent evolution. Positive $\Lambda$ yields eternal late-time accelerated expansion; zero $\Lambda$ gives the flat matter solution; negative $\Lambda$ leads to eventual recollapse in this dust model. If instead one assumes dust and zero $\Lambda$ while allowing curvature, $q_0=\Omega_{m0}/2$ determines whether the matter universe is closed and recollapsing or expands indefinitely.

Without a fixed [equation of state](../../../../equation-of-state.md) and its future evolution, two present derivatives do **not** uniquely fix the future. For a concrete counterexample in units with $t_0=0$, the positive scale factors $a_+(t)=1+t+t^2/2$ and $a_-(t)=1+t+t^2/2-t^3$ have the same $a,\dot a,\ddot a$ now. The former expands forever; the latter turns around and later reaches zero. Different time-dependent matter stresses can support their different flat expansion histories. Thus the printed sufficiency statement needs the cosmological-model assumption.
