<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [cosmic distance ladder](../../../../cosmic-distance-ladder.md) starts by calibrating a physical baseline. Radar echoes from planets or spacecraft give a round-trip light time and hence a distance $d=c\Delta t/2$, after accounting for orbital motion and propagation delays. Combining this with orbital geometry fixes the Earth-Sun orbital scale; directly timing a reflected pulse from the solar surface is not required.

That baseline supplies annual [stellar parallax](../../../../stellar-parallax.md): for small parallax angle $p$, $d\simeq b/p$, or $d$ in parsecs equals $1/p$ when $p$ is in arcseconds and $b$ is one astronomical unit. Nearby parallax distances calibrate stellar [absolute magnitudes](../../../../absolute-magnitude.md). [Main-sequence fitting](../../../../main-sequence-fitting.md) extends the scale to star clusters, and the calibrated [Cepheid period-luminosity relation](../../../../cepheid-period-luminosity-relation.md) gives [luminosities](../../../../luminosity.md) for Cepheids visible in nearby galaxies.

Cepheid-host galaxies then calibrate [Type Ia supernovae](../../../../type-ia-supernova.md). After light-curve and colour standardization, their high [luminosities](../../../../luminosity.md) permit distances to much more remote galaxies. For a source of calibrated [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md) $L$ and observed bolometric flux $F$,

$$
\boxed{d_L=\sqrt{\frac{L}{4\pi F}},\qquad
m-M=5\log_{10}\left(\frac{d_L}{10\,\mathrm{pc}}\right)}
$$

with consistent bandpass definitions and corrections. [Interstellar extinction](../../../../interstellar-extinction.md), composition, stellar-population differences and calibration uncertainty must be propagated through the steps. Redshift can subsequently provide a distance within a fitted cosmological model; using it alone cannot independently calibrate the [Hubble constant](../../../../hubble-constant.md).
