<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Normalize the [scale factor](../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md) to $a(t)=R(t)/R(t_0)$, with $a(t_0)=1$. Along a radial [null geodesic](../../../../null-geodesic.md), $d\chi=c\,dt/a(t)$. Two successive wave crests sent by the same comoving emitter and received by the same comoving observer traverse the same [comoving distance](../../../../comoving-radial-distance.md), so

$$
\int_{t_e}^{t_0}\frac{c\,dt}{a(t)}
=\int_{t_e+\delta t_e}^{t_0+\delta t_0}\frac{c\,dt}{a(t)}.
$$

To first order, $\delta t_0/a(t_0)=\delta t_e/a(t_e)$. Frequencies are inverse proper periods for the [comoving observers](../../../../comoving-observer.md), giving the [cosmological redshift](../../../../cosmological-redshift.md)

$$
\boxed{1+z=\frac{\lambda_0}{\lambda_e}
=\frac{R(t_0)}{R(t_e)}=\frac1{a(t_e)}.}
$$

Expansion stretches wavelengths, so a spectral line of known rest wavelength gives $z=\lambda_{\rm obs}/\lambda_{\rm rest}-1$.

At small $z$, the light travel time is short compared with the expansion time. Expanding $R(t_e)$ gives $z\simeq H_0(t_0-t_e)$ and $d\simeq c(t_0-t_e)$, hence

$$
\boxed{cz\simeq H_0d,\qquad H_0=\left.\frac{\dot R}{R}\right|_{t_0}.}
$$

Combine independently calibrated [luminosity distances](../../../../luminosity-distance.md) with galaxy spectral redshifts and fit the slope. Nearby peculiar velocities must be allowed for, while high-redshift data require the full distance-redshift relation rather than the linear approximation.
