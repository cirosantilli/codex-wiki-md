<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use [metric signature](../../../../metric-signature.md) $(-,+,+,+)$ and $x^0=ct$. At an event in the ship's instantaneous rest [local inertial frame](../../../../local-inertial-frame.md), $u^\alpha=(c,0,0,0)$. Since its squared norm is a scalar,

$$
\boxed{u^\alpha u_\alpha=-c^2}
$$

in every coordinate system. Equivalently it follows directly from $ds^2=-c^2d\tau^2$ and $u^\alpha=dx^\alpha/d\tau$.

[Metric compatibility](../../../../metric-compatibility.md) lets us differentiate this identity covariantly:

$$
0=\frac D{d\tau}(g_{\alpha\beta}u^\alpha u^\beta)
=2u_\alpha a^\alpha,
\qquad\boxed{u_\alpha a^\alpha=0.}
$$

In the instantaneous rest [local inertial frame](../../../../local-inertial-frame.md), the last equation gives $a^0=0$. The spatial components are the acceleration recorded by an onboard accelerometer, $\mathbf a_{\rm rest}$, whose magnitude is $g$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{a^\alpha a_\alpha=|\mathbf a_{\rm rest}|^2=g^2.}
$$

This [proper acceleration](../../../../proper-acceleration.md) is zero in ideal [free fall](../../../../free-fall.md), even when the coordinate position has a nonzero second derivative. A coordinate acceleration and an accelerometer reading are different quantities.
