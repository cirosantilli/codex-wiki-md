<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Outside the core, $f''=0$ and the metric is locally flat: set $\widetilde\phi=(1-\lambda)\phi$. Static test particles have no Newtonian-like attraction because $g_{00}$ is constant and $\Gamma^r{}_{00}=0$. Local trajectories are straight lines in locally unwrapped coordinates, so a very thin dark core is hard to identify through ordinary orbital accelerations or local [tidal forces](../../../../tidal-force.md).

The global angular range is nevertheless $2\pi(1-\lambda)$ rather than $2\pi$. The missing angle $\delta=2\pi\lambda$ can give [gravitational lensing](../../../../gravitational-lensing-split.md) with two nearly identical, essentially undistorted images of a background galaxy, and multiple such pairs aligned along the projected object. For a small deficit, a straight string and favourable geometry, their angular separation is of order $\delta D_{ls}/D_s$, with an additional inclination factor for a tilted string. The matching spectra and common line of image pairs distinguish this from an ordinary compact lens.

A moving [cosmic string](../../../../cosmic-string.md) can also induce a step in the [cosmic microwave background](../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-split.md) [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) across its path. That signature needs motion in addition to the static geometry supplied here. These are consequences of the exterior conical geometry; interpreting the inferred deficit as a positive mass per length requires the regular-axis/core assumptions distinguished in part [b](../b.md).
