<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[Big Bang nucleosynthesis](../../../../big-bang-nucleosynthesis.md) is a kinetic process in an expanding universe. A common particle temperature does not imply chemical equilibrium for every nuclear reaction: reaction rates must also exceed the expansion rate and connect the required species efficiently. Around $10^9\,\mathrm K$, nuclear abundances retain the history of earlier freeze-out and cannot be inferred from equilibrium Boltzmann factors alone.

In particular, weak reactions cease to maintain [neutron-proton chemical equilibrium](../../../../neutron-proton-chemical-equilibrium.md). Subsequent [free-neutron decay after freeze-out](../../../../free-neutron-decay-after-freeze-out.md) changes the surviving neutron inventory. The large photon-to-baryon ratio delays deuterium survival through photodissociation; once the [deuterium bottleneck](../../../../deuterium-bottleneck.md) opens, reactions rapidly bind many surviving neutrons into helium-4. Coulomb barriers, decreasing density and further freeze-out prevent unrestricted equilibration of all nuclei. The final helium abundance therefore requires a reaction network and expansion history, not just the temperature and nuclear binding energies.

For illustration, if essentially all available neutrons end in helium-4 and the [neutron-to-proton ratio](../../../../neutron-to-proton-ratio.md) at that stage is $r$, simple particle counting gives $Y_{\rm He}\simeq2r/(1+r)$. The crucial input $r$ is itself set by out-of-equilibrium history. Kinetic thermalization of the plasma can remain effective while these composition-changing processes are out of equilibrium.
