<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The scaling solution has $\rho=3M^2p^2/t^2$ and $\rho+P=\dot\varphi^2=4M^2/(\lambda^2t^2)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{w=-1+\frac{\lambda^2}{3},\qquad q_0=\frac1p-1=\frac{\lambda^2}{2}-1.}
$$

More generally any real canonical [scalar field](../../../../scalar-field.md) with positive total [energy density](../../../../energy-density.md) obeys $\rho+P=\dot\varphi^2\ge0$, so **it cannot have $w<-1$**. A negative potential does not reverse this conclusion.

The proper [particle horizon](../../../../particle-horizon.md) is $a(t)\int_0^t dt'/a(t')$. For $a\propto t^p$ the lower-limit integral diverges exactly when $p\ge1$. Thus, assuming the same power law extends to the initial boundary, there is no finite [particle horizon](../../../../particle-horizon.md) for $\boxed{0<\lambda^2\le2}$. For the strict inequality, $p>1$, the proper [cosmological event horizon](../../../../cosmological-event-horizon.md) and present age are

$$
\boxed{R_{\rm EH,0}=\frac{t_{\rm now}}{p-1}=\frac{2}{(2-\lambda^2)H_0},\qquad
t_{\rm now}=\frac{p}{H_0}=\frac{2}{\lambda^2H_0}.}
$$

At $\lambda^2=2$, $a\propto t$ has an infinite particle-horizon integral but also an infinite future event-horizon integral, so no finite event horizon exists. The age remains $H_0^{-1}$. The $\lambda=0$ constant-potential case is separate: its flat [de Sitter](../../../../de-sitter-spacetime.md) patch has event-horizon radius $H_0^{-1}$ and no finite-age power-law [Big Bang](../../../../big-bang.md).
