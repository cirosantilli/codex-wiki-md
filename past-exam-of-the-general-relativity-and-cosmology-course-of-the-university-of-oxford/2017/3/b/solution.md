<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a nonzero plane-wave [trace-reversed metric perturbation](../../../../trace-reversed-metric-perturbation.md),

$$
\Box\left(A^{\alpha\beta}e^{ik_\gamma x^\gamma}\right)
=-(k_\gamma k^\gamma)A^{\alpha\beta}e^{ik_\gamma x^\gamma}.
$$

The [wave equation](../../../../wave-equation-split.md) therefore requires a null [wave vector](../../../../wavevector.md). The [Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity](../../../../lorenz-gauge-in-linearized-gravity.md) supplies transversality:

$$
\boxed{k_\gamma k^\gamma=0,\qquad k_\alpha A^{\alpha\beta}=0.}
$$

The amplitude is symmetric because it represents a [metric perturbation](../../../../linearized-gravity-split.md); its real part is the physical wave. The two displayed conditions do not by themselves make it spatially transverse and traceless: the additional reduction to [transverse-traceless gauge](../../../../transverse-traceless-gauge.md) uses the remaining [residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity](../../../../residual-gauge-symmetry-of-linearized-gravity.md).
