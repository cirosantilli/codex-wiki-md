<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Normalize the present [scale factor](../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md) to $a_0=1$. Conservation of the number of electrons associated with the baryons makes their physical [number density](../../../../number-density.md) scale as $a^{-3}$. At this early epoch they are free rather than bound in neutral atoms, so

$$
\boxed{
n_e=n_{e,0}\left(\frac{a_0}{a}\right)^3
=0.2(10^6)^3
=2\times10^{17}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}.
}
$$

Here the specified present density is interpreted as the conserved total electron abundance, including electrons that might be bound today. A present free-electron density would require an ionization-fraction correction.

Density alone does not determine whether a gas is relativistic; its random kinetic energy must be compared with the rest energy. In the usual thermal [Hot Big Bang model](../../../../hot-big-bang-model.md), use the measured [cosmic microwave background](../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-split.md) temperature $T_0\simeq2.7\,\mathrm K$ and the [temperature scaling of thermal photon number and energy densities](../../../../temperature-scaling-of-thermal-photon-number-and-energy-densities.md), $T_\gamma\propto a^{-1}$. Tight coupling then gives

$$
T_e\simeq T_\gamma\simeq2.7\times10^6\,\mathrm K,\qquad
k_BT_e\simeq230\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

Since $m_ec^2\simeq5.1\times10^5\,\mathrm{eV}$,

$$
\boxed{
\frac{k_BT_e}{m_ec^2}\simeq4.5\times10^{-4}\ll1:
\quad\text{the electrons are nonrelativistic}.
}
$$

Their thermal speed is of order $\sqrt{3k_BT_e/m_e}\simeq0.04c$. The gas is fully ionized because its temperature is far above the [recombination temperature](../../../../recombination-temperature.md), but ionization does not imply relativistic electrons. Thermal electron-positron pairs are exponentially suppressed at this temperature.[Section 2, thermal history and recombination: present photon temperature, electron mass scale, and temperature-redshift scaling](../../../../cosmology-by-david-tong.md)

The given age and scale-factor ratio should be treated as independent supplied estimates in the following rate comparison. They do not describe the same epoch in standard radiation-era expansion: $a/a_0=10^{-6}$ normally corresponds to an age of order a year, not $10^4$ years. The density and temperature estimates above depend on the scale-factor ratio, not on assigning it the stated age.
