<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For statistically independent [Thomson scattering](../../../../thomson-scattering.md) events, the probability of no scattering over a proper distance $s$ is $\exp(-n_e\sigma_e s)$. The [photon mean free path in an ionized universe](../../../../photon-mean-free-path-in-an-ionized-universe.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{
\ell_\gamma=\frac1{n_e\sigma_e}
=\frac1{(2\times10^{17})(6.7\times10^{-29})}
\simeq7.5\times10^{10}\,\mathrm m.
}
$$

This is about half an [astronomical unit](../../../../astronomical-unit.md). It is a proper distance at that epoch, not a present-day comoving distance. The [Thomson scattering](../../../../thomson-scattering.md) approximation is appropriate because the electrons are nonrelativistic and typical photon energies are much smaller than $m_ec^2$. The source's spelling of the process is a typographical error; the physical process is Thomson scattering.
