<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

As the [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) falls, electrons and protons form neutral [hydrogen](../../../../hydrogen.md) through [cosmological recombination](../../../../recombination-cosmology.md). The free [electron](../../../../electron.md) fraction drops sharply, and so does the scattering rate $\Gamma_\gamma=n_e\sigma_Tc$. When this rate becomes comparable to or smaller than the [Hubble parameter](../../../../hubble-parameter.md), the plasma becomes transparent and [photon decoupling](../../../../photon-decoupling.md) occurs. The [Thomson scattering](../../../../thomson-scattering.md) cross-section itself does not abruptly vanish. The rapid change is in the abundance of free targets.

The [Hydrogen binding energy](../../../../hydrogen-binding-energy.md) is $13.6\,\mathrm{eV}$, but recombination occurs at a much smaller thermal energy, of order $0.3\,\mathrm{eV}$, or a few thousand kelvin. There are roughly a billion photons per baryon, so the high-energy tail can keep hydrogen ionized long after $k_BT$ drops below the binding energy. The falling exponential ionizing tail then makes the transition comparatively sharp. Last scattering follows when sufficiently few electrons remain free.[Section 2, recombination and photon decoupling: small baryon-to-photon ratio, thermal tail, and the distinction between recombination and last scattering](../../../../cosmology-by-david-tong.md)

To estimate a horizon, specify an expansion model as well as the redshift. The proper [particle horizon](../../../../particle-horizon.md) at emission is

$$
d_{\mathrm{ph}}(t_*)=a(t_*)\int_0^{t_*}\frac{c\,dt}{a(t)}.
$$

As a transparent analytic estimate, approximate a spatially flat [matter-dominated universe](../../../../matter-domination.md) by $a(t)=(t/t_{\mathrm{now}})^{2/3}$ throughout. Then

$$
d_{\mathrm{ph}}(t_*)=3ct_*,
\qquad
t_*=\frac{t_{\mathrm{now}}}{(1+z_*)^{3/2}}.
$$

Taking the illustrative present value $H_0=70\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, this model has $t_{\mathrm{now}}=2/(3H_0)\simeq9.3\times10^9\,\mathrm{yr}$. At $z_*=10^3$,

$$
\boxed{
t_*\simeq3\times10^5\,\mathrm{yr},\qquad
d_{\mathrm{ph}}(t_*)\simeq0.27\,\mathrm{Mpc}.
}
$$

This is a proper horizon radius at emission; its corresponding present comoving length is about $270\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.

For the same model the comoving distance to the [Cosmic microwave background last-scattering surface](../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-last-scattering-surface.md) and its [angular diameter distance](../../../../angular-diameter-distance.md) are

$$
\chi_*=3ct_{\mathrm{now}}
\left[1-(1+z_*)^{-1/2}\right],
\qquad
D_A=\frac{\chi_*}{1+z_*}.
$$

Therefore the angular extent associated with one radial horizon length is

$$
\boxed{
\theta_{\mathrm{ph}}\simeq\frac{d_{\mathrm{ph}}}{D_A}
=\frac1{\sqrt{1+z_*}-1}
\simeq0.033\,\mathrm{rad}\simeq1.9^\circ.
}
$$

This is an order-one-degree estimate. Including the earlier [radiation domination](../../../../radiation-domination.md) and late [dark energy](../../../../dark-energy.md) changes the coefficient; it does not make the horizon span the sky. A horizon patch diameter has twice this angle, whereas the acoustic scale uses a [sound horizon](../../../../sound-horizon.md) and differs again. The redshift by itself, without an expansion model or normalization, is insufficient for a unique horizon distance.

The [horizon problem](../../../../horizon-problem.md) is that the observed [cosmic microwave background](../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-split.md) has nearly the same mean [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) across angular separations vastly greater than this scale. In the purely decelerating [Hot Big Bang model](../../../../hot-big-bang-model.md), sufficiently separated last-scattering regions had neither time to exchange signals nor a common post-Big-Bang causal region that could establish their observed thermal uniformity. The puzzle concerns the missing causal explanation of those initial correlations; it is not an inconsistency of specifying uniform initial conditions mathematically. An earlier period of [cosmic inflation](../../../../cosmic-inflation-split.md) can enlarge a formerly causally connected region beyond the later observable horizon.
