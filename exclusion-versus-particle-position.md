# Exclusion versus particle position

↑ **Parent:** [Pauli exclusion principle](pauli-exclusion-principle.md)

Exclusion constrains complete [one-particle states](one-particle-state.md), not a collection of little spatial boxes. In particular, two opposite-[spin](spin.md) [electrons](electron.md) in the same [spatial orbital](spatial-orbital.md) have

$$
\Psi(\mathbf r_1,s_1;\mathbf r_2,s_2)=\phi_a(\mathbf r_1)\phi_a(\mathbf r_2)\frac{\chi_\uparrow(s_1)\chi_\downarrow(s_2)-\chi_\downarrow(s_1)\chi_\uparrow(s_2)}{\sqrt2}.
$$

The spatial factor is symmetric; the [spin-one-half singlet state](spin-one-half-singlet-state.md) supplies the required antisymmetry. At $\mathbf r_1=\mathbf r_2$, this [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) need not vanish for opposite [spin](spin.md) labels. For equal [spin](spin.md) labels it does vanish. In a continuous position description, an exact coincident point has zero measure anyway; the useful comparison concerns joint [probability densities](probability-density.md) or detection in finite regions. Equal positions do not imply equal complete [quantum states](quantum-state.md).

The [fermionic occupation numbers](fermionic-occupation-number.md) $n_\alpha=0,1$ refer to a chosen [orthonormal basis](orthonormal-basis.md) of [one-particle states](one-particle-state.md). A [quantum superposition](quantum-superposition.md) need not have definite [occupation numbers](occupation-number.md) in that basis, and an [expectation value](expectation-value.md) $\langle n_\alpha\rangle$ can be fractional. For example, one [electron](electron.md) in $(u+v)/\sqrt2$ has mean [occupation number](occupation-number.md) $1/2$ in each of two [orthogonal](orthogonal-vectors.md) [spin orbitals](spin-orbital.md), without splitting into two particles. The [canonical anticommutation relations](canonical-anticommutation-relations.md) give $(a_\alpha^\dagger)^2=0$ and make $a_\alpha^\dagger a_\alpha$ a [projector](projection-linear-algebra.md), so its [expectation value](expectation-value.md) stays between zero and one in every normalized [quantum state](quantum-state.md).
