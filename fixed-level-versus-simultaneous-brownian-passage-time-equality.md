# Fixed-level versus simultaneous Brownian passage-time equality

↑ **Parent:** [Brownian first-passage time](brownian-first-passage-time.md)

For standard [Brownian motion](brownian-motion-split.md), set $H_a=\inf\{t:B_t\geq a\}$ and $T_a=\inf\{t:B_t>a\}$. The [Strong Markov property](strong-markov-property.md) and immediate positive excursions give $T_a=H_a$ [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md) for each fixed $a\geq0$. However, these level-indexed processes are not [indistinguishable stochastic processes](indistinguishability-of-stochastic-processes.md). [Almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md) the random level $A=\sup_{t\leq1}B_t$ is positive and exceeds $B_1$. It is first attained before time one but is not exceeded until after time one, so $H_A<1<T_A$. The simultaneous-equality [event](event.md) therefore has probability zero. The strict passage process $T_a$ is the right-continuous choice needed for a [subordinator](subordinator.md).
