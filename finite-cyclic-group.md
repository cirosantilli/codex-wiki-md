# Finite cyclic group

↑ **Parent:** [Cyclic group](cyclic-group.md)

A [finite cyclic group](finite-cyclic-group.md) of order $n$ is isomorphic to the additive [group](group-split.md) $C_n=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$. The [group isomorphism](group-isomorphism.md) sends the [congruence class](congruence-class.md) of $k$ to $g^k$, where $g$ is a chosen [generator of a group](generator-of-a-group.md). For $n=6$, the [identity element](identity-element.md) is $0$, $4+5=3$ modulo six, and the [inverse element](inverse-element.md) of $4$ is $2$.

One [finite cyclic group](finite-cyclic-group.md) can have several choices of [generator of a group](generator-of-a-group.md). Adding $1$ visits all six elements before returning to zero; adding $5$ also visits all six, in the reverse order. Adding $2$ reaches only $\{0,2,4\}$ from zero, and adding $3$ reaches only $\{0,3\}$. The following [permutations](permutation.md) of the six elements show what each repeated step can reach.

<a id="image-repeated-addition-modulo-six-one-cycle-two-cycles-or-three-cycles"></a>
![](cyclic-group-generator-cycles.png)

**[Figure 2](#image-repeated-addition-modulo-six-one-cycle-two-cycles-or-three-cycles). Repeated addition modulo six: one cycle, two cycles, or three cycles**. Each arrow applies $x\mapsto x+k$ modulo six. The panels use $k=1,2,3$. Colors distinguish the separate cycles; only the cycle containing zero is the [cyclic subgroup](cyclic-subgroup.md) generated by $k$. For $k=2$, the other cycle is the [coset](coset.md) $\{1,3,5\}$; for $k=3$, the other two cycles are its other [cosets](coset.md).

This gives a precise version of a familiar scheduling question: does a fixed step visit every position, or repeatedly miss some? The [generators of a finite cyclic group](generators-of-a-finite-cyclic-group.md) are exactly the steps [coprime](coprime-integers.md) to the total number of positions. Changing the step changes the orbit through the same underlying [group](group-split.md); the diagram does not replace its [group operation](group-operation.md) with a different one.
