# Hardy-Weinberg principle

↑ **Parent:** [Genetics](genetics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Weinberg_principle)

The [Hardy-Weinberg principle](hardy-weinberg-principle.md) describes the [genotype](genotype.md) proportions produced by independently combining two parental [gametes](gamete.md) from the same [allele](allele.md) pool. For [alleles](allele.md) $A,a$ with [allele frequencies](allele-frequency.md) $p,q$, where $p+q=1$, the [Hardy-Weinberg proportions](hardy-weinberg-principle.md) are

$$
P(AA)=p^2,\qquad P(Aa)=2pq,\qquad P(aa)=q^2.
$$

The two ordered [heterozygote](heterozygote.md) outcomes contribute $pq+qp$. These proportions sum to $(p+q)^2=1$, and their implied $A$ [allele frequency](allele-frequency.md) is $p^2+(2pq)/2=p$. Reshuffling copies therefore preserves their frequencies.

For stable [Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium](hardy-weinberg-principle.md) across generations, use a large population with [random mating](panmixia.md), equal [allele frequencies](allele-frequency.md) in the parental pools, ordinary [Mendelian segregation](mendelian-segregation.md), and no [natural selection](natural-selection.md), [mutation](mutation.md), [gene flow](gene-flow.md) or [genetic drift](genetic-drift.md). Negligible [genetic drift](genetic-drift.md) is an idealization of large population size. The [one-generation random-mating equilibrium](one-generation-random-mating-equilibrium.md) needs independent union of [gametes](gamete.md); persistence additionally requires processes that change [allele frequencies](allele-frequency.md) to be absent.

At $p=0.70,q=0.30$, the predicted [genotype](genotype.md) proportions are $0.49,0.42,0.09$. For 100 sampled individuals the [expected values](expected-value-split.md) of the counts are $49,42,9$, rather than the $25,50,25$ of a specified $Aa\times Aa$ cross. The latter fixes both parental [genotypes](genotype.md); [Hardy-Weinberg proportions](hardy-weinberg-principle.md) average over population mating.

An observed population need not have these proportions. For example, $70\ AA$, no $Aa$, and $30\ aa$ among 100 individuals still gives $p=0.70$, but has no [heterozygotes](heterozygote.md). [Random mating](panmixia.md) predicts $0.42$ [heterozygosity](heterozygosity.md) in the next generation under the stated model. A finite sample can also depart by chance, so a discrepancy is a starting point for investigating [inbreeding](inbreeding.md), [population stratification](population-stratification.md) or [genotyping error](genotyping-error.md), rather than proof of one cause.

Hardy's 1908 note made the conceptual payoff explicit: a dominant [phenotype](phenotype.md) does not by itself make its [allele](allele.md) spread. [Complete dominance](complete-dominance.md) describes expression; changes in frequency require a separate mechanism such as [natural selection](natural-selection.md). See [Hardy, Mendelian Proportions in a Mixed Population, Science 28 (1908), 49–50](https://www-leland.stanford.edu/~huatang/gene244/readings/HardyWeinberg.pdf).
