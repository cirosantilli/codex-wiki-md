# Low-temperature energy of gapless bosonic excitations

↑ **Parent:** [Bose-Einstein distribution](bose-einstein-distribution.md)

In $d$ dimensions, isotropic modes with dispersion $\epsilon=A k^s$ have [density of states](density-of-states.md) proportional to $\epsilon^{d/s-1}$. Their equilibrium number is not conserved, so the excitation [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) is zero. Integrating $\epsilon D(\epsilon)/(e^{\epsilon/(k_BT)}-1)$ and rescaling by $k_BT$ gives the displayed energy power and [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) proportional to $T^{d/s}$. In three dimensions, linear acoustic [phonons](phonon.md) give $T^4$ energy and $T^3$ heat capacity; quadratic gapless ferromagnetic [magnons](magnon.md) give $T^{5/2}$ energy and $T^{3/2}$ heat capacity. A gap changes the asymptotically low-temperature law to activated behaviour. The mode approximation applies below the relevant band cutoff and when interactions between excitations are negligible.
