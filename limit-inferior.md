# Limit inferior

↑ **Parent:** [Limit inferior and limit superior](limit-inferior-and-limit-superior.md)

The limit inferior of a real [sequence](sequence.md) is

$$
\liminf_{n\to\infty}a_n=\sup_N\inf_{n\geq N}a_n,
$$

allowing a value in the [extended real number line](extended-real-number-line.md). For a finite value $L$, it has two complementary meanings: for every $\varepsilon>0$, all sufficiently late terms satisfy $a_n>L-\varepsilon$, while arbitrarily late terms satisfy $a_n<L+\varepsilon$. The first prevents persistent dips below $L$; the second prevents any larger number from being the true lower edge.

For $a_n=(-1)^n(1+1/n)$, the lower bounds in [limit inferior and limit superior](limit-inferior-and-limit-superior.md) approach $-1$. Every late term eventually exceeds $-1-\varepsilon$, and the [subsequence](subsequence.md) at [odd integers](odd-integer.md) comes arbitrarily close to $-1$. For $a_n=1/n$, the lower bound of every infinite [tail of a sequence](tail-of-a-sequence.md) is already zero, although no term equals zero. Thus a [limit inferior](limit-inferior.md) need not be the [minimum](minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) of any tail.
