# Completeness of a continuous weighted uniform norm

↑ **Parent:** [Supremum norm](supremum-norm.md)

Let $K$ be a nonempty [compact space](compact-space.md) and let $\alpha:K\to\mathbb R$ be continuous and nowhere zero. On $C(K)$, define $\|f\|_\alpha=\sup_{x\in K}|\alpha(x)f(x)|$. Multiplication by $\alpha$ is an isometry onto $C(K)$ with its ordinary [uniform norm](supremum-norm.md), because division by the continuous nonvanishing $\alpha$ provides the inverse. Thus the weighted space is a [Banach space](banach-space-split.md). Equivalently, the [extreme value theorem](extreme-value-theorem.md) gives $0<m=\min|\alpha|\leq\max|\alpha|=M<\infty$, and $m\|f\|_\infty\leq\|f\|_\alpha\leq M\|f\|_\infty$. A weighted [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md) is uniformly Cauchy, has a continuous uniform limit, and the upper bound gives convergence also in the weighted norm.
