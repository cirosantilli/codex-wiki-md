# Two-dimensional Landau effective-mass relation

↑ **Parent:** [Fermi-liquid effective mass](fermi-liquid-effective-mass.md)

For an isotropic spinless continuum [Fermi liquid](fermi-liquid-split.md) with the [Principle of Galilean relativity](principle-of-galilean-relativity.md), let $D^*=Am^*/(2\pi\hbar^2)$ be the total [density of states](density-of-states.md) and use a [Landau quasiparticle energy functional](landau-quasiparticle-energy-functional.md) whose double sum has no explicit $1/A$. Then

$$
\frac{m^*}{m}=1+D^*\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\,f(\phi)\cos\phi.
$$

Indeed, a small boost has radial displacement $p_0\cos\theta$. The one-[quasiparticle](quasiparticle.md) term costs $Np_0^2/(2m^*)$, and the quadratic interaction multiplies that by $D^*\int f\cos\phi\,d\phi/(2\pi)$, since $\int\cos(\theta+\phi)\cos\theta\,d\theta=\pi\cos\phi$. Matching the [Galilean boost energy of an interacting gas](galilean-boost-energy-of-an-interacting-gas.md) proves the formula. With [Fourier series](fourier-series-split.md) $D^*f=F_0+\sum_{\ell\geq1}F_\ell\cos(\ell\phi)$, the angular average is $F_1/2$. If the functional instead contains $1/A$, use its intensive kernel $Af$ and the [density of states](density-of-states.md) per area.
