# Algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Area of mathematics](area-of-mathematics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra)

Algebra studies [equations](equation.md), [algebraic operations](algebraic-operation-split.md), and the structures that make their rules work. Its elementary side expresses unknown quantities through [variables](variable-mathematics.md) and transforms [mathematical expressions](expression-mathematics.md); its structural side asks which arguments apply to whole families of [groups](group-split.md), [rings](ring.md), [fields](field.md), and [vector spaces](vector-space-split.md). The useful step is identifying exactly which properties a calculation needs, so that it can be reused in a new setting.

For example, the [polynomial equation](polynomial-equation.md) $x^2-3x+2=0$ over the [real numbers](real-number.md) factors as $(x-1)(x-2)=0$, giving roots $1$ and $2$. The [distributive property](distributive-property.md) justifies the factorization, while the absence of [zero divisors](zero-divisor.md) justifies concluding that one factor must vanish. Over the [finite field](finite-field.md) $\mathbb F_2$, the same integer coefficients reduce to $x^2+x=x(x+1)$, with roots $0$ and $1$. The symbols alone do not specify the arithmetic: the coefficient [field](field.md) is part of the problem.

In a [ring](ring.md) with [zero divisors](zero-divisor.md), a familiar step can fail. Modulo $8$, all four residue classes $1,3,5,7$ satisfy $x^2=1$. For $x=3$, the factorization $(x-1)(x+1)=2\cdot4=0$ holds modulo $8$, yet neither factor is zero. This is why the [field](field.md) hypothesis is useful when reasoning about [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md), and why solving an [equation](equation.md) over the [integers](integer-split.md), over the [real numbers](real-number.md), or in [modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic-split.md) can give different answers.

The structure is also useful beyond scalar arithmetic. [Matrix multiplication](matrix-multiplication.md) composes [linear maps](linear-map.md), and multiplication in a [group](group-split.md) can compose [symmetries](symmetry-physics.md). These operations need not be [commutative](commutativity.md), but [associativity](associative-property.md) still permits a product of three transformations to be regrouped. A theorem depending only on [associativity](associative-property.md), an [identity element](identity-element.md), and [inverse elements](inverse-element.md) can apply to many different [groups](group-split.md) at once. A [homomorphism](homomorphism.md) identifies which operations survive when one structure is mapped to another.

Historically, al-Khwārizmī's treatise organizes equations involving squares, unknown quantities, and numbers. Frederic Rosen's 1831 English translation includes the calculation now written $x^2+10x=39$ and geometric explanations for [completing the square](completing-the-square.md). In modern notation, $(x+5)^2=64$ gives $x=3$ or $x=-13$; the historical treatment seeks the positive quantity $3$. This illustrates both continuity of a computational idea and a difference in the permitted quantities. [Al-Khwārizmī, Compendium on calculating by completion and reduction, Rosen's 1831 translation, pp. 5–11](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Compendious_Book_on_Calculation_by_Completion_and_Balancing/Compendium_on_calculating_by_completion_and_reduction)
