# Vanishing two-to-one forcing coefficient for Stokes convection

↑ **Parent:** [Spatially forced convection amplitude equation](spatially-forced-convection-amplitude-equation.md)

In the free-slip [Stokes flow](stokes-flow-split.md) [temperature](temperature.md) model, let the critical [temperature](temperature.md) be $g=\sin\pi z$ and vertical [velocity](velocity.md) $f=sg$, $s=k_c^2+\pi^2$. A forced positive second harmonic $(W,\Theta)e^{2ik_cx}$ couples to the negative critical harmonic. Its [temperature](temperature.md) [solvability condition](solvability-condition.md) has integrand

$$
g(2f'\Theta+f\Theta'+W'g/2+Wg')=(sg^2\Theta+g^2W/2)'.
$$

The identity follows by substituting $f=sg$ and differentiating. Its integral is zero because $g$ vanishes on both plates, regardless of the forced boundary value of $\Theta$. Thus the first resonant forcing coefficient vanishes. A generic nonzero conjugate-amplitude term cannot be inferred from wave-number matching alone.
