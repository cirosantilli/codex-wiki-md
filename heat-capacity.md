# Heat capacity

↑ **Parent:** [Heat](heat-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_capacity)

Heat capacity measures the [heat](heat-split.md) supplied per small [temperature](temperature-split.md) increase along a specified process:

$$
C_X=\left(\frac{\delta Q}{dT}\right)_X.
$$

The subscript $X$ records the constraint, such as fixed [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) or fixed [pressure](pressure-split.md). The units are $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$. The symbol $\delta Q$ emphasizes that [heat](heat-split.md) is a transfer along a path, rather than a [state function](state-function.md) with a differential $dQ$. Consequently, an unspecified [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) does not determine the required [heat](heat-split.md).

For a [closed system](closed-system.md) of fixed composition undergoing a [quasistatic process](quasistatic-process.md), assume that only [pressure](pressure-split.md)-[volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) [work](work.md) occurs, with no [friction](friction.md) and no change in bulk [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) or [potential energy](potential-energy.md). The [first law of thermodynamics](first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) and $H=U+pV$, where $U$ is [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md) and $H$ is [enthalpy](enthalpy-split.md), then give

$$
\delta Q=dU+p\,dV=dH-V\,dp,\qquad
C_V=\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial T}\right)_V,\qquad
C_p=\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial T}\right)_p.
$$

These [partial derivatives](partial-derivative.md) define [heat capacity at constant volume](heat-capacity-at-constant-volume.md) and [heat capacity at constant pressure](heat-capacity-at-constant-pressure.md), with the amount and composition also held fixed. Integrating along the selected constraint gives $Q=\int_{T_1}^{T_2}C_X(T)\,dT$ within a single [thermodynamic phase](thermodynamic-phase.md); $Q=C_X\Delta T$ requires constant $C_X$. [4 Classical Thermodynamics](statistical-physics-by-david-tong/4-classical-thermodynamics.md)

For a computed example, take a [monatomic gas](monatomic-gas.md) in the classical [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) regime, with [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) $n=1\,\mathrm{mol}$ and stipulated [molar gas constant](molar-gas-constant.md) $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$. The [equipartition theorem](equipartition-theorem.md) gives $U=3nRT/2$, and the [ideal gas law](ideal-gas-law.md) gives $pV=nRT$. Thus

$$
C_V=\frac32nR=12.471\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}},\qquad
C_p=\frac52nR=20.785\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$

For the same [temperature](temperature-split.md) increase $\Delta T=10\,\mathrm K$, the two processes require

$$
Q_V=124.71\,\mathrm J,\qquad Q_p=207.85\,\mathrm J,\qquad
\Delta U_V=\Delta U_p=124.71\,\mathrm J,\qquad
W_V=0,\quad W_p=p\Delta V=nR\Delta T=83.14\,\mathrm J.
$$

Here [work](work.md) done by the [gas](gas.md) is positive. At fixed [pressure](pressure-split.md), the extra [heat](heat-split.md) supplies expansion [work](work.md); the [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md) increase is the same because [internal energy of an ideal gas](internal-energy-of-an-ideal-gas.md) depends only on [temperature](temperature-split.md). Their final [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) values differ. [OpenStax, Heat capacities of an ideal gas](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/3-5-heat-capacities-of-an-ideal-gas)

Once its constraint and [temperature](temperature-split.md) dependence are known, it predicts heating requirements and supplies [entropy](entropy-split.md) changes through [entropy from heat capacity](entropy-from-heat-capacity.md).
