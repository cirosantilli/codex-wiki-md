# Differential magnetic susceptibility

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic susceptibility](magnetic-susceptibility.md)

[Differential magnetic susceptibility](differential-magnetic-susceptibility.md) is the local slope at a specified bias [magnetic field intensity](magnetic-field-intensity.md), holding [temperature](temperature.md) and other stated constraints fixed. It equals [volume magnetic susceptibility](volume-magnetic-susceptibility.md) only for a linear response through the origin. For example, $M=M_s\tanh(H/H_s)$ has $M/H=(M_s/H_s)\tanh(x)/x$, but $dM/dH=(M_s/H_s)/\cosh^2(x)$, where $x=H/H_s$. At $x=1$ these are about $0.762$ and $0.420$ times their common zero-field slope. A finite sweep chord is another quantity, not automatically this [derivative](derivative.md).

If the differentiated field is instead the internal [magnetic flux density](magnetic-field.md), the longitudinal [chain rule](chain-rule.md) applied to $B_{\rm int}=\mu_0(H_{\rm int}+M)$ gives

$$
\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial B_{\rm int}}\right)_T
=\frac{\chi_{\rm diff}}{\mu_0(1+\chi_{\rm diff})}.
$$

This differs from the vacuum/applied-$B_0$ conversion in [volume magnetic susceptibility](volume-magnetic-susceptibility.md). It assumes a differentiable single branch with locally invertible $B_{\rm int}(H_{\rm int})$.
