A [connected set](../../../../../connected-set.md) is one whose relative topology admits no decomposition into two disjoint nonempty open sets. A [path-connected](../../../../../path-connected-space.md) set permits, between any two points, a continuous map $\gamma:[0,1]\to A$ with the prescribed endpoints.

Path connectedness implies connectedness, because a continuous image of an interval is connected. The converse can fail, for example for the [topologist's sine curve](../../../../../topologist-s-sine-curve.md). On open subsets of Euclidean spaces $\mathbb R^n$, the two properties are equivalent; more generally this holds in locally path-connected spaces.
