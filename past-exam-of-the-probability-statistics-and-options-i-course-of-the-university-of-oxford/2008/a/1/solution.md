↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The hypothesis is a [two-transitive group action](../../../../two-transitive-group-action.md). Given $\alpha,\beta\ne\omega_1$, apply it to $(\omega_1,\alpha)$ and $(\omega_1,\beta)$. The resulting element fixes $\omega_1$ and sends $\alpha$ to $\beta$, so the [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) acts transitively on the remaining $n-1$ points.

The action on all of $\Omega$ is also transitive. By the [orbit-stabilizer theorem](../../../../orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md),

$$
|G|=n|\operatorname{Stab}_G(\omega_1)|,\qquad
|\operatorname{Stab}_G(\omega_1)|=(n-1)|\operatorname{Stab}_G(\omega_1,\omega_2)|.
$$

Thus $\boxed{n(n-1)\mid |G|}$. The diagonal pairs form one orbit, while two-transitivity makes all ordered unequal pairs a second orbit. Equality is preserved by a group action, so these cannot mix. There are **exactly two orbits on $\Omega\times\Omega$**.
