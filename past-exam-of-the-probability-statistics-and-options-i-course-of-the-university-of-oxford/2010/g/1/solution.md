<h1 id="g/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Independent endpoint-fixed variations of the two coordinates give the [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md)

$$
\frac d{dt}F_{\dot u}-F_u=0,\qquad
\frac d{dt}F_{\dot v}-F_v=0.
$$

Differentiating the sphere parametrization gives the [Riemannian metric](../../../../riemannian-metric-split.md) $ds^2=a^2(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2)$. Its energy functional is the stated $I$. Its stationary paths are [geodesics](../../../../geodesic.md) with affine, constant-speed parametrization; conversely a geodesic can be so parametrized. This explains the use of energy rather than arc length.

For $F=a^2(\dot\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\dot\phi^2)/2$, the equations are

$$
\boxed{\ddot\theta-\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\dot\phi^2=0,\qquad
\frac d{dt}(\sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi)=0.}
$$

The second gives the conserved [generalized momentum](../../../../generalized-momentum.md) $\sin^2\theta\dot\phi=h$. Since $F$ has no explicit time dependence, its [Beltrami identity](../../../../beltrami-identity.md) gives constant $\dot\theta F_{\dot\theta}+\dot\phi F_{\dot\phi}-F=F$. Dividing by $a^2/2$ gives the second first integral,

$$
\boxed{\dot\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\dot\phi^2=E.}
$$

Thus $E$ is the squared speed divided by $a^2$, while $h$ records the rotational symmetry about the polar axis. The coordinate equations apply away from the polar coordinate singularities.
