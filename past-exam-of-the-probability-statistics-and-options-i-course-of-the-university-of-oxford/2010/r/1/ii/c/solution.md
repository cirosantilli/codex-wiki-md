<h1 id="r/1/ii/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A triangle $x_1y_1y_2$ forces at least three vertex colours. Three suffice: colour $x_1,x_2$ blue; $y_1,y_3,y_4$ orange; and $y_2,y_5$ green, as in the drawing. Hence **the [chromatic number](../../../../../../chromatic-number.md) is three**.

The five [edges](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) incident to $x_1$ force at least five edge colours. An explicit proper five-edge-colouring is

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 &y_1&y_2&y_3&y_4&y_5\\\hline
x_1y_j&1&2&3&4&5\\
x_2y_j&2&3&4&5&1
\end{array}
$$

Give $y_1y_2$ colour 5 and $y_1y_5$ colour 3. At $y_1$ the incident colours are $1,2,5,3$; at $y_2$ they are $2,3,5$; at $y_5$ they are $5,1,3$; all are distinct. The two remaining [vertices](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) each have two distinct incident colours. Thus **the [edge chromatic number](../../../../../../edge-chromatic-number.md) is five**.
