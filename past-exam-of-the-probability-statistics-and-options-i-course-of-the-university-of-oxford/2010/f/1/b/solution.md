<h1 id="f/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For fixed $x,y$, the map $z\mapsto z^3+z+xy-1$ is strictly increasing because its derivative is $3z^2+1>0$, and it tends to opposite infinities at the two ends of the real line. The [intermediate value theorem](../../../../../intermediate-value-theorem.md) gives exactly one [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md), defining $g(x,y)$.

For $F(x,y,z)=z^3+z+xy-1$, $F_z=3z^2+1$ never vanishes. The [implicit function theorem](../../../../../implicit-function-theorem.md) supplies a $C^1$ solution locally around every $(x,y)$. Uniqueness makes all these local functions agree on overlaps, proving $g\in C^1(\mathbb R^2)$. Implicit differentiation gives

$$
Dg(x,y)=-\frac{(y,x)}{3g(x,y)^2+1}.
$$

At $(1,1)$ the [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) is zero, so **$Dg(1,1)=(-1,-1)$**.
