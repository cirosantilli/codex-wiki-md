<h1 id="a/1/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Reduce the monic integer [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) modulo $p$. Every $u\in\mathbb F_p$ satisfies $u^p=u$, so $u^p-u-1=-1\ne0$. Thus the reduction has no [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) in $\mathbb F_p$. If it had a proper factor, part 2 would force such a [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md), a contradiction. Hence it is [irreducible](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) over $\mathbb F_p$.

The criterion of [irreducibility by degree-preserving reduction modulo a prime](../../../../../irreducibility-by-degree-preserving-reduction-modulo-a-prime.md), which follows from [Gauss's lemma](../../../../../gauss-s-lemma-riemannian-geometry.md), now applies because the [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is monic and its [degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) is preserved on reduction. Consequently **$X^p-X-1$ is [irreducible](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) in $\mathbb Q[X]$**.
