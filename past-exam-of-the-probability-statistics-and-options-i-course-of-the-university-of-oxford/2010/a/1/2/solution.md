<h1 id="a/1/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The intended hypothesis must be a proper monic factor, with $1\leq r\leq p-1$. Without that restriction the printed deduction is false: every [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) has the displayed trivial factorization with $r=p$, even when it has no [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) in $F$.

By part 1, the roots in a [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md) are distinct and have the form $\alpha+j$. The [degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md)-$r$ monic factor is the product of $r$ corresponding linear factors. Comparing the coefficient of $X^{r-1}$ using [Vieta's formulas](../../../../../vieta-s-formulas.md) gives

$$
-a_1=\sum_{j\in J}(\alpha+j)=r\alpha+\sum_{j\in J}j,\qquad |J|=r.
$$

Both $a_1$ and the sum of prime-[field](../../../../../field.md) elements lie in $F$. Since $1\leq r<p$, the scalar $r$ is nonzero and invertible in $F$. Therefore $\alpha=(-a_1-\sum_{j\in J}j)/r\in F$, and part 1 proves **the [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) splits into distinct linear factors over $F$**. In particular an [Artin–Schreier polynomial](../../../../../artin-schreier-polynomial.md) of prime [degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) is either [irreducible](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) or splits completely.
