↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The observed statistic $94.34$ exceeds the supplied $0.999$ quantile $13.82$ for two degrees of freedom. Thus **reject [independence](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md), with $p<0.001$**.

For one child of each sex among the first two, the three-child count is $3393$, below its [independence](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) expectation $3763.1$. Equivalently, the two-child count is $13079$, above its expectation $16472-3763.1=12708.9$. These departures have exactly the direction predicted by the suggestion. Thus **the data support the proposed association**: mixed-sex first pairs are associated with a greater tendency to stop at two children.
