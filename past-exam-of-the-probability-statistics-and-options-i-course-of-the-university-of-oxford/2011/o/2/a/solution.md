The [Neyman-Pearson lemma](../../../../../neyman-pearson-lemma.md) concerns testing a simple null density $f_0$ against a simple alternative $f_1$. A test that rejects where $f_1/f_0>c$, accepts where the ratio is below $c$, and if necessary randomizes on the boundary to have null rejection probability $\alpha$, is a [most powerful test](../../../../../most-powerful-test.md) among tests of size at most $\alpha$. Thus a likelihood-ratio threshold maximizes power at the specified simple alternative. The boundary randomization is relevant for discrete distributions.
