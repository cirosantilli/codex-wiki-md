<h1 id="o/1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Since the true mean is known to be zero, the [chi-squared distribution](../../../../../chi-squared-distribution.md) gives $Q/\theta\sim\chi_n^2$. Let $q_\beta$ be the $\beta$ quantile of this law. The probability

$$
\mathbb P\left(q_{\alpha/2}\leq Q/\theta\leq q_{1-\alpha/2}\right)=1-\alpha
$$

rearranges, using positivity, to the exact [confidence interval](../../../../../confidence-interval.md)

$$
\boxed{\left[\frac{\sum_iX_i^2}{q_{1-\alpha/2}},\ \frac{\sum_iX_i^2}{q_{\alpha/2}}\right].}
$$

There are $n$ degrees of freedom, because no unknown mean was fitted.
