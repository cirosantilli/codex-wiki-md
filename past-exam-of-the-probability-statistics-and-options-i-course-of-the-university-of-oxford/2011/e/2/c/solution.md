<h1 id="e/2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the quotient map $q:X\to X/{\sim}$, the [quotient topology](../../../../../quotient-topology.md) is defined by

$$
U\subseteq X/{\sim}\text{ open}\iff q^{-1}(U)\text{ open in }X.
$$

Take the [Hausdorff space](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) $X=\mathbb R$ and identify two numbers precisely when they are both rational or both irrational. The quotient has two points. The inverse images of its singleton subsets are $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q$, neither of which is open in $\mathbb R$, since each is dense and has dense complement. Hence the quotient has the [indiscrete topology](../../../../../indiscrete-topology.md) and is not [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md).
