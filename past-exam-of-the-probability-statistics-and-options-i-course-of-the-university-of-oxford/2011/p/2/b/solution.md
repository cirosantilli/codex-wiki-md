<h1 id="p/2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Since $T_2=2x^2-1$, take $x_1=-1/\sqrt2$, $x_2=1/\sqrt2$. Exactness for $T_0$ and $T_1$ requires $A+B=\pi$ and $(-A+B)/\sqrt2=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{x_{1,2}=\mp\frac1{\sqrt2},\qquad A=B=\frac\pi2.}
$$

For $T_2$, both node values and its weighted integral vanish. For $T_3=4x^3-3x$, oddness and symmetric nodes likewise give zero on both sides. Therefore $R(T_j)=0$ for $j=0,1,2,3$. These four [Chebyshev polynomials](../../../../../chebyshev-polynomial.md) have successive degrees zero through three and form a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) of the degree-at-most-three polynomial space. By [linearity](../../../../../linearity.md), the rule is exact on that whole space: this is two-node [Gauss–Chebyshev quadrature](../../../../../chebyshev-gauss-quadrature.md).
