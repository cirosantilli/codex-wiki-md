<h1 id="e/2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $A$ has a smallest element $a_0$, any open cover has a member containing $a_0$. This member is either the whole line or a ray $(c,\infty)$ with $c<a_0$, so it already contains all of $A$. Thus a single member suffices and $A$ is [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md).

If $A$ has no smallest element, the family $\{(a,\infty):a\in A\}$ covers $A$: each point has another point of $A$ below it. A finite subfamily has union $(\min_j a_j,\infty)$, which misses its smallest listed endpoint, itself a point of $A$. No finite subcover exists. Therefore **a nonempty subset is [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) exactly when it has a smallest element** in this [topology](../../../../../../topology-split.md).
