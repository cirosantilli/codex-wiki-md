<h1 id="r/1/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

The square $Q_2$ and the drawn cube $Q_3$ are planar. For $k\geq4$, the subdivision in part (v) prevents planarity by [Kuratowski's theorem](../../../../../kuratowski-s-theorem.md). One can also check the edge count directly: a triangle-free simple [planar graph](../../../../../planar-graph-split.md) with $n\geq3$ vertices has at most $2n-4$ edges, whereas $Q_k$ has $n=2^k$ and $m=k2^{k-1}\geq2n$. Thus

$$
\boxed{Q_k\text{ is planar exactly for }k=2,3\text{ in the requested range }k\geq2.}
$$
