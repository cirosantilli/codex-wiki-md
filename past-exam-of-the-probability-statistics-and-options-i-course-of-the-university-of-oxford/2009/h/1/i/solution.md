<h1 id="h/1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md) is $L=\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2/2-f(r)$. Away from a collision, its equations of motion are

$$
m_1\ddot q_1=-\frac{f'(r)}r(q_1-q_2)=:F,\qquad
m_2\ddot q_2=-F,\qquad m_3\ddot q_3=0.
$$

Summing forces proves conservation of total [linear momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md). The total [mechanical energy](../../../../../mechanical-energy.md) $E=\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2/2+f(r)$ is also conserved: its derivative is $F\cdot(\dot q_1-\dot q_2)+f'(r)\dot r=0$, because $\dot r=(q_1-q_2)\cdot(\dot q_1-\dot q_2)/r$.

For the first proposed [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md),

$$
\frac d{dt}(m_1q_1\times\dot q_1)=q_1\times F
=\frac{f'(r)}r q_1\times q_2,
$$

which need not vanish. Thus **particle one's [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) about the fixed origin is not generally conserved**. Its force is central relative to particle two, not necessarily relative to that origin.
