↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

A finite [soluble group](../../../../solvable-group.md) is one whose [derived series](../../../../derived-series.md) $G^{(0)}=G$, $G^{(j+1)}=[G^{(j)},G^{(j)}]$ reaches the trivial group. Equivalently it has a finite normal series with abelian factors. If $H\leq G$, induction using [commutator](../../../../commutator.md) containment gives $H^{(j)}\leq G^{(j)}$ for every $j$. Thus termination for $G$ implies termination for $H$, proving **every [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) of a [soluble group](../../../../solvable-group.md) is soluble**.

For $S_4$, the chain

$$
\{1\}\triangleleft V_4\triangleleft A_4\triangleleft S_4
$$

has abelian factors $V_4$, $C_3$ and $C_2$. Here $V_4$ consists of the identity and the three double transpositions. It is normal because conjugation permutes those double transpositions. Hence **$S_4$ is soluble**.

An example neither soluble nor simple is **$S_5$**. Its [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) $A_5$ is proper, nontrivial and normal, so $S_5$ is not simple. The group $A_5$ is nontrivial and perfect: its generating three-cycles are commutators of double transpositions, using two additional symbols, so $[A_5,A_5]=A_5$. For example the [commutator](../../../../commutator.md) of $(12)(45)$ and $(23)(45)$ is a three-cycle, and conjugation and inversion give all three-cycles. Thus $A_5$ is not soluble, and [subgroup](../../../../subgroup.md) inheritance then proves $S_5$ is not soluble either.
