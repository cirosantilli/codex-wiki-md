<h1 id="a/1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md) in $\mathbb C$ is the smallest subfield containing $\mathbb Q$ and every [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) of the [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md). The sixth roots of unity are powers of $\zeta_6=e^{i\pi/3}$, so the [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md) of $x^6-1$ is $\mathbb Q(\zeta_6)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$.

For $x^6+x^3+1=(x^9-1)/(x^3-1)$, the roots are exactly the primitive ninth roots of unity, all powers of $\zeta_9=e^{2\pi i/9}$. Thus its [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md) is $\mathbb Q(\zeta_9)$. The supplied irreducibility makes the displayed degree-six [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) the [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) of $\zeta_9$, giving

$$
\boxed{[\mathbb Q(\zeta_9):\mathbb Q]=6.}
$$
