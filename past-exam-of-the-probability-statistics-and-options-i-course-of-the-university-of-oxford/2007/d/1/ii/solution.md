<h1 id="d/1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the increasing nonnegative measurable functions $f_n(x)=x^{-2}\mathbf1_{[1/n,1]}(x)$. Their pointwise limit is the given function except at irrelevant endpoint choices, and the [fundamental theorem of calculus](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) on $[1/n,1]$ gives

$$
\int_{\mathbb R}f_n(x)\,dx=\int_{1/n}^1x^{-2}dx=n-1.
$$

[monotone convergence](../../../../../monotone-convergence-theorem.md) therefore gives $\int f=\infty$. Since $f\geq0$, this is also $\int|f|$, and **$f$ is not [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md)**. Measurability alone does not make a finite integral.
