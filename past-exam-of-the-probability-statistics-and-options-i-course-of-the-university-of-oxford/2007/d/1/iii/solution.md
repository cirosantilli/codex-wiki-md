Similarly set $g_n(x)=x^{1/2}\mathbf1_{[1/n,1]}(x)$. These nonnegative measurable functions increase to $g$ almost everywhere. The [fundamental theorem of calculus](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) gives

$$
\int g_n(x)dx=\frac23\left(1-n^{-3/2}\right).
$$

By [monotone convergence](../../../../../monotone-convergence-theorem.md),

$$
\boxed{\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)dx=\frac23.}
$$

The integral is finite and $g\geq0$, so $g$ is [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md). Identifying Lebesgue and ordinary integrals of continuous functions on compact intervals justifies each truncated calculation.
