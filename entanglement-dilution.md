# Entanglement dilution

↑ **Parent:** [Entangled state](entangled-state.md)

Entanglement dilution uses [local operations and classical communication](local-operations-and-classical-communication.md) to prepare partially entangled states from [maximally entangled states](maximally-entangled-state.md), such as [Bell pairs](bell-pair.md). For many identical bipartite pure targets, the asymptotic cost in Bell pairs per target, allowing a vanishing approximation error, is their [entanglement entropy](entanglement-entropy.md) in bits. The [quantum typical subspace](quantum-typical-subspace.md) of the target's [Schmidt coefficients](schmidt-coefficient.md) has almost all its weight and dimension at most $2^{m(S+\delta)}$. A maximally entangled state of this dimension can be converted to the normalized truncated target by [Nielsen's pure-state conversion theorem](nielsen-s-pure-state-conversion-theorem.md). The resulting state approaches the full target as the retained weight tends to one. [Average monotonicity of pure-state entanglement entropy](average-monotonicity-of-pure-state-entanglement-entropy.md) and entropy continuity give the converse lower bound. This is the reverse asymptotic task to [entanglement concentration](entanglement-concentration.md).
