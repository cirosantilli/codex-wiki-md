# Cartesian powers of the Cantor set

↑ **Parent:** [Cantor set](cantor-set.md)

For the middle-third [Cantor set](cantor-set.md) $C$ and positive integer $d$, $C^d$ is compact and [totally disconnected](totally-disconnected-space.md). A connected subset projects to a point in each coordinate and is therefore itself a point. At level $n$, the product has $2^{dn}$ cubes of side $3^{-n}$. These covers give dimension at most $d\log2/\log3$. The product digit probability assigns each cube mass $2^{-dn}$; a ball of radius comparable to $3^{-n}$ meets only a bounded number of cubes, so its mass is at most a constant times $r^{d\log2/\log3}$. The cover-cost argument for [Hausdorff dimension](hausdorff-dimension.md) proves the matching lower bound. Thus compact totally disconnected Euclidean subsets can have arbitrarily large Hausdorff dimension when the ambient dimension is allowed to increase.
