# Arctangent addition formula

↑ **Parent:** [Inverse tangent](inverse-tangent.md)

For real $A,B$ with $AB<1$, the principal [inverse tangent](inverse-tangent.md) satisfies

$$
\boxed{\arctan A+\arctan B=\arctan\frac{A+B}{1-AB}.}
$$

To prove it, multiply the [complex numbers](complex-number.md) $1+iA$ and $1+iB$. Their product is $(1-AB)+i(A+B)$, whose positive [real part](real-part.md) places its [complex argument](argument-complex-analysis.md) in $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$. The individual [complex arguments](argument-complex-analysis.md) are $\arctan A$ and $\arctan B$, and their sum is in $(-\pi,\pi)$. Multiplication adds these [complex arguments](argument-complex-analysis.md), so the positive [real part](real-part.md) forces the sum into the same principal range and gives the displayed formula.

If $AB>1$, both $A,B$ have the same sign. Add $\pi$ to the right-hand side when $A,B>0$, and subtract $\pi$ when $A,B<0$. If $AB=1$, the sum is $\pi/2$ or $-\pi/2$ according to the sign of $A+B$. These cases record the branch information that can be lost by applying the [tangent addition formula](tangent-addition-formula.md) alone.
