# Benney equation

↑ **Parent:** [Falling film flow](falling-film-flow.md)

The [Benney equation](benney-equation.md) is a [long-wave approximation](long-wave-approximation.md) for a gravity-driven [thin film](thin-film.md) that expresses its [volume flux per unit width](volume-flux-per-unit-width.md) locally in terms of height and its [derivatives](derivative.md). For surface-velocity scaling on a plane inclined by $\alpha$, one normalization is

$$
q[h]=\frac23h^3+\varepsilon\left[-\frac23h^3h_x\cot\alpha+S h^3h_{xxx}+\frac8{15}\mathrm{Re}\,h^6h_x\right].
$$

Here $\varepsilon$ is the film aspect ratio, $\mathrm{Re}$ the thickness-based [Reynolds number](reynolds-number.md), and $S$ the [capillary parameter for a falling film](capillary-parameter-for-a-falling-film.md). The leading [velocity](velocity.md) is parabolic. Its first inertial correction uses the leading [mass conservation](mass-conservation.md) equation to eliminate the time [derivative](derivative.md) of the height. This local closure requires the correction to remain small; it does not describe arbitrary large-amplitude short-wave dynamics.
