# ATLAS evidence for light-by-light scattering in 2017

↑ **Parent:** [Light-by-light scattering](light-by-light-scattering.md)

The [ATLAS experiment](atlas-experiment.md) used [lead](lead.md) ion collisions at the [Large Hadron Collider](large-hadron-collider.md) to study the vacuum [QED](quantum-electrodynamics.md) process. In an [ultra-peripheral collision](ultra-peripheral-collision.md), the nuclei pass without an ordinary nuclear collision; their strong electromagnetic fields supply nearly real [photons](photon.md). Selecting two nearly back-to-back [photons](photon.md) with little other detector activity isolated the rare channel. The 2017 result used $480\,\mu\mathrm b^{-1}$ at $\sqrt{s_{NN}}=5.02\,\mathrm{TeV}$ and found 13 candidates against an expected background of $2.6\pm0.7$. This was evidence, with a reported significance of $4.4$ [standard deviations](standard-deviation.md).[Abstract: sample size, collision energy, candidate count, background and fiducial photon selection](evidence-for-light-by-light-scattering-in-heavy-ion-collisions-with-the-atlas-detector-at-the-lhc-by-atlas-collaboration.md)[14 August 2017 statement: significance and dedicated two-photon trigger with otherwise little detector activity](https://atlas.cern/updates/press-statement/atlas-sees-first-direct-evidence-light-light-scattering-high-energy)
