# DNA sequencing

↑ **Parent:** [Molecular genetics](molecular-genetics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_sequencing)

[DNA sequencing](dna-sequencing.md) determines the order of [nucleotide](nucleotide.md) bases in [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md). Sequence data can test molecular rearrangements, as in the [functional plastid-to-nucleus gene transfer experiment](functional-plastid-to-nucleus-gene-transfer-experiment.md), or support [phylogenetics](phylogenetics.md) after a suitable [sequence alignment](sequence-alignment.md).

An ordered [nucleotide sequence](nucleic-acid-sequence.md) answers a different question from fragment size. The eight-[nucleotide](nucleotide.md) strands ACGTTAGC and AACCGGTT have equal lengths and identical counts of [adenine](adenine.md), [cytosine](cytosine.md), [guanine](guanine.md) and [thymine](thymine.md), yet different orders. A length-compatible [gel band](gel-band.md) therefore cannot identify either strand by itself. [Sanger sequencing](sanger-sequencing.md) makes length informative by deliberately coupling each stopping position to the identity of its last added [nucleotide](nucleotide.md).

That coupling is the beautiful experimental idea: convert an invisible order into a collection of ordered endpoints. [Sanger ladder reading](sanger-ladder-reading.md) recovers the newly synthesized strand; [base pairing](base-pairing.md) and the [reverse complement](reverse-complement.md) then connect that readout to the original template. The distinction also matters when checking a [mutation](mutation.md): a reliable local [nucleotide sequence](nucleic-acid-sequence.md) supports a particular molecular change, while its effect on a [phenotype](phenotype.md) requires additional evidence.
