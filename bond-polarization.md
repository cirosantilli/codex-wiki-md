# Bond polarization

↑ **Parent:** [Electronegativity](electronegativity-split.md)

[Bond polarization](bond-polarization.md) is unequal sharing of [electron density](electron-density.md) across a [chemical bond](chemical-bond-split.md). An [electronegativity](electronegativity-split.md) difference is a heuristic for its direction and tendency, not a unique [partial atomic charge](partial-atomic-charge.md).

A simple two-site model makes the notation explicit. Represent each site by effective positive [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $+e$, and allocate expected [electron](electron.md) populations $1-s$ to $A$ and $1+s$ to $B$, with $0\leq s<1$. Here $e$ is the [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md); these effective sites omit the internal structure of real [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). Subtraction gives

$$
q_A=e-e(1-s)=se,\qquad q_B=e-e(1+s)=-se,\qquad q_A+q_B=0.
$$

For the stipulated illustration $s=0.35$, the populations are $0.65$ and $1.35$, and the effective [partial atomic charges](partial-atomic-charge.md) are $+0.35e$ and $-0.35e$. Fractional expected populations do not split an individual [electron](electron.md) into pieces. The total population remains two, and the pair remains neutral. No formula connecting $s$ to $\Delta\chi$ has been assumed; many [electron density](electron-density.md) distributions and partitioning conventions can share one qualitative [electronegativity](electronegativity-split.md) ordering.
