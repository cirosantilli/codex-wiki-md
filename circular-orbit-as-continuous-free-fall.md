# Circular orbit as continuous free fall

↑ **Parent:** [Circular orbit](circular-orbit.md)

For a fixed spherical gravitating source, place a [test particle](test-particle.md) at $(a,0)$ with initial [velocity](velocity.md) $(0,\sqrt{\mu/a})$, where $a$ is outside the source. Its exact [circular orbit](circular-orbit.md) is

$$
\mathbf r(t)=a(\cos\omega t,\sin\omega t),\qquad \omega=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{a^3}}.
$$

Differentiating twice gives $\ddot{\mathbf r}=-\omega^2\mathbf r=-\mu\mathbf r/a^3$, precisely the [Newtonian gravitational field](newtonian-gravitational-field.md) at each point of that circle. There is no outward physical [force](force-split.md) to cancel gravity in this [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md). Gravity supplies the inward [centripetal acceleration](centripetal-acceleration.md) that continually changes the [velocity](velocity.md)'s direction.

![](circular-orbit-continuous-free-fall.png)

**[Figure 7](#image-a-circular-free-fall-orbit-inward-acceleration-and-the-straight-path-the-same-initial-velocity-would-produce-without-gravity). A circular free-fall orbit, inward acceleration, and the straight path the same initial velocity would produce without gravity**.

Over a short interval, the displacement from the straight tangent path begins inward with magnitude $\tfrac12(\mu/a^2)t^2$. The [curvature](curvature.md) of the orbit matters at the same order: the particle moves sideways while continually falling away from its instantaneous tangent. The full circular solution remains at radius $a$ rather than striking the source.

Its [speed](speed.md) is $\sqrt{\mu/a}$, its [oscillation period](period-of-an-oscillation.md) is $2\pi\sqrt{a^3/\mu}$, and its [specific orbital energy](specific-orbital-energy.md) is $-\mu/(2a)$. Removing gravity while keeping the initial [velocity](velocity.md) would give the straight path $(a,a\omega t)$, rather than a stationary particle. This contrast explains why orbital [weightlessness](weightlessness.md) is compatible with a substantial gravitational [force](force-split.md): a freely coasting spacecraft and its contents fall together.
