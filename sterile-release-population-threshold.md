# Sterile-release population threshold

↑ **Parent:** [Mathematical biology](mathematical-biology-split.md)

For the population map $f(N)=\alpha N^2/(\beta N^2+S)$, extinction is a superstable [fixed point](fixed-point.md). Below $S_c$, two positive [fixed points](fixed-point.md) $N_\pm=(\alpha\pm\sqrt{\alpha^2-4\beta S})/(2\beta)$ exist. Their multipliers are $1\mp\sqrt{\alpha^2-4\beta S}/\alpha$, making the lower one a threshold and the upper one attracting. A [saddle-node bifurcation](saddle-node-bifurcation.md) removes them at $S_c$; above it $f(N)<N$ for every positive population, so every nonnegative orbit tends to extinction.
