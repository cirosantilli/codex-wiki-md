# Extended Euclidean algorithm

↑ **Parent:** [Euclidean algorithm](euclidean-algorithm.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_Euclidean_algorithm)

The extended Euclidean algorithm also computes [integers](integer.md) $x,y$ satisfying $ax+by=\gcd(a,b)$, as in [Bézout's identity](bezout-s-identity.md). Back-substitution in the calculation under the [Euclidean algorithm](euclidean-algorithm.md) gives

$$
18=54-36=54-(198-3\cdot54)=4(252-198)-198=4\cdot252-5\cdot198.
$$

Thus $(x,y)=(4,-5)$ solves $252x+198y=18$. The [linear Diophantine equation](linear-diophantine-equation.md) $252x+198y=c$ is solvable exactly when $18$ divides $c$: necessity follows from common [divisibility](divisibility.md), and multiplying this identity proves sufficiency. For [coprime integers](coprime-integers.md) $a,b$, the coefficient of $a$ is a [modular inverse](modular-multiplicative-inverse.md) of $a$ modulo $b$, and the coefficient of $b$ is a [modular inverse](modular-multiplicative-inverse.md) of $b$ modulo $a$, when the respective modulus is greater than one.
