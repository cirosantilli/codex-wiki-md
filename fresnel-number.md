# Fresnel number

↑ **Parent:** [Fresnel diffraction](fresnel-diffraction.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresnel_number)

A [Fresnel number](fresnel-number.md) compares squared aperture size with the product of [wavelength](wavelength.md) and propagation distance. For an [optical slit](optical-slit.md) of full width $D=2a$, explicitly distinguish the full-width and half-width conventions:

$$
N_D=\frac{D^2}{\lambda z},\qquad N_a=\frac{a^2}{\lambda z}=\frac{N_D}{4},\qquad\Delta\phi_{\rm edge-centre}=\frac{\pi a^2}{\lambda z}=\pi N_a=\frac{\pi N_D}{4}.
$$

The full-width convention is used throughout the [Fresnel slit propagation](fresnel-slit-propagation.md) example below. The definition $D^2/(\lambda z)$ also appears in the Delft treatment.[Interactive Optics by Sander Konijnenberg, Aurèle J.L. Adam and H. Paul Urbach; Fresnel and Fraunhofer Approximations, equations (390)–(392): aperture diameter convention](interactive-optics-by-sander-konijnenberg-aurele-j-l-adam-and-h-paul-urbach/scalar-diffraction-optics.md)

For uniform incident [wave phase](phase-waves.md), $N_D\ll1$ makes the omitted aperture [wave phase](phase-waves.md) small and gives the [Fraunhofer diffraction](fraunhofer-diffraction.md) regime. When $N_D$ is of order one or larger, retain it and use [Fresnel diffraction](fresnel-diffraction.md), provided the [paraxial approximation](paraxial-approximation.md) still holds. These are continuous changes in accuracy, rather than a physical boundary at one selected number. For a rectangular [aperture](aperture.md), check both transverse dimensions. Incident [wavefront](wavefront.md) curvature contributes its own [wave phase](phase-waves.md) through $U_0$; the aperture-to-screen [Fresnel number](fresnel-number.md) alone does not describe a spherical source's illumination.

The scaling is revealing: doubling the [optical slit](optical-slit.md) width at fixed [wavelength](wavelength.md) and distance multiplies the [Fresnel number](fresnel-number.md) by four. Maintaining the same [Fresnel number](fresnel-number.md) then requires four times the distance. Two systems can consequently have the same dimensionless pattern despite very different physical lengths.
