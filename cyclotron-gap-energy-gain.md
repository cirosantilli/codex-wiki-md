# Cyclotron gap energy gain

↑ **Parent:** [Cyclotron resonance condition](cyclotron-resonance-condition.md)

Define $\Delta V=\Phi_{\mathrm{entry}}-\Phi_{\mathrm{exit}}$ as the [electric potential](electric-potential.md) drop along the particle's passage. If the [electric field](electric-field-split.md) changes negligibly during that short crossing, the [work](work.md) gives

$$
\Delta K=q\Delta V.
$$

The sign matters: a positive [ion](ion.md) gains [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) while going to lower [electric potential](electric-potential.md); a negative [ion](ion.md) needs the opposite drop. A fixed-sign field would accelerate on one crossing and decelerate on the next. The alternating field must reverse before the particle comes back.

For a fixed [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) sign and circulation direction, absorb this alternating crossing direction into the definition of the arrival [phase](phase-waves.md) $\phi_n$. If $V_0$ is the peak [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) across the whole gap, rather than the amplitude of either electrode relative to ground, a thin-gap model is

$$
K_{n+1}=K_n+|q|V_0\cos\phi_n.
$$

Arrival at $\phi_n=0$ gives the largest accelerating kick; $|\phi_n|<\pi/2$ gives a positive kick. Arrival at $\phi_n=\pi$ gives deceleration. For an ideal classical particle initially on crest, the [cyclotron resonance condition](cyclotron-resonance-condition.md) keeps successive arrival [phases](phase-waves.md) equal. It does not automatically bring an initially mistimed particle onto crest.
