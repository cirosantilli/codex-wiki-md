# Countable additivity

↑ **Parent:** [Measure](measure-split.md)

For pairwise disjoint [measurable sets](measurable-set.md) $E_n$, countable additivity means

$$
\mu\!\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_n\right)
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(E_n).
$$

The infinite [series](series-mathematics.md) is the [limit](limit-of-a-function.md) of its nonnegative partial sums. This controls an entire [countable union](countable-union.md), not merely each finite stage. If $q_1,q_2,\ldots$ enumerate the [rational numbers](rational-number.md) in $[0,1]$, their finite collections have [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) zero, and [countable additivity](countable-additivity.md) gives their whole [union](set-union.md) [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) zero. Their being a [dense set](dense-set.md) does not change this calculation.

The axiom makes no assertion about an arbitrary [uncountable set](uncountable-set.md) of disjoint pieces. In particular, $[0,1]$ is the [union](set-union.md) of its [singletons](singleton-mathematics.md), each of [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) zero, yet its [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) is one. Extending additivity to all such [unions](set-union.md) would contradict interval length.
