# Zero-sum game

↑ **Parent:** [Game theory](game-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-sum_game)

In a [zero-sum game](zero-sum-game.md), the players' [payoffs](payoff.md) sum to zero at every [pure strategy](pure-strategy.md) [strategy profile](strategy-profile.md). For two players this means $u_2=-u_1$: maximizing the column player's [payoff](payoff.md) is minimizing the row player's. A single [payoff matrix](payoff-matrix.md) $A$ therefore specifies a finite two-player [matrix game](matrix-game.md), and [independent](independent-random-variables.md) [mixed strategies](mixed-strategy.md) $x,y$ give [expected payoffs](expected-payoff.md) $x^{\mathsf T}Ay$ and $-x^{\mathsf T}Ay$.

[Matching pennies](matching-pennies.md) and the usual [rock paper scissors](rock-paper-scissors.md) are two-player [zero-sum games](zero-sum-game.md). The [asymmetric coordination example](asymmetric-coordination-example.md) is not: its total [payoff](payoff.md) is $5$ when choices match and $0$ otherwise. Applying a two-player [minimax theorem](minimax-theorem.md) to general [bimatrix games](bimatrix-game.md) would discard this possibility of shared gains. For a two-player [zero-sum game](zero-sum-game.md), however, [optimal mixed strategies](optimal-mixed-strategy.md) guarantee the [value of a zero-sum game](value-of-a-zero-sum-game.md), and pairing any two opposing optimal strategies gives a [Nash equilibrium](nash-equilibrium.md).
