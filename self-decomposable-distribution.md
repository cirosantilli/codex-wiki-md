# Self-decomposable distribution

↑ **Parent:** [Probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-decomposable_distribution)

A [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) is self-decomposable if, for every $0<c<1$, an independent remainder $R_c$ can be chosen so that the displayed equality in [distribution](distribution-mathematical-analysis.md) holds. A [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md) $\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ admits a [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md) remainder with [mean](expected-value-split.md) $(1-c)\mu$ and [variance](variance-split.md) $(1-c^2)\sigma^2$. For an [exponential distribution](exponential-distribution.md) of rate $\lambda$, choose $R_c=0$ with [probability](probability.md) $c$ and otherwise an independent [exponential distribution](exponential-distribution.md) of rate $\lambda$. Its [moment-generating function](moment-generating-function.md) is $c+(1-c)\lambda/(\lambda-t)=(\lambda-ct)/(\lambda-t)$; multiplication by $M_X(ct)$ proves the claim using the [uniqueness theorem for moment-generating functions](uniqueness-theorem-for-moment-generating-functions.md).
