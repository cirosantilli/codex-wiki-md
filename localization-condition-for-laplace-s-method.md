<h1 id="localization-condition-for-laplace-s-method">Localization condition for Laplace's method</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Laplace's method](laplace-s-method.md)

A unique nondegenerate interior minimum does not by itself ensure that a integral treated by [Laplace's method](laplace-s-method.md) is localized there. A sufficient condition is a positive gap above the minimum outside every sufficiently small fixed neighbourhood, together with finiteness of the integral for some positive exponent. The exterior contribution is then exponentially negligible. Without localization, $g(y)=y^2(1-y)^4$ on $(-1,1)$ has its unique interior minimum at zero with $g''(0)=2$, but its boundary contribution is at least $e^{-1}n^{-1/4}$ and dominates the nominal $n^{-1/2}$ interior Gaussian contribution.
