# Positive-definite operator

↑ **Parent:** [Self-adjoint operator](self-adjoint-operator.md)

A [self-adjoint operator](self-adjoint-operator.md) $A$ is positive definite when $\langle Av,v\rangle>0$ for every nonzero vector $v$ in its domain. In finite dimension it is invertible and has positive [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md). In infinite dimension, strict positivity of individual quadratic-form values need not give a bounded inverse; the stronger uniform bound $\langle Av,v\rangle\geq c\|v\|^2$ with $c>0$ supplies coercivity.

For maps $\beta:U\to V$ and $\gamma:V\to W$ between finite-dimensional [inner product spaces](inner-product-space.md), with $\operatorname{im}\beta=\ker\gamma$, the operator $\beta\beta^*+\gamma^*\gamma$ is positive definite. Its [quadratic form](quadratic-form.md) is $\|\beta^*v\|^2+\|\gamma v\|^2$, whose zero set is $\operatorname{im}\beta\cap(\operatorname{im}\beta)^\perp=\{0\}$. This uses the [adjoint operator](adjoint-operator.md) relation $\ker\beta^*=(\operatorname{im}\beta)^\perp$.
