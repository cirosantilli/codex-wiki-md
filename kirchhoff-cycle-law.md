# Kirchhoff cycle law

↑ **Parent:** [Current flow](current-flow.md)

The [Kirchhoff cycle law](kirchhoff-cycle-law.md) says the directed sum of [voltage](voltage.md) drops around every [graph cycle](cycle-in-a-graph.md) is zero. For unit [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) resistance this is the sum of signed currents around the [graph cycle](cycle-in-a-graph.md). With unit [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) resistance, the current is a [voltage](voltage.md) gradient: define the [voltage](voltage.md) difference by summing drops along a [graph path](path-in-a-graph.md). The difference of the sums along two [graph paths](path-in-a-graph.md) is a sum around closed walks; decomposing these into [graph cycles](cycle-in-a-graph.md) makes it zero. Together with the [Kirchhoff node law](kirchhoff-node-law.md), the resulting [Graph Laplacian](laplacian-matrix.md) equation determines current uniquely on a finite connected network.
