# Uniform spanning tree of a recurrent infinite graph

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform spanning tree](uniform-spanning-tree.md)

On a connected locally finite [recurrent graph](recurrent-graph.md), root [Wilson's algorithm](wilson-s-algorithm.md) at a fixed [graph vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) $r$ and process a countable enumeration of the [graph vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md). Every walk hits the existing [tree](tree-graph-theory.md) [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md), since it contains $r$. Each attached [graph path](path-in-a-graph.md) is finite, and the union is a connected acyclic spanning subgraph. Its law is the infinite [uniform spanning tree](uniform-spanning-tree.md).

It is also the common free and wired finite-volume [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) along every [graph exhaustion](graph-exhaustion.md). For a finite [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) set, run a finite initial segment of the chosen enumeration containing all its endpoints. Its membership is then permanently decided, because later attachments have new internal [graph vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md). Only finitely many finite random-walk trajectories were used, and their [graph vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md) and [graph neighbours](neighbour-of-a-vertex.md) fit inside all sufficiently large exhaustion sets. Coupling then gives convergence of those finite-dimensional [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) laws for both boundary conventions. Finite [Wilson's algorithm](wilson-s-algorithm.md) is root/order independent, so the limiting law is too. There is no uniform counting measure on all infinite [spanning trees](spanning-tree.md); the definition is this limiting [probability](probability.md) law.
