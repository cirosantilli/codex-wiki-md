# Hook length

↑ **Parent:** [Hook of a Young diagram](hook-of-a-young-diagram.md)

The hook length of cell $(i,j)$ in the [Young diagram](young-diagram.md) of a [partition of an integer](partition-of-an-integer.md) $\lambda$ is the number of cells in its [Young-diagram hook](hook-of-a-young-diagram.md):

$$
h_{i,j}(\lambda)=\lambda_i-j+\lambda'_j-i+1.
$$

Here $\lambda'$ is the [conjugate partition](conjugate-partition.md). There is one hook length for every cell, so the [multiset](multiset.md) of hook lengths has [cardinality](cardinality.md) $|\lambda|$.

For $\lambda=(3,2)$ and $\lambda'=(2,2,1)$, compute $h_{1,1}=(3-1)+(2-1)+1=4$ and $h_{1,2}=(3-2)+(2-1)+1=3$. Continuing cell by cell gives the [hook graph of a partition](hook-graph-of-a-partition.md)

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}4&3&1\\2&1&\end{array}
$$

or the row array $[[4,3,1],[2,1]]$. Its [hook product](hook-product-of-a-partition.md) is $4\cdot3\cdot1\cdot2\cdot1=24$. A cell has [hook length](hook-length.md) one exactly when it is a [Removable node of a Young diagram](removable-node-of-a-young-diagram.md): it has no cell to its right or below. Here these are $(1,3)$ and $(2,2)$. Transposing the [Young diagram](young-diagram.md) gives the array $[[4,2],[3,1],[1]]$ with the same [hook product](hook-product-of-a-partition.md); this explains geometrically why conjugate shapes have equal counts in the [hook-length formula](hook-length-formula.md).
