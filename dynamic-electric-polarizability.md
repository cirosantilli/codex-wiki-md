# Dynamic electric polarizability

↑ **Parent:** [Electric polarizability](electric-polarizability.md)

The dynamic electric polarizability relates the induced [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) at a driving frequency to the applied [electric field](electric-field-split.md) at that frequency in [linear response theory](linear-response-theory.md). A complex coefficient can encode both a dispersive phase and absorption. For a charged one-dimensional [quantum harmonic oscillator](quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md) with charge magnitude $e$, mass $m$, and natural frequency $\omega_0$, the undamped response is

$$
\alpha(\omega)=\frac{e^2}{m(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)}.
$$

One derivation uses the oscillator [electric-dipole selection rule](electric-dipole-selection-rule.md) and first-order [time-dependent perturbation theory](time-dependent-perturbation-theory.md). The same result follows from the classical [Lorentz oscillator model](lorentz-oscillator-model.md): $m\ddot z+m\omega_0^2z=-eE$ and $d=-ez$ imply $\ddot d+\omega_0^2d=e^2E/m$. At exact resonance there is no bounded periodic response without damping; a finite switch-on produces secular growth rather than a finite steady-state value of the pole expression.
