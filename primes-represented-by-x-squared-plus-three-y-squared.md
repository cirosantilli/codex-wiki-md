# Primes represented by x squared plus three y squared

↑ **Parent:** [Binary quadratic form](binary-quadratic-form.md)

Necessity follows by reducing modulo three. For a [prime](prime-number.md) $p\equiv1\pmod3$, [quadratic reciprocity](quadratic-reciprocity.md) gives $(-3/p)=1$, so the [discriminant criterion for prime representation by a binary quadratic form](discriminant-criterion-for-prime-representation-by-a-binary-quadratic-form.md) constructs a primitive positive definite form of [discriminant of a binary quadratic form](discriminant-of-a-binary-quadratic-form.md) $-12$ representing $p$. Reduction gives $|b|\leq a\leq c$ and $a\leq2$. The only primitive reduced form is $[1,0,3]$: the alternatives $[2,\pm2,2]$ are not primitive. Integral equivalence preserves represented integers, establishing sufficiency.
