# Differentiability of the square of a continuous function

↑ **Parent:** [Differentiability](differentiability.md)

At a point with $f(x)\ne0$, factor the difference of squares and divide by $f(x+h)+f(x)\to2f(x)$ to show that [differentiability](differentiability.md) of $f^2$ is equivalent to that of $f$. At a zero of $f$, its square has a local minimum; if [differentiable](differentiable-function.md), its derivative must vanish. The difference quotient then vanishes exactly when $f(x+h)^2/|h|\to0$, equivalently $f(x+h)/\sqrt{|h|}\to0$. For example, $f(t)=|t|^\alpha$ with $1/2<\alpha<1$ is not [differentiable](differentiable-function.md) at zero, but its square is.
