# Preserve the domain when simplifying a function

↑ **Parent:** [Domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md)

An algebraic simplification proves equality only where both original and transformed expressions are defined. If a cancelled factor vanishes at a point, the simplified expression may instead describe an extension to that point. For example,

$$
\frac1{1+\tan x}=\frac{\cos x}{\cos x+\sin x}
$$

holds only when $\cos x\ne0$ and $\cos x+\sin x\ne0$. The right side is zero at $x=\pi/2+k\pi$, but the left side is undefined there. A graph of the original [function](function-split.md) must retain those holes. The extension may be useful, but it is a different [function](function-split.md) with a larger [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md).
