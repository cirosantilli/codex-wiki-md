# Volume-constrained capillary energy of a cylindrical coating

↑ **Parent:** [Capillary instability of a thin film on a rigid cylinder](capillary-instability-of-a-thin-film-on-a-rigid-cylinder.md)

Expand the area of an axisymmetric cylindrical free surface to second order in film thickness and eliminate the first-order area term using conservation of the exact liquid volume. Up to positive factors and constants this gives the displayed [surface energy](surface-energy.md). Its variation is $\delta\mathcal E/\delta h=-h-h_{zz}$. The [thin-film equation](thin-film-equation.md) is the conserved [gradient flow](gradient-flow.md) $h_t=\partial_z[h^3\partial_z(\delta\mathcal E/\delta h)]$. For periodic boundaries, or boundaries with vanishing energy flux,

$$
\frac{d\mathcal E}{dt}=-\int h^3(h_z+h_{zzz})^2\,dz\le0.
$$

The decrease in capillary [surface energy](surface-energy.md) supplies [viscous dissipation](viscous-dissipation.md). On an infinite coating with unequal far films, use a local energy balance including boundary fluxes rather than an unsubtracted infinite energy integral.
