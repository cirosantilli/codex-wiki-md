↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

At low density the per-generation growth factor is approximately $\alpha/\beta$. The power $\gamma$ controls how sharply crowding reduces recruitment or survival when resources become limiting; at high density $F(u)\sim\alpha u^{1-\gamma}$. Larger $\gamma$ therefore means a stronger [overcompensation in a population model](../../../../../overcompensation-in-a-population-model.md).

The flip threshold says that sufficiently strong density feedback can destabilize a constant positive [biological population](../../../../../population-biology.md) and create alternating generations, under the usual nondegeneracy conditions. **A local loss of fixed-point stability alone does not establish [chaos](../../../../../chaos.md)**, nor does it prove every ecological system with stronger crowding must follow the same route.
