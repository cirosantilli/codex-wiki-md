<h1 id="b/4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$ with $r>0$. Dotting the [vector field](../../../../../../vector-field.md) with the radial direction and taking its angular cross-product gives

$$
\boxed{\dot r=r[1-r^2(\cos^2\theta+3\sin^2\theta)]=r[1-r^2(2-\cos2\theta)],\qquad\dot\theta=2.}
$$

The angular factor ranges from one to three. Hence every radial component is outward for $0<r<1/\sqrt3$, and every radial component is inward for $r>1$. The limiting radii are

$$
\boxed{r_1=1/\sqrt3,\qquad r_2=1.}
$$

There is a minor strictness point in the wording. At $r_1$ the outward inequality is non-strict, with tangencies where $\sin^2\theta=1$; at $r_2$ the inward inequality is non-strict, with tangencies where $\sin\theta=0$. Thus these are the supremum and infimum for strict crossing at all angles. The weak inequalities at the endpoints are exactly what is needed for the closed trapping [annulus](../../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md) in the next part.
