<h1 id="b/4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The planar [divergence](../../../../../divergence.md) is $\nabla\cdot F=-(\Omega+x^2)<0$ for $\Omega>0$. Since the plane is simply connected, the [Bendixson-Dulac theorem](../../../../../bendixson-dulac-theorem.md) excludes any nonconstant [periodic orbit](../../../../../periodic-orbit.md): the flux across a closed trajectory would be zero because the [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md) is tangent, whereas [Green's theorem](../../../../../green-theorem.md) makes it the strictly negative integral of the [divergence](../../../../../divergence.md) over the enclosed region.

Alternatively define $E=y^2/2-x^2/2+x^4/4$. Direct differentiation gives

$$
\boxed{\dot E=-(\Omega+x^2)y^2.}
$$

A nonconstant periodic solution would require the integral of this strictly dissipative expression over a period to vanish. That forces $y\equiv0$ and hence an [equilibrium](../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md), a contradiction. This energy argument identifies the damping mechanism behind the geometric criterion.
