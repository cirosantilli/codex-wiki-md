<h1 id="b/4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $F(x,y)=(y,x-\Omega y-x^3-x^2y)$. Every term changes sign when both variables change sign, so $F(-x,-y)=-F(x,y)$. Consequently the [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) of $(-x(t),-y(t))$ equals $F(-x(t),-y(t))$, proving the stated [rotational symmetry](../../../../../rotational-symmetry.md).

**This is equivariance under a half-turn, not time reversibility.** [Reversibility of an autonomous differential equation](../../../../../reversibility-of-an-autonomous-differential-equation.md) requires a reverser $R$ satisfying $F(Rz)=-DR(z)F(z)$, so that $Rz(-t)$ is a solution. The half-turn $R=-I$ would require $F(-z)=F(z)$, the opposite of what holds. The usual mechanical candidate $R(x,y)=(x,-y)$ also fails when damping is present: its second equation would require $(\Omega+x^2)y=-(\Omega+x^2)y$ identically. The [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) relates two orbits with the same direction of time, and does not reverse their arrows.
