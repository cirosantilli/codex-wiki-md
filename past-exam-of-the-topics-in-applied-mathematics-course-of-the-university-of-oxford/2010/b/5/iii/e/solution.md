<h1 id="b/5/iii/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

One explicit future itinerary is obtained by concatenating all binary words, ordered first by length and then lexicographically:

$$
\boxed{0\,1\,00\,01\,10\,11\,000\,001\,010\,011\,100\,101\,110\,111\,\cdots.}
$$

Extend it arbitrarily to negative indices, for example by zeros. Every finite binary block occurs in its forward itinerary, and indeed occurs arbitrarily far out as a subword of longer words. Its shift orbit is therefore dense in the full two-symbol shift space. It is not eventually periodic: arbitrarily long runs of zeros and ones occur, together with all other finite words.

The [full shift](../../../../../../full-shift.md) has sensitive dependence because sequences agreeing on an arbitrarily large initial window can differ just beyond it, and shifting brings that disagreement into view. Thus this itinerary supplies a dense aperiodic orbit in the chaotic symbolic system assumed by the question. A merely nonrepeating sequence would not be enough: restricted aperiodic sequences can have much simpler, nonchaotic orbit closures. Nor does symbolic description alone assign a numerical [Lyapunov exponent](../../../../../../lyapunov-exponent.md) without information about the underlying map and its coding.
