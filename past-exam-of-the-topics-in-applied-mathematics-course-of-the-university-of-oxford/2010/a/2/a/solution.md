<h1 id="a/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [law of mass action](../../../../../law-of-mass-action.md) makes each elementary [reaction rate](../../../../../reaction-rate.md) proportional to the product of its reactant [concentrations](../../../../../concentration.md), with the stoichiometric change determining its contribution. Let $A=A_0$ be maintained constant, and let $D_X,D_Y>0$ be the [diffusion coefficients](../../../../../diffusion-coefficient.md). The first reaction consumes two $X$ molecules, the second creates one net $X$, the third consumes one each of $X,Y$, and the fourth consumes $Y$ while creating $X$. [Fick's laws](../../../../../fick-s-laws.md) for [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) give

$$
\boxed{X_t=D_XX_{xx}-2k_1X^2+k_2A_0X-k_3XY+k_4A_0Y,\qquad Y_t=D_YY_{xx}-k_3XY-k_4A_0Y.}
$$

Here the standard deterministic convention takes the event rate of the first reaction as $k_1X^2$, so its loss is $2k_1X^2$. A convention absorbing a combinatorial factor into $k_1$ leads to the same nondimensional model after consistently redefining that rate constant. The maintained reactant $A$ makes this an open chemical model; there is no [conservation law](../../../../../conservation-law.md) requiring $A$ to decrease with reaction progress.
