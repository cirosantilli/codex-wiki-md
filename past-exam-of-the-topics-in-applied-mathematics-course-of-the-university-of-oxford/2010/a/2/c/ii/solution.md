↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Yes.** An immobile $Y$ [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) can still change locally through reaction while diffusing $X$ transports activation. Vanishing [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion.md) of one component does not prohibit a propagating reaction front.

In the invariant special case $v=0$, the first equation is already [Fisher's equation](../../../../../../fisher-s-equation.md) with growth coefficient one, so it has travelling fronts regardless of $D$. A [one-diffuser reaction front](../../../../../../one-diffuser-reaction-front.md) also gives a coupled example with nonzero $v$. For $z=\xi-c\tau$, the equations with $D=0$ are

$$
U''+cU'+U(1-U-\beta V)=0,\qquad cV'=\delta UV.
$$

For $0<\delta<2$, choose $k=\sqrt{1-\delta/2}$, $\beta v_0=2-\delta$, and set

$$
\boxed{U(z)=\frac1{1+e^{-kz}},\quad V(z)=v_0[1-U(z)]^2,\quad c=-\frac\delta{2k}.}
$$

Indeed $U'=kU(1-U)$ and $V'/V=-2kU$, giving the second equation. In the first, division by $U(1-U)$ leaves $k^2(1-2U)+ck+1-\beta v_0(1-U)$, whose constant and $U$ coefficients both vanish with these choices. Ahead of the left-moving wave $U=0,V=v_0$; behind it $U=1,V=0$. This explicitly demonstrates a travelling front with substrate depletion but no $Y$ [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion.md), rather than relying solely on the uncoupled special case.
