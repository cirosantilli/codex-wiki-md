<h1 id="a/3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A uniform [steady state](../../../../../../steady-state.md) satisfies $f(u^*,v^*)=g(u^*,v^*)=0$. Set $u=u^*+\tilde u$, $v=v^*+\tilde v$ and keep only first-order terms in the perturbations. Since the base state is constant, its [Laplacian](../../../../../../laplacian.md) vanishes. [Taylor expansion](../../../../../../taylor-expansion.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\partial_t\binom{\tilde u}{\tilde v}=\mathcal D\nabla^2\binom{\tilde u}{\tilde v}+J\binom{\tilde u}{\tilde v},\quad\mathcal D=\begin{pmatrix}D_u&0\\0&D_v\end{pmatrix},\quad J=\begin{pmatrix}f_u&f_v\\g_u&g_v\end{pmatrix}_{(u^*,v^*)}.}
$$

The zero-flux [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) linearize to homogeneous Neumann conditions, $\partial_n\tilde u=\partial_n\tilde v=0$. The off-diagonal entries of the Jacobian describe the cross-effects of the two chemicals, while the diagonal entries describe local self-response.
