<h1 id="a/3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Domain geometry can select biological pattern dimension even when the same reaction parameters and [diffusion coefficients](../../../../../../diffusion-coefficient.md) are used. A sufficiently wide developing tissue can admit instabilities with variation in two spatial directions; nonlinear development may produce spots, labyrinths or other two-dimensional patterns. A thin strip suppresses transverse unstable modes, allowing primarily bands whose [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) varies along its long axis and is nearly uniform across its width.

This is a mechanism for shape-dependent pattern selection during growth: increasing tissue width can admit additional transverse modes without changing the local chemical interactions. Linear instability predicts initial wavelengths and permitted directions, not the eventual stable spot or stripe pattern; nonlinear saturation and boundary geometry determine that later outcome. Zero-flux boundaries also mean these modes arise through internal redistribution and reaction rather than externally prescribed chemical [concentrations](../../../../../../concentration.md).
