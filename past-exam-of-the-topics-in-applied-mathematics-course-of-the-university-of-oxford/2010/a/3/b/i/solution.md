<h1 id="a/3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For Neumann boundaries the correct [eigenfunctions](../../../../../../eigenfunction.md) include zero indices:

$$
\phi_{n_x,n_y}=\cos(n_x\pi x/L_x)\cos(n_y\pi y/L_y),\qquad n_x,n_y\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}.
$$

Their squared [wave number](../../../../../../wavenumber.md) is $q=(n_x\pi/L_x)^2+(n_y\pi/L_y)^2$. The constant pair $(0,0)$ is reaction-stable. Instability requires $q_-<q<q_+$, using the band derived above. If both side lengths are large, the wave-number spacings are small enough that admissible modes can enter the band in either direction, including mixed modes. This does not mean the single $(1,1)$ mode necessarily stays unstable as lengths tend to infinity: its [wave number](../../../../../../wavenumber.md) eventually tends to zero below the band. Rather, increasingly fine wave-number spacing permits some suitable higher integers.

[Thin-domain Turing mode selection](../../../../../../thin-domain-turing-mode-selection.md) applies if $L_y<\pi/\sqrt{q_+}$, every mode with $n_y\geq1$ has $q\geq(\pi/L_y)^2>q_+$ and is stable. A sufficiently large $L_x$ can nevertheless admit an integer $n_x\geq1$ with $q_-<(n_x\pi/L_x)^2<q_+$ and $n_y=0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\hbox{a sufficiently thin domain suppresses transverse variation but can retain longitudinal instability}.}
$$

The printed range starting both indices at one excludes these purely longitudinal modes and cannot imply the requested conclusion. It also omits the constant mode. Further, each allowed spatial [eigenfunction](../../../../../../eigenfunction.md) generally has two temporal [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) branches of $J-q\mathcal D$, with coefficients along their [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md); a single unrestricted scalar $\lambda(q)$ is shorthand for those branches rather than a complete general expansion.
