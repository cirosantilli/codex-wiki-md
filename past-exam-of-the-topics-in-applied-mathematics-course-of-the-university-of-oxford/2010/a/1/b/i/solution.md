<h1 id="a/1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A steady population $N_*$ is a root $h(N_*)=0$. To linearize one needs $h$ differentiable near that root, an extra local regularity assumption beyond the source's continuity. Setting $N=N_*+\eta$ gives $\dot\eta=h'(N_*)\eta+o(\eta)$, hence $\eta(t)=\eta(0)e^{h'(N_*)t}$ at linear order.

Thus **$h'(N_*)<0$ gives linear asymptotic stability and $h'(N_*)>0$ gives instability**. If $h'(N_*)=0$, [linearization](../../../../../../linearization.md) is inconclusive. In a one-dimensional [phase line](../../../../../../phase-line.md), the more general nonlinear test is whether the [vector field](../../../../../../vector-field.md) points towards the root from both sides. At the physical boundary $N=0$, only the right-hand side matters. Continuity alone does not guarantee uniqueness of trajectories, so a standard well-defined flow also presumes a local uniqueness condition, such as local Lipschitz continuity.
