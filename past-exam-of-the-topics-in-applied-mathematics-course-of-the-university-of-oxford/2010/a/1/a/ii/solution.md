<h1 id="a/1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $N_*>0$ be a [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md) and put $N_t=N_*+\eta_t$. [Taylor expansion](../../../../../../taylor-expansion.md) gives $\eta_{t+1}=g'(N_*)\eta_t+O(\eta_t^2)$, so the linear perturbation is $\eta_t=g'(N_*)^t\eta_0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{|g'(N_*)|<1}
$$

is the condition for linear asymptotic stability. A multiplier in $(0,1)$ gives monotone decay; one in $(-1,0)$ gives alternating decay. If its magnitude exceeds one, perturbations grow. Magnitude one is nonhyperbolic and needs nonlinear analysis.

[Strict concavity](../../../../../../strict-concavity.md) and $g(N_*)/N_*=1$ already give $g'(N_*)<1$. Thus the remaining stability requirement is **$g'(N_*)>-1$**. If $g$ is additionally nonnegative on the entire half-line, [strict concavity](../../../../../../strict-concavity.md) prevents its [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) becoming negative there, since it would then force $g$ eventually negative. Under that stronger global population-map assumption the positive [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md) is automatically stable. The printed local slope and [concavity](../../../../../../concave-function.md) information, without that global nonnegativity requirement, does not by itself rule out a flip instability.
