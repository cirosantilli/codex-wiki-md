<h1 id="a/1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For an autonomous scalar differential equation, [linear stability](../../../../../../linear-stability.md) of a simple [equilibrium point](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) $N_e$ is determined by the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) $f_N(N_e,a)=P'(N_e)$: a negative [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) attracts nearby solutions and a positive [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) gives [repulsion in a dynamical system](../../../../../../repulsion-in-a-dynamical-system.md) of nearby solutions.

Here $f(0,a)=1+a\ge1/2>0$ throughout the prescribed [mathematical model parameter](../../../../../../mathematical-model-parameter.md) range, while $f(N,a)\to-\infty$ as $N\to\infty$. If there is only one [equilibrium point](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md), the [function graph](../../../../../../graph-of-a-function.md) must cross the axis on one of its decreasing branches, outside the interval between its two extrema. The crossing is simple and $P'(N_e)<0$. Thus **the unique [equilibrium point](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) is linearly [asymptotically stable](../../../../../../asymptotic-stability.md)**. At a [saddle-node bifurcation](../../../../../../saddle-node-bifurcation.md) there are two distinct [equilibrium points](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md), one of them double, so that case does not fall under the one-equilibrium hypothesis.
