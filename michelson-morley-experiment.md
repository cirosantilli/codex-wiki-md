# Michelson-Morley experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Tests of special relativity](tests-of-special-relativity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelson–Morley_experiment)

An [interferometer](interferometer.md) compares round-trip [light](light.md) travel times along perpendicular arms as its orientation changes. The absence of the classical stationary-aether fringe shift at the expected scale constrains orientation dependence of the round-trip [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). The result is consistent with [special relativity](special-relativity-split.md), but alone does not select it uniquely from every alternative theory.[1887 report, interferometer arrangement and comparison of predicted and observed orientation-dependent shifts](on-the-relative-motion-of-the-earth-and-the-luminiferous-ether-by-albert-abraham-michelson-and-edward-w-morley.md)

**[Figure 1](#image-the-1887-michelson-morley-experiment-apparatus). The 1887 Michelson-Morley experiment apparatus**. [Source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Michelson\_morley\_experiment\_1887.jpg). Historical photograph credited by Wikimedia Commons to Case Western Reserve University; public domain in the United States. The stone support floated in a trough of [mercury](mercury-element.md) so that the [interferometer](interferometer.md) could turn.

The instrument's stone support and floating mounting allowed the experimenters to change its orientation while limiting mechanical disturbance. The measured quantity was the [fringe shift](fringe-shift.md) produced as the apparatus rotated, rather than a direct timing of a single [light](light.md) pulse.[1887 report, apparatus description and observation procedure](on-the-relative-motion-of-the-earth-and-the-luminiferous-ether-by-albert-abraham-michelson-and-edward-w-morley.md)
