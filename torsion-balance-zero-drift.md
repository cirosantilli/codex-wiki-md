# Torsion balance zero drift

↑ **Parent:** [Torsion balance](torsion-balance.md)

[Torsion balance zero drift](torsion-balance-zero-drift.md) is slow motion of the unloaded reference [angle](angle.md) $\beta_0(t)$ during a measurement. If the [torsion constant](torsion-constant.md) remains fixed, a constant applied [torque](torque.md) gives $\beta_{\mathrm{eq}}=\beta_0(t)+\tau_{\mathrm{ext}}/\kappa$. For two equal and opposite applied [torques](torque.md) measured at the same reference zero, their [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) [angles](angle.md) differ by $2\tau_{\mathrm{ext}}/\kappa$. A stable offset cancels; a changing zero contributes $\beta_0(t_+)-\beta_0(t_-)$ and does not cancel automatically.

A simple symmetric sequence makes the limitation explicit. Let $b_1,b_2,b_3$ be settled [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) [angles](angle.md) for applied [torques](torque.md) $+\tau,-\tau,+\tau$ at equally spaced times $t_0-h,t_0,t_0+h$. If the unloaded zero is linear in [time](time-in-physics.md), $\beta_0(t)=u+vt$, and $\kappa$ and $\tau$ remain fixed, then

$$
\widehat\tau=\frac\kappa2\left(\frac{b_1+b_3}{2}-b_2\right)=\tau.
$$

The arithmetic average of the outer zero values equals the middle zero, so both the constant offset and linear drift cancel. Curved zero motion, unequal time spacing, changing [torsion constant](torsion-constant.md), changing signal strength, or unsettled [oscillations](oscillation.md) violate these assumptions. This is an algebraic control example, not a claim that every [torsion balance](torsion-balance.md) follows a linear drift law.
