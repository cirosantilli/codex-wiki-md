## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

The passage belongs to the English reception of [Euclid](../mathematics.md#euclid)'s [number theory](../number-theory.md). [Henry Billingsley](../mathematics.md#henry-billingsley)'s [The Elements of Geometrie by Henry Billingsley](../mathematics-bibliography.md#the-elements-of-geometrie-by-henry-billingsley), published in 1570, brought the [Elements](../euclidean-geometry.md#euclid-s-elements) into English with extensive explanatory material. The original title page advertises translation and annotations; the extract's worked examples show what explanatory prose could add to a compact [definition](../mathematical-logic.md#definition-mathematics). [Henry Billingsley](../mathematics.md#henry-billingsley)'s numbering calls this Definition 23 of Book VII; the familiar modern numbering is VII, Definition 22. The difference is editorial, not a difference in the [perfect number](../integer.md#perfect-number) being defined.[Title page and Book VII, Definition 23, in the Text Creation Partnership transcription](../mathematics-bibliography.md#the-elements-of-geometrie-by-henry-billingsley)[Definition and guide](../mathematics-bibliography.md#euclid-s-elements-by-euclid/book-vii-definition-22)

Here a number's parts are its positive [proper divisors](../divisibility.md#proper-divisor), rather than arbitrary pieces of a [sum](../arithmetic.md#sum) or all smaller [integers](../integer.md). In modern notation the [definition](../mathematical-logic.md#definition-mathematics) is

$$
\boxed{\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\1\leq d<n}}d=n,\qquad\text{equivalently }\sigma(n)=2n,}
$$

where $\sigma$ is the [sum-of-divisors function](../arithmetic-function.md#sum-of-divisors-function). Thus $12$ is not a [perfect number](../integer.md#perfect-number): its positive [proper divisors](../divisibility.md#proper-divisor) total $1+2+3+4+6=16$. It is essential to exclude $n$ itself: including it would double the required [sum](../arithmetic.md#sum). The pairing of a [divisor](../divisibility.md#divisor) $d$ with the fraction $1/(n/d)$ of the whole explains the extract's language of halves, thirds and so on. Its examples are complete checks because they identify every positive [proper divisor](../divisibility.md#proper-divisor), not merely some convenient decomposition.

The larger mathematical context is the construction in [Book IX, Proposition 36](../mathematics-bibliography.md#euclid-s-elements-by-euclid/book-ix-proposition-36). If $q=2^p-1$ is a [prime number](../prime-number.md), then $N=2^{p-1}q$ is a [perfect number](../integer.md#perfect-number). To reconstruct the argument, [prime factorization](../prime-number.md#fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic) gives all its [divisors](../divisibility.md#divisor) as $2^j$ and $q2^j$, with $0\leq j\leq p-1$. A [finite geometric series](../sequence-and-series.md#finite-geometric-series) therefore gives

$$
\sigma(N)=(1+q)\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}2^j
=2^p(2^p-1)=2N.
$$

The choices $p=2,3,5,7$ produce $6,28,496,8128$. The [primality](../prime-number.md#primality) assumption matters: choosing $p=4$ produces $120$, whose [sum-of-divisors function](../arithmetic-function.md#sum-of-divisors-function) is $360$, not $240$. The construction gives a reason for the examples and connects [perfect numbers](../integer.md#perfect-number) with [Mersenne primes](../integer.md#mersenne-prime).[Statement, construction and guide](../mathematics-bibliography.md#euclid-s-elements-by-euclid/book-ix-proposition-36)

The extract itself supplies a [definition](../mathematical-logic.md#definition-mathematics) and examples, not a classification or a [mathematical proof](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-proof) that such numbers are rare. In particular, the converse that every even [perfect number](../integer.md#perfect-number) has this form belongs to the later [Euclid-Euler theorem](../integer.md#euclid-euler-theorem); it should not be read back into this passage. The historically significant feature is **the transmission of deductive arithmetic through vernacular explanation**: a book called geometry also teaches the [divisibility](../divisibility.md) theory needed for a general construction.

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

The figure illustrates [Pierre de Fermat](../mathematics.md#pierre-de-fermat)'s use of [adequality](../mathematics.md#adequality) to construct a [tangent](../geometry-and-topology.md#tangent) to a [parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#parabola). Reading the local exam image is decisive: $D$ is the [vertex of a parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#vertex-of-a-parabola), $DC$ lies on its [axis of symmetry](../euclidean-geometry.md#axis-of-symmetry), $B$ lies on the [parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#parabola), and the proposed [tangent](../geometry-and-topology.md#tangent) $BE$ meets the extended axis at $E$. The horizontal through the auxiliary point $O$ meets the axis at $I$; $O$ lies on $BE$, outside the [parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#parabola). In particular, $O$ must not be treated as a second point of the [parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#parabola). The target is the [subtangent](../geometry-and-topology.md#subtangent) $CE$. The diagram and this construction occur in the tangent discussion associated with [Varia opera mathematica by Pierre de Fermat](../mathematics-bibliography.md#varia-opera-mathematica-by-pierre-de-fermat). The 1679 book was posthumous: its date does not date the invention of the method.[1679 title page and bibliographical record, ETH-Bibliothek](../mathematics-bibliography.md#varia-opera-mathematica-by-pierre-de-fermat)[Tangent construction, with the same B, C, D, E, I, O labels; the method circulated in the 1630s](../mathematics-bibliography.md#fermat-s-rule-for-maxima-and-minima-by-the-garden-of-archimedes)

Write $d=CD>0$, $a=CE>0$, and $e=CI$, with $I$ between $C$ and $D$. These are lengths, and the increment $e$ is distinct from the labelled point $E$. Then $DI=d-e$ and $EI=a-e$. The [parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#parabola)'s characteristic relation makes the square of an ordinate proportional to its distance from the [vertex of a parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#vertex-of-a-parabola). At height $I$, the actual ordinate of the [parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#parabola) is shorter than $OI$. Hence

$$
\frac{d}{d-e}>\frac{BC^2}{OI^2}
=\frac{a^2}{(a-e)^2},
$$

where the equality follows from [similar triangles](../euclidean-geometry.md#similar-triangle) $BCE$ and $OIE$.

The method of [adequality](../mathematics.md#adequality) replaces the comparison by a relation used to extract the condition of contact:

$$
d(a-e)^2\mathrel{\sim}a^2(d-e).
$$

Expanding and cancelling $da^2$ gives $-2dae+de^2\mathrel{\sim}-a^2e$. Divide by the nonzero increment $e$, then suppress the remaining term containing $e$. The result is $a^2=2da$, so, since $a>0$,

$$
\boxed{CE=2CD.}
$$

Thus $DE=DC$: the intersection with the axis lies as far above the [vertex of a parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#vertex-of-a-parabola) as $C$ lies below it. This is a concrete construction, not just a rule for computing a numerical slope.

A modern check clarifies why the calculation succeeds without pretending that [Pierre de Fermat](../mathematics.md#pierre-de-fermat) used a modern [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function). Put $D=(0,0)$, let $y$ increase down the axis, and write the [parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#parabola) as $x^2=ky$, $k>0$. For $B=(b,d)$, with $b^2=kd$, the proposed [tangent](../geometry-and-topology.md#tangent) through $E=(0,-d)$ is $y=2bx/k-d$. Substitution gives

$$
x^2-ky=(x-b)^2.
$$

It meets the [parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#parabola) with a [double root](../polynomial.md#double-root) at $B$, and for every other point its $y$ coordinate is less than that of the [parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#parabola). This also verifies the strict comparison used for $O$.

The significance is **a systematic algebraic condition for geometric contact**. Subtracting neighbouring expressions, dividing by an increment, and discarding its remaining terms anticipates [differentiation](../calculus.md#differentiation). But the historical question is precisely what licenses the last step. The symbol $\sim$ above marks [adequality](../mathematics.md#adequality), not ordinary equality at a finite displacement. Treating the construction as an already stated [epsilon-delta definition of a limit](../calculus.md#epsilon-delta-definition-of-a-limit) would conceal the methodological innovation and its unresolved explanatory burden.

[Alexandre-Théophile Vandermonde](../mathematics.md#alexandre-theophile-vandermonde) presents a programme in [elimination theory](../algebraic-geometry.md#elimination-theory): remove [unknowns](../algebra.md#unknown) from simultaneous [polynomial equations](../polynomial.md#polynomial-equation) in a way that works uniformly as the number of [equations](../algebra.md#equation) and their [polynomial degrees](../polynomial.md#degree-of-a-polynomial) vary. The immediate achievement is narrower than that programme: a general construction for [linear equations](../linear-algebra.md#linear-equation). The academy year 1772 used in the exam is not the printing date of the memoir, which appeared in 1776. Its compact indices distinguished the equation containing a [coefficient](../vector-space.md#coefficient) from the [unknown](../algebra.md#unknown) it multiplied; they were not powers. That notational separation is crucial to interpreting the work.[Opening, printed pp. 516–517; academy year 1772, printed 1776](../mathematics-bibliography.md#memoire-sur-l-elimination-by-alexandre-theophile-vandermonde)[§2.1, discussion and reproduction of Vandermonde's coefficient notation; bibliography](../mathematics-bibliography.md#a-case-of-mathematical-eponymy-the-vandermonde-determinant-by-bernard-ycart)

In modern language take a [system of linear equations](../linear-algebra.md#system-of-linear-equations) $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ with $n$ [unknowns](../algebra.md#unknown). Its [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) is an alternating expression in the [coefficients](../vector-space.md#coefficient):

$$
D=\det A=\sum_{\pi\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\prod_{i=1}^n a_{i,\pi(i)}.
$$

Each [product](../arithmetic.md#product-of-numbers) selects one [coefficient](../vector-space.md#coefficient) from each equation and each [unknown](../algebra.md#unknown)'s column. The [sign of a permutation](../finite-group-theory.md#sign-of-a-permutation) determines whether it is added or subtracted. This makes the number $n$ a genuine parameter of a general rule, rather than merely supplying separately memorised formulas for two or three [unknowns](../algebra.md#unknown). The notation here is modern, not a transcription of [Alexandre-Théophile Vandermonde](../mathematics.md#alexandre-theophile-vandermonde)'s symbols.

For two [unknowns](../algebra.md#unknown), multiply $ax+by=u$ by $d$ and $cx+dy=v$ by $b$, then subtract. Similarly eliminate $x$. This gives

$$
(ad-bc)x=du-bv,\qquad(ad-bc)y=av-cu.
$$

The same principle in arbitrary size is [Cramer's rule](../multilinear-algebra.md#cramer-s-rule). If $A_j(\mathbf{b})$ is obtained by replacing column $j$ of $A$ by $\mathbf{b}$, then

$$
\boxed{x_j=\frac{\det A_j(\mathbf{b})}{\det A}\quad\text{when }\det A\ne0.}
$$

An original verification uses the [multilinearity of the determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#multilinearity-of-the-determinant): replace $\mathbf{b}$ by the column combination $\sum_k x_k A_k$. Every resulting [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) vanishes through repeated columns except the $k=j$ term, which is $x_j\det A$. This explains how an elimination expression isolates one [unknown](../algebra.md#unknown).

The condition $D\ne0$ is substantive. If $D=0$, multiplying by an expression which vanishes cannot justify division. For instance, $x+y=1$ and $2x+2y=3$ are inconsistent, whereas replacing $3$ by $2$ gives infinitely many solutions. Thus an elimination formula must distinguish exceptional [coefficients](../vector-space.md#coefficient) and preserve the connection to the original [system of linear equations](../linear-algebra.md#system-of-linear-equations). Nor does the displayed rule solve the much broader problem of arbitrary [nonlinear equations](../algebra.md#nonlinear-equation).

The passage's importance is **the move from particular elimination calculations towards a theory of alternating expressions**. It would be misleading to turn this into the claim that the familiar [Vandermonde determinant](../galois-theory.md#vandermonde-determinant), formed from powers of variables, is what this extract displays. A positional upper index and an exponent have different meanings; the history of that eponym requires separate evidence. Likewise, modern [matrix algebra](../associative-algebra.md#matrix-algebra) is a useful explanatory language here, not the institutional or conceptual setting of an eighteenth-century memoir.[§§2.1–2.4, separating coefficient indices, difference-products and the later eponym](../mathematics-bibliography.md#a-case-of-mathematical-eponymy-the-vandermonde-determinant-by-bernard-ycart)

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

[Joseph Fourier](../mathematics.md#joseph-fourier)'s [Théorie analytique de la chaleur by Joseph Fourier](../mathematics-bibliography.md#theorie-analytique-de-la-chaleur-by-joseph-fourier) (1822) makes [trigonometric series](../sequence-and-series.md#trigonometric-series) instruments for solving problems of [heat conduction](../heat.md#thermal-conduction). A prescribed [temperature](../temperature.md) distribution need not be given by one [power series](../sequence-and-series.md#power-series): it may describe different heating on different parts of a body. The extract occurs in Chapter III, §233, where the earlier assumption of an odd-power expansion is relaxed. The innovation concerns both representation and what can count as a [function](../function.md) in an analytical calculation.[§233, integral formula; §§163–169, the boundary-value problem motivating the expansions](../mathematics-bibliography.md#theorie-analytique-de-la-chaleur-by-joseph-fourier/propagation-de-la-chaleur-dans-un-solide-rectangle-infini)

Use a separate integration variable $t$ to unpack the printed notation. The proposed [Fourier sine series](../fourier-series.md#fourier-sine-series) has [coefficients](../vector-space.md#coefficient)

$$
\boxed{b_n=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi f(t)\sin(nt)\,dt,\qquad
f(x)\sim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_n\sin(nx).}
$$

The integral signs in the extract denote [definite integrals](../integral.md#definite-integral) over $[0,\pi]$, not [antiderivatives](../integral.md#antiderivative); the author explicitly supplies these bounds in the surrounding sentence. Their absent printed bounds are a notational convention, not permission to choose arbitrary constants of integration.

The key calculation is the [orthogonality](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) relation

$$
\int_0^\pi\sin(nt)\sin(mt)\,dt=
\begin{cases}\pi/2&n=m,\\0&n\ne m.\end{cases}
$$

The [product-to-sum formulas](../geometry-and-topology.md#product-to-sum-formula) prove this by integrating cosines of integer multiples of $t$. If an expansion can be integrated term by term, multiplication by $\sin(mt)$ followed by integration selects exactly one [coefficient](../vector-space.md#coefficient). This derives the only possible [coefficients](../vector-space.md#coefficient), but does not by itself prove the expansion converges to the proposed [function](../function.md). That distinction is central to assessing the extract's claim.

For a revealing example take $f(x)=1$ on $0<x<\pi$. Direct integration gives $b_n=2(1-(-1)^n)/(\pi n)$, and therefore

$$
1=\frac4\pi\left(\sin x+\frac{\sin3x}{3}+\frac{\sin5x}{5}+\cdots\right),\qquad0<x<\pi.
$$

Every term is zero at $0$ and $\pi$, so the [series](../sequence-and-series.md#series-mathematics) cannot take the value $1$ at those endpoints. Its natural interpretation uses the [odd extension](../calculus.md#odd-extension) followed by [periodic extension](../function.md#periodic-extension); the endpoints become [jump discontinuities](../calculus.md#jump-discontinuity). More generally, for a piecewise continuously differentiable [function](../function.md), the [Fourier series](../fourier-series.md) converges at an interior jump to the average of the two [one-sided limits](../calculus.md#one-sided-limit), irrespective of an individually assigned value there. Changing one value also leaves the [integrals](../integral.md) unchanged. Consequently the unqualified assertion of representation for every arbitrary [function](../function.md) cannot be a modern pointwise theorem. [Dirichlet's theorem on Fourier series](../fourier-series.md#dirichlet-s-theorem-on-fourier-series) supplies sufficient conditions and the correct distinction between continuity points and jumps; its 1829 source explicitly addresses the proof problem.[1829, opening discussion and concluding convergence statement](../mathematics-bibliography.md#sur-la-convergence-des-series-trigonometriques-qui-servent-a-representer-une-fonction-arbitraire-entre-des-limites-donnees-by-peter-gustav-lejeune-dirichlet)

The physical payoff is explicit. For a rod scaled to $0<x<\pi$ with zero end [temperature](../temperature.md), [separation of variables](../partial-differential-equation.md#separation-of-variables) in the [heat equation](../diffusion-equation.md#heat-equation) produces

$$
u(x,\tau)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_n e^{-\kappa n^2\tau}\sin(nx),\qquad\kappa>0.
$$

Each mode solves $u_\tau=\kappa u_{xx}$ and the end [boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#boundary-condition); the [Fourier sine coefficients](../fourier-series.md#fourier-sine-coefficient) encode the initial [temperature](../temperature.md). This rod is an explanatory example, distinct from the stationary rectangular-solid problem opening [Joseph Fourier](../mathematics.md#joseph-fourier)'s Chapter III. The factor $e^{-\kappa n^2\tau}$ damps high modes rapidly for positive time. **The representation of arbitrary data makes the physical solution possible; the proof of that representation remains a separate task.**

[Carl Friedrich Gauss](../mathematics.md#carl-friedrich-gauss) is defending [complex numbers](../complex-analysis.md#complex-number) as intelligible mathematical objects. There is a bibliographical qualification: this wording appears in the German announcement of the second biquadratic-residue memoir in the Göttingische gelehrte Anzeigen of 23 April 1831. The associated Latin [Theoria residuorum biquadraticorum, commentatio secunda by Carl Friedrich Gauss](../mathematics-bibliography.md#theoria-residuorum-biquadraticorum-commentatio-secunda-by-carl-friedrich-gauss) appeared in 1832. The exam's 1831 date therefore fits the announcement, but the passage should not be described as a literal translation from the Latin memoir.[23 April 1831, concluding general remarks on terminology and planar representation](../mathematics-bibliography.md#announcement-of-the-second-biquadratic-residue-memoir-by-carl-friedrich-gauss)[ETH-Bibliothek original-edition record: 1832](../mathematics-bibliography.md#theoria-residuorum-biquadraticorum-commentatio-secunda-by-carl-friedrich-gauss)

The proposed directional vocabulary is mathematically specific. Represent $z=a+bi$ by the point $(a,b)$ in the [complex plane](../complex-analysis.md#complex-plane). The addition of [complex numbers](../complex-analysis.md#complex-number) adds components. Multiplication of [complex numbers](../complex-analysis.md#complex-number) is

$$
(a+bi)(c+di)=(ac-bd)+(ad+bc)i.
$$

Multiplication by $-1$ reverses direction through a half-turn, while multiplication by $i$ sends $(a,b)$ to $(-b,a)$, a quarter-turn. Consequently $i^2=-1$ says that two quarter-turns make a half-turn. More generally the [moduli](../complex-analysis.md#modulus) multiply and the [complex arguments](../complex-analysis.md#argument-complex-analysis) add. These are coherent operations extending [arithmetic](../arithmetic.md); an unfortunate name supplies no mathematical objection to them.

The memoir's setting is [number theory](../number-theory.md), specifically the question of whether a [integer congruence](../modular-arithmetic.md#integer-congruence) $x^4\equiv a\pmod p$ is soluble, and the reciprocal relations between such questions. Passing to the [Gaussian integers](../commutative-algebra.md#gaussian-integer) $\mathbb Z[i]$ provides arithmetic that is closed under [addition](../arithmetic.md#addition) and [multiplication](../arithmetic.md#multiplication). For example,

$$
\boxed{5=(2+i)(2-i),\qquad N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2,\qquad N(zw)=N(z)N(w).}
$$

The [Gaussian integer norm](../commutative-algebra.md#gaussian-integer-norm) follows directly by multiplying by the [complex conjugate](../complex-analysis.md#complex-conjugate). Here it gives $N(2+i)=5$ and $N(5)=25$, consistent with the factorization. An ordinary [prime number](../prime-number.md) may therefore have factors in the larger arithmetic without any contradiction: the permissible [factors](../statistical-modelling.md#regression-factor) have changed. The original announcement explicitly motivates the extension of [arithmetic](../arithmetic.md) to these numbers and describes their square-lattice representation.[Opening explanation of complex integral numbers and the later lattice discussion](../mathematics-bibliography.md#announcement-of-the-second-biquadratic-residue-memoir-by-carl-friedrich-gauss)

It is helpful to separate two domains. Arbitrary [complex numbers](../complex-analysis.md#complex-number) form a [field](../algebra.md#field), since the inverse of nonzero $a+bi$ is $(a-bi)/(a^2+b^2)$. The [Gaussian integers](../commutative-algebra.md#gaussian-integer) form a [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring), but not a [field](../algebra.md#field): $1/2$ is not a [Gaussian integer](../commutative-algebra.md#gaussian-integer). Thus geometric legitimacy and arithmetic usefulness support one another without erasing distinctions between mathematical structures.

**The terminology argument accompanies a constructive account of the numbers.** It is not a claim that renaming alone proves a theorem, or that all earlier work with [complex numbers](../complex-analysis.md#complex-number) had lacked meaning. A [mathematical proof](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-proof) still has to establish the laws of the extended arithmetic and the assertions about [biquadratic residues](../modular-arithmetic.md#biquadratic-residue). [Carl Friedrich Gauss](../mathematics.md#carl-friedrich-gauss)'s geometric explanation makes those operations accessible while his arithmetic application gives a strong reason to accept the enlarged domain.

[Heinrich Weber](../mathematics.md#heinrich-martin-weber)'s 1882 paper concerns [prime numbers](../prime-number.md) represented by [quadratic forms](../quadratic-form.md). Its [definition](../mathematical-logic.md#definition-mathematics) abstracts a [finite group](../group.md#finite-group) from the nature of its elements: what matters is a closed [binary operation](../algebraic-operation.md#binary-operation), [associativity](../algebraic-operation.md#associative-property), and left and right [cancellation laws](../algebraic-operation.md#cancellation-law). Here degree $h$ means the number of elements, now called the [group order](../group.md#order-of-a-finite-group); it is not the number of letters acted on by a [permutation group](../finite-group-theory.md#permutation-group). This matters when relating the passage to earlier work on substitutions. The [definition](../mathematical-logic.md#definition-mathematics) fits the use of [groups](../group.md) of classes of [quadratic forms](../quadratic-form.md), where the elements are already mathematical objects more elaborate than individual permutations.[Mathematische Annalen 20 (1882), 301–329; opening group definition as reproduced in the exam](../mathematics-bibliography.md#beweis-des-satzes-dass-jede-eigentlich-primitive-quadratische-form-unendlich-viele-primzahlen-darzustellen-fahig-ist-by-heinrich-weber)[Discussion of Weber's 1882 definition and its class-group setting](../mathematics-bibliography.md#the-abstract-group-concept-by-the-mactutor-history-of-mathematics-archive)

The apparent omission of an [identity element](../algebraic-operation.md#identity-element) and [inverse elements](../algebraic-operation.md#inverse-element) is not fatal. **Every nonempty [finite cancellative semigroup](../algebraic-operation.md#finite-cancellative-semigroup) is a [group](../group.md).** Here is a proof identifying exactly where the assumption of a [finite set](../set.md#finite-set) enters. For fixed $a\in G$, the [maps](../function.md#function-class) $L_a(x)=ax$ and $R_a(x)=xa$ are [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function) by cancellation. An [injective function](../algebra.md#injective-function) from a [finite set](../set.md#finite-set) to itself is [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function), so choose $e$ with $ae=a$. For every $x$,

$$
a(ex)=(ae)x=ax,
$$

and left cancellation gives $ex=x$. To show $e$ is also a right [identity element](../algebraic-operation.md#identity-element), use

$$
(xe)x=x(ex)=xx.
$$

Right cancellation gives $xe=x$. Thus the same $e$ works on both sides for every $x$; it has not merely been chosen as an element fixing $a$.

[Surjectivity](../algebra.md#surjective-function) now gives $b,c$ with $ab=e$ and $ca=e$. By [associativity](../algebraic-operation.md#associative-property),

$$
c=ce=c(ab)=(ca)b=eb=b.
$$

So $b$ is a two-sided [inverse element](../algebraic-operation.md#inverse-element) of $a$. Since $a$ was arbitrary, the usual [group axioms](../group.md#group-axioms) follow. The nonempty hypothesis is tacit in a system of $h$ elements intended to have positive $h$: the empty system would satisfy the displayed universal conditions vacuously but would have no [identity element](../algebraic-operation.md#identity-element).

The infinite case exposes the force of the qualification. The positive [integers](../integer.md) under [addition](../arithmetic.md#addition) are closed, associative and cancellative, but have no additive [identity element](../algebraic-operation.md#identity-element). Even the nonnegative [integers](../integer.md) have an [identity element](../algebraic-operation.md#identity-element) without additive [inverse elements](../algebraic-operation.md#inverse-element) for positive elements. The failure is precisely that [injective functions](../algebra.md#injective-function) of an infinite [set](../set.md) to itself need not be [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function). [Heinrich Weber](../mathematics.md#heinrich-martin-weber)'s conditions are therefore not an unrestricted replacement for the modern [group axioms](../group.md#group-axioms).

Historically the abstraction is important because it allows arguments to be applied across different kinds of elements. An assumption of [commutativity](../algebraic-operation.md#commutativity) is absent: class-group applications do not make commutativity part of the general [definition](../mathematical-logic.md#definition-mathematics). For example, the [symmetric group](../finite-group-theory.md#symmetric-group) $S_3$ meets the conditions but is [noncommutative](../algebraic-operation.md#noncommutativity). The [definition](../mathematical-logic.md#definition-mathematics) also does not require a prior presentation as permutations, although [Cayley's theorem](../group-theory.md#cayley-s-theorem) provides such a representation. The passage documents **the separation of an algebraic structure from any one representation of it**, with [finiteness](../set.md#finiteness) carrying logical work that later axiom systems usually assign to explicit [identity element](../algebraic-operation.md#identity-element) and [inverse element](../algebraic-operation.md#inverse-element) axioms.

[Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics.md#augustin-louis-cauchy)'s importance lies in the interaction of foundations, methods and applications. He made the justification of [limits](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function), [series](../sequence-and-series.md#series-mathematics) and [integrals](../integral.md) a programme for teaching and research; developed [complex analysis](../complex-analysis.md) into a source of powerful general methods; and contributed to [permutation groups](../finite-group-theory.md#permutation-group), [geometry](../geometry-and-topology.md#geometry) and [continuum mechanics](../continuum-mechanics.md). His work is best assessed through particular achievements and their limitations, rather than by treating every theorem now bearing his name as a modern statement he already possessed. His engineering training and subsequent mathematical career also make a rigid separation of pure and applied work historically unhelpful.[Biographical account of engineering education and mathematical research](../mathematics-bibliography.md#augustin-louis-cauchy-by-the-mactutor-history-of-mathematics-archive)

The [Cours d'analyse by Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics-bibliography.md#cours-d-analyse-by-augustin-louis-cauchy) (1821) makes [convergence](../calculus.md#convergence) a condition requiring argument. A [series](../sequence-and-series.md#series-mathematics) is studied through its [partial sums](../sequence-and-series.md#partial-sum); a [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function) describes a variable approaching a fixed value; an [infinitesimal](../calculus.md#infinitesimal) is a variable tending to zero, rather than simply a mysterious fixed quantity smaller than every positive number. The introduction explicitly challenges unrestricted transfers of rules from finite expressions to infinite ones. This is not an abandonment of calculation: it seeks to identify when calculations are valid.[Introduction; Chapter II, continuity; Chapter VI, convergence](../mathematics-bibliography.md#cours-d-analyse-by-augustin-louis-cauchy)

For example, the [Cauchy criterion for series](../sequence-and-series.md#cauchy-criterion-for-series) asks whether tails can all be made small:

$$
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N\ \forall m>n\geq N:
\left|\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}a_k\right|<\varepsilon.
$$

This is a modern formulation of the convergence requirement, with the [completeness of the real numbers](../sequence-and-series.md#completeness-of-the-real-numbers) understood. Merely requiring $a_n\to0$ is weaker. In the [harmonic series](../sequence-and-series.md#harmonic-series), grouping the terms from $2^j+1$ to $2^{j+1}$ gives a block of at least $1/2$, so its tails cannot satisfy the criterion. In contrast, the [root test](../sequence-and-series.md#root-test) compares a tail with a [geometric series](../sequence-and-series.md#geometric-series): if $\limsup |a_n|^{1/n}<q<1$, then eventually $|a_n|\leq q^n$, which proves [absolute convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#absolute-convergence). Such arguments explain the usefulness of convergence tests instead of reducing [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics.md#augustin-louis-cauchy)'s contribution to a vocabulary of definitions.

His [Résumé des leçons sur le calcul infinitésimal by Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics-bibliography.md#resume-des-lecons-sur-le-calcul-infinitesimal-by-augustin-louis-cauchy) (1823) extends the programme to [differentiation](../calculus.md#differentiation) and [integration](../integral.md). For a continuous [function](../function.md) on a compact [interval](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics), an [integral](../integral.md) can be approached through sums of values times subinterval lengths. A modern justification uses [uniform continuity](../topological-analysis.md#uniform-continuity): for a partition of mesh at most $\delta$, two choices of sample points give sums differing by at most $(b-a)\omega_f(\delta)$, where the [modulus of continuity](../topological-analysis.md#modulus-of-continuity) $\omega_f(\delta)$ tends to zero. Refinement then establishes the common [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function). This reconstruction makes clear why control of error, rather than a geometrical picture of area alone, is decisive. It does not attribute the later full [Riemann integral](../real-analysis.md#riemann-integral) theory for discontinuous [functions](../function.md) to the 1823 text.[1823 original edition, lessons on derivatives and definite integrals](../mathematics-bibliography.md#resume-des-lecons-sur-le-calcul-infinitesimal-by-augustin-louis-cauchy)

The achievement has limits. The 1821 [Sum theorem of Cauchy](../sequence-and-series.md#sum-theorem-of-cauchy) asserts continuity of sums of convergent [series](../sequence-and-series.md#series-mathematics) of continuous [functions](../function.md) in terms that, under a pointwise reading, are insufficient. The original proof treats the tail as negligible without establishing the uniform control needed near the point concerned. A modern diagnostic is $f_n(x)=x^n$ on $[0,1]$: every $f_n$ is continuous, but its [pointwise limit](../sequence-and-series.md#pointwise-limit) is zero for $x<1$ and one at $1$. Thus a proof must distinguish [pointwise convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#pointwise-convergence) from [uniform convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#uniform-convergence). [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics.md#augustin-louis-cauchy)'s use of variable and infinitesimal language deserves historical interpretation, but it does not license silently importing modern hypotheses into his printed theorem.[Chapter VI, §I, Theorem I and preceding remainder argument](../mathematics-bibliography.md#cours-d-analyse-by-augustin-louis-cauchy)

In [complex analysis](../complex-analysis.md), the [Mémoire sur les intégrales définies, prises entre des limites imaginaires by Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics-bibliography.md#memoire-sur-les-integrales-definies-prises-entre-des-limites-imaginaires-by-augustin-louis-cauchy) (1825) exemplifies a new productive setting for [integration](../integral.md). A [contour integral](../complex-analysis.md#contour-integral) depends on a path in the [complex plane](../complex-analysis.md#complex-plane); deforming that path and accounting for [isolated singularities](../isolated-singularity.md) can evaluate otherwise difficult [definite integrals](../integral.md#definite-integral). In modern form, [Cauchy's integral theorem](../complex-analysis.md#cauchy-s-integral-theorem) applies to a [holomorphic function](../holomorphic-function.md) on a suitable domain and [Cauchy integral formula](../analysis.md#cauchy-integral-formula) states, for a circle enclosing $z$ and lying with its interior in that domain,

$$
\boxed{f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\gamma\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,d\zeta.}
$$

The importance is structural: local values and [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) are determined by data on a surrounding path. Historical proofs and hypotheses should be distinguished from later general forms of the theorem.[1825 original edition, contour integration and the treatment of singularities](../mathematics-bibliography.md#memoire-sur-les-integrales-definies-prises-entre-des-limites-imaginaires-by-augustin-louis-cauchy)

A short modern consequence measures the power of this method. For an [entire function](../holomorphic-function.md#entire-function) bounded by $M$, the derivative version of [Cauchy integral formula](../analysis.md#cauchy-integral-formula) on a circle of radius $R$ gives $|f'(z)|\leq M/R$. Since $R$ is arbitrary, $f'=0$, proving [Liouville's theorem](../complex-analysis.md#liouville-theorem). If a nonconstant [polynomial](../polynomial.md) $P$ had no [root](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-of-a-root-system), then $1/P$ would be an [entire function](../holomorphic-function.md#entire-function), bounded outside a large disc by the leading term and inside the disc by [compactness](../topology.md#compact-space). [Liouville's theorem](../complex-analysis.md#liouville-theorem) would force it to be constant, a contradiction. This proves the [fundamental theorem of algebra](../algebra.md#fundamental-theorem-of-algebra). The chain is an assessment of the reach of the methods, not a claim that [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics.md#augustin-louis-cauchy) alone originated every named result.

[Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics.md#augustin-louis-cauchy)'s [algebra](../algebra.md) concerns concrete [permutations](../combinatorics.md#permutation) as well as analytical formulas. His 1845 investigations developed the theory of substitutions; [Cauchy theorem for groups](../finite-group-theory.md#cauchy-theorem-for-groups) says that a [prime number](../prime-number.md) dividing a finite [group order](../group.md#order-of-a-finite-group) occurs as an [element order](../group-theory.md#order-of-a-group-element). One can appreciate its generality from a modern argument: rotate the coordinates of the tuples $(g_1,\ldots,g_p)$ satisfying $g_1\cdots g_p=e$. Rotation preserves this condition, since $g_2\cdots g_pg_1=g_1^{-1}(g_1\cdots g_p)g_1=e$. There are $|G|^{p-1}$ such tuples; nonfixed rotation [orbits](../dynamical-systems.md#orbit-dynamical-system) have $p$ elements, and fixed tuples correspond to $g^p=e$. When $p\mid|G|$, the number of fixed tuples is divisible by $p$, so besides $e$ there is an element of [element order](../group-theory.md#order-of-a-group-element) $p$. This is explanatory mathematics, not [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics.md#augustin-louis-cauchy)'s original proof. The dating itself needs care: [Peter Michael Neumann](../mathematics.md#peter-m-neumann)'s archival argument places the major burst of work in 1845 despite an 1844 date traditionally attached to a volume.[1989, opening argument and discussion of the 1845 papers](../mathematics-bibliography.md#on-the-date-of-cauchy-s-contributions-to-the-founding-of-the-theory-of-groups-by-peter-michael-neumann)

Finally, [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics.md#augustin-louis-cauchy)'s work on [elasticity](../elasticity-physics.md) shows the strength of making a local mathematical object out of physical interactions. Instead of describing only a force perpendicular to a surface, one studies the [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) on a plane through a point, with its direction dependent on the plane's [unit normal](../differential-geometry.md#unit-normal). In modern notation the relation is $\mathbf t(\mathbf n)=\boldsymbol\sigma\mathbf n$, where the [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor) contains the necessary directional information. Together with [balance of linear momentum](../continuum-mechanics.md#balance-of-linear-momentum), this yields equations for the internal motion of a body. The modern tensor notation is later explanatory language; the conceptual contribution is the reduction of the forces on arbitrary internal planes to a finite collection of local components.[Dictionary of Scientific Biography, section on mathematical physics, reproducing Cauchy's 1822 explanation of pressures and tensions](../mathematics-bibliography.md#augustin-louis-cauchy-by-hans-freudenthal)

**[Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics.md#augustin-louis-cauchy)'s lasting contribution was a powerful research programme, not a finished foundation.** His insistence on justified passage to a [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function) reshaped [analysis](../analysis.md), while [complex integration](../complex-analysis.md#complex-integration), substitutions and local stresses opened methods whose value extended beyond their first applications. The inadequacy of a [pointwise convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#pointwise-convergence) hypothesis, the need to state domains in [complex analysis](../complex-analysis.md), and the later construction of the [real numbers](../arithmetic.md#real-number) show that nineteenth-century rigour remained a collective and revisable achievement. These qualifications strengthen an assessment of his influence by identifying what later mathematicians could extend and repair.

Between 1600 and 1900, [algebra](../algebra.md) broadened from arts of working with unknown quantities and solving [equations](../algebra.md#equation) into the study of several systems of symbolic operations and increasingly general [algebraic structures](../algebra.md#algebraic-structure). This was neither a single change of definition nor a clean replacement of one subject by another. [Polynomial equations](../polynomial.md#polynomial-equation) remained central while new meanings accumulated. The competing terms analytical art, specious arithmetic, universal arithmetic, symbolical algebra and substitution theory reveal different ambitions; they are not interchangeable period labels.

Near 1600, [François Viète](../mathematics.md#francois-viete)'s [In artem analyticem isagoge by François Viète](../mathematics-bibliography.md#in-artem-analyticem-isagoge-by-francois-viete) (1591) supplies a particularly revealing starting point. His analytical art uses letters for known quantities as well as unknown ones. In his terminology, [logistica numerosa](../mathematics.md#logistica-numerosa) concerns numerical calculation and [logistica speciosa](../mathematics.md#logistica-speciosa) calculation with species, or general magnitudes. Vowels represent unknowns and consonants known magnitudes. This allows a single relation to represent many particular problems, but it is still tied to geometrical homogeneity: a square and a length do not simply become quantities of the same kind because they are written with letters.[1591, opening analytical programme and rules of species; terminology and notation discussed in the MacTutor Viète biography](../mathematics-bibliography.md#in-artem-analyticem-isagoge-by-francois-viete)[Discussion of known and unknown symbols and dimensional homogeneity](../mathematics-bibliography.md#francois-viete-by-john-joseph-o-connor-and-edmund-frederick-robertson)

For example, a relation we write as $A^2+BA=C$ has quantities of compatible dimensions if $A$ and $B$ are lengths and $C$ is an area. Letters do more than abbreviate a numerical question: they express relations between whole families of magnitudes. The phrase specious arithmetic therefore indicates [arithmetic](../arithmetic.md) carried out on species; analysis stresses the art of reasoning from an assumed unknown towards its determination. Neither term should be reduced to the modern subject [real analysis](../real-analysis.md).

[René Descartes](../mathematics.md#rene-descartes)'s [La Géométrie by René Descartes](../mathematics-bibliography.md#la-geometrie-by-rene-descartes) (1637) demonstrates another relation between [algebra](../algebra.md) and [geometry](../geometry-and-topology.md#geometry). A chosen unit segment lets [multiplication](../arithmetic.md#multiplication), [division](../arithmetic.md#division-mathematics) and extraction of [square roots](../algebra.md#square-root) be represented by constructions of lengths. The product of two measured lengths can itself be represented by a length relative to that unit. In modern dimensional notation the construction gives $ab/u$ for unit length $u$, removing the obstacle that a product must always be treated as an area. The text's [equations](../algebra.md#equation) can then organise geometrical problems and classify [curves](../topology.md#curve) by their algebraic complexity. Its title remains geometry: [algebra](../algebra.md) serves a geometrical programme rather than announcing a present-day department of [abstract algebra](../algebra.md#abstract-algebra).[Book I, operations on lines and choice of unit; Book II, treatment of curves](../mathematics-bibliography.md#la-geometrie-by-rene-descartes)

A further term, [universal arithmetic](../mathematics.md#universal-arithmetic), stresses the extension of calculation from particular numbers to general quantities. [Isaac Newton](../mathematics.md#isaac-newton)'s [Arithmetica universalis by Isaac Newton](../mathematics-bibliography.md#arithmetica-universalis-by-isaac-newton), issued in 1707, makes the term visible in a title whose subtitle concerns composition and resolution. A title is evidence for the vocabulary, not a sufficient definition of all contemporary usage.[1707 title page and original-edition record, New York Public Library](../mathematics-bibliography.md#arithmetica-universalis-by-isaac-newton) In [Leonhard Euler](../mathematics.md#leonhard-euler)'s [Elements of Algebra by Leonhard Euler](../mathematics-bibliography.md#elements-of-algebra-by-leonhard-euler), published in German in 1770, general numerical calculation and the solution of [equations](../algebra.md#equation) remain a substantial conception of the subject. For instance, allowing negative and [complex roots](../polynomial.md#complex-root) changes the admissible answers to [polynomial equations](../polynomial.md#polynomial-equation) while preserving the central question of determining an unknown.[1770 original publication and later English edition; sections on operations and equations](../mathematics-bibliography.md#elements-of-algebra-by-leonhard-euler)

The eighteenth-century [elimination theory](../algebraic-geometry.md#elimination-theory) exemplified by [Alexandre-Théophile Vandermonde](../mathematics.md#alexandre-theophile-vandermonde)'s memoir broadens the scale of the task. Instead of one numerical [quadratic equation](../polynomial.md#quadratic-equation), one seeks a rule for an arbitrary number of simultaneous [linear equations](../linear-algebra.md#linear-equation). The [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) is initially valuable because it effects that elimination. This is a different generality from the later study of a [group](../group.md): the objects being determined are still unknown quantities, and symbolic generality concerns the number and [coefficients](../vector-space.md#coefficient) of the [equations](../algebra.md#equation). The case cautions against using generality alone as a test for whether [algebra](../algebra.md) has become abstract.[Opening programme and positional coefficient notation](../mathematics-bibliography.md#memoire-sur-l-elimination-by-alexandre-theophile-vandermonde)

In nineteenth-century British discussion, [George Peacock](../mathematics.md#george-peacock)'s [A Treatise on Algebra by George Peacock](../mathematics-bibliography.md#a-treatise-on-algebra-by-george-peacock) (1830) distinguishes [arithmetical algebra](../mathematics.md#arithmetical-algebra) from [symbolical algebra](../mathematics.md#symbolical-algebra). If the symbols mean positive numbers, $a-(a+b)$ has no positive-number result. Yet algebraists manipulate this expression without restriction. [George Peacock](../mathematics.md#george-peacock) therefore seeks principles for the manipulation of signs which are not confined by their original arithmetical interpretation. The contrast is explicitly argued in his preface: [arithmetic](../arithmetic.md) suggests the laws, while the symbolic science requires foundations adequate to its more general conclusions.[1830 preface, especially the discussion of a−(a+b) and arithmetic as a science of suggestion](../mathematics-bibliography.md#a-treatise-on-algebra-by-george-peacock)

The familiar extension $(-1)(-1)=1$ illustrates the stakes. From [distributivity](../algebraic-operation.md#distributive-property), $0=(-1)(1+(-1))=-1+(-1)(-1)$, so the rule is forced if these operations obey the assumed laws. This explains how a law can survive a change in interpretation. It does not establish that every familiar law must survive every extension: once the allowable systems themselves change, an argument must state which laws it assumes. The name [symbolical algebra](../mathematics.md#symbolical-algebra) consequently carries a stronger methodological claim than simply writing an unknown as $x$.

[William Rowan Hamilton](../mathematics-author.md#william-rowan-hamilton)'s work on [quaternions](../algebra-over-a-field.md#quaternion) supplies a decisive example of that change. His publications of the 1840s describe a new system of imaginaries in [algebra](../algebra.md). With quaternion units,

$$
\boxed{i^2=j^2=k^2=-1,\qquad ij=k,\qquad ji=-k.}
$$

Thus [multiplication](../arithmetic.md#multiplication) need not satisfy the [commutativity](../algebraic-operation.md#commutativity) even when it remains [associative](../algebraic-operation.md#associative-property) and supports division by nonzero elements. The new system is not merely a further list of roots of old [polynomial equations](../polynomial.md#polynomial-equation). It has its own operations, interpretation and laws. [William Rowan Hamilton](../mathematics-author.md#william-rowan-hamilton)'s title makes explicit that a plurality of calculi can belong within [algebra](../algebra.md).[Philosophical Magazine series beginning in 1844, archived by Trinity College Dublin](../mathematics-bibliography.md#on-quaternions-or-on-a-new-system-of-imaginaries-in-algebra-by-william-rowan-hamilton)

[George Boole](../mathematics.md#george-boole)'s [An Investigation of the Laws of Thought by George Boole](../mathematics-bibliography.md#an-investigation-of-the-laws-of-thought-by-george-boole) (1854) offers a different interpretation of symbols. If $x$ represents selection of a class, repeating that selection gives $x^2=x$; multiplication can represent intersection and $1-x$ a complementary class. His logical calculus borrows from numerical [algebra](../algebra.md) but also restricts interpretation: his sum of classes is not automatically unrestricted [set union](../set.md#set-union), and intermediate symbolic expressions need not name classes directly. It would therefore be inaccurate to identify all of his rules with a modern [Boolean algebra](../mathematical-logic.md#boolean-algebra). The example nevertheless shows why algebraic reasoning could extend beyond magnitudes.[Chapters II–III, laws of class symbols and the special law x²=x](../mathematics-bibliography.md#an-investigation-of-the-laws-of-thought-by-george-boole)

The shift can also take a concrete linear form. [Arthur Cayley](../mathematics-author.md#arthur-cayley)'s [A Memoir on the Theory of Matrices by Arthur Cayley](../mathematics-bibliography.md#a-memoir-on-the-theory-of-matrices-by-arthur-cayley) (1858) treats [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) with operations of their own. The [identity matrix](../vector-space.md#identity-matrix) is no longer the scalar $1$, though it plays an analogous role; [matrix multiplication](../vector-space.md#matrix-multiplication) is generally [noncommutative](../algebraic-operation.md#noncommutativity). For example,

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

produce $AB=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ and $BA=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$. This is a checkable reason why universal arithmetic can no longer mean that every rule of ordinary numbers applies unchanged.[1858, opening definitions and operations](../mathematics-bibliography.md#a-memoir-on-the-theory-of-matrices-by-arthur-cayley)

Meanwhile, [substitution theory](../finite-group-theory.md#substitution-theory) studies [permutations](../combinatorics.md#permutation) and their compositions, originally in close relation to [polynomial equations](../polynomial.md#polynomial-equation). [Évariste Galois](../mathematics.md#evariste-galois)'s work connects the [solvability by radicals](../galois-theory.md#solvability-by-radicals) of an equation with its associated [group](../group.md). [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics.md#augustin-louis-cauchy)'s work on substitutions and [Heinrich Weber](../mathematics.md#heinrich-martin-weber)'s 1882 [definition](../mathematical-logic.md#definition-mathematics) illustrate successive forms of generalisation: from particular permutations towards arbitrary elements satisfying operational conditions. This is not a claim that one man supplied the complete modern hierarchy of [groups](../group.md), [rings](../commutative-algebra.md#ring) and [fields](../algebra.md#field) at once. It identifies the change in the question: one can now investigate what follows from the laws of an operation, independently of whether the elements are numbers, substitutions or classes.[Nineteenth-century development from substitutions to abstract definitions](../mathematics-bibliography.md#the-abstract-group-concept-by-the-mactutor-history-of-mathematics-archive)

**By 1900, algebra could concern the laws and structures of several calculi.** Earlier meanings persisted in teaching and in the theory of [equations](../algebra.md#equation). The strongest account of the change therefore combines continuity with enlargement: letters for general quantities, symbolic operations with explicit laws, nonnumerical interpretations, and structural questions about those laws. Analytical art foregrounded a method of discovery, universal arithmetic foregrounded general calculation, symbolical algebra foregrounded formal operations, and substitution theory foregrounded a particular class of transformations. Their differing uses help explain the expansion more precisely than a single date for the birth of abstraction.

Standards of [mathematical proof](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-proof) changed between the seventeenth and nineteenth centuries because both the objects studied and the operations performed on them changed. A finite geometrical construction, an infinite expansion, and an abstract [group](../group.md) raise different questions about justification. There was no universal seventeenth-century indifference to rigour followed by its sudden nineteenth-century invention. The more defensible thesis is that successful methods repeatedly exposed unstated assumptions; later work made some of those assumptions into explicit definitions, hypotheses and existence arguments.

[Pierre de Fermat](../mathematics.md#pierre-de-fermat)'s [adequality](../mathematics.md#adequality) illustrates the distinction between an effective method and its justification. For $F(a)=a(L-a)$, comparing neighbouring expressions gives

$$
F(a+e)-F(a)=e(L-2a)-e^2.
$$

Dividing by $e$ and suppressing the remaining term in $e$ produces $a=L/2$. There is an independent finite verification:

$$
\boxed{F(a)=\frac{L^2}{4}-\left(a-\frac L2\right)^2\leq\frac{L^2}{4}.}
$$

Thus the result can be secure even when the general procedure raises a question: why is the increment first nonzero for division and then discarded? In the [parabola](../euclidean-geometry.md#parabola) construction of question 2, the same issue appears in replacing a strict geometrical comparison by [adequality](../mathematics.md#adequality) and extracting the [subtangent](../geometry-and-topology.md#subtangent). A historical explanation must describe this operation on its own terms rather than silently equate it with a modern [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative).[Procedure of adequality and tangent example](../mathematics-bibliography.md#fermat-s-rule-for-maxima-and-minima-by-the-garden-of-archimedes)

[Isaac Newton](../mathematics.md#isaac-newton) and [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](../mathematics.md#gottfried-wilhelm-leibniz) supplied different languages for the new [calculus](../calculus.md). [Isaac Newton](../mathematics.md#isaac-newton)'s [Introduction to the Quadrature of Curves by Isaac Newton](../mathematics-bibliography.md#introduction-to-the-quadrature-of-curves-by-isaac-newton), first published with the 1704 Opticks, presents quantities generated by motion and considers their rates of change. Its mature language of first and last ratios seeks exact relations as increments vanish; it should not be confused with simply asserting that a small error is harmless. [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](../mathematics.md#gottfried-wilhelm-leibniz)'s 1684 [Nova methodus pro maximis et minimis by Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](../mathematics-bibliography.md#nova-methodus-pro-maximis-et-minimis-by-gottfried-wilhelm-leibniz) presents a calculus of differentials and operational rules. Their common problem was to explain why procedures using vanishing increments establish exact results, while their chosen objects and modes of explanation differed.[Opening discussion of generated quantities and fluxions; 1704 publication distinguished from the archived 1711 Latin and 1710 English texts](../mathematics-bibliography.md#introduction-to-the-quadrature-of-curves-by-isaac-newton)[Acta Eruditorum, October 1684, 467–473, original pages reproduced by the Mathematical Association of America](../mathematics-bibliography.md#nova-methodus-pro-maximis-et-minimis-by-gottfried-wilhelm-leibniz)

[George Berkeley](../README.md#george-berkeley)'s [The Analyst by George Berkeley](../mathematics-bibliography.md#the-analyst-by-george-berkeley) (1734) exposes the logical pressure point. For the cube,

$$
\frac{(x+h)^3-x^3}{h}=3x^2+3xh+h^2\qquad(h\ne0).
$$

The finite identity does not become a legal division at $h=0$. The modern repair is to keep $h\ne0$ throughout the identity and prove that its right-hand side tends to $3x^2$. That [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function) is different from a quotient evaluated at zero. [George Berkeley](../README.md#george-berkeley)'s criticism therefore concerns the grounds of inference, not whether the established rules often produce correct answers. His theological polemic also reminds us that standards of [mathematical proof](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-proof) could be contested outside specialist calculation.[1734, §§13–18, critique of evanescent increments and changes of hypothesis](../mathematics-bibliography.md#the-analyst-by-george-berkeley)

The infinite introduced another burden. [Joseph Fourier](../mathematics.md#joseph-fourier)'s 1822 use of [trigonometric series](../sequence-and-series.md#trigonometric-series) to represent arbitrary [functions](../function.md) greatly extended what calculations in [mathematical physics](../mathematical-physics.md) could express. But deriving [Fourier coefficients](../fourier-series.md#fourier-coefficient) by [orthogonality](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) only shows what those [coefficients](../vector-space.md#coefficient) must be if the proposed operations are legitimate. It does not prove [convergence](../calculus.md#convergence), equality with the initial data, or interchange of an [integral](../integral.md) and an infinite [sum](../arithmetic.md#sum). The example of a constant expanded in a [Fourier sine series](../fourier-series.md#fourier-sine-series) makes a hidden qualification visible: the terms all vanish at the endpoints, even when the assigned data do not. The correct theorem must specify the domain and mode of representation.[§233, broad representation claim and integral coefficients](../mathematics-bibliography.md#theorie-analytique-de-la-chaleur-by-joseph-fourier/propagation-de-la-chaleur-dans-un-solide-rectangle-infini)

[Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](../mathematics.md#peter-gustav-lejeune-dirichlet)'s 1829 memoir supplies a particularly precise example of changing proof obligations. It criticises a convergence argument of [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics.md#augustin-louis-cauchy)'s which used complex substitution into an arbitrary [function](../function.md) and an insufficient comparison between signed [series](../sequence-and-series.md#series-mathematics). [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](../mathematics.md#peter-gustav-lejeune-dirichlet) supplies the instructive pair

$$
a_n=\frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt n},\qquad
b_n=\frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt n}+\frac1n.
$$

The first [series](../sequence-and-series.md#series-mathematics) converges by the [alternating series test](../sequence-and-series.md#alternating-series-test); the second diverges because their difference is the [harmonic series](../sequence-and-series.md#harmonic-series). Nevertheless $b_n/a_n=1+(-1)^n/\sqrt n\to1$. Thus asymptotic equality of terms does not transfer convergence between conditionally convergent signed [series](../sequence-and-series.md#series-mathematics). A comparison principle valid for positive terms needs additional justification when cancellation is involved. The example refutes a mode of inference rather than merely correcting a numerical answer.[1829, opening objections and explicit counterexample](../mathematics-bibliography.md#sur-la-convergence-des-series-trigonometriques-qui-servent-a-representer-une-fonction-arbitraire-entre-des-limites-donnees-by-peter-gustav-lejeune-dirichlet)

[Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics.md#augustin-louis-cauchy)'s own foundations programme is part of this change. The 1821 [Cours d'analyse by Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics-bibliography.md#cours-d-analyse-by-augustin-louis-cauchy) makes [limits](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function), [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) and [series convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#series-convergence) central to elementary reasoning. Yet its [Sum theorem of Cauchy](../sequence-and-series.md#sum-theorem-of-cauchy) also shows why announcing rigour does not make every proof adequate. In modern terminology, continuous [functions](../function.md) can have a discontinuous [pointwise limit](../sequence-and-series.md#pointwise-limit). An explicit [series](../sequence-and-series.md#series-mathematics) version is

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(x^n-x^{n+1})=
\begin{cases}x&0\leq x<1,\\0&x=1.
\end{cases}
$$

Each term is a [polynomial](../polynomial.md) and the [partial sum](../sequence-and-series.md#partial-sum) is $x-x^{N+1}$. The convergence does not hold in [uniform convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#uniform-convergence): with $x_N=1-1/N$, the error $x_N^{N+1}$ tends to $e^{-1}$ rather than zero. The modern [uniform limit theorem](../sequence-and-series.md#uniform-limit-theorem) repairs the inference by requiring one bound on the error to work throughout the domain. This example diagnoses the missing control under a pointwise reading; it does not settle every historical interpretation of [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../mathematics.md#augustin-louis-cauchy)'s infinitesimal language.[Chapter VI, §I, sum theorem and preceding proof](../mathematics-bibliography.md#cours-d-analyse-by-augustin-louis-cauchy)

The issue is the order of dependence in quantifiers. [Pointwise convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#pointwise-convergence) permits the threshold $N$ to depend on both the error tolerance and the point. [Uniform convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#uniform-convergence) requires a threshold depending only on the tolerance. If $f_N$ is continuous near $x_0$ and the convergence is uniform, choose $N$ so that $|f-f_N|<\varepsilon/3$ nearby, then use the continuity of this fixed $f_N$ to make $|f_N(x)-f_N(x_0)|<\varepsilon/3$. The [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) proves $|f(x)-f(x_0)|<\varepsilon$. Writing out this proof reveals what a vague statement that the tail is small leaves unproved.

Another nineteenth-century development concerns the foundations of the [real numbers](../arithmetic.md#real-number) themselves. [Richard Dedekind](../mathematics.md#richard-dedekind)'s [Continuity and Irrational Numbers by Richard Dedekind](../mathematics-bibliography.md#continuity-and-irrational-numbers-by-richard-dedekind) (1872) asks for an arithmetical account of the continuity often borrowed from a drawn line. A [Dedekind cut](../sequence-and-series.md#dedekind-cut) separates the [rational numbers](../number-theory.md#rational-number) into lower and upper classes. For instance,

$$
A=\{q\in\mathbb Q:q<0\text{ or }q^2<2\},\qquad B=\mathbb Q\setminus A
$$

defines a gap in the [rational numbers](../number-theory.md#rational-number) corresponding to $\sqrt2$. The cut is determined without assuming that a rational number fills it. The extended number system then supports existence theorems that a geometrical picture alone had suggested.[1872 preface and §§III–V; English translation in Essays on the Theory of Numbers](../mathematics-bibliography.md#continuity-and-irrational-numbers-by-richard-dedekind)

This matters for apparently elementary proofs. Suppose $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ with $f(a)<0<f(b)$. Repeated [bisection](../numerical-analysis.md#bisection-method) constructs nested [intervals](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics) whose endpoint values have opposite signs and whose lengths tend to zero. To conclude that there is a common limiting point, one needs the [completeness of the real numbers](../sequence-and-series.md#completeness-of-the-real-numbers). Continuity then forces its value to be zero. Over the [rational numbers](../number-theory.md#rational-number), $f(q)=q^2-2$ changes sign between $1$ and $2$ but has no rational zero. Thus even a proof that avoids pictures must explain the existence of its limiting object. [Richard Dedekind](../mathematics.md#richard-dedekind)'s account makes that requirement an explicit part of the mathematical setting, rather than an intuition about a continuous line.[§VII, existence of limits and its relation to completeness](../mathematics-bibliography.md#continuity-and-irrational-numbers-by-richard-dedekind)

There is a parallel change in [algebra](../algebra.md). [Heinrich Weber](../mathematics.md#heinrich-martin-weber)'s 1882 [definition](../mathematical-logic.md#definition-mathematics) in question 6 describes arbitrary elements through laws of composition. To assess such a definition one proves implications between [axioms](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom). The passage's [finite set](../set.md#finite-set) hypothesis implies that cancellative multiplication has an [identity element](../algebraic-operation.md#identity-element) and [inverse elements](../algebraic-operation.md#inverse-element); without that hypothesis, the positive [integers](../integer.md) under [addition](../arithmetic.md#addition) supply a counterexample. This is a new kind of proof obligation: one must establish that a proposed abstract [definition](../mathematical-logic.md#definition-mathematics) has the intended consequences, rather than rely on their familiarity in particular numerical or permutation examples.[1882 opening definition](../mathematics-bibliography.md#beweis-des-satzes-dass-jede-eigentlich-primitive-quadratische-form-unendlich-viele-primzahlen-darzustellen-fahig-ist-by-heinrich-weber)

**The principal change was towards explicit control of operations, hypotheses and existence.** Effective symbolic algorithms persisted, and geometric reasoning remained valuable. What became increasingly demanding was the passage from success in examples to a general claim: an infinitesimal step needed justification, an infinite expansion needed a convergence theorem, a limiting point needed a complete number system, and an abstract definition needed its consequences proved. The examples also prevent a simple progress story: nineteenth-century mathematicians disagreed about admissible reasoning, and a programme devoted to rigour could itself become the object of a successful critique.
