↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

[Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../../../augustin-louis-cauchy.md)'s importance lies in the interaction of foundations, methods and applications. He made the justification of [limits](../../../limit-of-a-function.md), [series](../../../series-mathematics.md) and [integrals](../../../integral-split.md) a programme for teaching and research; developed [complex analysis](../../../complex-analysis-split.md) into a source of powerful general methods; and contributed to [permutation groups](../../../permutation-group.md), [geometry](../../../geometry.md) and [continuum mechanics](../../../continuum-mechanics-split.md). His work is best assessed through particular achievements and their limitations, rather than by treating every theorem now bearing his name as a modern statement he already possessed. His engineering training and subsequent mathematical career also make a rigid separation of pure and applied work historically unhelpful.[Biographical account of engineering education and mathematical research](../../../augustin-louis-cauchy-by-the-mactutor-history-of-mathematics-archive.md)

The [Cours d'analyse by Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../../../cours-d-analyse-by-augustin-louis-cauchy.md) (1821) makes [convergence](../../../convergence.md) a condition requiring argument. A [series](../../../series-mathematics.md) is studied through its [partial sums](../../../partial-sum.md); a [limit](../../../limit-of-a-function.md) describes a variable approaching a fixed value; an [infinitesimal](../../../infinitesimal.md) is a variable tending to zero, rather than simply a mysterious fixed quantity smaller than every positive number. The introduction explicitly challenges unrestricted transfers of rules from finite expressions to infinite ones. This is not an abandonment of calculation: it seeks to identify when calculations are valid.[Introduction; Chapter II, continuity; Chapter VI, convergence](../../../cours-d-analyse-by-augustin-louis-cauchy.md)

For example, the [Cauchy criterion for series](../../../cauchy-criterion-for-series.md) asks whether tails can all be made small:

$$
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N\ \forall m>n\geq N:
\left|\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}a_k\right|<\varepsilon.
$$

This is a modern formulation of the convergence requirement, with the [completeness of the real numbers](../../../completeness-of-the-real-numbers.md) understood. Merely requiring $a_n\to0$ is weaker. In the [harmonic series](../../../harmonic-series.md), grouping the terms from $2^j+1$ to $2^{j+1}$ gives a block of at least $1/2$, so its tails cannot satisfy the criterion. In contrast, the [root test](../../../root-test.md) compares a tail with a [geometric series](../../../geometric-series.md): if $\limsup |a_n|^{1/n}<q<1$, then eventually $|a_n|\leq q^n$, which proves [absolute convergence](../../../absolute-convergence.md). Such arguments explain the usefulness of convergence tests instead of reducing [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../../../augustin-louis-cauchy.md)'s contribution to a vocabulary of definitions.

His [Résumé des leçons sur le calcul infinitésimal by Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../../../resume-des-lecons-sur-le-calcul-infinitesimal-by-augustin-louis-cauchy.md) (1823) extends the programme to [differentiation](../../../differentiation.md) and [integration](../../../integral-split.md). For a continuous [function](../../../function-split.md) on a compact [interval](../../../interval-mathematics.md), an [integral](../../../integral-split.md) can be approached through sums of values times subinterval lengths. A modern justification uses [uniform continuity](../../../uniform-continuity.md): for a partition of mesh at most $\delta$, two choices of sample points give sums differing by at most $(b-a)\omega_f(\delta)$, where the [modulus of continuity](../../../modulus-of-continuity.md) $\omega_f(\delta)$ tends to zero. Refinement then establishes the common [limit](../../../limit-of-a-function.md). This reconstruction makes clear why control of error, rather than a geometrical picture of area alone, is decisive. It does not attribute the later full [Riemann integral](../../../riemann-integral.md) theory for discontinuous [functions](../../../function-split.md) to the 1823 text.[1823 original edition, lessons on derivatives and definite integrals](../../../resume-des-lecons-sur-le-calcul-infinitesimal-by-augustin-louis-cauchy.md)

The achievement has limits. The 1821 [Sum theorem of Cauchy](../../../sum-theorem-of-cauchy.md) asserts continuity of sums of convergent [series](../../../series-mathematics.md) of continuous [functions](../../../function-split.md) in terms that, under a pointwise reading, are insufficient. The original proof treats the tail as negligible without establishing the uniform control needed near the point concerned. A modern diagnostic is $f_n(x)=x^n$ on $[0,1]$: every $f_n$ is continuous, but its [pointwise limit](../../../pointwise-limit.md) is zero for $x<1$ and one at $1$. Thus a proof must distinguish [pointwise convergence](../../../pointwise-convergence.md) from [uniform convergence](../../../uniform-convergence.md). [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../../../augustin-louis-cauchy.md)'s use of variable and infinitesimal language deserves historical interpretation, but it does not license silently importing modern hypotheses into his printed theorem.[Chapter VI, §I, Theorem I and preceding remainder argument](../../../cours-d-analyse-by-augustin-louis-cauchy.md)

In [complex analysis](../../../complex-analysis-split.md), the [Mémoire sur les intégrales définies, prises entre des limites imaginaires by Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../../../memoire-sur-les-integrales-definies-prises-entre-des-limites-imaginaires-by-augustin-louis-cauchy.md) (1825) exemplifies a new productive setting for [integration](../../../integral-split.md). A [contour integral](../../../contour-integral.md) depends on a path in the [complex plane](../../../complex-plane.md); deforming that path and accounting for [isolated singularities](../../../isolated-singularity-split.md) can evaluate otherwise difficult [definite integrals](../../../definite-integral.md). In modern form, [Cauchy's integral theorem](../../../cauchy-s-integral-theorem.md) applies to a [holomorphic function](../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on a suitable domain and [Cauchy integral formula](../../../cauchy-integral-formula.md) states, for a circle enclosing $z$ and lying with its interior in that domain,

$$
\boxed{f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\gamma\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,d\zeta.}
$$

The importance is structural: local values and [derivatives](../../../derivative.md) are determined by data on a surrounding path. Historical proofs and hypotheses should be distinguished from later general forms of the theorem.[1825 original edition, contour integration and the treatment of singularities](../../../memoire-sur-les-integrales-definies-prises-entre-des-limites-imaginaires-by-augustin-louis-cauchy.md)

A short modern consequence measures the power of this method. For an [entire function](../../../entire-function.md) bounded by $M$, the derivative version of [Cauchy integral formula](../../../cauchy-integral-formula.md) on a circle of radius $R$ gives $|f'(z)|\leq M/R$. Since $R$ is arbitrary, $f'=0$, proving [Liouville's theorem](../../../liouville-theorem.md). If a nonconstant [polynomial](../../../polynomial-split.md) $P$ had no [root](../../../root-of-a-root-system.md), then $1/P$ would be an [entire function](../../../entire-function.md), bounded outside a large disc by the leading term and inside the disc by [compactness](../../../compact-space.md). [Liouville's theorem](../../../liouville-theorem.md) would force it to be constant, a contradiction. This proves the [fundamental theorem of algebra](../../../fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md). The chain is an assessment of the reach of the methods, not a claim that [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../../../augustin-louis-cauchy.md) alone originated every named result.

[Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../../../augustin-louis-cauchy.md)'s [algebra](../../../algebra-split.md) concerns concrete [permutations](../../../permutation.md) as well as analytical formulas. His 1845 investigations developed the theory of substitutions; [Cauchy theorem for groups](../../../cauchy-theorem-for-groups.md) says that a [prime number](../../../prime-number-split.md) dividing a finite [group order](../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) occurs as an [element order](../../../order-of-a-group-element.md). One can appreciate its generality from a modern argument: rotate the coordinates of the tuples $(g_1,\ldots,g_p)$ satisfying $g_1\cdots g_p=e$. Rotation preserves this condition, since $g_2\cdots g_pg_1=g_1^{-1}(g_1\cdots g_p)g_1=e$. There are $|G|^{p-1}$ such tuples; nonfixed rotation [orbits](../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) have $p$ elements, and fixed tuples correspond to $g^p=e$. When $p\mid|G|$, the number of fixed tuples is divisible by $p$, so besides $e$ there is an element of [element order](../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $p$. This is explanatory mathematics, not [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../../../augustin-louis-cauchy.md)'s original proof. The dating itself needs care: [Peter Michael Neumann](../../../peter-m-neumann.md)'s archival argument places the major burst of work in 1845 despite an 1844 date traditionally attached to a volume.[1989, opening argument and discussion of the 1845 papers](../../../on-the-date-of-cauchy-s-contributions-to-the-founding-of-the-theory-of-groups-by-peter-michael-neumann.md)

Finally, [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../../../augustin-louis-cauchy.md)'s work on [elasticity](../../../elasticity-physics-split.md) shows the strength of making a local mathematical object out of physical interactions. Instead of describing only a force perpendicular to a surface, one studies the [traction](../../../traction.md) on a plane through a point, with its direction dependent on the plane's [unit normal](../../../unit-normal.md). In modern notation the relation is $\mathbf t(\mathbf n)=\boldsymbol\sigma\mathbf n$, where the [Cauchy stress tensor](../../../cauchy-stress-tensor.md) contains the necessary directional information. Together with [balance of linear momentum](../../../balance-of-linear-momentum.md), this yields equations for the internal motion of a body. The modern tensor notation is later explanatory language; the conceptual contribution is the reduction of the forces on arbitrary internal planes to a finite collection of local components.[Dictionary of Scientific Biography, section on mathematical physics, reproducing Cauchy's 1822 explanation of pressures and tensions](../../../augustin-louis-cauchy-by-hans-freudenthal.md)

**[Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../../../augustin-louis-cauchy.md)'s lasting contribution was a powerful research programme, not a finished foundation.** His insistence on justified passage to a [limit](../../../limit-of-a-function.md) reshaped [analysis](../../../analysis-split.md), while [complex integration](../../../complex-integration.md), substitutions and local stresses opened methods whose value extended beyond their first applications. The inadequacy of a [pointwise convergence](../../../pointwise-convergence.md) hypothesis, the need to state domains in [complex analysis](../../../complex-analysis-split.md), and the later construction of the [real numbers](../../../real-number.md) show that nineteenth-century rigour remained a collective and revisable achievement. These qualifications strengthen an assessment of his influence by identifying what later mathematicians could extend and repair.
