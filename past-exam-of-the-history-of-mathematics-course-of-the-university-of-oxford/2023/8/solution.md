Between 1600 and 1900, [algebra](../../../algebra-split.md) broadened from arts of working with unknown quantities and solving [equations](../../../equation.md) into the study of several systems of symbolic operations and increasingly general [algebraic structures](../../../algebraic-structure.md). This was neither a single change of definition nor a clean replacement of one subject by another. [Polynomial equations](../../../polynomial-equation.md) remained central while new meanings accumulated. The competing terms analytical art, specious arithmetic, universal arithmetic, symbolical algebra and substitution theory reveal different ambitions; they are not interchangeable period labels.

Near 1600, [François Viète](../../../francois-viete.md)'s [In artem analyticem isagoge by François Viète](../../../in-artem-analyticem-isagoge-by-francois-viete.md) (1591) supplies a particularly revealing starting point. His analytical art uses letters for known quantities as well as unknown ones. In his terminology, [logistica numerosa](../../../logistica-numerosa.md) concerns numerical calculation and [logistica speciosa](../../../logistica-speciosa.md) calculation with species, or general magnitudes. Vowels represent unknowns and consonants known magnitudes. This allows a single relation to represent many particular problems, but it is still tied to geometrical homogeneity: a square and a length do not simply become quantities of the same kind because they are written with letters.[1591, opening analytical programme and rules of species; terminology and notation discussed in the MacTutor Viète biography](../../../in-artem-analyticem-isagoge-by-francois-viete.md)[Discussion of known and unknown symbols and dimensional homogeneity](../../../francois-viete-by-john-joseph-o-connor-and-edmund-frederick-robertson.md)

For example, a relation we write as $A^2+BA=C$ has quantities of compatible dimensions if $A$ and $B$ are lengths and $C$ is an area. Letters do more than abbreviate a numerical question: they express relations between whole families of magnitudes. The phrase specious arithmetic therefore indicates [arithmetic](../../../arithmetic-split.md) carried out on species; analysis stresses the art of reasoning from an assumed unknown towards its determination. Neither term should be reduced to the modern subject [real analysis](../../../real-analysis-split.md).

[René Descartes](../../../rene-descartes.md)'s [La Géométrie by René Descartes](../../../la-geometrie-by-rene-descartes.md) (1637) demonstrates another relation between [algebra](../../../algebra-split.md) and [geometry](../../../geometry.md). A chosen unit segment lets [multiplication](../../../multiplication.md), [division](../../../division-mathematics.md) and extraction of [square roots](../../../square-root.md) be represented by constructions of lengths. The product of two measured lengths can itself be represented by a length relative to that unit. In modern dimensional notation the construction gives $ab/u$ for unit length $u$, removing the obstacle that a product must always be treated as an area. The text's [equations](../../../equation.md) can then organise geometrical problems and classify [curves](../../../curve.md) by their algebraic complexity. Its title remains geometry: [algebra](../../../algebra-split.md) serves a geometrical programme rather than announcing a present-day department of [abstract algebra](../../../abstract-algebra.md).[Book I, operations on lines and choice of unit; Book II, treatment of curves](../../../la-geometrie-by-rene-descartes.md)

A further term, [universal arithmetic](../../../universal-arithmetic.md), stresses the extension of calculation from particular numbers to general quantities. [Isaac Newton](../../../isaac-newton.md)'s [Arithmetica universalis by Isaac Newton](../../../arithmetica-universalis-by-isaac-newton.md), issued in 1707, makes the term visible in a title whose subtitle concerns composition and resolution. A title is evidence for the vocabulary, not a sufficient definition of all contemporary usage.[1707 title page and original-edition record, New York Public Library](../../../arithmetica-universalis-by-isaac-newton.md) In [Leonhard Euler](../../../leonhard-euler.md)'s [Elements of Algebra by Leonhard Euler](../../../elements-of-algebra-by-leonhard-euler.md), published in German in 1770, general numerical calculation and the solution of [equations](../../../equation.md) remain a substantial conception of the subject. For instance, allowing negative and [complex roots](../../../complex-root.md) changes the admissible answers to [polynomial equations](../../../polynomial-equation.md) while preserving the central question of determining an unknown.[1770 original publication and later English edition; sections on operations and equations](../../../elements-of-algebra-by-leonhard-euler.md)

The eighteenth-century [elimination theory](../../../elimination-theory.md) exemplified by [Alexandre-Théophile Vandermonde](../../../alexandre-theophile-vandermonde.md)'s memoir broadens the scale of the task. Instead of one numerical [quadratic equation](../../../quadratic-equation.md), one seeks a rule for an arbitrary number of simultaneous [linear equations](../../../linear-equation.md). The [determinant](../../../determinant.md) is initially valuable because it effects that elimination. This is a different generality from the later study of a [group](../../../group-split.md): the objects being determined are still unknown quantities, and symbolic generality concerns the number and [coefficients](../../../coefficient.md) of the [equations](../../../equation.md). The case cautions against using generality alone as a test for whether [algebra](../../../algebra-split.md) has become abstract.[Opening programme and positional coefficient notation](../../../memoire-sur-l-elimination-by-alexandre-theophile-vandermonde.md)

In nineteenth-century British discussion, [George Peacock](../../../george-peacock.md)'s [A Treatise on Algebra by George Peacock](../../../a-treatise-on-algebra-by-george-peacock.md) (1830) distinguishes [arithmetical algebra](../../../arithmetical-algebra.md) from [symbolical algebra](../../../symbolical-algebra.md). If the symbols mean positive numbers, $a-(a+b)$ has no positive-number result. Yet algebraists manipulate this expression without restriction. [George Peacock](../../../george-peacock.md) therefore seeks principles for the manipulation of signs which are not confined by their original arithmetical interpretation. The contrast is explicitly argued in his preface: [arithmetic](../../../arithmetic-split.md) suggests the laws, while the symbolic science requires foundations adequate to its more general conclusions.[1830 preface, especially the discussion of a−(a+b) and arithmetic as a science of suggestion](../../../a-treatise-on-algebra-by-george-peacock.md)

The familiar extension $(-1)(-1)=1$ illustrates the stakes. From [distributivity](../../../distributive-property.md), $0=(-1)(1+(-1))=-1+(-1)(-1)$, so the rule is forced if these operations obey the assumed laws. This explains how a law can survive a change in interpretation. It does not establish that every familiar law must survive every extension: once the allowable systems themselves change, an argument must state which laws it assumes. The name [symbolical algebra](../../../symbolical-algebra.md) consequently carries a stronger methodological claim than simply writing an unknown as $x$.

[William Rowan Hamilton](../../../william-rowan-hamilton.md)'s work on [quaternions](../../../quaternion.md) supplies a decisive example of that change. His publications of the 1840s describe a new system of imaginaries in [algebra](../../../algebra-split.md). With quaternion units,

$$
\boxed{i^2=j^2=k^2=-1,\qquad ij=k,\qquad ji=-k.}
$$

Thus [multiplication](../../../multiplication.md) need not satisfy the [commutativity](../../../commutativity.md) even when it remains [associative](../../../associative-property.md) and supports division by nonzero elements. The new system is not merely a further list of roots of old [polynomial equations](../../../polynomial-equation.md). It has its own operations, interpretation and laws. [William Rowan Hamilton](../../../william-rowan-hamilton.md)'s title makes explicit that a plurality of calculi can belong within [algebra](../../../algebra-split.md).[Philosophical Magazine series beginning in 1844, archived by Trinity College Dublin](../../../on-quaternions-or-on-a-new-system-of-imaginaries-in-algebra-by-william-rowan-hamilton.md)

[George Boole](../../../george-boole.md)'s [An Investigation of the Laws of Thought by George Boole](../../../an-investigation-of-the-laws-of-thought-by-george-boole.md) (1854) offers a different interpretation of symbols. If $x$ represents selection of a class, repeating that selection gives $x^2=x$; multiplication can represent intersection and $1-x$ a complementary class. His logical calculus borrows from numerical [algebra](../../../algebra-split.md) but also restricts interpretation: his sum of classes is not automatically unrestricted [set union](../../../set-union.md), and intermediate symbolic expressions need not name classes directly. It would therefore be inaccurate to identify all of his rules with a modern [Boolean algebra](../../../boolean-algebra.md). The example nevertheless shows why algebraic reasoning could extend beyond magnitudes.[Chapters II–III, laws of class symbols and the special law x²=x](../../../an-investigation-of-the-laws-of-thought-by-george-boole.md)

The shift can also take a concrete linear form. [Arthur Cayley](../../../arthur-cayley.md)'s [A Memoir on the Theory of Matrices by Arthur Cayley](../../../a-memoir-on-the-theory-of-matrices-by-arthur-cayley.md) (1858) treats [matrices](../../../matrix.md) with operations of their own. The [identity matrix](../../../identity-matrix.md) is no longer the scalar $1$, though it plays an analogous role; [matrix multiplication](../../../matrix-multiplication.md) is generally [noncommutative](../../../noncommutativity.md). For example,

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

produce $AB=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ and $BA=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$. This is a checkable reason why universal arithmetic can no longer mean that every rule of ordinary numbers applies unchanged.[1858, opening definitions and operations](../../../a-memoir-on-the-theory-of-matrices-by-arthur-cayley.md)

Meanwhile, [substitution theory](../../../substitution-theory.md) studies [permutations](../../../permutation.md) and their compositions, originally in close relation to [polynomial equations](../../../polynomial-equation.md). [Évariste Galois](../../../evariste-galois.md)'s work connects the [solvability by radicals](../../../solvability-by-radicals.md) of an equation with its associated [group](../../../group-split.md). [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](../../../augustin-louis-cauchy.md)'s work on substitutions and [Heinrich Weber](../../../heinrich-martin-weber.md)'s 1882 [definition](../../../definition-mathematics.md) illustrate successive forms of generalisation: from particular permutations towards arbitrary elements satisfying operational conditions. This is not a claim that one man supplied the complete modern hierarchy of [groups](../../../group-split.md), [rings](../../../ring.md) and [fields](../../../field.md) at once. It identifies the change in the question: one can now investigate what follows from the laws of an operation, independently of whether the elements are numbers, substitutions or classes.[Nineteenth-century development from substitutions to abstract definitions](../../../the-abstract-group-concept-by-the-mactutor-history-of-mathematics-archive.md)

**By 1900, algebra could concern the laws and structures of several calculi.** Earlier meanings persisted in teaching and in the theory of [equations](../../../equation.md). The strongest account of the change therefore combines continuity with enlargement: letters for general quantities, symbolic operations with explicit laws, nonnumerical interpretations, and structural questions about those laws. Analytical art foregrounded a method of discovery, universal arithmetic foregrounded general calculation, symbolical algebra foregrounded formal operations, and substitution theory foregrounded a particular class of transformations. Their differing uses help explain the expansion more precisely than a single date for the birth of abstraction.
