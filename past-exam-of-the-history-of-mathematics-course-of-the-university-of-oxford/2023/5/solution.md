↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

[Carl Friedrich Gauss](../../../carl-friedrich-gauss.md) is defending [complex numbers](../../../complex-number.md) as intelligible mathematical objects. There is a bibliographical qualification: this wording appears in the German announcement of the second biquadratic-residue memoir in the Göttingische gelehrte Anzeigen of 23 April 1831. The associated Latin [Theoria residuorum biquadraticorum, commentatio secunda by Carl Friedrich Gauss](../../../theoria-residuorum-biquadraticorum-commentatio-secunda-by-carl-friedrich-gauss.md) appeared in 1832. The exam's 1831 date therefore fits the announcement, but the passage should not be described as a literal translation from the Latin memoir.[23 April 1831, concluding general remarks on terminology and planar representation](../../../announcement-of-the-second-biquadratic-residue-memoir-by-carl-friedrich-gauss.md)[ETH-Bibliothek original-edition record: 1832](../../../theoria-residuorum-biquadraticorum-commentatio-secunda-by-carl-friedrich-gauss.md)

The proposed directional vocabulary is mathematically specific. Represent $z=a+bi$ by the point $(a,b)$ in the [complex plane](../../../complex-plane.md). The addition of [complex numbers](../../../complex-number.md) adds components. Multiplication of [complex numbers](../../../complex-number.md) is

$$
(a+bi)(c+di)=(ac-bd)+(ad+bc)i.
$$

Multiplication by $-1$ reverses direction through a half-turn, while multiplication by $i$ sends $(a,b)$ to $(-b,a)$, a quarter-turn. Consequently $i^2=-1$ says that two quarter-turns make a half-turn. More generally the [moduli](../../../modulus.md) multiply and the [complex arguments](../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) add. These are coherent operations extending [arithmetic](../../../arithmetic-split.md); an unfortunate name supplies no mathematical objection to them.

The memoir's setting is [number theory](../../../number-theory-split.md), specifically the question of whether a [integer congruence](../../../integer-congruence.md) $x^4\equiv a\pmod p$ is soluble, and the reciprocal relations between such questions. Passing to the [Gaussian integers](../../../gaussian-integer.md) $\mathbb Z[i]$ provides arithmetic that is closed under [addition](../../../addition.md) and [multiplication](../../../multiplication.md). For example,

$$
\boxed{5=(2+i)(2-i),\qquad N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2,\qquad N(zw)=N(z)N(w).}
$$

The [Gaussian integer norm](../../../gaussian-integer-norm.md) follows directly by multiplying by the [complex conjugate](../../../complex-conjugate.md). Here it gives $N(2+i)=5$ and $N(5)=25$, consistent with the factorization. An ordinary [prime number](../../../prime-number-split.md) may therefore have factors in the larger arithmetic without any contradiction: the permissible [factors](../../../regression-factor.md) have changed. The original announcement explicitly motivates the extension of [arithmetic](../../../arithmetic-split.md) to these numbers and describes their square-lattice representation.[Opening explanation of complex integral numbers and the later lattice discussion](../../../announcement-of-the-second-biquadratic-residue-memoir-by-carl-friedrich-gauss.md)

It is helpful to separate two domains. Arbitrary [complex numbers](../../../complex-number.md) form a [field](../../../field.md), since the inverse of nonzero $a+bi$ is $(a-bi)/(a^2+b^2)$. The [Gaussian integers](../../../gaussian-integer.md) form a [ring](../../../ring.md), but not a [field](../../../field.md): $1/2$ is not a [Gaussian integer](../../../gaussian-integer.md). Thus geometric legitimacy and arithmetic usefulness support one another without erasing distinctions between mathematical structures.

**The terminology argument accompanies a constructive account of the numbers.** It is not a claim that renaming alone proves a theorem, or that all earlier work with [complex numbers](../../../complex-number.md) had lacked meaning. A [mathematical proof](../../../mathematical-proof.md) still has to establish the laws of the extended arithmetic and the assertions about [biquadratic residues](../../../biquadratic-residue.md). [Carl Friedrich Gauss](../../../carl-friedrich-gauss.md)'s geometric explanation makes those operations accessible while his arithmetic application gives a strong reason to accept the enlarged domain.
