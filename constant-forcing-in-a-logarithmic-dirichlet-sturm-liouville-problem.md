# Constant forcing in a logarithmic Dirichlet Sturm-Liouville problem

↑ **Parent:** [Logarithmic change of variable in a Sturm-Liouville problem](logarithmic-change-of-variable-in-a-sturm-liouville-problem.md)

For $\ell>0$, the [logarithmic change of variable in a Sturm-Liouville problem](logarithmic-change-of-variable-in-a-sturm-liouville-problem.md) sends this equation to $-Y''-\alpha Y=1$ on $(0,\ell)$. The normalized [eigenfunctions](eigenfunction.md) are $\sqrt{2/\ell}\sin(n\pi t/\ell)$, with [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) $\lambda_n=(n\pi/\ell)^2-\alpha$. If every $\lambda_n$ is nonzero, the [Sturm-Liouville eigenfunction expansion](sturm-liouville-eigenfunction-expansion.md) gives

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\sum_{\substack{n\geq1\\n\text{ odd}}}\frac{4\sin(n\pi\log x/\ell)}{n\pi[(n\pi/\ell)^2-\alpha]}.}
$$

Indeed the constant function has sine coefficients $2[1-(-1)^n]/(n\pi)$; division by each eigenvalue solves the equation in the operator domain. The series is uniformly convergent, with coefficients $O(n^{-3})$, while applying the differential operator is justified in the weighted square-integrable sense. At a zero eigenvalue the [solvability condition at a Sturm-Liouville eigenvalue](solvability-condition-at-a-sturm-liouville-eigenvalue.md) must instead be checked: an odd resonant index makes the constant forcing incompatible, whereas an even resonant index permits solutions with an arbitrary multiple of that zero mode.
