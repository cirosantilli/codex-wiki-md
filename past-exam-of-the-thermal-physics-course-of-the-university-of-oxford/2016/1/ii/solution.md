<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

With a thermally insulating boundary, $\delta Q=0$. If the slow piston motion is frictionless and reversible, it is an [isentropic process](../../../../isentropic-process.md):

$$
\boxed{S=\mathrm{constant}.}
$$

The [first law of thermodynamics](../../../../first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) gives $dU=-p\,dV$, so expansion lowers the [internal energy](../../../../internal-energy-split.md) and [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md). For constant [heat capacity ratio](../../../../heat-capacity-ratio.md) $\gamma$, $pV^\gamma$ and $TV^{\gamma-1}$ are constant combinations of [state functions](../../../../state-function.md). Insulation alone does not guarantee constant [entropy](../../../../entropy-split.md): an irreversible [adiabatic process](../../../../adiabatic-process.md) can produce [entropy](../../../../entropy-split.md). The conclusion uses the intended reversible, quasistatic piston idealization.
