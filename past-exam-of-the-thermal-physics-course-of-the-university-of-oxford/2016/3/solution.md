<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The [Clapeyron equation](../../../clausius-clapeyron-relation.md) for molar [latent heat](../../../latent-heat.md) $L$ is $dp/dT=L/[T(V_{\rm vap}-V_{\rm liq})]$. Neglect the liquid [molar volume](../../../molar-volume.md), treat the vapor as an [ideal gas](../../../ideal-gas-split.md), and approximate $L$ as constant over the interval. Then

$$
\frac{d\ln p}{dT}\simeq\frac{L}{RT^2},\qquad
\ln\frac{p_h}{p_l}=\frac LR\left(\frac1{T_l}-\frac1{T_h}\right).
$$

Using one atmosphere as $101325\,\mathrm{Pa}$ gives

$$
\boxed{L\simeq\frac{R\ln(101325/133)}{1/1.2-1/4.2}=92.7\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}.}
$$

This is the effective molar [enthalpy of vaporization](../../../enthalpy-of-vaporization.md) inferred from the [Integrated Clausius-Clapeyron relation](../../../integrated-clausius-clapeyron-relation.md). If $L$ varies with [temperature](../../../temperature-split.md), the inferred value is weighted by $1/T^2$, not an unweighted arithmetic mean. Real helium is not exactly an [ideal gas](../../../ideal-gas-split.md) over the whole interval, and its liquid changes superfluid state within it, so this is an estimate under the stated approximations rather than a precise temperature-independent material constant.
