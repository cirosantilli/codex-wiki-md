<h1 id="pascal-s-triangle">Pascal's triangle</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Binomial coefficient](binomial-coefficient.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal's_triangle)

Row $n$ of Pascal's triangle consists of the [binomial coefficients](binomial-coefficient.md) $\binom n0,\ldots,\binom nn$, beginning with row $0$. Boundary entries are one, and [Pascal's identity](pascal-s-rule.md) says each interior entry is the sum of the two entries above it. The [hockey-stick identity](hockey-stick-identity.md) evaluates sums along its diagonals.

The highlighted entry in the [binomial coefficient](binomial-coefficient.md) illustration is $\binom52=10$. Split two-person teams according to whether they contain the fifth person: six choose both members from the other four, and four contain the fifth person together with one of those four. Thus $10=6+4$. All entries can be generated by the same local rule, so a large table of counts needs no repeated enumeration of its [subsets](subset.md).

[Blaise Pascal](blaise-pascal.md)'s Traité du triangle arithmétique, published in 1665, presents an arithmetic array and connects it to combinations, games of chance, and powers of binomial expressions. This makes the connection between a counting table and several applications visible in the original work. [Pascal, Traité du triangle arithmétique, 1665 edition](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_UqgUAAAAQAAJ) The University of Cambridge's account of the treatise explains its 1654 composition and the organization of these applications.
