<h1 id="pascal-s-triangle">Pascal's triangle</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Binomial coefficient](binomial-coefficient-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal's_triangle)

Row $n$ of Pascal's triangle consists of the [binomial coefficients](binomial-coefficient-split.md) $\binom n0,\ldots,\binom nn$, beginning with row $0$. Boundary entries are one, and [Pascal's identity](pascal-s-rule.md) says each interior entry is the sum of the two entries above it. The [hockey-stick identity](hockey-stick-identity.md) evaluates sums along its diagonals.

The highlighted entry in the [binomial coefficient](binomial-coefficient-split.md) illustration is $\binom52=10$. Split two-person teams according to whether they contain the fifth person: six choose both members from the other four, and four contain the fifth person together with one of those four. Thus $10=6+4$. All entries can be generated by the same local rule, so a large table of counts needs no repeated enumeration of its [subsets](subset.md).

**Reading row seven.** Label the entries in each row from $k=0$ on the left through $k=n$ on the right. Rows zero through seven are shown below. The highlighted entry $T_{7,3}=35$ comes from $T_{6,2}=15$ and $T_{6,3}=20$. Starting with row zero $(1)$, the edges remain one and [Pascal's rule](pascal-s-rule.md) determines every interior entry, so [mathematical induction](mathematical-induction.md) uniquely determines the whole array.

**[Figure 4](#image-rows-zero-through-seven-of-pascal-s-triangle-with-t-7-3-t-6-2-plus-t-6-3-15-plus-20-35). Rows zero through seven of Pascal's triangle, with $T_{7,3}=T_{6,2}+T_{6,3}=15+20=35$**. The index $k$ increases from left to right in each row; it counts north steps in the [north-east lattice path](north-east-lattice-path.md) interpretation. Each row index $n$ counts all steps.

The triangular and rectangular layouts describe the same entries. For a [north-east lattice path](north-east-lattice-path.md) ending at $(a,b)$, set $n=a+b$ and $k=b$, so $P(a,b)=T_{n,k}$. The predecessor $(a-1,b)$ has indices $(n-1,k)$, while $(a,b-1)$ has indices $(n-1,k-1)$. Thus the grid's west and south arrivals are exactly the triangle's two parents. This change of indices explains why the [north-east lattice path recurrence](north-east-lattice-path-recurrence.md) and [Pascal's rule](pascal-s-rule.md) agree.

There is also a global check on the local construction: row $n$ sums to $2^n$. Every [subset](subset.md) of an $n$-element [set](set-split.md) appears exactly once among the classes of sizes $0,\ldots,n$, and each element has two inclusion choices by the [rule of product](rule-of-product.md). In row seven, $1+7+21+35+35+21+7+1=128=2^7$. Replacing every [subset](subset.md) by its [complement](complement-of-a-set.md) reverses the row, explaining $T_{n,k}=T_{n,n-k}$. The [binomial theorem](binomial-theorem.md) packages the whole row as the [coefficients](coefficient.md) of $(1+x)^n$.

[Blaise Pascal](blaise-pascal.md)'s Traité du triangle arithmétique, published in 1665, presents an arithmetic array and connects it to combinations, games of chance, and powers of binomial expressions. This makes the connection between a counting table and several applications visible in the original work.
