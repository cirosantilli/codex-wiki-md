<h1 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The massive [Dirac propagator](../../../../../dirac-propagator.md) is

$$
S_F(x)=\int\frac{d^Dk}{(2\pi)^D}\frac{i(\not k+m)e^{-ik\cdot x}}{k^2-m^2+i0}.
$$

Use the [gamma matrix trace identities](../../../../../gamma-matrix-trace-identities.md) $\operatorname{tr}\gamma^\mu=0$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\rho)=4g^{\mu\rho}$. They give

$$
\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu S_F(0)]=4i\int\frac{d^Dk}{(2\pi)^D}\frac{k^\mu}{k^2-m^2+i0}=\boxed{0}.
$$

The integrand is odd under $k\mapsto-k$, and [dimensional regularization](../../../../../dimensional-regularization.md) preserves that symmetry. The same vanishing holds for $S=iS_F$. One must not assert that $S(0)$ itself is zero: its term proportional to $m\mathbf1$ generally is nonzero and divergent, but its trace against a single [gamma matrix](../../../../../gamma-matrices.md) is zero. This one-photon [fermion loop](../../../../../fermion-loop.md) is also the simplest instance of [Furry's theorem](../../../../../furry-s-theorem.md).
