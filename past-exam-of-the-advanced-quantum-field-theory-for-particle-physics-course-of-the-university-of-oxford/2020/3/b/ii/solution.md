<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

An ordinary bosonic [Gaussian functional integral](../../../../../gaussian-functional-integral.md) produces an inverse determinant. An integral over independent [Grassmann fields](../../../../../grassmann-field.md) $c,\bar c$ instead produces a determinant, allowing a local action to represent the [Faddeev-Popov determinant](../../../../../faddeev-popov-determinant.md). With one convenient antighost sign convention,

$$
\det\mathcal M\ \propto\ \int\mathcal D\bar c\,\mathcal Dc\,\exp\left(i\int d^4x\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{gh}}\right),\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{gh}}=-\bar c(\partial^2+2\lambda A^\mu\partial_\mu)c.}
$$

Multiplying the operator by the field-independent factor $-i$ changes only the normalization of its determinant. After [integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md), an equivalent density is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{gh}}=(\partial_\mu\bar c)(\partial^\mu c)-2\lambda\bar c A^\mu\partial_\mu c.
$$

Thus the [Faddeev-Popov ghost fields](../../../../../faddeev-popov-ghost.md) have a massless kinetic term and a photon-ghost-antighost interaction proportional to $\lambda$. They are auxiliary anticommuting scalar fields, not physical external [particles](../../../../../particle-physics.md). Closed [ghost loops](../../../../../ghost-loop.md) carry the minus sign of [Grassmann variables](../../../../../grassmann-variable.md). Replacing $\bar c$ by $-\bar c$ reverses both displayed signs without changing any observable; both the kinetic and interaction conventions must be changed together.
