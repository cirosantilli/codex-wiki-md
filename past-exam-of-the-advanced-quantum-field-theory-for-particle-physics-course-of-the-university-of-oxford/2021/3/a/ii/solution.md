<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $g$ and $g'$ be the two [gauge couplings](../../../../../gauge-coupling.md). In [unitary gauge](../../../../../unitary-gauge.md), $\Phi=(0,0,v+h)^T$ and the [weak hypercharge](../../../../../hypercharge.md) term in the [gauge covariant derivative](../../../../../gauge-covariant-derivative.md) vanishes. Using $(T^a)_{bc}=-i\epsilon_{abc}$, the kinetic term becomes

$$
\frac12(D_\mu\Phi)^T(D^\mu\Phi)
=\frac12(\partial h)^2+\frac12g^2(v+h)^2[W_\mu^1W^{1\mu}+W_\mu^2W^{2\mu}].
$$

Thus, in the basis $(W^1,W^2,W^3,B)$, the [gauge-boson mass matrix](../../../../../gauge-boson-mass-matrix.md) and its [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are

$$
\boxed{M_V^2=g^2v^2\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0,0),\qquad
m_{W^\pm}=gv=\frac{gm}{2\sqrt\lambda},\quad m_{W^3}=m_B=0.}
$$

Here $W^\pm=(W^1\mp iW^2)/\sqrt2$. The two massless neutral [gauge bosons](../../../../../gauge-boson.md) may be rotated to any orthonormal basis; no neutral mass term selects a unique mixing angle.

Expanding the same kinetic term to first order in $h$ gives

$$
\mathcal L_{hVV}=g^2vh[(W^1)^2+(W^2)^2]=2g^2vhW^+_\mu W^{-\mu}.
$$

Functional differentiation gives the [Feynman rules](../../../../../feynman-rules.md)

$$
\boxed{hW^+_\mu W^-_\nu:\quad 2ig^2v\,g_{\mu\nu}
=\frac{2im_W^2}{v}g_{\mu\nu},}
$$

and, in the real basis, $hW^a_\mu W^b_\nu:2ig^2v\delta^{ab}g_{\mu\nu}$ for $a,b\in\{1,2\}$. All such three-point [Feynman vertices](../../../../../interaction-vertex.md) involving $W^3$ or $B$ vanish. The factor two in the real basis comes from the two identical [gauge boson](../../../../../gauge-boson.md) fields; in the charged basis it is already explicit in $\mathcal L_{hVV}$.
