<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For commuting [real scalar fields](../../../../../real-scalar-field.md), remove the [closed fermion loop sign](../../../../../closed-fermion-loop-sign.md) and replace the [Dirac propagators](../../../../../dirac-propagator.md) by [scalar propagators](../../../../../scalar-propagator.md). There are no [spinor](../../../../../spinor.md) indices, hence no [matrix trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md). With two independent [real scalar fields](../../../../../real-scalar-field.md) $\chi_1,\chi_2$ and interaction $g\phi\chi_1\chi_2$, the same normalization gives

$$
\boxed{G_{c,\chi_1\chi_2}^{(2)}=-g^2\int d^4x\,d^4y\,
D(x_a-x)D(y_a-y)G_1(x-y)G_2(y-x).}
$$

The overall sign has reversed relative to part (i) because $(ig)^2$ is now unaccompanied by a [closed fermion loop sign](../../../../../closed-fermion-loop-sign.md). The same formula holds in the source's kernel convention, since there are again four [propagators](../../../../../propagator.md).

There is an additional convention to specify if the two symbols are intended to denote the _same_ [real scalar field](../../../../../real-scalar-field.md). For the literal interaction $g\phi\chi^2$, there are two identical connected [Wick contractions](../../../../../wick-contraction.md) between $\chi^2(x)$ and $\chi^2(y)$, so the coefficient becomes $-2g^2$. If the conventional interaction is instead $(g/2)\phi\chi^2$, each vertex is $ig$ and the loop has [symmetry factor](../../../../../feynman-diagram-symmetry-factor.md) $1/2$, giving coefficient $-g^2/2$. In both interpretations the [closed fermion loop sign](../../../../../closed-fermion-loop-sign.md) and [spinor](../../../../../spinor.md) [matrix trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) disappear; the differing numerical factor comes from the interaction normalization and field identity.
