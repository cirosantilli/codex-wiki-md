<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At a stationary symmetry-breaking [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md) of a gauge-invariant scalar potential, the angular field has zero potential curvature. Its free [kinetic term](../../../../../kinetic-term.md) is $(\partial b)^2/2$, while the displayed gauge-fixing term adds $-\xi g^2v^2b^2/2$. Hence

$$
\boxed{D_b(p)=\frac{i}{p^2-\xi M_A^2+i0},\qquad m_b^2=\xi M_A^2.}
$$

This is the would-be [Goldstone boson](../../../../../goldstone-boson.md), whose gauge-dependent pole accompanies the longitudinal pole in [i](../i.md); it is not an additional physical scalar particle of that [mass](../../../../../mass.md). For $M_A\ne0$, fixed finite $p$ and $\xi\to+\infty$,

$$
D_b(p)\sim-\frac{i}{\xi M_A^2}\longrightarrow0.
$$

The angular field becomes infinitely heavy in this gauge and is absent from the [unitary gauge](../../../../../unitary-gauge.md) propagating description. The qualification about fixed [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) matters: loop integrations probe arbitrarily large $p$, so one cannot automatically interchange the $\xi\to\infty$ limit with ultraviolet integration. If $gv=0$, there is no massive Higgs phase and the asserted heavy-field limit does not follow. A separate radial [Higgs boson](../../../../../higgs-boson.md) may remain; its [mass](../../../../../mass.md) depends on the unspecified potential and is not determined by this gauge-fixing term.
