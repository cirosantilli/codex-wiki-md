<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Put $U=e^{-i\alpha\gamma_5/2}$. The transformed [Dirac adjoint](../../../../../dirac-adjoint.md) is $\bar\psi'=\psi^\dagger U^\dagger\gamma^0=\bar\psi U$, since $\gamma^0$ anticommutes with $\gamma_5$. It is therefore incorrect to transform the two sides of a Yukawa bilinear with mutually inverse matrices.

Write $M(\phi)=\phi_1+i\gamma_5\phi_2$. The transformed [Yukawa interaction](../../../../../yukawa-interaction.md) is invariant exactly when

$$
U M(\phi')U=M(\phi).
$$

Since $M$ commutes with $\gamma_5$, this is $M(\phi')=e^{i\alpha\gamma_5}M(\phi)$. Use [i](../i.md) and equate the coefficients of $\mathbf1$ and $i\gamma_5$:

$$
\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}\phi_1'\\\phi_2'\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\alpha&-\sin\alpha\\\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\phi_1\\\phi_2\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

This is the global [SO(2)](../../../../../special-orthogonal-group-in-two-dimensions.md) rotation of a [chiral scalar-doublet Yukawa model](../../../../../chiral-scalar-doublet-yukawa-model.md). Equivalently, $\Phi=\phi_1+i\phi_2$ transforms as $\Phi'=e^{i\alpha}\Phi$, while the two chiral [spinor](../../../../../spinor.md) components transform with opposite half-angle phases. The sign of the scalar rotation follows from the negative sign in the specified [fermion](../../../../../fermion.md) transformation.
