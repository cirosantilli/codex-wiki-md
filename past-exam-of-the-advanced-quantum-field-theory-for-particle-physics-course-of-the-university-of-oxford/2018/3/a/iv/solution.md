<h1 id="3/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The scalar potential is

$$
V=-\frac{\mu^2}{2}(\phi_1^2+\phi_2^2)
+\frac\lambda4(\phi_1^2+\phi_2^2)^2
=\frac\lambda4\left(\phi_1^2+\phi_2^2-\frac{\mu^2}{\lambda}\right)^2
-\frac{\mu^4}{4\lambda}.
$$

Since $\mu^2,\lambda>0$, all minima form the [circle](../../../../../circle.md) $\phi_1^2+\phi_2^2=v^2$, with $v=\mu/\sqrt\lambda>0$. Choose the representative [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md) $\langle\phi_1\rangle=v$, $\langle\phi_2\rangle=0$, and write $\phi_1=v+h$, $\phi_2=\pi$. A nontrivial scalar rotation moves this [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md) to another minimum, so the continuous symmetry undergoes [spontaneous symmetry breaking](../../../../../spontaneous-symmetry-breaking.md). The invariant action and the selected ground state have different stabilizers; the [circle](../../../../../circle.md) of equivalent minima is not evidence that the action lost its symmetry.

The Yukawa terms become

$$
\mathcal L_Y=-gv\bar\psi\psi-gh\bar\psi\psi-ig\pi\bar\psi\gamma_5\psi.
$$

Comparison with the free [Dirac equation](../../../../../dirac-equation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{m_\psi=gv\ \text{as the signed mass coefficient},\qquad
m_{\rm physical}=|g|v.}
$$

For the usual choice $g>0$, this is simply $gv$. The absolute value matters if the real coupling is negative: the dispersion depends on $m_\psi^2$, and a field redefinition can change the [mass](../../../../../mass.md) coefficient's sign. There is no explicit symmetry-breaking [fermion](../../../../../fermion.md) [mass](../../../../../mass.md) parameter in the original theory.

The scalar symmetry is broken completely. Because the [spinor](../../../../../spinor.md) transformation uses a half angle, $\alpha=2\pi$ leaves the scalars fixed while sending $\psi\to-\psi$; this discrete [fermion](../../../../../fermion.md) parity is unbroken in the full combined transformation group. It does not remove the broken continuous generator or the associated [Goldstone boson](../../../../../goldstone-boson.md).
