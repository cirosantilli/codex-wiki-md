<h1 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The scalar kinetic and potential terms depend only on the rotation-invariant combinations $\partial_\mu\phi_1\partial^\mu\phi_1+\partial_\mu\phi_2\partial^\mu\phi_2$ and $\phi_1^2+\phi_2^2$. They are unchanged by the constant [SO(2)](../../../../../special-orthogonal-group-in-two-dimensions.md) rotation. The Yukawa term is invariant by [ii](../ii.md). Finally, anticommutation gives $U\gamma^\mu=\gamma^\mu U^{-1}$, so

$$
\bar\psi'i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi'
=\bar\psi U i\gamma^\mu U\partial_\mu\psi
=\bar\psi i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi.
$$

No derivative of $U$ appears because the parameter is global. Thus the entire classical [Lagrangian density](../../../../../lagrangian-density.md) has the combined [chiral symmetry](../../../../../chiral-symmetry.md).

An explicit [Dirac mass term](../../../../../dirac-mass-term.md) instead changes as

$$
-m\bar\psi\psi\longmapsto
-m\bar\psi e^{-i\alpha\gamma_5}\psi
=-m\cos\alpha\,\bar\psi\psi+im\sin\alpha\,\bar\psi\gamma_5\psi.
$$

It is not invariant for arbitrary $\alpha$ unless $m=0$. For the concrete choice $\alpha=\pi$, the original [mass term](../../../../../mass-term.md) reverses sign. The invariant Yukawa term can nevertheless generate a [mass](../../../../../mass.md) after a [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md) is selected, because the scalar field transforms along with the [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md); this is spontaneous rather than explicit breaking.
