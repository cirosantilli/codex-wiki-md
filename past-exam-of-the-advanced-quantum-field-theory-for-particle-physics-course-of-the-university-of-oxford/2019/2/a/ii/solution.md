<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $\epsilon$ here be a constant infinitesimal transformation parameter, unrelated to the [dimensional regularization](../../../../../dimensional-regularization.md) parameter in question 1. The matter and Maxwell terms undergo an ordinary infinitesimal [gauge transformation](../../../../../gauge-transformation.md) with $\alpha=\epsilon\phi$, and their variations cancel as before. The [gauge fixing](../../../../../gauge-fixing.md) contribution is

$$
\delta\mathcal L_{\mathrm{gf}}=-\frac\epsilon\xi B\partial^2\phi.
$$

The extra real [scalar field](../../../../../scalar-field.md) kinetic term varies by

$$
\delta\left(\frac12\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi\right)=\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\delta\phi=-\frac\epsilon\xi(\partial_\mu\phi)(\partial^\mu B).
$$

Adding the two gives a total derivative:

$$
\boxed{\delta\mathcal L=-\frac\epsilon\xi\partial_\mu(B\partial^\mu\phi),\qquad\delta S=0.}
$$

The last equality uses vanishing boundary contributions, for example sufficiently fast decay of the fields. No field equations were used. This [gauge-scalar global symmetry of covariantly fixed QED](../../../../../gauge-scalar-global-symmetry-of-covariantly-fixed-qed.md) leaves the action invariant even though the density need not be invariant pointwise; the scalar's variation supplies exactly the missing boundary-term cancellation.
