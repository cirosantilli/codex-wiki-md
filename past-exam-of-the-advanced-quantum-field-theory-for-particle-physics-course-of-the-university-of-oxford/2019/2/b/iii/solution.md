<h1 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For [Møller scattering](../../../../../moller-scattering.md), both the $t$-channel and $u$-channel [photon](../../../../../photon.md) exchange [tree-level Feynman diagrams](../../../../../tree-level-feynman-diagram.md) contribute, as shown in the two remaining lower panels. Exchanging the identical final [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) reverses the sign of the [scattering amplitude](../../../../../scattering-amplitude.md), so the [fermionic sign](../../../../../fermionic-sign.md) fixes

$$
\mathcal M=e^2\left(\frac{J_t}{t}-\frac{J_u}{u}\right),\qquad
J_t=[\bar u_3\gamma^\mu u_1][\bar u_4\gamma_\mu u_2],\quad
J_u=[\bar u_4\gamma^\mu u_1][\bar u_3\gamma_\mu u_2].
$$

The two direct squares follow from the preceding distinct-lepton calculation:

$$
\frac14\sum|J_t|^2=2(s^2+u^2),\qquad
\frac14\sum|J_u|^2=2(s^2+t^2).
$$

The interference cannot be discarded because the final [particles](../../../../../particle-physics.md) are identical. Summing their [spins](../../../../../spin.md) joins all four external [Dirac spinors](../../../../../dirac-spinor.md) into a single [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum J_tJ_u^*&=\operatorname{tr}(\not p_3\gamma^\mu\not p_1\gamma^\nu\not p_4\gamma_\mu\not p_2\gamma_\nu)\\
&=-32(p_3\cdot p_4)(p_1\cdot p_2)=-8s^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The result from part (i) was used with relabelled contracted indices. Its negative sign combines with the explicit subtraction of the two exchange [scattering amplitudes](../../../../../scattering-amplitude.md), so the interference contribution to the averaged square is positive:

$$
-\frac{2e^4}{4tu}\operatorname{Re}\sum J_tJ_u^*=\frac{4e^4s^2}{tu}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\langle|\mathcal M|^2\rangle=2e^4\left[\frac{s^2+u^2}{t^2}+\frac{s^2+t^2}{u^2}+\frac{2s^2}{tu}\right].}
$$

This is symmetric under $t\leftrightarrow u$, as required by the identical final [electrons](../../../../../electron.md). There is no additional $1/2!$ in the squared [scattering amplitude](../../../../../scattering-amplitude.md). Such a factor is used when integrating the [cross section](../../../../../cross-section-physics.md) over a final-state region that counts both orderings of the identical pair; alternatively one integrates over one representative half of that region.
