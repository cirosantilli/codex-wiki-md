<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [point mutation](../../../../point-mutation.md) changes a nucleotide at one genomic position. Replication errors and chemical damage can generate [substitutions](../../../../nucleotide-substitution.md); repair and other proofreading processes influence the retained [mutation rate](../../../../mutation-rate.md). A coding change may be synonymous or alter an [amino acid](../../../../amino-acid.md), while a noncoding change can affect regulation. Its evolutionary effect depends on context, so [mutation](../../../../mutation.md), functional consequence and eventual [fixation](../../../../fixation-probability.md) are distinct events.

[Mutation](../../../../mutation.md) supplies new variants, while [genetic drift](../../../../genetic-drift.md), [selection](../../../../natural-selection.md) and population history determine their persistence. Under a simple neutral model, increasing population size raises the number of new variants but lowers each one's [fixation](../../../../fixation-probability.md) [probability](../../../../probability.md); the neutral [substitution](../../../../nucleotide-substitution.md) rate is approximately the [mutation rate](../../../../mutation-rate.md). Selective constraint, generation time and lineage-specific processes can therefore complicate a universal [molecular clock](../../../../molecular-clock.md). Molecular evolution is not directed towards anticipated needs.

To infer a [phylogeny](../../../../phylogenetic-tree.md), compare [homologous](../../../../biological-homology.md) positions in appropriately chosen [orthologous](../../../../orthology.md) sequences and estimate the tree and branch lengths under a [substitution](../../../../nucleotide-substitution.md) model. Shared derived characters support [common ancestry](../../../../common-descent.md), but reversals and parallel [substitutions](../../../../nucleotide-substitution.md) mean raw mismatches underestimate evolutionary distance. In a simple equal-rates four-state illustration, a mismatch fraction $p$ gives corrected pairwise distance $d=-(3/4)\log(1-4p/3)$. The formula's strong divergence as $p\to3/4$ reflects saturation and loss of reliable historical information, not arbitrarily precise evidence for an ancient split.

The [molecular-clock calibration and rate-time identifiability](../../../../molecular-clock-calibration-and-rate-time-identifiability.md) issue is fundamental: branch lengths first measure expected [substitutions](../../../../nucleotide-substitution.md) per site. A clock relates them to time by $d_i=r_it_i$; for two equal-rate lineages separated for time $t$, pairwise distance is $d=2rt$, so $t=d/(2r)$. Absolute times require external information, such as independently dated fossils or geological constraints, or informative sampling dates when the data contain a temporal signal. Sequence differences alone generally identify only rate-times-time, not both quantities separately.

Time-calibrated inference should propagate uncertainty in topology, [substitution](../../../../nucleotide-substitution.md) rates, ancestral-state reconstruction and calibration bounds. Relaxed clocks permit different lineage rates. Fossil minima are not automatically exact split dates; orthology mistakes, [incomplete lineage sorting](../../../../incomplete-lineage-sorting.md), [recombination](../../../../genetic-recombination.md), [horizontal transfer](../../../../horizontal-gene-transfer.md) and [selection](../../../../natural-selection.md) can cause discordance between one [gene tree](../../../../phylogenetic-tree.md) and [species](../../../../species.md) history. Multiple independent loci and well-justified calibrations make a stronger historical argument than a single uncalibrated sequence comparison. No unique numerical tree or date can be constructed here without actual sequence observations and calibration data.
