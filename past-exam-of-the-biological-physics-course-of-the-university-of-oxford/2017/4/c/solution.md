<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

An increased late inward [sodium](../../../../sodium.md) current can delay repolarization if fast [inactivation](../../../../ion-channel-inactivation.md) is incomplete or slowed, if channels reopen excessively, or if the [voltage](../../../../voltage.md) range of persistent/window current is enlarged. A larger brief peak current alone is not equivalent to a sustained depolarizing current. Conversely, reduced outward [potassium](../../../../potassium.md) current can prolong the [action potential](../../../../action-potential.md) through fewer functional surface channels, reduced [conductance](../../../../electrical-conductance.md), slower or less favourable activation, or altered deactivation/availability. Either change shifts the balance towards inward current during repolarization.

These are distinct biophysical routes to [Long QT syndrome](../../../../long-qt-syndrome.md), not claims that every subtype has the same channel defect.[GeneReviews, Long QT Syndrome Overview, updated 2024: sodium gain-of-function and potassium loss-of-function mechanisms](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1129/) The experimental association of [sodium-channel](../../../../voltage-gated-sodium-channel.md) abnormalities with inherited disease supports the inward-current mechanism.[1995 human genetic study linking cardiac sodium-channel defects to inherited long-QT syndrome](../../../../cardiac-sodium-channel-mutations-in-patients-with-long-qt-syndrome-an-inherited-cardiac-arrhythmia-by-qing-wang-and-others.md)
