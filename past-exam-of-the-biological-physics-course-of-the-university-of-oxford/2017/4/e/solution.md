<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

A [two-state](../../../../two-state-ion-channel-gating.md) closed–open scheme at fixed [voltage](../../../../voltage.md) relaxes monotonically and cannot by itself produce a sodium-current transient during a maintained step with constant driving [force](../../../../force-split.md). A minimal [sodium](../../../../sodium.md) scheme therefore includes an unavailable state: $C\rightleftarrows O\rightleftarrows I$, with voltage-dependent activation, [inactivation](../../../../ion-channel-inactivation.md) and recovery; closed-state recovery pathways may be added when needed. Opening is initially rapid, occupancy then accumulates in $I$, and recovery on repolarization restores an activatable population.

For the illustrated [potassium](../../../../potassium.md) current, a closed–open model gives sustained opening, but several activation steps can account for its initial delay. A conventional illustrative coarse-grained description is

$$
I_{\rm Na}=\overline g_{\rm Na}m^3h(V-E_{\rm Na}),\qquad
I_{\rm K}=\overline g_{\rm K}n^4(V-E_{\rm K}),
$$

with each gate following $\dot q=\alpha_q(V)(1-q)-\beta_q(V)q$. Fast $m$ activation, subsequent decrease of $h$, and slower increase of $n$ produce the two qualitative records. The powers are phenomenological gate assumptions, not values uniquely inferred from these traces or an exact structural count for every cardiac channel.

A 1-Hz cardiac rhythm has about a second between successive activations, whereas a 100-Hz neuronal rhythm has only $10\,\mathrm{ms}$; the available resting interval is shorter still after subtracting the [action potential](../../../../action-potential.md). Recovery or deactivation with a timescale comparable to that interval can then leave residual [inactivation](../../../../ion-channel-inactivation.md) or [potassium](../../../../potassium.md) activation, giving use-dependent changes and [refractoriness](../../../../neuronal-refractory-period.md). Millisecond or faster relaxation usually completes much more readily during a long diastolic interval. The particular [two-state](../../../../two-state-ion-channel-gating.md) A and B in [d](../d.md) would both nearly equilibrate over a full $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ at their stipulated constant rates; their values alone do not demonstrate high-frequency failure. Real comparison requires voltage-dependent recovery rates and the different cardiac and neuronal waveforms. A frozen-rate model cannot quantitatively predict either firing pattern from frequency alone.
