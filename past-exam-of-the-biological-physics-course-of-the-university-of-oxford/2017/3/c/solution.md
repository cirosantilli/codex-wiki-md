<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

After a domain unfolds, its backbone joins the same continuous chain as previously unfolded material. The new branch differs mainly by an added [contour length](../../../../polymer-contour-length.md), while the local backbone bending statistics remain similar. It is therefore consistent to fit successive branches with different $L_j$ and one common $L_p\simeq0.8\,\mathrm{nm}$. [Tension](../../../../tension.md) is transmitted through serial portions of the same chain; one should not add their tensile [forces](../../../../force-split.md) as though they were independent parallel springs.

The [persistence length](../../../../persistence-length.md) is short compared with the contour increment per repeat, so each released domain contributes many flexible bending segments. Stretching suppresses those thermal modes and raises [free energy](../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md) through loss of [entropy](../../../../entropy-split.md), which is precisely the worm-like-chain mechanism. The common $L_p$ is a property of the unfolded backbone under the measurement conditions, not the [folded domain](../../../../protein-domain.md)'s axial length or the number of domains already released. With $T=300\,\mathrm K$ only as an illustrative value, $k_BT L_p\simeq3.3\,\mathrm{pN\,nm^2}$.

A common persistence-length fit is evidence for approximately repeat-independent chain flexibility over the observed branches; it is not proof that a continuum inextensible model describes every molecular scale. Residual folded structure, heterogeneous linkers, force-induced backbone stretching, and very short-chain effects can cause deviations. The formal [force](../../../../force-split.md) divergence at $x=L$ signals the model's inextensibility assumption, not a physical infinite tensile [force](../../../../force-split.md).
