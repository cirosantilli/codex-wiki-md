<h1 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take $V=V_{\rm inside}-V_{\rm outside}$. Equality of [electrochemical potentials](../../../../../electrochemical-potential.md) for a monovalent cation gives the [Nernst equilibrium potential](../../../../../nernst-equilibrium-potential.md)

$$
E=\frac{RT}{F}\ln\frac{c_{\rm outside}}{c_{\rm inside}}.
$$

At $T=293.15\,\mathrm K$, $RT/F=25.26\,\mathrm{mV}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{E_{\rm Na}=25.26\ln(120/15)=+52.5\,\mathrm{mV},\qquad
E_{\rm K}=25.26\ln(5/100)=-75.7\,\mathrm{mV}.}
$$

The signs refer to the stated electrode convention, not to the direction of current at every voltage. Activities are approximated by [concentrations](../../../../../concentration.md) and the bulk ionic [concentrations](../../../../../concentration.md) are treated as fixed during a single spike.
