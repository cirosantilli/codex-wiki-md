<h1 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Add an unavailable nonconducting inactivated state to the two-state gating scheme, for example

$$
C\mathrel{\mathop{\rightleftarrows}^{k_o(V)}_{k_c(V)}}O
\mathrel{\mathop{\rightleftarrows}^{k_i(V)}_{k_r(V)}}I.
$$

The probabilities obey

$$
\dot p_O=k_op_C-(k_c+k_i)p_O+k_rp_I,\qquad
\dot p_I=k_ip_O-k_rp_I,\qquad p_C+p_O+p_I=1.
$$

[Depolarization](../../../../../depolarization.md) increases activation, initially raising $p_O$; subsequent transfer to $I$ reduces current while voltage is held constant. Slow recovery at the depolarized voltage permits most channels to become unavailable. [Repolarization](../../../../../repolarization.md) promotes recovery and deactivation. More closed states and closed-state [inactivation](../../../../../ion-channel-inactivation.md) may be needed for quantitative data, but this minimal extension already explains the rise followed by decay that fixed-rate $C\leftrightarrow O$ gating cannot produce from a closed population.
