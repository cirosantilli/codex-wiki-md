<h1 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [sodium](../../../../../sodium.md)-channel gain-of-function mechanism in [Long QT syndrome](../../../../../long-qt-syndrome.md) can reduce the [inactivation](../../../../../ion-channel-inactivation.md) rate $k_i$ or increase recovery/reopening from inactivated states, leaving a small persistent open population. The resulting late inward [sodium](../../../../../sodium.md) current opposes repolarizing outward currents while $V<E_{\rm Na}$, prolonging the cardiac [action potential](../../../../../action-potential.md). Slower entry into $I$ would also extend the typical conducting episode relative to its normal duration. The altered effect is prolonged [sodium](../../../../../sodium.md) entry, not reversal of [sodium](../../../../../sodium.md)'s charge or a pump-driven uphill flux through the channel.

The precise rate change depends on the [mutation](../../../../../mutation.md): the clinical label alone does not uniquely specify one transition constant. Long QT syndromes also include other channel mechanisms; the question here concerns its stated [sodium](../../../../../sodium.md)-channel variant.[Sodium-channel defects associated with the inherited repolarization phenotype](../../../../../cardiac-sodium-channel-mutations-in-patients-with-long-qt-syndrome-an-inherited-cardiac-arrhythmia-by-qing-wang-and-others.md)
