↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The term $V/(D_3\lambda)$ is the bulk-[diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) search cost: increasing the effective length of [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) searched after each encounter enlarges the useful capture region and reduces this contribution. The term $L\lambda/D_1$ is the accumulated sliding cost. Approximately $L/\lambda$ [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) landing-and-sliding episodes, each taking order $\lambda^2/D_1$, give $L\lambda/D_1$. These are scaling estimates; geometric constants and correlated revisits are omitted by the supplied model.

Thus the [facilitated target search](../../../../../facilitated-target-search.md) is slow as $\lambda\to0$, because too little sequence is examined per encounter, and slow at very large $\lambda$, because sliding and repeated local exploration take too long. A finite intermediate minimum balances 1D scanning against 3D relocation. The right panel of the figure in [solution](../../a/solution.md) shows the two contributions and their sum. The model requires a useful range of sliding lengths below the full [chromosome](../../../../../chromosome.md) length, finite positive [diffusion coefficients](../../../../../diffusion-coefficient.md) and effective relocation between episodes; it is not a claim that arbitrarily large $\lambda$ is physically available.
