<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The given sphere has radius $a=5\,\mathrm{nm}$. The [Stokes–Einstein relation](../../../../../stokes-einstein-relation.md) at an illustrative $T=300\,\mathrm K$ gives

$$
D_A=\frac{k_BT}{6\pi\eta a}=4.39\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}=4.39\,\mu\mathrm m^2\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}.
$$

For $N=30$ well-mixed independent searchers in cell volume $V$, the target-arrival rate is $Nk_a/V$. The corresponding mean first arrival time is

$$
\boxed{\tau_3\simeq\frac{V}{30\,4\pi D_Ar_0}
=0.121\,\mathrm s\left(\frac{V}{1\,\mu\mathrm m^3}\right)\left(\frac{5\,\mathrm{nm}}{r_0}\right)\left(\frac{300\,\mathrm K}{T}\right).}
$$

The source does not fix $V$, the reaction radius or [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). For an order-of-magnitude estimate, a bacterial volume around $1\,\mu\mathrm m^3$ and reaction radius $5$–$10\,\mathrm{nm}$ give about $0.06$–$0.12\,\mathrm s$. A 20-base-pair B-form site is about $6.8\,\mathrm{nm}$ long, supporting a nanometre-scale capture radius but not uniquely defining its spherical value. The estimate is the ideal [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md)-limited time, not a measured intracellular search time: nonspecific binding, orientational matching, crowding and limited accessibility are excluded.
