<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Treat the target as a stationary perfectly absorbing sphere of reaction radius $r_0$, surrounded by an effectively uniform reservoir of [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) [number density](../../../../../number-density.md) $C_A$. At [steady state](../../../../../steady-state.md) the radial [diffusion equation](../../../../../diffusion-equation-split.md) gives

$$
\nabla^2c=0,\quad c(r_0)=0,\quad c(\infty)=C_A,\qquad c(r)=C_A\left(1-\frac{r_0}r\right).
$$

The inward capture rate per target is the [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md) of the [Fick's first law](../../../../../fick-s-first-law.md):

$$
J=4\pi r_0^2D_A\left.\frac{dc}{dr}\right|_{r_0}=4\pi D_Ar_0C_A.
$$

For dilute independent targets of [number density](../../../../../number-density.md) $C_B$, the volume association rate is $JC_B$, so

$$
\boxed{k_a=4\pi D_Ar_0.}
$$

This is the [Smoluchowski association rate](../../../../../smoluchowski-association-rate.md), in volume per time because the [concentrations](../../../../../concentration.md) are number densities. If the target also diffuses, use the relative [diffusion coefficient](../../../../../diffusion-coefficient.md) $D_A+D_B$. Orientation constraints, incomplete absorption and [chromosome](../../../../../chromosome.md) geometry can reduce the actual association rate relative to this ideal collision limit.
