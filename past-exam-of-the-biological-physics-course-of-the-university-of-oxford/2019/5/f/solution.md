<h1 id="5/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

After the load is removed, a reversibly stretched [DNA](../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) [molecule](../../../../molecule-split.md) relaxes towards a thermally fluctuating coil, releasing stored [free energy](../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) as [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) through the surrounding fluid. There are many more disordered chain conformations than strongly aligned ones, so [entropy](../../../../entropy-split.md) favours retraction; viscous drag sets how rapidly it occurs. At long times an unconstrained [ideal chain](../../../../ideal-chain.md) has zero mean end-to-end [vector](../../../../vector.md) but a nonzero typical size, $R_{\rm rms}=b\sqrt N$; it does not contract to a point.

At finite [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) the chain continues fluctuating, and an anchored or otherwise constrained [molecule](../../../../molecule-split.md) need not regain an isotropic ensemble. If stretching broke attachments, denatured the [DNA](../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) or caused irreversible structural changes, recovery can be incomplete or hysteretic. Those outcomes cannot be decided from the ideal FJC alone.
