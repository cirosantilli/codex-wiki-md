<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At [equilibrium](../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md), [detailed balance](../../../../detailed-balance.md) requires zero net [probability](../../../../probability.md) current along each reversible link: $k_fp_A^{\rm eq}=k_bp_B^{\rm eq}$. The [equilibrium](../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) probabilities satisfy $p_i^{\rm eq}\propto e^{-G_i/(k_BT)}$, where each state [free energy](../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) includes its internal degeneracy. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{k_f}{k_b}=\frac{p_B^{\rm eq}}{p_A^{\rm eq}}
=e^{-(G_B-G_A)/(k_BT)}.}
$$

In the source sketch B is lower, and the labelled vertical drop is naturally $\Delta G_{AB}=G_A-G_B>0$. With that drop convention the ratio is $\boxed{k_f/k_b=e^{\Delta G_{AB}/(k_BT)}}$. If instead $\Delta G_{AB}$ means the conventional reaction change $G_B-G_A$, the exponent has the minus sign. For example a drop of $2k_BT$ gives a forward/backward ratio $e^2$, not $e^{-2}$.
