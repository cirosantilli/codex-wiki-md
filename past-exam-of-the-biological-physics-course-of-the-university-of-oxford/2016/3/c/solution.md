<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

At $T=310.15$ K, $RT/F=26.72666$ mV. Always retain the valence sign. For the two monovalent positive [ions](../../../../ion.md),

$$
\boxed{E_K=26.72666\ln(4/155)=-97.74\ \mathrm{mV},\qquad
E_{\rm Na}=26.72666\ln(145/12)=+66.60\ \mathrm{mV}.}
$$

For [calcium](../../../../calcium.md), $z=2$ and the specified [voltage](../../../../voltage.md) is positive:

$$
\boxed{c_{{\rm Ca},\rm in}=1.5\exp[-2(130)/26.72666]\ \mathrm{mM}
=8.938\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{mM}\simeq89.4\ \mathrm{nM}.}
$$

For chloride, $z=-1$ reverses the sign in the [Nernst equation](../../../../nernst-equation.md):

$$
-90=-26.72666\ln(c_{{\rm Cl},\rm out}/4),\qquad
\boxed{c_{{\rm Cl},\rm out}=116.0\ \mathrm{mM}.}
$$

These values use [thermodynamic activities](../../../../thermodynamic-activity.md) approximated by the given [concentrations](../../../../concentration.md). [Calcium](../../../../calcium.md) is the **free** [concentration](../../../../concentration.md) relevant to its [electrochemical potential](../../../../electrochemical-potential.md), rather than total bound plus free intracellular [calcium](../../../../calcium.md). Ratios use matching [concentration](../../../../concentration.md) units, so a ratio of millimolar values is already dimensionless. Failure to include the factor of two for [calcium](../../../../calcium.md) or the negative charge of chloride would change the answer substantially.
