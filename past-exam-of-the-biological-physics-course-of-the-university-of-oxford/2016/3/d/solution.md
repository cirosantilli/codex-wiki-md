<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At a common membrane [voltage](../../../../voltage.md), an [ion](../../../../ion.md) is in electrochemical equilibrium only if its own [Nernst potential](../../../../nernst-equilibrium-potential.md) equals that [voltage](../../../../voltage.md). The four independently calculated equilibrium voltages are

$$
\boxed{E_K=-97.74\ \mathrm{mV},\quad E_{\rm Na}=+66.60\ \mathrm{mV},\quad
E_{\rm Ca}=+130\ \mathrm{mV},\quad E_{\rm Cl}=-90\ \mathrm{mV}.}
$$

Thus at $V_m=-90$ mV only chloride is at its listed equilibrium [voltage](../../../../voltage.md). [Potassium](../../../../potassium.md) has an outward positive-current driving [voltage](../../../../voltage.md) $V_m-E_K=+7.74$ mV. [Sodium](../../../../sodium.md) and [calcium](../../../../calcium.md) have negative driving voltages, $-156.60$ and $-220$ mV respectively, favoring inward positive-ion movement when their channels are open. Chloride has zero net electrochemical drive under these ideal conditions.

A resting potential is not a claim that every permeant [chemical species](../../../../chemical-species.md) is in [thermodynamic equilibrium](../../../../thermodynamic-equilibrium.md). A membrane with several [electrical conductances](../../../../electrical-conductance.md) can have zero **total** [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) even though individual ionic [electric currents](../../../../electric-current.md) flow and cancel. Pumps maintain the [concentration](../../../../concentration.md) differences by consuming [Gibbs free energy](../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md). Conversely, a closed channel can prevent substantial [ion](../../../../ion.md) flux even when the [ion](../../../../ion.md)'s [electrochemical potential](../../../../electrochemical-potential.md) difference is nonzero: absence of flux due to impermeability is not the same as electrochemical equilibrium. The listed [concentrations](../../../../concentration.md) could not all be passive equilibrium distributions at one [voltage](../../../../voltage.md) because their [Nernst potentials](../../../../nernst-equilibrium-potential.md) differ.
