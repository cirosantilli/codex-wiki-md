<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use the given spherical-shell [force balance](../../../../force-balance.md) directly; $R_{\rm out}=30$ nm is the **loaded** radius. The radial enlargement is

$$
\Delta R=R_{\rm out}-R_0=\frac{2\pi R_{\rm out}^2p}{k}.
$$

Since $1\ \mathrm{pN\,nm^{-2}}=10^6$ Pa, the [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md) is $p=40\times101325\ \mathrm{Pa}=4.053\ \mathrm{pN\,nm^{-2}}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\Delta R=\frac{2\pi(30\ \mathrm{nm})^2(4.053\ \mathrm{pN\,nm^{-2}})}
{5\times10^5\ \mathrm{pN\,nm^{-1}}}=0.04584\ \mathrm{nm}.}
$$

The diameter increases by $2\Delta R=0.09168$ nm, and $R_0=29.95416$ nm. The relative radial or diametral enlargement is about $1.53\times10^{-3}$, only $0.153\%$. Under this stipulated shell model the container is therefore very stiff despite the substantial internal [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md).

The factor of two follows from the particular [elastic energy](../../../../elastic-energy.md) convention implicit in the question: differentiating $k(R-R_0)^2$ gives $2k(R-R_0)$. Replacing it by the derivative of $k(R-R_0)^2/2$ would change the answer incorrectly. The calculation treats the wall by an effective spherical radius; local deformation or discrete facets of an icosahedral shell are not resolved by this one-coordinate model.
