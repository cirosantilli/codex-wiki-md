<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

If the ideal-chain segment count and segment length remain fixed, the [entropic spring](../../../../entropic-spring.md) constant is proportional to absolute [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{k(60^\circ\mathrm C)=k(25^\circ\mathrm C)\frac{333.15}{298.15}
\simeq2.464\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}
=2.464\,\mathrm{pN\,nm^{-1}}.}
$$

This is an increase by about $11.7\%$. The factor $k_BT$ multiplies the [free energy](../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) penalty for the loss of chain configurations; a larger [force](../../../../force-split.md) is required to impose the same normalized weak extension at higher [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md).

Not degrading does not guarantee that a real [protein](../../../../protein-split.md)'s folded state, effective [spring constant](../../../../spring-constant.md) or [solvent](../../../../solvent.md) interactions remain unchanged. Thermal unfolding and changes in [persistence length](../../../../persistence-length.md) can occur without covalent degradation. Those changes modify the experimental curve and may invalidate a fixed-$N,b$ comparison. The boxed value is the explicit ideal-[FJC](../../../../ideal-chain.md) prediction under fixed segment geometry, not an unconditional thermal-[stability](../../../../stability-of-a-numerical-method.md) prediction for the [protein](../../../../protein-split.md).
