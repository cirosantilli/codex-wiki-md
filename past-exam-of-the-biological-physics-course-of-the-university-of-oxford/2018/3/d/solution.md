<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The equivalent circuit has [membrane capacitance](../../../../membrane-capacitance.md) in parallel with [electrical conductance](../../../../electrical-conductance.md)–battery branches: voltage-dependent [sodium](../../../../sodium.md) and [potassium](../../../../potassium.md) conductances in series with their [reversal potentials](../../../../reversal-potential.md), a [chloride](../../../../chloride.md) [electrical conductance](../../../../electrical-conductance.md) in series with $E_{Cl}$, and other leak conductances. For externally supplied [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md),

$$
C_m\dot V=I_{\rm ext}-g_{Na}(V,t)(V-E_{Na})-g_K(V,t)(V-E_K)
-g_{Cl}(V-E_{Cl})-g_L(V-E_L).
$$

The model figure in [solution](../../2/c/solution.md) includes this circuit and an illustrative passive response. Although [chloride](../../../../chloride.md) [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) is small when $V\simeq E_{Cl}$, a perturbation produces the restoring [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) $\delta I_{Cl}=g_{Cl}\delta V$. A large [chloride](../../../../chloride.md) [electrical conductance](../../../../electrical-conductance.md) therefore shunts perturbations, lowers input resistance and shortens the passive [time constant](../../../../time-constant.md), $R_{\rm in}\simeq1/\sum g_j$ and $\tau_m\simeq C_m/\sum g_j$.

Reducing that [electrical conductance](../../../../electrical-conductance.md) increases the voltage response to a given [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) or extracellular-[potassium](../../../../potassium.md) disturbance and weakens stabilization near rest, making repeated excitation easier. There need not be an instantaneous shift of resting voltage if $E_{Cl}$ equals the old rest exactly. In skeletal muscle, loss of ClC-1-mediated stabilization is associated with myotonic hyperexcitability.[Channel defects associated with inherited muscle hyperexcitability](../../../../the-skeletal-muscle-chloride-channel-in-dominant-and-recessive-human-myotonia-by-manuela-c-koch-and-others.md)

Later cessation of contraction can be explained by sustained [depolarization](../../../../depolarization.md) causing [voltage-gated sodium channel](../../../../voltage-gated-sodium-channel.md) [ion-channel inactivation](../../../../ion-channel-inactivation.md) and failure to recover: fewer available channels support progressively smaller or less frequent spikes until [depolarization](../../../../depolarization.md) block occurs. Accumulated extracellular [potassium](../../../../potassium.md) in restricted spaces and incomplete recovery between spikes can promote that [depolarization](../../../../depolarization.md). This requires the nonlinear voltage-gating dynamics; a passive [chloride](../../../../chloride.md) resistor and capacitor alone cannot generate or terminate an action-potential train. The stated [membrane permeability](../../../../membrane-permeability.md) change does not uniquely determine the time to block or prove one particular ionic accumulation history.
