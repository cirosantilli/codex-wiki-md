<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Including both end faces of the perfect cylinder, with $r=1\,\mu\mathrm m$ and $\ell=100\,\mu\mathrm m$,

$$
\mathcal A=2\pi r\ell+2\pi r^2=6.346\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m^2},\qquad
\mathcal V=\pi r^2\ell=3.142\times10^{-16}\,\mathrm{m^3}
=3.142\times10^{-13}\,\mathrm L.
$$

The [membrane capacitance](../../../../membrane-capacitance.md) is $C=0.01\mathcal A=6.346\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm F$. The required net inward [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md) is $Q=C\Delta V=5.394\times10^{-13}\,\mathrm C$, or $Q/e\simeq3.37\times10^6$ monovalent ions. If [sodium](../../../../sodium.md) supplies that net [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md), [Faraday constant](../../../../faraday-constant.md) $F=N_Ae$ gives

$$
\boxed{\Delta[\mathrm{Na}^+]=\frac{C\Delta V}{F\mathcal V}
=1.780\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}
=0.0178\,\mathrm{mM}=17.8\,\mu\mathrm M.}
$$

The final [concentration](../../../../concentration.md) is about $15.0178\,\mathrm{mM}$, only $0.119\%$ above the starting value. Neglecting the end faces changes the estimate by $1\%$, giving about $17.6\,\mu\mathrm M$. Over $2\,\mathrm{ms}$ the capacitive [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md) corresponds to mean net [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) $Q/\Delta t\simeq0.270\,\mathrm{nA}$; the duration does not otherwise enter the total [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md).

This is a net capacitive estimate, not a measurement of total [sodium](../../../../sodium.md) influx. Simultaneous outward [potassium](../../../../potassium.md) [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) makes actual [sodium](../../../../sodium.md) entry larger, and most bulk ions remain near their original [concentrations](../../../../concentration.md) during one spike. Long-term pumps restore gradients after repeated firing; they are not required to replace the entire intracellular [sodium](../../../../sodium.md) pool during the two-millisecond voltage rise.
