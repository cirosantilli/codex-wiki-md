<h1 id="6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[Single-molecule fluorescence microscopy](../../../../single-molecule-fluorescence-microscopy.md) can localize isolated [fluorescent proteins](../../../../fluorescent-protein.md) and link successive positions into trajectories. For ideal free [Brownian motion](../../../../brownian-motion-split.md) projected into two dimensions, [mean squared displacement](../../../../mean-squared-displacement.md) satisfies $\langle|\mathbf r(t+\Delta t)-\mathbf r(t)|^2\rangle=4D\Delta t$. A fit of the short-lag slope therefore estimates the [diffusion coefficient](../../../../diffusion-coefficient.md).

If each measured coordinate has independent localization error of [variance](../../../../variance-split.md) $s^2$, two measured positions give a constant offset $4s^2$ in the two-dimensional [mean squared displacement](../../../../mean-squared-displacement.md). Finite exposure averages the motion and biases that relation too. Estimates should therefore account for localization error, motion blur, missed links, and confinement. Bound [RNA polymerase](../../../../rna-polymerase.md) and freely moving [RNA polymerase](../../../../rna-polymerase.md) can form distinct displacement populations, so pooling them into one straight line can hide binding. Short-lag trajectories and state-dependent fits distinguish rapid motion from slow or chromosomally bound states; a long-lag plateau can reflect confinement rather than a vanishing microscopic [diffusion coefficient](../../../../diffusion-coefficient.md).
