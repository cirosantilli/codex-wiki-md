<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[ATP synthase](../../../../atp-synthase.md) couples a membrane-embedded rotary motor to a soluble catalytic head. Its membrane sector contains a rotating ring of c subunits and a stationary a subunit providing offset proton-access pathways. Its catalytic sector contains an $\alpha_3\beta_3$ head with catalytic sites primarily on the $\beta$ subunits, a central rotating shaft, and a peripheral stator that prevents the head simply co-rotating.

A [proton-motive force](../../../../proton-motive-force.md), combining a [membrane potential](../../../../membrane-potential-split.md) and a proton [chemical potential](../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) difference, drives directional proton passage and ring rotation. Rotation of the central shaft changes the conformations and binding affinities of successive catalytic sites, allowing [ADP](../../../../adenosine-diphosphate.md) and inorganic phosphate to bind, form [ATP](../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md), and release it in a coordinated cycle. The [ATP synthase rotary mechanism](../../../../atp-synthase-rotary-mechanism.md) therefore couples ion transport, torque, and chemical work rather than requiring a proton to react directly at every catalytic site.

The motor is reversible: sufficiently favorable [ATP](../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) hydrolysis can drive proton pumping against its [electrochemical potential](../../../../electrochemical-potential.md) gradient. In [mitochondria](../../../../mitochondria.md), the respiratory chain establishes the gradient used for oxidative [ATP](../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) production; analogous coupling operates in bacterial membranes and in chloroplast photophosphorylation. The number of protons per revolution depends on the c-ring stoichiometry, whereas a full catalytic rotation normally produces three [ATP](../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) molecules. It is therefore inappropriate to assign one universal integer proton-to-ATP ratio to all organisms. Transport and leakage can increase the whole-cell energetic cost beyond the motor's ideal ratio.
