<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The lipid [cell membrane](../../../../cell-membrane-split.md) separates ionic solutions and has selective [ion channels](../../../../ion-channel.md), ion pumps, and a finite [capacitance](../../../../capacitance.md). Ionic gradients and unequal permeability allow a small charge separation to generate a [membrane potential](../../../../membrane-potential-split.md). The bulk compartments can remain almost electrically neutral even when the narrow interfacial layers carry opposite [electric charges](../../../../electric-charge-split.md).

For one permeant ionic species with valence $z$, its [electrochemical potential](../../../../electrochemical-potential.md) is $\widetilde\mu=\mu^0+k_BT\log a+ze\phi$, where $a$ is its activity. With no applied drive and no net ionic current, electrochemical equilibrium across a permeable membrane gives

$$
k_BT\log a_{\rm in}+ze\phi_{\rm in}
=k_BT\log a_{\rm out}+ze\phi_{\rm out}.
$$

Hence, with the inside-minus-outside convention,

$$
\boxed{\Psi_m=\phi_{\rm in}-\phi_{\rm out}=\frac{k_BT}{ze}\log\frac{a_{\rm out}}{a_{\rm in}}.}
$$

For dilute ideal solutions the activities may be replaced by [concentrations](../../../../concentration.md). At $25^\circ\mathrm C$ this is $(59.16\,\mathrm{mV}/z)\log_{10}(c_{\rm out}/c_{\rm in})$. The resulting [Nernst equilibrium potential](../../../../nernst-equilibrium-potential.md) balances diffusive and electrical driving, not necessarily equal [concentrations](../../../../concentration.md). Physically there must be countercharge, such as impermeant counterions; the single-species idealization concerns the permeant current. With multiple permeant species, zero total current need not mean zero current of each species, and the resting [membrane potential](../../../../membrane-potential-split.md) is not generally any single species' [Nernst equilibrium potential](../../../../nernst-equilibrium-potential.md).
