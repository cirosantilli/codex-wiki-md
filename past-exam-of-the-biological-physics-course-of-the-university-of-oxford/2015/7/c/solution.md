<h1 id="7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Including both flat ends of the cylindrical cell, its membrane [surface area](../../../../surface-area.md) is

$$
A=2\pi r\ell+2\pi r^2
=2\pi(10^{-6})(100\times10^{-6})+2\pi(10^{-6})^2
=6.34602\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m^2}.
$$

The [capacitance](../../../../capacitance.md) is $C=(0.01\,\mathrm{F\,m^{-2}})A=6.34602\,\mathrm{pF}$. The [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md) needed for the specified [depolarization](../../../../depolarization.md) is therefore

$$
\Delta Q=C\Delta\Psi=5.39411\times10^{-13}\,\mathrm C,\qquad
\boxed{N=\frac{|\Delta Q|}{e}=3.36674\times10^6\text{ monovalent ions}.}
$$

Ignoring the ends gives $3.33\times10^6$, only about one percent smaller. Inward movement of positive [ions](../../../../ion.md), or outward movement of negative [ions](../../../../ion.md), produces the stated positive change in the inside-minus-outside [membrane potential](../../../../membrane-potential-split.md).

The cell [volume](../../../../volume.md) is $\pi r^2\ell=3.14159\times10^{-16}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Distributed over this [volume](../../../../volume.md), the transferred number would correspond to only $0.0177955\,\mathrm{mM}$. Relative to an **illustrative**, not source-specified, $100\,\mathrm{mM}$ internal ionic pool, that is a fraction $1.78\times10^{-4}$. Thus a substantial change in [membrane potential](../../../../membrane-potential-split.md) requires movement of only a tiny fraction of bulk [ions](../../../../ion.md); it does not require wholesale depletion of the intracellular solution. The separated [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md) is concentrated near the two membrane surfaces.
