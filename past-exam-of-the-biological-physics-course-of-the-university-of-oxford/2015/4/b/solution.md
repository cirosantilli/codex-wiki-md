<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Fit each pre-unfolding branch with its own [polymer contour length](../../../../polymer-contour-length.md) $L$ and a [persistence length](../../../../persistence-length.md) $L_p$, allowing for the measured extension zero. The [worm-like chain force-extension interpolation](../../../../worm-like-chain-force-extension-interpolation.md) has the limits

$$
f=\frac{k_BT}{L_p}\left[\frac1{4(1-x/L)^2}-\frac14+\frac xL\right]
\sim\frac{3k_BT}{2L_pL}x\quad(x/L\ll1),\qquad
f\sim\frac{k_BT}{4L_p(1-x/L)^2}\quad(x/L\to1).
$$

Thus the low-extension slope and the high-extension curvature constrain the flexible segment's length and bending stiffness. The [persistence length](../../../../persistence-length.md) is related to the bending modulus by $\kappa=k_BT L_p$. Successive increases in fitted $L$ measure contour released by unfolding, after subtracting the original folded domain's projected length. With an independently known contour increment per residue, this also estimates how many residues became available. A common fitted $L_p$ across branches supports a common type of unfolded chain; a systematic change can signal altered interactions or a poor model.

For a thermal [worm-like chain](../../../../worm-like-chain.md), $\langle\mathbf t(s)\cdot\mathbf t(0)\rangle=e^{-s/L_p}$. Consequently $s=L_p$ gives $\langle\cos\theta\rangle=1/e$, and $\arccos(1/e)=68.4^\circ$. The latter is an angle associated with the **mean cosine**, not generally the mean angle itself. For example, angles $0$ and $90^\circ$ with equal probabilities have mean angle $45^\circ$, but the inverse cosine of their mean cosine is $60^\circ$. This distinction makes the source's informal angular description precise.
