<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A major source of [ATP](../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) is [chemiosmosis](../../../../chemiosmosis.md): an [electron transport chain](../../../../electron-transport-chain.md) uses downhill electron transfer to establish a [proton-motive force](../../../../proton-motive-force.md), and [ATP synthase](../../../../atp-synthase.md) converts that [electrochemical gradient](../../../../electrochemical-gradient.md) into [ATP](../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) formation. In aerobic [oxidative phosphorylation](../../../../oxidative-phosphorylation.md), reduced carriers supply electrons, ultimately reducing oxygen to [water](../../../../water.md); selected respiratory complexes couple the favorable [oxidation-reduction reaction](../../../../redox.md) to uphill [proton](../../../../proton.md) translocation. In a [mitochondrion](../../../../mitochondria.md), this takes place across the inner membrane, creating a relatively positive, acidic exterior side and a relatively negative, alkaline matrix. [Bacteria](../../../../bacteria.md) use their [cell membrane](../../../../cell-membrane-split.md). In photosynthetic systems, light-driven electron transfer can supply the gradient instead. [Substrate-level phosphorylation](../../../../substrate-level-phosphorylation.md) is another [ATP](../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md)-producing route, so the description is not universal to every organism and condition.

The membrane $F_O$ region of [ATP synthase](../../../../atp-synthase.md) contains a rotating ring of c-subunits next to subunit a. Two offset aqueous half-channels expose the ring's proton-binding sites to opposite membrane sides. [Proton](../../../../proton.md) binding and release bias ring rotation down the [electrochemical gradient](../../../../electrochemical-gradient.md). The central shaft transfers [torque](../../../../torque.md) to the $F_1$ catalytic head, while a peripheral stator prevents the head from simply co-rotating. This is the [ATP synthase rotary mechanism](../../../../atp-synthase-rotary-mechanism.md), rather than unrestricted [proton](../../../../proton.md) leakage through a pore.

The three catalytic beta subunits cycle through different binding states as the shaft turns: [ADP](../../../../adenosine-diphosphate.md) and [inorganic phosphate](../../../../inorganic-phosphate.md) bind, bound [ATP](../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) is favored, then [ATP](../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) is released. The binding-change mechanism uses mechanical [work](../../../../work.md) to alter binding affinities and product release as well as the chemical step. A full coupled revolution produces three [ATP](../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md) molecules; a ring with $n_c$ c-subunits transfers approximately $n_c$ [protons](../../../../proton.md) per revolution in the tightly coupled model, or $n_c/3$ per [ATP](../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md).[Proton-translocation half-channels and rotor architecture](../../../../structure-of-a-bacterial-atp-synthase-by-hui-guo-and-others.md)[Rotary coupling and c-ring stoichiometry](../../../../rotary-catalysis-of-fof1-atp-synthase-by-rikiya-watanabe.md)

The overall cycle must have a favorable [Gibbs free energy](../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md) change. If the [proton-motive force](../../../../proton-motive-force.md) becomes too small, synthesis stops; under suitable conditions the same [molecular motor](../../../../molecular-motor.md) can reverse, using [ATP hydrolysis](../../../../atp-hydrolysis.md) to pump [protons](../../../../proton.md) uphill. A catalyst changes kinetics, not the thermodynamic condition for synthesis.
