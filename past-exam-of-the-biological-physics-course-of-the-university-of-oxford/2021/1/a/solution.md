<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At the initial negative [membrane potential](../../../../membrane-potential-split.md), most [voltage-gated potassium channels](../../../../voltage-gated-potassium-channel.md) have a closed activation gate. [Depolarization](../../../../depolarization.md) moves charged residues in each [voltage-sensing domain](../../../../voltage-sensing-domain.md), particularly the S4 helices, through the [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md). Coupling between the [voltage-sensing domains](../../../../voltage-sensing-domain.md) and the pore opens the intracellular gate. A classical [voltage-gated potassium channel](../../../../voltage-gated-potassium-channel.md) has four subunits, each with six transmembrane helices; S5, the pore loop and S6 contribute to the central pathway and [selectivity filter](../../../../selectivity-filter.md). The rising open [probability](../../../../probability.md) produces increasing outward [potassium](../../../../potassium.md) [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md), since the imposed positive [membrane potential](../../../../membrane-potential-split.md) is above the [potassium](../../../../potassium.md) [Nernst equilibrium potential](../../../../nernst-equilibrium-potential.md). Here outward [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) is positive: $I_K=N\gamma p_O(V-E_K)$, where $N$ is channel number and $\gamma$ is single-channel [electrical conductance](../../../../electrical-conductance.md).

The [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) subsequently falls because the rapidly inactivating Shaker form exhibits [ball-and-chain inactivation](../../../../ball-and-chain-inactivation.md). An intracellular N-terminal domain, tethered to the channel, blocks the open pore. The [ion channel](../../../../ion-channel.md) can therefore stop conducting while the [membrane potential](../../../../membrane-potential-split.md) remains depolarized and the activation machinery remains activated. This is [ion-channel inactivation](../../../../ion-channel-inactivation.md), rather than reversal of the voltage step, loss of the ionic driving force, or ordinary gate closure. [Repolarization](../../../../repolarization.md) favors removal of the block and recovery of channel availability. Slower [C-type inactivation](../../../../c-type-inactivation.md) involving the outer pore can coexist, but is not the principal explanation of the rapid decay described here.

Several independent observations connect this functional explanation to structure. Small transient gating [electric currents](../../../../electric-current.md) report movement of sensor [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md) before ionic conduction; charge-neutralization studies of S4/S2 change the measured gating charge and activation. Structural studies resolve separate sensor and pore regions.[Gating-charge measurements identify voltage-sensing residues](../../../../voltage-sensing-residues-in-the-s2-and-s4-segments-of-the-shaker-k-plus-channel-by-s-a-seoh-and-others.md)[Voltage-sensor and pore architecture of Kv1.2](../../../../crystal-structure-of-a-mammalian-voltage-dependent-shaker-family-k-plus-channel-by-stephen-b-long-and-others.md) Historical N-terminal removal abolished rapid [ion-channel inactivation](../../../../ion-channel-inactivation.md) while preserving activation, and the corresponding free peptide restored block from the cytoplasmic side. These results separate the activation gate from the tethered blocker and identify which side of the [cell membrane](../../../../cell-membrane-split.md) contains the blocking domain.[N-terminal perturbations distinguish rapid inactivation from activation](../../../../biophysical-and-molecular-mechanisms-of-shaker-potassium-channel-inactivation-by-toshinori-hoshi-and-others.md)[Cytoplasmic peptide restores inactivation to channels lacking the native N-terminal domain](../../../../restoration-of-inactivation-in-mutants-of-shaker-potassium-channels-by-a-peptide-derived-from-shb-by-william-n-zagotta-and-others.md)
