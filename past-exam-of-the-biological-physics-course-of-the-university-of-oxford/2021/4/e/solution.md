<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The specified screened-sphere charging model gives $8.00\times10^5\,\mathrm{pN\,nm}$, about $3.8$ times the supplied experimental magnitude. It is much closer than the unscreened result. The separate ideal [counterion](../../../../counterion.md)-compression estimate gives $1.91\times10^5\,\mathrm{pN\,nm}$, within about $9\%$ of that magnitude, but this numerical proximity does not validate its rough geometry.

Remaining differences can arise from the unpacked reference self-energy, [counterion condensation](../../../../counterion-condensation.md), ion exchange with the surrounding reservoir, nonuniform [charge density](../../../../charge-density.md), nonlinear [electrostatic screening](../../../../electrostatic-screening.md), finite [DNA](../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) thickness and the actual [capsid](../../../../viral-capsid.md) shape. [DNA bending energy](../../../../dna-bending-energy.md), [hydration forces](../../../../hydration-force.md) and configurational [entropy](../../../../entropy-split.md) also contribute. The experiment measures a process-dependent [work](../../../../work.md) or inferred [free energy](../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md), whereas the simple screened sphere computes just one model contribution. Neither a zero-net-charge argument nor agreement of one approximate number establishes an exact total energy.
