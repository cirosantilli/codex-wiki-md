<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The stipulated [DNA](../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) [volume](../../../../volume.md) is $V_D=20000\,\mathrm{nm^3}$ and the spherical [capsid](../../../../viral-capsid.md) [volume](../../../../volume.md) is $V_c=4\pi(20\,\mathrm{nm})^3/3=33510\,\mathrm{nm^3}$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\phi=\frac{V_D}{V_c}=0.597\simeq60\%.}
$$

The packed [DNA](../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) occupies a substantial fraction of the available space: the remaining nominal fraction is $40.3\%$. Compared with the paper's stated $0.71$ reference, it reaches about $84\%$ of that value. This indicates dense confinement, but does not prove crystalline order or specify interstrand spacing. [DNA](../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) is a connected elongated [polymer](../../../../polymer-split.md), rather than a collection of independent hard spheres.

There is also a numerical qualification: the exact densest equal-[sphere packing](../../../../sphere-packing.md) fraction is $\pi/(3\sqrt2)\simeq0.74048$, not $0.71$. Using it changes the comparison to about $81\%$ without changing the calculated $0.597$ [volume fraction](../../../../volume-fraction.md) or its physical interpretation.
