<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the continuum cylindrical-pore estimate, the [electrical resistance](../../../../electrical-resistance.md) is $R_p=\rho\ell/(\pi a^2)$, with radius $a=0.075\,\mathrm{nm}$, length $\ell=0.5\,\mathrm{nm}$ and [electrical resistivity](../../../../electrical-resistivity.md) $\rho=80\,\Omega\,\mathrm{cm}=0.80\,\Omega\,\mathrm m$. Therefore

$$
R_p=2.26\times10^{10}\,\Omega,\qquad
\boxed{g_p=R_p^{-1}=44.2\,\mathrm{pS}}.
$$

Unrestricted approach from the reservoirs removes access barriers, but still permits a continuum spreading or [access resistance of a circular pore](../../../../access-resistance-of-a-circular-pore.md). Including $\rho/(4a)$ on each side gives

$$
R_{\rm access}=\frac{\rho}{2a}=5.33\times10^9\,\Omega,\qquad
\boxed{g_{\rm total}=\left[\frac{\rho\ell}{\pi a^2}+\frac{\rho}{2a}\right]^{-1}\simeq35.8\,\mathrm{pS}}.
$$

Thus the usual estimate neglecting the access contribution is about $44\,\mathrm{pS}$, or about $36\,\mathrm{pS}$ with spreading resistance. A $1.5$-ångström diameter is at the molecular scale, smaller than hydrated [ions](../../../../ion.md); bulk [electrical resistivity](../../../../electrical-resistivity.md) cannot quantitatively describe dehydration, selectivity or discrete-ion transport. These values are the requested continuum estimates, not evidence that such a pore freely passes hydrated [potassium](../../../../potassium.md) [ions](../../../../ion.md).
