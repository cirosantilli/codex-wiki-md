The rising sigmoid shows activation by [depolarization](../../../../depolarization.md): $P_o$ is almost zero at very negative [membrane potential](../../../../membrane-potential-split.md), rises steeply around $-20\,\mathrm{mV}$, and approaches about $0.8$ at positive [membrane potential](../../../../membrane-potential-split.md). The graph describes the fraction of time a single [ion channel](../../../../ion-channel.md) is open, not a continuously variable opening amplitude. A convenient phenomenological fit is

$$
P_o(V)=\frac{P_{\max}}{1+\exp[-z_{\rm eff}e(V-V_{1/2})/(k_BT)]},
\qquad P_{\max}\simeq0.8,\quad V_{1/2}\simeq-20\,\mathrm{mV}.
$$

Here $V_{1/2}$ refers to half the maximum, about $0.4$, not absolute $P_o=0.5$. The effective gating charge describes the steepness; a sigmoid alone does not identify the microscopic gating sequence or explain why the observed maximum is below one.

A [voltage-gated potassium channel](../../../../voltage-gated-potassium-channel.md) has four pore-forming subunits. Each canonical subunit contains an S1–S4 [voltage-sensing domain](../../../../voltage-sensing-domain.md) and S5–S6 pore elements. Positive charges in S4 respond to the [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md); their displacement during [depolarization](../../../../depolarization.md) changes the energetic preference of sensor conformations. Coupling through the sensor–pore interface favors opening of the intracellular pore gate. The [potassium](../../../../potassium.md) selectivity filter is structurally distinct from this voltage sensor.

Several independent observations support this account. [Voltage clamp](../../../../voltage-clamp.md) detects transient gating [electric currents](../../../../electric-current.md) before pore conduction, even when ionic conduction is blocked: charge movement precedes opening. Charge-neutralization studies change total gating charge and voltage dependence, with contributions from charged S4 residues and an S2 residue. Accessibility and voltage-sensitive optical measurements support conformational motion rather than permeating [potassium](../../../../potassium.md) itself acting as the sensor. Structural measurements locate the charged sensor beside the pore. These observations constrain a coupled moving-sensor mechanism, while the exact displacement cannot be deduced from this $P_o$ curve alone.[Abstract: gating-charge measurements identify contributions from S2 and S4 charged residues](../../../../voltage-sensing-residues-in-the-s2-and-s4-segments-of-the-shaker-k-plus-channel-by-s-a-seoh-and-others.md)[Structure of Kv1.2: separate voltage-sensor and pore domains and their coupling](../../../../crystal-structure-of-a-mammalian-voltage-dependent-shaker-family-k-plus-channel-by-stephen-b-long-and-others.md)
