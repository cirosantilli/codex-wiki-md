<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In [atomic force microscopy](../../../../atomic-force-microscopy.md), one end of the [polyacrylic acid](../../../../polyacrylic-acid.md) chain is attached to the substrate and the other to a compliant cantilever tip. Controlled separation displaces the cantilever; calibrated cantilever stiffness converts its deflection into [force](../../../../force-split.md). The molecular extension is the tip–surface separation after correcting for cantilever deflection and instrumental offsets. A quasi-static interpretation requires relaxation to be fast compared with the pulling motion.

At low [force](../../../../force-split.md) a coiled [polymer](../../../../polymer-split.md) has many available configurations and extends mainly by changing segment orientations. This is the [entropic spring](../../../../entropic-spring.md) regime. As the available contour is aligned, the loss of configurational [entropy](../../../../entropy-split.md) becomes steep. For an inextensible three-dimensional [freely jointed chain](../../../../ideal-chain.md),

$$
\frac{\langle z\rangle}{Nb}=\mathcal L\!\left(\frac{fb}{k_BT}\right),\qquad
\mathcal L(x)=\coth x-\frac1x,
$$

where $\mathcal L$ is the [Langevin function](../../../../langevin-function.md). The limiting extension is the [polymer contour length](../../../../polymer-contour-length.md) $Nb$; the ideal model approaches it only as $f\to\infty$. Real [polymer backbone](../../../../polymer-backbone.md) stretching permits extension beyond the fitted rigid-link contour, explaining why the experimental curve lies on the more extensible side at high [force](../../../../force-split.md).

The original trace has a sharp force drop after its peak. Finite contour length explains the steep preceding rise, but does not itself cause that drop: release of an attachment, desorption or mechanical failure ends tensile loading. The graph alone does not distinguish these possibilities. If the intact chain is released from tension, thermal motion restores a coil with characteristic [end-to-end distance of a polymer chain](../../../../end-to-end-distance-of-a-polymer-chain.md) of order $b\sqrt N$, because this macrostate has many more configurations. Recoil is reversible for an intact equilibrating chain; irreversible [chain scission](../../../../chain-scission.md) or attachment loss can prevent repetition of the same pulling curve.
