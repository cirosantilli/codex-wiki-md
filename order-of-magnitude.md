# Order of magnitude

↑ **Parent:** [Logarithmic scale](logarithmic-scale.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_magnitude)

For positive quantities, a difference of one [order of magnitude](order-of-magnitude.md) means a factor of ten; a difference of $n$ [orders of magnitude](order-of-magnitude.md) means a factor of $10^n$. The logarithmic difference $\log_{10}(x_2/x_1)$ records this comparison, without subtracting the quantities themselves. For example, $1000$ is three [orders of magnitude](order-of-magnitude.md) above one, regardless of a common change of units. Roughly assigning a single value to an [order of magnitude](order-of-magnitude.md) also requires a rounding convention; powers of ten are the reference levels.
