# Complete one-consistent theories can prove false sentences

↑ **Parent:** [One-consistency](one-consistency.md)

Given a bounded-complete [one-consistent](one-consistency.md) [arithmetic](arithmetic-split.md) [first-order theory](first-order-theory.md) with a false theorem, enumerate [first-order sentences](first-order-sentence.md) and successively choose [one-consistent](one-consistency.md) one-sentence extensions. Every stage has [Sigma-1 soundness](sigma-1-soundness.md), and a false Sigma-1 theorem in the [union](set-union.md) would already have a [formal proof](formal-proof.md) at one stage. The [union](set-union.md) is therefore [one-consistent](one-consistency.md) and complete while retaining the false theorem. It cannot be omega-consistent, since a complete bounded-complete omega-consistent [first-order theory](first-order-theory.md) is [sound](soundness.md).
