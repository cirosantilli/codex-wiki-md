# Coma (optics)

↑ **Parent:** [Seidel aberration](seidel-aberration.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coma_(optics))

[Coma](coma-optics.md) makes an off-axis [optical point source](optical-point-source.md) appear asymmetric, often with a compact head and a flaring tail like a comet. Different parts of the [optical pupil](optical-pupil.md) send its [light rays](light-ray.md) to different transverse image positions. A sharp central [star](star.md) image therefore does not guarantee sharp images across an [optical telescope](optical-telescope.md)'s [field of view](field-of-view.md). In an idealized, aligned system with [rotational symmetry](rotational-symmetry.md), [coma](coma-optics.md) vanishes on the [optical axis](optical-axis.md); misalignment can introduce [coma](coma-optics.md) even at the nominal field centre.[Amateur Telescope Optics, §4.2, opening paragraphs and Figure 39: field coma versus misalignment coma](amateur-telescope-optics-by-vladimir-sacek/coma-aberration.md)

[Coma](coma-optics.md) differs from [spherical aberration](spherical-aberration.md), whose dependence on [optical pupil](optical-pupil.md) radius can blur an on-axis point without selecting a transverse direction. [Astigmatism](astigmatism-optical-systems.md) gives different [optical foci](focus-optics.md) in two perpendicular sections of an off-axis bundle; a through-focus sequence can change its elongated image's orientation. [Diffraction](diffraction.md) persists when these [optical aberrations](optical-aberration.md) vanish. A real asymmetric [point spread function](point-spread-function-split.md) may mix several effects, so its appearance alone is not a unique diagnosis of [coma](coma-optics.md).

The classical primary description belongs to the nineteenth-century [Seidel aberration](seidel-aberration.md) classification of five primary monochromatic image errors.[Amateur Telescope Optics, §3.5.1, opening paragraph: historical classification and equation 5](amateur-telescope-optics-by-vladimir-sacek/seidel-aberrations-wavefront-aberration-function.md) It remains useful in modern design: OpticStudio's [Seidel Coefficients by ANSYS, Inc.](seidel-coefficients-by-ansys-inc.md) documentation lists [coma](coma-optics.md) as $W_{131}$ and tabulates each surface's contribution as well as the system sum (Discussion). Cancellation of contributions can reduce [coma](coma-optics.md) without making every surface individually free of it. The same documentation limits this calculation to specified systems with axially symmetric surfaces; higher-order terms and broken [rotational symmetry](rotational-symmetry.md) require more general [ray tracing](ray-tracing.md).
