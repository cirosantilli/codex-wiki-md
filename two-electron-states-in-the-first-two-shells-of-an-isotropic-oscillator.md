# Two-electron states in the first two shells of an isotropic oscillator

↑ **Parent:** [Exchange symmetry of two identical spin-one-half fermions](exchange-symmetry-of-two-identical-spin-one-half-fermions.md)

The spatial ground orbital of a three-dimensional [quantum harmonic oscillator](quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md) has $l=0$ and energy $3\hbar\omega_0/2$; its first excited shell has $l=1$ and energy $5\hbar\omega_0/2$. Two identical [electrons](electron.md) both in the ground orbital must form the [singlet state](singlet-state.md), giving one state at total energy $3\hbar\omega_0$.

At energy $4\hbar\omega_0$, one electron occupies each shell. There are two ground-shell spin orbitals and six first-excited-shell spin orbitals, hence twelve [Slater determinants](slater-determinant.md). Equivalently, $0\otimes1$ gives $L=1$; symmetric spatial combinations with the [singlet state](singlet-state.md) give three states, while antisymmetric spatial combinations with the [triplet states](spin-one-half-triplet-state.md) give nine. Distinct occupied orbitals allow both spatial exchange symmetries, so both spin sectors must be kept.
