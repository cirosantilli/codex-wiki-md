# Thermal expansion

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_expansion)

Thermal expansion is a change in a body's dimensions as its [temperature](temperature-split.md) changes under a specified mechanical constraint. A heated [gas](gas.md) under a freely moving loaded piston usually increases its [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) at fixed [pressure](pressure-split.md); heating the same [gas](gas.md) in a rigid vessel instead raises its [pressure](pressure-split.md). The [coefficient of thermal expansion](coefficient-of-thermal-expansion.md) quantifies the first response. Expansion can be negative, so the name does not assert that every substance grows on heating.

For an [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) at fixed [pressure](pressure-split.md) and particle number, $V=Nk_BT/P$ gives $V_2/V_1=T_2/T_1$. Heating from $300\,\mathrm K$ to $330\,\mathrm K$ therefore increases the [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) by exactly $10\%$ within the [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) model. For a [solid](solid.md), specify whether the measurement concerns length or [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md): for uniform expansion in all three directions, the volumetric coefficient is three times the [linear thermal expansion coefficient](linear-thermal-expansion-coefficient.md), because $V\propto L^3$. Direction-dependent expansion requires the separate dimensional responses. [G. Ceder, MIT thermodynamics notes, section 1.14: linear and volumetric expansion](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-20-materials-at-equilibrium-sma-5111-fall-2003/e402e3f0f555e15ff3af29b5b44b4e57_notes1.pdf)
