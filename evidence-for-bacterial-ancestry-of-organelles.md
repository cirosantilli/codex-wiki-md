# Evidence for bacterial ancestry of organelles

↑ **Parent:** [Endosymbiotic theory](endosymbiotic-theory.md)

Resemblance is a clue, not sufficient evidence of [common descent](common-descent.md). A small size, two surrounding membranes, division, retained [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) or bacterial-looking [ribosomes](ribosome.md) does not uniquely identify an ancestor. Internal membranes exist outside these [organelles](organelle.md), and a similar function can favor similar structures. The stronger question is whether independently examined molecular characters support the same historical relationship.

A concrete genomic observation is the mitochondrial sequence reported in 1997 for [Reclinomonas americana](reclinomonas-americana.md): $69{,}034$ [base pairs](base-pair.md) containing 97 identified [genes](gene.md), including four for a bacterial-type multisubunit [RNA polymerase](rna-polymerase.md). This is a reported [genome](genome.md) inventory, not a calculation from the pictured shape of a [mitochondrion](mitochondria.md). Retention of such bacterial features strengthens the ancestry argument; a living [Reclinomonas americana](reclinomonas-americana.md) is not itself an ancestral [eukaryote](eukaryote.md). The inspected abstract supplies these observations but not the detailed sequencing protocol or contamination controls, so those details are not attributed to this report here. [Abstract, Nature 387 (1997), pp. 493–497: genome length, gene inventory and bacterial-type RNA polymerase](an-ancestral-mitochondrial-dna-resembling-a-eubacterial-genome-in-miniature-by-b-franz-lang-and-others.md)

A sequence-based investigation obtains [nucleotide sequences](nucleic-acid-sequence.md) by [DNA sequencing](dna-sequencing.md), identifies comparable [genes](gene.md), makes a [sequence alignment](sequence-alignment.md) and uses [phylogenetics](phylogenetics.md) to infer relationships. The readout is a proposed relationship with support and uncertainty, not a photograph of an ancient encounter. Comparisons of [plastid](plastid.md) sequences with bacterial sequences support cyanobacterial ancestry, while the exact closest sampled cyanobacterial group has depended on the chosen data and inference. A bacterial affinity in one [gene](gene.md) is weaker than coherent evidence across several [genes](gene.md) and cellular systems. [“The endosymbiont: when and who?”: alternative phylogenetic placements and lateral gene transfer](origin-and-evolution-of-plastids-and-photosynthesis-in-eukaryotes-by-geoffrey-i-mcfadden.md)

For such an analysis, useful checks include distinguishing [organelle](organelle.md) [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) from bacterial contamination, verifying sequence quality, comparing corresponding rather than unrelated [genes](gene.md), sampling several bacterial groups and testing sensitivity to the [sequence alignment](sequence-alignment.md) and evolutionary [mathematical model](mathematical-model.md). These are methodological requirements, not a claim that this article has rerun a particular published analysis. [Horizontal gene transfer](horizontal-gene-transfer.md), [gene](gene.md) loss and unequal evolutionary change can make an individual [gene](gene.md)'s history differ from a whole [organism](organism.md)'s history. A supported bacterial origin therefore need not identify an extant donor [species](species.md), an exact date or the original mode of entry. [“The endosymbiont: when and who?”: ancient origin and confounding lateral transfer](origin-and-evolution-of-plastids-and-photosynthesis-in-eukaryotes-by-geoffrey-i-mcfadden.md)
