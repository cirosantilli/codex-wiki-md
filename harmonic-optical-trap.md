# Harmonic optical trap

↑ **Parent:** [Optical tweezers](optical-tweezers.md)

A harmonic [optical trap](optical-tweezers.md) approximates one coordinate near a stable [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) by a quadratic [potential energy](potential-energy.md). Measure [displacement](displacement.md) $x$ from that [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md), set its [potential energy](potential-energy.md) to zero there, and take a positive effective [spring constant](spring-constant.md) $k$, also called the trap stiffness:

$$
U(x)=\frac12kx^2,\qquad F(x)=-\frac{dU}{dx}=-kx,\qquad k>0.
$$

The [restoring force](restoring-force.md) points left when $x>0$ and right when $x<0$. This is a local model: a [Taylor series](taylor-series.md) of a smooth [potential energy](potential-energy.md) about a nondegenerate [local minimum](local-minimum.md) gives $k=U''(0)>0$ as its leading curvature. A real [optical trap](optical-tweezers.md) has a finite capture region; the endlessly rising parabola does not describe its escape boundary. A three-dimensional [optical trap](optical-tweezers.md) can have different stiffnesses along different axes, and nonconservative optical [forces](force-split.md) can limit the [potential energy](potential-energy.md) description.
