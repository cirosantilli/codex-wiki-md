# Anchored variation norm

↑ **Parent:** [Total variation of a function](total-variation-of-a-function.md)

On the [vector space](vector-space-split.md) of [continuous functions](continuous-function.md) $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ of [bounded variation](total-variation-of-a-function.md) with $f(0)=0$, the [total variation of a function](total-variation-of-a-function.md) is a [norm](norm.md). Its [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md) and [absolute homogeneity of a norm](absolute-homogeneity-of-a-norm.md) follow by applying the corresponding [absolute value](absolute-value.md) identities to every [partition of an interval](partition-of-an-interval.md), then taking the [supremum](supremum.md). The [partition of an interval](partition-of-an-interval.md) $(0,t,1)$ gives $|f(t)|\leq V_0^1(f)$, so zero [total variation of a function](total-variation-of-a-function.md) forces $f=0$. The same inequality gives $\|f\|_\infty\leq\|f\|_V$, proving that convergence for this [norm](norm.md) implies [uniform convergence](uniform-convergence.md). Without $f(0)=0$, every constant [function](function-split.md) would have zero [total variation of a function](total-variation-of-a-function.md).
