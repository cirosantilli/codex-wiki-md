<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Yes: every scheme is a Kolmogorov space.** Choose an [affine open subscheme](../../../../../affine-open-subscheme.md) $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ containing $x$. If $y\notin V$, take $U=V$.

Otherwise, the distinct points $x,y\in V$ correspond to distinct [prime ideals](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) $\mathfrak p_x,\mathfrak p_y\subseteq A$. There is an element in one of these [prime ideals](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) and not the other. After interchanging the names of the two points if necessary, choose

$$
a\in\mathfrak p_x\setminus\mathfrak p_y.
$$

The [principal open subscheme](../../../../../principal-open-subscheme.md)

$$
\boxed{U=D(a)=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}A:a\notin\mathfrak p\}}
$$

contains $y$ and does not contain $x$. It is an [open subset](../../../../../open-set.md) of $V$, hence an [open subset](../../../../../open-set.md) of $X$ because $V$ is open in $X$. This establishes $(*)$ in every case, proving [schemes are Kolmogorov spaces](../../../../../schemes-are-kolmogorov-spaces.md).
