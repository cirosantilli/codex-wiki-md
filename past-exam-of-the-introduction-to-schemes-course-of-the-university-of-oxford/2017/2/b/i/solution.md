<h1 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $C=\overline{\{z\}}$, where the [closure](../../../../../closure-topology.md) is taken in the underlying [topological space](../../../../../topological-space.md) of $X$. It is nonempty because it contains $z$.

Suppose $C=A\cup B$ for two [closed subsets](../../../../../closed-set.md) of $C$ in the [subspace topology](../../../../../subspace-topology.md). Since $C$ is a [closed subset](../../../../../closed-set.md) of $X$, both $A$ and $B$ are also [closed subsets](../../../../../closed-set.md) of $X$. The point $z$ lies in one of them, say $A$. By the defining minimality of the [closure](../../../../../closure-topology.md), every [closed subset](../../../../../closed-set.md) of $X$ containing $z$ contains $C$. Hence $C\subseteq A$, so $A=C$.

Thus $C$ cannot be expressed as the union of two proper [closed subsets](../../../../../closed-set.md), proving that **$C$ is an irreducible closed subset**. This proves [closure of a point is irreducible](../../../../../closure-of-a-point-is-irreducible.md) in every [topological space](../../../../../topological-space.md); the [scheme](../../../../../scheme.md) assumption is unnecessary. Equivalently, $z$ is a [generic point](../../../../../generic-point.md) of its own [closure](../../../../../closure-topology.md).
