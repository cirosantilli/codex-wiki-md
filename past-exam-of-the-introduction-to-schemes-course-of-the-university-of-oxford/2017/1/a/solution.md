<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Apply the surjectivity of $f(Y)$ to the [identity morphism](../../../../identity-morphism.md) $1_Y$. It gives a [morphism of schemes](../../../../morphism-of-schemes.md) $g:Y\to X$ satisfying $f\circ g=1_Y$. To obtain the other identity, compare $g\circ f$ and $1_X$ in $X(X)$. Their images under $f(X)$ agree, since

$$
f\circ(g\circ f)=(f\circ g)\circ f=f=f\circ1_X.
$$

The injectivity of $f(X)$ therefore gives $g\circ f=1_X$. Thus

$$
\boxed{f\text{ is an isomorphism of schemes, with inverse }g.}
$$

Only surjectivity for the test [scheme](../../../../scheme.md) $Y$ and injectivity for the test [scheme](../../../../scheme.md) $X$ were needed. This is the isomorphism-detection argument behind the [Yoneda lemma](../../../../yoneda-lemma.md) for the [functor of points](../../../../functor-represented-by-a-scheme.md).
