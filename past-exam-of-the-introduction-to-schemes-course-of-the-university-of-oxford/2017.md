<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Apply the surjectivity of $f(Y)$ to the [identity morphism](../algebra.md#identity-morphism) $1_Y$. It gives a [morphism of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) $g:Y\to X$ satisfying $f\circ g=1_Y$. To obtain the other identity, compare $g\circ f$ and $1_X$ in $X(X)$. Their images under $f(X)$ agree, since

$$
f\circ(g\circ f)=(f\circ g)\circ f=f=f\circ1_X.
$$

The injectivity of $f(X)$ therefore gives $g\circ f=1_X$. Thus

$$
\boxed{f\text{ is an isomorphism of schemes, with inverse }g.}
$$

Only surjectivity for the test [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme) $Y$ and injectivity for the test [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme) $X$ were needed. This is the isomorphism-detection argument behind the [Yoneda lemma](../functor.md#yoneda-lemma) for the [functor of points](../locally-ringed-space.md#functor-represented-by-a-scheme).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

**No.** Let $k=\mathbb F_2$ be the [field with two elements](../algebra.md#field-with-two-elements), put $A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$, and take the [closed immersion](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-immersion)

$$
\boxed{f:\operatorname{Spec}k\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}A}
$$

induced by the [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $q:A\twoheadrightarrow k$ with $q(\varepsilon)=0$.

For every [field](../algebra.md#field) $K$, a [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $\alpha:A\to K$ must send $\varepsilon$ to zero: $\alpha(\varepsilon)^2=0$, and a [field](../algebra.md#field) has no nonzero [nilpotent elements](../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent). Consequently $\alpha$ factors uniquely through $q$. Passing from [ring homomorphisms](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) to [morphisms of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) between [affine schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme) gives a [bijection](../function.md#bijection)

$$
f(\operatorname{Spec}K):\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Sch}}(\operatorname{Spec}K,\operatorname{Spec}k)
\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Sch}}(\operatorname{Spec}K,\operatorname{Spec}A).
$$

This concerns all [morphisms of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes), without requiring them to be over $k$: if $K$ does not have [characteristic](../algebra.md#characteristic-of-a-field) two, both sets are empty; otherwise each contains exactly one element.

Nevertheless, $f$ is not an [isomorphism of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#isomorphism-of-schemes). Its map on [global sections](../sheaf-of-modules.md#global-section) is $q$, whose [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism) contains the nonzero element $\varepsilon$, so $q$ is not a [ring isomorphism](../algebra.md#ring-isomorphism). The target is the [nonreduced scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#nonreduced-scheme) defined by the [dual numbers](../commutative-algebra.md#dual-number), whereas the source is a [reduced scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#reduced-scheme). This illustrates why [field-valued points do not detect nilpotents](../locally-ringed-space.md#field-valued-points-do-not-detect-nilpotents).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

**Yes.** First, the hypothesis implies that $f(T)$ is injective for every [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme) $T$, even when $T$ is not an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme). Indeed, suppose $a,b:T\to X$ satisfy $f\circ a=f\circ b$. Choose an [affine open cover](../topology.md#affine-open-cover) $T=\bigcup_\lambda V_\lambda$. On each $V_\lambda$, injectivity of $f(V_\lambda)$ gives $a|_{V_\lambda}=b|_{V_\lambda}$. By [gluing morphisms of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#gluing-morphisms-of-schemes), $a=b$.

Now choose an [affine open cover](../topology.md#affine-open-cover) $Y=\bigcup_i U_i$, and denote its inclusion maps by $j_i:U_i\hookrightarrow Y$. Surjectivity of $f(U_i)$ gives [morphisms of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) $g_i:U_i\to X$ with $f\circ g_i=j_i$. On $U_i\cap U_j$, the two restrictions have the same composite with $f$. By the injectivity just established for arbitrary test [schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme),

$$
g_i|_{U_i\cap U_j}=g_j|_{U_i\cap U_j}.
$$

In particular, the overlaps need not be [affine schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme). [Gluing morphisms of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#gluing-morphisms-of-schemes) supplies $g:Y\to X$ with $g|_{U_i}=g_i$, and $f\circ g=1_Y$ because this holds on every $U_i$.

Finally,

$$
f\circ(g\circ f)=f=f\circ1_X.
$$

Injectivity of $f(X)$, already proved without an affineness assumption, gives $g\circ f=1_X$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f\text{ is an isomorphism of schemes}.}
$$

This proves the relevant instance of [affine test schemes determine scheme morphisms](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-test-schemes-determine-scheme-morphisms): the restricted [functor of points](../locally-ringed-space.md#functor-represented-by-a-scheme) still retains the information needed to construct the inverse.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

The [localization of a ring](../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) $R_r$ is generated over $R$ by the single element $1/r$, since every fraction can be written as

$$
\frac{a}{r^n}=\phi(a)(1/r)^n.
$$

The [presentation of a localization at one element](../commutative-algebra.md#presentation-of-a-localization-at-one-element) is

$$
\boxed{R_r\cong R[t]/(rt-1),\qquad t\longmapsto1/r.}
$$

To verify the presentation even when $R$ has [zero divisors](../mathematics.md#zero-divisor), observe that $r$ is invertible in $R[t]/(rt-1)$ with inverse $t$. Hence the map from $R$ to this [quotient ring](../commutative-algebra.md#quotient-ring) extends uniquely to $R_r$. This extension and the displayed map are inverse [ring homomorphisms](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism): they agree on $R$ and send the designated inverse of $r$ to the designated inverse of $r$.

Thus $R_r$ is a [finitely generated algebra over a ring](../commutative-algebra.md#finitely-generated-algebra-over-a-ring), and the criterion for a [morphism of finite type](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-finite-type) between [affine schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme) applies to $u:\operatorname{Spec}R_r\to\operatorname{Spec}R$. There is just one [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme) in the required cover of the source, with one algebra generator. Therefore **$u$ is of finite type**. If $r$ is [nilpotent](../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent), the same presentation is the [zero ring](../commutative-algebra.md#zero-ring), and the source is the empty [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme); the conclusion still holds.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Put $C=\overline{\{z\}}$, where the [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology) is taken in the underlying [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space) of $X$. It is nonempty because it contains $z$.

Suppose $C=A\cup B$ for two [closed subsets](../topology.md#closed-set) of $C$ in the [subspace topology](../topology.md#subspace-topology). Since $C$ is a [closed subset](../topology.md#closed-set) of $X$, both $A$ and $B$ are also [closed subsets](../topology.md#closed-set) of $X$. The point $z$ lies in one of them, say $A$. By the defining minimality of the [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology), every [closed subset](../topology.md#closed-set) of $X$ containing $z$ contains $C$. Hence $C\subseteq A$, so $A=C$.

Thus $C$ cannot be expressed as the union of two proper [closed subsets](../topology.md#closed-set), proving that **$C$ is an irreducible closed subset**. This proves [closure of a point is irreducible](../algebraic-geometry.md#closure-of-a-point-is-irreducible) in every [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space); the [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme) assumption is unnecessary. Equivalently, $z$ is a [generic point](../algebraic-geometry.md#generic-point) of its own [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology).

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

**Yes: every scheme is a Kolmogorov space.** Choose an [affine open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-open-subscheme) $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ containing $x$. If $y\notin V$, take $U=V$.

Otherwise, the distinct points $x,y\in V$ correspond to distinct [prime ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) $\mathfrak p_x,\mathfrak p_y\subseteq A$. There is an element in one of these [prime ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) and not the other. After interchanging the names of the two points if necessary, choose

$$
a\in\mathfrak p_x\setminus\mathfrak p_y.
$$

The [principal open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#principal-open-subscheme)

$$
\boxed{U=D(a)=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}A:a\notin\mathfrak p\}}
$$

contains $y$ and does not contain $x$. It is an [open subset](../topology.md#open-set) of $V$, hence an [open subset](../topology.md#open-set) of $X$ because $V$ is open in $X$. This establishes $(*)$ in every case, proving [schemes are Kolmogorov spaces](../locally-ringed-space.md#schemes-are-kolmogorov-spaces).

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2017](2017.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

Write $\mathbb P^r=\mathbb P^r_k$ over its ground [field](../algebra.md#field); the argument also works over any [Noetherian](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) affine base. Since $\iota$ is a [closed immersion](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-immersion), $X$ is a [noetherian scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#noetherian-scheme) and a [quasi-compact scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-scheme). Let

$$
L=\iota^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^r}(1),\qquad F(n)=F\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}L^{\otimes n}.
$$

Here the [pullback of a sheaf of modules](../sheaf-of-modules.md#pullback-of-a-sheaf-of-modules) makes $L$ an [invertible sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#line-bundle), the restriction of the [twisting sheaf on projective space](../sheaf-of-modules.md#twisting-sheaf-on-projective-space). It is an [ample line bundle](../sheaf-of-modules.md#ample-line-bundle).

By [global generation by sufficiently positive twists](../sheaf-of-modules.md#global-generation-by-sufficiently-positive-twists), choose $n\geq0$ such that $F(n)$ is a [globally generated sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#globally-generated-sheaf). Finitely many [global sections](../sheaf-of-modules.md#global-section) suffice: at each point, finite generation of the [stalk](../sheaf-of-modules.md#stalk-of-a-sheaf) of the [coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#coherent-sheaf) $F(n)$ lets us select finitely many generating germs of [global sections](../sheaf-of-modules.md#global-section). Those sections generate on a neighbourhood, because their [cokernel sheaf](../algebraic-geometry.md#cokernel-sheaf) is a [coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#coherent-sheaf) with zero [stalk](../sheaf-of-modules.md#stalk-of-a-sheaf) at that point. A finite subcover, using that $X$ is a [quasi-compact scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-scheme), supplies finitely many [global sections](../sheaf-of-modules.md#global-section) generating everywhere. This is [finite global generation on a quasi-compact scheme](../sheaf-of-modules.md#finite-global-generation-on-a-quasi-compact-scheme), and gives a surjective [sheaf morphism](../algebraic-geometry.md#morphism-of-sheaves)

$$
\mathcal O_X^{\oplus m}\twoheadrightarrow F(n).
$$

Tensor with $(L^\vee)^{\otimes n}$, where $L^\vee$ is the [dual of a sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#dual-of-a-sheaf). Here [tensoring with an invertible sheaf is exact](../sheaf-of-modules.md#tensoring-with-an-invertible-sheaf-is-exact): locally the [tensor product of sheaves](../sheaf-of-modules.md#tensor-product-of-sheaves) is tensoring with a free module of rank one. Using the permitted identity $L\otimes L^\vee\cong\mathcal O_X$, we obtain

$$
\boxed{G=\bigl((L^\vee)^{\otimes n}\bigr)^{\oplus m}=\mathcal O_X(-n)^{\oplus m},\qquad G\twoheadrightarrow F\longrightarrow0.}
$$

A finite [direct sum of sheaves](../algebraic-geometry.md#direct-sum-of-sheaves) of [invertible sheaves](../sheaf-of-modules.md#line-bundle) is a [locally free sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#locally-free-sheaf) of finite rank, and it is a [coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#coherent-sheaf) on the [noetherian scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#noetherian-scheme) $X$. This constructs the required $G$. No regularity or smoothness assumption on $X$ is needed.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Use homogeneous coordinates $x_0,\ldots,x_r$ on [projective space](../projective-space.md) and let

$$
U_i=X\cap D_+(x_i),\qquad 0\leq i\leq r.
$$

These $r+1$ [open subschemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-subscheme) cover $X$. Each is a [closed subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-subscheme) of the [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme) $D_+(x_i)$, hence is an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme).

Every nonempty finite intersection is affine as well. Indeed, inside $D_+(x_{i_0})$ the intersection with $D_+(x_{i_1}),\ldots,D_+(x_{i_p})$ is the [principal open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#principal-open-subscheme) obtained by inverting

$$
\prod_{j=1}^p\frac{x_{i_j}}{x_{i_0}}.
$$

Intersecting it with the [closed subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-subscheme) $X$ gives $U_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap U_{i_p}$, again an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme).

For any [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf) $F$, [vanishing of quasi-coherent cohomology on an affine scheme](../sheaf-of-modules.md#vanishing-of-quasi-coherent-cohomology-on-an-affine-scheme) makes every such intersection acyclic for $F$. The [acyclic cover theorem](../sheaf-of-modules.md#leray-s-theorem) therefore identifies the [sheaf cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#sheaf-cohomology) of $F$ with the [Čech cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#cech-cohomology) of this cover. Its alternating [Čech cochain complex](../sheaf-of-modules.md#cech-cochain-complex) has terms

$$
\check C^p(\mathcal U,F)=\prod_{0\leq i_0<\cdots<i_p\leq r}\Gamma(U_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap U_{i_p},F),
$$

which vanish for $p>r$: one cannot choose $p+1$ distinct indices from $r+1$ indices. Consequently

$$
\boxed{H^k(X,F)=0\quad(k>r),\qquad N=\max\{1,r\}.}
$$

The choice $N=\max\{1,r\}$ meets the strict requirement $N>0$ even when $r=0$. It is independent of $F$, and applies to all [quasi-coherent sheaves](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf), including those which are not [coherent sheaves](../sheaf-of-modules.md#coherent-sheaf). This is the [cohomological dimension bound from an affine cover](../sheaf-of-modules.md#cohomological-dimension-bound-from-an-affine-cover).

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Fix a [coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#coherent-sheaf) $F$ and an integer $k>M$. Set $K_0=F$. Repeatedly apply [a](#3/a) to construct [short exact sequences of sheaves](../algebraic-geometry.md#short-exact-sequence-of-sheaves)

$$
0\longrightarrow K_{j+1}\longrightarrow G_j\longrightarrow K_j\longrightarrow0,
$$

where each $G_j$ is a [coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#coherent-sheaf) and a [locally free sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#locally-free-sheaf). Each [kernel sheaf](../algebraic-geometry.md#kernel-sheaf) $K_{j+1}$ is a [coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#coherent-sheaf) because $X$ is a [noetherian scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#noetherian-scheme). Thus the construction can be repeated as many times as needed; it does not require the [kernel sheaves](../algebraic-geometry.md#kernel-sheaf) themselves to be [locally free sheaves](../sheaf-of-modules.md#locally-free-sheaf).

For every integer $q>M$, the [long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#long-exact-sequence-in-sheaf-cohomology) contains

$$
H^q(X,G_j)\longrightarrow H^q(X,K_j)\xrightarrow{\ \delta_j\ }H^{q+1}(X,K_{j+1})\longrightarrow H^{q+1}(X,G_j).
$$

Both outer groups vanish by the hypothesis, since $q$ and $q+1$ exceed $M$. Hence $\delta_j$ is an [isomorphism](../algebra.md#isomorphism). Iterating this [dimension shifting in sheaf cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#dimension-shifting-in-sheaf-cohomology) gives

$$
H^k(X,F)\cong H^{k+1}(X,K_1)\cong\cdots\cong H^{k+s}(X,K_s).
$$

Choose $s\geq0$ with $k+s>N$, where $N$ is the uniform bound from [b](#3/b). The [coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#coherent-sheaf) $K_s$ is a [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf), so [b](#3/b) makes the last group zero. Therefore

$$
\boxed{H^k(X,F)=0\quad\text{for every coherent sheaf }F\text{ and every }k>M.}
$$

The argument uses a finite initial segment of a [locally free resolution](../sheaf-of-modules.md#locally-free-resolution). There is no need to assume that every [coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#coherent-sheaf) has a finite [locally free resolution](../sheaf-of-modules.md#locally-free-resolution): the uniform bound from [b](#3/b) is what terminates the argument.
