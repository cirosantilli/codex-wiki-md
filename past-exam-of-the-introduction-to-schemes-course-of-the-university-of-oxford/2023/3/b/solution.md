<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Suppose, to the contrary, that $H^1(X,\mathcal F)=0$ for every [quasi-coherent sheaf](../../../../quasi-coherent-sheaf.md) $\mathcal F$. Since $X$ is a [quasi-compact scheme](../../../../quasi-compact-scheme.md), part (a) makes it an [affine scheme](../../../../affine-scheme.md).

Every [affine scheme](../../../../affine-scheme.md) $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ is a [separated scheme](../../../../separated-scheme.md): its [diagonal morphism](../../../../diagonal-morphism.md) over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ is induced by the surjective [ring homomorphism](../../../../ring-homomorphism.md) $A\otimes_{\mathbb Z}A\to A$, $a\otimes b\mapsto ab$, so it is a [closed immersion](../../../../closed-immersion.md). This contradicts the assumption that $X$ is not a [separated scheme](../../../../separated-scheme.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{There exists a quasi-coherent }\mathcal F\text{ with }H^1(X,\mathcal F)\ne0.}
$$

In fact, the version of the [Serre criterion for affineness](../../../../cohomological-criterion-for-affineness.md) using ideals shows that $\mathcal F$ may be chosen to be a [quasi-coherent sheaf](../../../../quasi-coherent-sheaf.md) of ideals. This argument does not use a comparison with [Čech cohomology](../../../../cech-cohomology.md) on the nonseparated [scheme](../../../../scheme.md).
