<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

Write $h_T(S)=\operatorname{Hom}(S,T)$ for the [functor of points](../locally-ringed-space.md#functor-represented-by-a-scheme) of a [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme) $T$. A [subfunctor](../functor.md#subfunctor) $H\subseteq F$ is an [open subfunctor](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-subfunctor) if every [natural transformation](../functor.md#natural-transformation) $h_T\to F$ has a [pullback](../category.md#pullback-category-theory) represented by an [open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-subscheme) $U\subseteq T$:

$$
\boxed{h_T\times_F H\cong h_U,\qquad U\hookrightarrow T\text{ an open immersion}.}
$$

The displayed [natural isomorphism](../functor.md#natural-isomorphism) must identify the projection to $h_T$ with the [natural transformation](../functor.md#natural-transformation) induced by $U\hookrightarrow T$. Equivalently, for each $\xi\in F(T)$, there is an [open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-subscheme) $U\subseteq T$ such that a [morphism of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) $a:S\to T$ factors through $U$ exactly when $F(a)(\xi)\in H(S)$. The [pullback](../category.md#pullback-category-theory) is taken in the [functor category](../functor.md#functor-category), so this tests arbitrary families, rather than just underlying points.

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

A [Zariski cover](../algebraic-geometry.md#zariski-cover) of a [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme) $T$ is a family of [open immersions](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-immersion) $(j_i:U_i\hookrightarrow T)_{i\in I}$ whose images cover its underlying [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space):

$$
\boxed{T=\bigcup_{i\in I}j_i(U_i).}
$$

There is no finiteness requirement. Intersections used in the [sheaf on a site](../functor.md#sheaf-on-a-site) condition are the [pullbacks](../category.md#pullback-category-theory) $U_i\times_TU_j$, identified with the corresponding [open subschemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-subscheme) $U_i\cap U_j$.

<h4 id="1/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/a/iii)

A [presheaf](../algebraic-geometry.md#presheaf-of-sets-on-a-topological-space) $F:\mathsf{Sch}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Set}$ is a [Zariski sheaf](../functor.md#zariski-sheaf) when every [Zariski cover](../algebraic-geometry.md#zariski-cover) $(U_i\to T)$ gives an [equalizer](../category.md#equaliser) diagram

$$
\boxed{F(T)\longrightarrow\prod_iF(U_i)\rightrightarrows\prod_{i,j}F(U_i\times_TU_j).}
$$

Thus compatible local elements glue to a unique element of $F(T)$. Compatibility means that the two restrictions agree on every intersection. This includes the empty [Zariski cover](../algebraic-geometry.md#zariski-cover) of the empty [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme), so $F(\varnothing)$ is a singleton.

<h4 id="1/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#1/a/iv)

The [open affine representability criterion](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-affine-representability-criterion) says that $F$ is a [functor represented by a scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#functor-represented-by-a-scheme) exactly when $F$ is a [Zariski sheaf](../functor.md#zariski-sheaf) and admits a family of [open subfunctors](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-subfunctor) $F_i\subseteq F$, each represented by an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme), which covers $F$ in the following sense: for every [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme) $T$ and every $\xi\in F(T)$, the [open subschemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-subscheme) representing $h_T\times_FF_i$ cover $T$. In particular, the covering condition does not demand that every $\xi$ lie in one $F_i(T)$.

In symbols, for [affine schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme) $X_i$,

$$
\boxed{F_i\cong h_{X_i},\quad F_i\subseteq F\text{ open},\quad \bigcup_i(T\times_FF_i)=T,\quad F\text{ a Zariski sheaf}.}
$$

The family is indexed by a set. For necessity, take an [affine open cover](../topology.md#affine-open-cover) of a representing [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme); [morphisms of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) glue over a [Zariski cover](../algebraic-geometry.md#zariski-cover). For sufficiency, $F_i\times_FF_j$ is represented by an [open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-subscheme) of each $X_i,X_j$. The two descriptions identify these overlaps and satisfy the compatibility on triple overlaps because all maps come from the inclusions into $F$. Apply [gluing of schemes along open subschemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#gluing-of-schemes-along-open-subschemes) to obtain $X$. Local identification gives $h_X\to F$, and the [Zariski sheaf](../functor.md#zariski-sheaf) condition makes this a [natural isomorphism](../functor.md#natural-isomorphism): its elements can be constructed and compared on the pulled-back [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

A [morphism of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) $f:X\to Y$ defines a [natural transformation](../functor.md#natural-transformation) $g_f:g_X\to g_Y$ by $u\mapsto f\circ u$. We construct the inverse correspondence.

Let $\alpha:g_X\to g_Y$ be a [natural transformation](../functor.md#natural-transformation), and choose an [affine open cover](../topology.md#affine-open-cover) $(j_i:U_i\hookrightarrow X)$. Regard $j_i$ as an element of $g_X(U_i)$ and set

$$
f_i=\alpha_{U_i}(j_i):U_i\longrightarrow Y.
$$

Here and below the [equivalence of categories](../functor.md#equivalence-of-categories) in the question lets us write the restricted [functor of points](../locally-ringed-space.md#functor-represented-by-a-scheme) on [affine schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme), contravariantly, rather than on [commutative rings](../commutative-algebra.md#commutative-ring), covariantly. If $W\subseteq U_i\cap U_j$ is an [affine open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-open-subscheme), [naturality](../functor.md#naturality) for its two inclusions gives

$$
f_i|_W=\alpha_W(W\hookrightarrow X)=f_j|_W.
$$

Such $W$ cover the overlap even when that overlap is not an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme). Consequently the $f_i$ agree and glue to a [morphism of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) $f:X\to Y$.

It remains to check all elements of the restricted [functor of points](../locally-ringed-space.md#functor-represented-by-a-scheme), not only the chosen inclusions. Given an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme) $T$ and $u:T\to X$, cover each $u^{-1}(U_i)$ by [affine open subschemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-open-subscheme) $V$. On such a $V$, factor $u|_V=j_i\circ v$. Applying [naturality](../functor.md#naturality) first to $v:V\to U_i$ and then to $V\hookrightarrow T$ gives

$$
\alpha_T(u)|_V=\alpha_V(u|_V)=\alpha_{U_i}(j_i)\circ v=f\circ u|_V.
$$

These local equalities prove $\alpha_T(u)=f\circ u$. The glued $f$ is unique, since its restrictions to all $U_i$ are prescribed. Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Sch}}(X,Y)\cong\operatorname{Nat}(g_X,g_Y),}
$$

so the assignment is [fully faithful](../functor.md#full-and-faithful-functor).

If $g_X\cong g_Y$, the [natural isomorphism](../functor.md#natural-isomorphism) and its inverse correspond to [morphisms of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) $f:X\to Y$ and $h:Y\to X$. Their compositions induce the identity [natural transformations](../functor.md#natural-transformation), and faithfulness implies $hf=\operatorname{id}_X$, $fh=\operatorname{id}_Y$. Conversely, an [isomorphism of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#isomorphism-of-schemes) induces a [natural isomorphism](../functor.md#natural-isomorphism). Thus

$$
\boxed{X\cong Y\iff g_X\cong g_Y.}
$$

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/c/i)

For an [idempotent](../commutative-algebra.md#idempotent) $e\in R$, define the [principal open subset](../locally-ringed-space.md#principal-open-subscheme) $D(e)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R$. Since $e(1-e)=0$, a [prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) contains at least one of $e,1-e$, and it cannot contain both. Therefore

$$
D(e)=V(1-e),\qquad \operatorname{Spec}R=D(e)\sqcup D(1-e).
$$

Thus $D(e)$ is a [clopen set](../topology.md#clopen-set).

Conversely, let $C$ be a [clopen set](../topology.md#clopen-set) in $T=\operatorname{Spec}R$. The [structure sheaf](../locally-ringed-space.md#structure-sheaf-of-a-scheme) glues the section $1$ on $C$ and the section $0$ on its complement to a unique [global section](../sheaf-of-modules.md#global-section) $e\in\Gamma(T,\mathcal O_T)=R$. Checking on these two [open subsets](../topology.md#open-set) gives $e^2=e$, and $D(e)=C$. Conversely, the restriction of an [idempotent](../commutative-algebra.md#idempotent) $e$ to $D(e)$ is $1$ because $e$ is a [unit](../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring) there, and its restriction to $D(1-e)$ is $0$. Thus the two constructions are inverse, including for the zero [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring):

$$
\boxed{\{e\in R:e^2=e\}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \{C\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R:C\text{ clopen}\},\qquad e\longmapsto D(e).}
$$

<h4 id="1/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/c/ii)

Label the two charts of the [affine line with doubled origin](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-line-with-doubled-origin) by $A,B$, and use $t$ for their common coordinate. Let $u:T=\operatorname{Spec}R\to X$ be an $R$-valued element of the [functor of points](../locally-ringed-space.md#functor-represented-by-a-scheme). Its composite with $\phi$ corresponds to a unique $r\in R$ under the [affine-target adjunction for schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-target-adjunction-for-schemes):

$$
\phi u:T\longrightarrow\mathbb A^1_{\mathbb Z},\qquad t\longmapsto r.
$$

The [closed subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-subscheme) $Z=V(r)=\operatorname{Spec}(R/(r))$ maps into the disjoint union of the two zero sections,

$$
X\times_{\mathbb A^1_{\mathbb Z}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z
=A_0\sqcup B_0,\qquad A_0\cong B_0\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z.
$$

The inverse image $Z_A$ of $A_0$ is a [clopen set](../topology.md#clopen-set) in $Z$. By the [idempotent–clopen correspondence](../locally-ringed-space.md#idempotent-clopen-correspondence), it determines a unique [idempotent](../commutative-algebra.md#idempotent) $e\in R/(r)$, equal to $1$ on $Z_A$ and $0$ on the inverse image $Z_B$ of $B_0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{u\longmapsto(r,e)\in F(R),\qquad D_Z(e)=Z_A.}
$$

Over $\mathbb Z$, the two “origins” are entire zero sections, rather than single closed points; this construction uses their full [scheme-theoretic fibers](../algebraic-variety.md#scheme-theoretic-fiber).

<h4 id="1/c/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/c/iii)

Let $T=\operatorname{Spec}R$ and $Z=\operatorname{Spec}(R/(r))$. The [idempotent](../commutative-algebra.md#idempotent) $e$ determines the disjoint [clopen sets](../topology.md#clopen-set) $Z_A=D_Z(e)$ and $Z_B=D_Z(1-e)$. Each is closed in $T$. Define the [open subsets](../topology.md#open-set)

$$
T_A=T\setminus Z_B,\qquad T_B=T\setminus Z_A.
$$

They cover $T$ and satisfy $T_A\cap T_B=T\setminus Z=D(r)$. The [global section](../sheaf-of-modules.md#global-section) $r$ defines [morphisms of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) $T_A\to A$ and $T_B\to B$, both sending the coordinate $t$ to $r$. On their intersection $r$ is a [unit](../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring), so both maps land in the common [open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-subscheme) $A\cap B=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$, where they agree. They therefore glue uniquely:

$$
\boxed{u_{r,e}:T\longrightarrow X.}
$$

For an entirely explicit description, choose any lift $a\in R$ of $e$. Then $a(1-a)\in(r)$ and

$$
T_A=D(r)\cup D(a),\qquad T_B=D(r)\cup D(1-a).
$$

The equality $a+(1-a)=1$ proves that these [open subsets](../topology.md#open-set) cover. The inclusion $D(a(1-a))\subseteq D(r)$ proves that their intersection is exactly $D(r)$. Their description as complements of $Z_A,Z_B$ proves independence of the chosen lift. In particular, no lift of $e$ to an [idempotent](../commutative-algebra.md#idempotent) of $R$ is required.

<h4 id="1/c/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#1/c/iv)

Starting from $(r,e)$, the construction in (iii) preserves the composite to the [affine line](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-line) and sends $Z_A$ to the $A$ zero section and $Z_B$ to the $B$ zero section. Part (ii) therefore recovers $(r,e)$.

Conversely, start with $u:T\to X$ and construct $(r,e)$. The only points of $X$ outside $A$ lie on $B_0$, and the only points outside $B$ lie on $A_0$. Consequently $u^{-1}(A)=T\setminus Z_B=T_A$ and $u^{-1}(B)=T\setminus Z_A=T_B$. On each of these [open subsets](../topology.md#open-set), $u$ is the unique [morphism of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) to the appropriate [affine line](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-line) with coordinate $r$. Thus part (iii) recovers $u$.

Finally, a [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $R\to R'$ sends $r$ to $r'$ and induces $R/(r)\to R'/(r')$, sending $e$ to an [idempotent](../commutative-algebra.md#idempotent) $e'$. Inverse images of the [clopen sets](../topology.md#clopen-set) $Z_A,Z_B$ are precisely those determined by $e'$. The constructions therefore commute with this [base change](../locally-ringed-space.md#base-change-of-a-morphism-of-schemes). We have a [natural bijection](../functor.md#natural-bijection)

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Sch}}(\operatorname{Spec}R,X)\cong F(R),}
$$

so $F$ is a [functor represented by a scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#functor-represented-by-a-scheme), represented by the [affine line with doubled origin](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-line-with-doubled-origin). The representing [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme) is not required to be an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme).

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

For every [valuation ring](../commutative-algebra.md#valuation-ring) $D$ with [fraction field](../commutative-algebra.md#field-of-fractions) $K$, consider compatible [morphisms of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes)

$$
u:\operatorname{Spec}K\to X,\qquad v:\operatorname{Spec}D\to S,\qquad fu=v|_{\operatorname{Spec}K}.
$$

An extension is a [morphism of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) $\ell:\operatorname{Spec}D\to X$ satisfying $\ell|_{\operatorname{Spec}K}=u$ and $f\ell=v$. The [valuative criterion for universal closedness](../locally-ringed-space.md#valuative-criterion-for-universal-closedness) states

$$
\boxed{f\text{ universally closed}\iff f\text{ quasi-compact and every such pair admits an extension}.}
$$

Equivalently, among [quasi-compact morphisms](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-morphism), the existence condition characterizes [universal closedness](../locally-ringed-space.md#universally-closed-morphism). A [universally closed morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#universally-closed-morphism) is automatically a [quasi-compact morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-morphism); the converse direction of the criterion needs this compactness condition. All [valuation rings](../commutative-algebra.md#valuation-ring) are allowed, with arbitrary [fraction fields](../commutative-algebra.md#field-of-fractions); there is no [Noetherian ring](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) hypothesis and one cannot replace them by [discrete valuation rings](../commutative-algebra.md#discrete-valuation-ring) without additional assumptions.[Stacks Project, valuative criterion of universal closedness](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01KF)[Stacks Project, universally closed morphisms are quasi-compact](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04XU)

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

Use the compatible pair $u,v$ and the notion of extension from (i). The [valuative criterion for separatedness](../locally-ringed-space.md#valuative-criterion-for-separatedness) is

$$
\boxed{f\text{ separated}\iff f\text{ quasi-separated and every such pair has at most one extension}.}
$$

A [quasi-separated morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-separated-morphism) means one whose [diagonal morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#diagonal-morphism) is a [quasi-compact morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-morphism). A [separated morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-morphism) automatically has this property because its [diagonal morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#diagonal-morphism) is a [closed immersion](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-immersion). The [quasi-separated morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-separated-morphism) hypothesis is needed in the converse valuative test for arbitrary [schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme). Existence is not demanded.[Stacks Project, valuative criterion of separatedness](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01KY)

<h4 id="2/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/a/iii)

With the same compatible pair $u,v$, the [valuative criterion for properness](../locally-ringed-space.md#valuative-criterion-for-properness) is

$$
\boxed{f\text{ proper}\iff f\text{ is of finite type and quasi-separated, and every pair has exactly one extension}.}
$$

Equivalently, one can replace [finite type](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-finite-type) by being a [quasi-compact morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-morphism) and [locally of finite type](../locally-ringed-space.md#locally-of-finite-type). Necessity follows from the definition of a [proper morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#proper-morphism) and the existence and uniqueness criteria in (i) and (ii). Conversely, [finite type](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-finite-type) supplies the compactness needed in (i), while being a [quasi-separated morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-separated-morphism) supplies the compactness of the [diagonal morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#diagonal-morphism) needed in (ii). Thus the extension condition gives both [universal closedness](../locally-ringed-space.md#universally-closed-morphism) and [separatedness](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-morphism), and these together with [finite type](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-finite-type) give a [proper morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#proper-morphism). These hypotheses are part of the criteria, even though the question is phrased for unrestricted [morphisms of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes).[Stacks Project, valuative criterion for properness](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BX5)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Let $S'\to S$ be a [morphism of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) and set $X'=X\times_SS'$, with projection $f':X'\to S'$. Given compatible [morphisms of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) $u':\operatorname{Spec}K\to X'$ and $v':\operatorname{Spec}D\to S'$, compose them with the projections to obtain a valuative problem for $f$. An extension $\ell:\operatorname{Spec}D\to X$ for that problem and the prescribed $v'$ determine a unique [morphism of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes)

$$
\ell'=(\ell,v'):\operatorname{Spec}D\longrightarrow X\times_SS'.
$$

The [universal property of a fibre product](../locally-ringed-space.md#universal-property-of-a-fibre-product) shows that $\ell'$ extends $u'$ and lies over $v'$. Conversely, every extension $\ell'$ has such a first component $\ell$. Thus the sets of extensions in the two problems are in [bijection](../function.md#bijection).

If $f$ is a [universally closed morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#universally-closed-morphism), it is a [quasi-compact morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-morphism) and satisfies existence. The allowed stability of [quasi-compact morphisms](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-morphism) under [base change](../locally-ringed-space.md#base-change-of-a-morphism-of-schemes) and (a)(i) prove that $f'$ is a [universally closed morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#universally-closed-morphism). If $f$ is a [separated morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-morphism), it is a [quasi-separated morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-separated-morphism) and satisfies uniqueness. Stability of [quasi-separated morphisms](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-separated-morphism) under [base change](../locally-ringed-space.md#base-change-of-a-morphism-of-schemes) and (a)(ii) prove that $f'$ is a [separated morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-morphism).

If $f$ is a [proper morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#proper-morphism), the same [bijection](../function.md#bijection) supplies existence and uniqueness. The permitted stability under [base change](../locally-ringed-space.md#base-change-of-a-morphism-of-schemes) of [quasi-compact morphisms](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-morphism), [quasi-separated morphisms](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-separated-morphism) and [locally of finite type](../locally-ringed-space.md#locally-of-finite-type) supplies all remaining hypotheses of (a)(iii). Hence

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
f\text{ universally closed}&\Longrightarrow f'\text{ universally closed},\\
f\text{ separated}&\Longrightarrow f'\text{ separated},\\
f\text{ proper}&\Longrightarrow f'\text{ proper}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Let $X\xrightarrow fY\xrightarrow gS$ be [morphisms of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes). Start with $u:\operatorname{Spec}K\to X$ and $v:\operatorname{Spec}D\to S$ satisfying $gfu=v|_{\operatorname{Spec}K}$.

For existence, first apply the extension condition for $g$ to $fu$ and $v$, obtaining $w:\operatorname{Spec}D\to Y$. Then apply the extension condition for $f$ to $u,w$, obtaining $\ell:\operatorname{Spec}D\to X$. This $\ell$ solves the problem for $gf$. If both $f,g$ are [universally closed morphisms](../locally-ringed-space.md#universally-closed-morphism), both are [quasi-compact morphisms](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-morphism); their composite is a [quasi-compact morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-morphism) by the allowed stability fact. Part (a)(i) therefore proves that $gf$ is a [universally closed morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#universally-closed-morphism).

For uniqueness, suppose $\ell_1,\ell_2$ are extensions for $gf$. Uniqueness for $g$ gives $f\ell_1=f\ell_2=w$, and uniqueness for $f$, now over this same $w$, gives $\ell_1=\ell_2$. If $f,g$ are [separated morphisms](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-morphism), their composite is a [quasi-separated morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-separated-morphism) by the allowed stability fact. Part (a)(ii) proves that $gf$ is a [separated morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-morphism).

Finally, if $f,g$ are [proper morphisms](../locally-ringed-space.md#proper-morphism), these arguments give both existence and uniqueness. Stability under composition of [quasi-compact morphisms](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-morphism), [quasi-separated morphisms](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-separated-morphism) and [locally of finite type](../locally-ringed-space.md#locally-of-finite-type) gives the other hypotheses of (a)(iii). Consequently

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
f,g\text{ universally closed}&\Longrightarrow gf\text{ universally closed},\\
f,g\text{ separated}&\Longrightarrow gf\text{ separated},\\
f,g\text{ proper}&\Longrightarrow gf\text{ proper}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Let $x\in\operatorname{Frac}(D)$ satisfy a [monic polynomial](../polynomial.md#monic-polynomial) equation

$$
x^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0=0,\qquad a_i\in D.
$$

If $x=0$, there is nothing to prove. Suppose $x\notin D$. The defining property of a [valuation ring](../commutative-algebra.md#valuation-ring) gives $y=x^{-1}\in D$. Multiplying the equation by $y^{n-1}$ yields

$$
x=-a_{n-1}-a_{n-2}y-\cdots-a_0y^{n-1}\in D,
$$

a contradiction. Therefore

$$
\boxed{D\text{ is an integrally closed domain}.}
$$

This proof uses only the comparison of an element with its inverse; no discreteness or finite generation of the [valuation](../algebra.md#valuation) is needed.

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

Let $D$ be a [valuation ring](../commutative-algebra.md#valuation-ring), $K$ its [fraction field](../commutative-algebra.md#field-of-fractions), and let $u:\operatorname{Spec}K\to X$, $v:\operatorname{Spec}D\to S$ be a compatible pair. Since $D$ is a [local ring](../commutative-algebra.md#local-ring), every [open subset](../topology.md#open-set) of $\operatorname{Spec}D$ containing its unique [closed point](../topology.md#closed-point) is the whole space: a [principal open subset](../locally-ringed-space.md#principal-open-subscheme) containing the [maximal ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) is defined by a [unit](../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring). Choose an [affine open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-open-subscheme) $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq S$ containing the image under $v$ of that [closed point](../topology.md#closed-point). The entire $v$ factors through $V$.

Because $f$ is a [finite morphism](../algebraic-variety.md#finite-morphism), $f^{-1}(V)=\operatorname{Spec}B$ with $B$ a finite $A$-[module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics). The valuative problem is the compatible [ring homomorphisms](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism)

$$
A\longrightarrow D\subseteq K,\qquad \psi:B\longrightarrow K.
$$

Every $b\in B$ is an [integral element](../commutative-algebra.md#integral-element) over $A$. Mapping its [monic polynomial](../polynomial.md#monic-polynomial) equation to $K$ proves that $\psi(b)$ is an [integral element](../commutative-algebra.md#integral-element) over $D$. Part (c) gives $\psi(b)\in D$. Thus $\psi$ factors uniquely as a [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $B\to D\hookrightarrow K$, producing the unique extension $\operatorname{Spec}D\to X$.

For completeness, a [finite morphism](../algebraic-variety.md#finite-morphism) is a [quasi-compact morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-morphism) and of [finite type](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-finite-type): over $V$ its inverse image is an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme), and finite $A$-[module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) generators also generate it as an $A$-[algebra](../algebra.md). It is also a [separated morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-morphism). Affine-locally its [diagonal morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#diagonal-morphism) corresponds to the surjective multiplication [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism)

$$
B\otimes_AB\longrightarrow B,\qquad b\otimes c\longmapsto bc,
$$

and hence is a [closed immersion](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-immersion). In particular it is a [quasi-separated morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-separated-morphism). The [valuative criterion for properness](../locally-ringed-space.md#valuative-criterion-for-properness) now applies, giving

$$
\boxed{f\text{ finite}\Longrightarrow f\text{ proper}.}
$$

<h3 id="2/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#2/e)

Write $X=\operatorname{Spec}B$, $S=\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $A,B$ [integral domains](../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain), let $\alpha:A\to B$ be the [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism), and put $K=\operatorname{Frac}(B)$. We do not assume that $\alpha$ is injective. Write $A'=\alpha(A)\subseteq B\subseteq K$.

Let $D\subseteq K$ be any [valuation ring](../commutative-algebra.md#valuation-ring) containing $A'$, with [fraction field](../commutative-algebra.md#field-of-fractions) $K$. The canonical [morphism of schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) $\operatorname{Spec}K\to\operatorname{Spec}B$ and the [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $A\to A'\hookrightarrow D$ form a valuative problem for $f$. Since $f$ is a [proper morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#proper-morphism), there is an extension. On [coordinate rings](../algebraic-variety.md#coordinate-ring) it is a [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $\beta:B\to D$ such that

$$
B\xrightarrow{\beta}D\hookrightarrow K
$$

is the original inclusion of $B$ into $K$. Therefore every element of $B$ lies in $D$. Since $D$ was arbitrary, the stated description of [integral closure as an intersection of valuation rings](../commutative-algebra.md#integral-closure-as-an-intersection-of-valuation-rings) gives

$$
B\subseteq\bigcap_{\substack{D\subseteq K\text{ valuation ring}\\ A'\subseteq D}}D
=\overline{A'}^{\,K}.
$$

Thus every element of $B$ is an [integral element](../commutative-algebra.md#integral-element) over $A'$, and hence over $A$: lift the coefficients of its [monic polynomial](../polynomial.md#monic-polynomial) along the surjection $A\to A'$. The [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $\alpha$ is therefore an [integral extension](../commutative-algebra.md#integral-extension) in the sense of the question.

A [proper morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#proper-morphism) is of [finite type](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-finite-type), so in this affine situation $B=A[b_1,\ldots,b_s]$. If $b_i$ satisfies a [monic polynomial](../polynomial.md#monic-polynomial) of degree $n_i$, repeatedly reducing its powers shows that the finitely many [monomials](../polynomial.md#monomial)

$$
b_1^{e_1}\cdots b_s^{e_s},\qquad 0\leq e_i<n_i,
$$

generate $B$ as an $A$-[module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics). Equivalently, apply the allowed equivalence between finite and integral plus [finite type](../locally-ringed-space.md#morphism-of-finite-type). Hence

$$
\boxed{f\text{ proper between affine integral schemes}\Longrightarrow f\text{ finite}.}
$$

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

For a [quasi-compact scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-scheme) $X$, the [Serre criterion for affineness](../locally-ringed-space.md#cohomological-criterion-for-affineness) gives the equivalences

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
X\text{ affine}
&\iff H^1(X,\mathcal F)=0\text{ for every quasi-coherent }\mathcal O_X\text{-module }\mathcal F\\
&\iff H^q(X,\mathcal F)=0\text{ for every such }\mathcal F\text{ and every }q>0.
\end{aligned}}
$$

Indeed, an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme) has [vanishing of quasi-coherent cohomology on an affine scheme](../sheaf-of-modules.md#vanishing-of-quasi-coherent-cohomology-on-an-affine-scheme). Conversely, vanishing of the first [sheaf cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#sheaf-cohomology) for every [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf) includes every [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf) of ideals, which suffices for the criterion. No [separated scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-scheme) hypothesis is needed in this formulation. Nor may one test only the [structure sheaf](../locally-ringed-space.md#structure-sheaf-of-a-scheme).[Stacks Project, Serre's criterion for a quasi-compact scheme, using quasi-coherent ideals](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01XF)

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Suppose, to the contrary, that $H^1(X,\mathcal F)=0$ for every [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf) $\mathcal F$. Since $X$ is a [quasi-compact scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-scheme), part (a) makes it an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme).

Every [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme) $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ is a [separated scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-scheme): its [diagonal morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#diagonal-morphism) over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ is induced by the surjective [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $A\otimes_{\mathbb Z}A\to A$, $a\otimes b\mapsto ab$, so it is a [closed immersion](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-immersion). This contradicts the assumption that $X$ is not a [separated scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-scheme). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{There exists a quasi-coherent }\mathcal F\text{ with }H^1(X,\mathcal F)\ne0.}
$$

In fact, the version of the [Serre criterion for affineness](../locally-ringed-space.md#cohomological-criterion-for-affineness) using ideals shows that $\mathcal F$ may be chosen to be a [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf) of ideals. This argument does not use a comparison with [Čech cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#cech-cohomology) on the nonseparated [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme).

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Let $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $V=\operatorname{Spec}B$ be [affine open subschemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-open-subscheme) of the [separated scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-scheme) $X$. Its [diagonal morphism](../locally-ringed-space.md#diagonal-morphism) is a [closed immersion](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-immersion)

$$
\Delta:X\hookrightarrow X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}X.
$$

Restricting this [closed immersion](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-immersion) to the [open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-subscheme) $U\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}V$ identifies its inverse image with $U\cap V$. Hence

$$
U\cap V\hookrightarrow U\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}V
=\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_{\mathbb Z}B)
$$

is a [closed immersion](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-immersion). A [closed subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-subscheme) of an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme) is an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme), so

$$
\boxed{U\cap V\text{ is an affine open subscheme of }X.}
$$

The argument iterates to every finite intersection of [affine open subschemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-open-subscheme). The empty intersection of two disjoint charts causes no exception: the empty [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme) is $\operatorname{Spec}(0)$.

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

Suppose $X=U_1\cup\cdots\cup U_n$ with each $U_i$ an [affine open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-open-subscheme). Every [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme) is a [quasi-compact topological space](../topology.md#compact-space): a cover by [principal open subsets](../locally-ringed-space.md#principal-open-subscheme) $D(f_\lambda)$ means that the $f_\lambda$ generate the [unit ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#unit-ideal); an expression $1=\sum_{j=1}^r a_jf_{\lambda_j}$ gives a finite subcover. Every [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover) has a refinement by [principal open subsets](../locally-ringed-space.md#principal-open-subscheme), so this proves the general covering property.

Restrict an arbitrary [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover) of $X$ to each $U_i$ and choose a finite subcover there. The union of these finitely many finite families covers $X$. Thus $X$ is a [quasi-compact scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-scheme).

Now assume $X$ is a [separated scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-scheme). By (c), every finite intersection of the $U_i$ is an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme), and hence has vanishing positive [sheaf cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#sheaf-cohomology) for the [structure sheaf](../locally-ringed-space.md#structure-sheaf-of-a-scheme). This [affine open cover](../topology.md#affine-open-cover) therefore computes [sheaf cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#sheaf-cohomology) using its [Čech cochain complex](../sheaf-of-modules.md#cech-cochain-complex). Its degree $p$ term is

$$
\check C^p=\prod_{1\leq i_0<\cdots<i_p\leq n}\Gamma(U_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap U_{i_p},\mathcal O_X).
$$

There are no strictly increasing $(p+1)$-tuples when $p\geq n$, so those [Čech cochain groups](../sheaf-of-modules.md#cech-cochain-group) vanish. Consequently

$$
\boxed{H^m(X,\mathcal O_X)=0\qquad(m\geq n).}
$$

The same argument works for every [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf). The relevant comparison requires the acyclicity of the finite intersections; the bare assertion that arbitrary [Čech cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#cech-cohomology) equals [sheaf cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#sheaf-cohomology) would be too broad.

**Interpretation of the removed locus.** Over $\mathbb Z$, the notation $[1:0:0]$ specifies a section $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb Z}$, with image $V_+(x_1,x_2)\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$. We remove this entire [closed subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#closed-subscheme). This is the interpretation forced by the asserted [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover) $X=U_1\cup U_2$. Removing one closed point in a particular fiber would not give that [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover). The native PDF uses the same notation as the transcription.

Put $a=x_0/x_1$, $b=x_2/x_1$ on $U_1$. On $U_2$ put $c=x_0/x_2$, $d=x_1/x_2$. On their intersection $c=a/b$ and $d=b^{-1}$, giving [coordinate rings](../algebraic-variety.md#coordinate-ring)

$$
\Gamma(U_1,\mathcal O_X)=\mathbb Z[a,b],\qquad
\Gamma(U_2,\mathcal O_X)=\mathbb Z[a/b,b^{-1}],\qquad
\Gamma(U_1\cap U_2,\mathcal O_X)=L:=\mathbb Z[a,b,b^{-1}].
$$

The [Čech cochain complex](../sheaf-of-modules.md#cech-cochain-complex) for the [structure sheaf](../locally-ringed-space.md#structure-sheaf-of-a-scheme), using increasing distinct indices, is

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z[a,b]\oplus\mathbb Z[a/b,b^{-1}]
\xrightarrow{\delta}L\longrightarrow0,\qquad
\delta(f,g)=g-f.
$$

Thus $\check H^0$ is the intersection of the two subrings of $L$, while $\check H^1$ is the [quotient module](../module-theory.md#quotient-module) of $L$ by their additive sum.

The [Laurent polynomial ring](../commutative-algebra.md#laurent-polynomial-ring) $L$ is a free [abelian group](../group.md#abelian-group) on the [monomials](../polynomial.md#monomial) $a^ib^j$ with $i\geq0$, $j\in\mathbb Z$. The first subring has the [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) $j\geq0$. The second has the [basis](../vector-space.md#basis)

$$
c^id^k=a^ib^{-i-k},\qquad i,k\geq0,
$$

so its allowed exponents satisfy $j\leq-i$. A [monomial](../polynomial.md#monomial) belongs to both precisely when $i=j=0$. The [monomials](../polynomial.md#monomial) belonging to neither satisfy $i\geq2$ and $-i<j<0$. Since the subgroups are spanned by subsets of the same [basis](../vector-space.md#basis), their sum introduces no other relations. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\check H^0(\{U_1,U_2\},\mathcal O_X)&=\mathbb Z,\\
\check H^1(\{U_1,U_2\},\mathcal O_X)&=\bigoplus_{i\geq2}\ \bigoplus_{k=1}^{i-1}\mathbb Z\,[a^ib^{-k}]\\
&=\bigoplus_{m,n\geq1}\mathbb Z\!\left[\frac{x_0^{m+n}}{x_1^mx_2^n}\right],\\
\check H^q(\{U_1,U_2\},\mathcal O_X)&=0\qquad(q\geq2).
\end{aligned}}
$$

Here the square brackets denote [cohomology classes](../cohomology.md#cohomology-class), and the [direct sums](../vector-space.md#direct-sum) are as [abelian groups](../group.md#abelian-group). In particular, $[a^2b^{-1}]=[x_0^2/(x_1x_2)]$ is a nonzero [cohomology class](../cohomology.md#cohomology-class). This [scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#scheme) is a [separated scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#separated-scheme), being an [open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#open-subscheme) of [projective space](../projective-space.md) over $\mathbb Z$, and is a [quasi-compact scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-scheme) by (d). Both charts and their intersection are [affine schemes](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme), so this [Čech cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#cech-cohomology) equals [sheaf cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#sheaf-cohomology). The nonzero first [sheaf cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#sheaf-cohomology) of the [quasi-coherent](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf) [structure sheaf](../locally-ringed-space.md#structure-sheaf-of-a-scheme) rules out an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme):

$$
\boxed{X\text{ is not affine}.}
$$

<h4 id="3/f/i">i</h4>

<h5 id="3/f/i/solution">Solution</h5>

A [Noetherian scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#noetherian-scheme) is a [quasi-compact scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-scheme), so choose a finite [affine open cover](../topology.md#affine-open-cover) $X=\bigcup_{i=1}^s\operatorname{Spec}A_i$ with all $A_i$ [Noetherian rings](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring). The [nilradical sheaf](../locally-ringed-space.md#nilradical-sheaf) restricts on this chart to the [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf) associated to $\operatorname{Nil}(A_i)$: the [nilradical](../commutative-algebra.md#nilradical) commutes with [localization](../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring).

By the allowed fact, choose $d_i\geq1$ such that $\operatorname{Nil}(A_i)^{d_i}=0$ and put $m=\max_i d_i$. On every chart, the [ideal sheaf](../locally-ringed-space.md#ideal-sheaf-of-a-closed-subscheme) $\mathcal N^m$ is zero, and hence it is zero globally. Higher powers are also zero. Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathcal N^d=0\qquad\text{for every }d\geq m.}
$$

If $X$ is empty, take $m=1$. The finite [affine open cover](../topology.md#affine-open-cover) is essential for choosing one bound valid everywhere.

<h4 id="3/f/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#3/f)

<h5 id="3/f/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/f/ii)

Let $i:X_{\mathrm{red}}\hookrightarrow X$ be the [reduced induced subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#reduced-induced-subscheme), with [structure sheaf](../locally-ringed-space.md#structure-sheaf-of-a-scheme) $\mathcal O_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}=\mathcal O_X/\mathcal N$. Its underlying map of [topological spaces](../topology.md#topological-space) is a [homeomorphism](../topology.md#homeomorphism), since every [prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) contains the [nilradical](../commutative-algebra.md#nilradical).

Put $\mathcal G=\mathcal F/\mathcal N\mathcal F$. Multiplication by $\mathcal N$ annihilates $\mathcal G$, so its action by $\mathcal O_X$ factors through $\mathcal O_X/\mathcal N$. Thus $\mathcal G$ is naturally a [sheaf of modules](../sheaf-of-modules.md) over $\mathcal O_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}$. On an [affine open subscheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-open-subscheme) $\operatorname{Spec}A$, with $\mathcal F=\widetilde M$, it corresponds to $M/\operatorname{Nil}(A)M$ over $A/\operatorname{Nil}(A)$, so it remains a [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf).

The [sheaf cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#sheaf-cohomology) of a [sheaf of modules](../sheaf-of-modules.md) is the [sheaf cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#sheaf-cohomology) of its underlying [sheaf of abelian groups](../algebraic-geometry.md#sheaf-of-abelian-groups). Under the above [homeomorphism](../topology.md#homeomorphism), that underlying sheaf is identical on the two spaces: the [global sections functor](../category-theory.md#global-sections-functor) and its [flasque resolutions](../sheaf-of-modules.md#flasque-resolution) are identical. Equivalently, use [sheaf cohomology under a closed inclusion](../sheaf-of-modules.md#sheaf-cohomology-under-a-closed-inclusion) with $i_*\mathcal G$. Therefore there are canonical [isomorphisms](../algebra.md#isomorphism), in every degree,

$$
\boxed{H^q(X,\mathcal F/\mathcal N\mathcal F)\cong H^q(X_{\mathrm{red}},\mathcal F/\mathcal N\mathcal F)\qquad(q\geq0).}
$$

In particular, this gives the requested equality in degree one. Equality of the underlying [topological spaces](../topology.md#topological-space) alone would not compare arbitrary coefficient sheaves; the factorization of the action explains why the indicated coefficients are the same.

<h4 id="3/f/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#3/f)

<h5 id="3/f/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/f/iii)

Let $\mathcal F$ be any [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf) on $X$, and choose $m$ from (i). Filter it by the powers of the [nilradical sheaf](../locally-ringed-space.md#nilradical-sheaf):

$$
\mathcal F\supseteq\mathcal N\mathcal F\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathcal N^m\mathcal F=0.
$$

Each subquotient

$$
\mathcal Q_j=\mathcal N^j\mathcal F/\mathcal N^{j+1}\mathcal F,\qquad 0\leq j<m,
$$

is a [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf) annihilated by $\mathcal N$. Apply (ii) to the [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf) $\mathcal N^j\mathcal F$. Since $X_{\mathrm{red}}$ is an [affine scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#affine-scheme), [vanishing of quasi-coherent cohomology on an affine scheme](../sheaf-of-modules.md#vanishing-of-quasi-coherent-cohomology-on-an-affine-scheme) gives

$$
H^1(X,\mathcal Q_j)\cong H^1(X_{\mathrm{red}},\mathcal Q_j)=0.
$$

The [short exact sequences](../module-theory.md#short-exact-sequence)

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathcal N^{j+1}\mathcal F\longrightarrow\mathcal N^j\mathcal F\longrightarrow\mathcal Q_j\longrightarrow0
$$

yield the segments of the [long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology](../sheaf-of-modules.md#long-exact-sequence-in-sheaf-cohomology)

$$
H^1(X,\mathcal N^{j+1}\mathcal F)\longrightarrow H^1(X,\mathcal N^j\mathcal F)\longrightarrow H^1(X,\mathcal Q_j)=0.
$$

Starting from $\mathcal N^m\mathcal F=0$, descending [mathematical induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) makes every middle group zero, finally giving $H^1(X,\mathcal F)=0$.

This holds for every [quasi-coherent sheaf](../sheaf-of-modules.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf) $\mathcal F$. Since a [Noetherian scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#noetherian-scheme) is a [quasi-compact scheme](../locally-ringed-space.md#quasi-compact-scheme), the [Serre criterion for affineness](../locally-ringed-space.md#cohomological-criterion-for-affineness) in (a) applies and proves

$$
\boxed{X_{\mathrm{red}}\text{ affine}\Longrightarrow X\text{ affine}.}
$$

This is an instance of the general fact that [affineness is invariant under nilpotent thickening](../locally-ringed-space.md#affineness-is-invariant-under-nilpotent-thickening).
