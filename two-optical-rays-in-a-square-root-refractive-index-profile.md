# Two optical rays in a square-root refractive-index profile

↑ **Parent:** [Fermat principle](fermat-principle.md)

For [refractive index](refractive-index.md) $n(y)=c\sqrt y$ with $c>0$, the optical-length integrand is $c\sqrt{y}\sqrt{1+y'^2}$. The [Beltrami identity](beltrami-identity.md) gives $\sqrt y/\sqrt{1+y'^2}=\sqrt h$, while the full [Euler-Lagrange equation](euler-lagrange-equation.md) gives $2yy''=1+y'^2$. Together these yield the displayed parabolas and exclude a spurious constant solution of the squared first integral. Equal-height endpoints $(\pm L,Y)$ force $x_0=0$ and $h^2-Yh+L^2/4=0$. There are two positive stationary rays for $Y>L$, one coalesced ray for $Y=L$, and none of this smooth family for $Y<L$. For $L=1,Y=5/4$, the two paths are $y=1+x^2/4$ and $y=1/4+x^2$. [Fermat principle](fermat-principle.md) requires stationary optical length, not a global minimum over every competing path.
