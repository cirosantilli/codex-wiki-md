[Anxiety disorders](../../../anxiety-disorder.md) centrally involve [cognition](../../../cognition.md), but describing them solely as disorders of [cognition](../../../cognition.md) is too strong unless the term is defined so broadly that it loses explanatory value. [Threat appraisal](../../../threat-appraisal.md), [attention](../../../attention.md), interpretation and [memory](../../../memory-in-animals.md) can contribute to excessive [fear](../../../fear.md) and [avoidance](../../../avoidance-behavior.md). Bodily [arousal](../../../physiological-arousal.md), [learning](../../../learning.md) history, action and the environment also matter. A useful account specifies how these processes interact and asks whether changing a proposed cognitive process changes [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md), rather than simply observing that anxious people report anxious thoughts.

[David M. Clark](../../../david-m-clark.md)'s cognitive model of panic illustrates the explanatory gain. Ordinary sensations such as a racing heart can be interpreted as evidence of imminent catastrophe. [Fear](../../../fear.md) increases [arousal](../../../physiological-arousal.md), which provides further sensations for threatening interpretation. The account distinguishes the bodily input from its meaning and explains why similar sensations need not produce the same [fear](../../../fear.md) in different circumstances. It also predicts that precautions and [avoidance](../../../avoidance-behavior.md) can prevent disconfirmation. The 1986 paper supplied a mechanistic model, not by itself proof that every panic episode requires a consciously articulated catastrophic thought.[Behaviour Research and Therapy 24, 461–470; catastrophic interpretation model, not a new clinical trial](../../../a-cognitive-approach-to-panic-by-david-m-clark.md)

The distinction between [cognition](../../../cognition.md) and explicit verbal reasoning matters. A person may orient rapidly toward threat, experience an image or respond to a bodily sensation before they can describe a proposition. Absence of a reported sentence does not establish absence of [information processing](../../../information-processing.md). Equally, a theory should not rescue itself from every negative finding by postulating an inaccessible [belief](../../../belief.md). It must specify observable predictions and measures that can fail, including what would count as successful alteration of the proposed process.

Cross-sectional evidence is insufficient to establish direction. A threat interpretation could increase [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md), [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md) could bias the interpretation, or a third factor could influence both. Questionnaires can also partly measure the same evaluative content on both sides of the association. Longitudinal prediction improves temporal ordering but leaves [confounding](../../../confounding.md) possible. Experiments that manipulate interpretation or [attention](../../../attention.md) therefore offer a stronger test, especially when changes in the intended process are verified separately from changes in [symptoms](../../../symptom.md).

[Andrew Mathews](../../../andrew-mathews.md) and [Bundy Mackintosh](../../../bundy-mackintosh.md) reported five experiments inducing relatively threatening or benign interpretations of ambiguous material. Interpretative bias could be changed, but parallel effects on state [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md) depended on active processing of personally relevant meanings. This supports a causal contribution under specified conditions and shows that a changed task response need not automatically mean changed emotional experience. These experiments do not establish that an interpretation-training task is a complete treatment for a diagnosed [anxiety disorder](../../../anxiety-disorder.md).[Journal of Abnormal Psychology 109, 602–615; five experiments, ambiguity training, active meaning generation and state anxiety](../../../induced-emotional-interpretation-bias-and-anxiety-by-andrew-mathews-and-bundy-mackintosh.md)

[Colin MacLeod](../../../colin-macleod.md) and colleagues manipulated selective [attention](../../../attention.md) using a modified dot-probe procedure. The location of a subsequent probe was used to train [attention](../../../attention.md) in different directions relative to emotional material, followed by a standardised stress task. Differences in emotional vulnerability support a contribution from [attention](../../../attention.md) rather than association alone. Transfer from a laboratory induction to enduring clinical change, however, remains an additional question. [Learning](../../../learning.md) a task contingency, altered expectancies and the reliability of the process measure all require consideration.[Journal of Abnormal Psychology 111, 107–123; modified dot-probe training and responses to a standardised stress task](../../../selective-attention-and-emotional-vulnerability-assessing-the-causal-basis-of-their-association-through-the-experimental-manipulation-of-attentional-bias-by-colin-macleod-and-others.md)

Clinical treatment comparisons provide complementary evidence. [David M. Clark](../../../david-m-clark.md) and colleagues compared [cognitive therapy](../../../cognitive-therapy.md), applied relaxation and imipramine in [panic disorder](../../../panic-disorder.md), with self-exposure homework shared across conditions. The findings supported [cognitive therapy](../../../cognitive-therapy.md) as a useful treatment. They do not make the comparison a pure test of thought change against an absence of [learning](../../../learning.md) or behavior: treatment packages differ in multiple ways, and a pharmacological treatment can also alter the sensations and experiences from which [beliefs](../../../belief.md) are formed.[British Journal of Psychiatry 164, 759–769; treatment comparison, common self-exposure homework and follow-up](../../../a-comparison-of-cognitive-therapy-applied-relaxation-and-imipramine-in-the-treatment-of-panic-disorder-by-david-m-clark-and-others.md)

[Safety behavior](../../../safety-behavior.md) research further shows why a sharp cognition-versus-behavior division can mislead. [Paul Salkovskis](../../../paul-salkovskis.md) and colleagues found that changing precautions during [exposure](../../../exposure-therapy.md) altered catastrophic [beliefs](../../../belief.md) and [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md). The behavior may control which evidence is encountered and how success is explained. Conversely, a different explanation may make abandoning the behavior possible. These reciprocal effects favour a specified feedback system over treating observable action and [mental representation](../../../mental-representation.md) as competing, self-contained causes.[Behaviour Research and Therapy 37, 559–574; brief exposure manipulation and repeated behavioural test](../../../an-experimental-investigation-of-the-role-of-safety-seeking-behaviours-in-the-maintenance-of-panic-disorder-with-agoraphobia-by-paul-salkovskis-and-others.md)

[Learning](../../../learning.md) accounts also need not exclude [cognition](../../../cognition.md). An [inhibitory learning](../../../inhibitory-learning.md) approach proposes that [exposure](../../../exposure-therapy.md) establishes alternative expectations rather than necessarily erasing the original threat association. Context-dependent retrieval can explain [return of fear](../../../return-of-fear.md) after apparently successful practice. [Michelle G. Craske](../../../michelle-g-craske.md) and colleagues developed a translational framework around this distinction. It offers a way to connect expectation, experience and later retrieval, while its clinical implementation requires direct evaluation rather than assuming that a [learning](../../../learning.md) rationale proves a particular protocol optimal.[Behaviour Research and Therapy 58, 10–23; inhibitory-learning framework and illustrative strategies, not a component trial](../../../maximizing-exposure-therapy-an-inhibitory-learning-approach-by-michelle-g-craske-and-others.md)

The broader phrase '[anxiety disorders](../../../anxiety-disorder.md)' also conceals heterogeneity. The feared consequence in social [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md) differs from the interpretation of bodily sensations in panic or persistent [worry](../../../worry.md) about many possible outcomes. A common [information processing](../../../information-processing.md) principle can coexist with disorder-relevant mechanisms. Actual danger and social adversity must be assessed: an expectation is not distorted simply because it concerns something distressing. Genetic, developmental and physiological influences can affect sensitivity to threat without fixing the meaning or outcome of every encounter.

Strong evidence for a particular cognitive mechanism would combine an experimental manipulation, a successful process check, subsequent change in [fear](../../../fear.md) and transfer to relevant everyday behavior. A [factorial design](../../../factorial-design.md) could vary interpretation and a maintaining precaution separately, with matched contact and [exposure](../../../exposure-therapy.md) opportunities. Repeated measurements should establish whether the cognitive change precedes the outcome. Treatment randomisation identifies the package effect, but [mediation](../../../statistical-mediation.md) requires additional assumptions because the [mediator](../../../causal-mediator.md) itself has not necessarily been [randomised](../../../randomization.md).

Evidence against an exclusive account would include effective treatment without the predicted process change, or a precise null effect on [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md) after a successful, sufficiently strong manipulation of that process. Such a result might constrain the population or context in which the mechanism matters rather than rule out all cognitive contributions. Measurement sensitivity and alternative pathways must be considered without making the theory unfalsifiable.

The defensible conclusion is that [cognition](../../../cognition.md) contributes materially to the persistence and modification of [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md), with experimental evidence beyond introspective report. It is less defensible to claim that all [anxiety disorders](../../../anxiety-disorder.md) are caused only by consciously held false [beliefs](../../../belief.md), or that the success of [cognitive therapy](../../../cognitive-therapy.md) establishes that exclusivity. Mechanistic precision and evidence about causal interaction are more informative than choosing between [cognition](../../../cognition.md), the body and behavior as if only one could matter.
