Conditioning explains an important part of [reexperiencing](../../../reexperiencing.md): a cue associated with a traumatic event can later trigger [fear](../../../fear.md) and involuntary recollection. It does not, by itself, fully explain the content, sensory qualities, sense of present occurrence or persistence of every intrusion. The best answer therefore separates a mechanism for triggering a response from a theory of what is represented and why the representation is retrieved in this particular form.

In a simple conditioning account, an initially neutral cue becomes associated with an aversive event. A similar cue encountered later can evoke defensive [arousal](../../../physiological-arousal.md) even when the danger is absent. [Stimulus generalisation](../../../stimulus-generalisation.md) explains reactions to reminders that only resemble the original cue, while [avoidance](../../../avoidance-behavior.md) can limit opportunities for [learning](../../../learning.md) that the present context is safer. [Extinction](../../../extinction-learning.md) is not necessarily erasure: new [learning](../../../learning.md) can compete with an earlier association, and a changed context or delay can allow [fear](../../../fear.md) to return.

This account predicts cue-dependent episodes and bodily responses. A particular sound, smell or visual pattern may precipitate a sudden experience before the person identifies its connection to the [trauma](../../../psychological-trauma.md). Absence of consciously recognising the reminder does not mean that no cue was present. However, a [conditioned response](../../../conditioned-response.md) could be autonomic [arousal](../../../physiological-arousal.md) or an urge to escape; it need not include a vivid autobiographical image or the feeling that a past event is happening now. Those additional properties require explanation.

[Anke Ehlers](../../../anke-ehlers.md) and colleagues investigated the content and quality of [intrusive memories](../../../intrusive-memory.md) after different traumas. Intrusions often concerned sensory impressions just before the event or before its worst emotional moments. Their warning-signal [hypothesis](../../../hypothesis.md) proposes that these impressions acquired predictive significance. This is compatible with associative [learning](../../../learning.md), but also refines a simple replay account: what is reexperienced may be a cue that signalled worsening danger rather than a literal representation of the objectively most injurious moment. The descriptive observations motivate the [hypothesis](../../../hypothesis.md) without experimentally proving the original association.[Behaviour Research and Therapy 40, 995–1002; descriptions of intrusion content, sensory qualities and moments preceding worst impact](../../../the-nature-of-intrusive-memories-after-trauma-the-warning-signal-hypothesis-by-anke-ehlers-and-others.md)

The distinction matters because [trauma](../../../psychological-trauma.md) is an extended, interpreted event. A moment can become especially threatening when its meaning changes—for example, when a previously ambiguous situation is understood as inescapable. The relevant reminder may reflect that subjective transition. A model that treats the whole event as one undifferentiated shock misses the organisation of the remembered experience. Retrospective descriptions, however, can be reconstructed, so prospective or carefully controlled analogue evidence is useful alongside clinical interviews.

Conditioning experiments provide converging but narrower evidence. [Mohammed R. Milad](../../../mohammed-r-milad.md) and colleagues studied sixteen people with [PTSD](../../../post-traumatic-stress-disorder.md) and fifteen trauma-exposed controls using a two-day fear-conditioning and [extinction](../../../extinction-learning.md) procedure in a 3-T [fMRI](../../../functional-magnetic-resonance-imaging.md) scanner. Skin conductance indexed conditioned responding. The groups did not differ significantly in acquisition or immediate [extinction](../../../extinction-learning.md), but the [PTSD](../../../post-traumatic-stress-disorder.md) group showed poorer [extinction recall](../../../extinction-recall.md) the following day, with associated differences in relevant brain activity. This supports altered retention or retrieval of safety [learning](../../../learning.md); it did not directly measure autobiographical [flashbacks](../../../flashback.md) or establish whether the group difference preceded [trauma](../../../psychological-trauma.md).[Biological Psychiatry 66, 1075–1082; 16 PTSD/15 trauma-exposed controls, two-day conditioning, skin conductance and 3-T fMRI](../../../neurobiological-basis-of-failure-to-recall-extinction-memory-in-posttraumatic-stress-disorder-by-mohammed-r-milad-and-others.md)

[Memory](../../../memory-in-animals.md) theories address features left underspecified by a reflex account. [Chris R. Brewin](../../../chris-r-brewin.md) and colleagues' original dual-representation theory distinguished verbally accessible [memories](../../../memory-in-animals.md) from [memories](../../../memory-in-animals.md) automatically accessible through situational cues. It proposed that cue-triggered sensory representations can produce reliving without ordinary deliberate narrative retrieval. This is a theoretical architecture, not proof of two anatomically discrete stores. Its explanatory value depends on generating predictions that distinguish access, contextual organisation and response processes rather than relabelling the clinical phenomena.[Psychological Review 103, 670–686; original verbally/situationally accessible memory distinction](../../../a-dual-representation-theory-of-posttraumatic-stress-disorder-by-chris-r-brewin-and-others.md)

[Anke Ehlers](../../../anke-ehlers.md) and [David M. Clark](../../../david-m-clark.md)'s cognitive model emphasised a persistent [sense of current threat](../../../sense-of-current-threat.md) arising from negative [appraisals](../../../cognitive-appraisal.md) and properties of [trauma](../../../psychological-trauma.md) [memory](../../../memory-in-animals.md), including difficulty integrating the event into its broader autobiographical context. [Avoidance](../../../avoidance-behavior.md), suppression and other strategies can impede change. On this account, a reminder may retrieve an impression without sufficient information that the danger belongs to the past. [Appraisals](../../../cognitive-appraisal.md) of an intrusion as evidence of permanent damage or loss of control can then increase [distress](../../../distress.md) and monitoring, potentially maintaining the problem beyond the initial cue association.[Behaviour Research and Therapy 38, 319–345; current threat, trauma-memory properties and maintaining strategies](../../../a-cognitive-model-of-posttraumatic-stress-disorder-by-anke-ehlers-and-david-m-clark.md)

Experimental work on representation also constrains an exclusively fear-response account. [Emily A. Holmes](../../../emily-a-holmes.md) and colleagues used a trauma-film analogue and randomly allocated participants, after a thirty-minute interval, to a brief visuospatial game or no task. Intrusions during the next week were reduced in the task condition, while recognition performance did not differ significantly. This suggests that involuntary imagery and deliberate [memory](../../../memory-in-animals.md) performance can be affected differently. The analogue, comparator and limited follow-up do not establish prevention or treatment of clinical [PTSD](../../../post-traumatic-stress-disorder.md), nor prove that all recognition information was unchanged.[PLOS ONE 4, e4153; trauma-film analogue, 30-minute delay, ten-minute visuospatial task, one-week intrusions and recognition](../../../can-playing-the-computer-game-tetris-reduce-the-build-up-of-flashbacks-for-trauma-a-proposal-from-cognitive-science-by-emily-a-holmes-and-others.md)

[Reexperiencing](../../../reexperiencing.md) should also be distinguished from related events. [Rumination](../../../rumination.md) about why something happened, a future-oriented image and deliberate recollection are not interchangeable with a sensory [flashback](../../../flashback.md). Shame, guilt and grief can accompany intrusions as well as [fear](../../../fear.md). A broad associative model can incorporate links to these meanings and responses, but then it needs explicit representational and contextual assumptions. Saying that every retrieved mental event is a conditioned reaction risks making the account true by definition rather than experimentally informative.

A discriminating experiment could hold reminder [exposure](../../../exposure-therapy.md) constant while varying contextual information or its interpretation, with careful ethical safeguards and an analogue suitable for research. Measure autonomic response, involuntary imagery, deliberate recognition and sense of current occurrence separately. A reduction in bodily [fear](../../../fear.md) without a corresponding change in involuntary images would show that the endpoints dissociate; a contextual manipulation reducing reliving at comparable [arousal](../../../physiological-arousal.md) would support a role beyond response magnitude. Predefine [manipulation checks](../../../manipulation-check.md) and avoid treating absence of a reported image as proof that no [memory](../../../memory-in-animals.md) was activated.

Clinical studies should add prospective sampling of reminders and intrusions where feasible, rather than relying entirely on retrospective questionnaires. Repeated within-person observations can test whether contexts and [appraisals](../../../cognitive-appraisal.md) precede an episode, though they do not remove all [confounding](../../../confounding.md). Treatment effects can provide converging evidence, but improvement after a multicomponent therapy does not determine whether conditioning, [appraisal](../../../cognitive-appraisal.md) or [memory](../../../memory-in-animals.md) organisation was the necessary pathway.

Conditioning is consequently a useful account of why reminders become potent and why responses can recur after apparent safety [learning](../../../learning.md). A full explanation of [reexperiencing](../../../reexperiencing.md) also needs the representation retrieved, its contextual accessibility, the sense that it is occurring now and the meaning assigned to the experience. Associative, cognitive and [autobiographical memory](../../../autobiographical-memory.md) processes can therefore be complementary, provided their separate predictions remain open to test.
