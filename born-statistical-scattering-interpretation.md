# Born statistical scattering interpretation

↑ **Parent:** [Born rule](born-rule.md)

The scattering interpretation connects an incoming preparation to the relative frequency of outgoing directions and final atomic states. [Max Born](max-born.md)'s 1926 paper treats a distant incoming [electron](electron.md) and a [chemical atom](atom-physics.md), expands the outgoing [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) into final atomic states, and imposes [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md). Its translated correction footnote specifies the square of the coefficient; the modern [complex modulus](complex-modulus.md) notation below is ours.[Zur Quantenmechanik der Stoßvorgänge, 1926, pp. 863–867; inspected in D. H. Delphenich's translation, pp. 2–4, especially p. 3 equation (1) and footnote 1: squared coefficient](zur-quantenmechanik-der-stossvorgange-by-max-born.md)

In his 11 December 1954 lecture, [Max Born](max-born.md) recalled the difficulty of interpreting bound [electrons](electron.md) alone. He instead described an incident [electron](electron.md) wave generating an outgoing spherical wave, with direction-dependent squared amplitude determining relative scattering probabilities. Distinct final atomic states describe excitation with corresponding [electron](electron.md) [energy](energy-split.md) loss. This is a retrospective account of an interpretation, rather than the report of a new apparatus.[The statistical interpretation of quantum mechanics, lecture delivered 11 December 1954, printed pp. 261–262; inspected through the transcription at [https://studyres.com/doc/8624145/the-statistical-interpretation-of-quantum-mechanics,](https://studyres.com/doc/8624145/the-statistical-interpretation-of-quantum-mechanics,) paragraphs from the bound-electron difficulty through inelastic scattering](the-statistical-interpretation-of-quantum-mechanics-by-max-born.md)

The distinction from the modern [Born rule](born-rule.md) matters. The historical question was which collision outcome occurs: a direction of motion and a final atomic state. The general [projective measurement](projective-measurement.md) formula applies to arbitrary [observables](observable.md), including degenerate outcomes and [mixed states](mixed-state.md); a [positive operator-valued measure](positive-operator-valued-measure.md) describes still more general detector statistics. Those formulations should not be read back as the explicit contents of the short 1926 scattering paper. Nor does the scattering interpretation itself supply a rule for the conditional [post-measurement state](post-measurement-state.md).

Actual experimental settings are described in the [Davisson–Germer experiment](davisson-germer-experiment.md) and the [Franck-Hertz experiment](franck-hertz-experiment.md). The former scans collected [electric current](electric-current.md) as an [electron](electron.md) beam scatters from prepared [nickel](nickel.md); changing direction, accelerating [voltage](voltage.md) and surface preparation probes [electron diffraction](electron-diffraction.md). The latter uses acceleration through mercury vapor and a retarding collector to infer discrete [energy](energy-split.md) losses from current minima, with vapor density and retarding settings as controls. Angular resolution and surface backgrounds limit the first inference; initial [energy](energy-split.md) spread and collision positions limit the second. These linked experiments supply the preparation, apparatus and readout; the examples below are original calculations, not Born's measured data.
