<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

In a proton-proton collision, associate the reconstructed neutral [B meson](../../../../b-meson.md) with the accompanying opposite-side bottom [hadron](../../../../hadron.md). A direct semileptonic decay $b\to c\ell^-\bar\nu$ tags a $b$ on that side and hence an initial $\bar b$, or $B^0=d\bar b$, on the signal side. The opposite [charged lepton](../../../../charged-lepton.md) tags the conjugate assignment. Charged [kaons](../../../../kaon.md) from the opposite-side decay chain and same-side fragmentation tracks can supply additional statistical [flavour tagging](../../../../flavor-tagging.md) information; the common CP final state alone does not reveal the production [flavour](../../../../quark-flavor.md).

A magnetic spectrometer with precise silicon vertex tracking, charged-particle identification and [muon](../../../../muon.md) or [electron](../../../../electron.md) identification is suitable. A forward geometry can exploit the boosted bottom-hadron pairs at the [LHC](../../../../large-hadron-collider.md). It reconstructs displaced decay vertices, measures the signed tagging tracks, and infers the signal proper time from $t\simeq Lm_B/|\mathbf p_B|$. The supplied lifetime corresponds to a rest-frame mean flight length $c\tau\simeq0.45\ \mathrm{mm}$, enlarged by the [boost](../../../../lorentz-boost.md).

Even a perfect [detector](../../../../particle-detector.md) cannot remove physical mistags. An opposite-side neutral bottom meson can oscillate before its semileptonic decay, reversing the [lepton](../../../../lepton.md) charge relative to its production [flavour](../../../../quark-flavor.md). A cascade $b\to c\to\ell^+$ produces the charge opposite to a direct $b\to\ell^-$ decay. Additional bottom pairs or unrelated fragmentation [particles](../../../../particle-physics.md) can also make the chosen association incorrect. For mistag probability $w$, the measured asymmetry is diluted by $D=1-2w$; this is why the tagging calibration is part of a CP measurement.
