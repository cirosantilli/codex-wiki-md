<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take an on-shell [W boson](../../../../../w-boson.md) of fixed [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $M_W$, zero transverse recoil, negligible [charged-lepton](../../../../../charged-lepton.md) [mass](../../../../../mass.md), and unrestricted angular acceptance. In its rest frame the charged [lepton](../../../../../lepton.md) has [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) magnitude $p_0=M_W/2$. A longitudinal [Lorentz boost](../../../../../lorentz-boost.md) to the laboratory preserves [transverse momentum](../../../../../transverse-momentum.md), so

$$
p_T=p_0\sin\theta^\star,\qquad
c=\pm\sqrt{1-(p_T/p_0)^2}.
$$

Both angular roots contribute to the same $p_T$. Set $x=p_T/p_0$ and $a=\sqrt{1-x^2}$. The change-of-variable [Jacobian determinant](../../../../../jacobian-determinant.md) has $|dc/dp_T|=x/(p_0a)$, and the two-root angular weight is $\tfrac38[(1-a)^2+(1+a)^2]=\tfrac34(2-x^2)$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\frac1\Gamma\frac{d\Gamma}{dp_T}
=\frac{3}{4p_0}\frac{x(2-x^2)}{\sqrt{1-x^2}},\qquad
0<p_T<p_0,\qquad p_T^{\max}=\frac{M_W}{2}.}
$$

This is the ideal [W-decay Jacobian peak](../../../../../w-decay-jacobian-peak.md). The divergence at the endpoint is integrable; it is a change-of-variable effect, not an infinite decay probability. Reversing the polar axis does not change this [transverse momentum](../../../../../transverse-momentum.md) density, because the sum of the two roots removes the term odd in $c$.
