<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a beam with rms [geometric emittance](../../../../geometric-emittance.md) $\epsilon_y$, its vertical size at the collision point is $\sigma_y^\star=\sqrt{\epsilon_y\beta_y^\star}$. Reducing $\beta_y^\star$ therefore concentrates the beam, increasing the number of [particles](../../../../particle-physics.md) encountering the opposite beam per transverse area. For identical Gaussian beams colliding head-on,

$$
\mathcal L=\frac{n_b f_{\rm rev}N_1N_2}{4\pi\sigma_x^\star\sigma_y^\star}.
$$

Keep bunch populations, horizontal size, collision frequency and emittances fixed. If both vertical beta values are decreased by a factor $f>1$, meaning $\beta_{y,\rm new}^\star=\beta_{y,\rm old}^\star/f$, both vertical beam sizes decrease by $1/\sqrt f$, and

$$
\boxed{\frac{\mathcal L_{\rm new}}{\mathcal L_{\rm old}}=\sqrt f.}
$$

The [luminosity](../../../../collider-luminosity.md) is inversely proportional to the common vertical size, not its square, because only one transverse dimension has changed. The same ratio follows from the unequal-beam overlap denominator $\sqrt{\sigma_{y1}^2+\sigma_{y2}^2}$.

A [final-focus quadrupole](../../../../final-focus-quadrupole.md) system on either side of the interaction point forms a waist there. Strong [quadrupole](../../../../quadrupole-magnet.md) doublets or triplets, with suitable matching sections, focus both planes. At a symmetric waist $\beta'=0$ and the nearby drift gives $\beta(s)=\beta^\star+s^2/\beta^\star$, so small $\beta^\star$ requires large beam envelopes and strong optical gradients in the adjacent magnets. Chromatic correction is needed for off-momentum [particles](../../../../particle-physics.md). The formula assumes negligible crossing-angle and hourglass reductions; a bunch length comparable to $\beta^\star$ changes the effective overlap.
