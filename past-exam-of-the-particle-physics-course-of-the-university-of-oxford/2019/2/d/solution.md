<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For a paraxial orbit $z'\simeq p_z/p_s$, where $p_z$ is transverse [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) and $p_s$ longitudinal [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md). Acceleration mainly increases $p_s$, so a given transverse kick produces a smaller angular deviation. In an ideal matched accelerator without scattering or radiation, the conserved transverse canonical [phase-space](../../../../phase-space.md) area becomes a smaller area in the coordinates $(z,z')$. This is [adiabatic damping of geometric emittance](../../../../adiabatic-damping-of-geometric-emittance.md), not loss of canonical [phase-space](../../../../phase-space.md) volume.

The invariant [normalized emittance](../../../../normalized-emittance.md) is

$$
\epsilon_n=\beta_{\rm rel}\gamma_{\rm rel}\epsilon
=\frac{p_s}{m}\epsilon,
\qquad
\boxed{\epsilon\propto p_s^{-1}.}
$$

Here $\beta_{\rm rel}$ is speed in units of $c$, distinct from the lattice beta function. Neglecting dilution mechanisms, each acceleration stage thus supplies a beam with smaller angular spread and [geometric emittance](../../../../geometric-emittance.md) for injection into the next stage.

A chain of increasingly energetic [particle accelerators](../../../../particle-accelerator-split.md) lets each ring operate over a manageable range of [magnetic rigidity](../../../../magnetic-rigidity.md) and radio-frequency settings. It also avoids injecting a large-emittance low-energy beam directly into the small-aperture high-field main ring. Higher injection [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) reduces space-charge effects and improves capture and transmission. Emittance dilution, mismatching and collective instabilities can oppose the ideal damping; acceleration alone does not reduce [normalized emittance](../../../../normalized-emittance.md).
