<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[Quark mass](../../../../quark-mass.md) differences break [flavour symmetry](../../../../flavor-symmetry.md). [Isospin](../../../../isospin.md) exchanges $u,d$, so its strong breaking is controlled by the small difference $m_d-m_u$. [U-spin](../../../../u-spin.md) exchanges $d,s$, so its strong breaking involves the much larger $m_s-m_d$. Relative to the characteristic strong-interaction scale, the latter is much larger; this explains why [U-spin](../../../../u-spin.md) is a substantially poorer symmetry.

Flavour-dependent interactions are a second source. [Electromagnetism](../../../../electromagnetism-split.md) distinguishes $u$ from $d$ because $Q_u=2/3$ and $Q_d=-1/3$, and therefore breaks [isospin](../../../../isospin.md). However, $d,s$ both have charge $-1/3$, so their [electromagnetic couplings](../../../../electromagnetic-interaction.md) alone do **not** break [U-spin](../../../../u-spin.md) in the equal-mass limit. If two breaking mechanisms for each symmetry are required, the second common mechanism is the [weak interaction](../../../../weak-interaction.md): the [CKM matrix](../../../../cabibbo-kobayashi-maskawa-matrix.md) and weak [flavour](../../../../quark-flavor.md) transitions distinguish the flavours and do not conserve either strong-interaction [flavour symmetry](../../../../flavor-symmetry.md). The dominant difference in the quality of the two approximate strong symmetries is still the [mass](../../../../mass.md) splitting.
