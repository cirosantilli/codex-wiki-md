<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Expand the original [two-spinor](../../../../spinor.md) as $\chi=c_+\chi_++c_-\chi_-$. [Orthogonality](../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) gives

$$
c_+=\frac{\sqrt5}{3}\cos\frac\theta2+\frac23e^{-i\varphi}\sin\frac\theta2,\qquad
c_-=-\frac{\sqrt5}{3}e^{i\varphi}\sin\frac\theta2+\frac23\cos\frac\theta2.
$$

Let $r=|\mathbf p|/(E+m)$ and $w_\pm=(\chi_\pm,-\chi_\pm)^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt2$. These $w_\pm$ are [normalized](../../../../normalization.md), [left-chiral](../../../../chirality-physics.md) [helicity](../../../../helicity.md) [eigenvectors](../../../../eigenvector.md). Then

$$
\boxed{\psi_L=\sum_{\zeta=\pm1}\frac N{\sqrt2}c_\zeta(1-\zeta r)w_\zeta\,e^{i(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r-Et)}.}
$$

The two contributions have squared norms

$$
\boxed{\|\psi_{L,+}\|^2=|c_+|^2\frac{1-\beta}{2},\qquad
\|\psi_{L,-}\|^2=|c_-|^2\frac{1+\beta}{2},\qquad \beta=|\mathbf p|/E.}
$$

These are weights relative to the original [normalized](../../../../normalization.md) state; divide by their sum if conditioning on a [normalized](../../../../normalization.md) [left-chiral](../../../../chirality-physics.md) projection. In the ultrarelativistic limit $N\to1/\sqrt2$, $r\to1$, the [positive-helicity](../../../../helicity.md) [amplitude](../../../../probability-amplitude.md) is suppressed as $m/(2E)$, while the [negative-helicity](../../../../helicity.md) [amplitude](../../../../probability-amplitude.md) tends to $c_-$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\psi_L\longrightarrow c_-w_-e^{i(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r-Et)}.}
$$

For [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) specifically along $+z$, $c_+=\sqrt5/3$ and $c_-=2/3$: the original [helicity](../../../../helicity.md) [probabilities](../../../../probability.md) are $5/9,4/9$, the [left-chiral](../../../../chirality-physics.md) [Norm](../../../../norm.md) is $1/2-\beta/18$, and its limiting weight is $4/9$. The source does not specify that [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) direction, so these numerical [probabilities](../../../../probability.md) are a specialization, not the general answer.
