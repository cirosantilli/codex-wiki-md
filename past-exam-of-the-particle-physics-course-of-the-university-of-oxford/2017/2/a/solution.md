<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The squared invariant [amplitude](../../../../probability-amplitude.md) weights each accessible final configuration. The final [on-shell](../../../../on-shell.md) [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) measure counts the available states, and the four-dimensional [delta function](../../../../dirac-delta-function.md) imposes [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) and [momentum conservation](../../../../momentum-conservation.md):

$$
d\Phi_n=(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}\left(P-\sum_jp_j\right)\prod_j\frac{d^3p_j}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_j}.
$$

To prove its [Lorentz invariance](../../../../lorentz-invariance.md) for proper orthochronous transformations, write

$$
\boxed{\frac{d^3p}{2E_{\mathbf p}}=d^4p\,\delta(p^2-m^2)\Theta(p^0).}
$$

The four-volume measure has unit [Lorentz Jacobian](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md), $p^2$ is invariant, and the future [mass shell](../../../../mass-shell.md) is preserved. Likewise $\delta^{(4)}(\Lambda Q)=\delta^{(4)}(Q)$ because $|\det\Lambda|=1$. The product therefore proves that $d\Phi_n$ is invariant. [Spin](../../../../spin.md) averages and sums belong in the [amplitude](../../../../probability-amplitude.md) as appropriate; identical final particles require a [symmetry](../../../../symmetry-physics.md) divisor if the integration covers a fully labelled [phase space](../../../../lorentz-invariant-phase-space.md).

The initial $1/(2E_i)$ factors come from relativistic [normalization](../../../../normalization.md) of the incoming states. They are not individually invariant, so the displayed coordinate-time [transition rate](../../../../transition-intensity.md) must not be called a [Lorentz scalar](../../../../lorentz-scalar.md) merely because its final [phase space](../../../../lorentz-invariant-phase-space.md) is invariant. A one-particle decay has proper width $\Gamma_0=(2M_0)^{-1}\int|\mathcal M|^2d\Phi_n$ in its rest frame, while its laboratory rate is $\Gamma_{\mathrm{lab}}=(M_0/E_0)\Gamma_0$. A scattering [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md) instead uses the invariant incident flux $4\sqrt{(p_1\cdot p_2)^2-m_1^2m_2^2}$. This distinguishes invariance of [phase space](../../../../lorentz-invariant-phase-space.md) from transformation of a measured rate.
