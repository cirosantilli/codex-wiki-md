↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The principal passive-material requirement is a short [radiation length](../../../../radiation-length.md) in centimetres, normally obtained using a dense, high-atomic-number [absorber](../../../../sampling-calorimeter.md) such as lead or tungsten. It produces and contains an [electromagnetic shower](../../../../electromagnetic-shower.md) in a compact detector. Mechanical construction, transverse spread, cost, [radiation tolerance](../../../../radiation-damage.md) and inactive gaps also affect the choice, but the [absorber](../../../../sampling-calorimeter.md) is selected for [shower](../../../../electromagnetic-shower.md) formation rather than signal production.

[Liquid-argon](../../../../argon.md) gaps are a suitable active sampling medium. [Ionization](../../../../ionization.md) [electrons](../../../../electron.md) drift in an [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) and induce a measurable charge signal. Advantages include [radiation tolerance](../../../../radiation-damage.md), stable response and useful spatial segmentation. Disadvantages include cryogenic infrastructure, high-voltage and purity requirements, signal collection times and electronic-noise control. Only the active fraction is measured directly, so fluctuations of [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) shared between [absorber](../../../../sampling-calorimeter.md) and active gaps introduce a sampling contribution to the resolution. A high active fraction and fine sampling help, at the price of construction complexity and additional channels.
