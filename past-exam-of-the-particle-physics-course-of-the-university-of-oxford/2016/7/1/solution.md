<h1 id="7/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

[Cherenkov radiation](../../../../cherenkov-radiation.md) is coherent electromagnetic emission by a charged particle moving through a dielectric faster than the [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md) of light in that medium. The particle polarizes the material along its path. Wavelets interfere constructively on a cone when

$$
\boxed{\beta n(\omega)>1,\qquad\cos\theta_C=\frac{1}{\beta n(\omega)}}.
$$

A simple wavefront construction compares a particle displacement $vt$ with the light-wave displacement $ct/n$, giving the cone relation. The particle still travels slower than the vacuum [speed of light](../../../../speed-of-light.md); no violation of [special relativity](../../../../special-relativity-split.md) occurs. Dispersion makes the angle and threshold wavelength-dependent. The radiation has a continuous spectrum rather than the discrete line spectrum of an atomic transition.
