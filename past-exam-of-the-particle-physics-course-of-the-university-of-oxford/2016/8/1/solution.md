<h1 id="8/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

[Collider luminosity](../../../../collider-luminosity.md) is the proportionality factor between a process [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md) and its interaction rate: $dN/dt=\mathcal L\sigma$ before efficiency corrections. It has units of inverse area per time. [Integrated collider luminosity](../../../../integrated-collider-luminosity.md) is $\mathcal L_{\rm int}=\int\mathcal L(t)\,dt$, with inverse-area units, and gives $N=\sigma\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ for a constant [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md) and unit efficiency.

At an $e^+e^-$ collider, count small-angle [Bhabha scattering](../../../../bhabha-scattering.md) in a dedicated forward detector. Its calculable [quantum electrodynamics](../../../../quantum-electrodynamics.md) [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md) in a precisely defined angular acceptance provides $\mathcal L=N/(\epsilon\sigma\Delta t)$, after background and radiative corrections. The inner angular boundary is especially important because the rate is sharply forward-peaked.

At a [collider](../../../../collider.md) with two [proton](../../../../proton.md) beams, a [van der Meer scan](../../../../van-der-meer-scan.md) calibrates a visible interaction monitor by separating the beams horizontally and vertically while recording the overlap-dependent rate. Known bunch populations and the measured overlap give an absolute [collider luminosity](../../../../collider-luminosity.md) and hence the monitor's visible [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md). Its rate then tracks [collider luminosity](../../../../collider-luminosity.md) during normal running. Correlations between transverse coordinates require a nonfactorizing scan analysis rather than blindly multiplying two one-dimensional profiles.
