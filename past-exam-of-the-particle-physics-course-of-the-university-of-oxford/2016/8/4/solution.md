<h1 id="8/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For an [electron-positron collider](../../../../electron-positron-collider.md) of fixed bending radius $R$, [synchrotron radiation](../../../../synchrotron-radiation.md) rapidly becomes the principal obstacle. Multiplying the supplied radiated power by the revolution period $2\pi R/c$ gives

$$
U_0=\frac{e^2\gamma^4}{3\epsilon_0R},\qquad U_0[\mathrm{GeV}]\simeq8.85\times10^{-5}\frac{E[\mathrm{GeV}]^4}{R[\mathrm m]}.
$$

A bending radius of $3\ \mathrm{km}$ gives losses of about $2.95\ \mathrm{GeV}$ per turn at $100\ \mathrm{GeV}$ per beam, but $115\ \mathrm{GeV}$ per turn at $250\ \mathrm{GeV}$. At $100\ \mathrm{GeV}$, a $5\ \mathrm{mA}$ beam already radiates about $15\ \mathrm{MW}$. The radiofrequency system must replace this [energy](../../../../energy-split.md), and the vacuum chamber must absorb the radiation. The centre-of-mass [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is twice the beam [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) for equal opposite beams.

For a [collider](../../../../collider.md) with two [proton](../../../../proton.md) beams, the same [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) gives a much smaller [Lorentz factor](../../../../lorentz-factor.md). At fixed [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) and radius the radiated power scales as $m^{-4}$, so the suppression relative to [electrons](../../../../electron.md) is $(m_e/m_p)^4\simeq8.8\times10^{-14}$. The main practical limit is the dipole [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) through magnetic rigidity:

$$
p[\mathrm{GeV}/c]\simeq0.2998\,B[\mathrm T]R[\mathrm m].
$$

With $R\simeq2.8\ \mathrm{km}$ and $B\simeq8.3\ \mathrm T$, the beam [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is about $7\ \mathrm{TeV}$, or $14\ \mathrm{TeV}$ in the centre of mass. Fields near $16\ \mathrm T$ would give approximately $13.4\ \mathrm{TeV}$ per beam at that radius. These are realistic superconducting-magnet scales; a $27\ \mathrm{km}$ circumference includes straight sections, so its bending radius is less than $C/(2\pi)$. The [proton](../../../../proton.md) radiation loss near $7\ \mathrm{TeV}$ is only a few keV per turn, though high beam currents still create substantial cryogenic heat loads. Magnet technology, aperture, protection and stored beam [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) therefore dominate before the electron-like radiation limit.
