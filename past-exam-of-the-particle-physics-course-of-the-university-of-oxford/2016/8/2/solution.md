<h1 id="8/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

For two identical, head-on beams with [Gaussian distributions](../../../../normal-distribution.md), integrating the product of their normalized transverse densities gives an overlap $1/(4\pi\sigma_x\sigma_y)$. Each of the $n_b$ colliding bunch pairs crosses once per revolution, with $f_{\rm rev}\simeq c/C$. Thus

$$
\mathcal L=\frac{f_{\rm rev}n_bN_1N_2}{4\pi\sigma_x\sigma_y}=\frac{(2.998\times10^8/2.7\times10^4)\,2800\,(1.15\times10^{11})^2}{4\pi(2\times10^{-5})^2}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathcal L\simeq8.18\times10^{37}\ \mathrm{m}^{-2}\mathrm{s}^{-1}=8.18\times10^{33}\ \mathrm{cm}^{-2}\mathrm{s}^{-1}}.
$$

The factor $4\pi$ accounts for the convolution of two beams, rather than the $2\pi$ normalization of just one beam. The result assumes all stated bunch pairs collide at the chosen interaction point, speeds approximately $c$, zero crossing angle and negligible hourglass reduction. Additional interaction points do not multiply the local [collider luminosity](../../../../collider-luminosity.md) at a single detector.
