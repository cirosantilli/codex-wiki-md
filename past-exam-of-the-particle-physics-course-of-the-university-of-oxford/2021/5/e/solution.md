<h1 id="5/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Use a longitudinally segmented [electromagnetic calorimeter](../../../../electromagnetic-calorimeter.md) with fine transverse position readout. The [electromagnetic shower](../../../../electromagnetic-shower.md) centroids in two separated depth layers define a line, providing [photon](../../../../photon.md) pointing without assuming the longitudinal collision position. Combine this information with the reconstructed primary collision vertex from charged-particle [track reconstruction](../../../../track-reconstruction.md). The two lines from that vertex to the shower positions then determine the opening angle.

For an order-of-magnitude requirement, a single-direction precision of $10\ \mathrm{mrad}$ corresponds to a transverse position uncertainty of $15\ \mathrm{mm}$ over a $1.5\ \mathrm m$ vertex-to-shower lever arm, provided the vertex is known sufficiently well. Centroid reconstruction across finely segmented cells can reach this scale. At high [collision pileup](../../../../collision-pileup.md), identifying the correct primary vertex is essential: using the wrong vertex can dominate the angular error even if the shower position is accurately measured. Converted [photons](../../../../photon.md) offer an additional pointing constraint from the reconstructed $e^+e^-$ conversion tracks, but conversion is not required by the method described here.
