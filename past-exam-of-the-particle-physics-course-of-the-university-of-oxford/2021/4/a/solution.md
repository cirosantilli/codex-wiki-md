<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [chiral projectors](../../../../chiral-projector.md) $P_{L,R}=(1\mp\gamma^5)/2$ obey $\gamma^\mu P_L=P_R\gamma^\mu$, while the [Dirac adjoints](../../../../dirac-adjoint.md) are $\bar\psi_L=\bar\psi P_R$ and $\bar\psi_R=\bar\psi P_L$. Consequently the cross terms vanish and the [vector current](../../../../vector-current.md) is

$$
\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi=\bar\psi_L\gamma^\mu\psi_L+\bar\psi_R\gamma^\mu\psi_R.
$$

The vector interaction preserves [fermion chirality](../../../../chirality-physics.md) at a vertex and couples equally to left and right components. This statement about the vertex does not assert that a [fermion](../../../../fermion.md) [mass](../../../../mass.md) term preserves [chirality](../../../../chirality-physics.md). Under [parity](../../../../parity.md), left and right components are exchanged; their equal coefficients allow a parity-invariant contraction with a true vector field.

An axial vector must couple to the [axial current](../../../../axial-current.md) to obtain the analogous parity-invariant interaction. Since $\gamma^5\psi_L=-\psi_L$ and $\gamma^5\psi_R=\psi_R$,

$$
\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\psi=-\bar\psi_L\gamma^\mu\psi_L+\bar\psi_R\gamma^\mu\psi_R,
\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal L_A=-g_A(-\bar\psi_L\gamma^\mu\psi_L+\bar\psi_R\gamma^\mu\psi_R)A_\mu.}
$$

The relative sign changes; the overall sign is conventional. A pure axial interaction can itself conserve [parity](../../../../parity.md) when $A_\mu$ transforms as an axial vector. Simply contracting the original [vector current](../../../../vector-current.md) with an axial-vector field would instead produce a parity-odd interaction.
