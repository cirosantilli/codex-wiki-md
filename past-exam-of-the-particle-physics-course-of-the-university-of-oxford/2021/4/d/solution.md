<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Assume the same left and right couplings $g_l,g_r$ at the [electron](../../../../electron.md) and [muon](../../../../muon.md) vertices. This [lepton universality](../../../../lepton-universality.md) assumption is necessary to describe both vertices using just two numbers. Define $a=|g_l|^2$, $b=|g_r|^2$ and $z=\cos\theta^\star$. The two same-chirality channels have squared coupling weights $a^2,b^2$ and angular factor $(1+z)^2$; the other two each have weight $ab$ and factor $(1-z)^2$. Hence, for a common positive normalization $C$,

$$
\frac{d\sigma}{dz}=C\big[(a^2+b^2)(1+z)^2+2ab(1-z)^2\big]
=\boxed{C\big[(a+b)^2(1+z^2)+2(a-b)^2z\big].}
$$

If $|g_l/g_r|\ne1$, then $a\ne b$, so a forward-backward term survives. At the amplitude level, [parity](../../../../parity.md) interchanges the $g_l^2$ and $g_r^2$ channels; their unequal squared magnitudes show directly that the interaction violates [parity](../../../../parity.md). Equivalently the chiral current contains both [vector current](../../../../vector-current.md) and [axial current](../../../../axial-current.md) terms, with coefficients $(g_r+g_l)/2$ and $(g_r-g_l)/2$; unequal magnitudes preclude a pure parity eigen-current.

The odd-in-$z$ term is a useful signature within these assumptions, but it should not itself be called a parity-odd scalar: the unpolarized relative angle is unchanged when _all_ [momenta](../../../../momentum-split.md) are reversed. If the two species have independent couplings, the coefficient is instead proportional to $(a_e-b_e)(a_\mu-b_\mu)$, and a parity-violating coupling at only one vertex need not produce an unpolarized forward-backward asymmetry.
