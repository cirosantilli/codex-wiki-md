<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [tau-neutrino charged-current threshold](../../../../tau-neutrino-charged-current-threshold.md) requires specifying the stationary target and the lightest allowed final state. For a free [neutron](../../../../neutron-split.md), the lowest [charged current](../../../../charged-current.md) channel is $\nu_\tau n\to\tau^-p$. At threshold both final particles are at rest relative to one another in the [centre-of-momentum frame](../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md), so

$$
s=m_n^2+2m_nE_\nu=(m_p+m_\tau)^2,\qquad
E_\nu^{\rm thr}=\frac{(m_p+m_\tau)^2-m_n^2}{2m_n}\simeq3.45\,\mathrm{GeV}.
$$

The equal-[nucleon](../../../../nucleon.md)-mass approximation gives $m_\tau+m_\tau^2/(2m_N)\simeq3.46\,\mathrm{GeV}$. More generally $E_\nu^{\rm thr}=[(m_\tau+M_X)^2-M_T^2]/(2M_T)$ for a target of [mass](../../../../mass.md) $M_T$ and a recoil system with minimum allowed [invariant mass](../../../../invariant-mass.md) $M_X$. A stationary [electron](../../../../electron.md) in $\nu_\tau e^-\to\tau^-\nu_e$ instead gives $(m_\tau^2-m_e^2)/(2m_e)\simeq3.09\,\mathrm{TeV}$. The customary $3.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ answer therefore presumes a [nucleon](../../../../nucleon.md) target; nuclear thresholds and practical [deep inelastic scattering](../../../../deep-inelastic-scattering.md) thresholds differ.
