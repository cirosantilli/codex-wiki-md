<h1 id="7/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Use a high-multiplicity hadronic mode with only one invisible [neutrino](../../../../../neutrino.md), and reconstruct the total visible [four-momentum](../../../../../four-momentum.md) $p_h$. In the [tau lepton](../../../../../tau-particle.md) rest frame,

$$
E_h^*=\frac{m_\tau^2+m_h^2-m_{\nu_\tau}^2}{2m_\tau},\qquad m_h\leq m_\tau-m_{\nu_\tau}.
$$

Fit the endpoint of the hadronic [invariant mass](../../../../../invariant-mass.md) distribution, preferably together with its laboratory [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) boundary at known beam [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). The two-dimensional boundary uses more information than the single largest observed [mass](../../../../../mass.md). Detector smearing, unseen neutral products, backgrounds, beam radiation and hadronic spectral modeling must be included. Absence of a displaced endpoint generally sets an upper bound rather than a positive [neutrino mass](../../../../../neutrino-mass.md) measurement. Strictly the flavour state contains several [mass eigenstates](../../../../../mass-eigenstate.md); an unresolved endpoint constrains the corresponding weighted mass spectrum.
