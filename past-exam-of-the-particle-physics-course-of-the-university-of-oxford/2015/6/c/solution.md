<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $\chi_\pm=(1,\pm1)^T/\sqrt2$. The [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $\Sigma_1$ are $\pm1/2$, each with multiplicity two, and orthonormal bases for their [eigenspaces](../../../../eigenspace.md) are

$$
v_{\pm,1}=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,\pm1,0,0)^T,\qquad
v_{\pm,2}=\frac1{\sqrt2}(0,0,1,\pm1)^T.
$$

A general linear combination of the two positive-energy solutions has upper spinor $\xi=a\chi^1+b\chi^2$ and lower spinor $(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf p)\xi/(E+m)$. An x-spin [eigenvector](../../../../eigenvector.md) needs $\xi\propto\chi_\pm$ and the lower spinor to have the same $\sigma_1$ eigenvalue. The [Fixed-axis spin obstruction for positive-energy Dirac spinors](../../../../fixed-axis-spin-obstruction-for-positive-energy-dirac-spinors.md) is

$$
[\sigma_1,\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf p]\chi_\pm=2i(p_y\sigma_3-p_z\sigma_2)\chi_\pm.
$$

Its norm is $2\sqrt{p_y^2+p_z^2}$, so it vanishes exactly when $p_y=p_z=0$. For example with $\mathbf p=p\hat z$, the upper $\chi_+$ produces lower $\chi_-$, excluding a common [eigenvector](../../../../eigenvector.md) for every $p\ne0$. In the [nonrelativistic limit](../../../../nonrelativistic-limit.md), the lower components are $O(p/m)$, and $(\psi_1\pm\psi_2)/\sqrt2$ approach x-spin [eigenvectors](../../../../eigenvector.md). At rest they are exact.

The prompt's unrestricted claim of impossibility at relativistic [energies](../../../../energy-split.md) needs qualification: for [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) parallel to x these combinations remain exact [eigenvectors](../../../../eigenvector.md) at any [energy](../../../../energy-split.md). For a beam transverse to x, such as the conventional z-directed beam, the intended impossibility holds, and the lower components are no longer negligible. A transported relativistic [spin](../../../../spin.md) observable differs from this fixed matrix; [helicity](../../../../helicity.md) is the conserved projection along [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) for a free particle.
