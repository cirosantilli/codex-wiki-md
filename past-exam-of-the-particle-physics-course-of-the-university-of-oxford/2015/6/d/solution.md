<h1 id="6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

An [electron gun](../../../../electron-gun.md) releases [electrons](../../../../electron.md) by [thermionic emission](../../../../thermionic-emission-split.md), field emission or [photoelectric effect](../../../../photoelectric-effect.md), accelerates them through an electric potential, and uses electrostatic or magnetic lenses to form a beam in vacuum. A hot unpolarized cathode does not by itself select [spin](../../../../spin.md).

For a polarized source, illuminate a suitable semiconductor photocathode, commonly strained GaAs, with circularly polarized light near its band-gap threshold. Optical angular-momentum selection rules preferentially populate one conduction-electron [spin](../../../../spin.md); emission from a negative-electron-affinity surface extracts the selected population. Reversing the light's [circular polarization](../../../../circular-polarization.md) reverses the beam polarization. Strain and a suitable excitation wavelength suppress transitions that dilute the selection. Solenoids and spin rotators orient the [spin](../../../../spin.md) after acceleration, and a scattering polarimeter measures the achieved polarization; it need not be $100\%$.
