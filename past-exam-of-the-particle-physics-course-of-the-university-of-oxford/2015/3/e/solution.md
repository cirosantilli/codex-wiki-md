<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The four allowed two-real-[photon](../../../../photon.md) states are $\eta_c$, $\eta_c(2S)$, $\chi_{c0}$ and $\chi_{c2}$. Two [photons](../../../../photon.md) have $C=+1$, excluding $J/\psi$, $\psi(2S)$ and $h_c$; the [Landau-Yang theorem](../../../../landau-yang-theorem.md) excludes $\chi_{c1}$ because $J=1$. Both scalar and pseudoscalar two-[photon](../../../../photon.md) amplitudes exist, so $0^{++}$ and $0^{-+}$ are allowed. The [J/psi meson](../../../../j-psi-meson.md) was discovered first because its $1^{--}$ assignment permits direct, resonantly enhanced single-[photon](../../../../photon.md) formation in $e^+e^-$ and a clean dilepton peak in hadron production. The lighter $\eta_c$ cannot be formed by one [photon](../../../../photon.md); it needs less favorable production channels and has a broader two-[gluon](../../../../gluon.md) decay.

A spin-zero $\eta_c$ at rest has no preferred axis, so $d\Gamma/d\Omega_p$ is isotropic after summing final [spins](../../../../spin.md). A [J/psi meson](../../../../j-psi-meson.md) formed in unpolarized high-energy $e^+e^-$ has transverse $m=\pm1$ alignment. Its $p\bar p$ distribution can be written

$$
\frac{d\Gamma}{d\cos\theta_p}\propto a(1+\cos^2\theta_p)+b\sin^2\theta_p,\qquad a,b\geq0,
$$

where $a,b$ contain the two independent [helicity amplitudes](../../../../helicity-amplitude.md). It is generally anisotropic; the approximate massless, helicity-conserving limit has $b\to0$. The tabulated branching fraction does not fix $a/b$. An ensemble equally populated in all three [spin](../../../../spin.md) projections would instead be isotropic: alignment of the production ensemble matters.
