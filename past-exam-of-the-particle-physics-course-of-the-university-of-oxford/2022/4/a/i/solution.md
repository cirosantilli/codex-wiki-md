<h1 id="4/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md) is $d\mathbf p/dt=q\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$. Because it is perpendicular to the velocity, it changes the direction of [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) without changing its magnitude. For a [charged particle in a uniform magnetic field](../../../../../charged-particle-in-a-uniform-magnetic-field.md), the radius of curvature is $R=p/(|q|B)$. Over the short track, the bending angle is $\phi\simeq L/R$. Equivalently, integrate the force for a time $L/v$ while neglecting the small rotation in its direction. The magnitude of the acquired [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) component perpendicular to the incident direction is

$$
\boxed{|\Delta p_\perp|=p\sin\phi\simeq p\phi=|q|BL.}
$$

Its direction follows $q\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$; a signed component can therefore be written $qBL$ after fixing an oriented bending axis. Chord length and arc length agree at this order in $L/R$.
