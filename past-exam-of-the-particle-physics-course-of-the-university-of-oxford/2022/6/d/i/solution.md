<h1 id="6/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a rate measurement of the small [mixing angle](../../../../../mixing-angle.md) $\theta_{13}$, choose the first fast disappearance maximum,

$$
\Delta_{23}=\frac\pi2,\qquad
\boxed{L_{\rm opt}\simeq\frac{\pi E}{2k_{\rm practical}|\Delta m_{23}^2|}=1.24\ \mathrm{km}\quad(E=3\ \mathrm{MeV}).}
$$

A 47 km baseline instead lies near the slow [oscillation](../../../../../oscillation.md) maximum associated with $\theta_{12}$. Its much lower flux, approximately falling as $1/L^2$, and the many fast [oscillations](../../../../../oscillation.md) washed out by a reactor's broad [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) spectrum make it a poor choice for this simple $\theta_{13}$ design. A medium-baseline spectral experiment can still constrain [neutrino oscillation](../../../../../neutrino-oscillation.md) parameters if it has enough events and resolution; 47 km is not intrinsically useless, but it is not the optimal short-baseline rate experiment. In [a](../../a.md), $\theta_{13}$ also affects the slow envelope through $\cos^4\theta_{13}$, so the sensitivity there is not identically zero.
