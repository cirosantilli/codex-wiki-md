<h1 id="6/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Matched near and far [particle detectors](../../../../../particle-detector.md) are a good design. A near [particle detector](../../../../../particle-detector.md), close enough that the fast [oscillation phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) is small, measures the unoscillated reactor spectrum and normalization. A far [particle detector](../../../../../particle-detector.md) near the first fast maximum measures the disappearance. Their appropriately geometry-corrected ratio cancels much of the uncertain reactor flux and common [cross section](../../../../../cross-section-physics.md) and [detection efficiency](../../../../../detection-efficiency.md). Relative calibration, different backgrounds, multiple reactor distances and any residual near-site [oscillation](../../../../../oscillation.md) still need treatment. The near site should retain enough shielding to suppress cosmic backgrounds.[Section 14.6.4, Reactor Antineutrinos: relative near/far measurements of the reactor disappearance signal](../../../../../review-of-particle-physics-by-particle-data-group-2022.md)
