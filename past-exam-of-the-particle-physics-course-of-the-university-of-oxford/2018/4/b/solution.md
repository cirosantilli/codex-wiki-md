<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The two [Weyl spinors](../../../../weyl-spinor.md) transform in the inequivalent left- and [right-chiral](../../../../chirality-physics.md) representations of the proper [Lorentz group](../../../../lorentz-group.md). They are selected by $P_{L,R}=(1\mp\gamma^5)/2$ and have different [boost](../../../../lorentz-boost.md) transformation laws; [parity](../../../../parity.md) exchanges the two. A massive [Dirac equation](../../../../dirac-equation.md) couples them, whereas in the massless limit they can propagate independently.

For the particular $u$ printed in this paper, transforming its supplied Dirac $\gamma^5$ gives

$$
\boxed{\gamma_W^5=u\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\I&0\end{pmatrix}u^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\0&-I\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Thus the upper block is [right chiral](../../../../chirality-physics.md) and the lower block [left chiral](../../../../chirality-physics.md) in this convention. Many references use the opposite block order; neither convention changes physics, but they must not be mixed. [Chirality](../../../../chirality-physics.md) is not generally the same as [helicity](../../../../helicity.md) for a massive [electron](../../../../electron.md). The weak [charged current](../../../../charged-current.md) selects the [left-chiral](../../../../chirality-physics.md) [electron](../../../../electron.md) component, reducing to negative [helicity](../../../../helicity.md) at high [energy](../../../../energy-split.md).
