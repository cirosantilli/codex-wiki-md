<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Treat the $p^+$ strips as much more strongly [doped](../../../../semiconductor-doping.md) than the $n$ bulk, so the [depletion layer](../../../../depletion-region.md) lies predominantly in the latter. Fully ionized [donors](../../../../donor-impurity.md) leave uniform [space charge](../../../../space-charge.md) $\rho=eN_D$. In one dimension,

$$
\frac{dE}{dx}=\frac{eN_D}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{Si}}},\qquad \epsilon_{\mathrm{Si}}=\epsilon_r\epsilon_0.
$$

At the depletion boundary $x=d$, the minimum-bias solution has $E(d)=0$. Thus $E(x)=eN_D(x-d)/\epsilon_{\mathrm{Si}}$, and the magnitude of the potential drop is

$$
\boxed{V_{\mathrm{dep}}=\left|\int_0^dE(x)dx\right|=\frac{eN_Dd^2}{2\epsilon_r\epsilon_0}.}
$$

For $N_D=10^{18}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$, $d=300\,\mu\mathrm m$, and $\epsilon_r=11.6$, this is

$$
\boxed{V_{\mathrm{dep}}\simeq70.2\,\mathrm V.}
$$

The applied [reverse bias](../../../../reverse-bias.md) is approximately this value; more precisely the built-in [junction potential](../../../../built-in-potential.md) contributes to the total [depletion voltage](../../../../one-sided-depletion-voltage.md) and is subtracted from the required external bias. It is small on the scale of seventy volts and is not specified. Here the stated free-carrier density is used as the effective [donor density](../../../../donor-impurity.md) of the neutral bulk before depletion.
