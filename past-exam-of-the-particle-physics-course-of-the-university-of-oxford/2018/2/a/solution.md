<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [valence quark](../../../../valence-quark.md) distributions describe net [quark](../../../../quark.md) content: $\int u_v(x)dx=2$ and $\int d_v(x)dx=1$ for a [proton](../../../../proton.md). Their [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) densities $xu_v,xd_v$ are small at very small $x$, peak at moderate [momentum fractions](../../../../parton-momentum-fraction.md), and vanish toward $x=1$, where one [parton](../../../../parton.md) carrying almost all [proton](../../../../proton.md) [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) is unlikely. The larger $u$ distribution reflects two valence [up quarks](../../../../up-quark.md) and its comparatively harder shape; its pointwise value need not be exactly twice the down distribution.

The [sea quark](../../../../sea-quark.md) distribution is generated largely by [gluon](../../../../gluon.md) radiation and $g\to q\bar q$ splitting. It grows strongly toward small $x$ and falls at large $x$. The plotted sea curve is scaled by $0.05$, so it must be multiplied by twenty before numerical use. The total up-quark [PDF](../../../../parton-distribution-function.md) is

$$
\boxed{u(x)=u_v(x)+\bar u(x),}
$$

using the valence-up distribution and the up-sea distribution. The summed sea curve alone does not fix $\bar u$ without a [flavour](../../../../flavor-eigenstate.md) assumption. Under the later stated four-flavour symmetry, $\bar u=\Sigma/8$.
