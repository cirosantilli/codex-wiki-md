<h1 id="3/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

[Interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) [CP violation](../../../../../cp-violation.md) concerns the relative [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) between direct decay and decay after mixing. For a common [CP eigenstate](../../../../../cp-eigenstate.md) final state, it can occur with both $|q/p|=1$ and $|\bar A_f/A_f|=1$, so neither of the first two mechanisms is needed. In that limit $|\lambda|=1$, but

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Im}\lambda\ne0}
$$

produces different [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) terms for $\lambda$ and $\lambda^{-1}=\lambda^*$. With $\Delta m=m_h-m_\ell$,

$$
g_-g_+^*=\frac14\left[e^{-\Gamma_\ell t}-e^{-\Gamma_h t}+2i e^{-(\Gamma_\ell+\Gamma_h)t/2}\sin(\Delta m t)\right].
$$

The term proportional to $\operatorname{Im}\lambda\sin(\Delta m t)$ consequently reverses sign. In the CP-conserving limit, $\lambda$ is the real [CP](../../../../../cp-symmetry.md) [parity](../../../../../parity.md) of the final state in a consistent [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) convention. For non-CP-eigenstate final states, compare the full coefficients in CP-conjugate rates rather than imposing this simplified criterion.
