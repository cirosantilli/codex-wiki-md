# Logarithmic diffusion

↑ **Parent:** [Nonlinear diffusion equation](nonlinear-diffusion-equation.md)

For a positive density $u$, logarithmic diffusion is the [nonlinear diffusion equation](nonlinear-diffusion-equation.md) $u_t=\kappa\Delta\log u$, equivalently $u_t=\kappa\operatorname{div}(u^{-1}\nabla u)$. Its [diffusion coefficient](diffusion-coefficient.md) is inversely proportional to the density. In one dimension, a mass-$M$ [self-similar solution](similarity-solution.md) is

$$
u(x,t)=\frac{2\kappa t}{x^2+(2\pi\kappa t/M)^2}.
$$

Its integral is $M$, and substitution verifies the equation. This is $M$ times a [Cauchy distribution](cauchy-distribution.md) density of scale $2\pi\kappa t/M$. It tends weakly to $M$ times the [Dirac delta distribution](dirac-delta-function.md) as $t\downarrow0$; the slow algebraic tails illustrate the enhanced diffusion where density is small.
