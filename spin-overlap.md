# Spin overlap

↑ **Parent:** [Spin glass](spin-glass.md)

For two [spin configurations](spin-configuration.md) $s,t$ on the same $N$ sites, their [spin overlap](spin-overlap.md) is $+1$ when they agree everywhere and $-1$ when they are opposite everywhere. Two independent [canonical ensemble](canonical-ensemble-split.md) draws must use the same [quenched disorder](quenched-disorder.md). Otherwise their comparison mixes changes of thermal state with changes of material sample.

An original finite example uses the six equally weighted [ground states](ground-state.md) of the [frustrated Ising triangle](frustrated-ising-triangle.md). For each first configuration, the second has one identical choice, one fully reversed choice, two choices differing at one site and two differing at two sites. Thus the [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) of [spin overlap](spin-overlap.md) is

$$
P(q=1)=P(q=-1)=\frac16,\qquad
P(q=1/3)=P(q=-1/3)=\frac13.
$$

It is symmetric, with $\langle q\rangle=0$ but $\langle q^2\rangle=11/27$. At infinite [temperature](temperature-split.md), all eight [microstates](microstate.md) are equally probable and instead $\langle q^2\rangle=1/3$. This computable change in similarity statistics is a finite-model fact, not evidence for [replica symmetry breaking](replica-symmetry-breaking.md).
