# Image parity in gravitational lensing

↑ **Parent:** [Lensing magnification](lensing-magnification.md)

[Image parity in gravitational lensing](image-parity-in-gravitational-lensing.md) is the sign of the [determinant](determinant.md) of the source-from-image [Jacobian matrix](jacobian-matrix.md) $A=\partial\boldsymbol\beta/\partial\boldsymbol\theta$. A positive sign preserves the local handedness of a small source pattern; a negative sign reverses it. Negative signed [lensing magnification](lensing-magnification.md) therefore means a reflected orientation, not negative received light. This is a two-dimensional property: the [derivative](derivative.md) of a signed radial equation alone does not determine it.

For a [point-mass gravitational lens](point-mass-gravitational-lens.md), differentiate $\boldsymbol\beta=(1-\theta_E^2/r^2)\boldsymbol\theta$, where $r=|\boldsymbol\theta|$. The radial and tangential [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) of $A$ are

$$
\lambda_r=1+\frac{\theta_E^2}{r^2},\qquad\lambda_t=1-\frac{\theta_E^2}{r^2},\qquad\det A=1-\frac{\theta_E^4}{r^4}.
$$

Consequently the outer member of the [two images of a point-mass lens](two-images-of-a-point-mass-lens.md) has positive [image parity in gravitational lensing](image-parity-in-gravitational-lensing.md); the inner member has negative [image parity in gravitational lensing](image-parity-in-gravitational-lensing.md). At $r=\theta_E$ the tangential [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) vanishes: this is the [tangential critical curve of an axisymmetric lens](tangential-critical-curve-of-an-axisymmetric-lens.md), and its [gravitational-lensing caustic](gravitational-lensing-caustic.md) is the aligned source point. The signed [lensing magnification](lensing-magnification.md) is $\mu=1/\det A$, but measured total light uses $|\mu_+|+|\mu_-|$.
