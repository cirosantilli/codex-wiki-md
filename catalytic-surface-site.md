# Catalytic surface site

↑ **Parent:** [Heterogeneous catalysis](heterogeneous-catalysis.md)

A [catalytic surface site](catalytic-surface-site.md) is a location on a [solid](solid.md) surface capable of participating in a catalytic route. The schematic notation $\ast$ denotes a vacant site; $\mathrm A\ast$ denotes A bound there. A minimal illustrative [catalytic cycle](catalytic-cycle.md) is

$$
\mathrm A+\ast\rightleftharpoons\mathrm A\ast,
\qquad
\mathrm A\ast\rightleftharpoons\mathrm B+\ast.
$$

The first step represents [adsorption](adsorption.md); the second combines conversion and release. Cancelling $\ast$ and $\mathrm A\ast$ gives $\mathrm A\rightleftharpoons\mathrm B$. The site is regenerated even though its occupancy changes during the cycle. This is an original bookkeeping model, not a measured [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md) for [manganese dioxide](manganese-dioxide.md).

If the only site states are vacant and occupied by A, the site inventory obeys $n_\ast+n_{\mathrm A\ast}=n_{\mathrm{sites}}$. A blocked or inaccessible part of a particle does not contribute available [catalytic surface sites](catalytic-surface-site.md) merely because it contributes [mass](mass.md). Different surface structures can have different abilities to bind and convert [reactants](reactant.md); equal geometric [surface area](surface-area.md) need not mean equal catalytic activity.
