# Absorption time

↑ **Parent:** [First-passage time](first-passage-time.md)

For a discrete-time [Markov chain](markov-chain-split.md) and a set $A$ of [absorbing states](absorbing-state.md), its absorption time is the [hitting time](first-passage-time.md) $T_A=\inf\{m\geq0:X_m\in A\}$. It is zero when the initial state already lies in $A$, and infinite on a [sample path](sample-path.md) that never enters $A$. Finiteness [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md) need not imply a finite [expected value](expected-value-split.md). For a finite-state model, a uniform positive [probability](probability.md) of absorption within a fixed number of steps gives a [geometric series](geometric-series.md) bound on the survival [probabilities](probability.md) and proves that the [mean](expected-value-split.md) is finite. The [mean absorption time of a lazy symmetric random walk](mean-absorption-time-of-a-lazy-symmetric-random-walk.md) is an explicit example.
