<h1 id="2/c/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Deep currents and [tides](../../../../../tide.md) moving across rough topography, including the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, generate [internal waves](../../../../../internal-wave.md). Their breaking produces [turbulent mixing](../../../../../turbulent-mixing.md) across [isopycnals](../../../../../isopycnal.md), called [diapycnal mixing](../../../../../diapycnal-mixing.md). This transfers [buoyancy](../../../../../buoyancy.md) between deep and overlying waters, transforms dense water into lighter water, and contributes to the upward return needed to close the overturning. The required mechanical [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) ultimately comes from winds and [tides](../../../../../tide.md); roughness is a mechanism for converting this input into mixing, rather than an independent source of [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). Mixing is spatially concentrated, and local vertical motion depends on the divergence of the turbulent [buoyancy flux](../../../../../buoyancy-flux.md), so enhanced mixing does not imply uniform local upwelling everywhere over a ridge.[The Impact of Abyssal Mixing Parameterizations in an Ocean General Circulation Model, abstract and introduction: tidal internal-wave generation over rough topography and its role in overturning](https://www.whoi.edu/cms/files/vmix_38943.pdf)
