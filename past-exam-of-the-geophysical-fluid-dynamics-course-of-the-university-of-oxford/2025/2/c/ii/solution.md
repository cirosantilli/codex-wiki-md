<h1 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Dense water formed in marginal seas spills over sills and descends the continental slope as a [marginal-sea overflow](../../../../../marginal-sea-overflow.md). Strong [shear flow](../../../../../shear-flow.md) generates [turbulent mixing](../../../../../turbulent-mixing.md) and [entrainment](../../../../../fluid-entrainment.md) of surrounding, lighter water. The descending current's [volume flux](../../../../../volumetric-flow-rate.md) grows while its [density](../../../../../density.md) anomaly is diluted, modifying the depth at which it joins the deep circulation and the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and [salinity](../../../../../salinity.md) of the exported water. Thus the deep [volume flux](../../../../../volumetric-flow-rate.md) can substantially exceed the source overflow [volume flux](../../../../../volumetric-flow-rate.md); it cannot be inferred from northern surface cooling alone.[Anatomy of an overflow, section 2.11: observed downstream entrainment and dilution of the Faroe Bank Channel overflow](https://www2.whoi.edu/staff/lpratt/wp-content/uploads/sites/120/2019/07/PrattSec2.11_11823.pdf)
