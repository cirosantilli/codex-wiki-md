<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Hold $l,m,f_0,N$ fixed when differentiating the [dispersion relation](../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) of [Rossby waves](../../../../rossby-wave.md), so that $\alpha^2\geq0$ is independent of $k$. For $k\ne0$, stationary zonal phase requires

$$
0=\frac\omega k=U-\frac\beta{k^2+\alpha^2},
\qquad U=\frac\beta{k^2+\alpha^2}.
$$

The zonal [group velocity](../../../../group-velocity.md) measured relative to the ground is

$$
c_{g,x}=\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k}
=U+\frac{\beta(k^2-\alpha^2)}{(k^2+\alpha^2)^2}.
$$

Using the stationarity condition,

$$
\boxed{c_{g,x}=\frac{2\beta k^2}{(k^2+\alpha^2)^2}
=\frac{2Uk^2}{k^2+\alpha^2}>0.}
$$

Here $\beta=2\Omega\cos\phi/a>0$ on the ordinary midlatitude [beta plane](../../../../beta-plane.md), so the stationary [Rossby wave](../../../../rossby-wave.md) requires an eastward background current and carries its [wave packet](../../../../wave-packet.md) eastward. If $\alpha>0$, a real nonzero stationary $k$ exists only for $0<U<\beta/\alpha^2$. The restriction $k\ne0$ matters: a zonally uniform disturbance with $k=0$ has $\omega=0$ identically, but its zonal [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md) is undefined, so it is not a counterexample within the stated meaning of stationary waves.
