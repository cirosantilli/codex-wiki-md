# Catalysis

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical kinetics](chemical-kinetics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalysis)

[Catalysis](catalysis.md) accelerates a [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md) by providing an alternative [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md) with a more accessible barrier through its [transition states](transition-state.md). A [catalyst](catalyst.md) participates in the route and is regenerated. At fixed [temperature](temperature.md), [catalysis](catalysis.md) changes how quickly [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md) is reached without changing the [equilibrium constant](equilibrium-constant.md); it does not make an unfavourable overall change in [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md) favourable. [§12.7, opening definition and Figure 12.19: alternative mechanisms and regeneration](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others.md)[§13.3, Effect of a Catalyst: equilibrium composition is unchanged](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/13-3-shifting-equilibria-le-chatelier%E2%80%99s-principle.md)

In his 1909 lecture, [Wilhelm Ostwald](wilhelm-ostwald.md) recalled the introduction of the term [catalysis](catalysis.md) by [Jöns Jacob Berzelius](jons-jacob-berzelius.md) and emphasized measurable acceleration rather than merely naming an unexplained force. He also discussed [reaction intermediates](reaction-intermediate.md) and participation of the [catalyst](catalyst.md) in the steps whose sum gives the net [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md). This is a participant's retrospective account, not evidence that every catalytic [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md) is the same.[On Catalysis, lecture dated 12 December 1909; paragraphs beginning “The factor which Berzelius regarded” and “It is appropriate here”; editorial footnote says the lecture was reconstructed from notes](nobel-lectures-chemistry-1901-1921/on-catalysis.md)
