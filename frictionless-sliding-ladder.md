# Frictionless sliding ladder

↑ **Parent:** [Lagrangian mechanics](lagrangian-mechanics-split.md)

For a uniform slender ladder of length $2l$ sliding while touching a smooth wall and floor, use its angle $\theta$ from the vertical. Its [centre of mass](center-of-mass.md) is $(l\sin\theta,l\cos\theta)$ and its central [moment of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md) is $ml^2/3$. Hence the [Lagrangian](lagrangian.md) is $L=2ml^2\dot\theta^2/3-mgl\cos\theta$, and its [Euler-Lagrange equation](euler-lagrange-equation.md) is $\ddot\theta=3g\sin\theta/(4l)$. The contacts are physically possible only while their [normal forces](normal-force.md) are nonnegative. If released from rest at $\theta_0$, [energy conservation](conservation-of-energy.md) gives $\dot\theta^2=3g(\cos\theta_0-\cos\theta)/(2l)$, and the wall force becomes $3mg\sin\theta(3\cos\theta-2\cos\theta_0)/4$. Contact is lost at $\cos\theta=2\cos\theta_0/3$, after which the constrained equation no longer describes the motion.
