# Killing radical is a solvable ideal

↑ **Parent:** [Cartan criterion for semisimplicity](cartan-criterion-for-semisimplicity.md)

For a finite-dimensional complex [Lie algebra](lie-algebra-split.md) $L$, the radical $R$ of its [Killing form](killing-form.md) is a [Lie algebra ideal](ideal-of-a-lie-algebra.md) by invariance. For $x,y\in R$, their adjoint actions vanish on $L/R$, so $B_R(x,y)=B_L(x,y)=0$. The [Cartan solvability criterion](cartan-solvability-criterion.md) makes the image $\operatorname{ad}_R(R)$ solvable; its kernel is the abelian center, so $R$ is solvable. A [semisimple Lie algebra](semisimple-lie-algebra-split.md) has no nonzero solvable ideal, forcing its Killing radical to vanish.
