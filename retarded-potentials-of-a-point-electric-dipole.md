# Retarded potentials of a point electric dipole

↑ **Parent:** [Retarded potential](retarded-potential.md)

An ideal [electric dipole](electric-dipole.md) at the origin with smooth [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) $\mathbf p(t)$ has [Lorenz gauge](lorenz-gauge-condition.md) [electric potential](electric-potential.md) and [magnetic vector potential](magnetic-vector-potential.md), for $r>0$,

$$
\phi=\frac1{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p(t_0)}{r^2}+\frac{\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p'(t_0)}{cr}\right),\qquad
\mathbf A=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r}\mathbf p'(t_0),\qquad \mathbf n=\frac{\mathbf r}r.
$$

For a short proof, place [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) $sq$ at $s\mathbf p/(2q)$, $s=\pm1$, and let $q\to\infty$ with $\mathbf p$ fixed. The corresponding [retarded times](retarded-time.md) are $t_0+s\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p(t_0)/(2qc)+O(q^{-2})$. Each [Liénard–Wiechert potential](lienard-wiechert-potential.md) has denominator $r-s(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p+r\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p'/c)/(2q)+O(q^{-2})$. The opposite $q/r$ terms cancel, leaving the displayed [electric potential](electric-potential.md). The products of signed [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) and [velocity](velocity.md) add, giving the displayed [magnetic vector potential](magnetic-vector-potential.md). Bounded time [derivatives](derivative.md) on the relevant interval justify the [Taylor expansions](taylor-expansion.md).
