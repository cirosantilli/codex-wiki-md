# Protein quaternary structure

↑ **Parent:** [Protein structure](protein-structure.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_quaternary_structure)

The [protein quaternary structure](protein-quaternary-structure.md) is the arrangement of multiple [polypeptide chains](polypeptide.md) in a functional assembly. A single-chain [protein](protein-split.md) has [protein tertiary structure](protein-tertiary-structure.md) without requiring [protein quaternary structure](protein-quaternary-structure.md). For an exact bookkeeping example, two intact model chains of twelve [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md) each contain $2\times12=24$ residues and $2\times11=22$ [peptide bonds](peptide-bond.md). Assembly alone adds no [peptide bond](peptide-bond.md) joining their ends. Stable functional assembly should also be distinguished from [protein aggregation](protein-aggregation.md). See [Alberts et al.: proteins as assemblies of polypeptide subunits](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26830/).
