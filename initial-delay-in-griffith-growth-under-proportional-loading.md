# Initial delay in Griffith growth under proportional loading

↑ **Parent:** [Griffith fracture criterion](griffith-fracture-criterion.md)

For linear antiplane elasticity with body forces and Dirichlet data proportional to time, uniqueness gives $u(s,t)=tu(s,1)$ at any fixed crack length $s$. The minimized elastic-plus-load potential and its [energy release rate](energy-release-rate.md) scale quadratically. The local [Griffith fracture criterion](griffith-fracture-criterion.md) laws are $\dot\ell\geq0$, $G(\ell,t)\leq G_c$ and $\dot\ell(G_c-G)=0$, with positive toughness $G_c$.

If $G(s,1)$ is finite and continuous near the initial length, it is bounded there by $M$. Continuity of the crack path keeps it in that neighborhood for a positive interval. Choose the interval short enough that $t^2M<G_c$; complementarity then forces zero growth throughout it. This proves a positive initial waiting time without assuming the crack is stationary in advance.
