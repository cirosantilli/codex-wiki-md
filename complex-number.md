# Complex number

↑ **Parent:** [Complex analysis](complex-analysis-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_number)

A [complex number](complex-number.md) is $z=x+iy$, where $x,y$ are [real numbers](real-number.md) and the [imaginary unit](imaginary-unit.md) satisfies $i^2=-1$. The [real part](real-part.md) is $x$ and the [imaginary part](imaginary-part.md) is $y$: for $3+4i$ they are $3$ and $4$, respectively. Equality requires both coordinates to agree. Thus the [complex plane](complex-plane.md) gives each [complex number](complex-number.md) a concrete position, while ordinary [addition](addition.md) and [multiplication](multiplication.md) become geometric operations.

For $z=3+4i$ and $w=1+i$, [addition](addition.md) works coordinate by coordinate, whereas [multiplication](multiplication.md) uses $i^2=-1$:

$$
z+w=4+5i,\qquad zw=(3+4i)(1+i)=3+7i-4=-1+7i.
$$

These answers encode different motions: [addition](addition.md) by $w$ is a [translation](translation-geometry.md), while [multiplication](multiplication.md) by $w$ is a [rotation](rotation-mathematics.md) combined with a uniform change of size. Keeping these pictures distinct prevents a common beginner's confusion.

The [complex numbers](complex-number.md) form a [field](field.md): every nonzero $z$ has a [multiplicative inverse](multiplicative-inverse.md), computable using its [complex conjugate](complex-conjugate.md) and [modulus](modulus.md). They also supply the missing solutions of a [quadratic equation](quadratic-equation.md) such as $u^2+1=0$, namely $u=\pm i$. The surprising payoff is that extending arithmetic also makes [Euclidean geometry](euclidean-geometry.md) easier to calculate. For a systematic introduction to these definitions, see [Jeremy Orloff, MIT 18.04, topic 1, sections 1.3–1.5](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-04-complex-variables-with-applications-spring-2018/6eae09885b0fab3f068e31752198abea_MIT18_04S18_topic1.pdf).
