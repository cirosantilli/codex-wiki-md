# Isolated attosecond pulse

↑ **Parent:** [Attosecond physics](attosecond-physics.md)

An [isolated attosecond pulse](isolated-attosecond-pulse.md) has one dominant short burst within the observation window, with any satellite emission quantified. A broad [optical spectrum](optical-spectrum.md) alone cannot distinguish one burst from several. Selecting a generation interval and an appropriate spectral band can suppress neighboring bursts, but the resulting temporal contrast must still be tested.

For a simple calculation independent of any apparatus, let $\mathcal E(t)=f(t)+r e^{i\theta}f(t-\Delta)$, with two nonoverlapping copies of a short [optical pulse](optical-pulse.md). Its [optical spectrum](optical-spectrum.md) has intensity

$$
|\widetilde{\mathcal E}(\Omega)|^2
=|\widetilde f(\Omega)|^2\left[1+r^2+2r\cos(\Omega\Delta+\theta)\right].
$$

A satellite produces spectral fringes of angular spacing $2\pi/\Delta$ when the two bursts are coherent and resolved. For chosen field-amplitude ratio $r=0.2$, the satellite/main [energy](energy-split.md) ratio is $r^2=0.04$, while fringe visibility is $2r/(1+r^2)\simeq0.3846$. Weak satellite [energy](energy-split.md) can thus leave substantial fringes. Averaging an uncontrolled $\theta$ removes the interference term without removing the satellite: absence of fringes alone does not prove isolation.
