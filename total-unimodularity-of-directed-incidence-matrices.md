# Total unimodularity of directed incidence matrices

↑ **Parent:** [Totally unimodular matrix](totally-unimodular-matrix.md)

The directed vertex-edge [incidence matrix](incidence-matrix.md) has one $+1$ and one $-1$ per nonloop column and a zero column for a loop. Every square subdeterminant belongs to $\{0,1,-1\}$. Induct on its size: a column with no nonzero entries makes the [determinant](determinant.md) zero; a column with one nonzero entry permits a cofactor expansion to a smaller [determinant](determinant.md); and if every column has two nonzero entries, their opposite signs make the column sums zero, giving linearly dependent rows. Negating rows preserves absolute subdeterminants. Consequently the unsigned vertex-edge incidence [matrix](matrix.md) of a [bipartite graph](bipartite-graph-split.md) is totally unimodular: negate all rows in one part. The [assignment problem](assignment-problem.md) is an instance, with row vertices and column vertices forming the two parts.
