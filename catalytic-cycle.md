# Catalytic cycle

↑ **Parent:** [Catalysis](catalysis.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalytic_cycle)

A [catalytic cycle](catalytic-cycle.md) follows the changing forms of a [catalyst](catalyst.md) through a [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md) until its starting form is regenerated. The schematic binding cycle in [catalyst](catalyst.md) illustrates the bookkeeping: C is consumed and recovered, whereas AC is a [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md) formed and consumed. Cancelling both gives the net A-to-B [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md). Regeneration concerns the completed cycle; it does not imply that C avoids forming [chemical bonds](chemical-bond-split.md) or exchanging [electrons](electron.md). An alternative [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md) may have more [elementary reactions](elementary-reaction.md) than the uncatalysed route and still be faster.[§12.7, Figure 12.19 and Homogeneous Catalysts: alternative steps, intermediate formation and catalyst regeneration](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others.md)

For that reversible binding cycle, ideal [thermodynamic activities](thermodynamic-activity.md) give step [equilibrium constants](equilibrium-constant.md) $K_1=a_{\mathrm{AC}}/(a_{\mathrm A}a_{\mathrm C})$ and $K_2=a_{\mathrm B}a_{\mathrm C}/a_{\mathrm{AC}}$. Their [product](product-of-numbers.md) is $K_1K_2=a_{\mathrm B}/a_{\mathrm A}=K$: the same cancellations occur in the equilibrium bookkeeping. Choose consistent [standard states](standard-state.md) for the species. A proposed cycle with $K_1K_2\ne K$ for the same net conversion at the same [temperature](temperature-split.md) would contradict its specified [thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md). This statement concerns free-species [thermodynamic activities](thermodynamic-activity.md). Appreciable binding in AC can change how the conserved total amount is partitioned among free A, free B and bound AC. An assay counting bound and free material together therefore needs that additional balance; unchanged $a_{\mathrm B}/a_{\mathrm A}$ does not by itself guarantee identical analytical totals when the amount of C changes.
