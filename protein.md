# Protein

↑ **Parent:** [Biology](biology-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein)

A [protein](protein.md) is a biological [molecule](molecule.md) made of one or more [polypeptide chains](polypeptide.md): ordered [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md) joined by [peptide bonds](peptide-bond.md). [Biological translation](translation-biology.md) assembles a chain in the order specified by [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) [codons](codon.md). [Protein folding](protein-folding.md) then brings parts of that chain into spatial relationships that support its function. See [Cooper, The Cell: protein composition and structure](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9879/). These processes can overlap in a [biological cell](biological-cell.md); the sequence-to-shape explanation does not require waiting until [biological translation](translation-biology.md) finishes before any folding begins. See [Cooper, The Cell: protein folding and processing](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9843/).

The useful connection is from information to physical work. A [protein](protein.md)'s [protein primary structure](protein-primary-structure.md) records an order; its [protein tertiary structure](protein-tertiary-structure.md) can bring distant [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md) together at an [active site](active-site.md). An [enzyme](enzyme.md) uses such an arrangement to recognize a [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) and accelerate a [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md). Other [proteins](protein.md) serve as [receptors](receptor-biochemistry.md), [membrane transport proteins](membrane-transport-protein.md) or structural material. For example, [actin](actin.md) assembles into filaments whose mechanical properties can be described by [biopolymer mechanics](biopolymer-mechanics.md). See [Alberts et al., Molecular Biology of the Cell: protein function and binding](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26911/).

Order matters beyond composition. Let A stand for [alanine](alanine.md) and M for [methionine](methionine.md), and read from the [N-terminus](n-terminus.md) to the [C-terminus](c-terminus.md). The illustrative strings AMAMAMAMAMAM and AAAAAAMMMMMM each contain exactly six A and six M [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md). Each has $12-1=11$ [peptide bonds](peptide-bond.md) when made into one unbranched [polypeptide chain](polypeptide.md). In the first string all eleven neighboring pairs have different letters; in the second only the pair at positions 6 and 7 does. A composition [histogram](histogram.md) therefore loses information about which [amino acid residues](amino-acid-residue.md) are neighbors. Their arrangements can affect [protein folding](protein-folding.md) and function; these short invented strings neither specify a known functional [protein](protein.md) nor establish what shape either would adopt.

**[Figure 27](#image-from-amino-acid-order-to-an-imagined-fold-and-binding-site). From amino acid order to an imagined fold and binding site**. Original schematic using two twelve-residue strings with equal composition. The imagined fold uses the first string: colors and numbers preserve its [protein primary structure](protein-primary-structure.md), while positions 4 and 9 approach one another. The binding-site cartoon explains spatial cooperation, not an actual [enzyme](enzyme.md) or a measured [protein structure](protein-structure.md). No actual coordinates, physical scale or exact [protein folding](protein-folding.md) predictor are implied.

[Protein structure](protein-structure.md) is a moving arrangement, not a permanently rigid outline. [Substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) binding can change an [enzyme](enzyme.md)'s shape through [induced fit](induced-fit.md), and a functional [protein](protein.md) can include flexible regions. Sequence, surroundings and interacting [molecules](molecule.md) must therefore be considered together. See [Alberts et al.: binding, conformational changes and protein function](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26911/).
