# Completing the square

↑ **Parent:** [Polynomial](polynomial-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completing_the_square)

Completing the square rewrites a [quadratic polynomial](quadratic-polynomial.md) so that its [minimum](minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) or [maximum](maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) and its [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) become easy to read. The linear term is absorbed into a shift of the [variable](variable-mathematics.md); the resulting [polynomial identity](polynomial-identity.md) changes the description, rather than the values of the [polynomial](polynomial-split.md).

For [coefficients](coefficient.md) $a,b,c$ in the [real numbers](real-number.md), with $a\ne0$,

$$
\boxed{ax^2+bx+c
=a\left(x+\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2
+c-\frac{b^2}{4a}.}
$$

First factor out the [leading coefficient](leading-coefficient-of-a-polynomial.md) from $ax^2+bx$, then add and subtract $(b/(2a))^2$ inside the bracket. The cross term in $(x+b/(2a))^2$ is $(b/a)x$, exactly the term needed. Multiplication by $a$ restores $bx$. Thus the half-coefficient is $b/(2a)$, rather than $b/2$ unless the [quadratic polynomial](quadratic-polynomial.md) is a [monic polynomial](monic-polynomial.md). Expanding the right-hand side verifies the [polynomial identity](polynomial-identity.md) directly.[Intermediate Algebra 2e, §9.2: completing a square and normalizing a nonmonic quadratic](intermediate-algebra-2e-by-lynn-marecek-and-andrea-honeycutt-mathis/9-2-solve-quadratic-equations-by-completing-the-square.md)

For an original example,

$$
x^2+6x+13=(x^2+6x+9)+4=\boxed{(x+3)^2+4}.
$$

For every $x$ in the [real numbers](real-number.md), $(x+3)^2\geq0$, with equality only at $x=-3$. The [minimum](minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) is therefore $4$, reached at $-3$; no [calculus](calculus-split.md) is needed, and there are no [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) in the [real numbers](real-number.md). The expanded [coefficients](coefficient.md) conceal these conclusions more effectively than the completed square does.
