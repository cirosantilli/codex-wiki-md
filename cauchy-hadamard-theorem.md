# Cauchy-Hadamard theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Radius of convergence](radius-of-convergence.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy–Hadamard_theorem)

For the [power series](power-series.md) $\sum_{n\ge0}a_n(z-c)^n$, the [Cauchy-Hadamard theorem](cauchy-hadamard-theorem.md) gives

$$
\frac1R=\limsup_{n\to\infty}|a_n|^{1/n},
$$

with $R=\infty$ when the [limit superior](limit-superior.md) is zero, and $R=0$ when it is infinite. Away from the centre, the [root test](root-test.md) explains the formula: the [limit superior](limit-superior.md) of the term roots at displacement $z-c$ is $|z-c|/R$, interpreted as zero for $R=\infty$ and infinite for $R=0$. Values below one give [absolute convergence](absolute-convergence.md); values above one imply that the terms fail to tend to zero. A value of one leaves the boundary question open. At the centre the series converges regardless of $R$.

For $a_{2n}=1$ and $a_{2n+1}=0$, the coefficient roots alternate between one and zero, so their ordinary [limit](limit-of-a-function.md) does not exist. Their [limit superior](limit-superior.md) is nevertheless one, giving $R=1$ for $\sum_{n\ge0}z^{2n}=1/(1-z^2)$ inside the [unit disk](unit-disk.md). A ratio of consecutive [coefficients](coefficient.md) would be undefined at the zero coefficients; the root-limsup formula handles the gap directly.

In the first part of his 1892 study of [functions](function-split.md) given by [Taylor series](taylor-series.md), Jacques Hadamard introduced an upper-limit formulation for [sequences](sequence.md) and used coefficient roots to determine the [radius of convergence](radius-of-convergence.md). Sections 4–6 separate finite positive, zero, and infinite root-growth cases; the later parts study singularities and continuation. See [Hadamard, Essai sur l'étude des fonctions données par leur développement de Taylor, Journal de mathématiques pures et appliquées, fourth series 8 (1892), pp. 107–109](https://www.numdam.org/item/JMPA_1892_4_8__101_0.pdf). This original text explains the coefficient-growth question without implying that the study of [power series](power-series.md) began there.
