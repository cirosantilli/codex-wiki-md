# Heat transfer

↑ **Parent:** [Heat](heat-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_transfer)

[Heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) asks how quickly [heat](heat-split.md) crosses a boundary and how the [temperature](temperature-split.md) varies along its path. Its principal mechanisms are [thermal conduction](thermal-conduction.md), [convection](convection-split.md) and [thermal radiation](thermal-radiation.md). A warm wall can conduct [heat](heat-split.md) through its interior while exchanging [heat](heat-split.md) with moving air and emitting [thermal radiation](thermal-radiation.md) at its surfaces. Identifying these paths lets us estimate heating requirements and cooling times.

Distinguish the accumulated [heat](heat-split.md) $Q$ from its rate $\dot Q=dQ/dt$, a [power](power.md) measured in $\mathrm W$. The [heat flux](heat-flux-density.md) $q$ is the rate per unit [area](surface-area.md), measured in $\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$: a uniform normal [heat flux](heat-flux-density.md) across [area](surface-area.md) $A$ gives $\dot Q=Aq$. The steady bar in [thermal conduction](thermal-conduction.md) carries $q=400\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ through $A=0.01\,\mathrm{m^2}$, hence $\dot Q=4\,\mathrm W$; maintained for $60\,\mathrm s$, it transfers $Q=240\,\mathrm J$. This distinction makes a surface's intensity and a whole object's heating load easy to compare.
