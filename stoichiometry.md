# Stoichiometry

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoichiometry)

[Stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md) relates the quantities consumed and formed in a [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md). Its coefficients are ratios of entity counts, or equivalently of [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md), rather than ratios of [masses](mass.md). For the [water](water.md) [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md), consuming two [molecular hydrogen](molecular-hydrogen.md) [molecules](molecule.md) per [molecular oxygen](molecular-oxygen.md) [molecule](molecule.md) gives the same $2:1$ ratio as consuming two [moles](mole-unit.md) of [molecular hydrogen](molecular-hydrogen.md) per [mole](mole-unit.md) of [molecular oxygen](molecular-oxygen.md). Multiplying every entity count by the same factor preserves the ratio. See [Flowers et al., Chemistry 2e, reaction stoichiometry](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/4-3-reaction-stoichiometry).

Coefficients and subscripts do different jobs. The coefficient in $2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$ counts two [water](water.md) [molecules](molecule.md); the subscript says that each contains two [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). Changing coefficients scales quantities. Changing a subscript changes the specified [chemical substance](chemical-substance.md).

![](stoichiometry-particle-counts.png)

**[Figure 66](#image-the-same-stoichiometric-ratio-at-molecular-and-molar-scales). The same stoichiometric ratio at molecular and molar scales**. The upper schematic counts individual [molecules](molecule.md); the lower line multiplies each count by the [Avogadro constant](avogadro-constant.md) times one [mole](mole-unit.md). Circle sizes and positions are illustrative.

This distinction catches a common counting error: one [mole](mole-unit.md) of [water](water.md) [molecules](molecule.md) contains two [moles](mole-unit.md) of [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) and one [mole](mole-unit.md) of [oxygen](oxygen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). Always specify what is counted.

[Stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md) records reactant and product multiplicities in a [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md) or a [chemical reaction network](chemical-reaction-network.md). The change caused by one reaction is its [stoichiometric vector](stoichiometric-vector.md); multiplying by the reaction rate gives its contribution to the concentration equations. For two identical reactants the rate-constant convention absorbs any combinatorial factor.
