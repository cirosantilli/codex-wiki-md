# Gas-syringe dead-volume correction

↑ **Parent:** [Gas-syringe measurement of Boyle's law](gas-syringe-measurement-of-boyle-s-law.md)

In a [gas syringe](gas-syringe.md), the graduated barrel reading $V_{\rm read}$ can omit a fixed connecting or sensor [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) $V_d$ occupied by the same sealed [gas](gas.md). For a common settled [temperature](temperature-split.md), [Boyle's law](boyle-s-law.md) then requires

$$
p_1(V_{{\rm read},1}+V_d)=p_2(V_{{\rm read},2}+V_d),\qquad
\frac1p=\frac{V_{\rm read}}{C}+\frac{V_d}{C}.
$$

This second relation makes a graph of $1/p$ against $V_{\rm read}$ a straight line; its intercept divided by its [slope](slope.md) estimates $V_d$ under these assumptions. Unequal [temperatures](temperature-split.md) between the barrel and the connecting space require a different correction, analogous to the [gas thermometer dead-space correction](gas-thermometer-dead-space-correction.md).

For an original hypothetical example, let $V_d=1\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, $V_{{\rm read},1}=20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, $p_1=100\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and $V_{{\rm read},2}=10\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. The total [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) falls from $21$ to $11\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, so $p_2=100(21/11)\,\mathrm{kPa}\approx190.91\,\mathrm{kPa}$, rather than $200\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The omitted [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) makes the total compression ratio smaller than the barrel-reading ratio. These stipulated values are independent of the manufacturer's apparatus correction and are not measurements.
