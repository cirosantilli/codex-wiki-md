# Compact cosine trial bound for the quantum harmonic oscillator

↑ **Parent:** [Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle](rayleigh-ritz-variational-principle.md)

For the dimensionless [quantum harmonic oscillator](quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md) $H=-d^2/dx^2+x^2$, use the compactly supported trial $\psi_\gamma=\cos(\gamma x)$ on $|x|\leq\pi/(2\gamma)$ and zero elsewhere, with $\gamma>0$. It is continuous and belongs to the oscillator energy [quadratic form](quadratic-form-split.md) domain: its [weak derivative](weak-derivative.md) is square-integrable even though its ordinary derivative jumps at the endpoints. Exact [integration](integral-split.md) gives

$$
R(\gamma)=\frac{\int(|\psi_\gamma'|^2+x^2|\psi_\gamma|^2)dx}{\int|\psi_\gamma|^2dx}=\gamma^2+\frac{\pi^2-6}{12\gamma^2}.
$$

The minimum occurs at $\gamma^4=(\pi^2-6)/12$, giving the displayed upper bound, approximately $1.13572$, compared with exact [ground state](ground-state.md) value $1$. This illustrates why [Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle](rayleigh-ritz-variational-principle.md) trials need the energy form domain rather than the smaller [operator domain](operator-domain.md).
