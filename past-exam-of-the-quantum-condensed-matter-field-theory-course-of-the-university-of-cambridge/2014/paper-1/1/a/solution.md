<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the even periodic chain, every classical [Néel state](../../../../../neel-state.md) $\mathbf S_m=(-1)^mS\mathbf n$ has energy $-JNS^2$, with arbitrary unit vector $\mathbf n$. Thus there is a **continuous sphere of classical orientations**, not merely two Ising-like states. Opposite staggered directions are connected by a global spin rotation.

A quantum Néel product is not an eigenstate: transverse exchange flips opposite neighbouring spins. The ground state therefore contains quantum superpositions and zero-point [spin waves](../../../../../spin-wave.md), reducing the classical staggered moment. A finite isotropic balanced chain has a symmetry-invariant ground state; a symmetry-broken reference is an approximation for analysing fluctuations, not its exact finite-system eigenstate.
