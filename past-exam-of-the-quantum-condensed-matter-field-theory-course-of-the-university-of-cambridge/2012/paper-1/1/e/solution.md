<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The [Hellmann–Feynman theorem](../../../../../hellmann-feynman-theorem.md) applied to the source field normalization gives

$$
\frac{\partial E_0}{\partial g}=\left\langle\frac{\partial H}{\partial g}\right\rangle
=-2J\sum_n\langle S_n^z\rangle.
$$

Differentiating the [Ising-chain ground-state energy](../../../../../ising-chain-ground-state-energy.md), using $\partial_g\epsilon_k=4J^2(g-\cos k)/\epsilon_k$, therefore proves

$$
\boxed{\sum_n\langle S_n^z\rangle=\sum_k\frac{J(g-\cos k)}{\epsilon_k}.}
$$

This is the [Ising-chain field derivative and transverse polarization](../../../../../ising-chain-field-derivative-and-transverse-polarization.md), not its spontaneous x-order parameter. It tends to zero at g=0 and to N/2 at large positive field. The [critical transverse polarization of an Ising chain](../../../../../critical-transverse-polarization-of-an-ising-chain.md) is finite: in the thermodynamic critical limit this gives $\langle S^z\rangle=1/\pi$. A zero-energy isolated momentum is treated by the appropriate limiting state, rather than by assigning a value to a literal 0/0 summand.
