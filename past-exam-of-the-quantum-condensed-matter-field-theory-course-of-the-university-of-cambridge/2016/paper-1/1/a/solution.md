For the [XXZ spin chain](../../../../../xxz-spin-chain.md), write $\Delta=1+\alpha$ and replace each [spin](../../../../../spin.md) by a classical [vector](../../../../../vector.md) of length $S$. A bond is $JS^2\mathbf n_m^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{diag}(1,1,\Delta)\mathbf n_{m+1}$, whose minimum is $-JS^2\max(1,|\Delta|)$. For an even periodic chain all bonds can attain that bound simultaneously. The [classical ground states of an XXZ spin chain](../../../../../classical-ground-states-of-an-xxz-spin-chain.md) therefore give **$\alpha>0$ gives the two easy-axis Néel states** $\mathbf S_m=(-1)^m S\widehat{\mathbf z}$ and its reversal, with energy $-JNS^2(1+\alpha)$.

For **$-2<\alpha<0$ the [ground states](../../../../../ground-state.md) are planar Néel configurations**, $\mathbf S_m=(-1)^m S(\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi,0)$, with arbitrary $\varphi$: their degeneracy is a circle and their energy is $-JNS^2$. This includes the easy-plane interval used later. Part (a), however, does not restrict negative $\alpha$ to that interval. For $\alpha<-2$, $\Delta<-1$ and the lowest states are instead **the two uniform $z$-polarized ferromagnetic configurations**, with energy $JN\Delta S^2$. At $\alpha=-2$, a rotation by $\pi$ about $z$ on alternating sites maps the classical energy to an isotropic [ferromagnet](../../../../../ferromagnet.md): $\mathbf S_m=S((-1)^m n_x,(-1)^m n_y,n_z)$ for any [unit vector](../../../../../unit-vector.md) $\mathbf n$ is a [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md). This special degeneracy is a sphere. At the other boundary, $\alpha=0$, the ordinary Néel direction is arbitrary on a sphere. These statements concern classical minimizing configurations, not the degeneracy of the exact finite-chain quantum [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md).
