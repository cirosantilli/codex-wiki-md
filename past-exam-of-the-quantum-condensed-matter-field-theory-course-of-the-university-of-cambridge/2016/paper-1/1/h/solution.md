<h1 id="1/h/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [H](../h.md)

In this part $0<\Delta=1+\alpha<1$, so the ordered reference lies in the $xy$ plane. Choosing one direction breaks the continuous rotation [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) within that plane. **A gapless, linearly dispersing phase mode is therefore expected** in the spin-wave approximation, while tilting the Néel axis out of the plane costs anisotropy energy. It is incorrect to continue the easy-axis expansion about an unstable $z$-ordered reference and interpret its imaginary frequencies as physical modes.

For the [easy-plane XXZ antiferromagnetic spin-wave spectrum](../../../../../easy-plane-xxz-antiferromagnetic-spin-wave-spectrum.md), take the Néel direction along old $x$, then rotate alternating local [spins](../../../../../spin.md) so that all longitudinal components point up. One such local frame gives the full-zone quadratic coefficients

$$
A_k=1+\frac{\Delta-1}{2}\cos k,\qquad B_k=\frac{\Delta+1}{2}\cos k,
$$

and therefore

$$
\boxed{\epsilon_k=2JS\sqrt{(1-\cos k)(1+\Delta\cos k)}.}
$$

Near $k=0$, $\epsilon_k\simeq JS\sqrt{2(1+\Delta)}|k|$. The zone-edge mode has gap $\epsilon_\pi=2JS\sqrt{2(1-\Delta)}$. A different sublattice phase convention shifts the zero by $\pi$; folding into the magnetic zone displays an acoustic and a gapped polarization. The figure uses this explicitly chosen frame. One-dimensional long-wavelength fluctuations again limit the ordered-reference interpretation.
