<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a uniform time-independent saddle, extremizing the [action](../../../../../action.md) gives $(-\mu+g|\psi_0|^2)\psi_0=0$. Its grand-potential density is $f(\rho)=-\mu\rho+g\rho^2/2$, with $\rho=|\psi_0|^2\ge0$. Since $g>0$, the minimum at positive density for $\mu>0$ is

$$
\boxed{\rho_0=\mu/g,\qquad
\psi_0=\sqrt{\rho_0}\,e^{i\phi_0},\qquad
f_{\min}=-\mu^2/(2g).}
$$

For $\mu\le0$ the uniform mean-field minimum is the vacuum, rather than a positive-density condensate. At fixed nonzero density in the weakly interacting condensed regime, the saddle relation sets $\mu\simeq g\rho_0$. The mean-field one-body [density matrix](../../../../../density-matrix-split.md) factorizes as $\langle a^\dagger(\mathbf r)a(\mathbf r')\rangle\simeq\rho_0$, giving macroscopic occupation of the uniform mode. Depletion corrections are omitted at this stage.

The [action](../../../../../action.md) has the global particle-number [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) $\psi\mapsto e^{i\alpha}\psi$, $\bar\psi\mapsto e^{-i\alpha}\bar\psi$. Choosing one saddle phase breaks this $U(1)$ [symmetry](../../../../../symmetry-physics.md); the degenerate mean-field minima form the circle **$U(1)\simeq S^1$**. There is no energetic preference for a uniform value of $\phi_0$, but spatially varying phase costs [gradient energy](../../../../../gradient-energy.md). Consequently a neutral condensate has a gapless [Goldstone mode](../../../../../goldstone-boson.md) and a nonzero [phase stiffness](../../../../../phase-stiffness.md). The mode becomes sound once density dynamics is included. With $\hbar=1$ the [superfluid velocity](../../../../../superfluid-velocity.md) is $\nabla\phi/m$, so a single-valued field allows circulation in units $2\pi/m$ around a closed loop that avoids zeros of the amplitude. This phase coherence and rigidity are the physical content of the broken-symmetry description.

The claim is a mean-field saddle statement, not an assertion that every $d$ and [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) in the question supports true [long-range order](../../../../../long-range-order.md). In a finite number-conserving system $\langle a\rangle=0$; the symmetry-breaking [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) is defined with the [thermodynamic limit](../../../../../thermodynamic-limit.md) taken before an infinitesimal source is removed. In a homogeneous infinite short-range gas at nonzero [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), infrared phase fluctuations prohibit true condensation in $d\le2$. In one dimension they also destroy true condensate [long-range order](../../../../../long-range-order.md) at zero [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). A two-dimensional gas can nevertheless have [superfluidity](../../../../../superfluidity.md) and algebraically decaying correlations below a [Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition](../../../../../berezinskii-kosterlitz-thouless-transition.md). A finite trap can have substantial coherence without contradicting these thermodynamic statements.[abstract and pp.383–386; finite-temperature restriction](../../../../../existence-of-long-range-order-in-one-and-two-dimensions-by-pierre-hohenberg.md)
