<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the uniform [mean-field approximation](../../../../../mean-field-approximation.md) field $\mathbf M=M\hat{\mathbf e}_z$, the inverse fermion propagator is diagonal in [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md), frequency and spin: $-i\omega_l+\xi_k-\sigma M$. The [Grassmann Gaussian integral](../../../../../grassmann-gaussian-integral.md) therefore gives

$$
Z_{\rm uniform}=\int d\mu(M)\,e^{-\beta NM^2/(2U)}\prod_{k,\sigma=\pm1}\left[1+e^{-\beta(\xi_k-\sigma M)}\right].
$$

Here $d\mu(M)$ denotes the residual uniform-field measure; integration over its orientation only affects a nonextensive prefactor. Hence

$$
\boxed{S_{\rm eff}(M)=\frac{\beta NM^2}{2U}-\sum_{k,\sigma=\pm1}\ln\left[1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon_k-\mu-\sigma M)}\right].}
$$

The exchange field separates the two spin bands by $2M$. Integrating out the fermions is exact for a specified auxiliary field; restricting that field to be constant is the [mean-field approximation](../../../../../mean-field-approximation.md).
