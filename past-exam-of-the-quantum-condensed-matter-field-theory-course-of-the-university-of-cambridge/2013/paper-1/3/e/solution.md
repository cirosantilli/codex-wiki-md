<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Differentiate the uniform [effective action](../../../../../effective-action.md):

$$
0=\frac1\beta\frac{\partial S_{\rm eff}}{\partial M}=\frac{NM}{U}-\sum_{k,\sigma}\sigma n_F(\epsilon_k-\sigma M).
$$

To reproduce the printed coefficient, define the **spin-summed density of states per site** by

$$
\nu(\epsilon)=\frac2N\sum_k\delta(\epsilon-\epsilon_k),\qquad
\frac1N\sum_k f(\epsilon_k)=\frac12\int d\epsilon\,\nu(\epsilon)f(\epsilon).
$$

The [saddle-point approximation](../../../../../saddle-point-approximation.md) then gives

$$
\boxed{M=\frac U2\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}\int d\epsilon\,\nu(\epsilon)\,\sigma n_F(\epsilon-\sigma M).}
$$

The [uniform spin field and magnetization](../../../../../uniform-spin-field-and-magnetization.md) obey $M=U(n_\uparrow-n_\downarrow)=2U\langle S_z\rangle$ for occupations per site. If a one-spin [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md) were used instead, the factor $1/2$ would be absent. The spin field is an energy, rather than directly a magnetic moment.
