# X-ray diffraction

↑ **Parent:** [Crystal scattering](crystal-scattering.md)

[X-ray diffraction](x-ray-diffraction.md) is the angular redistribution of [X-ray](x-ray.md) [wave intensity](intensity-physics.md) through [wave interference](interference-wave-propagation.md) between scattered waves. In a [crystal structure](crystal-structure.md), repeated [lattice translations](lattice-translation.md) select discrete outgoing directions by the [Laue equations](laue-equations.md). A [photographic plate](photographic-plate.md) can therefore turn invisible atomic periodicity into a visible constellation of spots. Their geometry constrains the [Bravais lattice](bravais-lattice.md) and its orientation; their [wave intensities](intensity-physics.md) also depend on the [crystal basis structure factor](crystal-basis-structure-factor.md). Determining atomic positions belongs to [X-ray crystallography](x-ray-crystallography.md) and requires more information than recognizing a spot pattern.

The [Laue X-ray diffraction experiment of 1912](laue-x-ray-diffraction-experiment-of-1912.md) established this phenomenon experimentally. The [Laue method](laue-method.md) uses a fixed [crystal structure](crystal-structure.md) with a range of incident [wavelengths](wavelength.md); the [Bragg crystal reflection experiment of 1913](bragg-crystal-reflection-experiment-of-1913.md) instead measured angularly resolved signals with a moving detector. These are different ways of finding the same [coherent elastic scattering](coherent-elastic-scattering.md) directions.
