# Spherical wedge

↑ **Parent:** [Sphere](sphere.md)

A [spherical wedge](spherical-wedge.md) is the portion of a solid [sphere](sphere.md) between two meridional half-planes meeting at a dihedral angle $\beta$, with $0<\beta<2\pi$. Put the common axis along $z$ and its azimuthal bisector along $x$. For radius $a$, use [spherical coordinates](spherical-coordinate-system.md) $0\leq r\leq a$, $0\leq\theta\leq\pi$, $-\beta/2\leq\varphi\leq\beta/2$. Its [volume](volume.md) is $2\beta a^3/3$.

For constant [mass density](density.md), reflection symmetries put the [centre of mass](center-of-mass.md) on the bisector. The first moment is

$$
\int_Vx\,dV=\left(\int_0^a r^3\,dr\right)
\left(\int_0^\pi\sin^2\theta\,d\theta\right)
\left(\int_{-\beta/2}^{\beta/2}\cos\varphi\,d\varphi\right)
=\frac{\pi a^4}{4}\sin\frac\beta2.
$$

Dividing by [volume](volume.md) gives $\bar x=3\pi a\sin(\beta/2)/(8\beta)$. It decreases from the limiting value $3\pi a/16$ for an infinitesimal wedge to zero for a full sphere.

The curved boundary is a [spherical lune](spherical-lune.md). Its outward [vector area](vector-area.md) is $\pi a^2\sin(\beta/2)\mathbf e_x$. Indeed, its $x$ component has the same angular integrals as the first moment, multiplied by $a^2$; the other components cancel by symmetry. Its scalar [surface area](surface-area.md) is $2\beta a^2$, which is generally different from the magnitude of its [vector area](vector-area.md).
