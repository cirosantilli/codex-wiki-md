# Electrostatics

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrostatics)

[Electrostatics](electrostatics-split.md) studies time-independent [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) distributions and their [electric fields](electric-field-split.md). In [vacuum](vacuum.md), its local equations in [SI](international-system-of-units.md) units are

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0},\qquad \nabla\times\mathbf E=\mathbf0,\qquad \mathbf E=-\nabla\Phi,\qquad \nabla^2\Phi=-\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}.
$$

Here $\rho$ is [charge density](charge-density.md), $\epsilon_0$ is [vacuum permittivity](vacuum-permittivity.md), and $\Phi$ is [electric potential](electric-potential.md). [Gauss's law](gauss-s-law.md) supplies the [divergence](divergence.md) equation; the static form of [Faraday's law of induction](faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) supplies the zero [curl](curl.md). The [Poisson equation](poisson-s-equation.md) becomes [Laplace's equation](laplace-equation.md) where there is no [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md). Prescribed [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) give [electric fields](electric-field-split.md) through [Coulomb's law](coulomb-s-law.md) and the [superposition principle](superposition-principle.md); mobile [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) on an [electrical conductor](electrical-conductor.md) instead require solving for their distribution together with the [electric potential](electric-potential.md). [Richard Fitzpatrick, Electrostatics and magnetostatics: field equations and boundary data](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/em/lectures/node40.html).

The useful distinction is between prescribed sources and prescribed [boundary conditions](boundary-condition.md). On a grounded [electrical conductor](electrical-conductor.md), an external connection fixes its [electric potential](electric-potential.md) relative to an [electrical reference potential](electrical-reference-potential.md) and allows [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) to enter or leave. On an isolated [electrical conductor](electrical-conductor.md), total [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) is fixed and its constant [electric potential](electric-potential.md) is an unknown. A neutral isolated [electrical conductor](electrical-conductor.md) can therefore have a nonzero local [surface charge density](surface-charge-density.md) through [electrostatic induction](electrostatic-induction.md). Equal [electric potentials](electric-potential.md) also do not imply equal [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md): [charge sharing between distant connected spheres](charge-sharing-between-distant-connected-spheres.md) gives a simple geometric example. [Feynman, Leighton and Sands, II.6: charged conductors and connected-sphere estimates](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_06.html#Ch6-S6).

[Electrostatics](electrostatics-split.md) is useful because a spatial [boundary value problem](boundary-value-problem-split.md) replaces the need to follow every moving [charge carrier](charge-carrier.md) during [charge relaxation](charge-relaxation.md). It describes the settled state of a [capacitor](capacitor.md) and explains [electrostatic shielding](electrostatic-shielding.md) of sensitive equipment. Its assumptions must match the apparatus: stationary boundaries and sources, negligible time variation of the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md), and enough time for [charge relaxation](charge-relaxation.md). A metal carrying a maintained [electric current](electric-current.md) through finite [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) generally needs a nonzero internal [electric field](electric-field-split.md) by [Ohm's law](ohm-s-law.md); it is not a [conductor in electrostatic equilibrium](conductor-in-electrostatic-equilibrium.md). Static macroscopic [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) also does not mean that every [electron](electron.md) is motionless. [Feynman, Leighton and Sands, II.7.1: conductor potentials as boundary-value problems](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_07.html#Ch7-S1).
