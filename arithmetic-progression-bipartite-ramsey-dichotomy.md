# Arithmetic-progression bipartite Ramsey dichotomy

↑ **Parent:** [Gallai theorem for an integer lattice](gallai-theorem-for-an-integer-lattice.md)

Every red-blue [finite coloring](finite-coloring.md) of the pairs of [positive integers](positive-integer.md) has either a blue complete [arithmetic progression](arithmetic-progression.md) of any prescribed finite length $m$, or two disjoint $m$-term [arithmetic progressions](arithmetic-progression.md) with all cross-pairs red. To see the geometry, assign $(a,d)$ a chosen red pair of indices $(r,s)$ in its progression, assuming the first alternative fails. Apply the [Gallai theorem for an integer lattice](gallai-theorem-for-an-integer-lattice.md) to the fixed finite pattern

$$
\bigcup_{0\leq r<s<m}\{(si-rj,j-i):0\leq i,j<m\}.
$$

In a [monochromatic](monochromatic-set.md) copy $(a_0,d_0)+qF$ with common label $(r,s)$, the selected red pairs are precisely

$$
\bigl(a_0+rd_0+q(s-r)i,\ a_0+sd_0+q(s-r)j\bigr).
$$

The copy lies in the positive quadrant, so $d_0>q(m-1)$. Consequently the two displayed [arithmetic progressions](arithmetic-progression.md) are disjoint. Translating the finite pattern before applying the lattice theorem permits its initially negative coordinates.
