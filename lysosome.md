# Lysosome

↑ **Parent:** [Organelle](organelle.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysosome)

A [lysosome](lysosome.md) is an acidic digestive and recycling [organelle](organelle.md): one surrounding [lipid bilayer](lipid-bilayer.md) encloses a [lysosomal lumen](lysosomal-lumen.md) containing [acid hydrolases](acid-hydrolase.md). These [enzymes](enzyme-split.md) break down [proteins](protein-split.md), [nucleic acids](nucleic-acid.md), [carbohydrates](carbohydrate.md) and [lipids](lipid.md) by [hydrolysis](hydrolysis.md). Cargo can originate outside the [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md), entering through [endocytosis](endocytosis.md), or within its own [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md), delivered through [autophagy](autophagy.md). Digestion therefore serves both disposal and reuse.[The Cell: A Molecular Approach, 2nd edition by Geoffrey M. Cooper; Cooper, The Cell, 2nd edition (2000), Lysosomes: opening paragraph; Lysosomal Acid Hydrolases; Phagocytosis and Autophagy, Figure 9.37](the-cell-a-molecular-approach-2nd-edition-by-geoffrey-m-cooper/lysosomes.md)

**[Figure 13](#image-lysosome-compartment-cargo-delivery-and-recycling). Lysosome compartment, cargo delivery and recycling**. Original schematic: [endocytosis](endocytosis.md) and [macroautophagy](macroautophagy.md) supply material for digestion, while [secretory protein routing](secretory-protein-routing.md) supplies digestive [enzymes](enzyme-split.md). The enclosure at the right distinguishes [lysosomal lumen](lysosomal-lumen.md) from [cytosol](cytosol.md); arrows indicate routes, not measured rates. Shapes, sizes and cargo counts are illustrative.

An [endosome](endosome.md) sorts endocytosed cargo: some returns to the [cell membrane](cell-membrane-split.md), while some proceeds toward [lysosomes](lysosome.md). An [autophagosome](autophagosome.md) instead encloses material already in the [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md). Both routes bring cargo into contact with digestive [enzymes](enzyme-split.md); neither implies that every internalized [molecule](molecule-split.md) is destroyed.[Alberts and others, Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition (2002), Multiple Pathways Deliver Materials to Lysosomes, Figure 13-35](molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others/transport-from-the-trans-golgi-network-to-lysosomes.md)

Enzyme supply is a different itinerary from cargo disposal. [Secretory protein routing](secretory-protein-routing.md) and [protein targeting](protein-targeting.md) deliver newly made digestive [enzymes](enzyme-split.md) through the [endoplasmic reticulum](endoplasmic-reticulum.md), [Golgi apparatus](golgi-apparatus.md) and [trans-Golgi network](trans-golgi-network.md) toward [lysosomes](lysosome.md).[The Cell: A Molecular Approach, 2nd edition by Geoffrey M. Cooper; Protein Sorting and Export from the Golgi Apparatus, lysosomal enzyme targeting](the-cell-a-molecular-approach-2nd-edition-by-geoffrey-m-cooper/the-golgi-apparatus.md) The existing [membrane transport](membrane-transport.md) and [differential centrifugation](differential-centrifugation.md) articles explain the boundary-crossing and separation principles used below.

A [proteasome](proteasome.md) is a different digestive machine: a [protein](protein-split.md) complex without a surrounding [lipid bilayer](lipid-bilayer.md), acting on individual [proteins](protein-split.md) in the [cytosol](cytosol.md) and [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md). Many targets carry [ubiquitin](ubiquitin.md) chains. A [lysosome](lysosome.md) accepts a much broader range of cargo, including material delivered inside an [autophagosome](autophagosome.md). Thus an unwanted soluble [protein](protein-split.md) and an unwanted [mitochondrion](mitochondria.md) pose different disposal problems; a [proteasome](proteasome.md) does not swallow an intact [mitochondrion](mitochondria.md).[The Cell: A Molecular Approach, 2nd edition by Geoffrey M. Cooper; Cooper, The Cell, 2nd edition (2000), The Ubiquitin-Proteasome Pathway, Figure 7.39; Lysosomal Proteolysis, Figure 7.41](the-cell-a-molecular-approach-2nd-edition-by-geoffrey-m-cooper/protein-degradation.md)
