# Conjugate holomorphic functions do not exhaust the Bergman orthogonal complement

↑ **Parent:** [Bergman space](bergman-space.md)

In the area [Hilbert space](hilbert-space-split.md) of the unit disk, conjugates of [Bergman space](bergman-space.md) functions vanishing at zero are orthogonal to all holomorphic functions, since $\int_Dhg\,dA=\pi h(0)g(0)$. This follows from the angular mean-value property and the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md), which makes the product integrable. But the radial function $|z|^2-1/2$ is also orthogonal to every holomorphic function: its radial integral against the constant angular mean is zero. It is not conjugate holomorphic, since it is real-valued and nonconstant. Thus the conjugate-holomorphic family is a proper subset of the [orthogonal complement](orthogonal-complement.md).
