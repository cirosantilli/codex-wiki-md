# Fluorescence lifetime

↑ **Parent:** [Fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md)

For the constant-rate model in [fluorescence quantum yield](fluorescence-quantum-yield.md), [fluorescence lifetime](fluorescence-lifetime.md) is the mean residence [time](time-in-physics.md) in the emitting [excited state](excited-state.md) and the $1/e$ timescale of its [exponential decay](exponential-decay.md). The detected fluorescent [photon](photon.md) rate after an ideal instantaneous pulse is $I(t)=\eta k_{\rm r}N_0e^{-t/\tau}$ for $t\geq0$. Its amplitude and its decay timescale are separate quantities.

For a stipulated example, rates $(k_{\rm r},k_{\rm nr})=(0.05,0.20)\,\mathrm{ns}^{-1}$ give $\tau=4\,\mathrm{ns}$ and $\Phi=0.20$. Rates $(0.20,0.05)\,\mathrm{ns}^{-1}$ give the same $\tau$ but $\Phi=0.80$. Conversely, doubling $N_0$ doubles the ideal amplitude without changing $\tau$. Equal [fluorescence lifetimes](fluorescence-lifetime.md) need not imply equal brightness or equal [fluorescence quantum yields](fluorescence-quantum-yield.md).

Different environments can supply several populations, giving $I(t)=\sum_j A_j e^{-t/\tau_j}$ rather than one [exponential decay](exponential-decay.md). A fitted single timescale then depends on the selected model and detection band. [FRET donor lifetime](fret-donor-lifetime.md) uses the change in a donor's decay when transfer introduces an additional loss route; the [FRET](forster-resonance-energy-transfer.md) branch develops that interpretation.
