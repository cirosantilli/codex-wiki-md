# Conductivity-gradient term in magnetic induction

↑ **Parent:** [Resistive induction equation](resistive-induction-equation.md)

For scalar [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) $\sigma(\mathbf x)$ and constant permeability $\mu_0$, the moving-fluid Ohm law and the quasistatic [Ampere-Maxwell law](ampere-s-circuital-law.md) give $\mathbf E=-\mathbf u\times\mathbf B+\eta\nabla\times\mathbf B$, where $\eta=1/(\mu_0\sigma)$ is the [magnetic diffusivity](magnetic-diffusivity.md). Taking minus the [curl](curl.md) and using [Faraday's law of induction](faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) gives $\partial_t\mathbf B=\nabla\times(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B)-\nabla\times(\eta\nabla\times\mathbf B)$. Expanding this expression and imposing $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$ yields the title equation. The familiar constant-diffusivity [resistive induction equation](resistive-induction-equation.md) omits the last term because $\nabla\eta=0$; nonrelativistic motion alone does not establish that simplification.
