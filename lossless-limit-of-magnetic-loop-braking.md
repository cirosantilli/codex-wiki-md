# Lossless limit of magnetic loop braking

↑ **Parent:** [Terminal speed of a falling conducting loop](terminal-speed-of-a-falling-conducting-loop.md)

For an external loop flux $Kz$, positive current orientation and upward [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md) $KI$, including [self-inductance](self-inductance.md) gives $\mathcal L\dot I+RI=-K\dot z$ and $m\ddot z=-mg+KI$. At zero resistance total linked flux is conserved. If initially $I=0$ at $z=z_0$, then $I=-K(z-z_0)/\mathcal L$ and the mechanical motion is an undamped oscillator about $z_0-mg\mathcal L/K^2$. The finite-resistance terminal speed tends to zero as $R\to0$, but neglecting [self-inductance](self-inductance.md) is a singular approximation in that limit. A lossless loop does not dispose of [energy](energy.md) through [Joule heating](joule-heating.md).
