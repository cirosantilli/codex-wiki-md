# Precipitation (chemistry)

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_(chemistry))

[Precipitation](precipitation-chemistry.md) is formation of a [solid](solid.md) from a [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md); the formed [solid](solid.md) is a [precipitate](precipitate.md). Dissolved [chemical species](chemical-species.md) enter a separate [thermodynamic phase](thermodynamic-phase.md). A newly formed [solid](solid.md) can create a surface for [heterogeneous catalysis](heterogeneous-catalysis.md); observing it does not establish the composition or catalytic role of that surface.

For example, mixing suitable [chemical solutions](solution-chemistry.md) of [calcium chloride](calcium-chloride.md) and [sodium carbonate](sodium-carbonate.md) can form [calcium carbonate](calcium-carbonate.md):

$$
\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}(aq)+\mathrm{CO}_3^{2-}(aq)\longrightarrow\mathrm{CaCO}_3(s).
$$

Here $aq$ means dissolved in [water](water.md), and $s$ marks the [solid](solid.md). Both sides contain one [calcium](calcium.md) [chemical atom](atom-physics.md), one [carbon](carbon.md) [chemical atom](atom-physics.md), and three [oxygen](oxygen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md); net [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) is $+2-2=0$ on the left and zero on the right. The arrow describes the net change when [precipitation](precipitation-chemistry.md) occurs, rather than promising complete removal of either [ion](ion.md). The [calcium carbonate precipitation experiment](calcium-carbonate-precipitation-experiment.md) makes this phase separation visible.[Carbon Captured Chalk, Summary and Background for Teachers: sodium carbonate and calcium chloride form calcium carbonate](carbon-captured-chalk-by-tony-butterfield.md)

The attraction is that an invisible dissolved inventory becomes a recoverable [solid](solid.md). [Net ionic equations](net-ionic-equation.md) track that transfer; the [saturation criterion for precipitation](saturation-criterion-for-precipitation.md) determines whether it is favored under specified conditions. This combination supports separations and chemical analysis.[Chemistry 2e, §4.2, Precipitation Reactions and Solubility Rules: separations and analytical uses](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others.md)
