# Information entropy

↑ **Parent:** [Information theory](information-theory-split.md)

For a finite [discrete random variable](discrete-random-variable.md) with [probability mass function](probability-mass-function.md) $p$, its information entropy is the [expected value](expected-value-split.md) of its [information content](information-content.md):

$$
H(X)=-\sum_{x:p(x)>0}p(x)\log_2p(x)
=\mathbb E[-\log_2p(X)].
$$

Base-two [logarithms](logarithm.md) give [bits](bit.md); choosing a different base rescales the units. The convention $0\log_2 0=0$ is justified by a [limit](limit-of-a-function.md), so outcomes of zero [probability](probability.md) contribute nothing. The definition also applies to countable distributions, but the sum may be infinite.

A fair [bit](bit.md) has $H=1$. A certain outcome has $H=0$. A [Bernoulli random variable](bernoulli-distribution.md) that is one with [probability](probability.md) $0.1$ has $H=h_2(0.1)\approx0.469$ [bits](bit.md), because most of its outcomes are predictable. A rare outcome still has large [information content](information-content.md): the outcome of [probability](probability.md) $0.1$ contributes $-\log_2(0.1)\approx3.322$ [bits](bit.md) of surprise when it occurs. **Entropy is the average surprise, rather than the surprise of each outcome.**

For $m$ possible outcomes, $0\leq H(X)\leq\log_2m$, with the upper bound attained exactly by the [uniform distribution on a finite set](discrete-uniform-distribution.md). The [Gibbs inequality](gibbs-inequality.md) proves this by comparing $p$ with the uniform distribution: $D(p\Vert u)=\log_2m-H(X)\geq0$. Thus increasing predictability reduces the required average description length. The four-symbol [prefix code](prefix-code.md) in [information theory](information-theory-split.md) attains $H=7/4$ [bits](bit.md) exactly; for general probabilities, integer codeword lengths prevent such a perfect match for every individual symbol. [Shannon's source coding theorem](shannon-s-source-coding-theorem.md) explains how coding sufficiently long independent blocks approaches the entropy per symbol.

The [logarithm](logarithm.md) is especially natural because [independent](independent-random-variables.md) outcomes have additive surprise: $-\log_2(pq)=-\log_2p-\log_2q$. Taking [expected values](expected-value-split.md) gives additive [information entropy](information-entropy-split.md) for [independent random variables](independent-random-variables.md); for dependent ones, the [chain rule for information entropy](chain-rule-for-information-entropy.md) replaces the second term by [conditional entropy](conditional-entropy.md). This is why the same quantity can govern both a single observation and a long message.
