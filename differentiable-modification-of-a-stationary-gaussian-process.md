# Differentiable modification of a stationary Gaussian process

↑ **Parent:** [Mean-square derivative of a Gaussian process](mean-square-derivative-of-a-gaussian-process.md)

For a centered [Gaussian process](gaussian-process.md) with [covariance function](covariance-function.md) $K(s-t)$, twice continuous differentiability of $K$ and differentiability of $K''$ at zero imply a differentiable [modification of a stochastic process](modification-of-a-stochastic-process.md) on compact intervals. The [even function](even-function.md) $K''$ satisfies $K'''(0)=0$, so the mean-square derivative $Y$ has [variance](variance-split.md) of increments $2(K''(h)-K''(0))=o(|h|)$. A [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md) has fourth absolute moment three times the square of its [variance](variance-split.md), so [Kolmogorov continuity theorem](kolmogorov-continuity-theorem.md) gives a [continuous modification](continuous-modification.md) of $Y$. The [mean-square fundamental theorem of calculus](mean-square-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) identifies its path integral with a [modification of a stochastic process](modification-of-a-stochastic-process.md) of $X$. This argument does not require integrability of $K$ or continuity of $K'''$.
