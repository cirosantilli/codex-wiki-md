<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

**The characteristic effect is an irradiation-induced upward shift of the [ductile-to-brittle transition](../../elasticity-physics.md#ductile-to-brittle-transition) in ferritic [steel](../../chemical-substance.md#steel).** A component previously used above its transition can then fail by [cleavage fracture](../../elasticity-physics.md#cleavage-fracture) at the same service [temperature](../../temperature.md), with much less warning from plastic deformation.

[Ferrite](../../chemical-substance.md#ferrite) has a [body-centered cubic lattice](../../quantum-scattering.md#body-centered-cubic-lattice). At low [temperature](../../temperature.md), [dislocation glide](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-glide) becomes difficult enough that brittle crack propagation can precede substantial plastic relaxation. [Irradiation hardening](../../engineering.md#irradiation-hardening) raises the [yield strength](../../engineering.md#yield-strength) by introducing obstacles to [dislocation glide](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-glide); the competition with [cleavage fracture](../../elasticity-physics.md#cleavage-fracture) consequently shifts toward brittle failure. [Austenite](../../chemical-substance.md#austenite), with its [face-centered cubic lattice](../../quantum-scattering.md#face-centered-cubic-lattice), does not normally show this same sharp cleavage transition. This does not make austenitic [steel](../../chemical-substance.md#steel) immune to loss of [ductility](../../engineering.md#ductility), [helium embrittlement](../../engineering.md#helium-embrittlement) or other [radiation damage in materials](../../engineering.md#radiation-damage-in-materials).

Examine the fracture surface for [cleavage fracture](../../elasticity-physics.md#cleavage-fracture) facets and river patterns, and compare [Charpy impact test](../../engineering.md#charpy-impact-test) energy-versus-[temperature](../../temperature.md) curves from exposed material and an unirradiated reference of the same composition and heat treatment. The reference is important: a low impact energy from one failed specimen cannot establish an irradiation-induced shift. Transition curves can also use lateral expansion or the proportion of ductile fracture appearance.[§2, Background: absorbed energy, lateral expansion, shear fracture appearance and transition curves](../../engineering.md#nist-software-package-for-obtaining-charpy-transition-curves-by-enrico-lucon-and-others)

A direct [fracture toughness](../../elasticity-physics.md#fracture-toughness) test over the transition region complements the [Charpy impact test](../../engineering.md#charpy-impact-test); the two measurements are not interchangeable. Combine these observations with exposure history, local [temperature](../../temperature.md), [microstructure](../../engineering.md#microstructure) and crack location. Brittle appearance supports the failure mechanism, while a measured transition shift and suitable controls support its attribution to [irradiation](../../physics.md#irradiation).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

**Neutron capture by boron-10 can generate [helium](../../chemical-substance.md#helium), leading to [helium embrittlement](../../engineering.md#helium-embrittlement), especially at elevated [temperature](../../temperature.md).** The relevant [nuclear reaction](../../physics.md#nuclear-reaction) is

$$
{}^{10}_{5}\mathrm B+{}^{1}_{0}n\longrightarrow{}^{7}_{3}\mathrm{Li}+{}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He}.
$$

The emitted alpha particle eventually becomes a [helium](../../chemical-substance.md#helium) atom in the metal. The product branch can also involve gamma emission, without changing this helium-producing balance. The production depends on boron isotopic content and the neutron energy spectrum; a thermal component is particularly relevant to this reaction.

[Helium](../../chemical-substance.md#helium) has very low solubility in [steel](../../chemical-substance.md#steel) and can be trapped at [vacancies](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy), interfaces and [grain boundaries](../../physical-chemistry.md#grain-boundary). Boron already segregated near [grain boundaries](../../physical-chemistry.md#grain-boundary) can therefore create a locally unfavorable distribution of [helium](../../chemical-substance.md#helium). Stabilized helium–vacancy clusters develop into bubbles or cavities. Under tensile [stress](../../continuum-mechanics.md#stress), boundary cavities can grow and link, reducing [ductility](../../engineering.md#ductility), [fracture toughness](../../elasticity-physics.md#fracture-toughness) and creep-rupture life through [intergranular fracture](../../elasticity-physics.md#intergranular-fracture). Early thin-foil observations and mechanical tests associated high-temperature ductility loss with helium-containing boundary bubbles and their coalescence.[Abstract and opening discussion, Nature 211, pp.291–292: boron-10 reaction, electron microscopy and boundary-bubble mechanism](../../engineering.md#high-temperature-embrittlement-of-stainless-steel-irradiated-in-fast-fluxes-by-a-s-fraser-and-others)

[Irradiation temperature](../../exoplanet.md#irradiation-temperature) controls the transport and clustering of both [helium](../../chemical-substance.md#helium) and [vacancies](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy). At lower [temperature](../../temperature.md), reduced long-range transport can retain small trapped clusters in the matrix. These can contribute to [irradiation hardening](../../engineering.md#irradiation-hardening), but extensive boundary-cavity growth is kinetically less favorable. At higher [temperature](../../temperature.md), migration, cavity growth and [material creep](../../engineering.md#creep-deformation) become faster, making [intergranular fracture](../../elasticity-physics.md#intergranular-fracture) and loss of high-temperature [ductility](../../engineering.md#ductility) more likely. Trap distribution and prior [microstructure](../../engineering.md#microstructure) can redistribute the gas, so there is no composition-independent critical [temperature](../../temperature.md) or universal monotonic law.

The mechanical-test [temperature](../../temperature.md) also matters. Material irradiated cold can become embrittled during subsequent hot testing or service when trapped gas and defects become mobile. Distinguish gas production during [irradiation](../../physics.md#irradiation) from the later conditions under which its mechanical consequences are expressed.

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

**The key imbalance is preferential loss of [self-interstitials](../../physical-chemistry.md#self-interstitial) to biased [defect sinks](../../engineering.md#defect-sink), leaving a [vacancy supersaturation](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy-supersaturation) that can feed [void swelling](../../engineering.md#void-swelling).** The initial [displacement cascade](../../engineering.md#displacement-cascade) creates both [vacancies](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy) and [self-interstitials](../../physical-chemistry.md#self-interstitial), often in equal net numbers as [Frenkel pairs](../../physical-chemistry.md#frenkel-pair); it need not create more vacancies than interstitials at birth.

Many nearby [Frenkel pairs](../../physical-chemistry.md#frenkel-pair) recombine. Others separate, become trapped or form [dislocation loops](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-loop). Surviving [self-interstitials](../../physical-chemistry.md#self-interstitial) are usually much more mobile than [vacancies](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy) over the relevant range. [Dislocations](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation) also have an [interstitial bias](../../engineering.md#interstitial-bias): their elastic interaction with defects makes them more efficient sinks for [self-interstitials](../../physical-chemistry.md#self-interstitial). Preferential interstitial removal to the [dislocation](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation) structure can leave the mobile [vacancy](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy) population above its [thermal equilibrium](../../thermal-equilibrium.md) value.

A minimal [radiation damage rate theory](../../engineering.md#radiation-damage-rate-theory) makes the distinction explicit:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot c_i&=G-Kc_ic_v-D_i k_i^2c_i,\\
\dot c_v&=G-Kc_ic_v-D_v k_v^2(c_v-c_v^{\rm eq}).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $c_i,c_v$ are defect [number densities](../../statistical-physics.md#number-density), $G$ is the surviving mobile-pair production rate per volume, $K$ the recombination coefficient, $D_i,D_v$ the [diffusion](../../thermodynamics.md#diffusion) coefficients, and $k_i^2,k_v^2$ the respective [sink strengths](../../engineering.md#sink-strength). The vacancy loss term includes thermal emission in an effective form. Equal production terms do not require equal steady populations or equal partitioning among sinks. This simplified model omits an explicit cluster-size distribution.

A stable cavity receiving a greater vacancy influx than interstitial influx and vacancy emission grows. [Vacancies](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy) therefore condense into voids, producing [void swelling](../../engineering.md#void-swelling). [Helium](../../chemical-substance.md#helium) can stabilize small cavity nuclei, but a growing vacancy void need not be a high-pressure gas bubble. [Dislocation loops](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-loop), precipitates and changes in the [dislocation density](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-density) evolve alongside the voids and modify the competing [sink strengths](../../engineering.md#sink-strength).

There is no violation of atom conservation: atoms represented by removed [self-interstitials](../../physical-chemistry.md#self-interstitial) have joined extended defects or surfaces, while the corresponding unfilled lattice volume can accumulate in cavities. The classic electron-microscope observations of cavities in irradiated type316 [steel](../../chemical-substance.md#steel) provide direct evidence for this microscopic basis of swelling.[Opening description of Dounreay-irradiated specimens examined by thin-foil electron microscopy](../../engineering.md#voids-in-irradiated-stainless-steel-by-c-cawthorne-and-e-j-fulton)

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

**[Void swelling](../../engineering.md#void-swelling) is generally favored by an intermediate [irradiation temperature](../../exoplanet.md#irradiation-temperature) window.** The balance involves defect mobility, stable [nucleation](../../critical-phenomenon.md#nucleation), absorption at [defect sinks](../../engineering.md#defect-sink) and thermal emission; a larger [vacancy](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy) population alone does not guarantee a larger void.

At low [temperature](../../temperature.md), [vacancy](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy) migration is slow. [Self-interstitials](../../physical-chemistry.md#self-interstitial) may migrate and form [dislocation loops](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-loop), and small clusters can cause considerable [irradiation hardening](../../engineering.md#irradiation-hardening), while vacancies cannot readily feed large growing cavities. Defects retained in the matrix can evolve during later heating.

At intermediate [temperature](../../temperature.md), [vacancies](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy) can migrate far enough to reach stable cavities. [Interstitial bias](../../engineering.md#interstitial-bias) and the competition among [defect sinks](../../engineering.md#defect-sink) sustain a net vacancy flux into these cavities. Once the incubation period has produced suitable nuclei and sink populations, substantial [void swelling](../../engineering.md#void-swelling) can develop.

At high [temperature](../../temperature.md), the [thermal equilibrium](../../thermal-equilibrium.md) vacancy population and vacancy emission from small cavities rise. A small void's curvature further favors emission, so maintaining stable nuclei becomes harder. Recovery changes the [dislocation](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation) population, and recombination, gas transport and evolving precipitates also change the flux balance. The irradiation-driven [vacancy supersaturation](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy-supersaturation) can fall, suppressing void [nucleation](../../critical-phenomenon.md#nucleation) and net swelling. Existing cavities may still coarsen, and gas bubbles, [material creep](../../engineering.md#creep-deformation) and other damage can remain important.

Thus **low-temperature defect retention, intermediate-temperature cavity growth and high-temperature recovery/emission are different regimes**. The boundaries depend on [alloy](../../chemical-substance.md#alloy) composition, initial [microstructure](../../engineering.md#microstructure), gas production, accumulated [displacements per atom](../../engineering.md#displacements-per-atom) and [displacement dose rate](../../engineering.md#displacement-dose-rate). No alloy-independent peak [temperature](../../temperature.md) can be inferred from the paper's data.

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

**[Irradiation softening](../../engineering.md#irradiation-softening) occurs when irradiation removes pre-existing strengthening obstacles faster than it creates new ones.** It is particularly plausible in an initially hardened [alloy](../../chemical-substance.md#alloy), rather than an initially soft annealed reference.

In a material strengthened by [cold working](../../continuum-mechanics.md#cold-working), the starting [dislocation density](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-density) is high. Irradiation-generated [point defects](../../physical-chemistry.md#point-defect) can assist [dislocation climb](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-climb), rearrangement and annihilation, reducing the contribution of [work hardening](../../continuum-mechanics.md#work-hardening). A schematic estimate is

$$
\sigma_y=\sigma_0+\alpha\mu b\sqrt{\rho_d}+\Delta\sigma_p+\Delta\sigma_{\rm irr},
$$

where $\mu$ is the [shear modulus](../../elasticity-physics.md#shear-modulus), $b$ the magnitude of the [Burgers vector](../../physical-chemistry.md#burgers-vector), $\rho_d$ the [dislocation density](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-density), and the last two terms represent precipitate and irradiation-obstacle strengthening. It is an illustrative decomposition, not an exact additive law for every alloy. A sufficiently large decrease in the pre-existing contributions can exceed the increase in $\Delta\sigma_{\rm irr}$.

In a material strengthened by [precipitation hardening](../../chemical-substance.md#precipitation-hardening), ballistic mixing can dissolve or disorder the original obstacles. [Radiation-enhanced diffusion](../../engineering.md#radiation-enhanced-diffusion) can change their composition or accelerate coarsening; increasing obstacle spacing can reduce strength. In an ordered alloy, irradiation-induced loss of order can also remove a strengthening contribution. None of these outcomes is inevitable: [irradiation](../../physics.md#irradiation) can instead create new strengthening precipitates.

The required conditions therefore include an appropriate initial [microstructure](../../engineering.md#microstructure), enough [displacement dose](../../engineering.md#displacements-per-atom) to modify it, and a [temperature](../../temperature.md) and [displacement dose rate](../../engineering.md#displacement-dose-rate) at which obstacle removal or recovery can compete with new defect accumulation. Compare with an unirradiated specimen held for the same time at the same [temperature](../../temperature.md) to separate ordinary thermal aging from an irradiation effect.

A drop in [yield strength](../../engineering.md#yield-strength) or [hardness](../../engineering.md#hardness) need not restore [ductility](../../engineering.md#ductility): cavities, [helium embrittlement](../../engineering.md#helium-embrittlement) or localization of [dislocation glide](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-glide) can coexist with softening. Conversely, an initially annealed [steel](../../chemical-substance.md#steel) may show strong [irradiation hardening](../../engineering.md#irradiation-hardening) under the same exposure conditions.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

**[Irradiation creep](../../engineering.md#irradiation-creep) adds stress-directed, time-dependent strain driven by the irradiation-generated defect population.** It can be appreciable where ordinary thermal [material creep](../../engineering.md#creep-deformation) would be slow, because [irradiation](../../physics.md#irradiation) maintains mobile [point defects](../../physical-chemistry.md#point-defect) far above their equilibrium populations.

Absorption or emission of [vacancies](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy) and [self-interstitials](../../physical-chemistry.md#self-interstitial) permits [dislocation climb](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-climb). Applied [stress](../../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) can make differently oriented [dislocations](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation) or [dislocation loops](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-loop) absorb defects at different rates: [stress-induced preferential absorption](../../engineering.md#stress-induced-preferential-absorption) then produces a directional strain. Climb can also allow a pinned [dislocation](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation) to bypass an obstacle and continue [dislocation glide](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-glide), providing a second route to strain.

A useful small-strain empirical form, over a limited range of conditions, is

$$
\dot\varepsilon_{\rm irr}=B\sigma\dot d,
\qquad
\varepsilon_{\rm irr}\simeq B\sigma d
$$

for constant [stress](../../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) $\sigma$ and approximately constant irradiation-creep compliance $B$. Here $d$ is the accumulated [displacements per atom](../../engineering.md#displacements-per-atom) and $\dot d$ the [displacement dose rate](../../engineering.md#displacement-dose-rate); $B$ is measured per unit [stress](../../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) and per unit dpa. This relation explains why creep can scale approximately with both [stress](../../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) and [neutron flux](../../neutron.md#neutron-flux) when the damage spectrum is fixed. It is not a constitutive law valid at every dose or [temperature](../../temperature.md).

The total strain can also include thermal [material creep](../../engineering.md#creep-deformation), [void swelling](../../engineering.md#void-swelling), [irradiation growth](../../engineering.md#irradiation-growth) and changes due to precipitation or recovery. Stress-free swelling is not itself [irradiation creep](../../engineering.md#irradiation-creep). Experiments on pressurized type316 tubes separated these contributions and found that correcting stress-enhanced swelling materially affected comparisons between annealed and specimens strengthened by [cold working](../../continuum-mechanics.md#cold-working).[PNNL publication abstract; Journal of Nuclear Materials 367–370, pp.954–959, experimental separation of creep, swelling and precipitation strains](../../engineering.md#the-influence-of-cold-work-level-on-the-irradiation-creep-and-swelling-of-aisi-316-stainless-steel-by-e-r-gilbert-and-f-a-garner)

At sufficiently high [temperature](../../temperature.md), thermal [material creep](../../engineering.md#creep-deformation) can dominate. Changes in [microstructure](../../engineering.md#microstructure) caused by [irradiation hardening](../../engineering.md#irradiation-hardening), softening, bubbles and cavities can either alter the creep rate or shorten rupture life. Thus enhanced defect-driven creep is the central effect, while the net rate and lifetime remain material- and history-dependent.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Assume a homogeneous Fe–Cr–Ni [alloy](../../chemical-substance.md#alloy), normal incidence, independent scattering events and the stated effective [scattering cross-sections](../../quantum-scattering.md#scattering-cross-section). Neglect absorption, other constituents and changes in neutron energy within the thin sheet. The [atomic fractions](../../mixture.md#atomic-fraction) are not supplied; **take $x_{\rm Fe}=0.70$, $x_{\rm Cr}=0.20$, $x_{\rm Ni}=0.10$ as an explicit representative assumption**, not a composition measured or specified in the paper.

The conventional cell of a [face-centered cubic lattice](../../quantum-scattering.md#face-centered-cubic-lattice) contains

$$
8\left(\frac18\right)+6\left(\frac12\right)=4
$$

atoms. The [number density](../../statistical-physics.md#number-density) is therefore

$$
n=\frac4{a^3}=\frac4{(0.359\times10^{-9})^3}
=8.6452\times10^{28}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}.
$$

For a mixture, the scattering probability per unit path length is $\Sigma_s=\sum_j n_j\sigma_j=n\bar\sigma_s$, where

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar\sigma_s&=\sum_jx_j\sigma_j\\
&=[0.70(3.2)+0.20(2.8)+0.10(4.1)]\times10^{-28}\\
&=3.21\times10^{-28}\ \mathrm{m^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $\Sigma_s=27.751\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$. For thickness $t=10^{-3}\,\mathrm m$, the unscattered fraction $U$ satisfies $dU/dz=-\Sigma_sU$ with $U(0)=1$. The fraction undergoing at least one [neutron scattering](../../neutron.md#neutron-scattering) event is

$$
\boxed{P_s=1-e^{-\Sigma_st}=1-e^{-0.027751}=0.027370\simeq2.74\%.}
$$

The thin-sheet approximation gives $P_s\simeq\Sigma_st=2.78\%$, consistent to the accuracy of the assumed composition. A simple iron-dominated estimate $\bar\sigma_s\simeq3.2$ [barns](../../physics.md#barn-unit) gives essentially the same **about $3\%$ scattered** conclusion.

For any other assumed composition, use the displayed weighted formula; mass fractions must first be converted to [atomic fractions](../../mixture.md#atomic-fraction). Since the supplied elemental cross-sections span $2.8$–$4.1$ [barns](../../physics.md#barn-unit), any mixture of these three elements at the stated [number density](../../statistical-physics.md#number-density) gives approximately $2.39$–$3.48\%$. This is a bound within the idealized mixture model, not a claim that every such composition is stable [austenite](../../chemical-substance.md#austenite).

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

Use the same [atomic fractions](../../mixture.md#atomic-fraction) and collision assumptions as in part(c). Let $A$ be the irradiated area. The number of atoms in the sheet is $N_t=ntA$, while the number of incident [neutrons](../../neutron.md) is $\Phi A$, where $\Phi$ is the [neutron fluence](../../neutron.md#neutron-fluence).

Counting one [primary knock-on atom](../../engineering.md#primary-knock-on-atom) for each neutron that scatters and using $\nu=430$ displacements per recoil gives the first-collision estimate

$$
\begin{aligned}
d&=\frac{\nu\Phi A P_s}{ntA}\\
&=\frac{430(4\times10^{26})(0.0273697)}
{(8.6452\times10^{28})(10^{-3})}\\
&=54.45.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence **the nominal damage is about $55$ [displacements per atom](../../engineering.md#displacements-per-atom)** under the assumed composition. In the thin-sheet approximation the density and thickness cancel:

$$
\boxed{d\simeq\nu\Phi\bar\sigma_s
=430(4\times10^{26})(3.21\times10^{-28})=55.21.}
$$

The $1.4\%$ difference comes from using first-collision attenuation rather than the linear approximation. Treating repeated collisions after deflection would require angular and energy information not provided; this correction is already smaller than the precision warranted by the composition assumption.

There is also a counting convention in “other atoms”: $430$ counts secondary displacements, while including the displaced [primary knock-on atom](../../engineering.md#primary-knock-on-atom) itself gives $\nu=431$. The corresponding first-collision result is $54.58$ rather than $54.45$ dpa, an increase of only $0.23\%$. Both conventions round to about $55$ dpa. The numerical estimate is explicitly conditional on assigning the stated displacement yield to each scattering event.

A value greater than one is physically possible. [Displacements per atom](../../engineering.md#displacements-per-atom) counts displacement events relative to the initial atom inventory: atoms can be displaced repeatedly, and many [vacancies](../../physical-chemistry.md#vacancy) and [self-interstitials](../../physical-chemistry.md#self-interstitial) recombine. It does not mean $55$ surviving vacant sites per original lattice site, or that every atom has moved exactly $55$ times. This is a large nominal exposure for which evolving [dislocation loops](../../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-loop), cavities and [irradiation hardening](../../engineering.md#irradiation-hardening) matter; the surviving [microstructure](../../engineering.md#microstructure) additionally depends on [irradiation temperature](../../exoplanet.md#irradiation-temperature), [displacement dose rate](../../engineering.md#displacement-dose-rate), recombination and [defect sinks](../../engineering.md#defect-sink).
