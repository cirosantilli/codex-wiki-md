<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**[Irradiation softening](../../../../../irradiation-softening.md) occurs when irradiation removes pre-existing strengthening obstacles faster than it creates new ones.** It is particularly plausible in an initially hardened [alloy](../../../../../alloy.md), rather than an initially soft annealed reference.

In a material strengthened by [cold working](../../../../../cold-working.md), the starting [dislocation density](../../../../../dislocation-density.md) is high. Irradiation-generated [point defects](../../../../../point-defect.md) can assist [dislocation climb](../../../../../dislocation-climb.md), rearrangement and annihilation, reducing the contribution of [work hardening](../../../../../work-hardening.md). A schematic estimate is

$$
\sigma_y=\sigma_0+\alpha\mu b\sqrt{\rho_d}+\Delta\sigma_p+\Delta\sigma_{\rm irr},
$$

where $\mu$ is the [shear modulus](../../../../../shear-modulus.md), $b$ the magnitude of the [Burgers vector](../../../../../burgers-vector.md), $\rho_d$ the [dislocation density](../../../../../dislocation-density.md), and the last two terms represent precipitate and irradiation-obstacle strengthening. It is an illustrative decomposition, not an exact additive law for every alloy. A sufficiently large decrease in the pre-existing contributions can exceed the increase in $\Delta\sigma_{\rm irr}$.

In a material strengthened by [precipitation hardening](../../../../../precipitation-hardening.md), ballistic mixing can dissolve or disorder the original obstacles. [Radiation-enhanced diffusion](../../../../../radiation-enhanced-diffusion.md) can change their composition or accelerate coarsening; increasing obstacle spacing can reduce strength. In an ordered alloy, irradiation-induced loss of order can also remove a strengthening contribution. None of these outcomes is inevitable: [irradiation](../../../../../irradiation.md) can instead create new strengthening precipitates.

The required conditions therefore include an appropriate initial [microstructure](../../../../../microstructure.md), enough [displacement dose](../../../../../displacements-per-atom.md) to modify it, and a [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and [displacement dose rate](../../../../../displacement-dose-rate.md) at which obstacle removal or recovery can compete with new defect accumulation. Compare with an unirradiated specimen held for the same time at the same [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) to separate ordinary thermal aging from an irradiation effect.

A drop in [yield strength](../../../../../yield-strength.md) or [hardness](../../../../../hardness.md) need not restore [ductility](../../../../../ductility.md): cavities, [helium embrittlement](../../../../../helium-embrittlement.md) or localization of [dislocation glide](../../../../../dislocation-glide.md) can coexist with softening. Conversely, an initially annealed [steel](../../../../../steel.md) may show strong [irradiation hardening](../../../../../irradiation-hardening.md) under the same exposure conditions.
