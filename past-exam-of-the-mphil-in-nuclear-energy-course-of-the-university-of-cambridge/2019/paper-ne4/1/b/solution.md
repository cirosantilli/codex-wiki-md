<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The characteristic growth phenomena require an asymmetric material response and a way to retain irreversible change. **Low crystal symmetry and directional defect or deformation behaviour make [uranium](../../../../../uranium.md) and [zirconium](../../../../../zirconium.md) important examples**, whereas an ideal cubic material has isotropic linear [thermal expansion](../../../../../thermal-expansion.md) and no symmetry-selected traceless single-crystal growth tensor in an otherwise isotropic environment.

[alpha uranium](../../../../../alpha-uranium.md) is orthorhombic and has strongly anisotropic thermal response. In a textured [polycrystalline material](../../../../../polycrystalline-material.md), incompatible [crystal grain](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) [strains](../../../../../strain.md) can be accommodated asymmetrically over a thermal cycle and leave macroscopic distortion. The same metal can also show [irradiation growth](../../../../../irradiation-growth.md) through anisotropic irradiation-defect evolution. [zirconium](../../../../../zirconium.md) and its hexagonal close-packed alloys are major examples of [irradiation growth](../../../../../irradiation-growth.md); their basal and c-axis directions need not acquire the same [strain](../../../../../strain.md).

Cubic [austenitic steel](../../../../../austenitic-steel.md) does not exhibit the same intrinsic orientation-driven response merely because it is irradiated. It can nevertheless undergo [void swelling](../../../../../void-swelling.md), [irradiation creep](../../../../../irradiation-creep.md), thermal-stress [plasticity](../../../../../plasticity-physics.md) or distortion under gradients. Thus **absence of this growth mechanism does not imply dimensional stability under all reactor conditions**. Conversely, a noncubic crystal is not guaranteed to grow during every thermal cycle: constraints, inelastic accommodation and the [microstructure](../../../../../microstructure.md) still matter.
