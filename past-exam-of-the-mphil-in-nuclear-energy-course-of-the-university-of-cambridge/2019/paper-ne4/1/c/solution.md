<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

[crystal grain](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) structure controls both the source of incompatibility and its conversion into a bulk dimensional change. Separate **orientation distribution, [grain size](../../../../../grain-size.md), [crystal grain](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) shape and defect structure** rather than treating “fine grained” as a universal cure.

In thermal cycling, different [crystal grain](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) orientations rotate the [thermal expansion tensor](../../../../../thermal-expansion-tensor.md) into different laboratory directions. Bonding prevents each [crystal grain](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) from following its independent free [strain](../../../../../strain.md). Coarse [crystal grains](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) can produce larger surface relief and longer local mismatch distances; finer [crystal grains](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) can distribute the accommodation more uniformly. However, changing [grain size](../../../../../grain-size.md) also changes strength, boundary-mediated deformation and [creep](../../../../../creep-deformation.md). A fine but strongly textured material can still show directional growth, and enhanced boundary accommodation can itself be irreversible. [crystallographic texture](../../../../../crystallographic-texture.md) and the way mismatch is accommodated must therefore be measured separately.

For [irradiation growth](../../../../../irradiation-growth.md), [crystallographic texture](../../../../../crystallographic-texture.md) determines whether directional single-grain changes reinforce or cancel. An illustrative independent-grain average is

$$
\overline{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}^{g}=\sum_jv_jR_j\boldsymbol\varepsilon_j^{g}R_j^T,
$$

where $v_j$ is a [crystal grain](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) volume fraction and $R_j$ its orientation. A random orientation average of a traceless growth tensor is zero, whereas [crystallographic texture](../../../../../crystallographic-texture.md) can retain a preferred bulk [strain](../../../../../strain.md). For an ideal hexagonal [crystal grain](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) with principal growth [strains](../../../../../strain.md) $(g,g,-2g)$, the [strain](../../../../../strain.md) along a direction making angle $\theta$ to the c-axis is

$$
\varepsilon_d=g(1-3\cos^2\theta),\qquad
\langle\varepsilon_d\rangle=g\big(1-3\langle\cos^2\theta\rangle\big).
$$

Random [crystallographic texture](../../../../../crystallographic-texture.md) has $\langle\cos^2\theta\rangle=1/3$ and cancels this ideal directional growth. This is an orientation model, not proof that real [crystal grain](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) constraints or volume-changing processes vanish.

[grain boundaries](../../../../../grain-boundary.md) also absorb mobile defects. Boundary spacing and aspect ratio change directional sink distances, while [dislocation](../../../../../dislocation.md) [density](../../../../../density.md), [cold working](../../../../../cold-working.md) and [recrystallisation](../../../../../recrystallisation.md) change competing sinks and residual [stresses](../../../../../stress.md). These affect the single-grain response before it is averaged. In one model compared with zirconium-alloy experiments, increasing [grain size](../../../../../grain-size.md), reducing aspect ratio or reducing [dislocation](../../../../../dislocation.md) [density](../../../../../density.md) decreased predicted growth. That result is a useful counterexample to assuming that smaller [crystal grains](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) always reduce [irradiation growth](../../../../../irradiation-growth.md).[Transactions of the Japan Institute of Metals 29, 970–976, abstract and stated microstructural trends](../../../../../effects-of-microstructural-factors-on-irradiation-growth-in-zirconium-base-alloys-by-noriyuki-sadaoka-and-motomasa-fuse.md)
