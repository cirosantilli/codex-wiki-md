<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $SA=S$ for the exposed [glass](../../../../../glass.md) area. If $C_e$ is the blank-corrected dissolved mass concentration of element $e$, $x_e$ its initial [mass fraction](../../../../../mass-fraction.md) in the [glass](../../../../../glass.md) and $V$ the leachate volume, then

$$
NL_e=\frac{C_e}{x_e(SA/V)}=\boxed{\frac{C_eV}{x_eSA}}.
$$

The numerator is the released element mass measured in solution. Dividing by $x_e$ converts this to an equivalent mass of the original [glass](../../../../../glass.md), while dividing by $SA$ expresses that mass per exposed area. With $C_e$ in $\mathrm{g\,L^{-1}}$ and $SA/V$ in $\mathrm{m^2\,L^{-1}}$, $NL_e$ has units **$\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}}$ of glass-equivalent release**. The fraction $x_e$ must be used as a fraction, not an unconverted percentage.

These corrections allow comparison of specimens of different size and composition without confusing a lower concentration with better durability. State how the area was measured; geometric area and a rough powder's accessible area need not coincide. For repeated sampling, include element mass removed in earlier aliquots and the volume remaining; for an initial blank in the leachant, subtract its corresponding inventory. Surface cracking and evolving [porosity](../../../../../porosity.md) can make a rate based on initial area differ from a rate based on the true reactive area.

Under [congruent glass dissolution](../../../../../congruent-glass-dissolution.md), constituents enter solution in their bulk proportions, so their correctly normalised releases coincide. Preferential [sodium](../../../../../sodium.md) release by [ion exchange](../../../../../ion-exchange.md) can instead give $NL_{\rm Na}>NL_{\rm Si}$, while [silicon](../../../../../silicon.md) retention in a gel or secondary precipitate can depress its dissolved release. **Noncoincident curves show nonstoichiometric aqueous release, but do not uniquely identify the mechanism.** Conversely, solution coincidence does not by itself establish that no secondary reactions occurred. A complete inference compares solution inventories with altered solids.

Normalisation removes composition and first-order area/volume scaling; **it does not make different [pH](../../../../../ph.md), [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), solution composition or contact histories equivalent**. In a batch test, even the same initial [water](../../../../../water.md) changes composition and [pH](../../../../../ph.md) as reaction proceeds. Reporting those conditions and time is essential for a transferable comparison rather than treating $NL_e$ as a condition-independent material constant.
