<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**Reduce time, increase distance and use radiation-appropriate shielding.** At fixed conditions, accumulated [absorbed dose](../../../../../absorbed-dose.md) is proportional to exposure time. For an approximately isotropic point source, the [inverse-square law](../../../../../inverse-square-law.md) reduces the uncollided radiation field as distance increases. Confinement of radioactive material prevents contamination; it addresses a different pathway from attenuation of an external field.

For [alpha particles](../../../../../alpha-particle.md), short stopping ranges make light barriers sufficient against external particles, but confinement remains important. For [beta particles](../../../../../beta-particle.md), a low-[atomic number](../../../../../atomic-number.md) shield such as plastic limits both penetration and secondary [bremsstrahlung](../../../../../bremsstrahlung.md); high-[atomic number](../../../../../atomic-number.md) material can then attenuate residual photons. For penetrating [gamma rays](../../../../../gamma-ray.md), dense material such as [lead](../../../../../lead.md), or a thicker bulk shield such as concrete, provides attenuation. **One shield material is not equally appropriate for every radiation type.**[Time, distance and shielding; alpha, beta and gamma examples](../../../../../minimize-your-exposure-by-united-states-nuclear-regulatory-commission.md)[Appendix I, printed page I-2: low-atomic-number beta shielding and an outer high-atomic-number layer for bremsstrahlung](../../../../../consolidated-guidance-about-materials-licenses-volume-12-by-united-states-nuclear-regulatory-commission.md)
