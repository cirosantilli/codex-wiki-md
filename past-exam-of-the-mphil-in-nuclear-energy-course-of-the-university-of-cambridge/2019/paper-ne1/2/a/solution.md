<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**One energy group and a nearly isotropic local neutron distribution are the main approximations.** The [one-group neutron diffusion equation](../../../../../one-group-neutron-diffusion-equation.md) replaces the full energy-dependent transport problem by one scalar [neutron flux](../../../../../neutron-flux.md) and averaged coefficients. A single group cannot resolve spectral changes, resonance absorption or differences between rapidly moving and [thermal neutrons](../../../../../thermal-neutron.md); its effective parameters depend on the spectrum used for averaging.

The [diffusion approximation](../../../../../diffusion-approximation.md) also replaces angular transport by a current proportional to the negative [neutron flux](../../../../../neutron-flux.md) gradient. It works best where repeated scattering makes the angular distribution nearly isotropic over distances comparable to the field's variation length. It is less reliable close to a sharply localized source, a vacuum boundary, a strong absorber or an abrupt material interface, and in weakly scattering regions. Approximate boundary extrapolation does not restore the lost angular information.

Thus an apparently smooth solution of the [one-group neutron diffusion equation](../../../../../one-group-neutron-diffusion-equation.md) need not accurately represent a transport field outside the assumptions that produced its coefficients and [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md).
