↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**Delayed [neutrons](../../../../../neutron-split.md) are emitted after precursor radioactive transformations, rather than promptly at fission.** Some [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md)-rich [fission products](../../../../../fission-product.md) undergo beta decay into excited daughter states that can emit a [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md). The waiting time is governed chiefly by the precursor's radioactive lifetime, not by a [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) moving unusually slowly after it has already been emitted.

Although their fraction is small, [delayed neutrons](../../../../../delayed-neutron.md) introduce a much slower population timescale into the [nuclear chain reaction](../../../../../nuclear-chain-reaction.md). In the delayed-[neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md)-controlled regime, changes can therefore develop over times comparable with precursor lifetimes rather than only the [prompt neutron generation time](../../../../../prompt-neutron-generation-time.md). That timescale separation is central to qualitative reactor controllability. It does not imply that every positive-reactivity transient is slow.
