↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [point kinetics model](../../../../../point-kinetics-model.md) replaces the spatially and spectrally distributed [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) field by a single amplitude $n(t)$, effectively assuming a fixed or sufficiently slowly varying flux shape. The source amplitude is $s$. A single effective [delayed neutron precursor](../../../../../delayed-neutron-precursor.md) population $c(t)$, [delayed neutron fraction](../../../../../delayed-neutron-fraction.md) $\beta$ and decay rate $\lambda$ stand in for many precursor species with different lifetimes. $\Lambda$ is the [prompt neutron generation time](../../../../../prompt-neutron-generation-time.md) and $\rho$ the specified [reactor reactivity](../../../../../reactivity-nuclear-engineering.md).

The displayed equations omit explicit thermal-hydraulic, fuel-temperature and isotope-composition feedback. For the stated constant-coefficient transients, $\rho,\beta,\Lambda,\lambda$ are prescribed rather than being solved as evolving fields. The model is useful for an elementary timescale calculation; it does not establish the spatial stability or operating limits of an actual core.
