↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

With no delayed [neutrons](../../../../../../neutron-split.md), set $\beta=0$ and omit the precursor equation. The source-free model reduces to $\dot n=(\rho/\Lambda)n$, giving

$$
\boxed{T_{\rm prompt\ only}=\frac\Lambda\rho=0.500\ \mathrm s.}
$$

The delayed-[neutron](../../../../../../neutron-split.md) model's dominant e-folding time is about $121$ times larger. This comparison illustrates why a small [delayed neutron fraction](../../../../../../delayed-neutron-fraction.md) strongly alters the kinetics. It is a comparison of the supplied idealized models at the same imposed positive [reactivity](../../../../../../reactivity-nuclear-engineering.md), not a control prescription or a claim that every transient has the same timescale.
