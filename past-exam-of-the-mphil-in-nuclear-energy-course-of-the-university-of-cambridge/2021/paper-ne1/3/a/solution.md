<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**Cadmium acts as an energy-selective [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) absorber in the stated comparison.** Its large thermal capture [collision cross-section](../../../../../collision-cross-section.md) preferentially reduces the [thermal neutron](../../../../../thermal-neutron.md) field reaching the source material, while its much lower fast-[neutron capture](../../../../../neutron-capture.md) probability allows more of the emitted fast [neutrons](../../../../../neutron-split.md) to escape. This limits [thermal neutron](../../../../../thermal-neutron.md)-induced changes in the source while retaining its qualitative role as a [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) emitter.

The point is selectivity between incoming thermal and outgoing fast components, rather than a claim that cadmium is transparent to every [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) or that capture produces no secondary radiation. This explains the stated material choice at a conceptual level; no source construction, cladding dimensions or irradiation specification follows from it.
