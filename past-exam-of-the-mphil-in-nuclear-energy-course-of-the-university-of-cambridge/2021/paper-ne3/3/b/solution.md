↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The resulting [dislocation density](../../../../../dislocation-density.md) reflects competing production, growth and recovery rates. The [irradiation](../../../../../irradiation.md) spectrum and [displacement dose rate](../../../../../displacement-dose-rate.md) set the supply of recoil cascades and surviving [point defects](../../../../../point-defect.md). Cascade structure controls the fraction born as isolated defects or clusters; equal nominal [displacements per atom](../../../../../displacements-per-atom.md) can therefore give different defect populations.

[Temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) sets [vacancy](../../../../../vacancy.md) and interstitial mobilities and the rates of thermal emission and recovery. A simple [radiation damage rate theory](../../../../../radiation-damage-rate-theory.md) model illustrates the competition using number densities $c_i,c_v$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot c_i&=G-K_{\rm rec}c_ic_v-D_i k_i^2c_i,\\
\dot c_v&=G-K_{\rm rec}c_ic_v-D_v k_v^2c_v.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $G$ is the surviving mobile-pair production rate per volume, $K_{\rm rec}$ is the recombination coefficient, $D_i,D_v$ are [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) coefficients, and $k_i^2,k_v^2$ are [sink strengths](../../../../../sink-strength.md). The model omits explicit cluster-size populations and thermal-equilibrium emission; those must be added for a quantitative description of a particular exposure.

The initial [dislocation density](../../../../../dislocation-density.md), grain size, free surfaces, precipitates and existing cavities all change sink strengths. [Dislocations](../../../../../dislocation.md) can have an [interstitial bias](../../../../../interstitial-bias.md), so their capture rates for the two defect types differ. Solutes and transmutation gases alter defect binding and mobility. Crystal structure, elastic anisotropy and applied [stress](../../../../../stress.md) affect which loop orientations survive and grow.

For a number density $N_\ell$ of circular loops of radius $R$, their contribution to line length per volume is

$$
\boxed{\rho_\ell=2\pi R N_\ell.}
$$

Thus loop nucleation and loop growth both increase [dislocation density](../../../../../dislocation-density.md), while shrinkage, annihilation and absorption at other sinks can decrease it. A change in loop count alone does not determine the change in total line density.

Schematically, $\dot\rho_d$ is a production-and-growth contribution minus recovery and annihilation. An initial rise followed by approximate saturation is possible when these balance, but the saturation value and approach rate depend on the evolving [microstructure](../../../../../microstructure.md). There is no universal [dislocation density](../../../../../dislocation-density.md) fixed by dose alone.
