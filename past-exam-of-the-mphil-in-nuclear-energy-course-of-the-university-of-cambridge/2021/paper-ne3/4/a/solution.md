[Mixed oxide fuel](../../../../../mixed-oxide-fuel.md) begins as a specified mixture of uranium and plutonium oxides. A fuel pin develops strong radial [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) gradients during operation. The components and oxygen need not remain spatially uniform: their [chemical potentials](../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) depend on [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), local composition and [oxygen-to-metal ratio](../../../../../oxygen-to-metal-ratio.md).

[Actinide redistribution in oxide fuel](../../../../../actinide-redistribution-in-oxide-fuel.md) can involve thermally driven solid-state [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) and evaporation–condensation transport across pores or a central cavity. Oxygen redistributes as well, changing local oxidation states and the relative thermodynamic tendencies of uranium- and plutonium-bearing species. Pore migration, grain growth and central-void formation couple compositional redistribution to fuel restructuring. Differential local burnup also changes composition over time.

The direction and magnitude of plutonium migration cannot be inferred from a [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) gradient alone. In particular irradiated fast-reactor pins, enrichment around a central cavity has been observed, but the result depends on stoichiometry and operating history. [M. Bober](../../../../../m-bober.md) and colleagues investigated thermal-diffusion and evaporation–condensation effects using autoradiographs of sections of fuel irradiated in RAPSODIE.[Abstract: irradiated-pin autoradiographs, oxygen redistribution, and dependence on stoichiometric state](../../../../../plutonium-redistribution-in-fast-reactor-mixed-oxide-fuel-pins-by-m-bober-and-others.md)

Redistribution changes the radial [heat generation rate](../../../../../heat-generation-rate.md) and material properties. Concentrating more fissile material near an already hot region can raise local heat generation; changes in [thermal conductivity](../../../../../thermal-conductivity.md) and local melting behavior modify the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) response. Gas release, pore formation and fuel–cladding contact can also change thermal resistance and mechanical loading. A spatially averaged composition can therefore underestimate a local [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) or chemical interaction.

Segregated [fission products](../../../../../fission-product.md) add another concern: mobile species can accumulate at colder interfaces or react with the cladding, while insoluble products form separate phases. Fuel performance must therefore track composition, [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and [microstructure](../../../../../microstructure.md) together, rather than treating segregation as merely a cosmetic change in an otherwise fixed fuel.
