For [zirconium alloys](../../../../../zirconium-alloys.md), the practical upper [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) is governed by time-dependent [corrosion](../../../../../corrosion.md), [material creep](../../../../../creep-deformation.md) and loss of [ductility](../../../../../ductility.md), well before bulk melting. In ordinary water-reactor service, cladding surfaces are commonly in the several-hundred-degree range, with normal operation around the low-to-mid $300\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$ range in a [pressurized water reactor](../../../../../pressurized-water-reactor.md). A quoted service limit must specify alloy, [coolant](../../../../../coolant.md) chemistry, exposure time, [stress](../../../../../stress.md) and accumulated hydrogen; there is no universal [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) at which every zirconium tube abruptly fails.

Increasing [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) accelerates [zirconium-steam oxidation](../../../../../zirconium-steam-oxidation.md) and creep. Oxide growth increases thermal resistance, and hydrogen uptake can leave a hydride-containing cladding with reduced resistance to cracking. A transient can also cause ballooning or rupture as hot material loses strength under internal [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). Oxygen absorbed beneath the oxide stabilizes a brittle oxygen-rich region; a tube that survives the hot part of a transient can still have poor [ductility](../../../../../ductility.md) during cooling and quenching.

For scale, the established US emergency-core-cooling criterion discussed by the [United States Nuclear Regulatory Commission fuel-damage criteria](../../../../../united-states-nuclear-regulatory-commission-fuel-damage-criteria.md) limits peak cladding [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) to $2200\,{}^\circ\mathrm{F}$, about $1204\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$, together with an oxidation criterion. This is an accident-analysis criterion, rather than a proposed continuous service [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md).[Issue170, description of the10CFR50.46 peak-temperature and17% oxidation criteria](../../../../../united-states-nuclear-regulatory-commission-fuel-damage-criteria.md)

These material constraints limit allowable fuel heat flux and [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) excursions and require adequate heat removal and thermal margin. A small increase in [coolant](../../../../../coolant.md) [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) or oxide thermal resistance can raise the underlying metal [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and accelerate degradation. Alloy changes, protective coatings and alternative claddings aim to improve that margin, while still satisfying the neutronic and mechanical requirements.
