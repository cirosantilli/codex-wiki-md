<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The low-temperature zirconium matrix has a [hexagonal close packing](../../../../../hexagonal-close-packing.md) structure. Its crystallographic $a$ and $c$ directions therefore have different [elasticity](../../../../../elasticity-physics-split.md), [thermal expansion](../../../../../thermal-expansion.md) and available [slip systems](../../../../../slip-system.md). Producing tubing also develops a [crystallographic texture](../../../../../crystallographic-texture.md), so averaging over grains does not necessarily remove these directional effects.

Different expansion along the crystal axes produces intergranular thermal [stresses](../../../../../stress.md) during [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) changes. Under load, easy [dislocation glide](../../../../../dislocation-glide.md) modes may accommodate some strains much more readily than others; additional slip or [crystal twinning](../../../../../crystal-twinning.md) is needed for general compatibility. Consequently strength and [ductility](../../../../../ductility.md) can depend on the axial, circumferential and radial orientation of the tube.

[Irradiation](../../../../../irradiation.md) creates [vacancies](../../../../../vacancy.md) and [self-interstitials](../../../../../self-interstitial.md) that cluster into [dislocation loops](../../../../../dislocation-loop.md) with different crystallographic orientations. Their anisotropic accumulation can produce [irradiation growth](../../../../../irradiation-growth.md) even without external [stress](../../../../../stress.md). Different directions can elongate or contract while the net volume change remains small. Texture turns these grain-scale strains into macroscopic length and diameter changes, bowing and [stresses](../../../../../stress.md) between constrained components. This mechanism is distinct from the added cavity volume of [void swelling](../../../../../void-swelling.md).

The crystallographic character of [irradiation](../../../../../irradiation.md) loops matters: atomistic calculations by [Rory Hulse](../../../../../rory-hulse.md) and [Christopher P. Race](../../../../../christopher-p-race.md) examine several possible loop types and show that loop energy and shape depend on orientation and size. A single generic spherical-defect description therefore misses important aspects of zirconium's dimensional response.[Abstract and loop-orientation results; the study is atomistic modelling, not a direct measurement of every loop population](../../../../../an-atomistic-modelling-study-of-the-properties-of-dislocation-loops-in-zirconium-by-rory-hulse-and-christopher-p-race.md)

Hydrogen also produces direction-sensitive damage. [Zirconium hydrides](../../../../../zirconium-hydride.md) are brittle precipitates whose preferred orientation depends on crystal texture and [stress](../../../../../stress.md) history. Radially oriented hydrides can offer crack paths across a thin tube wall under circumferential tension. Controlling texture, hydrogen pickup and hydride orientation helps manage that risk; changing the hexagonal structure's orientation cannot by itself eliminate [corrosion](../../../../../corrosion.md) or [irradiation](../../../../../irradiation.md) damage.
