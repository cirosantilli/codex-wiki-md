<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) controls the competition between defect creation, [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md), recombination, sink absorption and thermal [vacancy](../../../../../vacancy.md) emission. Consequently [void swelling](../../../../../void-swelling.md) is normally largest in an intermediate [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) window, whose location depends on alloy, dose rate and exposure history.

At low [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), many generated defects lack sufficient mobility to feed a growing cavity. Considerable [irradiation hardening](../../../../../irradiation-hardening.md) can still occur through small defect clusters, while large-scale void growth remains limited.

At intermediate [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), [vacancies](../../../../../vacancy.md) and interstitials migrate sufficiently to establish the biased defect fluxes described above. Cavity nuclei can survive and receive a net [vacancy](../../../../../vacancy.md) flux, producing strong swelling.

At sufficiently high [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), thermal [vacancy](../../../../../vacancy.md) emission, recombination, recovery and changes in sink populations compete more strongly with [vacancy](../../../../../vacancy.md) absorption. Stable nucleation and net cavity growth can become less favorable. This does not imply that every radiation effect disappears: [material creep](../../../../../creep-deformation.md), precipitation, segregation and gas-bubble evolution can remain important.

For austenitic stainless steels, the relevant range is typically several hundred degrees Celsius; an alloy-independent peak [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) is not justified. Raising the [displacement dose rate](../../../../../displacement-dose-rate.md) changes the balance between production and thermally activated processes and can shift both the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) window and the incubation dose. Temperatures from accelerated ion or electron experiments should therefore be interpreted together with dose rate, implantation, specimen thickness and surface-sink effects.
