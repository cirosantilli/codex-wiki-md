↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**[Synroc](../../../../../synroc.md) immobilises waste [ions](../../../../../ion.md) in selected durable crystalline hosts**, rather than trying to put the whole inventory into one universal site. The underlying philosophy is that [minerals](../../../../../mineral.md) retaining analogous elements over geological times suggest useful [crystal structures](../../../../../crystal-structure.md) for engineered containment. These natural analogues motivate the choice; their survival does not prove that every synthetic composition, damaged microstructure or groundwater environment will behave identically. This was the rationale of the original mineral-analogue proposal.[Nature 278, 219–223, abstract and mineral-analogue argument](../../../../../immobilisation-of-high-level-nuclear-reactor-wastes-in-synroc-by-a-e-ringwood-and-others.md)

A representative [Synroc-C](../../../../../synroc-c.md) assemblage includes [zirconolite](../../../../../zirconolite.md), $\mathrm{CaZrTi_2O_7}$, as an [actinide](../../../../../actinide.md) host; a [hollandite waste-form phase](../../../../../hollandite-waste-form-phase.md), represented by $\mathrm{BaAl_2Ti_6O_{16}}$, whose tunnels accommodate large [cations](../../../../../cation.md) such as [caesium](../../../../../caesium.md); and a [perovskite waste-form phase](../../../../../perovskite-waste-form-phase.md), $\mathrm{CaTiO_3}$, which can accept [strontium](../../../../../strontium.md) on [calcium](../../../../../calcium.md) sites and other constituents with suitable [charge compensation in a crystal](../../../../../charge-compensation-in-a-crystal.md). Titanium-oxide phases provide additional accommodation and composition flexibility. The actual phases are [solid solutions](../../../../../solid-solution.md), not simply pure end-member [minerals](../../../../../mineral.md) mixed with an inert waste powder.[Key Engineering Materials 48–50, 717–721; ANSTO abstract identifying the multiphase titanate assemblage](../../../../../crystal-chemistry-and-phase-manipulation-in-synroc-by-e-r-vance-and-others.md)

Manufacture combines a characterised waste feed with selected oxide-forming precursors, converts the mixture to a suitable powder or [calcination](../../../../../calcination.md) product, and consolidates it while forming the host phases. [hot pressing](../../../../../hot-pressing.md) and [hot isostatic pressing](../../../../../hot-isostatic-pressing.md) are consolidation approaches; the latter uses pressure acting approximately equally in all directions. The objective is a dense [ceramic](../../../../../ceramic.md) with the intended phase assemblage and low connected [porosity](../../../../../porosity.md), contained in an appropriate [radioactive waste package](../../../../../radioactive-waste-package.md). Verify density and phase composition, use [microscopy](../../../../../microscopy.md) to find segregated constituents or pores, and test [leaching](../../../../../leaching.md) of the actual product. Successful densification alone does not establish radionuclide retention.[Institutional technology overview, waste-form design, characterisation and hot isostatic pressing](../../../../../ansto-synroc-radioactive-waste-treatment-technology-by-the-australian-nuclear-science-and-technology-organisation.md)
