<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use a **[single-pass flow-through leach test](../../../../../single-pass-flow-through-leach-test.md)**. A controlled leachant passes once over a known [glass](../../../../../glass.md) [surface area](../../../../../surface-area.md), and successive effluent samples are analysed after the initial transient. [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and [pH](../../../../../ph.md) are controlled, and fresh solution removes dissolved products so that they do not accumulate towards saturation with respect to the dissolving [glass](../../../../../glass.md). This approaches the [forward glass dissolution rate](../../../../../forward-glass-dissolution-rate.md) at those specified conditions.

At steady state, with $Q$ expressed as volume per time,

$$
\boxed{r_e=\frac{Q(c_{e,\rm out}-c_{e,\rm in})}{f_eS}}.
$$

Increase $Q/S$ across a series of tests until the normalised rate becomes independent of further dilution, within analytical uncertainty. A genuine plateau supports approach to the dilute-solution limit; it should not be assumed from one arbitrarily chosen flow rate. Check blanks, inlet [concentrations](../../../../../concentration.md), [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), [pH](../../../../../ph.md), actual flow, fines and exposed [surface area](../../../../../surface-area.md). At high flow the [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) increment becomes small, so an uncorrected background can manufacture an apparent rate increase.

The resulting **maximum is the forward rate for the specified [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), [pH](../../../../../ph.md) and [glass](../../../../../glass.md)**, not an absolute maximum over every possible environment. It also differs from the lower residual rate that can develop in a static, product-rich leachate. Primary measurements demonstrate the method and the importance of background subtraction and flow variation.[1997, experimental method and abstract, including the nonphysical flow-rate increase caused by inadequate background subtraction](../../../../../measurement-of-kinetic-rate-law-parameters-on-a-na-ca-al-borosilicate-glass-for-low-activity-waste-by-b-p-mcgrail-and-others.md)
