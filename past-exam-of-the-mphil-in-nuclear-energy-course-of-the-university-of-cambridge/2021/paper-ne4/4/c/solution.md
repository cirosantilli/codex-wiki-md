<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $c_e$ be the measured mass [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) of element $e$, $c_{e,b}$ its blank [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md), $V$ the leachate volume, $f_e$ the element's [mass fraction](../../../../../mass-fraction.md) in the original [glass](../../../../../glass.md), and $S$ the exposed [glass](../../../../../glass.md) [surface area](../../../../../surface-area.md). For one closed test, the [normalised elemental release](../../../../../normalised-elemental-release.md) is

$$
\boxed{NL_e=\frac{(c_e-c_{e,b})V}{f_eS}}.
$$

If [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) is in $\mathrm{g\,L^{-1}}$, volume in litres and area in square metres, $NL_e$ is in **$\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}}$ of glass-equivalent release**. Dividing by $f_e$ removes the trivial difference caused by loading different amounts of the measured element; dividing by $S$ removes the first-order geometrical scale difference. Normalising only by initial element content or reporting raw solution [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) does not accomplish both corrections.

For repeated sampling with leachant replacement, use cumulative released mass: add the mass removed in all earlier aliquots to the mass present in the final remaining solution, with the corresponding blank correction. A flow-through experiment instead integrates $Q(t)[c_{e,\rm out}(t)-c_{e,\rm in}(t)]$ over time. State how [surface area](../../../../../surface-area.md) was determined and whether it is held at its initial value in the reported normalisation; cracks, fines and alteration can change the genuinely reactive area.

Compare $NL_e(t)$ for several constituents. In [congruent glass dissolution](../../../../../congruent-glass-dissolution.md), the different element-normalised curves coincide, provided the released elements remain in solution. Preferential early alkali release can indicate [ion exchange](../../../../../ion-exchange.md), whereas lower dissolved [silicon](../../../../../silicon.md) or [actinide](../../../../../actinide.md) release can reflect an alteration layer, sorption or secondary-phase [precipitation](../../../../../precipitation-chemistry.md). [boron](../../../../../boron.md) is often useful as a relatively mobile dissolution tracer, but not if the leachant contains a large [boron](../../../../../boron.md) background or if it is itself retained. A low aqueous [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) of one constituent is therefore **not automatically a low glass-dissolution rate**.

A near-linear $NL_e(t)$ interval gives an approximately constant [normalised elemental release](../../../../../normalised-elemental-release.md) rate. A square-root time dependence can be consistent with diffusion-limited transport, and decreasing slopes can indicate saturation or a protective alteration layer; no one curve shape proves a unique mechanism. Compare solution inventories with the altered solid by [microscopy](../../../../../microscopy.md), composition and phase analysis. Differences in [pH](../../../../../ph.md), [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), leachant composition, duration and surface-area-to-volume ratio remain even after this mass-and-area normalisation. Careful background subtraction and control of solution conditions are experimentally consequential, as demonstrated in primary flow-through measurements.[Journal of Nuclear Materials 249, 175–189; abstract on duration, background subtraction and solution-condition dependence](../../../../../measurement-of-kinetic-rate-law-parameters-on-a-na-ca-al-borosilicate-glass-for-low-activity-waste-by-b-p-mcgrail-and-others.md)
