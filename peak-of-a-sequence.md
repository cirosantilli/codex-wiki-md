# Peak of a sequence

↑ **Parent:** [Monotone subsequence theorem](monotone-subsequence-theorem.md)

A peak of a [sequence](sequence.md) of [real numbers](real-number.md) is an index $k$ whose value is at least every later value. This is a condition on the entire tail, stronger than comparing only adjacent terms. Equality is allowed: every index of a constant [sequence](sequence.md) is a [peak of a sequence](peak-of-a-sequence.md).

Infinitely many [peaks of a sequence](peak-of-a-sequence.md), listed in increasing order of index, give a nonincreasing [monotone subsequence](monotone-subsequence.md). If only finitely many exist, every index beyond the last has a larger later term; repeatedly choosing one gives a strictly increasing [monotone subsequence](monotone-subsequence.md). This proves the [monotone subsequence theorem](monotone-subsequence-theorem.md).

A [peak of a sequence](peak-of-a-sequence.md) of $(-a_n)$ supplies a lower bound for all subsequent terms of $(a_n)$. If both $(a_n)$ and $(-a_n)$ have a [peak of a sequence](peak-of-a-sequence.md), the two selected values bound a common tail. Adding bounds for the finite initial segment proves that $(a_n)$ is a [bounded sequence](bounded-sequence.md).
