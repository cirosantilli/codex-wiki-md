# Euler relation in thermodynamics

↑ **Parent:** [Extensive quantity](extensive-quantity.md)

For a single-component equilibrium system with extensive [internal energy](internal-energy.md) $E(S,V,N)$, scaling its [entropy](entropy.md), [volume](volume.md) and particle number together gives $E(\lambda S,\lambda V,\lambda N)=\lambda E(S,V,N)$. The [Euler theorem for homogeneous functions](euler-theorem-for-homogeneous-functions.md) therefore gives $E=S\partial_SE+V\partial_VE+N\partial_NE$. The [first law of thermodynamics](first-law-of-thermodynamics.md) identifies these derivatives as $T,-P,\mu$, respectively, proving $E=TS-PV+\mu N$. Dividing by volume yields the [entropy density](entropy-density.md) formula $s=(\rho+P-\mu n)/T$. This step fixes the entropy normalization rather than merely identifying its differential. Differentiating the Euler relation and subtracting $dE=T\,dS-P\,dV+\mu\,dN$ gives the [Gibbs-Duhem equation](gibbs-duhem-equation.md) $S\,dT-V\,dP+N\,d\mu=0$. The argument requires extensivity; it is not an assumption about arbitrary nonadditive systems.
