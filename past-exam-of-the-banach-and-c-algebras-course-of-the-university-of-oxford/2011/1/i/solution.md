**The even-power family is dense on $[1,2]$.** It is the algebra $A=\{p(t^2):p\in\mathbb C[s]\}$. It contains constants, is closed under [complex conjugation](../../../../complex-conjugation.md), and separates points because the real function $t\mapsto t^2$ is injective on this positive interval. The complex Stone-Weierstrass theorem therefore applies.

Equivalently, the squaring map is a [homeomorphism](../../../../homeomorphism.md) from $[1,2]$ onto $[1,4]$. Given $f\in C([1,2])$, approximate the [continuous function](../../../../continuous-function.md) $s\mapsto f(\sqrt s)$ uniformly on $[1,4]$ by complex [polynomials](../../../../polynomial-split.md), using the [Weierstrass approximation theorem](../../../../weierstrass-approximation-theorem.md) on the real and imaginary parts. Substitution $s=t^2$ produces exactly the required even-power approximants. The positive interval is what lets even powers distinguish all its points.
