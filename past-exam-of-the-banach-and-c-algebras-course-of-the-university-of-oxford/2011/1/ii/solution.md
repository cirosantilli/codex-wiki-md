<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**The odd-power family is also dense on $[1,2]$.** It is $B=tA$, where $A$ is the even-power family in part (i). Multiplication by $t$ is an invertible bounded [linear map](../../../../linear-map.md) on $C([1,2])$, with inverse division by $t$, since $t\geq1$.

For an explicit approximation, take any $f\in C([1,2])$ and $\epsilon>0$. The quotient $f(t)/t$ is continuous, so part (i) gives an even [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) $a$ with $\|a-f/t\|_\infty<\epsilon/2$. Then $ta$ belongs to the odd-power family and

$$
\boxed{\|ta-f\|_\infty\leq2\|a-f/t\|_\infty<\epsilon.}
$$

Although this family is not a [subalgebra](../../../../subalgebra.md) and does not contain the constant function exactly, it can approximate every [continuous function](../../../../continuous-function.md), including constants. This proves [even and odd polynomial density on a positive interval](../../../../even-and-odd-polynomial-density-on-a-positive-interval.md) without incorrectly applying the unital algebra theorem directly to the odd family.
