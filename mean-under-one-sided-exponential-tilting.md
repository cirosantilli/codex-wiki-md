# Mean under one-sided exponential tilting

↑ **Parent:** [Exponential tilting](exponential-tilting.md)

Suppose $\phi(\lambda)=\mathbb E e^{\lambda X}<\infty$ for all $\lambda\geq0$. Under the [exponential tilting](exponential-tilting.md) $d\mu^\lambda=e^{\lambda x}d\mu/\phi(\lambda)$, $X$ is an [integrable random variable](integrable-random-variable.md) whenever $\lambda>0$: its negative exponential tail is bounded, and its positive contribution is dominated by a larger positive [exponential moment](exponential-moment.md). [Differentiation under the integral sign](differentiation-under-the-integral-sign.md) gives the displayed mean. At the endpoint $\lambda=0$, the positive part of $X$ is integrable, but the negative part need not be. The right derivative $\phi'_+(0)=\mathbb E X$ is valid as an extended value, possibly $-\infty$; a finite mean at zero requires an extra negative-tail integrability assumption.
