# Inverse function rule

↑ **Parent:** [Inverse function](inverse-function.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_function_rule)

Suppose $f$ is [continuously differentiable](continuously-differentiable-function.md) near $a$ with $f'(a)\ne0$. It has a local [inverse function](inverse-function.md) $g$ near $b=f(a)$, and

$$
g'(b)=\frac1{f'(a)}=\frac1{f'(g(b))}.
$$

The [chain rule](chain-rule.md) applied to $g(f(x))=x$ gives the formula. The nonzero [derivative](derivative.md) condition also ensures local invertibility; it does not assert a global [inverse function](inverse-function.md) on an arbitrary larger [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md). For $f(x)=x^2$ at $a=2$, the corresponding output is $b=4$, and the [square root](square-root.md)'s [derivative](derivative.md) there is $1/4$. At $a=0$, this division is unavailable: the nonnegative restriction still has a [square root](square-root.md) inverse, but its finite [derivative](derivative.md) at zero does not exist. [OpenStax, the inverse function rule and corresponding evaluation points](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/3-7-derivatives-of-inverse-functions).
