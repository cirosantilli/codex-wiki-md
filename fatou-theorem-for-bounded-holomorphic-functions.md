# Fatou theorem for bounded holomorphic functions

↑ **Parent:** [H-infinity space](h-infinity-space.md)

A bounded [holomorphic function](holomorphic-function.md) on the [unit disc](unit-disc.md) has finite [nontangential limits](nontangential-limit.md) almost everywhere on its boundary. To outline a proof, bounded real and imaginary [harmonic](harmonic-function.md) parts have $L^\infty$ boundary representations: apply the [Banach-Alaoglu theorem](banach-alaoglu-theorem.md) to their circular traces and pass to the limit in the [Poisson integral on the unit disk](poisson-integral-on-the-unit-disk.md). Almost every boundary point is a [Lebesgue point](lebesgue-point.md). The fact that a [Poisson integral converges nontangentially at Lebesgue points](poisson-integral-converges-nontangentially-at-lebesgue-points.md) gives the desired limits. Their $L^\infty$ norm equals the interior supremum norm, and the [Poisson integral](poisson-integral.md) recovers the original function. Exceptional points can lack even radial limits; $\exp(i\operatorname{Log}(1-z))$ is a bounded example at the boundary point one.
