# Mass ratio from spring oscillation frequencies

↑ **Parent:** [Monomial uncertainty propagation](monomial-uncertainty-propagation.md)

For the same massless [spring](spring.md) obeying [Hooke's law](hooke-s-law.md), the [frequency](frequency.md) is $\nu=(2\pi)^{-1}\sqrt{k/m}$. The displayed [mass](mass.md) ratio follows. For small [independent](independent-random-variables.md) frequency errors with common fractional [standard uncertainty](standard-uncertainty.md) $\epsilon$, [monomial uncertainty propagation](monomial-uncertainty-propagation.md) gives $\sigma_R/R\simeq\sqrt8\epsilon$. If $\epsilon$ instead describes individual measurements and each [sample mean](sample-mean.md) uses $n$ independent trials, its [standard error](standard-error.md) is smaller by $\sqrt n$, giving $\sigma_R/R\simeq\sqrt{8/n}\epsilon$. Four times as many trials halve this statistical uncertainty; a common calibration error needs its [covariance](covariance.md) included.
