# Cayley transform of a Hermitian matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Operator theory](linear-operator-theory-split.md)

For a [Hermitian matrix](hermitian-operator.md) $A$, both $A+iI$ and $A-iI$ are invertible because every [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) of $A$ is real. The displayed rational function is a [unitary matrix](unitary-matrix.md): taking its [conjugate transpose](conjugate-transpose.md) and commuting the rational factors gives $U^\dagger U=I$. It maps each [eigenvector](eigenvector.md) of $A$ with [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$ to the same vector with [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) $(\lambda-i)/(\lambda+i)$, a [complex number](complex-number.md) of modulus one different from one. The [inverse Cayley transform of a unitary matrix without eigenvalue one](inverse-cayley-transform-of-a-unitary-matrix-without-eigenvalue-one.md) recovers $A$.

Another common convention is $C=(I+iA)^{-1}(I-iA)$, which sends $\lambda$ to $(1-i\lambda)/(1+i\lambda)$ and avoids eigenvalue minus one. The two conventions are related by $C=-U^{-1}$; the inverse formula must match the chosen convention.
