<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

A convenient sufficient condition is that $f\in C^2(\mathbb R^d)$ has a bounded [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient). The [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) for standard $d$-dimensional [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) gives

$$
f(B_t)-\frac12\int_0^t\Delta f(B_s)\,ds
=f(0)+\sum_{j=1}^d\int_0^t\partial_jf(B_s)\,dB_s^j.
$$

If $|\nabla f|\leq C$, the [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) gives

$$
\mathbb E\left|\sum_j\int_0^t\partial_jf(B_s)\,dB_s^j\right|^2
=\mathbb E\int_0^t|\nabla f(B_s)|^2\,ds\leq C^2t.
$$

Thus the right side is a [square-integrable](../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function) [martingale](../martingale.md), rather than merely a [local martingale](../martingale.md#local-martingale). In particular,

$$
\boxed{f\in C^2(\mathbb R^d),\quad \sup_x|\nabla f(x)|<\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M^f\text{ is a martingale}.}
$$

There are many nonconstant examples, such as $f(x)=\sin x_1$ or $f(x)=x_1$. More generally it is enough that $\mathbb E\int_0^t|\nabla f(B_s)|^2ds<\infty$ for each finite $t$. Smooth [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) with [compact support](../function.md#compact-support) satisfy the bounded-gradient condition and will provide the domain in the next part.

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

Take

$$
\boxed{\mathcal D(\Delta/2)=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^d).}
$$

Part (i) proves that [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) solves this [martingale problem](../stochastic-calculus.md#martingale-problem). For the converse, let $X$ be any continuous [adapted process](../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) satisfying this [martingale problem](../stochastic-calculus.md#martingale-problem) and $X_0=0$.

Set $\tau_n=\inf\{t\geq0:|X_t|\geq n\}$. Choose a [smooth cutoff function](../analysis.md#smooth-cutoff-function) $\chi_n$ equal to one on a neighbourhood of the closed ball of radius $n$. Apply the [martingale problem](../stochastic-calculus.md#martingale-problem) to $\chi_n(x)x_i$ and $\chi_n(x)x_ix_j$, and stop at $\tau_n$. Inside that ball, their [Laplace operators](../partial-differential-equation.md#laplace-operator) are respectively $0$ and $2\delta_{ij}$. The [optional sampling theorem](../martingale.md#optional-sampling-theorem-for-a-supermartingale) therefore shows that

$$
X^i_{t\wedge\tau_n},\qquad
X^i_{t\wedge\tau_n}X^j_{t\wedge\tau_n}-\delta_{ij}(t\wedge\tau_n)
$$

are [martingales](../martingale.md). Continuity makes $\tau_n\uparrow\infty$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Hence every $X^i$ is a [continuous local martingale](../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale). The [Itô product rule](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-product-rule) and the fact that a [continuous finite-variation local martingale is constant](../martingale.md#continuous-finite-variation-local-martingale-is-constant) identify its [quadratic covariations](../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation):

$$
[X^i,X^j]_t=\delta_{ij}t.
$$

Here $\delta_{ij}$ is the [Kronecker delta](../multilinear-algebra.md#kronecker-delta).

The [Lévy characterization of multidimensional Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md#levy-characterization-of-multidimensional-brownian-motion) now applies. To see explicitly why it identifies the entire law, for $\xi\in\mathbb R^d$ the [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) makes

$$
E_t(\xi)=\exp\!\left(i\xi\cdot X_t+\frac12|\xi|^2t\right)
$$

a complex [local martingale](../martingale.md#local-martingale). Its absolute value on $[0,T]$ is at most $e^{|\xi|^2T/2}$, so the [bounded local martingale criterion](../martingale.md#bounded-local-martingale-criterion) makes it a true [martingale](../martingale.md) there. For $0\leq s\leq t$,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E\!\left[e^{i\xi\cdot(X_t-X_s)}\mid\mathcal F_s\right]
=e^{-|\xi|^2(t-s)/2}.}
$$

This conditional [characteristic function](../characteristic-function.md) identifies $X_t-X_s$ as having a centered [multivariate normal distribution](../probability-and-statistics.md#multivariate-normal-distribution) with [covariance matrix](../variance.md#covariance-matrix) $(t-s)I$, [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of $\mathcal F_s$. Together with continuity and $X_0=0$, these are exactly the defining properties of standard $d$-dimensional [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md). Thus the [martingale problem for Brownian motion](../stochastic-calculus.md#martingale-problem-for-brownian-motion) has a unique law.

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

**The existence assertion is false under the hypotheses printed in the source.** No measurability of $\theta$ is specified. Even requiring $\theta$ to be a [Borel measurable function](../measure-theory.md#borel-measurable-function) does not suffice: a coefficient can be positive at every point while approaching zero too rapidly near the starting point. This is the obstruction described by [pointwise positivity does not ensure weak existence](../stochastic-calculus.md#pointwise-positivity-does-not-ensure-weak-existence).

Here is a counterexample in dimension one, with $\Theta=1$:

$$
\theta(0)=1,\qquad \theta(x)=\min(x^2,1)\quad(x\ne0).
$$

Suppose that a solution to the [martingale problem](../stochastic-calculus.md#martingale-problem) with domain $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ existed. The cutoff argument from part (a)(ii), now with $\mathcal L=\theta\Delta$, makes $X$ a [continuous local martingale](../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) with

$$
[X]_t=2\int_0^t\theta(X_s)\,ds\leq2t.
$$

The criterion for a [continuous local martingale with bounded quadratic variation](../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale-with-bounded-quadratic-variation) makes it a [square-integrable](../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function) [martingale](../martingale.md), and the [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) gives $\mathbb EX_t^2=\mathbb E[X]_t$.

Apply the [occupation-times formula](../brownian-motion.md#occupation-times-formula) with the indicator of the singleton $\{0\}$. Since that singleton has zero [Lebesgue measure](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure),

$$
0=\int_0^t\mathbf1_{\{X_s=0\}}\,d[X]_s
=2\int_0^t\mathbf1_{\{X_s=0\}}\,ds.
$$

Thus $X$ spends zero ordinary time at zero. Away from zero, $\theta(x)\leq x^2$, so

$$
0\leq\mathbb EX_t^2
=2\mathbb E\int_0^t\theta(X_s)\,ds
\leq2\int_0^t\mathbb EX_s^2\,ds.
$$

The [Gronwall inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#gronwall-inequality) forces $\mathbb EX_t^2=0$ for every $t$. Continuity then forces $X\equiv0$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence), contradicting either the zero occupation time just proved or $[X]_t=2t$ for that constant path. Consequently

$$
\boxed{0<\theta\leq\Theta\text{, even Borel measurable, does not ensure existence}.}
$$

For completeness, the intended construction works if $\theta$ is a [Borel measurable function](../measure-theory.md#borel-measurable-function) and bounded away from zero on every compact set. In particular, it works for continuous strictly positive $\theta$. Let $B$ be standard $d$-dimensional [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md), and define a clock and its inverse by

$$
A_u=\int_0^u\frac{dr}{2\theta(B_r)},\qquad
\tau_t=\inf\{u\geq0:A_u>t\},\qquad X_t=B_{\tau_t}.
$$

The Brownian path on each finite interval has compact range, so the added hypothesis makes $A_u$ finite. The clock is continuous and strictly increasing, and $A_u\geq u/(2\Theta)$, so $A_u\to\infty$. Its inverse is continuous and satisfies $\tau_t\leq2\Theta t$. Each $\tau_t$ is a [stopping time](../martingale.md#stopping-time) for the Brownian [filtration](../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory).

For $f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^d)$, the [optional sampling theorem](../martingale.md#optional-sampling-theorem-for-a-supermartingale) applied at these bounded random times shows that

$$
f(B_{\tau_t})-\frac12\int_0^{\tau_t}\Delta f(B_r)\,dr
$$

is a true [martingale](../martingale.md) in the [filtration](../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) $\mathcal G_t=\mathcal F^B_{\tau_t}$. The inverse-clock substitution $d\tau_t=2\theta(X_t)dt$, valid almost everywhere, rewrites it as

$$
f(X_t)-\int_0^t\theta(X_s)\Delta f(X_s)\,ds.
$$

This proves the [Brownian time change for a variable diffusion speed](../stochastic-calculus.md#brownian-time-change-for-a-variable-diffusion-speed):

$$
\boxed{X_t=B_{A^{-1}(t)}\text{ solves the corrected martingale problem}.}
$$

The factor $2$ in the clock is necessary because standard [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) has [infinitesimal generator](../stochastic-process.md#infinitesimal-generator-stochastic-processes) $\Delta/2$.

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

A sufficient condition is that $x\mapsto\sqrt{2\theta(x)}$ is [globally Lipschitz](../real-analysis.md#globally-lipschitz-function). A more familiar, stronger condition implying this is that $\theta$ is [globally Lipschitz](../real-analysis.md#globally-lipschitz-function) and $\theta\geq\varepsilon>0$. Both conditions repair the existence issue in part (i).

To connect this to the suggested [stochastic differential equation](../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation), apply the coordinate and coordinate-product cutoff tests to any solution of the [martingale problem](../stochastic-calculus.md#martingale-problem). As before they give

$$
[X^i,X^j]_t=2\delta_{ij}\int_0^t\theta(X_s)\,ds.
$$

Define, using [stochastic integrals](../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-integral) against the coordinates of $X$,

$$
W_t^i=\int_0^t\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\theta(X_s)}}\,dX_s^i.
$$

These integrals exist even without a global positive lower bound: their [quadratic variations](../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) are exactly $t$. Indeed,

$$
[W^i,W^j]_t
=\int_0^t\frac{1}{2\theta(X_s)}\,d[X^i,X^j]_s
=\delta_{ij}t.
$$

The [Lévy characterization of multidimensional Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md#levy-characterization-of-multidimensional-brownian-motion) identifies $W$ as standard $d$-dimensional [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md). By [associativity of stochastic integration](../stochastic-calculus.md#associativity-of-stochastic-integration),

$$
\boxed{dX_t=\sqrt{2\theta(X_t)}\,dW_t,\qquad X_0=0.}
$$

The diffusion matrix $\sqrt{2\theta(x)}I$ is [globally Lipschitz](../real-analysis.md#globally-lipschitz-function) under the stated condition, and boundedness of $\theta$ gives bounded growth. The [global existence theorem for stochastic differential equations with Lipschitz coefficients](../stochastic-calculus.md#global-existence-theorem-for-stochastic-differential-equations-with-lipschitz-coefficients) gives a unique [strong solution of a stochastic differential equation](../stochastic-calculus.md#strong-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation) and [uniqueness in law](../stochastic-calculus.md#uniqueness-in-law). Every solution to the [martingale problem](../stochastic-calculus.md#martingale-problem) has just been represented as a [weak solution of a stochastic differential equation](../stochastic-calculus.md#weak-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation) of this form, so they all have the same law. Conversely, the [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) applied to any $f\in C_c^\infty$ gives a true [martingale](../martingale.md), since its stochastic integrand is bounded. Thus the [martingale problem](../stochastic-calculus.md#martingale-problem) is well posed under this sufficient condition.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Write $N_t=M_t-M_0$ and $V_t=\langle M\rangle_t=[N]_t$. The [Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem](../martingale.md#dambis-dubins-schwarz-theorem) supplies a standard [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) $W$ such that

$$
N_t=W_{V_t}.
$$

On the event $V_\infty=\infty$, continuity and monotonicity imply that the range of $t\mapsto V_t$ is all of $[0,\infty)$.

For completeness, [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) hits every positive integer [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). The [Brownian reflection principle](../brownian-motion.md#reflection-principle-wiener-process) gives, for $a>0$,

$$
\mathbb P\!\left(\sup_{0\leq u\leq R}W_u\geq a\right)
=2\mathbb P(W_R\geq a)\longrightarrow1\qquad(R\to\infty).
$$

Intersect over integer $a$. The times of these increasingly large values must escape every compact interval because each Brownian path is continuous there. Therefore $\limsup_{u\to\infty}W_u=\infty$. The unbounded clock transfers these values to arbitrarily late times for $N$, and adding the finite initial value $M_0$ preserves unboundedness. Hence

$$
\boxed{\limsup_{t\to\infty}|M_t|=\infty\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

This is an instance of [infinite quadratic variation forces unbounded oscillations](../martingale.md#infinite-quadratic-variation-forces-unbounded-oscillations); applying the same argument to $-W$ also gives negative oscillations.

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

For $t>0$, $B_t$ has the [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution) $N(0,t)$. Evaluate its [Gaussian integral](../integral.md#gaussian-integral):

$$
L(t,\alpha,0)
=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\int_{\mathbb R}
\exp\!\left[-\left(\alpha+\frac1{2t}\right)x^2\right]dx
=\frac1{\sqrt{1+2\alpha t}}.
$$

At $t=0$ the defining random variable is one, consistent with the same formula. Thus

$$
\boxed{L(t,\alpha,0)=(1+2\alpha t)^{-1/2}\qquad(t,\alpha\geq0).}
$$

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

Let $Y_t=-\beta\int_0^tB_u\,dB_u-\int_0^th_u\,du$. Its [quadratic variation](../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) is $[Y]_t=\beta^2\int_0^tB_u^2du$. The [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) for $Z=e^Y$ gives

$$
dZ_t=-\beta Z_tB_t\,dB_t
+Z_t\left(\frac{\beta^2}{2}B_t^2-h_t\right)dt.
$$

Choose

$$
\boxed{h_t=\frac{\beta^2}{2}B_t^2,\qquad dZ_t=-\beta B_tZ_t\,dB_t,\quad Z_0=1.}
$$

The chosen process is continuous and [adapted](../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process), hence [progressively measurable](../stochastic-process.md#progressive-measurability). Its time integral is finite on compact intervals because Brownian paths are continuous. The resulting $Z$ is the [stochastic exponential](../stochastic-calculus.md#doleans-dade-exponential) of $-\beta\int_0^\cdot B_u\,dB_u$, and is a positive [local martingale](../martingale.md#local-martingale).

<h4 id="2/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/a/iii)

The [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) for $B_t^2$ yields $\int_0^tB_u\,dB_u=(B_t^2-t)/2$. Substituting into the expression for $Z_t$ gives the pathwise identity

$$
Z_t=\exp\!\left[-\frac\beta2B_t^2+\frac{\beta t}{2}
-\frac{\beta^2}{2}\int_0^tB_u^2\,du\right].
$$

Consequently

$$
Z_t\exp\!\left[\left(\frac\beta2-\alpha\right)B_t^2-\frac{\beta t}{2}\right]
=\exp\!\left[-\alpha B_t^2-\frac{\beta^2}{2}\int_0^tB_u^2\,du\right].
$$

Taking [expected values](../expected-value.md) proves

$$
\boxed{L(t,\alpha,\beta)
=\mathbb E\!\left[Z_t\exp\!\left(\left(\frac\beta2-\alpha\right)B_t^2-\frac{\beta t}{2}\right)\right].}
$$

The random variable on the preceding line is bounded by one for $\alpha\geq0$, so its integrability is immediate.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

The expression obtained in part (a)(iii) gives a direct deterministic bound. For every fixed $T<\infty$ and $0\leq s\leq T$,

$$
0<Z_s
=\exp\!\left[\frac{\beta s}{2}-\frac\beta2B_s^2
-\frac{\beta^2}{2}\int_0^sB_u^2du\right]
\leq e^{\beta T/2}.
$$

Thus the [bounded local martingale criterion](../martingale.md#bounded-local-martingale-criterion) makes $Z$ a true [martingale](../martingale.md) on every finite time interval. More explicitly, if $\sigma_n$ is a [localizing sequence](../martingale.md#localizing-sequence), the identities $\mathbb E[Z_{t\wedge\sigma_n}\mid\mathcal F_s]=Z_{s\wedge\sigma_n}$ pass to the limit by [dominated convergence](../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem), using the displayed bound for $t\leq T$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[Z_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=Z_s,\qquad \mathbb EZ_t=1\quad(0\leq s\leq t<\infty).}
$$

This establishes exactly the finite-horizon property needed for the subsequent change of measure.

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

The relation between $B$ and $W$ is the linear equation $dB_s=dW_s-\beta B_sds$ with $B_0=0$. Multiplication by the deterministic integrating factor $e^{\beta s}$ and the [Itô product rule](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-product-rule) give

$$
d(e^{\beta s}B_s)=e^{\beta s}\,dW_s.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{B_s=\int_0^se^{-\beta(s-u)}\,dW_u
=W_s-\beta\int_0^se^{-\beta(s-u)}W_u\,du.}
$$

The second equality follows by [semimartingale integration by parts](../stochastic-calculus.md#semimartingale-integration-by-parts). It expresses $B$ as an explicit function of the continuous path of $W$, using only an ordinary time integral. This inversion holds before changing measure; $W$ is already a [continuous semimartingale](../stochastic-calculus.md#continuous-semimartingale) under the original measure.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/c/i)

Fix $t<\infty$. Part (b)(i) makes $d\mathbb Q_t=Z_t\,d\mathbb P$ a [probability measure](../probability-space.md#probability-measure) on $\mathcal F_t$, equivalent to $\mathbb P$ since $Z_t>0$. Its density process on $[0,t]$ is $Z_s=\mathbb E[Z_t\mid\mathcal F_s]$. The [Girsanov theorem](../stochastic-calculus.md#girsanov-theorem), applied to the [stochastic exponential](../stochastic-calculus.md#doleans-dade-exponential) with integrand $-\beta B_s$, says that

$$
W_s=B_s+\beta\int_0^sB_u\,du,\qquad 0\leq s\leq t,
$$

is standard [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) under $\mathbb Q_t$. The required local square-integrability follows from path continuity, and the true [martingale](../martingale.md) property was proved in part (b)(i).

By part (b)(ii), under $\mathbb Q_t$ the process $B$ is therefore the zero-started [Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process](../gaussian-process.md#ornstein-uhlenbeck-process):

$$
\boxed{dB_s=dW_s-\beta B_sds,\qquad
B_s=\int_0^se^{-\beta(s-u)}\,dW_u,\quad 0\leq s\leq t.}
$$

This identifies its full law on continuous path space, rather than just its one-time marginal. It is a centered [Gaussian process](../gaussian-process.md), since it consists of deterministic [Itô integrals](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) against [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md). For $r,s\in[0,t]$, the [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) gives its [covariance function](../stochastic-process.md#covariance-function):

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}_{\mathbb Q_t}(B_r,B_s)
=\int_0^{r\wedge s}e^{-\beta(r-u)}e^{-\beta(s-u)}du
=\frac{e^{-\beta|s-r|}-e^{-\beta(s+r)}}{2\beta}.}
$$

In particular, its [Ornstein-Uhlenbeck transition distribution](../gaussian-process.md#ornstein-uhlenbeck-transition-distribution) at the terminal time is

$$
B_t\sim N(0,v_t),\qquad v_t=\frac{1-e^{-2\beta t}}{2\beta}.
$$

<h4 id="2/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/c/ii)

Put $c=\beta/2-\alpha$. The change of measure and part (a)(iii) yield

$$
L(t,\alpha,\beta)=e^{-\beta t/2}\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_t}e^{cB_t^2}.
$$

For a centered [normal random variable](../probability-distribution.md#gaussian-random-variable) $G$ of [variance](../variance.md) $v>0$, a [Gaussian integral](../integral.md#gaussian-integral) gives the [Gaussian quadratic exponential moment](../gaussian-process.md#gaussian-quadratic-exponential-moment)

$$
\mathbb Ee^{cG^2}=(1-2cv)^{-1/2},\qquad 1-2cv>0.
$$

Here $c\leq\beta/2$ and $\beta v_t<1/2$ for finite positive $t$, so the required positivity holds, including when $c>0$. Thus

$$
L(t,\alpha,\beta)
=\left[e^{\beta t}\left(1-(\beta-2\alpha)
\frac{1-e^{-2\beta t}}{2\beta}\right)\right]^{-1/2}.
$$

Expanding the two exponentials in terms of [hyperbolic functions](../calculus.md#hyperbolic-function) gives

$$
\boxed{L(t,\alpha,\beta)
=\left(\cosh(\beta t)+\frac{2\alpha}{\beta}\sinh(\beta t)\right)^{-1/2}\quad(\beta>0).}
$$

At $t=0$ this is one. As $\beta\downarrow0$, the denominator tends to $1+2\alpha t$, recovering part (a)(i). At $\alpha=0$ it gives the [Laplace transform of the integrated square of Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md#laplace-transform-of-the-integrated-square-of-brownian-motion). The joint terminal-and-integral transform is the [Joint Laplace transform of Brownian square and its time integral](../brownian-motion.md#joint-laplace-transform-of-brownian-square-and-its-time-integral).

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2025](2025.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

Write the diffusion and [drift coefficients](../stochastic-calculus.md#drift-coefficient) as

$$
\sigma(x)=\frac1{1+x^2},\qquad b(x)=-\frac1{2(1+x^2)^2}.
$$

Both are continuously differentiable, with

$$
\sigma'(x)=-\frac{2x}{(1+x^2)^2},\qquad
b'(x)=\frac{2x}{(1+x^2)^3}.
$$

These derivatives are bounded. For example $2|x|\leq1+x^2$ bounds both absolute values by one. The [mean value theorem](../calculus.md#mean-value-theorem) therefore makes both coefficients [globally Lipschitz](../real-analysis.md#globally-lipschitz-function). They are also bounded, so satisfy the [linear growth condition for an SDE](../stochastic-calculus.md#linear-growth-condition-for-an-sde).

The [global existence theorem for stochastic differential equations with Lipschitz coefficients](../stochastic-calculus.md#global-existence-theorem-for-stochastic-differential-equations-with-lipschitz-coefficients) consequently gives

$$
\boxed{\text{a global strong solution, unique up to indistinguishability}.}
$$

In particular, there is no finite explosion time. The solution has continuous paths, so all subsequent level-hitting and stopping arguments apply.

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

Apply the [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) to $M_t=e^{X_t}$. Since $d[X]_t=\sigma(X_t)^2dt$ and $b=-\sigma^2/2$, the two [drift](../stochastic-calculus.md#drift-coefficient) terms cancel:

$$
dM_t=e^{X_t}dX_t+\frac12e^{X_t}d[X]_t
=\frac{e^{X_t}}{1+X_t^2}\,dB_t.
$$

The integrand is continuous and hence locally square integrable after stopping at bounded levels of $X$. Thus $M$ is a positive [continuous local martingale](../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale), with $M_0=1$ and [quadratic variation](../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation)

$$
\boxed{M_t=1+\int_0^t\frac{e^{X_s}}{1+X_s^2}\,dB_s,\qquad
[M]_t=\langle M\rangle_t=\int_0^t\frac{e^{2X_s}}{(1+X_s^2)^2}\,ds.}
$$

The cancellation is the reason $e^x$ is the relevant [scale function](../stochastic-calculus.md#scale-function-stochastic-processes) for this diffusion.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

Take $L>0$, as needed for a nontrivial interval. Since $X$ is continuous and [adapted](../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process), for every level $a$ and $t\geq0$,

$$
\{T_a\leq t\}
=\left\{\inf_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_s-a|=0\right\}.
$$

The infimum is attained on this compact interval and can be computed on the countable dense set $(\mathbb Q\cap[0,t])\cup\{t\}$. It is therefore $\mathcal F_t$-measurable. Thus $T_a$ is a [stopping time](../martingale.md#stopping-time), and so is $T=T_{-L}\wedge T_L$, because $\{T\leq t\}=\{T_{-L}\leq t\}\cup\{T_L\leq t\}$.

By continuity, $X_{t\wedge T}\in[-L,L]$, even before knowing that $T$ is finite. Hence

$$
e^{-L}\leq M_{t\wedge T}\leq e^L.
$$

Stopping preserves the [local martingale](../martingale.md#local-martingale) property. The [bounded local martingale criterion](../martingale.md#bounded-local-martingale-criterion) now shows that $M^T=(M_{t\wedge T})_{t\geq0}$ is a [uniformly integrable martingale](../martingale.md#uniformly-integrable-martingale); its deterministic bound also gives square integrability. In particular,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb EM_{t\wedge T}=1,\qquad
\mathbb EM_{t\wedge T}^2\leq e^{2L}.}
$$

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

The stopped representation from part (a)(ii) and the [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) give the exact second-moment identity

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[M_{t\wedge T}^2]
=1+\mathbb E\int_0^{t\wedge T}\frac{e^{2X_s}}{(1+X_s^2)^2}\,ds.}
$$

The cross term with the initial value is zero because the stopped [stochastic integral](../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-integral) has zero mean. This is the useful finite-time computation; evaluating the remaining occupation integral explicitly is unnecessary for proving integrability of $T$.

For $|x|\leq L$,

$$
\frac{e^{2x}}{(1+x^2)^2}\geq c_L,
\qquad c_L=\frac{e^{-2L}}{(1+L^2)^2}>0.
$$

Combining the second-moment identity with part (i) yields

$$
1+c_L\mathbb E[t\wedge T]\leq\mathbb E[M_{t\wedge T}^2]\leq e^{2L}.
$$

Let $t\to\infty$ and use [monotone convergence](../measure-theory.md#monotone-convergence-theorem):

$$
\boxed{\mathbb ET\leq e^{2L}(1+L^2)^2(e^{2L}-1)<\infty.}
$$

In particular $T<\infty$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence), a conclusion obtained without assuming finite exit in the stopping argument.

One can also evaluate the limiting second moment exactly. At exit $M_T$ is either $e^{-L}$ or $e^L$, so

$$
M_T^2=(e^L+e^{-L})M_T-1.
$$

By [bounded convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#bounded-convergence-theorem) and part (i), $\mathbb EM_T=1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb E[M_{t\wedge T}^2]
=\mathbb EM_T^2=e^L+e^{-L}-1.}
$$

The same bound also holds at every finite time, by applying $(y-e^{-L})(y-e^L)\leq0$ to $y=M_{t\wedge T}$ and taking [expected values](../expected-value.md). It improves the preceding bound to $\mathbb ET\leq c_L^{-1}(e^L+e^{-L}-2)$ if desired.

<h4 id="3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/b/iii)

Let $S=T_b\wedge T_{-a}$ and $p=\mathbb P(T_b<T_{-a})$. Set $R=\max(a,b)$. The exit time $S$ from $(-a,b)$ is at most the exit time from $(-R,R)$, so part (ii) gives $\mathbb ES<\infty$. Continuity ensures that exactly one of the two different boundary levels is reached at $S$.

The stopped [local martingale](../martingale.md#local-martingale) $e^{X_{t\wedge S}}$ lies in $[e^{-a},e^b]$, hence is a [uniformly integrable martingale](../martingale.md#uniformly-integrable-martingale). The [optional sampling theorem](../martingale.md#optional-sampling-theorem-for-a-supermartingale), justified at the terminal time also by [bounded convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#bounded-convergence-theorem), gives

$$
1=\mathbb Ee^{X_S}=pe^b+(1-p)e^{-a}.
$$

Thus the [boundary hitting probability from a diffusion scale function](../stochastic-calculus.md#boundary-hitting-probability-from-a-diffusion-scale-function) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(T_b<T_{-a})
=\frac{1-e^{-a}}{e^b-e^{-a}}
=\frac{e^a-1}{e^{a+b}-1}.}
$$

For integer $n\to\infty$, the events $\{T_b<T_{-n}\}$ increase to $\{T_b<\infty\}$. Indeed, on any path with finite $T_b$, continuity makes the path bounded below on $[0,T_b]$, so some lower barrier $-n$ has not yet been hit. The reverse inclusion is immediate. Taking the limit in the displayed probability gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(T_b<\infty)=e^{-b}\qquad(b>0).}
$$

<h4 id="3/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/b/iv)

Since $X_0=0$, its [supremum](../real-analysis.md#supremum) $X^\star$ is nonnegative. For $b>0$, continuity gives

$$
\{X^\star>b\}=\bigcup_{n\geq1}\{T_{b+1/n}<\infty\}.
$$

The events increase with $n$. Part (iii) therefore yields $\mathbb P(X^\star>b)=\lim_ne^{-(b+1/n)}=e^{-b}$. Letting $b\downarrow0$ gives $\mathbb P(X^\star>0)=1$; letting $b\to\infty$ gives finiteness [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Consequently

$$
\boxed{X^\star\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1),\qquad
\mathbb P(X^\star\leq x)=
\begin{cases}0,&x<0,\\1-e^{-x},&x\geq0.\end{cases}}
$$

Its [probability density function](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function) is $e^{-x}\mathbf1_{\{x>0\}}$, with no atom at zero. Using strict exceedances above also avoids presupposing that the infinite-horizon [supremum](../real-analysis.md#supremum) is attained.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Let $K=\sup_x f(x)<\infty$ and let $C$ be a [Lipschitz constant](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-constant) for $f$. Since

$$
|f(x)^2-f(y)^2|\leq2KC|x-y|,
$$

both coefficients $f$ and $-f^2/2$ are bounded and [globally Lipschitz](../real-analysis.md#globally-lipschitz-function). The [global existence theorem for stochastic differential equations with Lipschitz coefficients](../stochastic-calculus.md#global-existence-theorem-for-stochastic-differential-equations-with-lipschitz-coefficients) gives a unique global [strong solution of a stochastic differential equation](../stochastic-calculus.md#strong-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation).

The same [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) cancellation as before gives

$$
d(e^{X_t})=e^{X_t}f(X_t)\,dB_t,\qquad
[e^X]_t=\int_0^te^{2X_s}f(X_s)^2\,ds.
$$

For $S=T_{-a}\wedge T_b$, the stopped exponential is bounded between $e^{-a}$ and $e^b$. Continuity and strict positivity of $f$ give

$$
c_{a,b}:=\min_{x\in[-a,b]}e^{2x}f(x)^2>0.
$$

The [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) therefore yields

$$
1+c_{a,b}\mathbb E[t\wedge S]
\leq\mathbb E e^{2X_{t\wedge S}}\leq e^{2b}.
$$

The [monotone convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#monotone-convergence-theorem) proves $\mathbb ES<\infty$. The bounded [optional stopping](../martingale.md#optional-sampling-theorem-for-a-supermartingale) argument is now identical to part (b)(iii), and gives

$$
\mathbb P(T_b<T_{-a})=\frac{1-e^{-a}}{e^b-e^{-a}},\qquad
\mathbb P(T_b<\infty)=e^{-b}.
$$

Passing from level hitting to strict exceedances as in part (b)(iv),

$$
\boxed{X^\star\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1).}
$$

No global positive lower bound on $f$ was needed: compact-interval positivity suffices for each exit argument. The law is independent of the particular $f$ because the [scale function](../stochastic-calculus.md#scale-function-stochastic-processes) is always $s(x)=e^x$: its [infinitesimal generator](../stochastic-process.md#infinitesimal-generator-stochastic-processes) satisfies

$$
\mathcal Ls=\frac{f^2}{2}(s''-s')=0.
$$

This is the [exponential scale function for a drift tied to diffusivity](../stochastic-calculus.md#exponential-scale-function-for-a-drift-tied-to-diffusivity). Changing $f$ changes how quickly the path is traversed, but leaves these boundary probabilities and the maximum law unchanged.
