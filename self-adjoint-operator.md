# Self-adjoint operator

↑ **Parent:** [Operator theory](linear-operator-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint_operator)

A [densely defined operator](densely-defined-operator.md) $T$ on a [Hilbert space](hilbert-space-split.md) is self-adjoint when it equals its [adjoint operator](adjoint-operator.md), including equality of their [operator domains](operator-domain.md):

$$
D(T)=D(T^*),\qquad Tv=T^*v\quad(v\in D(T)).
$$

For a [bounded linear operator](continuous-linear-operator.md) defined on the whole [Hilbert space](hilbert-space-split.md), this is equivalent to

$$
\langle Tv,w\rangle=\langle v,Tw\rangle
$$

for all vectors $v,w$. For an [unbounded self-adjoint operator](unbounded-self-adjoint-operator.md), the domain condition is essential: the inner-product identity on $D(T)$ alone gives only a [symmetric operator](symmetric-operator.md).
